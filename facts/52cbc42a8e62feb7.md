## Statement

Let \(v:[0,1]\to(0,\infty)\) be nondecreasing and concave, with the
canonical one-sided endpoint values.  For \(0<\lambda<r<1\), put
\[
\psi_\lambda(u)=
\max\{-v(u),\min\{u-\lambda,v(u)\}\},\qquad
I=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,
\]
\[
p=\frac12+\frac1{2I}\int_0^1\psi_\lambda(u)\,du,
\qquad
q(r)=\frac{\int_0^r v(u)\,du}{I}.
\]
Then
\[
p<\frac49\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q(r)>p\quad\hbox{and}\quad 4p+5q(r)>4.
\]

## Proof

We use the following notation, common to the accepted dependencies.  Since
\(v(u)+u-\lambda\) is strictly increasing, there is a unique left clipping
point \(\alpha\in[0,\lambda)\): it is \(0\) if
\(v(0)\geq\lambda\), and otherwise it is the unique solution of
\(v(\alpha)=\lambda-\alpha\).  Since
\(v(u)-u+\lambda\) is concave and is positive at \(u=\lambda\), its
nonnegative superlevel set to the right of \(\lambda\) is
\([\lambda,\beta]\) for a unique \(\beta\in(\lambda,1]\).  Thus
\[
\psi_\lambda(u)=
\begin{cases}
-v(u),&0\leq u\leq\alpha,\\
u-\lambda,&\alpha\leq u\leq\beta,\\
v(u),&\beta\leq u\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
X=\int_0^\alpha v,\qquad
R=\int_0^\lambda v,\qquad
T=\int_\beta^1v,\qquad
C=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-(\lambda-\alpha)^2}{2},
\]
and \(J=\int_0^1\psi_\lambda\).  Direct integration gives
\[
J=-X+C+T.
\]
Define
\[
A=9J+I,\qquad K=5J+8R-3I.
\]
Then
\[
p-\frac49=\frac{A}{18I},
\qquad
5p+4q(\lambda)-4=\frac{K}{2I}.                 \tag{1}
\]
It therefore suffices to prove
\[
A<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K>0.              \tag{2}
\]

If \(\alpha=0\), (2), including both possible right clipping
alternatives and strictness, is exactly accepted fact
`98a062f9a5c41e1f`.  Hence assume \(\alpha>0\).

We first reduce to full right clipping.  If \(\beta<1\), restrict the
width to \([0,\beta]\).  With
\[
I_c=I-T,\qquad J_c=J-T,
\]
and with \(A_c,K_c\) defined from \(I_c,J_c,R\) by the same formulas,
\[
A=A_c+10T,\qquad K=K_c+2T.                     \tag{3}
\]
Because \(T>0\), any counterexample \(A<0,\ K\leq0\) would give
\(A_c<0,\ K_c<0\).  The rescaling
\[
\widehat v(u)=\frac{v(\beta u)}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\alpha=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{\beta}
\]
divides all relevant areas, hence \(A_c,K_c\), by \(\beta^2\) and
turns the right clipping point into \(1\).  Consequently it remains
only to prove (2) when
\[
v(1)\geq1-\lambda.                             \tag{4}
\]

Put
\[
x=\alpha,\qquad \ell=\lambda-\alpha,\qquad
b=1-\lambda,\qquad s=b+\ell=1-x.
\]
Thus \(v(x)=\ell\).  Condition (4) is exactly the full-right clipping
condition; in general \(v(1)\) need not equal \(b\).

Let \(\mu=-D^2v\) be the nonnegative curvature measure.  Curvature at
or before \(x\) can be deleted without changing \(v\) on \([x,1]\).
More precisely, define
\[
\bar v(u)=v(u)+\int_{(0,x]}
       \bigl(t-\min\{u,t\}\bigr)\,\mu(dt)
\]
and
\[
\Delta=\frac12\int_{(0,x]}t^2\,\mu(dt).
\]
Then \(\bar v\) is still positive, nondecreasing, and concave; it
agrees with \(v\) on \([x,1]\), so the clipping points are unchanged.
Moreover,
\[
\bar I=I+\Delta,\quad
\bar R=R+\Delta,\quad
\bar X=X+\Delta,\quad
\bar J=J-\Delta,
\]
and hence
\[
\bar A=A-8\Delta,\qquad \bar K=K.               \tag{5}
\]
Thus a counterexample to (2) remains a counterexample after all
curvature at or before \(x\) is removed.  We henceforth replace \(v\)
by \(\bar v\).

Let \(h=v'_-(1)\geq0\).  The remaining curvature is supported in
\((x,1)\), and the standard concave-function representation is
\[
v(u)=\ell+h(u-x)
 +\int_{(x,1)}\bigl(\min\{u,t\}-x\bigr)\,\mu(dt).       \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\mu\) is finite because \(x>0\) and
\(\int t\,\mu(dt)<\infty\).  Thus all following integrations and
interchanges are immediate.  Representation (6) uses only
\(v(x)=\ell\), concavity, and monotonicity.  Neither (6) nor the
coefficient identities (7)--(8) below use equality at \(u=1\), or
even the lower bound in (4); that bound is used only to identify the
full-right clipping formula \(J=-X+C\), with
\(C=(b^2-\ell^2)/2\).

Define
\[
D=\frac{16\ell-9(b+\ell)^2}{2},\qquad
K_0=\frac{5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2}{2},
\]
\[
a_*(t)=\frac{-t^2+2t+9x^2-2x}{2},
\]
and
\[
k_*(t)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(t-x)(16\ell-5t+11x-6)}2,
  &x\leq t\leq\lambda,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{8\ell^2+3t^2-6t-3x^2+6x}{2},
  &\lambda\leq t\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]
Applying the linear functionals \(A\) and \(K\) to (6), or simply
integrating each truncated ray, gives
\[
A=-D+a_*(1)h+\int_{(x,1)}a_*(t)\,\mu(dt),        \tag{7}
\]
\[
K=K_0+k_*(1)h+\int_{(x,1)}k_*(t)\,\mu(dt).       \tag{8}
\]
Notice that \(a_*(t)>0\) on \([x,1]\): its numerator equals
\(8x^2>0\) at \(x\), and its derivative is \(2(1-t)\geq0\).
Therefore \(A<0\) implies
\[
D>0,\qquad
a_*(1)h+\int_{(x,1)}a_*(t)\,\mu(dt)<D.           \tag{9}
\]

We now use the dimension-free pointwise dual proved in accepted fact
`47f573be580936d8`.  To record precisely the part being used, set
\[
p_0=\frac{\ell}{s},\qquad q=\frac{t-x}{s},\qquad
G(q)=8x^2+s^2q(2-q).
\]
Then
\[
2a_*(x+qs)=G(q),\qquad
2k_*(x+qs)=
\begin{cases}
s^2q(16p_0-6-5q),&0\leq q\leq p_0,\\
s^2\bigl(8p_0^2-3q(2-q)\bigr),&p_0\leq q\leq1.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{10}
\]
The proof of `47f573be580936d8` establishes the following alternative
on the whole closed interval \(0\leq q\leq1\):

* if \(k_*(1)\geq0\), then \(k_*(t)\geq0\) for every
  \(t\in[x,1]\), and \(K_0>0\);
* if \(k_*(1)<0\), then, with
  \[
  \eta=-\frac{k_*(1)}{a_*(1)}>0,
  \]
  one has
  \[
  k_*(t)+\eta a_*(t)\geq0\quad(x\leq t\leq1),
  \qquad K_0-\eta D\geq0.                       \tag{11}
  \]

The closed left and right endpoint specializations of (10)--(11) are
also the accepted facts `e6e9c30888a62b44` and
`26736129ee04123f`, respectively.  In particular, atoms at
\(t=\lambda\), or limiting atoms at either endpoint, cause no
strictness gap.  Accepted fact `644583f5590ae85e` gives the
corresponding finite-curvature localization of any putative bad
width; (7)--(11) apply directly to the full curvature measure and
therefore avoid enumerating the localized three-kink orders.

If the first alternative holds, (8) immediately yields
\[
K\geq K_0>0.
\]
If the second alternative holds, integrate (11), use \(h\geq0\),
and then use the strict budget (9):
\[
\begin{aligned}
K
&\geq K_0-\eta\left(
 a_*(1)h+\int_{(x,1)}a_*(t)\,\mu(dt)\right)\\
&>K_0-\eta D\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (2) under full right clipping.  Equations (3)--(5) then
prove it for every \(\alpha>0\), and the accepted \(\alpha=0\) theorem
completes the proof of (2) in all cases.

Finally suppose \(p<4/9\).  By (1), \(A<0\), hence \(K>0\).  If
\(q(\lambda)\leq p\), then
\[
5p+4q(\lambda)-4\leq9p-4<0,
\]
contradicting \(K>0\) and (1).  Thus \(q(\lambda)>p\).  Also (1)
gives
\[
5p+4q(\lambda)>4.
\]
Since \(v>0\) and \(r>\lambda\), \(q(r)>q(\lambda)\).  Therefore
\[
q(r)>p,\qquad
4p+5q(r)>4p+5q(\lambda)>5p+4q(\lambda)>4,
\]
as required.
