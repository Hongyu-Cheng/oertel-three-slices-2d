## Statement

For a planar convex body \(A\), write
\[
d_A(x)=\inf\{|A\cap Q|:Q\text{ is a closed halfplane containing }x\},
\qquad
D_A(v)=\{x\in A:d_A(x)\geq v\}.
\]
Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\]
put \(A_1=\Delta\), and take either ordered pair
\[
(A_0,A_2)=\left(\Delta,\frac14\Delta\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(A_0,A_2)=\left(\frac14\Delta,\Delta\right).
\]
Let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A_0)\cup
(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr).
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact rational polytope whose integer-height sections are exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\).  Its area triple lies in the full strict residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  With
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
one has, for every
\[
t-\frac{4a_1}{9}\leq u\leq\frac{4m}{9},
\]
and every \(x_0\in D_{A_0}(u)\), \(x_2\in D_{A_2}(u)\),
\[
\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\notin D_{A_1}(t-u).
\]
Thus the proposed intersection fails for the full continuum of \(u\), including both endpoints.  Separately, if \(g=(1/3,1/3)\), then the true locked conclusion holds:
\[
h_S((1,g))\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Because \(\Delta\) is convex and contains the origin,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac{\Delta+\frac14\Delta}{2}
=\frac58\Delta
\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
The sections of \(C\) at heights \(0\) and \(2\) are plainly \(A_0\) and \(A_2\).  If a convex combination of points at heights \(0,1,2\) has height \(1\), its weights \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) satisfy \(\lambda_0=\lambda_2\).  Its planar component is therefore a convex combination of a point of \(A_1\) and a point of \((A_0+A_2)/2\), hence belongs to \(A_1\) by (1).  The reverse inclusion is part of the definition of \(C\), so the height-one section is exactly \(A_1\).  Since the height coordinate of \(C\) lies in \([0,2]\), these are all its integer-height sections.

The exact areas and target are
\[
\{a_0,a_2\}=\left\{\frac12,\frac1{32}\right\},
\qquad
a_1=\frac12,
\qquad
T=\frac{33}{32},
\qquad
t=\frac{11}{48},
\qquad
m=\frac1{32}.
\tag{2}
\]
In the normalization of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`,
\[
M=\frac12,\qquad r=\frac{m}{M}=\frac1{16},\qquad
s=\frac{a_1}{M}=1.
\]
Here \(0<r<9/25\), and the strict residual bounds are
\[
L(r)=1-r=\frac{15}{16}<1<
\frac{17}{16}=1+r=U(r).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus this compatible triple lies in the asserted full strict residual strip, with either ordering convention for the two outer slices covered by \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\) and \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\).

Equations (2) give the complete closed allocation interval
\[
\left[t-\frac{4a_1}{9},\frac{4m}{9}\right]
=\left[\frac1{144},\frac1{72}\right].
\tag{4}
\]
Fix any \(u\) in (4), including either endpoint, and any
\[
x_0\in D_{A_0}(u),\qquad x_2\in D_{A_2}(u).
\]
Depth regions are subsets of their defining bodies, so
\[
z:=\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}
\in\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac58\Delta.
\tag{5}
\]
Writing \(z=(z_1,z_2)\), equation (5) gives
\[
z_1,z_2\geq0,\qquad z_1+z_2\leq\frac58.
\tag{6}
\]
The closed halfplane
\[
Q_z=\{(w_1,w_2):w_1+w_2\leq z_1+z_2\}
\]
contains \(z\).  Its intersection with \(A_1=\Delta\) is a right triangle, so (6) yields
\[
|A_1\cap Q_z|
=\frac{(z_1+z_2)^2}{2}
\leq\frac{25}{128}
<\frac{31}{144},
\qquad
\frac{31}{144}-\frac{25}{128}
=\frac{23}{1152}>0.
\tag{7}
\]
On the other hand, (2) and (4) give
\[
t-u\in\left[\frac{31}{144},\frac29\right].
\tag{8}
\]
By (7) and the definition of planar halfspace depth,
\[
d_{A_1}(z)\leq |A_1\cap Q_z|
<\frac{31}{144}\leq t-u.
\]
Therefore \(z\notin D_{A_1}(t-u)\).  The argument used only that \(u\) belongs to the full closed interval (4), so it proves failure simultaneously for every such \(u\), including \(u=t-4a_1/9\) and \(u=4m/9\).

Finally, this failure is only a failure of the proposed point-pair route.  Accepted fact `23dcab2387927bc4` proves for the ordering
\[
(A_0,A_2)=\left(\Delta,\frac14\Delta\right)
\]
that, at the centroid \(g=(1/3,1/3)\) of \(A_1\),
\[
h_S((1,g))\geq\frac{11}{48}=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
For the reverse ordering, the affine isometry
\[
(x,w)\longmapsto(2-x,w)
\]
maps the first three-slice set onto the second, fixes \((1,g)\), maps closed halfspaces bijectively to closed halfspaces, and preserves the slice measure.  Thus the same depth bound holds after swapping \(A_0,A_2\).  Hence the true locked depth conclusion holds in both outer-slice orders even though the stated depth-region intersection is empty for the entire allowed continuum of \(u\).
