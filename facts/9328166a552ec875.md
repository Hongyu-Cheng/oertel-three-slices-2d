## Statement

Let \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty, compact, and convex, let \(r:\mathbb S^1\to\mathbb R\) be continuous, and define
\[
\ell_A(q)=\min_{z\in A}q\cdot z,\qquad
s(q)=\max\{\ell_A(q),r(q)\}.
\]
Then
\[
W:=A\cap\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}\{z:q\cdot z\ge r(q)\}
=\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}\{z:q\cdot z\ge s(q)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(W\ne\varnothing\) if and only if both of the following conditions hold:

1. \(s(q)+s(-q)\le0\) for every \(q\in\mathbb S^1\).

2. For every noncollinear triple \(q_1,q_2,q_3\in\mathbb S^1\) and every \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3>0\) satisfying
\[
\alpha_1q_1+\alpha_2q_2+\alpha_3q_3=0,
\]
one has
\[
\alpha_1s(q_1)+\alpha_2s(q_2)+\alpha_3s(q_3)\le0.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus continuity is not the missing hypothesis: the criterion is a family of numerical cross-direction inequalities.

For the accepted three-slice profile, put \(t=2T/9\), \(r(q)=\rho_q(t)\), and \(A=A_1\). Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` identifies (1) with the exact depth region \(D_t\). Under a global counterexample, the same accepted fact produces a noncollinear triple and positive weights for which
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_iq_i=0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i\rho_{q_i}(t)>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, it is enough to prove the reverse inequality in (3) for every positive-spanning triple. Accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` supplies continuity of \(q\mapsto\rho_q(t)\) and the unique continuous allocation \(q\mapsto a_*(q)\), but these properties do not imply that reverse inequality.

Indeed, there is an abstract model satisfying a stronger antipodal structure. Write \(q=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), take \(A\) to be the closed unit disk, and set
\[
r(q)=\cos(3\theta).
\tag{4}
\]
On the allocation interval \([0,1]\), set
\[
U_q(a)=r(q)+a-\frac12,\qquad
V_q(a)=r(q)+\frac12-a.
\tag{5}
\]
The function \(U_q\) is strictly increasing in \(a\), \(V_q\) is strictly decreasing, and
\[
\min_{0\le a\le1}\max\{U_q(a),V_q(a)\}=r(q)
\]
has the unique continuous optimizer \(a_*(q)=1/2\). In addition,
\[
r(-q)=-r(q),\qquad
a_*(-q)=1-a_*(q),
\tag{6}
\]
\[
U_{-q}(1-a)=-U_q(a),\qquad
V_{-q}(1-a)=-V_q(a).
\tag{7}
\]
Nevertheless, its Wulff intersection is empty. This model is an obstruction to a proof from the accepted continuity and antipodal/topological properties; it is not a counterexample to the locked geometric theorem.

## Proof

First prove (1). Every \(z\in A\) satisfies \(q\cdot z\ge\ell_A(q)\) for every \(q\), so the left side of (1) is contained in the right side. Conversely, suppose \(z\notin A\), and let \(x\in A\) minimize \(\|z-x\|\). With \(q=(x-z)/\|x-z\|\), the projection inequality
\[
(z-x)\cdot(y-x)\le0\qquad(y\in A)
\]
gives \(q\cdot y\ge q\cdot x>q\cdot z\) for every \(y\in A\). Hence \(q\cdot z<\ell_A(q)\le s(q)\), so \(z\) does not belong to the right side of (1). A point in that right side therefore lies in \(A\) and also satisfies every threshold \(r(q)\), proving equality. Compactness of \(A\) also gives continuity of \(\ell_A\), and hence of \(s\), although the intersection criterion below only needs finiteness.

If \(z\in W\), adding the constraints for \(q\) and \(-q\) gives
\[
0=q\cdot z+(-q)\cdot z\ge s(q)+s(-q).
\]
Likewise, multiplying three constraints by positive \(\alpha_i\) and adding gives
\[
0=\left(\sum_i\alpha_iq_i\right)\cdot z
\ge\sum_i\alpha_i s(q_i).
\]
Thus conditions 1 and 2 are necessary.

Assume conditions 1 and 2. Any two halfplanes
\[
H_q=\{z:q\cdot z\ge s(q)\}
\]
intersect. If their normals are linearly independent, the two boundary equations have a solution. Equal normals give nested halfplanes. Opposite normals give the scalar interval
\[
s(q)\le q\cdot z\le-s(-q),
\]
which is nonempty by condition 1.

Let \(e_1,e_2\) be the coordinate unit vectors. Condition 1 makes
\[
R=H_{e_1}\cap H_{-e_1}\cap H_{e_2}\cap H_{-e_2}
\]
a nonempty compact rectangle. If \(\bigcap_qH_q\) were empty, the open sets
\[
R\cap\{z:q\cdot z<s(q)\}\qquad(q\in\mathbb S^1)
\]
would cover \(R\). Compactness would give a finite subcover. Planar Helly would then give three of the corresponding closed halfplanes with empty intersection. Since every pair intersects, this triple is minimally infeasible.

For completeness, write its inequalities as \(Qz\ge b\), where the rows of \(Q\) are \(q_i^{\mathsf T}\) and \(b_i=s(q_i)\). Infeasibility means that \(b\) is not in the closed polyhedral cone
\[
\{Qz-u:z\in\mathbb R^2,\ u\in\mathbb R_+^3\}.
\]
Strict separation from this cone supplies \(\alpha\ge0\) such that
\[
Q^{\mathsf T}\alpha=0,\qquad \alpha\cdot b>0.
\tag{8}
\]
Every \(\alpha_i\) is positive, because otherwise (8) would already certify infeasibility of a one- or two-element subfamily, contrary to pairwise intersection. The directions cannot be collinear: a collinear positive dependence reduces (8) to a positive multiple of \(s(q)+s(-q)>0\), contradicting condition 1. Hence condition 2 applies and contradicts (8). This proves sufficiency and the criterion.

Now verify its relevance to the accepted profile. Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` gives finiteness of every \(\rho_q(t)\), the exact identity
\[
D_t=A_1\cap\bigcap_q\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q(t)\},
\]
and, under a global counterexample at \(t=2T/9\), a minimal infeasible triple of profile halfplanes. Its Farkas weights are positive and give exactly (3). In that regime every slice area is less than \(T/2\). If \(\mu=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), then
\[
a_1+\mu=T-\max\{a_0,a_2\}>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9}=t.
\]
Therefore the range hypothesis of accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` holds, and that fact proves that both \(\rho_q(t)\) and its unique optimizer \(a_*(q)\) depend continuously on \(q\). These verify all continuity and selection hypotheses proposed in the subgoal. The exact unresolved implication is the reverse of (3). It is the profile-halfplane version of the balance condition (2); accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` removes the \(A_1\)-support term by showing that a global failure has a three-profile-halfplane certificate rather than a one- or two-constraint certificate involving \(A_1\).

It remains to verify the obstruction. For the unit disk, \(\ell_A(q)=-1\), while (4) takes values in \([-1,1]\), so \(s=r\). Formula (4) is continuous and satisfies \(r(-q)=-r(q)\). Equations (5) give
\[
\max\{U_q(a),V_q(a)\}=r(q)+\left|a-\frac12\right|,
\]
so the unique optimizer and value are as stated. Direct substitution proves (6) and (7). Replacing \(U_q(0)\) by \(-\infty\) in the first branch and \(V_q(1)\) by \(-\infty\) in the second branch, as in the accepted zero-demand convention, leaves the two endpoint objective values and the unique interior optimizer unchanged.

Let \(q_1,q_2,q_3\) have angles \(0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3\). Then
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=0,\qquad r(q_1)=r(q_2)=r(q_3)=1.
\]
If a point satisfied the three Wulff inequalities, adding them would give
\[
0=(q_1+q_2+q_3)\cdot z\ge3,
\]
which is impossible. Thus the Wulff intersection is empty even though every antipodal pair is exactly balanced and the allocation has the strong equivariance (6)-(7).

Finally, an interval-valued selection itself has no degree obstruction. If a continuous \(a:\mathbb S^1\to[0,1]\) satisfies \(a(-q)=1-a(q)\), then
\[
H(q,\tau)=(1-\tau)a(q)+\frac{\tau}{2}
\]
is an equivariant homotopy to the constant \(1/2\), since \(H(-q,\tau)=1-H(q,\tau)\). The example already uses that constant selection. Hence a KKM, degree, or antipodal argument based only on continuity of the allocation cannot establish nonemptiness. A successful proof must derive the numerical triple-balance inequality from additional cross-direction convex-geometric structure of the cap profiles.
