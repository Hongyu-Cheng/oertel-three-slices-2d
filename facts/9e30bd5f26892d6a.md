## Statement

The all-double integer calibration from fact 0835a8b11411ade7 is not realizable by convex two-dimensional polytopes \(A_0,A_2,K\) in one common affine sign plane satisfying the offset and midpoint conditions of fact 0e51a47a9add571f.

More generally, in every such common-plane realization for which the four nonzero \(A_2\)-cell areas satisfy
\[
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=q_{123}=|A_2|/4,
\]
one has
\[
\bigl(1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)\bigr)^2 r_{123}\le q_{123}.
\]
Since \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\), this also gives \(r_{123}<q_{123}/4\).

## Proof

Write \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), set
\[
\ell_i=f_i+L_i,
\qquad
\gamma=1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>2,
\]
and let
\[
T_\gamma=\{x:\ell_1(x)\le0,\ \ell_2(x)\le0,\ \ell_3(x)\le0\},
\qquad
T_1=\{x:f_1(x)\le0,\ f_2(x)\le0,\ f_3(x)\le0\}.
\]

First consider \(Q=A_2\).  For each \(i\), let \(C_i=Q\cap\{\ell_i\ge0\}\).  Under the stated four-cell support, the interiors of \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) are pairwise disjoint: a nonempty open intersection would lie in an \(A_2\)-cell with at least two positive \(\ell\)-coordinates, whereas every such cell has prescribed area zero.  Up to cutting lines of area zero,
\[
|C_1|=q_{23},\qquad |C_2|=q_{13},\qquad |C_3|=q_{12}.
\]
Thus the equal-quarter hypothesis gives
\[
|C_1|+|C_2|+|C_3|=3|Q|/4.
\]
It also gives
\[
\sqrt{|C_1|/|Q|}+\sqrt{|C_2|/|Q|}+\sqrt{|C_3|/|Q|}=3/2.
\]
The sharp three-cap inequality and its equality characterization from fact 8234d1112ec2f031 therefore imply that \(Q\) is a triangle and the three lines \(\ell_i=0\) are its medial lines, with the halfplanes \(\ell_i\ge0\) cutting off its three corner triangles.  Consequently the opposite halfplanes cut out the central medial triangle globally:
\[
T_\gamma=Q_{123}.
\]
In particular,
\[
|T_\gamma|=q_{123}.
\]

It remains to compare this source central triangle with the middle central sign triangle.  The affine map
\[
F:x\longmapsto(f_1(x),f_2(x),f_3(x))
\]
is an affine isomorphism from the common plane onto \(\{z_1+z_2+z_3=-1\}\), by fact 0e51a47a9add571f.  The image of \(T_1\) is
\[
\Delta_1=\{z:z_i\le0,\ z_1+z_2+z_3=-1\}.
\]
For \(x\in T_\gamma\), put \(w_i=f_i(x)+L_i\).  Then \(w_i\le0\) and
\[
w_1+w_2+w_3=-1+(L_1+L_2+L_3)=-\gamma.
\]
Hence
\[
F(T_\gamma)=-(L_1,L_2,L_3)+\gamma\Delta_1.
\]
Thus \(T_\gamma\) is a translate of the dilation by factor \(\gamma\) of \(T_1\).  Area therefore scales by \(\gamma^2\):
\[
|T_\gamma|=\gamma^2|T_1|.
\]

The middle central cell is \(R_{123}=K\cap T_1\).  Therefore
\[
r_{123}=|K\cap T_1|
\le |T_1|
=\frac{|T_\gamma|}{\gamma^2}
=\frac{q_{123}}{\gamma^2},
\]
which is the claimed universal inequality.  Since \(\gamma>2\), it also yields \(r_{123}<q_{123}/4\).

For the integer calibration of fact 0835a8b11411ade7,
\[
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=q_{123}=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
r_{123}=3.
\]
The universal inequality would require
\[
3\gamma^2\le1.
\]
But \(\gamma>2\) implies \(3\gamma^2>12>1\), a contradiction.  Hence no common-plane \(A_2,K\) pair realizes even these source and middle cells.  A fortiori no triple \(A_0,A_2,K\) can realize the full \(P,Q,R\) table, crossings, and midpoint inclusion.
