## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Write
\[
\bar M=|A_0|\ge \bar m=|A_2|,\qquad
\bar k=|K|,\qquad
\bar T=\bar M+\bar m+\bar k,\qquad
\bar t=\frac{2\bar T}{9}.
\]
Assume the residual-area hypothesis of accepted fact \(15bb2c1680a0254e\): with
\[
r=\frac{\bar m}{\bar M},\qquad s=\frac{\bar k}{\bar M},\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
one has \(0<r<\rho\) and \(L(r)<s<U(r)\), where
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le 9/25,\\
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le 1/2,\\
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions whose linear parts are pairwise nonparallel and such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(L_1,L_2,L_3\in\mathbb R\) satisfy
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,\qquad
L_1=\min_{A_0}f_1,
\]
and put
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0.
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), define
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad r_i=f_i,
\]
on \(A_0,A_2,K\), respectively, and define the original cap areas
\[
\bar u_i=|A_0\cap\{p_i\le0\}|,\qquad
\bar v_i=|A_2\cap\{q_i\le0\}|,\qquad
\bar c_i=|K\cap\{r_i\le0\}|.
\]
Assume
\[
\bar c_i+\bar u_i+\bar v_i=\bar t\quad(i=2,3),
\qquad
\bar c_i+\bar m\ge\bar t\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]

Define
\[
\Phi_0(w)=(p_1(w)-\delta,p_2(w)),\qquad
\Phi_2(w)=(q_1(w)+\delta+2,q_2(w)),
\]
\[
\Phi_1(w)=(r_1(w)+1,r_2(w)),
\]
and
\[
P=\Phi_0(A_0),\qquad Q=\Phi_2(A_2),\qquad R=\Phi_1(K).
\]
All three maps are affine bijections with the same linear part. If
\[
J=|\det D(f_1,f_2)|>0,
\]
then every planar area is multiplied by \(J\). In particular, set
\[
M=|P|=J\bar M,\qquad m=|Q|=J\bar m,\qquad
k=|R|=J\bar k,\qquad t=J\bar t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and
\[
u_i=J\bar u_i,\qquad v_i=J\bar v_i,\qquad c_i=J\bar c_i.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R,\qquad \min_Px=-\delta.
\]
On \(P,Q,R\), respectively, the source functions are related to the normalized coordinates by
\[
(p_1,p_2,p_3)=(x+\delta,\ y,\ -x-y),
\]
\[
(q_1,q_2,q_3)=(x-\delta-2,\ y,\ -x-y),
\]
\[
(r_1,r_2,r_3)=(x-1,\ y,\ -x-y).
\]
Thus the direction-\(2\) cutting lines all become \(y=0\), the direction-\(3\) cutting lines all become \(x+y=0\), and the direction-\(1\) cutting lines become
\[
x=-\delta\ \text{on }P,\qquad
x=\delta+2\ \text{on }Q,\qquad
x=1\ \text{on }R.
\]
The corresponding lower caps are
\[
P\cap\{x\le-\delta\},\qquad
Q\cap\{x\le\delta+2\},\qquad
R\cap\{x\le1\}.
\]

For \(E\in\{P,Q,R\}\), define
\[
\alpha_E=|E\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
\beta_E=|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t.
\]
Moreover, with
\[
b=|Q\cap\{x\ge\delta+2\}|,\qquad
d=|R\cap\{x\ge1\}|,
\]
one has
\[
b=m-v_1,\qquad d=k-c_1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
v_1<m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b>0,
\]
\[
c_1+m\ge t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k-d+m\ge t,
\]
and
\[
c_1+v_1\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]
All these identities are unchanged if strict and non-strict inequalities are interchanged only on a displayed cutting line.

## Proof

Write
\[
f_i(w)=\lambda_i(w)+\gamma_i,
\]
where \(\lambda_i\) is linear. Since the linear parts of \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) are nonzero and nonparallel, the linear map
\[
\Lambda(w)=(\lambda_1(w),\lambda_2(w))
\]
is invertible. Hence
\[
\Psi(w)=(f_1(w),f_2(w))
\]
is an affine bijection with linear part \(\Lambda\). The three displayed maps are
\[
\Phi_0=\Psi+(-L_1-\delta,-L_2),
\]
\[
\Phi_2=\Psi+(L_1+\delta+2,L_2),
\]
\[
\Phi_1=\Psi+(1,0).
\]
They therefore have the common invertible linear part \(\Lambda\), and their common absolute Jacobian is
\[
J=|\det\Lambda|=|\det D(f_1,f_2)|>0.
\]

Let \(a\in A_0\), \(q\in A_2\), and \(z=(a+q)/2\). Affineness gives
\[
\Psi(z)=\frac{\Psi(a)+\Psi(q)}2.
\]
The two outer translation vectors have average
\[
\frac{(-L_1-\delta,-L_2)+(L_1+\delta+2,L_2)}2=(1,0).
\]
It follows that
\[
\frac{\Phi_0(a)+\Phi_2(q)}2
=\Psi(z)+(1,0)
=\Phi_1(z).
\]
Since \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\), every such point belongs to \(R\), proving
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R.
\]

For \(w\in A_0\), the first coordinate of \(\Phi_0(w)\) is
\[
x=f_1(w)-L_1-\delta.
\]
Because \(L_1=\min_{A_0}f_1\), this is at least \(-\delta\), and compactness of \(A_0\) supplies a point where equality holds. Therefore
\[
P\subseteq\{x\ge-\delta\},\qquad \min_Px=-\delta.
\]
In particular, \(x=-\delta\) is a supporting line that touches \(P\).

The affine identity \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\) and the definition of \(\delta\) give
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3
=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)
=\delta,
\]
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3
=-1+(L_1+L_2+L_3)
=-\delta-2,
\]
and
\[
r_1+r_2+r_3=-1.
\]
On \(P\), the definitions \(x=p_1-\delta\) and \(y=p_2\) therefore give
\[
p_1=x+\delta,\qquad p_2=y,\qquad
p_3=\delta-p_1-p_2=-x-y.
\]
On \(Q\), the definitions \(x=q_1+\delta+2\) and \(y=q_2\) give
\[
q_1=x-\delta-2,\qquad q_2=y,\qquad
q_3=-\delta-2-q_1-q_2=-x-y.
\]
On \(R\), the definitions \(x=r_1+1\) and \(y=r_2\) give
\[
r_1=x-1,\qquad r_2=y,\qquad
r_3=-1-r_1-r_2=-x-y.
\]

These identities give every sign convention directly. On each body, the direction-\(2\) source function is \(\le0,=0,\ge0\), respectively, exactly when \(y\le0,=0,\ge0\). The direction-\(3\) source function is \(\le0,=0,\ge0\), respectively, exactly when \(x+y\ge0,=0,\le0\); its strict signs are reversed in the same way. In the contact direction,
\[
p_1\le0\Longleftrightarrow x\le-\delta,\qquad
q_1\le0\Longleftrightarrow x\le\delta+2,\qquad
r_1\le0\Longleftrightarrow x\le1,
\]
with equality and both strict signs corresponding identically. This proves all cutting-line and cap correspondences. Since \(p_1\ge0\) on \(A_0\), its lower cap is precisely the touching set on \(x=-\delta\).

For every measurable \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) and each \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), the affine change-of-variables formula gives
\[
|\Phi_j(B)|=J|B|.
\]
Thus
\[
|P|=J\bar M,\qquad |Q|=J\bar m,\qquad |R|=J\bar k,
\]
and
\[
J\bar t
=\frac{2J(\bar M+\bar m+\bar k)}9
=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9=t.
\]
Every cap area is multiplied by this same \(J\).

The residual regime from accepted fact \(15bb2c1680a0254e\) is preserved exactly because
\[
\frac mM=\frac{J\bar m}{J\bar M}=r,\qquad
\frac kM=\frac{J\bar k}{J\bar M}=s.
\]
Hence the same strict inequalities \(0<r<\rho\) and \(L(r)<s<U(r)\), including their piecewise endpoints, hold after normalization. The ordering \(M\ge m\) is also preserved.

The direction-\(2\) cap correspondences and area scaling give
\[
\alpha_P=J\bar u_2=u_2,\qquad
\alpha_Q=J\bar v_2=v_2,\qquad
\alpha_R=J\bar c_2=c_2.
\]
Multiplying the original direction-\(2\) crossing equality by \(J\) yields
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R
=J(\bar u_2+\bar v_2+\bar c_2)
=J\bar t=t.
\]
For direction \(3\), the sign reversal \(p_3,q_3,r_3\le0\Longleftrightarrow x+y\ge0\) gives
\[
\beta_P=J\bar u_3=u_3,\qquad
\beta_Q=J\bar v_3=v_3,\qquad
\beta_R=J\bar c_3=c_3.
\]
Multiplying the direction-\(3\) crossing equality by \(J\) therefore gives
\[
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t.
\]

Each displayed cutting boundary is an affine line and hence has planar area zero. Consequently, replacing \(<\) by \(\le\), or \(>\) by \(\ge\), changes none of the cap areas. In particular,
\[
v_1=|Q\cap\{x\le\delta+2\}|
\]
and
\[
b=|Q\cap\{x\ge\delta+2\}|
=|Q\cap\{x>\delta+2\}|
=m-v_1.
\]
Likewise,
\[
c_1=|R\cap\{x\le1\}|
\]
and
\[
d=|R\cap\{x\ge1\}|
=|R\cap\{x>1\}|
=k-c_1.
\]
Thus
\[
v_1<m\Longleftrightarrow m-v_1>0
\Longleftrightarrow b>0.
\]
Also,
\[
c_1+m\ge t
\Longleftrightarrow
(k-d)+m\ge t
\Longleftrightarrow
k-d+m\ge t.
\]
Finally,
\[
c_1+v_1\ge t
\Longleftrightarrow
(k-d)+(m-b)\ge t
\Longleftrightarrow
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]
This proves the required normalization and exact equivalence without using or asserting the missing tail inequality.
