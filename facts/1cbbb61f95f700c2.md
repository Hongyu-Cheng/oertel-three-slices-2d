## Statement

Let
\[
A=[-1/2,1/2]^2,\qquad B=[-1/8,1/8]^2,
\]
and set
\[
A_0=A_1=A,\qquad A_2=B,\qquad
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A)\cup
(\{1\}\times A)\cup(\{2\}\times B)\bigr).
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact rational polytope whose integer-height sections are
exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\).  For
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),\qquad
\mu=\mathcal H_2|_S,\qquad T=\mu(S)=\frac{33}{16},
\]
the point
\[
p=(1/2,0,0)\in\operatorname{conv}(S)
\]
has ordinary three-dimensional halfspace depth
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\mu}(p)=1\ge\frac T4=\frac{33}{64}.
\]
Nevertheless,
\[
\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)\le\frac9{16}<\frac89
=\frac89\operatorname{depth}_{\mu}(p).
\]
Hence the universal multiplicative \(8/9\)-rounding statement is false.
On the other hand,
\[
h_S((0,0,0))=\frac12>\frac{11}{24}=\frac{2T}{9},
\]
so this example does not refute the locked theorem or the threshold-only
rounding statement.

## Proof

First verify compatibility and the integer sections.  Since \(A\) and \(B\)
are axis-parallel squares centered at the origin,
\[
\frac{A+B}{2}=[-5/16,5/16]^2\subseteq A.
\]
The first-coordinate projection of \(C\) is \([0,2]\), so there are no
integer-height sections outside \(0,1,2\).  Heights \(0\) and \(2\) are
extreme, and hence their sections are \(A\) and \(B\), respectively.  If
\((1,w)\in C\), aggregate a convex representation by height:
\[
(1,w)=\lambda_0(0,a_0)+\lambda_1(1,a_1)+\lambda_2(2,b),
\]
where \(a_0,a_1\in A\), \(b\in B\), the coefficients are nonnegative, and
sum to \(1\).  The first-coordinate equation gives
\(\lambda_0=\lambda_2=t\in[0,1/2]\).  Therefore
\[
w=(1-2t)a_1+2t\frac{a_0+b}{2}\in A,
\]
because both \(a_1\) and \((a_0+b)/2\) lie in \(A\).  The reverse inclusion
at height \(1\) is part of the definition of \(C\).  This proves the claimed
sections.  Their areas are \(1,1,1/16\), giving \(T=33/16\).

We next compute the depth of \(p\).  It is enough first to consider a proper
closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(p\).  Such a halfspace has
the form
\[
H_{\alpha,v}
=\{(x,w):\alpha(x-1/2)+\langle v,w\rangle\le0\},
\]
where \((\alpha,v)\ne(0,0)\).  Suppose \(v\ne0\), and write
\[
F(c)=\bigl|A\cap\{w:\langle v,w\rangle\le c\}\bigr|.
\]
The boundary line of every such planar cap has area zero.  Central symmetry
of \(A\) therefore gives
\[
F(-c)=1-F(c).
\]
The contributions of slices \(0\) and \(1\) to \(H_{\alpha,v}\) are
\[
F(\alpha/2)\quad\text{and}\quad F(-\alpha/2),
\]
so they sum exactly to \(1\).  The slice-\(2\) contribution is nonnegative,
and hence \(\mu(H_{\alpha,v})\ge1\).

If \(v=0\), then \(\alpha>0\) gives the horizontal halfspace
\(\{x\le1/2\}\), of mass \(1\), while \(\alpha<0\) gives
\(\{x\ge1/2\}\), of mass \(1+1/16\).  Thus every boundary-through-\(p\)
halfspace has mass at least \(1\).  For an arbitrary closed halfspace
containing \(p\), translating its boundary parallel toward \(p\) produces a
boundary-through-\(p\) halfspace contained in the original one.  Its mass is
at least \(1\), so the original mass is also at least \(1\).  The halfspace
\(\{x\le1/2\}\) has mass exactly \(1\), proving
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\mu}(p)=1.
\]

We now upper-bound the depth of every supported point by explicit finite
tilts.  If \(K\) is either \(A\) or \(B\), \(z\in K\), and a line is drawn
through \(z\), the two closed planar sides have total area \(|K|\), because
the line has area zero.  After choosing the smaller side and orienting its
normal, there is a nonzero \(v\) such that
\[
\bigl|K\cap\{w:\langle v,w-z\rangle\le0\}\bigr|
\le\frac{|K|}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Take first \(y=(0,z)\) with \(z\in A\), and choose \(v\) by (1).  Compactness
allows a finite \(R>0\) such that
\[
-R<\min_{w\in A}\langle v,w-z\rangle,\qquad
-2R<\min_{w\in B}\langle v,w-z\rangle.
\]
The closed halfspace
\[
\{(x,w):\langle v,w-z\rangle\le-Rx\}
\]
contains \(y\), cuts from slice \(0\) exactly the cap in (1), and misses
slices \(1\) and \(2\).  Hence \(h_S(y)\le1/2\).

For \(y=(2,z)\) with \(z\in B\), choose \(v\) by (1) for \(K=B\), and take
a finite \(R>0\) so large that
\[
-2R<\min_{w\in A}\langle v,w-z\rangle,\qquad
-R<\min_{w\in A}\langle v,w-z\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
\{(x,w):\langle v,w-z\rangle\le R(x-2)\}
\]
contains \(y\), misses slices \(0\) and \(1\), and cuts a cap of area at
most \(|B|/2=1/32\) from slice \(2\).  Thus \(h_S(y)\le1/32\).

Finally, take \(y=(1,z)\) with \(z\in A\), and choose \(v\) by (1) for
\(K=A\).  Choose a finite \(R>0\) satisfying
\[
-R<\min_{w\in A}\langle v,w-z\rangle,\qquad
R>\max_{w\in B}\langle v,w-z\rangle.
\]
The closed halfspace
\[
\{(x,w):\langle v,w-z\rangle\le R(x-1)\}
\]
contains \(y\), misses slice \(0\), cuts from slice \(1\) the cap in (1),
and contains all of slice \(2\).  Its mass is at most
\[
\frac12+\frac1{16}=\frac9{16}.
\]
Combining the three slice cases proves
\[
\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)\le\frac9{16}.
\]
Since \(9/16<8/9\) and \(\operatorname{depth}_{\mu}(p)=1\), no supported
point retains \(8/9\) of the actual depth of \(p\).

It remains to check explicitly that the locked conclusion is not contradicted.
Let \(y_0=(0,0,0)\).  Translate the boundary of any closed halfspace
containing \(y_0\) toward \(y_0\).  A boundary-through-\(y_0\) halfspace has
the form
\[
\{(x,w):\alpha x+\langle v,w\rangle\le0\}.
\]
If \(v\ne0\), its slice-\(0\) cap through the center of the centrally
symmetric square \(A\) has area exactly \(1/2\).  If \(v=0\), the halfspace
contains all of slice \(0\).  Thus every such halfspace has mass at least
\(1/2\).  The finite-tilt construction for slice \(0\), with \(z=0\), gives
a halfspace of mass exactly \(1/2\), so \(h_S(y_0)=1/2\).  Finally,
\[
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{2}{9}\frac{33}{16}=\frac{11}{24}
<\frac12.
\]
This proves both the counterexample to actual-depth \(8/9\) preservation and
the stated non-refutation of the threshold-only claim.
