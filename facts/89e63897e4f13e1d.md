## Statement

Assume the Regime A scalar hypotheses and notation of accepted fact `879a3c45b5f50670`. Write the two closed endpoint cell rows as
\[
(p,x_i,x_\ell,\zeta)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(q,b_i,b_\ell,\omega),
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
x_i=u_i-\zeta,\quad x_\ell=u_\ell-\zeta,\quad
b_i=v_i-\omega,\quad b_\ell=v_\ell-\omega.
\]
Thus
\[
p+x_i+x_\ell+\zeta=1,\qquad
q+b_i+b_\ell+\omega=r.
\tag{2}
\]

Consider only the following scalar requirements:
\[
c_i,c_\ell\ge0,\qquad
c_i+c_\ell\ge s-c_j,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
c_i+x_i+\zeta+b_i+\omega<t,\qquad
c_\ell+x_\ell+\zeta+b_\ell+\omega<t,
\tag{4}
\]
and the necessary scalar consequence of high-high midpoint containment in the \(j\)-cap,
\[
\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\le c_j.
\tag{5}
\]
After \(c_i,c_\ell\) are projected out, (2)-(5), nonnegativity of all cells, and \(q>0\) are equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
p>0,\quad q>0,\quad \zeta,\omega\ge0,\quad
p+\zeta\le1,\quad q+\omega\le r,\\
p+q>1+r+\zeta+\omega-\delta,\qquad
\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\le c_j,\\
x_i+x_\ell=1-p-\zeta,\qquad
b_i+b_\ell=r-q-\omega,
\end{gathered}}
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\delta=2t-s+c_j\), and the last line allows every nonnegative split. This is an exact scalar projection only; it does not assert that every point of (6) is realized by convex bodies.

Every point of (6) also satisfies
\[
c_j>\frac t2+\frac{\zeta+\omega}{5},
\qquad
8r+\frac95(\zeta+\omega)<1+s.
\tag{7}
\]

Now fix precisely
\[
(r,s,t,c_i,c_\ell,c_j)
=
\left(
\frac1{16},\frac{47}{50},\frac{89}{200},
\frac{43}{100},\frac{43}{100},\frac{29}{80}
\right).
\tag{8}
\]
The rows
\[
\left(\frac{499}{500},\frac1{1000},\frac1{1000},0\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac1{25},\frac9{800},\frac9{800},0\right)
\tag{9}
\]
satisfy all scalar requirements (2)-(5), the Regime A window, and the root tail. In contrast, replacing only the two rows in (9) by the calibrated rows of accepted fact `bc026780453747b8`,
\[
\left(\frac{97}{100},\frac3{200},\frac3{200},0\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac9{200},\frac1{160},\frac1{160},\frac1{200}\right),
\tag{10}
\]
while holding all six quantities in (8) fixed violates both inequalities in (4). Therefore the universal fixed-cap scalar substitution
\[
\text{arbitrary scalar-feasible rows}\longmapsto\text{the rows in (10)}
\]
is invalid. This statement neither supplies an actual Alternative 1 counterexample nor excludes a geometric reduction based on additional constraints.

## Proof

Put
\[
m_i=x_i+\zeta+b_i+\omega,\qquad
m_\ell=x_\ell+\zeta+b_\ell+\omega.
\tag{11}
\]
If cap values satisfy (3)-(4), then
\[
s-c_j
\le c_i+c_\ell
<2t-m_i-m_\ell,
\]
and hence
\[
m_i+m_\ell<2t-s+c_j=\delta.
\tag{12}
\]
Conversely, suppose (12) holds. The Regime A window in `879a3c45b5f50670` gives
\[
\delta<\frac13<t<s.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, \(s>1-r\) gives \(t>4/9\), while \(r<1/3\) and \(s>1-r\) give
\[
7s>7(1-r)>2(1+r),
\]
which is equivalent to \(t<s\).
Thus \(t-m_i\) and \(t-m_\ell\) are positive, and
\[
(t-m_i)+(t-m_\ell)>s-c_j.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(s-c_j>0\), the open interval
\[
\left(
\max\{0,s-c_j-(t-m_\ell)\},
\min\{t-m_i,s-c_j\}
\right)
\]
is nonempty by (14). Choose \(c_i\) in it and put
\[
c_\ell=s-c_j-c_i.
\]
Then \(c_i,c_\ell\ge0\), (3) holds with equality, and both inequalities in (4) are strict. If \(s-c_j=0\), choose \(c_i=c_\ell=0\). The resulting cap values are also at most \(s\), because they are less than \(t<s\).

Using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_i+m_\ell
&=x_i+x_\ell+b_i+b_\ell+2\zeta+2\omega\\
&=1+r-p-q+\zeta+\omega.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore (12) is equivalent to the strict inequality for \(p+q\) in (6). All other cell conditions in (6) are exactly the nonnegative solutions of (2). Adjoining (5) proves the claimed equivalence.

Because \(p,q>0\), equation (6) gives
\[
4c_j
\ge(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2
>p+q
>1+r+\zeta+\omega-\delta.
\tag{15}
\]
The identity
\[
1+r-\delta=\frac52t-c_j
\]
turns (15) into the first inequality in (7). Combining that inequality with the root tail \(c_j+r<t\) gives
\[
r+\frac{\zeta+\omega}{5}<\frac t2
=\frac{1+r+s}{9},
\]
which is the second inequality in (7).

For (8), one has
\[
\delta=2t-s+c_j=\frac5{16}.
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover,
\[
0<\frac1{16}<\frac15,\qquad
\frac{15}{16}<\frac{47}{50}<\frac{17}{16},
\]
so these values lie in the first Regime A strip, and
\[
c_j+r=\frac{17}{40}<\frac{89}{200}=t,
\qquad
\delta=\frac5{16}\ge\frac1{16}=r.
\tag{17}
\]
Also
\[
0<\frac5{16}<\frac{2-s-r}{3}
=\frac{133}{400}<\frac13.
\]

The rows in (9) have totals \(1\) and \(1/16\), with \(q=1/25>0\). Their two low marginals are equal:
\[
m_i=m_\ell
=\frac1{1000}+\frac9{800}
=\frac{49}{4000}.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence, for both \(h=i,\ell\),
\[
c_h+m_h
=\frac{43}{100}+\frac{49}{4000}
=\frac{1769}{4000}
<\frac{1780}{4000}
=t.
\tag{19}
\]
The covering inequality in (3) holds because
\[
c_i+c_\ell+c_j
=\frac{489}{400}
>\frac{47}{50}=s.
\tag{20}
\]
Finally,
\[
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{499}{500}}+\sqrt{\frac1{25}}
\right)^2
<\frac{29}{80}.
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, after multiplication by \(4\), inequality (21) is
\[
\frac25\sqrt{\frac{499}{500}}<\frac{103}{250},
\]
and its left side is less than \(2/5=100/250\). This verifies every scalar condition claimed for (9).

For the calibrated rows (10), each low marginal is instead
\[
\frac3{200}+\frac1{160}+\frac1{200}
=\frac{21}{800}.
\tag{22}
\]
With the six quantities in (8) unchanged,
\[
c_h+\frac{21}{800}
=\frac{43}{100}+\frac{21}{800}
=\frac{365}{800}
>\frac{356}{800}
=t
\qquad(h=i,\ell).
\tag{23}
\]
Thus the row replacement fails (4), proving exactly the stated failure of fixed-cap scalar substitution. Since no convex bodies are constructed, the proof makes no geometric-counterexample or general geometric-reduction claim.
