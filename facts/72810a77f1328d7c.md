## Statement

For \(r_0\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
H_{r_0}=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=r_0\},
\qquad
\Phi_{r_0}(u,v)=\left(\frac{u+v}{2},\frac{v-u}{2},r_0-v\right),
\]
and use normalized planar area
\[
|Q|_*=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\mathcal H_2(Q).
\]
Let \(q>1\) and \(a,b,c,d,h,\varepsilon,\eta>0\), and define
\[
P_0=\Phi_{1+q}\!\left(
\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,h),(b,h),(b,h+\varepsilon)\}
\right),
\]
\[
P_2=\Phi_{1-q}\!\left(
\operatorname{conv}\{(-c,-d),(c,-d),(0,-d+\eta)\}
\right).
\]
Assume that \(P_0,P_2\) are nondegenerate, that the centroid incidence of \(P_0\) is \(I_0=\{1\}\), that the one-positive \(2\)-cell
\[
P_0\cap\{x_2>0,\ x_1<0,\ x_3<0\}
\]
has positive relative area, and that \(P_2\) is coordinatewise nonpositive. Put
\[
A=|P_0|_*,
\qquad
B=|P_2|_*,
\]
and assume
\[
0<A\le B,
\qquad
\frac AB<\frac38,
\qquad
A+B>1,
\qquad
B<1+A.
\]
Then, with
\[
r=\frac{a+b}{c},
\]
one has \(r>3\), and every nondegenerate triangle \(P_1\subset H_1\) satisfying
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1
\]
obeys the parameter-uniform lower bound
\[
|P_1|_*
\ge \frac{Br}{2}+\frac{A}{r}
>\frac{12}{11}>1.
\]
Consequently the family contains no rational triangle \(P_1\) of normalized area one, with centroid incidence \(I_1=\{2\}\) or otherwise.

## Proof

Write
\[
s=1+q,
\qquad
L=a+b.
\]
The centroid of the source triangle defining \(P_0\) has \((u,v)\)-coordinates
\[
\bar u=\frac{2b-a}{3},
\qquad
\bar v=h+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.
\]
Under \(\Phi_s\), its second and third coordinates are
\[
\frac{\bar v-\bar u}{2},
\qquad
s-\bar v.
\]
Since \(I_0=\{1\}\), both are strictly negative. Hence
\[
\bar u>\bar v>s,
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
2b-a>3s.
\tag{1}
\]

The positive one-positive \(2\)-cell supplies a point
\(\Phi_s(u,v)\in P_0\) for which
\[
\frac{u+v}{2}<0,
\qquad
s-v<0.
\]
Thus \(v>s\) and \(u<-v<-s\). Every point of the source triangle has
\(u\ge-a\), so
\[
a>s.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) gives \(b>2s\), and hence
\[
L=a+b>3s.
\tag{3}
\]

At the source vertex \((c,-d)\) of \(P_2\), coordinatewise nonpositivity gives
\[
\frac{c-d}{2}\le0,
\qquad
(1-q)+d\le0.
\]
Therefore
\[
c\le d\le q-1<s.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) imply
\[
r=\frac{L}{c}>3.
\tag{5}
\]

The map \(\Phi_{r_0}\) multiplies ordinary \((u,v)\)-area by
\(\sqrt3/2\), so normalized area is exactly ordinary source area. Thus
\[
A=\frac{L\varepsilon}{2},
\qquad
B=c\eta.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{\varepsilon/L}{\eta/c}
=\frac{2A}{Br^2}
<\frac{3/4}{9}
=\frac1{12}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(T_0,T_2\) denote the two source triangles in the definitions of
\(P_0,P_2\). Affinity of the maps and
\((s+(1-q))/2=1\) give
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}
=\Phi_1\!\left(\frac{T_0+T_2}{2}\right).
\tag{8}
\]
The counterclockwise edge vectors of \(T_0\) are
\[
(L,0),\quad(0,\varepsilon),\quad(-L,-\varepsilon),
\]
and those of \(T_2\) are
\[
(2c,0),\quad(-c,\eta),\quad(-c,-\eta).
\]
The horizontal edges combine in the Minkowski sum. Inequality (7), namely
\(\varepsilon/L<\eta/c\), fixes the cyclic order of the remaining two
down-left edges. It follows by adding the edge vectors that, after translating
\((T_0+T_2)/2\) by
\[
-\left(\frac{-a-c}{2},\frac{h-d}{2}\right),
\]
its vertices in counterclockwise order are
\[
\left(0,0\right),
\quad
\left(\frac{L+2c}{2},0\right),
\quad
\left(\frac{L+2c}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right),
\]
\[
\left(\frac{L+c}{2},\frac{\varepsilon+\eta}{2}\right),
\quad
\left(\frac c2,\frac{\eta}{2}\right).
\tag{9}
\]

Apply the determinant-one linear map
\[
\Xi(x,y)=\left(\frac{x}{c},cy\right).
\]
Translation and \(\Xi\) preserve ordinary area, containment, and the class of
triangles. By (6), the pentagon in (9) becomes
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4\},
\]
where
\[
p_0=(0,0),
\qquad
p_1=\left(\frac{r+2}{2},0\right),
\qquad
p_2=\left(\frac{r+2}{2},\frac Ar\right),
\]
\[
p_3=\left(\frac{r+1}{2},\frac Ar+\frac B2\right),
\qquad
p_4=\left(\frac12,\frac B2\right).
\tag{10}
\]

Put
\[
D=\frac r2+\frac{A}{rB}.
\tag{11}
\]
The four points
\[
z_0=(0,0),
\qquad
z_1=(D,0),
\qquad
z_2=\left(D+\frac12,\frac B2\right),
\qquad
z_3=\left(\frac12,\frac B2\right)
\tag{12}
\]
form a parallelogram contained in \(K\). Indeed,
\[
\frac{A}{rB}
<\frac{3/8}{3}
=\frac18,
\]
so \(z_1\) lies in the relative interior of the bottom edge \(p_0p_1\).
Also
\[
\lambda=1-\frac{2A}{rB}
\]
lies strictly between zero and one, and direct substitution gives
\[
p_2+\lambda(p_3-p_2)=z_2.
\]
Thus \(z_2\in p_2p_3\), while \(z_0=p_0\) and \(z_3=p_4\). Convexity of
\(K\) proves that the parallelogram
\[
\Pi=\operatorname{conv}\{z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3\}
\]
is contained in \(K\). Its area is
\[
|\Pi|
=\det\begin{pmatrix}D&1/2\\0&B/2\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{BD}{2}
=\frac{Br}{4}+\frac{A}{2r}.
\tag{13}
\]

We use the elementary affine-invariant fact that a parallelogram contained in
a triangle has at most half the area of that triangle. For completeness,
send the containing triangle affinely to
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad |\Delta|=\frac12.
\]
Write the parallelogram vertices as
\[
z\pm\frac u2\pm\frac v2
\]
and write \(z_i,u_i,v_i\) for their three barycentric coordinates and
increments. Nonnegativity of all four vertices gives
\[
z_i\ge\frac{|u_i|+|v_i|}{2}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Since \(\sum_i z_i=1\) and
\(\sum_i u_i=\sum_i v_i=0\), define
\[
\mu(u)=\max\{|u_1|,|u_2|,|u_1+u_2|\}
=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3|u_i|,
\]
and similarly for \(v\). Then
\[
\mu(u)+\mu(v)\le1.
\tag{14}
\]
The unit ball of \(\mu\) in the first two barycentric coordinates is the
hexagon with vertices
\[
(1,0),(1,-1),(0,-1),(-1,0),(-1,1),(0,1).
\]
For each pair of its vertices, the absolute determinant is at most one.
Convexity in each argument therefore gives
\[
|\det(u,v)|
\le\mu(u)\mu(v)
\le\frac{(\mu(u)+\mu(v))^2}{4}
\le\frac14.
\]
This determinant is the parallelogram area in \(\Delta\), whereas
\(|\Delta|=1/2\). Affine invariance proves the claimed factor-two bound.

Every triangle containing \(K\) therefore has ordinary area at least, by
(13),
\[
2|\Pi|=\frac{Br}{2}+\frac{A}{r}.
\tag{15}
\]
It remains to compare this exact lower bound with one. From
\(A/B<3/8\) and \(A+B>1\),
\[
1<A+B<\frac{11B}{8},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
B>\frac8{11}.
\tag{16}
\]
Using (5), (15), (16), and \(A>0\), every containing triangle has area
\[
\frac{Br}{2}+\frac{A}{r}
>\frac{3B}{2}
>\frac{12}{11}
>1.
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, (8), the definition of normalized area, and the determinant-one
normalization show that the same bound (17) is exactly the normalized-area
bound for every triangle \(P_1\subset H_1\) containing
\((P_0+P_2)/2\). Hence no normalized-area-one middle triangle exists.
The rationality and centroid-incidence requirements can only restrict the
class further, completing the parameter-uniform obstruction.
