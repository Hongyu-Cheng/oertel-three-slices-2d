## Statement

Assume the actual dominant all-double counterexample hypotheses and notation of accepted facts `174b54e9843ef8fc` and `2f637c4da2df28f1`.  Put
\[
\Xi=U+q+E_R,\qquad
B=\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{9}.
\]
The all-double crossing identity from accepted fact `0819231d0af83197` is
\[
2M=4m+k+3\Xi,                                            \tag{1}
\]
and hence
\[
k=2M-4m-3\Xi,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9=\frac{2(M-m-\Xi)}3.                  \tag{2}
\]
For every \(i\),
\[
c_i=h-u_i-m+x_i,\qquad
k-c_i=k-h+u_i+m-x_i.                                    \tag{3}
\]

Let \(g_0,g_2\) be the outer centroids and
\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}.
\]
If \(f_i(z)<0\), then
\[
c_i>B.                                                   \tag{4}
\]
If \(f_i(z)=0\), then
\[
c_i\geq B.                                               \tag{5}
\]
If \(f_i(z)>0\), then
\[
k-c_i>B.                                                 \tag{6}
\]

The strict bounds retain the following quantitative side-gap refinement.  Write
\[
w_{s,i}=\max_{A_s}f_i-\min_{A_s}f_i
\qquad(s=0,2),
\]
and, for \(\tau\geq0\), define
\[
d^-_{s,i}(\tau)
=\left|A_s\cap
\{f_i\leq f_i(g_s)+w_{s,i}\tau\}\right|,
\]
\[
d^+_{s,i}(\tau)
=\left|A_s\cap
\{f_i\geq f_i(g_s)-w_{s,i}\tau\}\right|.
\]
For every \(\tau_0,\tau_2>0\),
\[
w_{0,i}\tau_0+w_{2,i}\tau_2<-2f_i(z)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_i\geq
\frac{\left(\sqrt{d^-_{0,i}(\tau_0)}
+\sqrt{d^-_{2,i}(\tau_2)}\right)^2}{4}>B,               \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
w_{0,i}\tau_0+w_{2,i}\tau_2<2f_i(z)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k-c_i\geq
\frac{\left(\sqrt{d^+_{0,i}(\tau_0)}
+\sqrt{d^+_{2,i}(\tau_2)}\right)^2}{4}>B.               \tag{8}
\]
Accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives
\[
f_1(z)\geq\Gamma>0,                                      \tag{9}
\]
where
\[
\Gamma=
\frac{w_{0,1}(4M-9u_1)}{24M}
+
\frac{w_{2,1}(9x_1-5m)}{24m}.
\]
Thus (8) is valid for \(i=1\) whenever
\[
w_{0,1}\tau_0+w_{2,1}\tau_2<2\Gamma.                    \tag{10}
\]
Moreover, at least one \(j\in\{2,3\}\) satisfies
\[
-2f_j(z)\geq1+f_1(z)\geq1+\Gamma,                       \tag{11}
\]
so (7) is valid for that \(j\) whenever
\[
w_{0,j}\tau_0+w_{2,j}\tau_2<1+\Gamma.                   \tag{12}
\]

Define
\[
\mathcal N_i=
5M-16m-6\Xi-2\sqrt{Mm}+9(x_i-u_i),                      \tag{13}
\]
\[
\mathcal P_i=
11M-22m-21\Xi-2\sqrt{Mm}-9(x_i-u_i).                    \tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
c_i\gtreqless B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal N_i\gtreqless0,
\qquad
k-c_i\gtreqless B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_i\gtreqless0.                                \tag{15}
\]
Since accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives
\[
z\in R_2\cup R_3\cup R_{23},
\]
the complete finite sign audit is:
\[
z\in R_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_1>0,\quad\mathcal N_2>0,\quad\mathcal P_3>0,  \tag{16}
\]
\[
z\in R_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_1>0,\quad\mathcal P_2>0,\quad\mathcal N_3>0,  \tag{17}
\]
\[
z\in R_{23}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_1>0,\quad
\mathcal N_2,\mathcal N_3\geq0,\quad
\mathcal N_2+\mathcal N_3>0.                             \tag{18}
\]
In every case, some \(j\in\{2,3\}\) has \(\mathcal N_j>0\).  Since
\[
q+x_2+x_3=m-x_1<\frac{4m}{9},
\]
this implies the new necessary scalar inequality
\[
5M-12m-6\Xi-9q-2\sqrt{Mm}>0.                            \tag{19}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{M}{m}>
\left(
\frac{1+\sqrt{61+30\Xi/m+45q/m}}5
\right)^2.                                               \tag{20}
\]

## Proof

For a fixed \(i\), let
\[
C_{s,i}^-=
A_s\cap\{f_i\leq f_i(g_s)\},
\qquad s=0,2.
\]
Each is a closed halfplane cap containing the centroid of its two-dimensional convex body.  The planar centroid inequality used in accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` gives
\[
|C_{0,i}^-|\geq\frac{4M}{9},
\qquad
|C_{2,i}^-|\geq\frac{4m}{9}.                             \tag{21}
\]
If \(f_i(z)\leq0\), affinity and the midpoint inclusion give
\[
\frac{C_{0,i}^-+C_{2,i}^-}{2}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_i\leq f_i(z)\}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}.
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski, again in the form used in accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc`, yields
\[
\sqrt{c_i}
\geq
\frac{\sqrt{|C_{0,i}^-|}+\sqrt{|C_{2,i}^-|}}2
\geq
\frac{\sqrt M+\sqrt m}{3}.
\]
This proves (5) and the non-strict version of (4).

If \(f_i(z)<0\), choose positive \(a_0,a_2\) with
\[
a_0+a_2<-2f_i(z).
\]
The enlarged caps
\[
A_s\cap\{f_i\leq f_i(g_s)+a_s\}
\]
each have area strictly larger than the corresponding cap in (21): the centroid is an interior point, \(f_i\) is nonconstant, and moving its centroid cutting line by any positive amount adds a positive-area slab unless the cap has already become all of \(A_s\), in which case the strict inequality is immediate.  Their midpoint lies in \(K\cap\{f_i<0\}\).  Brunn-Minkowski proves (4).  Taking
\[
a_s=w_{s,i}\tau_s
\]
proves (7).

The proof of (6) and (8) is identical with the upper centroid caps
\[
C_{s,i}^+=A_s\cap\{f_i\geq f_i(g_s)\}.
\]
If \(f_i(z)>0\), small positive downward shifts whose sum is less than \(2f_i(z)\) keep their midpoint body in \(K\cap\{f_i>0\}\), whose area is \(k-c_i\).  This proves (6) and (8).  Equations (9)-(10) are exactly the quantitative \(f_1\)-margin from accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc`.  Since
\[
f_2(z)+f_3(z)=-1-f_1(z),
\]
one of the two summands is at most half of the right side.  This proves (11)-(12).

Substituting (2) into (3), and comparing with
\[
9B=M+m+2\sqrt{Mm},
\]
gives
\[
9(c_i-B)=\mathcal N_i,
\qquad
9(k-c_i-B)=\mathcal P_i.
\]
This proves (15).

In the exact cell \(R_2\), the signs are
\[
f_1(z)>0,\qquad f_2(z)<0,\qquad f_3(z)>0.
\]
The second sign is strict because
\[
f_2(z)=-1-f_1(z)-f_3(z)<-1.
\]
Applying (4) and (6) proves (16).  The same argument with \(2,3\) exchanged proves (17).  In \(R_{23}\), both \(f_2(z),f_3(z)\leq0\), while they cannot both vanish because \(f_1(z)>0\) and the three functions sum to \(-1\).  Thus (5) holds for both indices and (4) for at least one, proving (18).

Choose \(j\in\{2,3\}\) with \(\mathcal N_j>0\).  Accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` gives
\[
x_j<\frac{4m}{9}-q
\]
because \(q+x_2+x_3<4m/9\), while \(u_j\geq0\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
0<\mathcal N_j
&=5M-16m-6\Xi-2\sqrt{Mm}+9(x_j-u_j)\\
&<5M-12m-6\Xi-9q-2\sqrt{Mm}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (19).  Dividing by \(m\), putting \(y=\sqrt{M/m}>0\), and solving
\[
5y^2-2y-\left(12+\frac{6\Xi+9q}{m}\right)>0
\]
proves (20).

For completeness, the positive dominant-coordinate inequality does not close the branch.  From \(\mathcal P_1>0\) and
\[
u_1<x_1-\frac{5m}{9}
\]
one obtains
\[
11M-27m-21\Xi-2\sqrt{Mm}>0.
\]
This already follows from the accepted inequality
\[
M>3m+3\Xi
\]
in accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc`: after division by \(m\), its left side at \(M/m=3+3\Xi/m\) is
\[
6+12\Xi/m-2\sqrt{3+3\Xi/m}>0,
\]
and it increases with \(M/m\).

The new disjunctive system also remains exactly scalar-feasible.  For example, take
\[
m=1,\quad M=4,\quad
q=\frac1{100},\quad E_R=\frac1{100},\quad
(u_1,u_2,u_3)=
\left(\frac1{500},\frac1{250},\frac1{250}\right),
\]
\[
x_1=\frac45,\qquad
x_2=x_3=\frac{19}{200}.
\]
Then \(U=1/100\), \(\Xi=3/100\), and (1) fixes
\[
k=\frac{391}{100},\qquad
T=\frac{891}{100},\qquad
h=\frac{99}{50}.
\]
The crossings give
\[
c_1=\frac{889}{500},\qquad
c_2=c_3=\frac{1071}{1000},
\]
while \(B=1\).  Thus all scalar inequalities required by the \(R_{23}\) line (18) hold strictly, and
\[
k-c_1=\frac{533}{250}>1.
\]
All three no-half inequalities hold because
\[
M,m,k<\frac{T}{2}=\frac{891}{200}.
\]
The Scott quantities from accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` are
\[
M+m-k=\frac{109}{100},
\]
\[
u_1+m-x_1=\frac{101}{500},
\qquad
u_2+m-x_2=u_3+m-x_3=\frac{909}{1000},
\]
so the unique dominant index and Scott inequality are satisfied.  The complete cap data can be realized by nonnegative exact-cell masses, for instance
\[
p_0=\frac{399}{100},\quad
p_1=\frac1{500},\quad
p_2=p_3=\frac1{250},
\]
\[
q_{123}=\frac1{100},\quad
q_{23}=\frac45,\quad
q_{13}=q_{12}=\frac{19}{200},
\]
with all other \(P\)- and \(Q\)-cell masses zero, and
\[
r_{123}=\frac1{1000},\quad
r_{12}=\frac1{500},\quad
r_{13}=r_{23}=\frac3{1000},
\]
\[
r_1=\frac{443}{250},\quad
r_2=\frac{213}{200},\quad
r_3=\frac{133}{125}.
\]
They sum to \(k\), have the displayed \(c_i\), and give \(E_R=1/100\).  Thus (19)-(20) remove a genuine ratio subregion but do not by themselves contradict the accepted scalar, crossing, Scott, and no-half constraints.
