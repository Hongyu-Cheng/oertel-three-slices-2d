## Statement

Fix \(x,\ell,b>0\) with
\[
x+\ell+b=1,
\qquad
\lambda=x+\ell,
\]
and define
\[
D=\frac{16\ell-9(b+\ell)^2}{2},
\qquad
K_0=\frac{5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2}{2},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
a_*(t)=\frac{-t^2+2t+9x^2-2x}{2},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
k_*(t)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(t-x)(16\ell-5t+11x-6)}2,
&x\leq t\leq\lambda,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{8\ell^2+3t^2-6t-3x^2+6x}{2},
&\lambda\leq t\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(h\geq0\) and every finite nonnegative Borel measure
\(\mu\) on \([x,1]\),
\[
a_*(1)h+\int_x^1a_*(t)\,d\mu(t)<D
\tag{4}
\]
implies
\[
K_0+k_*(1)h+\int_x^1k_*(t)\,d\mu(t)>0.
\tag{5}
\]

## Proof

Put
\[
s=b+\ell=1-x,
\qquad
p=\frac{\ell}{s}.
\tag{6}
\]
The parameter assumptions give the complete normalized domain
\[
0<s<1,
\qquad
0<p<1.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(t=x+qs\), one has \(0\leq q\leq1\), with
\(t=\lambda\) exactly when \(q=p\).  Direct substitution into
(1)--(3) gives
\[
2D=s(16p-9s),
\qquad
2K_0=s^2(16p^2-16p+5),
\tag{8}
\]
\[
2a_*(x+qs)=G(q):=8x^2+s^2q(2-q),
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
2k_*(x+qs)=
\begin{cases}
s^2\kappa_L(q),&0\leq q\leq p,\\
s^2\kappa_R(q),&p\leq q\leq1,
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
where
\[
\kappa_L(q)=q(16p-6-5q),
\qquad
\kappa_R(q)=8p^2-3q(2-q).
\tag{11}
\]
At the shared boundary,
\[
\kappa_L(p)=\kappa_R(p)=p(11p-6),
\tag{12}
\]
so \(k_*\) is continuous at \(\lambda\).  The affine \(h\)-ray is the
right ray at \(q=1\):
\[
2a_*(1)=G(1)=8x^2+s^2,
\qquad
2k_*(1)=s^2\kappa_h,
\qquad
\kappa_h:=8p^2-3.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(x>0\), equation (9) gives \(G(q)>0\) on the entire closed
interval.  Thus \(a_*\) is positive, while \(a_*\) and \(k_*\) are
bounded Borel functions on \([x,1]\).  All integrals below are
therefore finite.  The left side of (4) is nonnegative, so its being
strictly smaller than \(D\) forces
\[
D>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Define the following explicit nonnegative \(A\)-budget multiplier:
\[
\eta=
\begin{cases}
0,&\kappa_h\geq0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{s^2(3-8p^2)}{G(1)},&\kappa_h<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]
We prove, throughout the full parameter domain (7), the ray and
constant slacks
\[
k_*(t)+\eta a_*(t)\geq0
\qquad(x\leq t\leq1),
\tag{16}
\]
\[
K_0-\eta D\geq0.
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose first that \(\kappa_h\geq0\), so \(\eta=0\).  For
\(p\leq q\leq1\),
\[
\kappa_R(q)=\kappa_h+3(1-q)^2\geq0.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\kappa_h\geq0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p\geq\sqrt{\frac38}>\frac6{11}.
\]
Hence, for \(0\leq q\leq p\),
\[
16p-6-5q\geq11p-6>0,
\]
and \(\kappa_L(q)\geq0\), including its value \(0\) at \(q=0\).
This proves (16).  The shared value at \(t=\lambda\) is (12), while
at \(t=1\) the ray slack is \(s^2\kappa_h/2\geq0\), with equality
exactly when \(\kappa_h=0\).  Also
\[
K_0
=\frac{s^2}{2}
\left(16\left(p-\frac12\right)^2+1\right)>0,
\tag{19}
\]
which proves (17) in this case.

Now suppose that \(\kappa_h<0\).  Then (15) is equivalently
\[
\eta=-\frac{k_*(1)}{a_*(1)}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
For the right piece, direct expansion gives the exact identity
\[
\kappa_R(q)G(1)-\kappa_hG(q)
=8(1-q)^2(s^2p^2+3x^2)\geq0
\qquad(p\leq q\leq1).
\tag{21}
\]
For the left piece, define
\[
F(q)=\kappa_L(q)G(1)-\kappa_hG(q).
\]
This is a quadratic in \(q\) whose leading coefficient is
\[
8\bigl(s^2(p^2-1)-5x^2\bigr)<0.
\tag{22}
\]
It is therefore strictly concave.  At the endpoints of the full
left interval,
\[
F(0)=8x^2(3-8p^2)>0,
\tag{23}
\]
while (12) and (21) give
\[
F(p)=8(1-p)^2(s^2p^2+3x^2)>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Concavity implies \(F(q)>0\) for every \(0\leq q\leq p\).
Multiplication of (16) by the positive factor
\[
\frac{2G(1)}{s^2}
\]
turns it into \(F(q)\geq0\) on the left and into (21) on the right.
This proves the ray slack (16) everywhere.

The boundary audit is exact.  At \(t=\lambda\), equation (12) makes
the two formulas for \(k_*\) identical, and their common slack is the
strictly positive quantity in (24), multiplied by
\(s^2/(2G(1))\).  At \(t=1\), equation (21) has the factor
\((1-q)^2=0\), and (20) gives the exact equality
\[
k_*(1)+\eta a_*(1)=0.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus atoms of \(\mu\) at either boundary are included.

It remains to prove (17) when \(\kappa_h<0\).  Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(s,p)={}&9(1-p)^2s^2\\
&+(16p^3-32p^2+26p-10)s\\
&+16p^2-16p+5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Exact expansion of (1), (8), (13), and (15) gives
\[
K_0-\eta D=\frac{4s^2}{G(1)}P(s,p).
\tag{27}
\]
We check its sign on the whole rectangle (7), not merely on the
subregion \(\kappa_h<0\).  As a quadratic in \(s\), \(P\) has
positive leading coefficient \(9(1-p)^2\) and discriminant
\[
\operatorname{disc}_sP
=16(p-1)^2(2p-1)^2(4p^2-4p-5).
\tag{28}
\]
For every \(0<p<1\),
\[
4p^2-4p-5=-5-4p(1-p)<0.
\tag{29}
\]
Therefore (28) is strictly negative unless \(p=1/2\), because
\(p=1\) is outside (7).  Hence \(P(s,p)>0\) for \(p\ne1/2\).
At the remaining value,
\[
P\left(s,\frac12\right)=\frac{(3s-2)^2}{4}\geq0.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(P(s,p)\geq0\) throughout (7).  Since \(s>0\) and
\(G(1)>0\), equation (27) proves (17), including the possible zero
constant slack at \(p=1/2,\ s=2/3\).

We now integrate (16).  Nonnegativity and finiteness of \(\mu\) give
\[
\int_x^1k_*(t)\,d\mu(t)
\geq
-\eta\int_x^1a_*(t)\,d\mu(t).
\tag{31}
\]
The same pointwise inequality at \(t=1\), multiplied by \(h\geq0\),
gives
\[
k_*(1)h\geq-\eta a_*(1)h.
\tag{32}
\]
If \(\eta>0\), combining (31)--(32) and using the strict premise (4)
and (17) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_0+k_*(1)h+\int_x^1k_*(t)\,d\mu(t)
&\geq
K_0-\eta
\left(a_*(1)h+\int_x^1a_*(t)\,d\mu(t)\right)\\
&>
K_0-\eta D\\
&\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
If \(\eta=0\), equations (16), (19), and (31)--(32) instead give the
direct strict bound
\[
K_0+k_*(1)h+\int_x^1k_*(t)\,d\mu(t)\geq K_0>0.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus (5) holds in every parameter case and for every finite
nonnegative Borel measure on \([x,1]\).
