## Statement

Assume the pinch normalization and all moving-frame conclusions of accepted
fact `f882a944b9fbef55`:
\[
|A_{0,n}|=1,\qquad |A_{2,n}|=r_n\to0,\qquad
|A_{1,n}|=s_n,\qquad 1-r_n<s_n<1+r_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Let an arbitrary normalized minimal certificate from accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` have middle-cap areas \(p_{i,n}\), actual paired endpoint
contributions \(e_{i,n}\), and
\[
p_{i,n}+e_{i,n}<t_n,\qquad
t_n=\frac{2(1+r_n+s_n)}9.
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{4s_n/3-\sum_i p_{i,n}}{r_n}\le0.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_i p_{i,n}\le\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof

Apply the common affine normalization and height shear in
`f882a944b9fbef55`.  On an arbitrary compactified subsequence, write the
transformed middle bodies as
\[
K_n:=K_{1,n}\longrightarrow T
\]
in Hausdorff distance, where \(T\) is a nondegenerate triangle of area
\(\kappa>0\).  There are determinants \(\kappa_n\to\kappa\) such that
\[
|K_n|=\kappa_ns_n.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(g_n\) be the centroid of \(K_n\), and let \(g\) be the centroid of
\(T\).  Bounded Hausdorff convergence and dominated convergence give
\[
g_n\longrightarrow g.
\tag{6}
\]

After the row permutation supplied by `f882a944b9fbef55`, choose the moving
oriented affine line coordinates exactly as in accepted fact
`33f64ca8c4f58f43`:
\[
\widetilde C_{i,n}
=K_n\cap\{\ell_{i,n}\ge c_{i,n}\},
\qquad
|\widetilde C_{i,n}|=\kappa_np_{i,n},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\ell_{i,n}\longrightarrow\beta_i,\qquad
c_{i,n}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) are the affine barycentric coordinates of
\(T\), and convergence of the affine functions is uniform on the common
bounding ball.  In particular, their linear parts converge.  The interiors
\(\{\ell_{i,n}>c_{i,n}\}\) cover the whole plane.

Define the signed displacement of the \(i\)-th moving line from the parallel
line through the actual moving centroid by
\[
d_{i,n}:=c_{i,n}-\ell_{i,n}(g_n).
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (6)-(8) and \(\beta_i(g)=1/3\) give
\[
d_{i,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]

We first record the exact moving-body layer formula.  For \(u\) in a fixed
small neighborhood of \(0\), put
\[
B_{i,n}(u)
=\left|K_n\cap
\{\ell_{i,n}\ge\ell_{i,n}(g_n)+u\}\right|
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
L_{i,n}(u)
=\frac{\mathcal H^1\!\left(
K_n\cap\{\ell_{i,n}=\ell_{i,n}(g_n)+u\}
\right)}
{\|\nabla\ell_{i,n}\|}.
\tag{12}
\]
The planar layer formula gives, with the integral interpreted in its signed
sense,
\[
B_{i,n}(v)-B_{i,n}(u)
=-\int_u^vL_{i,n}(z)\,dz.
\tag{13}
\]
This includes levels passing through a polygon vertex: such levels form a
finite null set in the integral, and no strict-to-closed correction occurs.

The chord density in (12) has the following sequential limit:
\[
u_n\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_{i,n}(u_n)\longrightarrow\frac{4\kappa}{3}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, after affine coordinate changes converging to the identity, the
line in (12) becomes a fixed horizontal line converging to
\(\{\beta_i=1/3\}\).  Hausdorff convergence of full-dimensional convex
bodies implies convergence of the two endpoints of every such interior
section: outer convergence follows directly from Hausdorff convergence,
while every compact subsegment of the relative interior of the limiting
section lies in \(K_n\) for all large \(n\).  Hence the section lengths
converge.  For the limiting triangle,
\[
\left|T\cap\{\beta_i\ge1/3+u\}\right|
=\kappa(2/3-u)^2
\tag{15}
\]
near \(u=0\), so its layer density at \(0\) is \(4\kappa/3\), proving
(14).

Because there are only three rows, (10) and the sequential formulation
(14) give a number \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) such that
\[
\left|L_{i,n}(u)-\frac{4\kappa}{3}\right|
\le\varepsilon_n
\quad
\left(
1\le i\le3,\ |u|\le\max_j|d_{j,n}|
\right).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (7), (9), (11), and (13) give the exact signed first-order expansion
\[
\kappa_np_{i,n}
=B_{i,n}(0)-\frac{4\kappa}{3}d_{i,n}+R_{i,n},
\qquad
|R_{i,n}|\le\varepsilon_n|d_{i,n}|.
\tag{17}
\]
This is an expansion of the actual cap in the actual body \(K_n\), not an
area comparison with \(T\).

Every halfplane in (11) at \(u=0\) has the centroid \(g_n\) on its
boundary.  The planar Grünbaum inequality therefore gives
\[
B_{i,n}(0)\ge\frac49|K_n|
=\frac{4\kappa_ns_n}{9}.
\tag{18}
\]
On the other hand, (2) and \(e_{i,n}\ge0\) give the individual transformed
cap upper bounds
\[
\kappa_np_{i,n}<\kappa_nt_n.
\tag{19}
\]
The available excess above the centroidal lower bound is exactly
\[
\kappa_n\left(t_n-\frac{4s_n}{9}\right)
=\frac{2\kappa_n(1+r_n-s_n)}9,
\tag{20}
\]
and (1) gives
\[
0<1+r_n-s_n<2r_n.
\tag{21}
\]

For all large \(n\), (16) also gives
\[
L_{i,n}(u)\ge\frac{2\kappa}{3}
\]
throughout the displayed interval.  If \(d_{i,n}<0\), equations
(13), (18), and (19) yield
\[
\frac{2\kappa}{3}(-d_{i,n})
\le B_{i,n}(d_{i,n})-B_{i,n}(0)
<\frac{2\kappa_n(1+r_n-s_n)}9.
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently, with \(d^-=\max\{-d,0\}\),
\[
\sum_i d_{i,n}^-=O(r_n).
\tag{23}
\]
This step covers all three possible activations: the centroid may be
strictly inside a cap, strictly outside it, or on its boundary.  At
\(d_{i,n}=0\), there is no layer and no error.

It remains to control the positive offsets.  Since the three limiting
gradients \(\nabla\beta_i\) are pairwise nonparallel and satisfy
\(\sum_i\nabla\beta_i=0\), for all large \(n\) there are unique positive
numbers \(a_{i,n}\), normalized by
\[
\sum_i a_{i,n}=3,
\qquad
\sum_i a_{i,n}\nabla\ell_{i,n}=0,
\tag{24}
\]
and
\[
a_{i,n}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{25}
\]
The affine function
\[
\sum_i a_{i,n}(\ell_{i,n}-c_{i,n})
\]
is constant.  Its constant value is strictly positive.  To see this
without a closure gap, if it were nonpositive, solve
\(\ell_{1,n}=c_{1,n}\) and \(\ell_{2,n}=c_{2,n}\).  Equation (24) would
then make \(\ell_{3,n}-c_{3,n}\le0\) at that point, which would belong to
none of the three open caps.  This contradicts the strict middle-plane
cover.  Evaluating the positive constant at \(g_n\) and using (9) gives
\[
\sum_i a_{i,n}d_{i,n}<0.
\tag{26}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_i a_{i,n}d_{i,n}^+
<
\sum_i a_{i,n}d_{i,n}^-.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (23), (25), and (27) prove
\[
\sum_i|d_{i,n}|=O(r_n).
\tag{28}
\]

Sum (17) and use (18):
\[
\sum_i\kappa_np_{i,n}
\ge
\frac{4\kappa_ns_n}{3}
-\frac{4\kappa}{3}\sum_i d_{i,n}
-\varepsilon_n\sum_i|d_{i,n}|.
\tag{29}
\]
The strict Farkas relation also gives the required sign for the first-order
sum.  Indeed, by (25)-(26),
\[
\sum_i d_{i,n}
=\sum_i a_{i,n}d_{i,n}
+\sum_i(1-a_{i,n})d_{i,n}
\le
\max_i|1-a_{i,n}|\sum_i|d_{i,n}|
=o(r_n),
\tag{30}
\]
where (28) is used in the last equality.  Rearranging (29), then applying
(28)-(30), gives
\[
\frac{4\kappa_ns_n}{3}
-\sum_i\kappa_np_{i,n}
\le o(r_n).
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(\kappa_n\to\kappa>0\), division by \(\kappa_n r_n\)
proves on the present compactified subsequence that
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{4s_n/3-\sum_i p_{i,n}}{r_n}\le0.
\tag{31a}
\]

We now remove the subsequence restriction.  Put
\[
\Delta_n=
\frac{4s_n/3-\sum_i p_{i,n}}{r_n}.
\]
If the original sequence had
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Delta_n>0,
\]
there would be a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and a subsequence
\((n_k)\) such that
\[
\Delta_{n_k}\ge\epsilon
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{31b}
\]
Apply the compactification of accepted fact `f882a944b9fbef55` to this
subsequence and pass to a further compactified subsequence.  The complete
calculation (5)-(31) applies to that further subsequence, so (31a) forces
its normalized deficit to have limsup at most \(0\).  This contradicts the
fixed lower bound (31b), which survives passage to every further
subsequence.  Therefore the original sequence satisfies
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Delta_n\le0,
\]
which proves (3).  In particular, \(\Delta_n\le2/3\) for all sufficiently
large \(n\), proving (4).

Finally, accepted fact `33f64ca8c4f58f43` uses the exact paired endpoint
threshold identity
\[
x_{i,n}+y_{i,n}=2c_{i,n}
\]
and proves, uniformly both when the small endpoint tends to a point and
when it tends to a nondegenerate segment, that strict lowness forces
\[
\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_i p_{i,n}>\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (4) and (32) are incompatible.  Hence neither limiting type can
occur for a genuine strict-low pinch sequence.
