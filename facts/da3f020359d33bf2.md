## Statement

Let
\[
\mathcal A=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^3:
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1\right\},
\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\subset\mathcal A,
\]
and normalize planar area so that \(|\Delta|=1\).  For a compact
two-dimensional convex body \(B\subset\mathcal A\), define
\[
w_j(B)=\max_B\lambda_j-\min_B\lambda_j
\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathcal A\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq\Delta.
\tag{1}
\]
Suppose that, for some \(u\ge v>0\),
\[
|A_0|=u^2,\qquad |A_2|=v^2,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
w_j(A_0)=u,\qquad w_j(A_2)=v
\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
M=u^2,\qquad m=v^2,\qquad k=1,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9=\frac{2(1+u^2+v^2)}9,
\tag{4}
\]
and assume that this area triple lies in the exact strict residual
domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.

Choose
\[
0\le s_j\le1\quad(j=1,2,3),
\qquad
s_1+s_2+s_3<1,
\tag{5}
\]
put
\[
d=1-s_1-s_2-s_3>0,
\qquad
f_j(\lambda)=\frac{s_j-\lambda_j}{d},
\tag{6}
\]
and define
\[
c_j=|\Delta\cap\{f_j\le0\}|,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j+|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
       +|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\qquad
I_j=\{a\in\mathbb R:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then the \(f_j\) are nonconstant, have pairwise nonparallel linear
parts, and satisfy \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\).  For every
\(q\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
c_q+m<t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I_i=\varnothing
\quad\text{for some }i\ne q.
\tag{9}
\]
No condition \(D_0\le m\) is needed.  In particular, (9) holds on the
entire part of this family where
\[
D_0=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}
=\frac{2u^2-v^2-1}{3}>v^2=m.
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

In the coordinates \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) on \(\mathcal A\), the
linear parts of \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) are
\((1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\).  Multiplication by the common nonzero scalar
\(-1/d\) proves that the \(f_j\) are nonconstant with pairwise
nonparallel linear parts.  Also,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3f_j
=\frac{s_1+s_2+s_3-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3)}d
=-1.
\]

We first establish the cap bound that replaces the exact homothetic
triangle profile.  Let \(B\subset\mathcal A\) be a compact
two-dimensional convex body, fix \(j\), and write
\[
\alpha=\min_B\lambda_j,\qquad
\beta=\max_B\lambda_j,\qquad
w=\beta-\alpha>0.
\]
For \(0\le x\le w\), set
\[
C(x)=B\cap\{\lambda_j\ge\beta-x\}.
\]
Convexity gives
\[
(1-\theta)C(x_1)+\theta C(x_2)
\subseteq
C((1-\theta)x_1+\theta x_2)
\qquad(0\le\theta\le1).
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski therefore shows that
\(x\mapsto |C(x)|^{1/2}\) is concave.  The support face \(C(0)\) has
planar area zero, including when it is a segment, while \(C(w)=B\).
Concavity above the endpoint chord gives
\[
|C(x)|^{1/2}\ge\frac{x}{w}|B|^{1/2}
\qquad(0\le x\le w).
\tag{11}
\]
For \(c>0\), write
\[
[x]_c=\min\{c,\max\{0,x\}\}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(|B|=c^2\) and \(w_j(B)=c\), then (11), together with the empty
and full cap states, gives the global bound
\[
\left|B\cap\{\lambda_j\ge\max_B\lambda_j-x\}\right|
\ge [x]_c^2
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\tag{13}
\]
At \(x<0\) the cap is empty, at \(x=0\) it is a null support face, and
at \(x\ge c\) it is all of \(B\), so (13) includes every improper and
support-contact state.

For \(r\in\{0,2\}\), define
\[
\alpha_{rj}=\min_{A_r}\lambda_j,\qquad
\beta_{rj}=\max_{A_r}\lambda_j.
\]
By (3),
\[
\beta_{0j}-\alpha_{0j}=u,\qquad
\beta_{2j}-\alpha_{2j}=v.
\tag{14}
\]
Taking the minimum of \(\lambda_j\) in (1) gives
\[
\frac{\alpha_{0j}+\alpha_{2j}}2\ge0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\beta_{0j}+\beta_{2j}
=\alpha_{0j}+\alpha_{2j}+u+v
\ge u+v.
\tag{15}
\]

Fix \(j\) and \(a\in\mathbb R\), and put
\[
x=\beta_{0j}-s_j+da,
\qquad
R_j=\beta_{0j}+\beta_{2j}-2s_j.
\tag{16}
\]
The raw depths, measured from the upper supports, of the two endpoint
caps in (8) are \(x\) and \(R_j-x\), respectively.  Hence (13) gives
\[
|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|
\ge [x]_u^2+[R_j-x]_v^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(d>0\), \(x\) ranges over all of \(\mathbb R\) as \(a\) does.
Define
\[
\phi(D)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb R}
\left([x]_u^2+[D-x]_v^2\right).
\tag{18}
\]
For \(u\ge v>0\), its exact value is
\[
\phi(D)=
\begin{cases}
0,&D\le0,\\[1mm]
\min\{D^2/2,v^2\},&D>0.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, if \(D\le0\), every \(x\in[D,0]\) makes both terms zero.
Suppose \(D>0\).  If \(x\le0\), the second term is at least
\(\min\{D^2,v^2\}\).  If \(x\ge D\), the first term is at least
\(\min\{D^2,u^2\}\ge\min\{D^2,v^2\}\).  If \(0<x<D\) and either cap
is full, the sum is at least \(v^2\).  If neither is full, then
\[
x^2+(D-x)^2\ge\frac{D^2}{2}.
\]
This proves the lower bound in (19).  For
\(0<D\le\sqrt2\,v\), \(x=D/2\) attains \(D^2/2\).  For
\(D\ge\sqrt2\,v\), \(x=0\) makes the first cap a null support contact
and the second cap full, attaining \(v^2\).  Thus (19) includes
\(D=0\), \(D=\sqrt2\,v\), and all clamping boundaries.  It also shows
that \(\phi\) is nondecreasing.

Taking the infimum in (17), then using (15), (16), and monotonicity,
gives
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|
\right)
\ge \phi(R_j)
\ge \phi(u+v-2s_j).
\tag{20}
\]

The middle cap in direction \(j\) is the corner triangle
\(\Delta\cap\{\lambda_j\ge s_j\}\).  Therefore
\[
c_j=(1-s_j)^2,
\tag{21}
\]
including \(s_j=0\) and \(s_j=1\).

It remains to prove the scalar payment.  Put
\[
w=u+v,\qquad E=u^2+v^2,\qquad R_*=w-\frac23.
\tag{22}
\]
We claim
\[
9\phi(R_*)>2(E-1).
\tag{23}
\]
If \(R_*\le0\), then \(w\le2/3\), and \(u,v>0\) gives
\[
E=u^2+v^2<w^2\le\frac49<1.
\]
Thus (23) follows from \(\phi(R_*)=0\).

Suppose \(R_*>0\).  Formula (19) gives
\[
9\phi(R_*)=
\min\left\{\frac92R_*^2,9v^2\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
The exact residual domain in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`
contains \(M-m<k\), which here is
\[
u^2-v^2<1.
\tag{25}
\]
It follows that
\[
2(E-1)<4v^2<9v^2.
\tag{26}
\]
The other branch of (24) is also strictly larger than \(2(E-1)\),
because
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac92\left(w-\frac23\right)^2-2(E-1)
&=\frac52w^2+4uv-6w+4\\
&=\frac52\left(w-\frac65\right)^2
  +4uv+\frac25
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (24)-(27) prove (23).

Condition (5) implies that some index \(j\) satisfies \(s_j<1/3\).
For every such index, (20)-(23) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}\mu_j(a)
&\ge (1-s_j)^2+\phi(u+v-2s_j)\\
&>\frac49+\phi(R_*)\\
&>\frac49+\frac{2(E-1)}9
=\frac{2(1+E)}9=t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The first strict inequality uses \(s_j<1/3\), while monotonicity of
\(\phi\) is needed only non-strictly.  Thus
\[
I_j=\varnothing
\qquad\text{whenever }s_j<\frac13.
\tag{29}
\]

Now assume \(c_q+m<t\).  Compactness makes all endpoint supports of
\(f_q\) finite, so one can choose a finite \(a\) satisfying
\[
a<\min_{A_0}f_q,
\qquad
-a>\max_{A_2}f_q.
\]
At this shift the \(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap is full.
Consequently,
\[
\mu_q(a)=c_q+m<t,
\]
so \(I_q\ne\varnothing\).  By (29), \(q\) cannot satisfy
\(s_q<1/3\).  At least one index does satisfy that strict inequality,
and it must therefore be an \(i\ne q\).  Equation (29) proves (9).

All caps above are closed.  Each cutting line has planar area zero
because the corresponding affine function is nonconstant.  Thus
replacing an open complement by a closed cap changes no area, and the
empty, full, support-face, limiting, and equality states used in
(11)-(29) require no additional regularity or approximation.

We finish with an exact rational example showing that the hypotheses
are genuinely nonhomothetic.  Let
\[
g=\frac13(1,1,1),
\]
and, for \(0\le a\le1/2\), define zero-sum vectors
\[
p(a)=\left(a,\frac12,-a-\frac12\right),
\qquad
q(a)=\left(1-a,-\frac12,a-\frac12\right),
\]
and the centrally symmetric parallelogram
\[
Q(a)=\left\{sp(a)+rq(a):
-\frac12\le s,r\le\frac12\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
In \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\)-coordinates,
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
a&1-a\\[1mm]
1/2&-1/2
\end{pmatrix}
=-\frac12.
\]
Since \(\Delta\) has coordinate area \(1/2\), the normalized area of
\(Q(a)\) is \(1\).  Moreover,
\[
w_j(Q(a))=|p_j(a)|+|q_j(a)|=1
\qquad(j=1,2,3),
\tag{31}
\]
where the restriction \(0\le a\le1/2\) is used for \(j=3\).

Take
\[
A_0=g+\frac{103}{100}Q(0),
\qquad
A_2=g+\frac3{10}Q\left(\frac14\right),
\qquad
K=\Delta.
\tag{32}
\]
Every coordinate of each \(Q(a)\) lies in \([-1/2,1/2]\).  Hence
every coordinate of the midpoint body in (1) is at least
\[
\frac13-\frac14\left(\frac{103}{100}+\frac3{10}\right)
=\frac1{1200}>0.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus compatibility is strict.

The two endpoint parallelograms are not homothetic.  In the first two
coordinates, the four determinants comparing an edge direction of
\(Q(0)\) with one of \(Q(1/4)\) are
\[
-\frac18,\qquad -\frac38,\qquad \frac58,\qquad -\frac18,
\]
so no edge direction of one is parallel to an edge direction of the
other.  They are also not homothetic to the triangular body \(K\).

The areas are
\[
M=\frac{10609}{10000},\qquad
m=\frac9{100},\qquad
k=1.
\tag{34}
\]
Writing \(r=m/M\) and \(s=k/M\), one has
\[
r=\frac{900}{10609}<\frac9{25},
\]
and
\[
1-r=\frac{9709}{10609}
<\frac{10000}{10609}=s
<\frac{11509}{10609}=1+r.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus (34) lies strictly in the first branch of the exact residual
domain in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Also,
\[
D_0=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}
=\frac{5159}{15000},
\qquad
D_0-m=\frac{3809}{15000}>0.
\tag{36}
\]

Choose
\[
s_1=\frac{379}{1000},
\qquad
s_2=s_3=\frac{31}{100},
\qquad
d=\frac1{1000}.
\tag{37}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
f_1=379-1000\lambda_1,\qquad
f_2=310-1000\lambda_2,\qquad
f_3=310-1000\lambda_3.
\]
Here
\[
t=\frac{21509}{45000},
\qquad
c_1=\frac{385641}{1000000},
\qquad
c_2=c_3=\frac{4761}{10000}.
\tag{38}
\]
The tail inequality and all three strict middle-cap inequalities hold:
\[
t-(c_1+m)=\frac{21031}{9000000}>0,
\qquad
t-c_2=t-c_3=\frac{169}{90000}>0.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(j=2,3\), (15) gives
\[
R_j\ge
\frac{103}{100}+\frac3{10}-2\frac{31}{100}
=\frac{71}{100}
>\sqrt2\,\frac3{10}.
\tag{40}
\]
Equations (19)-(21) therefore yield
\[
\inf_a\mu_j(a)\ge c_j+m
=\frac{5661}{10000}
=t+\frac{7931}{90000}>t
\qquad(j=2,3).
\tag{41}
\]
Thus \(I_1\ne\varnothing\) and \(I_2=I_3=\varnothing\).  This is a
strictly compatible rational polygonal instance with \(D_0>m\), all
three middle caps below \(t\), and nonhomothetic endpoint bodies.
