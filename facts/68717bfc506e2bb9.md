## Statement

Assume the common affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact 0e51a47a9add571f, and suppose the strict rational table in the registered subgoal is realized.  There is no such realization for which \(A_2\) is a triangle.

More precisely, every such full realization forces the global \(Q\)-central triangle
\[
\mathcal T_Q
=
\{x:f_i(x)+L_i\le0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}
\]
to satisfy
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}.
\]
If \(A_2\) is a triangle with
\[
q_N=\frac25,
\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15,
\]
then instead
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\le16|A_2|=16.
\]
These two bounds are incompatible.

## Proof

Accepted fact b1eaab0ae37367d1 applies to every full realization of the registered table and gives directly
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}.
\tag{1}
\]
It remains to prove the triangular upper bound.  Suppose for contradiction that
\[
Q:=A_2
\]
is a triangle of area one, and let
\[
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3
\]
be its barycentric coordinate functions, labeled at its vertices \(v_1,v_2,v_3\).  Put
\[
\ell_i=f_i+L_i.
\]
Up to their zero-area cutting lines, the three positive outer caps are
\[
C_i=Q\cap\{\ell_i>0\},
\qquad
|C_i|=\frac15.
\tag{2}
\]
Their interiors are pairwise disjoint.  Indeed, an open overlap of \(C_i\) and \(C_j\) would have positive area in a \(Q\)-cell with at least two positive \(\ell\)-coordinates, whereas every such cell is zero in the all-double support.

Every positive proper halfplane cap of a triangle strictly contains at least one triangle vertex.  Pairwise interior-disjoint caps cannot strictly contain the same vertex, because relative neighborhoods of that vertex inside the triangle would overlap.  If one cap strictly contained two vertices, only the third vertex would remain available to both other positive caps, which is impossible for the same reason.  Hence the three caps strictly contain three distinct vertices, one each.  After relabeling, \(C_i\) is the corner triangle at \(v_i\).  This also covers a cutting line through another vertex: the corresponding edge fraction below is then one.

Let the line bounding \(C_1\) meet the edges \(v_1v_2\) and \(v_1v_3\) at fractions
\[
a,\qquad \frac1{5a}
\]
from \(v_1\).  Let the analogous fractions for \(C_2\), measured from \(v_2\) along \(v_2v_1\) and \(v_2v_3\), be
\[
b,\qquad \frac1{5b},
\]
and those for \(C_3\), measured from \(v_3\) along \(v_3v_1\) and \(v_3v_2\), be
\[
c,\qquad \frac1{5c}.
\]
The products equal \(1/5\) because every corner cap has one fifth of the area of \(Q\).  All six fractions lie in \((0,1]\).  Disjointness along the edges \(v_1v_2\) and \(v_1v_3\) gives
\[
a+b\le1,
\qquad
\frac1{5a}+c\le1.
\]
Eliminating \(a\) yields
\[
(1-b)(1-c)\ge\frac15.
\tag{3}
\]

The cap line at \(v_2\) has barycentric equation
\[
\frac{\alpha_1}{b}
+\frac{\alpha_3}{1/(5b)}
=1,
\]
and \(v_2\) makes its left-hand side zero.  Thus the cap side is the side where the left-hand side is at most one, while the central side \(\ell_2\le0\), which contains \(\mathcal T_Q\), is the side where it is at least one.  The analogous orientation at \(v_3\) gives, throughout \(\mathcal T_Q\),
\[
\frac{\alpha_1}{b}
+\frac{\alpha_3}{1/(5b)}
\ge1,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha_1}{c}
+\frac{\alpha_2}{1/(5c)}
\ge1.
\tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
d=\frac1{5b},
\qquad
e=\frac1{5c},
\qquad
R=\frac1{5b^2}+\frac1{5c^2}.
\]
If some \(x\in\mathcal T_Q\) satisfied \(\alpha_1(x)=-t\) with \(t\ge1\), inequalities (4) would give
\[
\alpha_3(x)\ge d+t\frac{d}{b},
\qquad
\alpha_2(x)\ge e+t\frac{e}{c}.
\]
Since the barycentric coordinates sum to one,
\[
1+t
=\alpha_2(x)+\alpha_3(x)
\ge d+e+tR.
\tag{5}
\]

We now show that (5) is impossible.  By Cauchy--Schwarz and (3),
\[
\sqrt{bc}
+\sqrt{(1-b)(1-c)}
\le1,
\]
and hence, with
\[
z=1-\frac1{\sqrt5},
\]
one has
\[
\sqrt{bc}\le z.
\tag{6}
\]
AM--GM and (6) give
\[
d+e
=\frac15\left(\frac1b+\frac1c\right)
\ge\frac{2}{5z},
\]
\[
R
=\frac15\left(\frac1{b^2}+\frac1{c^2}\right)
\ge\frac{2}{5z^2}
=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{4}
>1.
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover,
\[
d+e+R
\ge
\frac{2}{5z}+\frac{2}{5z^2}
=\frac{25+7\sqrt5}{20}
>2,
\tag{8}
\]
where the last strict inequality follows from \(7\sqrt5>15\).
Using \(t\ge1\) and \(R>1\), equations (7)--(8) yield
\[
d+e+tR-(1+t)
=(d+e-1)+t(R-1)
\ge d+e+R-2
>0.
\]
This contradicts (5).  Thus every point of \(\mathcal T_Q\) satisfies
\[
\alpha_1>-1.
\]
Cyclic repetition gives
\[
\mathcal T_Q
\subseteq
\{x:\alpha_i(x)\ge-1\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{9}
\]

The triangle on the right of (9) is the image of \(Q=\{\alpha_i\ge0\}\) under a homothety of linear factor four: the coordinates
\[
\beta_i=\frac{\alpha_i+1}{4}
\]
are nonnegative and sum to one.  Its area is therefore \(16|Q|=16\).  Hence
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\le16.
\tag{10}
\]
The lower bound (1) is \(169/10>16\), contradicting (10).  No realization with triangular \(A_2\) exists.  The argument does not constrain a nonsymmetric \(A_2\) with four or more vertices.
