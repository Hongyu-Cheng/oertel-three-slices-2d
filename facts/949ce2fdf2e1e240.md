## Statement

For a planar convex body \(A\) and \(z\in A\), define its unnormalized planar
halfspace depth by
\[
d_A(z):=
\inf\bigl\{|A\cap Q|:
Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ is a closed halfplane and }z\in Q\bigr\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), and \(c\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
G_i^u(c):=
\left|A_i\cap\{w\in\mathbb R^2:\langle u,w\rangle\leq c\}\right|.
\]
In the setting of the locked problem, for every \(z\in A_0\) and every
\(z\in A_2\),
\[
h_S((0,z))=d_{A_0}(z),
\qquad
h_S((2,z))=d_{A_2}(z).
\tag{2}
\]
For every \(z\in A_1\),
\[
h_S((1,z))
=
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+
\inf_{\substack{c_0,c_2\in\mathbb R\\
c_0+c_2=2\langle u,z\rangle}}
\bigl(G_0^u(c_0)+G_2^u(c_2)\bigr)
\right].
\tag{3}
\]
For each fixed \(u\), the inner infimum in (3) is attained.

The two slice-parallel halfspaces through \((1,z)\) are
\(\{x\leq1\}\) and \(\{x\geq1\}\). Their exact masses are
\[
a_0+a_1=T-a_2,
\qquad
a_1+a_2=T-a_0.
\tag{4}
\]
They must be separated before deriving (3), because their boundary contains
the whole middle slice and hence has positive slice measure. Nevertheless,
each term in (4) is strictly larger than the infimum over tilted halfspaces,
so removing them gives the exact equality (3). Moreover, if
\(a_i<T/2\) for every \(i\), then both terms in (4) are strictly larger than
\(T/2\), and therefore strictly larger than \(2T/9\).

## Proof

We first record the elementary planar facts used below. For \(u\in\mathbb
S^1\), compactness of \(A_i\) gives finite support values
\[
\ell_i(u):=\min_{w\in A_i}\langle u,w\rangle,
\qquad
r_i(u):=\max_{w\in A_i}\langle u,w\rangle.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
G_i^u(c)=0\quad\text{if }c<\ell_i(u),
\qquad
G_i^u(c)=a_i\quad\text{if }c\geq r_i(u).
\tag{6}
\]
For any \(c\), the set
\(A_i\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle=c\}\) lies in an affine line and has
planar area zero. Hence the closed boundary in the definition of \(G_i^u\)
does not create a planar atom. In particular, dominated convergence shows
that \(c\mapsto G_i^u(c)\) is continuous, and
\[
G_i^u(c)+G_i^{-u}(-c)=a_i.
\tag{7}
\]

Every proper closed planar halfplane containing \(z\) can be oriented as
\(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq c\}\), where \(u\in\mathbb S^1\) and
\(\langle u,z\rangle\leq c\). Moving its boundary parallel to \(z\) replaces
it by the contained halfplane
\(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle\}\). Conversely, every such
boundary-through-\(z\) halfplane is admissible. Both choices of side occur
because \(u\) ranges over the full circle. Therefore
\[
d_{A_i}(z)
=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}G_i^u(\langle u,z\rangle).
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (7), with \(c=\langle u,z\rangle\), also gives
\[
d_{A_i}(z)\leq\frac{a_i}{2}<a_i.
\tag{9}
\]

We now prove the first identity in (2). Put \(s=\langle u,z\rangle\).
Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, specialized to \(j=0\), says that the
tilted halfspace with parameters \(u,\lambda\) has mass
\[
G_0^u(s)+G_1^u(s-\lambda)+G_2^u(s-2\lambda),
\tag{10}
\]
and that the two horizontal masses are \(a_0\) and \(T\). The expression in
(10) is at least \(G_0^u(s)\). Conversely, by (5), one can choose a finite
\(\lambda\) so large that
\[
s-\lambda<\ell_1(u),
\qquad
s-2\lambda<\ell_2(u).
\]
Then (6) makes both unwanted terms in (10) exactly zero. Thus the infimum
over \(\lambda\), for this fixed orientation \(u\), is exactly \(G_0^u(s)\).
Taking the infimum over \(u\), using (8), and then using (9) shows
\[
h_S((0,z))
=\min\{d_{A_0}(z),a_0,T\}
=d_{A_0}(z).
\tag{11}
\]

For \(z\in A_2\), the tilted mass from the same accepted fact is
\[
G_0^u(s+2\lambda)+G_1^u(s+\lambda)+G_2^u(s),
\tag{12}
\]
and the horizontal masses are \(T\) and \(a_2\). Choosing a finite
\(\lambda\) sufficiently negative makes the first two thresholds in (12)
strictly smaller than \(\ell_0(u)\) and \(\ell_1(u)\). The same argument
gives
\[
h_S((2,z))
=\min\{d_{A_2}(z),T,a_2\}
=d_{A_2}(z),
\tag{13}
\]
which proves (2). Compactness is what turns the formal limits
\(\lambda\to+\infty\) in (10) and \(\lambda\to-\infty\) in (12) into exact
equalities for finite parameters.

It remains to prove (3). Fix \(z\in A_1\), \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), and put
\(s=\langle u,z\rangle\). For a nonhorizontal boundary-through-\((1,z)\)
halfspace, accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` gives the exact mass
\[
G_0^u(s+\lambda)+G_1^u(s)+G_2^u(s-\lambda).
\tag{14}
\]
The substitution
\[
c_0=s+\lambda,
\qquad
c_2=s-\lambda
\tag{15}
\]
is a bijection from \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) to the affine line
\[
c_0+c_2=2s.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the infimum of (14) over all tilted halfspaces is exactly the right
side of (3).

There is no hidden limiting case in the inner infimum. For fixed \(u\), its
variable part is the continuous function
\[
c_0\longmapsto G_0^u(c_0)+G_2^u(2s-c_0).
\tag{17}
\]
For all sufficiently negative \(c_0\), compact support makes (17) exactly
\(a_2\). For all sufficiently positive \(c_0\), it is exactly \(a_0\).
Hence its infimum is attained on a bounded closed interval. Equivalently,
the two tails in the infimal convolution are genuine compact-support values,
not unverified limits.

Fact `400272f6de6c67bb` still places the two horizontal terms (4) beside the
tilted infimum. They cannot simply be identified with
\(\lambda=\pm\infty\) in (14). Indeed, every finite tilted halfspace cuts
the middle slice by a planar halfplane through \(z\), while a horizontal
halfspace has the entire \(A_1\) in its boundary. The latter boundary has
two-dimensional Hausdorff measure \(a_1\), in contrast to the null affine
lines in the nonhorizontal slices. This explains both the separate terms
and the use of closed halfspaces.

We finally show that these separate terms do not change the minimum. By
(7), for any initial \(u\), at least one of \(u\) and \(-u\), denoted again
by \(u\), satisfies
\[
G_1^u(s)\leq\frac{a_1}{2},
\qquad s=\langle u,z\rangle.
\tag{18}
\]
Choose \(\lambda\) sufficiently positive in (14). Compactness then gives
\[
G_0^u(s+\lambda)=a_0,
\qquad
G_2^u(s-\lambda)=0,
\]
so the tilted infimum is at most
\[
a_0+G_1^u(s)
\leq a_0+\frac{a_1}{2}
<a_0+a_1.
\tag{19}
\]
Choosing \(\lambda\) sufficiently negative instead gives
\[
G_0^u(s+\lambda)=0,
\qquad
G_2^u(s-\lambda)=a_2,
\]
and hence the tilted infimum is at most
\[
a_2+G_1^u(s)
\leq a_2+\frac{a_1}{2}
<a_1+a_2.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus both horizontal terms are strictly dominated, and the exact minimum
formula in `400272f6de6c67bb` reduces to (3).

If \(a_i<T/2\) for all \(i\), then
\[
T-a_2>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9},
\qquad
T-a_0>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{21}
\]
Therefore the horizontal halfspaces cannot obstruct the target depth in the
balanced region. Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` is used only to justify
this global reduction: any counterexample to the locked target must lie in
the strict balanced region. The identities (2) and (3) themselves use only
the exact halfspace formula `400272f6de6c67bb` and the elementary arguments
above.
