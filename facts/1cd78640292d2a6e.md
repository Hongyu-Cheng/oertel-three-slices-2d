## Statement

Assume the full opposite common-plane hypotheses of `2b42506f5de09be1`, the full-section conventions of `989c9b585d2b42cc`, and the \(A_2\) coordinates and offsets of `fa0e57b225178b3b`.  The slope argument below is the opposite-terminal analogue of the global width argument in `c5b7a9a9bad7377d`, but it uses the \(Q_N\)-area and all three full chords simultaneously rather than an independent two-chord cap inequality.

Put
\[
 T=-q_j=S-U+V,\qquad R=-q_\ell=-V,
\]
and abbreviate
\[
 J=J_2,\qquad H=H_2,\qquad K=K_2,\qquad
 q=v+J,
\]
\[
 \theta=S-u-H,\qquad
 B=\theta+H=S-u,\qquad
 C=S+v,\qquad D=S+q.
\]
Thus \(J,H,K>0\), \(u,v,w\ge0\), where \(w=w_0\), and
\[
 u=0\Longleftrightarrow v=0,\qquad
 \theta=0\Longleftrightarrow w=0.
\]
In the \((T,R)\)-plane the three full chords are
\[
 T=0,\quad R\in[w,w+K],
\]
\[
 R=0,\quad T\in[\theta,B],
\]
\[
 T+R=S,\quad R\in[-q,-v].
\]
Their coordinate-area scale is \(\Delta\).  The cell \(Q_N\) has physical area \(g\) and lies in \(T,R>0\), while \(Q_{k\ell}\) has physical area \(s\) and is the terminal cap \(T<0,R>0\).

First suppose \(\theta,w>0\).  Define three lower-endpoint pairs
\[
 (r_1,t_1)=(w,\theta),\qquad
 (r_2,t_2)=(w+v,C),\qquad
 (r_3,t_3)=(w+q,D),
\]
and three upper-endpoint pairs
\[
 (p_1,z_1)=(w+K,B),\qquad
 (p_2,z_2)=(w+K+v,C),\qquad
 (p_3,z_3)=(w+K+q,D).
\]
For every \(i,j\in\{1,2,3\}\), take the polynomial endpoint-order branch
\[
 r_i t_k\ge r_k t_i\quad(k=1,2,3),
\qquad
 p_j z_k\le p_k z_j\quad(k=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
On this branch put
\[
 {\cal N}_i
 =
 \bigl(2\Delta g-2\theta K-2H(w+K)\bigr)t_i
 +r_iH(2\theta+H),
\tag{2}
\]
\[
 {\cal E}_{ij}=r_i z_j-p_jt_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Every actual polygon satisfies
\[
 2\Delta s\,({\cal N}_i)_+
 \le K^2B^2t_i,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 2\Delta s\,({\cal E}_{ij})_+
 \le K^2t_i z_j.
\tag{5}
\]

On the literal zero-offset stratum \(\theta=w=0\), use the two lower pairs
\[
 (r_2,t_2)=(v,C),\qquad
 (r_3,t_3)=(q,D),
\]
and the three upper pairs
\[
 (p_1,z_1)=(K,H),\qquad
 (p_2,z_2)=(K+v,C),\qquad
 (p_3,z_3)=(K+q,D).
\]
Choose \(i\in\{2,3\}\) and \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\) by the same polynomial max/min conditions (1), restricted to the available lower indices, and put
\[
 {\cal N}_i^0=(2\Delta g-2KH)t_i+r_iH^2,
\qquad
 {\cal E}_{ij}^0=r_i z_j-p_jt_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 2\Delta s\,({\cal N}_i^0)_+
 \le K^2H^2t_i,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
 2\Delta s\,({\cal E}_{ij}^0)_+
 \le K^2t_i z_j.
\tag{8}
\]

Equivalently, on a positive-offset branch define
\[
 d_{ij}
 =
 \max\left\{
 \frac{{\cal N}_i}{B^2t_i},
 \frac{{\cal E}_{ij}}{t_i z_j}
 \right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The global family places the terminal area in
\[
 0\le s\le \frac{K^2}{2\Delta d_{ij}}
 \quad\hbox{if }d_{ij}>0,
\tag{10}
\]
and places no upper restriction on \(s\) if \(d_{ij}\le0\).  On the zero-offset stratum, replace the first denominator in (9) by \(H^2t_i\) and use (6).  Thus (4)-(5) and (7)-(8) are the exact finite polynomial form of the range imposed by this global three-chord slope bound.

The weak order conditions in (1) include every ratio tie.  The stratum \(u=v=0\), where \(B=C\), is included without division by \(u+v\).  The separate \(\theta=w=0\) formulas divide by neither \(\theta\) nor \(w\).  The cases \({\cal N}_i=0\) and \({\cal E}_{ij}=0\) include tangent and support-face equalities.  Each positive-part inequality is literally the finite polynomial disjunction in which its argument is nonpositive, or its argument is positive and the displayed polynomial inequality holds.

Finally, adjoin (1)-(8) to the inherited finite system of `6090beea12ef2770`.  The resulting augmented system is nonempty.  Its displayed strict rational point selects \(i=j=1\) in the positive-offset family and satisfies both new positive-part inequalities strictly inside their inactive branches.

## Proof

Let \(I\) be the \(T\)-projection of \(A_2\).  For \(x\in I\), write
\[
 h(x)=\max\{R:(x,R)\in A_2\},\qquad
 \ell(x)=\min\{R:(x,R)\in A_2\},
\]
\[
 W(x)=h(x)-\ell(x).
\]
The function \(h\) is concave, \(\ell\) is convex, and \(W\) is nonnegative and concave.  The positive \(Q_N\) and \(Q_{k\ell}\) cells put \(0\) in the interior of \(I\), so all one-sided polygonal slopes used below are finite.  Set
\[
 a=h'_+(0),\qquad b=\ell'_+(0),\qquad d=W'_-(0).
\]
Concavity of \(W\) gives
\[
 d\ge W'_+(0)=a-b.
\tag{11}
\]

The full \(T=0\) chord gives
\[
 h(0)=w+K,\qquad \ell(0)=w,\qquad W(0)=K.
\tag{12}
\]
Every point on the other two full chords belongs to its vertical fiber.  Convexity of \(\ell\), concavity of \(h\), and (12) therefore give, when \(\theta>0\),
\[
 b\le-\frac w\theta,\qquad
 b\le-\frac{w+v}{C},\qquad
 b\le-\frac{w+q}{D},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
 a\ge-\frac{w+K}{B},\qquad
 a\ge-\frac{w+K+v}{C},\qquad
 a\ge-\frac{w+K+q}{D}.
\tag{14}
\]
For example, the point \((C,-v)\) gives
\(\ell(C)\le-v\le h(C)\), which yields the middle inequalities in (13)-(14); the point \((D,-q)\) gives the last pair.  No assumption about which polygon edge contains a chord endpoint is used.

On branch (1),
\[
 \lambda:=\max\left\{\frac w\theta,
 \frac{w+v}{C},\frac{w+q}{D}\right\}
 =\frac{r_i}{t_i},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
 \rho:=\min\left\{\frac{w+K}{B},
 \frac{w+K+v}{C},\frac{w+K+q}{D}\right\}
 =\frac{p_j}{z_j}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (11), (13), and (14) imply
\[
 d\ge\lambda-\rho
 =\frac{{\cal E}_{ij}}{t_i z_j}.
\tag{17}
\]
They also imply \(b\le-\lambda\) and hence
\[
 a\le d+b\le d-\lambda.
\tag{18}
\]

We next use the whole \(Q_N\)-area.  Since
\[
 B=S-u\le S+v=C<D,
\tag{19}
\]
there is no point of \(A_2\) with \(T>B\) and \(R>0\).  Indeed, if \(v>0\), joining such a point to the chord point \((C,-v)\) would meet \(R=0\) at a \(T\)-coordinate strictly larger than \(B\), contradicting the full \(R=0\) chord.  If \(v=0\), then the literal stratum gives \(u=0\), hence \(B=C=S\); joining instead to \((D,-q)\), where \(q=J>0\), gives the same contradiction.  This is the step that uses the third full chord and rules out a hidden positive-\(R\) tail.

For \(0\le x\le\theta\), the \(Q_N\)-length is at most \(W(x)\).  For \(\theta\le x\le B\), it is at most \(h(x)\).  The left derivative \(d\) is a supergradient of \(W\), while (18) bounds the right supergradient of \(h\).  Consequently
\[
 W(x)\le K+dx\quad(0\le x\le\theta),
\tag{20}
\]
\[
 h(x)\le w+K+(d-\lambda)x
 \quad(\theta\le x\le B).
\tag{21}
\]
Using (19) to exclude any omitted tail and integrating (20)-(21) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta g
 &\le
 \theta K+\frac{d\theta^2}{2}
 +H(w+K)
 +\frac{(d-\lambda)(B^2-\theta^2)}2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(B^2-\theta^2=H(2\theta+H)\), this is
\[
 d\ge
 \frac{
 2\Delta g-2\theta K-2H(w+K)
 +\lambda H(2\theta+H)}
 {B^2}
 =
 \frac{{\cal N}_i}{B^2t_i}.
\tag{23}
\]

Up to cutting-line boundaries, the whole \(T<0\) part is the terminal cell \(Q_{k\ell}\), so its coordinate area is \(\Delta s\).  If a lower bound \(L\) for \(d\) is positive, concavity and (12) give, for \(x\le0\),
\[
 0\le W(x)\le K+dx\le K+Lx.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \Delta s\le\frac{K^2}{2d}\le\frac{K^2}{2L}.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply (24) first with the lower bound in (23) and then with the lower bound in (17).  If the respective numerator is nonpositive, the assertion is automatic.  Clearing only the positive denominators \(B,t_i,z_j\) gives exactly (4) and (5).

Now suppose \(\theta=w=0\).  The full horizontal chord is \(0\le T\le H\).  The two diagonal chord endpoints give
\[
 b\le-\max\left\{\frac vC,\frac qD\right\}
 =-\frac{r_i}{t_i},
\]
while (14) remains valid with \((p_1,z_1)=(K,H)\).  Thus (17) is unchanged.  On \(0\le x\le H\), the \(Q_N\)-length is \(h(x)\), and (18) gives
\[
 \Delta g
 \le\int_0^H\bigl(K+(d-\lambda)x\bigr)\,dx
 =KH+\frac{(d-\lambda)H^2}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 d\ge\frac{{\cal N}_i^0}{H^2t_i}.
\]
Applying (24) and clearing the positive denominators gives (7)-(8).  This proves every positive-offset, zero-offset, endpoint-tie, tangent, and support-face branch.  The branch conditions and the two-case expansions of the positive parts make the family finite and polynomial.

It remains to decide the augmented inherited system.  At the strict rational point of `6090beea12ef2770`,
\[
 \Delta=1,\quad g=20,\quad s=\frac3{20},\quad S=102,
\]
\[
 J=\frac{11}{50},\quad H=\frac14,\quad K=\frac15,
\quad u=v=\frac3{10},\quad w=50,\quad
 \theta=\frac{2029}{20}.
\]
Hence
\[
 q=\frac{13}{25},\qquad
 B=\frac{1017}{10},\qquad
 C=\frac{1023}{10},\qquad
 D=\frac{2563}{25}.
\]
The lower-ratio comparisons selecting \(i=1\) are strictly
\[
 r_1t_2-r_2t_1=\frac{2413}{200}>0,
\qquad
 r_1t_3-r_3t_1=\frac{373}{500}>0.
\]
The upper-ratio comparisons selecting \(j=1\) are strictly
\[
 p_1z_2-p_2z_1=-\frac{39}{100}<0,
\qquad
 p_1z_3-p_3z_1=-\frac{293}{25}<0.
\]
On this branch the two polynomial positive-part arguments are
\[
 {\cal N}_1=-\frac{65861}{1000}<0,
\qquad
 {\cal E}_{11}=-\frac{779}{100}<0.
\]
Thus the left sides of (4)-(5) are both zero, while their right sides are positive.  Every inherited nonidentity inequality and every inherited branch sign is strict by `6090beea12ef2770`; the four new branch comparisons and both new inactive-branch signs are also strict.  The same rational point is therefore a relative-interior point of the augmented finite semialgebraic system.  This proves exact nonemptiness of that augmented necessary system.  It does not assert completion of the displayed data to a common-plane polygon.
