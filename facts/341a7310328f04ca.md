## Statement

For
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1/2)\},\\
A_1&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/2,1/2),(0,0),(3/4,0),
                         (1/2,1/4),(-1/2,3/4)\},\\
A_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(-1,1)\},
\end{aligned}
\]
use strict target \(11/48\), and define \(M_r(c)\) and \(R_r\) as in
accepted facts `3c143dfcc90fab8e` and `8b8d446672f7036b`.  If
\[
u_1(\lambda)=(1,\lambda),\qquad u_2=(-1,0),\qquad u_3=(0,-1),
\qquad 1\leq\lambda\leq2,
\]
then
\[
R_1(\lambda)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}
\]
and
\[
R_1(\lambda)+R_2+\lambda R_3
\leq
\frac{\sqrt{78}+2\sqrt{26}-20}{12}<0.
\]
Consequently, no three halfspaces with these three middle-plane normals,
each of total three-slice mass strictly below \(11/48\), have height-one
trace interiors covering \(\mathbb R^2\).

## Proof

For the moving direction \(u_1(\lambda)=(1,\lambda)\), put
\[
F_\lambda(t)
=\left|A_1\cap\{(x,y):x+\lambda y\leq t\}\right|.
\]
We first audit \(F_\lambda(\lambda/2)\) exactly on the whole parameter
interval.  In counterclockwise order, write the vertices of \(A_1\) as
\[
\begin{aligned}
p&=(-1/2,1/2),&q&=(0,0),&r&=(3/4,0),\\
s&=(1/2,1/4),&v&=(-1/2,3/4).
\end{aligned}
\]
Their \(x+\lambda y\) values are
\[
\frac{\lambda-1}{2},\qquad 0,\qquad\frac34,\qquad
\frac{\lambda+2}{4},\qquad\frac{3\lambda-2}{4},
\tag{1}
\]
respectively.  The support-order boundaries on \(1\leq\lambda\leq2\)
are \(\lambda=1,5/3,2\).  Relative to the clipping level
\(t=\lambda/2\), the only additional vertex-crossing boundary is
\(\lambda=3/2\), when \(r\) lies on the cutting line.  The order crossing
at \(\lambda=5/3\) exchanges the values at \(r\) and \(v\), but both
vertices are already strictly below the clipping level there, so it does
not change the clipped polygon.

For every \(1\leq\lambda<2\), the cutting line meets the edge
\([s,v]\), whose supporting line is \(x+2y=1\), at the fixed point
\[
j=(0,1/2).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(1\leq\lambda\leq3/2\), its other nontrivial boundary intersection is
\[
i=(\lambda/2,0)\in[q,r].
\]
Thus the clipped polygon, with harmless coincidences allowed at the
endpoints, is
\[
A_1\cap\{x+\lambda y\leq\lambda/2\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{p,q,i,j,v\}.
\]
The shoelace formula gives
\[
2F_\lambda(\lambda/2)
=\left(\frac{\lambda}{4}-\frac14\right)-\left(-\frac58\right)
=\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac38,
\]
and hence
\[
F_\lambda(\lambda/2)=\frac{2\lambda+3}{16}
\qquad(1\leq\lambda\leq3/2).
\tag{3}
\]

If \(3/2\leq\lambda\leq2\), the vertex \(r\) is included and the cutting
line meets the edge \([r,s]\), whose supporting line is \(x+y=3/4\), at
\[
k=\left(\frac{\lambda}{4(\lambda-1)},
         \frac{2\lambda-3}{4(\lambda-1)}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
The complement of the clipped polygon in \(A_1\) is the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{s,k,j\}\).  Since
\[
k-s=
\frac{2-\lambda}{4(\lambda-1)}(1,-1),
\qquad
j-s=(-1/2,1/4),
\]
its area is
\[
\frac{2-\lambda}{32(\lambda-1)}.
\]
Using \(|A_1|=13/32\), we obtain
\[
F_\lambda(\lambda/2)
=\frac{13}{32}-\frac{2-\lambda}{32(\lambda-1)}
=\frac{14\lambda-15}{32(\lambda-1)}
\qquad(3/2\leq\lambda\leq2).
\tag{5}
\]
The expressions in (3) and (5) agree at \(\lambda=3/2\).  Formula (3)
also handles the support tie at \(\lambda=1\), while (5) gives \(13/32\)
at \(\lambda=2\), where the triangle in question degenerates and the
cutting line contains the full edge \([s,v]\).  Equations (1)--(5)
therefore include every support-order and clipping boundary in
\([1,2]\).

Both pieces are increasing: this is immediate in (3), and (5) can be
written as
\[
\frac7{16}-\frac{1}{32(\lambda-1)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
F_\lambda(\lambda/2)\geq F_1(1/2)
=\frac5{16}>\frac{11}{48}.
\tag{6}
\]
In the definition of the moving-direction endpoint,
\[
M_1(c)=
F_\lambda(c)+
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
\left|A_0\cap\{x+\lambda y\leq a\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{x+\lambda y\leq2c-a\}\right|
\right).
\]
Both terms inside the infimum are nonnegative, so (6) yields
\[
M_1(\lambda/2)\geq F_\lambda(\lambda/2)>\frac{11}{48}.
\tag{7}
\]
No support-order information from \(A_0\) or \(A_2\) is used here: their
contributions enter only through this uniform nonnegativity.
For each fixed slope, the three-slice mass is nondecreasing in \(c\);
taking the infimum over slopes preserves this monotonicity.  Hence (7)
implies
\[
R_1(\lambda)
=\sup\{c:M_1(c)<11/48\}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Accepted facts `3c143dfcc90fab8e` and `8b8d446672f7036b` give
\[
R_2=-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12},
\qquad
R_3=-\frac56+\frac{\sqrt{26}}{12}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\sqrt{26}>4\), the affine upper bound obtained from (8) and
(9) is increasing in \(\lambda\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_1(\lambda)+R_2+\lambda R_3
&\leq
-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12}
+\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{26}-4)}{12}\\
&\leq
\frac{\sqrt{78}+2\sqrt{26}-20}{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The exact comparisons \(78<9^2\) and \(26<(11/2)^2\) give
\[
\sqrt{78}+2\sqrt{26}<9+11=20,
\]
so the last member of (10) is strictly negative.

Finally, let three halfspaces with the displayed normals have height-one
thresholds \(c_1,c_2,c_3\) and total masses strictly below \(11/48\).
Then \(M_r(c_r)<11/48\), hence \(c_r\leq R_r\), and (10) gives
\[
c_1+c_2+\lambda c_3<0.
\tag{11}
\]
The closed complements of their height-one trace interiors are
\[
x+\lambda y\geq c_1,\qquad x\leq-c_2,\qquad y\leq-c_3.
\]
They have the common point \((-c_2,-c_3)\) whenever
\(c_1+c_2+\lambda c_3\leq0\), which holds by (11).  Thus the three trace
interiors do not cover the middle plane.  This excludes the full normal
wedge.
