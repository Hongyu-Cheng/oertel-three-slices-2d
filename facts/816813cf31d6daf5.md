## Statement

Let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).  Use the common-plane exact-cell
convention after accepted fact `8b9c428a36c45d68`.  Put
\[
\pi_s=f_s-L_s,\qquad
\theta_s=f_s+L_s,
\]
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,\qquad
\sigma=\delta+2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\gamma>0\) be the inverse area Jacobian of
\((\pi_i,\pi_j)\), equivalently of \((\theta_i,\theta_j)\).
Assume
\[
p_{ij}p_{ik}p_{jk}>0,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
q_j=0,\qquad
q_iq_kq_{ij}q_{ik}q_{jk}>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Then, by accepted fact `cc8c0bf6093b9357`,
\[
\overline{P_0}
=T_\delta
:=\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq\delta\},
\qquad
p_0=\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(B\) be any compact convex body satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq B,
\tag{5}
\]
and let \(\rho_G\) be its exact middle-cell areas.  For
\(s\in\{i,k\}\), define
\[
\Lambda_s=\frac{4\rho_s-p_0-q_s}{2p_0}.
\tag{6}
\]
If
\[
\Lambda_s<\sqrt{\frac{q_s}{p_0}}
\tag{7}
\]
for either \(s\), then no such \(B\) exists.  Otherwise define
\[
H_s
=\Lambda_s-\sqrt{\Lambda_s^2-\frac{q_s}{p_0}},
\qquad
H=\min\{H_i,H_k\},
\qquad
\lambda=1+\frac2\delta.
\tag{8}
\]
Every realization necessarily satisfies
\[
\boxed{\qquad
q_{ik}\geq
2p_0\left(\lambda H+\frac{H^2}{2}\right).
\qquad}
\tag{9}
\]

For the equality case, let \(h_s\) be the vertical height of
\(\overline{Q_s}\) in the coordinates used below and put
\(h=\min\{h_i,h_k\}\).  Equality in (9) requires
\[
h=\delta H.
\tag{10}
\]
For an endpoint index \(s\in\{i,k\}\) that both satisfies
\(H_s=H\) and realizes the minimum \(h_s=h\), equality further
requires
\[
\frac{t_s}{\delta}=\Lambda_s,\qquad
\frac{h_s}{\delta}=H_s,
\tag{11}
\]
and \(\overline{Q_s}\) must fill, up to null sets, every allowed
horizontal section of its aligned triangle through height \(h_s\).
No such conclusion is asserted for the other endpoint when it does
not realize the minimum.  Equality also requires \(Q_{ik}\), up to
null sets, to consist exactly of the forced intervening strip over
\(0<y<h\), with no additional positive-area \(Q_{ik}\)-part.

Substitution of the exact endpoint and target data in accepted fact
`9fdba5fffd591cda` violates (9), so (9) recovers that accepted
fixed-table exclusion.  No assertion is made here that (9) alone,
or (9) together with scalar crossing and cap constraints, excludes
every one-zero support pattern.

## Proof

Work in the affine coordinates
\[
x=\theta_i,\qquad
y=\theta_j,\qquad
\theta_k=-\sigma-x-y.
\tag{12}
\]
Let
\[
C_i=\overline{Q_i},\qquad
C_k=\overline{Q_k}.
\]
Every positive entry in (3) supplies a strict chamber point.
Segments from \(Q_i\) to \(Q_{ij}\) and \(Q_{ik}\), respectively,
give
\[
\min_{C_i}y=0,\qquad
\max_{C_i}(x+y)=-\sigma.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, the first segment crosses \(y=0\) while remaining in
\(x+y<-\sigma\), and the second crosses \(x+y=-\sigma\) while
remaining in \(y>0\).  Likewise, segments from \(Q_k\) to
\(Q_{ik}\) and \(Q_{jk}\) give
\[
\min_{C_k}x=\min_{C_k}y=0.
\tag{14}
\]

Define
\[
a_i=\min_{C_i}x,\qquad
t_i=-\sigma-a_i,\qquad
t_k=\max_{C_k}(x+y).
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) imply
\[
C_i\subseteq
\{(x,y):x\geq a_i,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq-\sigma\},
\tag{16}
\]
\[
C_k\subseteq
\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq t_k\}.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, \(t_i,t_k>0\).

For any compact convex planar set \(C\), put
\[
t(C)=\max_C(x+y)-\min_Cx-\min_Cy.
\]
The Minkowski support identity
\[
|T_\delta+C|_{\rm coord}
=\frac{\delta^2}{2}+|C|_{\rm coord}+\delta t(C)
\tag{18}
\]
follows by adding the three support strips of \(T_\delta\), whose
outward normals are proportional to
\((0,-1),(-1,0),(1,1)\).  Equivalently, it follows by expanding
the ordered edge vectors in the polygonal case and then using
polygonal approximation.  Equations (13)--(17) give
\[
t(C_i)=t_i,\qquad t(C_k)=t_k.
\tag{19}
\]

For \(s=i,k\), the two midpoint scores outside index \(s\) are
positive on \((P_0+Q_s)/2\).  Since the three middle scores sum to
\(-1\), the remaining score is negative.  Hence, up to null
cutting lines,
\[
\frac{\overline{P_0}+C_s}{2}\subseteq B\cap R_s.
\tag{20}
\]
Using (4), (18), and the area Jacobian in (20) gives
\[
\rho_s\geq
\frac14\left(p_0+q_s+\gamma\delta t_s\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\), equations (6) and (21) yield
\[
\frac{t_s}{\delta}\leq\Lambda_s.
\tag{22}
\]

Let
\[
h_s=\max\{y:(x,y)\in C_s\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) show that the vertical projection of each
\(C_s\) is \([0,h_s]\).  At height \(y\), equations
(16)--(17) bound its horizontal section length by \(t_s-y\).
Cavalieri integration gives
\[
\frac{q_s}{\gamma}
\leq t_sh_s-\frac{h_s^2}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
Normalize
\[
\widehat t_s=\frac{t_s}{\delta},\qquad
\widehat h_s=\frac{h_s}{\delta}.
\]
Using \(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\) and (22), equation (24) becomes
\[
\frac{q_s}{2p_0}
\leq
\widehat t_s\widehat h_s-\frac{\widehat h_s^2}{2}
\leq
\Lambda_s\widehat h_s-\frac{\widehat h_s^2}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Here
\[
0<\widehat h_s\leq\widehat t_s\leq\Lambda_s.
\]
The last expression in (25) is increasing on
\([0,\Lambda_s]\).  Its maximum is \(\Lambda_s^2/2\), proving the
impossibility assertion (7).
When (7) fails, solving the quadratic inequality on
\([0,\Lambda_s]\) gives
\[
\widehat h_s\geq
\Lambda_s-\sqrt{\Lambda_s^2-\frac{q_s}{p_0}}
=H_s.
\tag{26}
\]

For every
\[
0<y<h:=\min\{h_i,h_k\},
\]
the horizontal section of \(A_2\) contains a \(C_i\)-point at or to
the left of \(x=-\sigma-y\) and a \(C_k\)-point at or to the right
of \(x=0\).  Convexity forces the whole interval
\[
[-\sigma-y,0],
\]
which is precisely the \(Q_{ik}\)-strip at height \(y\).
Therefore
\[
\frac{q_{ik}}{\gamma}
\geq
\sigma h+\frac{h^2}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]
Equation (26) gives \(h/\delta\geq H\).  The right side of (27) is
strictly increasing for \(h\geq0\).  Using
\(\sigma/\delta=\lambda\) and
\(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\) proves (9).

If equality holds in (9), then equality must hold in the monotone
step from \(h/\delta\geq H\), proving (10), and in (27), proving
the stated exact-strip condition.  Suppose an endpoint \(s\)
satisfies \(H_s=H\) and \(h_s=h\).  Then (10) and (26) give
\(\widehat h_s=H_s\).  At this value, the last member of (25)
equals \(q_s/(2p_0)\) by the defining quadratic equation for
\(H_s\).  Hence both inequalities in (25) are equalities.
Because \(\widehat h_s>0\), the second equality forces
\(\widehat t_s=\Lambda_s\).  Equality in the first member of (25), or
equivalently in (24), forces almost every horizontal section to
have its full allowed length \(t_s-y\).  This proves (11) and the
claimed endpoint equality condition, without imposing it on an
endpoint that does not realize the minimum.

Finally, accepted fact `9fdba5fffd591cda` has
\((i,j,k)=(1,2,3)\), all three positive \(P\)-doubletons, and all
five positive \(Q\)-cells required in (3).  Its exact support
allowances and height calculation are equations (19)--(23) of that
fact, and its prescribed \(q_{13}\) is contradicted in equations
(23)--(24).  Thus it is an exact application of (9), as claimed.
