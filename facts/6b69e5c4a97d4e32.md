## Statement

Let \(\mathcal R_n\) be a residual counterexample sequence satisfying all
hypotheses of accepted fact `d1177b21864bbcc6`, normalized by
\[
q_n=1,\qquad
x_{1,n}=x_{2,n}=x_{3,n}=\frac5{21},\qquad
m_n=\frac{12}{7},
\qquad
D_n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Assume, as required of a residual counterexample by accepted facts
`2f9be517307232c7` and `15bb2c1680a0254e`, that no slice has at least half
the total area.  Then alternative (T) of `d1177b21864bbcc6` cannot occur.
Hence, after passage to the subsequence in that accepted dichotomy,
\[
\sup\{|z|:z\in\widetilde K_n\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

## Proof

Suppose alternative (T) occurs.  Use the normalized limiting cell masses
of accepted fact `d1177b21864bbcc6` and omit bars.  Put
\[
s_P=p_i+p_j+p_k,\qquad
d_P=p_{ik}+p_{jk},\qquad
d_R=r_{ik}+r_{jk}.
\tag{1}
\]
The only possibly nonzero masses in the bounded limit give
\[
M=p_0+s_P+d_P,
\qquad
\eta=s_P+4d_P+3d_R,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
u_k=p_k+d_P,\qquad
c_k=d_R.
\tag{3}
\]
All quantities in (1) are nonnegative.

We first justify the limiting balance and its endpoint.  At every finite
stage, \(M_n\geq m_n\), so \(M_n\) is the area of the larger outer slice,
\(m_n=12/7\) is the area of the other outer slice, and \(k_n\) is the
middle-slice area.  Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` certifies the target
whenever the larger outer slice has at least half the total area.  Thus a
residual counterexample must satisfy the strict reverse inequality
\[
M_n<m_n+k_n=\frac{12}{7}+k_n.
\tag{4}
\]
This is exactly the strict large-slice balance recorded in the residual
area system of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.

In alternative (T), accepted fact `d1177b21864bbcc6` gives convergence of
all transformed cell areas after division by the transformed central
\(Q\)-mass.  These normalized areas equal the original \(q_n=1\) area
ratios because the common affine multiplier cancels.  Hence
\[
M_n\longrightarrow M,\qquad k_n\longrightarrow k.
\]
Passing (4) to the limit loses strictness, and gives exactly
\[
M\leq\frac{12}{7}+k.
\tag{5}
\]

The limiting \(W=0\) identity in `d1177b21864bbcc6` is
\[
2p_0=\frac{69}{7}+k+\eta.
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (5), followed by (2), gives
\[
k\geq M-\frac{12}{7}
=p_0+s_P+d_P-\frac{12}{7}.
\tag{7}
\]
Substitute (2) and (7) into (6):
\[
\begin{aligned}
2p_0
&\geq
\frac{69}{7}
+p_0+s_P+d_P-\frac{12}{7}
+s_P+4d_P+3d_R\\
&=
p_0+\frac{57}{7}+2s_P+5d_P+3d_R.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
p_0\geq
\frac{57}{7}+2s_P+5d_P+3d_R.
\tag{8}
\]

We next audit the \(k\)-crossing.  The limiting crossing and (3) give
\[
u_k+\frac{31}{21}+d_R
=\frac{2(M+12/7+k)}9.
\tag{9}
\]
Multiplication by \(9\) and collection of the constants
\(9(31/21)-2(12/7)=93/7-24/7=69/7\) yield
\[
2(M+k)=9u_k+\frac{69}{7}+9d_R.
\tag{10}
\]
On the other hand, solving (6) for \(k\) and using (2) gives
\[
k=2p_0-\frac{69}{7}-s_P-4d_P-3d_R,
\]
and hence
\[
M+k
=3p_0-\frac{69}{7}-3d_P-3d_R.
\tag{11}
\]
Substitution of (11) into (10) gives
\[
6p_0-\frac{138}{7}-6d_P-6d_R
=9u_k+\frac{69}{7}+9d_R,
\]
or, after moving terms and dividing by \(3\),
\[
2p_0
=\frac{69}{7}+3u_k+2d_P+5d_R.
\tag{12}
\]
Every coefficient in (12) is therefore forced by the exact crossing and
\(W=0\), not by an inequality.

The limiting left tail in `d1177b21864bbcc6` is
\[
u_k\leq\frac5{21}.
\tag{13}
\]
Applying (13) to (12), and using
\(3(5/21)=5/7\), gives
\[
2p_0
\leq
\frac{74}{7}+2d_P+5d_R,
\qquad
p_0\leq
\frac{37}{7}+d_P+\frac52d_R.
\tag{14}
\]
Comparing (8) and (14) forces
\[
\frac{20}{7}
+2s_P+4d_P+\frac12d_R
\leq0.
\tag{15}
\]
This is impossible because \(s_P,d_P,d_R\geq0\) and \(20/7>0\).
Therefore alternative (T) cannot occur.

Accepted fact `d1177b21864bbcc6` exhausts every \(D_n\to0\) sequence,
after a subsequence, by alternatives (E) and (T).  Since (T) is now
excluded for residual counterexamples, only (E) remains, as claimed.
