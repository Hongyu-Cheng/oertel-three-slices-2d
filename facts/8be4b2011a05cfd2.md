## Statement

Let \(\Delta_0\) be a nondegenerate triangle, and let
\[
A_i=\lambda_i\Delta_0,\qquad \lambda_i>0,\qquad i=0,1,2,
\]
where all homotheties have the same center at one vertex of \(\Delta_0\). After possibly interchanging the two outer indices, assume \(\lambda_0\ge\lambda_2\), and set
\[
\Delta=\lambda_1\Delta_0,\qquad
a=\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_1},\qquad
c=\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1},\qquad
D=|\Delta|.
\]
Thus
\[
(A_0,A_1,A_2)=(a\Delta,\Delta,c\Delta),
\qquad a\ge c>0,
\]
and their areas are \(a^2D,D,c^2D\).

Call this triple residual when it is compatible and none of the large-slice, outer-centroid-area, or middle-cover sufficient criteria synthesized in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` applies. Then residuality is exactly equivalent to
\[
a\ge c>0,\qquad a+c\le2,
\]
\[
a^2<1+c^2,\qquad 1<a^2+c^2,
\]
\[
a^2+2>3c^2+2ac,\qquad 1+c^2<2a^2.
\tag{1}
\]

For a complete piecewise description, put
\[
x=\frac ca,\qquad
\theta=\frac{\sqrt{26}-1}{5}.
\]
Then (1) is exactly equivalent to \(c=ax\) and one of
\[
0<x\le\frac35,\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}<a<\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}},
\tag{2a}
\]
\[
\frac35<x\le\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}<a<
\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}},
\tag{2b}
\]
\[
\frac1{\sqrt2}<x<\theta,\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{2-x^2}}<a<
\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}}.
\tag{2c}
\]

To split this domain according to whether the larger outer slice is at least as large as the middle slice, define
\[
q=\frac{\sqrt{10}-1}{3}.
\]
The \(a\ge1\) part consists exactly of
\[
0<x\le\frac35,\qquad
1\le a<\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}},
\tag{3a}
\]
or
\[
\frac35<x<q,\qquad
1\le a<\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}}.
\tag{3b}
\]
The complementary \(a<1\) part consists exactly of
\[
0<x\le\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}<a<1,
\tag{4a}
\]
\[
\frac1{\sqrt2}<x\le q,\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{2-x^2}}<a<1,
\tag{4b}
\]
or
\[
q<x<\theta,\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{2-x^2}}<a<
\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}}.
\tag{4c}
\]

Every residual pair satisfies the stronger universal conclusions
\[
a>c>0,\qquad c<1,\qquad a+c<2,\qquad a^2<1+c^2.
\]
Thus \(c\le1\), \(a+c\le2\), and \(a^2<1+c^2\) hold in both normalizations. The condition \(a\ge1\) holds exactly in (3a)-(3b); in (4a)-(4c), the middle slice is strictly larger than both outer slices.

## Proof

Translate the common homothety center to the origin. Convexity gives
\[
a\Delta+c\Delta=(a+c)\Delta.
\]
Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` says that the three prescribed sets are compatible exactly when
\[
\frac{a\Delta+c\Delta}{2}\subseteq\Delta.
\]
Since the origin is a vertex of the nondegenerate triangle \(\Delta\), one has \(t\Delta\subseteq\Delta\) exactly when \(0<t\le1\). Hence compatibility is exactly
\[
a+c\le2.
\tag{5}
\]

Because \(a\ge c\), the largest outer area and the smaller outer area are
\[
M=a^2D,\qquad m=c^2D.
\]
Therefore the variables used in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` are
\[
r=\frac mM=\frac{c^2}{a^2}=x^2,
\qquad
s=\frac{|A_1|}{M}=\frac1{a^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Conversely,
\[
a=\frac1{\sqrt s},\qquad c=\sqrt{\frac rs},
\]
so (6) is a bijection between \(a\ge c>0\) and \(0<r\le1,\ s>0\). Notice that \(M\) is only the larger outer area. It is not necessarily the largest of all three areas, and
\[
a\ge1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\le1.
\tag{7}
\]

Under (6), the exact raw system in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` transforms as follows:
\[
s\ge\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a+c\le2,
\]
\[
s>1-r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a^2<1+c^2,
\]
\[
s<1+r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1<a^2+c^2,
\]
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a^2+2>3c^2+2ac,
\]
and
\[
s<2-r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1+c^2<2a^2.
\]
The first line is compatibility, independently obtained in (5). The next two lines are the strict failures of the large-slice criterion for the larger outer slice and the middle slice; failure for the smaller outer slice is automatic. The fourth and fifth lines are respectively the strict failures of the outer-centroid-area and middle-cover criteria. This proves the exact raw equivalence (1).

Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives the equivalent simplified strip
\[
0<r<\rho,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),
\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}=\theta^2.
\]
Substituting \(r=x^2\) and \(s=1/a^2\), the ranges \(r\le9/25\), \(9/25<r\le1/2\), and \(1/2<r<\rho\) become
\[
0<x\le\frac35,\qquad
\frac35<x\le\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt2}<x<\theta.
\]
On these three ranges the inequalities \(L(x^2)<1/a^2<U(x^2)\) are respectively
\[
1-x^2<\frac1{a^2}<1+x^2,
\]
\[
\frac{3x^2+2x-1}{2}<\frac1{a^2}<1+x^2,
\]
and
\[
\frac{3x^2+2x-1}{2}<\frac1{a^2}<2-x^2.
\]
Every displayed endpoint is positive on its stated range, so taking reciprocal square roots gives exactly (2a)-(2c).

It remains to split at \(a=1\). Every lower endpoint for \(a\) in (2a)-(2c) is strictly below \(1\), because \(x>0\) and \(x<\theta<1\). The upper endpoint in (2a) is strictly above \(1\). In (2b)-(2c), the upper endpoint satisfies
\[
\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}}
\begin{cases}
>1,&x<q,\\
=1,&x=q,\\
<1,&x>q,
\end{cases}
\]
because \(q\) is the positive root of \(3x^2+2x-3=0\). Moreover
\[
\frac35<\frac1{\sqrt2}<q<\theta<1.
\]
Indeed, \(9/25<1/2\); the inequality \(q^2>1/2\) is equivalent to \(169>160\); and if \(5x^2+2x-5\) is evaluated at \(q\), its value is \(2(q^2-1)<0\), while \(\theta\) is its positive root. Splitting (2a)-(2c) at \(a=1\) now gives exactly (3a)-(3b) and (4a)-(4c).

Finally, the residual strip has \(x<\theta<1\), so \(a>c\). The inequality \(a^2<1+c^2\) is already one of the strict failure conditions. Compatibility initially gives only \(a+c\le2\), but the two strict lower conditions strengthen it. Indeed, with
\[
B(r)=1-r,\qquad
C(r)=\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4},\qquad
E(r)=\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2},
\]
one has
\[
C(r)=\frac{B(r)+E(r)}2.
\]
Since residuality gives \(s>B(r)\) and \(s>E(r)\), it gives \(s>C(r)\), hence \(a+c<2\). If \(c\ge1\), then \(a>c\) would imply \(a+c>2\), a contradiction. Therefore \(c<1\). This proves all asserted universal conclusions and completes the equivalence.
