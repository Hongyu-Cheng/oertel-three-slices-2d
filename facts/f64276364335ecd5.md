## Statement

Let \(A_2\) be a convex quadrilateral of positive area, and let three affine
lines bound a global central triangle
\[
\mathcal T_Q=\{x:\ell_i(x)\le0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\]
Suppose the three closed outer caps
\[
C_i=A_2\cap\{\ell_i\ge0\}
\]
have pairwise disjoint interiors and each has area \(|A_2|/5\). Suppose also
that the caps strictly contain three distinct vertices \(v_1,v_2,v_3\),
one vertex per cap, while the fourth vertex \(v_4\) is strictly contained in
no cap. Then
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\le16|A_2|.
\tag{1}
\]
Cutting lines through polygon vertices are allowed.

In particular, an area-one quadrilateral with exact cell areas
\[
q_{123}=\frac25,
\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15
\]
cannot have this cap incidence in a full realization satisfying accepted fact
`b1eaab0ae37367d1`.

## Proof

After relabeling the capped vertices, the cyclic order is
\[
v_1,v_2,v_4,v_3.
\]
Use barycentric coordinates \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) relative to
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}.
\]
There are \(h,u,w>0\) such that
\[
v_4=(-h,u,w),
\qquad
u+w=1+h.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently
\[
|A_2|=(1+h)|P|.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
p=\frac{1+h}{5}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every cap therefore has area \(p|P|\).

For the cap at \(v_1\), write its fractions along \(v_1v_2\) and
\(v_1v_3\) as
\[
a,
\qquad
x=\frac pa.
\]
For the cap at \(v_2\), write the fractions along \(v_2v_1\) and
\(v_2v_4\) as
\[
b,
\qquad
y=\frac{p}{bw}.
\]
For the cap at \(v_3\), write the fractions along \(v_3v_1\) and
\(v_3v_4\) as
\[
c,
\qquad
z=\frac{p}{cu}.
\]
The area identities are the corner-triangle formula. The full corner
triangles at \(v_2,v_3\) have areas \(w|P|\) and \(u|P|\), respectively.
All six fractions lie in \((0,1]\). Disjointness along the two edges incident
with \(v_1\) gives
\[
a+b\le1,
\qquad
x+c\le1.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \(a,x<1\), so
\[
\frac15<p=ax<1.
\tag{6}
\]

After scaling each line equation by a positive constant, the three central
halfplanes are
\[
\frac{\alpha_2}{a}+\frac{\alpha_3}{x}\ge1,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\frac{\alpha_1}{b}
+\left(\frac b p+\frac{h}{bw}\right)\alpha_3
\ge1,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\frac{\alpha_1}{c}
+\left(\frac c p+\frac{h}{cu}\right)\alpha_2
\ge1.
\tag{9}
\]
For example, the second cap line meets \(v_2v_1\) at fraction \(b\) and
\(v_2v_4\) at fraction \(y=p/(bw)\). Substitution of those two points gives
(8). The capped vertex \(v_2\) makes the left side zero, so the cap side is
the side at most one and the central side is the displayed side at least one.
The other two equations and orientations follow identically.

We prove
\[
\mathcal T_Q
\subseteq
\{\alpha_i\ge-1\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{10}
\]
First control \(\alpha_1\). Set
\[
d=\left(\frac b p+\frac{h}{bw}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
e=\left(\frac c p+\frac{h}{cu}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
R=\frac db+\frac ec.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(y,z\le1\), one has \(bw\ge p\) and \(cu\ge p\), whence
\[
d\ge\frac{p}{b+h},
\qquad
e\ge\frac{p}{c+h}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (5) and \(ax=p\) give
\[
(1-b)(1-c)\ge p.
\tag{13}
\]

We need two elementary inequalities. For every \(h\ge0\),
\(p=(1+h)/5\), and \(b,c\in(0,1)\) satisfying (13),
\[
p\left(
\frac1{b(b+h)}+\frac1{c(c+h)}
\right)>1,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
p\left(
\frac{b+1}{b(b+h)}+\frac{c+1}{c(c+h)}
\right)\ge2.
\tag{15}
\]
To prove them, put \(X=1-b\) and \(Y=1-c\). The functions
\[
G(X)=\frac1{(1-X)(1+h-X)},
\]
\[
H(X)=\frac{2-X}{(1-X)(1+h-X)}
\]
are increasing and convex on \((0,1)\). Direct differentiation also shows
that \(G(e^t)\) and \(H(e^t)\) are convex in \(t\), because their second
derivatives are
\[
e^tG'(e^t)+e^{2t}G''(e^t)>0,
\qquad
e^tH'(e^t)+e^{2t}H''(e^t)>0.
\]
Since \(XY\ge p\), monotonicity first reduces the minimum to \(XY=p\);
convexity in \(\log X,\log Y\) then puts it at
\[
X=Y=\sqrt p.
\]
Writing \(q=\sqrt p\), so \(h=5q^2-1\), the two symmetric values in
(14)-(15) are
\[
\frac{2q}{(1-q)(5q-1)}>1,
\]
\[
\frac{2q(2-q)}{(1-q)(5q-1)}\ge2.
\]
The first comparison is equivalent to
\[
5q^2-4q+1>0,
\]
whose discriminant is negative. The second is equivalent to
\[
(2q-1)^2\ge0.
\]
This proves (14)-(15).

If some point of \(\mathcal T_Q\) had \(\alpha_1=-t\) with \(t>1\),
equations (8)-(9) would give
\[
1+t=\alpha_2+\alpha_3\ge d+e+tR.
\tag{16}
\]
But (12), (14), and (15) give
\[
R>1,
\qquad
d+e+R\ge2.
\]
Therefore
\[
d+e+tR-(1+t)
=(d+e+R-2)+(t-1)(R-1)>0,
\]
contradicting (16). Hence
\[
\alpha_1\ge-1
\quad\text{on }\mathcal T_Q.
\tag{17}
\]

Next control \(\alpha_2\). If \(\alpha_2=-t\) with \(t>1\), equations
(7) and (9) give
\[
1+t=\alpha_1+\alpha_3
\ge x+c+tR_2,
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
R_2=\frac{x^2}{p}+\frac{c^2}{p}+\frac hu.
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
\rho=\frac pu.
\tag{20}
\]
The inequalities \(z\le1\), \(y\le1\), and \(b\le1-a\) imply
\[
c\ge\rho,
\qquad
(1-a)w\ge p.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(a=p/x\), \(u=p/\rho\), and \(u+w=5p\), equation (21) yields
\[
\rho>\frac14,
\qquad
x\ge p\,\frac{5\rho-1}{4\rho-1}.
\tag{22}
\]
Also \(\rho<1\), because \(c\ge\rho\) and \(c<1\) by (5).

Both \(R_2\) and \(x+c+R_2\) increase with \(x\) and \(c\). With
\[
A(\rho)=\frac{5\rho-1}{4\rho-1},
\]
their lower bounds are
\[
pA(\rho)^2+5\rho+\frac{\rho^2-\rho}{p},
\tag{23}
\]
\[
pA(\rho)+\rho
+pA(\rho)^2+5\rho+\frac{\rho^2-\rho}{p}.
\tag{24}
\]
For fixed \(\rho\in(1/4,1)\), both expressions increase with \(p\). The
derivative of (23) is
\[
A(\rho)^2+\frac{\rho(1-\rho)}{p^2}>0,
\]
and (24) has the additional positive derivative \(A(\rho)\). Since
\(p>1/5\), it is enough to evaluate the limiting lower bound at \(p=1/5\).
Put
\[
X_0=\frac15\,\frac{5\rho-1}{4\rho-1}.
\]
Then
\[
X_0\rho-\frac1{10}
=
\frac{10\rho^2-6\rho+1}{10(4\rho-1)}
>0,
\tag{25}
\]
because the quadratic numerator has negative discriminant. The strict
inequality \(p>1/5\) makes (23) strictly larger than its limiting value at
\(p=1/5\), and therefore
\[
pA(\rho)^2+5\rho+\frac{\rho^2-\rho}{p}
>5(X_0^2+\rho^2)\ge10X_0\rho>1.
\tag{26}
\]
Also
\[
X_0+\rho=\frac{20\rho^2-1}{5(4\rho-1)}
\]
has its minimum on \((1/4,1)\) at
\[
\rho=\frac14+\frac{\sqrt5}{20},
\]
where its value is
\[
\frac{5+\sqrt5}{10}.
\]
Using \(X_0^2+\rho^2\ge(X_0+\rho)^2/2\), expression (24) is therefore
strictly larger than
\[
\frac{5+\sqrt5}{10}
+\frac52\left(\frac{5+\sqrt5}{10}\right)^2
=\frac54+\frac{7\sqrt5}{20}
>2.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (18), (26), and (27) contradict \(t>1\), because
\[
x+c+tR_2-(1+t)
=(x+c+R_2-2)+(t-1)(R_2-1)>0.
\]
Thus
\[
\alpha_2\ge-1.
\tag{28}
\]
Interchanging \(v_2\) with \(v_3\), and hence \(u,b,a\) with \(w,c,x\),
proves the same bound for \(\alpha_3\). This proves (10).

The triangle on the right side of (10) is the factor-four barycentric
dilation of \(P\). Indeed, if
\[
\beta_i=\frac{\alpha_i+1}{4},
\]
then \(\beta_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\beta_i=1\). Its area is \(16|P|\).
Therefore
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
\le16|P|
\le16|A_2|,
\]
which proves (1).

In a full area-one realization, accepted fact `b1eaab0ae37367d1` requires
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}>16.
\]
This contradicts (1), so the three-distinct-vertex quadrilateral incidence is
impossible.
