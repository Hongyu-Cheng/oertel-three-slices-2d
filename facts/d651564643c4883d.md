## Statement

Let
\[
v(u)=a+hu+d\min\{u,t\},
\qquad
a>0,\quad h\geq0,\quad d>0,\quad 0<t<1,
\]
and fix \(0<\lambda<1\).  Choose one of the following two left
clipping alternatives:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(L_i)}&
0<\alpha<\lambda,\qquad v(\alpha)=\lambda-\alpha,\\
\mathrm{(L_0)}&
\alpha=0,\qquad v(0)=a\geq\lambda.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
Independently, choose one of the following two right clipping
alternatives:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(R_i)}&
\lambda<\beta<1,\qquad v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda,\\
\mathrm{(R_1)}&
\beta=1,\qquad v(1)\geq1-\lambda.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,
&
R&=\int_0^\lambda v(u)\,du,\\
X&=\int_0^\alpha v(u)\,du,
&
T&=\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du,\\
C&=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-(\lambda-\alpha)^2}{2},
&
J&=-X+C+T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
For each of the four endpoint combinations from (1)--(2), assume one
of the two extreme weak kink orders
\[
t\leq\alpha
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\beta\leq t.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
I+9J<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5J+8R-3I>0.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, the required weak conclusion holds in all eight cells.

## Proof

We first prove a full-right lemma.  Assume \(\beta=1\), and suppose
either \(\alpha=0\), or \(0<\alpha<\lambda\) and \(v\) is affine on
\([\alpha,1]\).  We prove (5) under these hypotheses.

First take \(\alpha=0\).  Then \(X=T=0\), and
\[
J=C=\frac{(1-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2}{2}
=\frac12-\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Put \(\delta=\lambda-\tfrac12\).  The premise in (5) becomes
\[
I<9\delta.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(I>0\), it follows that \(\delta>0\).

Write \(w=v-a\).  This function is nonnegative and concave, with
\(w(0)=0\).  Hence, for \(0\leq x\leq1\),
\[
w(\lambda x)\geq\lambda w(x).
\]
Using \(a\geq\lambda\) from \(\mathrm{(L_0)}\), a change of variables
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R
&=a\lambda+\lambda\int_0^1w(\lambda x)\,dx\\
&\geq a\lambda+\lambda^2\int_0^1w(x)\,dx\\
&=\lambda^2I+a\lambda(1-\lambda)\\
&\geq\lambda^2I+\lambda^2(1-\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
K\geq(8\lambda^2-3)I
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(8\lambda^2-3\geq0\), then \(I\geq a\geq\lambda\), so
\[
K\geq8\lambda^2-8\lambda+\frac52
=8\left(\lambda-\frac12\right)^2+\frac12>0.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(8\lambda^2-3<0\), (7) reverses after multiplication by this
negative coefficient, and (9) yields
\[
K>4(16\lambda^3-7\lambda^2-8\lambda+4).
\tag{11}
\]
For \(y=2\lambda-1\in(0,1)\), the cubic on the right is
\[
2y^3+\frac{17}{4}y^2-\frac32y+\frac14.
\]
The quadratic part has leading coefficient \(17/4>0\) and
discriminant
\[
\left(-\frac32\right)^2
{}-4\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)\left(\frac14\right)
=-2<0.
\]
It is strictly positive, as is \(2y^3\).  This proves the full-right
lemma when \(\alpha=0\).

Now suppose \(0<\alpha<\lambda\), and assume that \(v\) is affine on
\([\alpha,1]\).  Put
\[
x=\alpha,\qquad
\ell=\lambda-\alpha,\qquad
b=1-\lambda,\qquad
L=b+\ell=1-x.
\tag{12}
\]
The left clipping equality says \(v(x)=\ell\).  Let \(s\geq0\) be the
slope of \(v\) on \([x,1]\).  Thus
\[
v(x+r)=\ell+sr\qquad(0\leq r\leq L).
\tag{13}
\]
Every slope before \(x\) is at least \(s\), by concavity.  Therefore
\[
v(u)\leq\ell-s(x-u)\qquad(0\leq u\leq x),
\]
and hence
\[
X\leq x\ell-\frac{s x^2}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]
For the one-kink width this is also immediate by integration: in the
order \(t\leq x\), the difference between the right side of (14) and
\(X\) is \(dt^2/2\); when the width is affine on all of \([0,1]\),
equality holds.

Since \(\beta=1\), formulas (12)--(13) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
I&=X+\ell L+\frac{sL^2}{2},\\
R&=X+\ell^2+\frac{s\ell^2}{2},\\
J&=-X+\frac{b^2-\ell^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Using (14) in \(A=I+9J\) gives
\[
A\geq A_0+A_s s,
\tag{16}
\]
where
\[
A_0=\frac{9L^2-16\ell}{2},
\qquad
A_s=\frac{L^2+8x^2}{2}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
In contrast, \(X\) cancels exactly from \(K\), and (15) gives
\[
K=K_0+K_s s,
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
K_0=\frac{5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2}{2}>0,
\qquad
K_s=\frac{8\ell^2-3L^2}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict sign in (19) follows from
\[
5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2
=(b+\ell)^2+4(b-\ell)^2>0.
\]

If \(A<0\), (16) implies \(A_0+A_s s<0\), so in particular
\(A_0<0\).  If \(K_s\geq0\), (18)--(19) give \(K>0\).  Suppose
instead that \(K_s<0\).  From (16),
\[
s<\frac{-A_0}{A_s},
\]
so
\[
K>
\frac{N}{A_s},
\qquad
N:=K_0A_s-K_sA_0.
\tag{20}
\]
It remains to check \(N\geq0\).

Set
\[
z=\frac{\ell}{L},\qquad
\varepsilon=16z-9L.
\tag{21}
\]
Here \(0<z<1\), and \(A_0<0\) is exactly
\(\varepsilon>0\).  Direct expansion of (17), (19), and (20), followed
by \(L=(16z-\varepsilon)/9\), gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{9N}{2L^2}
={}&\big((1-z)\varepsilon+5-24z+24z^2\big)^2\\
&+4(2z-1)^2(5+4z-4z^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative, because
\[
5+4z-4z^2=5+4z(1-z)>0.
\]
Thus \(N\geq0\), and the strict inequality in (20) gives \(K>0\).
This completes the full-right lemma.

We now reduce every interior-right cell to that lemma.  Suppose
\(\lambda<\beta<1\), and put
\[
I_c=I-T,\qquad J_c=J-T=-X+C.
\]
The integral \(R\) is unchanged by truncation at \(\beta\), and
\[
A=(I_c+9J_c)+10T,
\qquad
K=(5J_c+8R-3I_c)+2T.
\tag{23}
\]
Define
\[
\widehat v(u)=\frac{v(\beta u)}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\alpha=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat t=\frac{t}{\beta}.
\tag{24}
\]
The right clipping equality becomes
\[
\widehat v(1)=1-\widehat\lambda.
\]
The left clipping status is preserved exactly: if \(\alpha>0\), then
\[
\widehat v(\widehat\alpha)
=\widehat\lambda-\widehat\alpha,
\]
while, if \(\alpha=0\), then
\[
\widehat v(0)=\frac a\beta
\geq\frac\lambda\beta=\widehat\lambda.
\]
All area quantities scale by \(\beta^{-2}\), so
\[
\widehat A=\frac{I_c+9J_c}{\beta^2},
\qquad
\widehat K=\frac{5J_c+8R-3I_c}{\beta^2}.
\tag{25}
\]
If \(A<0\), then \(I_c+9J_c=A-10T<0\).

If \(t\leq\alpha\), then
\(\widehat t\leq\widehat\alpha\), and \(\widehat v\) is affine from
\(\widehat\alpha\) to \(1\).  If \(\beta\leq t\), then
\(\widehat t\geq1\), and \(\widehat v\) is affine on all of
\([0,1]\).  Thus, according as the left clipping point is interior or
zero, the corresponding part of the full-right lemma applies and
gives \(\widehat K>0\).  Equation (23) then gives \(K>0\).

For completeness, the nominal endpoint combinations which cannot
occur under \(0<t<1\) are also exact: \(t\leq\alpha=0\) is empty, and
\(\beta=1\leq t\) is empty.  Their formal weak boundaries \(t=0\) and
\(t=1\) satisfy the same proof, since the resulting width is affine
on the needed interval.  The genuine weak boundaries \(t=\alpha>0\)
and \(t=\beta<1\) are included directly above; in the latter case
\(\widehat t=1\).  Equality in either endpoint clipping condition is
also preserved by (24), and \(\beta=1\) is the case \(T=0\) in (23).
Hence every weak kink and clipping boundary among the eight cells is
covered.
