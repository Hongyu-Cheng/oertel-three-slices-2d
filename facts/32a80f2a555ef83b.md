## Statement

\[

The map in (3) has coordinate-area factor \(\Delta=1\).  The cell

\[
|B|_{\rm coord}=\Delta c=3,

For \(A_2\), put

\tag{9}
\]
Accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1` gives \(D\subseteq\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\).
Writing its two a priori arbitrary axis offsets explicitly, every
putative realization has

\[

\tag{11}

for some \(u_0,v_0\ge0\), and

Compatibility would further require \(r,s>0\) such that

\le

No common-plane polygons \(A_0,A_2\) satisfy (1)-(13), for any
\[
u_0,v_0\ge0,\qquad r,s>0.

Indeed, the following general one-sided endpoint-cap inequality rules
out \(A_0\) alone.

Let \(C\) be a two-dimensional compact convex polygon in affine
coordinates \((T,R)\), with coordinate area equal to \(\Delta>0\)
times physical area.  Suppose
\[
C\cap\{T=0\}
=\{(0,R):\gamma\le R\le\gamma+K\},
\tag{15}

where \(\gamma,K>0\), and suppose \((\tau,0)\in C\) for some
\(\tau>0\).  Let the physical area of \(C\cap\{T\le0\}\) be
\(d_->0\).  If
\[
h:=\frac{\gamma}{\tau}
-\frac{K^2}{2\Delta d_-}>0,
\tag{16}
\]
then
\[
\left|C\cap\{T\ge0,R\ge0\}\right|_{\rm coord}
\le
\frac{(\gamma+K)^2}{2h}.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently, if a cell of physical area \(c_+\) lies in this quadrant,
then necessarily
\[
2\Delta c_+
\left(
\frac{\gamma}{\tau}
-\frac{K^2}{2\Delta d_-}
\right)
\le(\gamma+K)^2
\qquad(h>0).
\tag{18}
\]

For (1)-(8),

This contradiction is independent of \(u_0,v_0,r,s\), so it includes

## Proof

We first prove the one-sided endpoint-cap inequality.  For every
\(T\) in the projection of \(C\), write its full \(R\)-section as
\[
[\ell(T),u(T)]
\]
and put
\[
W(T)=u(T)-\ell(T).
\tag{30}
\]
For a convex polygon, \(\ell\) is convex, \(u\) is concave, and
\(W\) is nonnegative, concave, and piecewise affine.  From (15),
\[
\ell(0)=\gamma,\qquad
u(0)=\gamma+K,\qquad
W(0)=K.
\tag{31}
\]
Positive cap area and the point \((\tau,0)\) place \(0\) in the
interior of the \(T\)-projection, so all right derivatives used below
exist.

The segment joining \((0,\gamma)\) to \((\tau,0)\) lies in \(C\).
Therefore, for \(0\le T\le\tau\),
\[
\ell(T)
\le
\gamma\left(1-\frac{T}{\tau}\right).
\]
Equality holds at \(T=0\), hence
\[
\ell'_+(0)\le-\frac{\gamma}{\tau}.
\tag{32}
\]

We next use the entire negative-side cap.  If \(W'_+(0)\le0\), then
\[
W'_+(0)\le\frac{K^2}{2\Delta d_-}
\tag{33}
\]
is automatic.  Suppose \(W'_+(0)>0\).  Since the right derivative of
a concave function is a supergradient, for every \(T\le0\) in the
support,
\[
W(T)\le K+W'_+(0)T.
\tag{34}
\]
Nonnegativity prevents the negative-side support from extending below
\(-K/W'_+(0)\).  Thus
\[
\Delta d_-
=\int_{T\le0}W(T)\,dT
\le
\int_{-K/W'_+(0)}^0
\bigl(K+W'_+(0)T\bigr)\,dT
=\frac{K^2}{2W'_+(0)}.
\tag{35}
\]
Rearrangement proves (33) in this case as well.

Because \(u=\ell+W\), equations (32)-(33) give
\[
u'_+(0)
\le
-\frac{\gamma}{\tau}
+\frac{K^2}{2\Delta d_-}
=-h.
\tag{36}
\]
Concavity of \(u\) now gives, for every \(T\ge0\) in the projection,
\[
u(T)\le u(0)+u'_+(0)T
\le\gamma+K-hT.
\tag{37}
\]
At level \(T\), the length of the part of the section with \(R\ge0\)
is at most \((u(T))_+\).  Since \(h>0\), integration of (37) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|C\cap\{T\ge0,R\ge0\}\right|_{\rm coord}
&\le
\int_0^\infty(\gamma+K-hT)_+\,dT\\
&=\frac{(\gamma+K)^2}{2h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
This proves (17), and multiplication by \(2h\) proves (18).

Apply the lemma to \(C=A_0\).  Equations (4)-(5) supply (15) and the
point \((\tau,0)=(3/2,0)\).  Exact support and (2) give the whole cap
identity (8), not merely the area of a separately chosen triangle.
The cell \(B\) is contained in
\[
A_0\cap\{T\ge0,R\ge0\},
\]
so (17) applies to its full coordinate area.  Substitution gives
(20)-(22).  Hence the required \(A_0\) does not exist.  It follows
before any use of \(A_2\) that no common-plane pair can exist, for any
of the offsets in (10)-(11) or coefficients in (13).

It remains only to verify that the displayed separate cells really do
pass the lower-level checkpoint.  The consecutive oriented edge
cross-products for the vertex order in (23) are
\[
\frac3{26},\quad
\frac52,\quad
\frac{105}{26},\quad
\frac1{20},\quad
\frac3{20},\quad
\frac4{25},
\tag{39}
\]
all positive.  For (24), they are
\[
\frac{55}{129},\quad
\frac35,\quad
\frac{44}{129},\quad
\frac35,\quad
\frac34,
\tag{40}
\]
also all positive.  Thus both displayed vertex lists are strictly
convex and counterclockwise.

The doubled shoelace area of \(B_*\) is
\[
\left(
\frac{51}{52}
+\frac{315}{52}
+\frac9{20}
\right)
-
\left(
\frac{15}{52}
+\frac65
\right)
=6,
\tag{41}
\]
so \(|B_*|=3\).  For \(D_*\), the doubled area is
\[
\frac{715}{129}+\frac{704}{129}-9
=11-9=2,
\tag{42}
\]
so \(|D_*|=1\).  Every non-axis vertex has both coordinates positive,
which proves the exact axis intersections (26)-(27).

The sums \(T+R\) at the vertices of \(B_*\) are
\[
\frac32,\quad\frac{17}{10},\quad3,\quad3,
\quad\frac9{10},\quad\frac45,
\]
so \(B_*\subseteq\{T+R\le3\}\) and equality is attained.  The sums
\(U+V\) at the vertices of \(D_*\) are
\[
3,\quad\frac{13}{4},\quad\frac{440}{129},
\quad\frac{16}{5},\quad3,
\]
so \(D_*\subseteq\{U+V\ge3\}\) and equality is attained.  This proves
(29), completes the all-offset impossibility, and isolates (18) as the
missing common-polygon constraint.
