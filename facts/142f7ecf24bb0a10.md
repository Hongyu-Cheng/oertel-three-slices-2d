## Statement

Let
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\]
be as in the locked problem, let \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and put
\[
q=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Suppose six closed halfspaces \(H_1,\ldots,H_6\) cover \(S\) and satisfy
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_j)<q\qquad(1\leq j\leq6). \tag{1}
\]
There are arbitrarily small outward parallel enlargements
\(\widetilde H_j\supset H_j\), still satisfying (1), for which one of the
following alternatives holds.

1. At most five of the enlarged halfspaces cover \(S\).  If exactly five
   are needed, this is an instance of the five-cap subgoal.  If at most
   four are needed, the union bound is already a contradiction.

2. After relabeling the caps and, if necessary, replacing the height
   \(h\) by \(2-h\), there are affine functions
   \[
   g_j(h,z)=u_j\mathbin{\cdot}z+\alpha_jh+\beta_j
   \qquad(1\leq j\leq6)
   \]
   such that
   \[
   \widetilde H_j=\{(h,z):g_j(h,z)\leq0\}, \tag{2}
   \]
   and the following conclusions all hold.

   Put \(L=\{1,2,3\}\) and \(U=\{4,5,6\}\).  There are positive
   \(\lambda_j\), \(j\in L\), positive \(\eta_k\), \(k\in U\), and levels
   \(1<\tau_-\leq\tau_+<2\) such that, identically in \((h,z)\),
   \[
   \sum_{j\in L}\lambda_jg_j(h,z)=h-\tau_-,
   \qquad
   \sum_{k\in U}\eta_kg_k(h,z)=\tau_+-h. \tag{3}
   \]
   The three open sections of the lower triple cover the whole \(z\)-plane
   exactly when \(h<\tau_-\), and its three closed sections cover the
   whole plane exactly when \(h\leq\tau_-\).  The upper triple has the
   reflected rule: its open sections cover exactly when \(h>\tau_+\), and
   its closed sections cover exactly when \(h\geq\tau_+\).  In particular,
   \(L\) covers the complete planes at heights \(0,1\), while \(U\) covers
   the complete plane at height \(2\).

   The complementary polyhedron
   \[
   P=\bigcap_{j=1}^6\{(h,z):g_j(h,z)\geq0\} \tag{4}
   \]
   is nonempty and satisfies
   \[
   P\subseteq\{(h,z):\tau_-\leq h\leq\tau_+\}
   \subseteq\{(h,z):1<h<2\}. \tag{5}
   \]
   Every inequality in (4) is indispensable.  More precisely, for each
   \(j\) there is a private point \((i_j,w_j)\in S\) such that
   \[
   g_j(i_j,w_j)<0,\qquad
   g_\ell(i_j,w_j)>0\quad(\ell\ne j). \tag{6}
   \]
   For \(k\in U\), necessarily \(i_k=2\).  For \(j\in L\), necessarily
   \(i_j\in\{0,1\}\).

   Define the actual cap sections and their areas by
   \[
   C_{ij}=A_i\cap\{z:g_j(i,z)\leq0\},
   \qquad c_{ij}=|C_{ij}|. \tag{7}
   \]
   Define also
   \[
   E_0=\sum_{j\in L}c_{0j}-a_0,\qquad
   E_1=\sum_{j\in L}c_{1j}-a_1,\qquad
   C_2=\sum_{j\in L}c_{2j}. \tag{8}
   \]
   Then \(E_0,E_1\geq0\), and the strict lower-triple budget is
   \[
   E_0+E_1+C_2<a_2-\frac T3. \tag{9}
   \]
   Consequently
   \[
   R_2:=
   A_2\cap\bigcap_{j\in L}\{z:g_j(2,z)>0\} \tag{10}
   \]
   is the intersection of \(A_2\) with an open triangle and satisfies
   \[
   |R_2|>\frac T3. \tag{11}
   \]
   Each of the three private points \(w_k\), \(k\in U\), belongs to
   \(R_2\), lies in the \(k\)-th upper cap, and lies strictly outside the
   other five caps.

   Finally, for every \(z\in R_2\), midpoint compatibility gives the
   strict inward-translate cover
   \[
   A_0\subseteq
   \bigcup_{j\in L}
   \left\{
   x:g_j(0,x)<-g_j(2,z)
   \right\}. \tag{12}
   \]
   Thus, if
   \[
   F_j(t)=
   \left|
   A_0\cap\{x:g_j(0,x)\leq t\}
   \right|
   \qquad(j\in L), \tag{13}
   \]
   then
   \[
   \sum_{j\in L}F_j(-g_j(2,z))\geq a_0
   \qquad(z\in R_2), \tag{14}
   \]
   and the exact surviving shifted-cap inequality is
   \[
   \sup_{z\in R_2}
   \sum_{j\in L}
   \left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
   +E_1+C_2
   <a_2-\frac T3. \tag{15}
   \]
   The upper triple retains its own actual leakage budget:
   \[
   \sum_{k\in U}(c_{0k}+c_{1k})
   +\left(\sum_{k\in U}c_{2k}-a_2\right)
   <\frac{2T}{3}-a_2. \tag{16}
   \]

Therefore, after the five-cap case is handled, it is enough to exclude the
two-triple slab geometry (2)-(16).  In particular, it is enough to prove
the reverse weak inequality to (15) under (2)-(14), (16), and the three
upper private-sector conditions (6).

## Proof

Write a proper closed halfspace as
\[
H_j=\{(h,z):\ell_j(h,z)\leq0\}.
\]
For \(\varepsilon>0\), its outward parallel enlargement is
\[
H_j^\varepsilon=\{(h,z):\ell_j(h,z)\leq\varepsilon\}.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), these sets decrease to \(H_j\).  The measure
on \(S\) is finite, so continuity from above gives
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_j^\varepsilon)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_j)<q. \tag{17}
\]
For a horizontal halfspace, choose \(\varepsilon\) smaller than the
distance to every support plane not already contained in \(H_j\).  Then no
new slice is crossed.  Hence all six halfspaces may be enlarged so little
that their masses remain below \(q\).  Every point of the original closed
cover now lies in the interior of at least one enlarged halfspace, so
\[
S\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^6\operatorname{int}\widetilde H_j. \tag{18}
\]

If some five enlarged closed halfspaces cover \(S\), alternative 1 holds.
Assume henceforth that no subfamily of at most five does so.  Set
\[
F_j=\mathbb R^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\widetilde H_j.
\]
These are closed halfspaces.  By (18),
\[
\left(C\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^6F_j\right)
\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)=\varnothing. \tag{19}
\]
Accepted fact `9b0c3eb7dcfba2c2` records that the Helly number of
\(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\) is six.  Apply it to the seven convex sets
\[
C,F_1,\ldots,F_6.
\]
If a mixed-integer empty subfamily of size at most six contained \(C\), at
most five of the open halfspaces
\(\operatorname{int}\widetilde H_j\) would cover \(S\).  If it omitted
\(C\) but used at most five of the \(F_j\), the corresponding open
halfspaces would cover all of \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), and hence
would again cover \(S\).  Both conclusions contradict the present branch.
The only possible Helly subfamily is therefore
\[
\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^6F_j\right)
\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)=\varnothing. \tag{20}
\]
Thus the six open halfspaces cover every integer-height plane, not only
the three bodies.

The ordinary intersection
\[
P=\bigcap_{j=1}^6F_j
\]
is nonempty.  Otherwise ordinary Helly in \(\mathbb R^3\) would give at
most four of the \(F_j\) with empty intersection.  Their complementary
open halfspaces would cover \(\mathbb R^3\), hence \(S\), whereas four
halfspaces of mass below \(q\) have total mass below
\(4q=8T/9<T\).

The projection of the nonempty convex set \(P\) onto the height axis is an
interval.  By (20) it contains no integer.  Hence it is contained in one
open unit interval.  The argument below identifies that interval exactly.

Choose affine functions \(g_j\) so that (2) holds and
\[
F_j=\{(h,z):g_j(h,z)\geq0\}.
\]
Fix an integer \(n\).  Equation (20) says that the six planar convex sets
\[
\{z:g_j(n,z)\geq0\},\qquad 1\leq j\leq6,
\]
have empty intersection.  Planar Helly gives an empty subfamily of at most
three.  Choose one which is inclusion-minimal and call its index set
\(J_n\).

We use the following elementary affine certificate.  If \(J\) is such a
minimal infeasible family at height \(n\), Farkas' alternative gives
numbers \(\rho_j>0\), \(j\in J\), with
\[
\sum_{j\in J}\rho_ju_j=0,\qquad
\Gamma_J(n):=
\sum_{j\in J}\rho_j(\alpha_jn+\beta_j)<0. \tag{21}
\]
Every coefficient is positive, since a zero coefficient would certify
infeasibility of a proper subfamily.  In particular,
\(\Gamma_J(h)\) is affine in \(h\), and
\[
\Gamma_J(h)<0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigcap_{j\in J}\{z:g_j(h,z)\geq0\}=\varnothing. \tag{22}
\]
Indeed, a common point would make every summand in the weighted sum
nonnegative.

Apply this to \(J_1\).  Since
\[
\Gamma_{J_1}(1)
=\frac{\Gamma_{J_1}(0)+\Gamma_{J_1}(2)}2<0,
\]
one of \(\Gamma_{J_1}(0),\Gamma_{J_1}(2)\) is negative.  Let \(r\) be that
endpoint and let \(s\) be the other endpoint.  By (22), the open caps
indexed by \(J_1\) cover the complete planes at heights \(1,r\).  The open
caps indexed by \(J_s\) cover the remaining plane.  Therefore
\[
\{\widetilde H_j:j\in J_1\cup J_s\}
\]
covers \(S\).  If the union has at most five indices, this is alternative
1, with the cases of at most four already impossible by the union bound.
In the surviving branch it must have six indices.  Since
\[
|J_1|\leq3,\qquad |J_s|\leq3,
\]
both sets have size three and they are disjoint.

Reflect the heights if necessary so that \(r=0\), \(s=2\), and relabel the
two triples as
\[
L=J_1=\{1,2,3\},\qquad
U=J_2=\{4,5,6\}. \tag{23}
\]
Each is a minimal infeasible triple.  Its three planar normals span
\(\mathbb R^2\): otherwise its feasibility problem would factor through a
line, where a minimal infeasible family has size at most two.  Consequently
the positive dependence in (21) is unique up to scaling.

For such a triple, the converse to (22) is exact.  The affine map
\[
z\longmapsto(g_j(h,z))_{j\in J}
\]
has as its image the full affine plane
\[
\left\{y\in\mathbb R^3:
\sum_{j\in J}\rho_jy_j=\Gamma_J(h)\right\}. \tag{24}
\]
If \(\Gamma_J(h)>0\), this plane contains the strictly positive vector
whose three coordinates equal
\(\Gamma_J(h)/\sum_j\rho_j\).  Thus the closed complementary sections have
a common point lying outside all three closed caps.  If
\(\Gamma_J(h)=0\), (24) contains the zero vector, while positive weights
exclude a vector with all coordinates strictly positive.  It follows that
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{open caps cover the plane}&\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma_J(h)<0,\\
\text{closed caps cover the plane}&\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma_J(h)\leq0.
\end{array} \tag{25}
\]

For the lower triple, \(\Gamma_L(0),\Gamma_L(1)<0\).  One must have
\(\Gamma_L(2)>0\).  If it were negative, the three open caps would cover
all three slices.  If it were zero, their closed sections would still
cover the plane at height \(2\).  Either way the three closed halfspaces
would cover \(S\), giving
\[
T\leq\sum_{j\in L}\mathcal H_2(S\cap\widetilde H_j)
<3q=\frac{2T}{3},
\]
a contradiction.  After positive rescaling of the Farkas weights,
\[
\Gamma_L(h)=h-\tau_-
\quad\text{for some }1<\tau_-<2. \tag{26}
\]

For the upper triple, \(\Gamma_U(2)<0\).  Similarly
\(\Gamma_U(0)>0\); if it were nonpositive, (25) and affine interpolation
would make the three closed upper caps cover all three slices.  Positive
rescaling gives
\[
\Gamma_U(h)=\tau_+-h
\quad\text{for some }0<\tau_+<2. \tag{27}
\]
Equations (26)-(27) prove (3) and all the plane-cover assertions.

Every point of \(P\) satisfies the three lower and the three upper
complementary inequalities.  By (24)-(27), its height must satisfy
\[
h\geq\tau_-,\qquad h\leq\tau_+.
\]
Since \(P\ne\varnothing\), this proves
\[
\tau_-\leq\tau_+.
\]
Together with \(1<\tau_-<2\), it improves (27) to
\[
1<\tau_-\leq\tau_+<2
\]
and proves (5).  This also identifies the unit slab containing \(P\).

It remains to retain global minimality.  For each \(j\), the other five
closed enlarged halfspaces do not cover \(S\).  Choose a point of \(S\)
outside all five.  By (18), it belongs to the interior of the remaining
halfspace.  This proves (6).  The lower triple covers the complete planes
at heights \(0,1\), so a private point for an upper cap cannot lie on
either plane.  It therefore lies in \(A_2\).  The upper triple covers the
complete plane at height \(2\), so a private point for a lower cap lies in
\(A_0\) or \(A_1\).  This proves the claimed witness incidence with actual
sections.

The area assertions now follow without replacing the sections by abstract
weights.  Since the lower triple covers the complete planes at heights
\(0,1\),
\[
\sum_{j\in L}c_{0j}=a_0+E_0,\qquad
\sum_{j\in L}c_{1j}=a_1+E_1,
\qquad E_0,E_1\geq0. \tag{28}
\]
Summing the three strict mass bounds for \(j\in L\) gives
\[
a_0+a_1+E_0+E_1+C_2
<\frac{2T}{3}. \tag{29}
\]
Since \(a_0+a_1=T-a_2\), (29) is exactly (9).  In particular
\[
C_2<a_2-\frac T3.
\]
The three lower caps miss precisely the open triangular region
\[
\bigcap_{j\in L}\{z:g_j(2,z)>0\};
\]
it is a triangle because the lower normals positively span the plane and
\(\Gamma_L(2)>0\).  Therefore
\[
|R_2|
=a_2-\left|A_2\cap\bigcup_{j\in L}\{g_j(2,\cdot)\leq0\}\right|
\geq a_2-C_2
>\frac T3,
\]
which proves (10)-(11).  The private-sign relations (6) put all three upper
private points in the stated sectors of \(R_2\).

For the midpoint step, convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1. \tag{30}
\]
Fix \(x\in A_0\) and \(z\in R_2\).  Then
\((x+z)/2\in A_1\).  The lower interiors cover the whole middle plane, so
for some \(j\in L\),
\[
g_j\left(1,\frac{x+z}{2}\right)<0.
\]
Affineness gives the exact midpoint identity
\[
2g_j\left(1,\frac{x+z}{2}\right)
=g_j(0,x)+g_j(2,z). \tag{31}
\]
Because \(z\in R_2\) makes \(g_j(2,z)>0\), (31) implies
\[
g_j(0,x)<-g_j(2,z)<0.
\]
This proves the actual inward-translate cover (12), hence (14).

For \(z\in R_2\), monotonicity of the cap profiles and (14) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
&\leq
\sum_{j\in L}F_j(0)-a_0\\
&=E_0.
\end{aligned} \tag{32}
\]
Take the supremum in (32) and combine it with (9) to obtain (15).

Finally, the upper triple covers \(A_2\), so
\(\sum_{k\in U}c_{2k}-a_2\geq0\).  Summing its three strict mass bounds
and subtracting \(a_2\) gives (16).  Every statement in the normal form
has now been proved.

\sup_{z\in R_2}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
+E_1+C_2
\geq a_2-\frac T3 \tag{33}
\]
for the two positively spanning affine triples (3), subject to
\(|R_2|>T/3\), the upper leakage budget (16), the three upper private
sectors in \(R_2\), and midpoint containment (30).  Inequality (33)
directly contradicts (15).  Unlike a free incidence table or a scalar mass
allocation, (33) retains the three actual cap profiles, their affine
height coupling, the large opposite triangular region, and all three
private witnesses.
