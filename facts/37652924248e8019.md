## Statement

Normalize \(|\Delta|=1\).  For a row vector \(h\in\mathbb R^3\) and a threshold \(z\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
\mathcal C(h;z)
=
\frac{\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:h\lambda\geq z\}\right|}{|\Delta|}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
p_0=\frac{3743567}{7758225},
\tag{2}
\]
the fixed level in the two-cap theorem of accepted fact
`b12dbfdde261e9de`.

The following general endpoint criterion holds.  Let
\(L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) have column sums one and three rows that
are nonconstant on \(\Delta\).  Let \(A,B>0\), let \(t\in\mathbb R\),
and put
\[
c_i=\mathcal C(L_{i*};0),
\qquad
\alpha_i=\frac{t-c_i}{A}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
If, after a row permutation,
\[
0<\alpha_1<p_0,\qquad
0<\alpha_2<p_0,\qquad
0<\alpha_3<\frac14,
\tag{4}
\]
then there are no matrices \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and
\(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfying
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\geq0
\qquad(k=1,2,3),
\tag{6}
\]
\[
Q:=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3>1,
\tag{7}
\]
and all three strict rows
\[
c_i+
A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{8}
\]
The criterion uses no determinant sign or cap-order assumption.

Apply this criterion to the exact survivor recorded in accepted fact
`86e9bf31e8f5df7a`:
\[
A=\frac{31}{250},\qquad
B=\frac{1123}{1000},\qquad
t=\frac{749}{1500},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-69&275/2&-69\\
-50&-81/2&90\\
120&-96&-20
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
There do not exist real, and hence there do not exist rational,
\(U,V,\sigma\) satisfying (5)-(6) and the three strict rows (8), for
any value of \(Q=\sum_i\sigma_i\).  Thus this fixed middle calibration
has a complete endpoint obstruction.

## Proof

We first prove the general \(Q>1\) criterion.  Suppose (5)-(8) hold.
For each row put
\[
\ell_k=\min_jU_{kj},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s=\mathbf1^\top\ell,\qquad
\rho=1-s.
\tag{11}
\]
Every column of \(U-\ell\mathbf1^\top\) is entrywise nonnegative and
has coordinate sum \(\rho\).  Since \(\det U\ne0\), equality \(s=1\)
is impossible, so \(\rho>0\).  Define
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\rho}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then \(X\) is entrywise nonnegative and column-stochastic, and
\[
U=(\rho I+\ell\mathbf1^\top)X.
\]
The matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det U=\rho^2\det X.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(X\) is invertible.  Put
\[
z_i(\lambda)=\rho(LX)_{i*}\lambda
\qquad(\lambda\in\Delta,\ i=1,2,3).
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(z_i\) is nonconstant.  Indeed, if
\((LX)_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top\), then
\(\mathbf1^\top X^{-1}=\mathbf1^\top\) gives
\[
L_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top X^{-1}=a\mathbf1^\top,
\]
contrary to the hypothesis on the rows of \(L\).  The column-sum
identities for \(L\) and \(X\) also give the pointwise identity
\[
z_1+z_2+z_3=\rho.
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(\theta_1,\theta_2\) be the unique upper-cap thresholds at level
\(p_0\), and let \(\theta_3\) be the unique upper-cap threshold at
level \(\alpha_3\):
\[
\mathcal C(z_1;\theta_1)
=\mathcal C(z_2;\theta_2)=p_0,
\qquad
\mathcal C(z_3;\theta_3)=\alpha_3.
\tag{16}
\]
These thresholds exist and are unique because the \(z_i\) are
nonconstant and the three target fractions lie strictly between zero
and one.

Apply the fixed-level theorem of accepted fact
`b12dbfdde261e9de` to
\[
f=z_1-\theta_1,\qquad g=z_2-\theta_2.
\]
It gives
\[
\frac{\left|\{z_1+z_2\leq\theta_1+\theta_2\}\right|}{|\Delta|}
\geq\frac14.
\tag{17}
\]
By (15), the set in (17) is
\[
\{z_3\geq\rho-\theta_1-\theta_2\}.
\]
Its area is strictly greater than \(\alpha_3\) by (4).  The cap
function of the nonconstant affine function \(z_3\) is strictly
decreasing between its support values, so (16) implies
\[
\theta_3>\rho-\theta_1-\theta_2,
\qquad
\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3>\rho.
\tag{18}
\]

Now put
\[
d=L\ell+\sigma.
\tag{19}
\]
Equation (12) gives, row by row,
\[
\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
=\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i).
\tag{20}
\]
The large-end cap in (8) is nonnegative, hence
\[
\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i)<\alpha_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{21}
\]
For \(i=1,2\), equations (4), (16), and monotonicity give
\[
\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i)<\alpha_i<p_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-d_i>\theta_i.
\]
For \(i=3\), equations (16) and (21) give the same conclusion.
Therefore
\[
d_i<-\theta_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{22}
\]
Summing (22) and using (18) yields
\[
\sum_i d_i<-\sum_i\theta_i<-\rho=s-1.
\tag{23}
\]
On the other hand, the column sums of \(L\), (7), and (19) give
\[
\sum_i d_i
=\mathbf1^\top L\ell+\sum_i\sigma_i
=s+Q>s+1,
\tag{24}
\]
contradicting (23).  This proves the general criterion.  Notice that
the proof did not use \(Y\), the sign of either determinant, or any
ordering of any endpoint row.

We now audit the fixed data.  Accepted fact `86e9bf31e8f5df7a`
gives
\[
c_1=\frac{75625}{170569},\qquad
c_2=\frac{90}{203},\qquad
c_3=\frac{10}{21}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substitution into (3) gives
\[
\alpha_1=\frac{14318681}{31725834},\qquad
\alpha_2=\frac{17047}{37758},\qquad
\alpha_3=\frac{81}{434}.
\tag{26}
\]
The exact margins required in (4) are
\[
p_0-\alpha_1
=\frac{2560078769551}{82045386161550}>0,
\tag{27}
\]
\[
p_0-\alpha_2
=\frac{104541853}{3367069650}>0,
\qquad
\frac14-\alpha_3=\frac{55}{868}>0.
\tag{28}
\]
All three rows in (10) are nonconstant, and its columns sum to one.
Thus the criterion excludes every \(Q>1\) completion.

It remains only to cover the other allocation sums.  Accepted fact
`09393c6edf1fc4e2` excludes all three strict rows whenever
\[
-1\leq Q\leq1.
\]
For \(Q<-1\), the same accepted fact excludes all three strict rows
when \(1+B\geq2A\).  Here the exact margin is
\[
1+B-2A=\frac{15}{8}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently no real completion exists for any \(Q\).  This proves the
fixed endpoint obstruction.
