## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,L\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq L.
\]
Let \(|\cdot|\) denote planar area. For every planar convex body \(K\) and \(x\in K\), define
\[
d_K(x)=\inf\bigl\{|K\cap Q|:Q\subset\mathbb R^2
\text{ is a closed planar halfplane containing }x\bigr\}.
\]
For \(i\in\{0,2\}\), put
\[
D_i=\max_{x\in A_i}d_{A_i}(x),
\qquad
D=\max\{D_0,D_2\},
\qquad
E=\min\{D_0,D_2\}.
\]
Set
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times L)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
and, for \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\bigl\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):H\subset\mathbb R^3
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\bigr\}.
\]
The two maxima defining \(D_0,D_2\), as well as the maximum below, are attained, and
\[
\max_{z\in L}h_S((1,z))
\ge E+\frac{(\sqrt D+\sqrt E)^2}{4}.
\]
Consequently, if \(E\ge 9D/25\), then
\[
\max_{z\in L}h_S((1,z))\ge D.
\]

## Proof

We first verify that the planar-depth maxima are genuine. For a fixed planar convex body \(K\), define
\[
F_K(x,u)=\left|K\cap
\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,x\rangle\}\right|
\qquad (x\in K,\ u\in\mathbb S^1).
\]
Moving the boundary of any halfplane containing \(x\) parallel toward \(x\) can only decrease its intersection with \(K\). Since both choices of side occur as \(u\) ranges over \(\mathbb S^1\),
\[
d_K(x)=\min_{u\in\mathbb S^1}F_K(x,u).
\tag{1}
\]
To justify both the minimum and continuity, suppose \(x_n\to x\) and \(u_n\to u\). For every \(w\) outside the line
\(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle=\langle u,x\rangle\}\), the indicators of the caps defining \(F_K(x_n,u_n)\) converge to the indicator of the cap defining \(F_K(x,u)\). The exceptional line has planar area zero. Dominated convergence therefore proves that \(F_K\) is continuous on the compact set \(K\times\mathbb S^1\). It follows from (1), by uniform continuity in the compact direction variable, that \(d_K\) is continuous on \(K\). Hence \(d_K\) attains its maximum.

These maxima are positive under the stated two-dimensional hypotheses. Indeed, if \(p\in\operatorname{int}K\), choose \(\rho>0\) such that the closed Euclidean disk \(\{v:\|v-p\|\le\rho\}\) is contained in \(K\). Every closed halfplane containing \(p\) contains at least one half of this disk, so
\[
d_K(p)\ge \frac{\pi\rho^2}{2}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(D_0,D_2,D,E>0\). Choose actual maximizers \(x_i\in A_i\) such that
\[
d_{A_i}(x_i)=D_i
\qquad (i=0,2),
\tag{3}
\]
and set
\[
z=\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
The compatibility hypothesis gives \(z\in(A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq L\).

We next record the exact middle-depth formula that will be bounded. The compatibility hypothesis places this triple in the setting of accepted fact 949ce2fdf2e1e240. Explicitly, let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A_0)\cup
(\{1\}\times L)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr).
\]
The sections of \(C\) at first coordinates \(0\) and \(2\) are \(A_0\) and \(A_2\). In a convex representation of a point of \(C\) with first coordinate \(1\), the weights on the levels \(0\) and \(2\) are equal, say to \(t\in[0,1/2]\). Its planar component is therefore
\[
(1-2t)\ell+2t\frac{v_0+v_2}{2}
\qquad
(\ell\in L,\ v_i\in A_i),
\]
which lies in \(L\) because \((v_0+v_2)/2\in(A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq L\) and \(L\) is convex. Conversely, every point of \(L\) occurs at level \(1\). Thus the level-\(1\) section is exactly \(L\), and there are no other integer levels.

For a planar body \(K\), write
\[
G_K^u(c)=\left|K\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le c\}\right|.
\]
Accepted fact 949ce2fdf2e1e240, with its middle body \(A_1\) identified with \(L\), gives for every \(w\in L\)
\[
h_S((1,w))
=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_L^u(\langle u,w\rangle)
+\inf_{\substack{c_0,c_2\in\mathbb R\\
c_0+c_2=2\langle u,w\rangle}}
\bigl(G_{A_0}^u(c_0)+G_{A_2}^u(c_2)\bigr)
\right].
\tag{5}
\]
This formula includes all nonhorizontal directions and all linked outer allocations, and that accepted fact proves that the separately treated horizontal halfspaces do not lower the value. Accepted fact a49f2b26cc468e4c, applied with \(B=(A_0+A_2)/2\), \(A_1=L\), and \(s=1\), also gives that the maximum of \(h_S((1,w))\) over \(w\in L\) is attained.

Fix an arbitrary \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), and define the two caps through the chosen planar-depth maximizers by
\[
K_i^u=A_i\cap
\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le\langle u,x_i\rangle\}
\qquad(i=0,2).
\tag{6}
\]
Each halfplane in (6) contains \(x_i\), so (3) and the definition of depth give
\[
|K_i^u|\ge D_i
\qquad(i=0,2).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(w_i\in K_i^u\), then
\[
\frac{w_0+w_2}{2}\in\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq L
\]
and, by (4),
\[
\left\langle u,\frac{w_0+w_2}{2}\right\rangle
\le\frac{\langle u,x_0\rangle+\langle u,x_2\rangle}{2}
=\langle u,z\rangle.
\]
Hence the required midpoint-cap inclusion is
\[
\frac{K_0^u+K_2^u}{2}
\subseteq
L\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le\langle u,z\rangle\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The sets \(K_0^u,K_2^u\) are nonempty compact convex sets, and (7) makes their areas positive. The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality
\[
|P+Q|^{1/2}\ge |P|^{1/2}+|Q|^{1/2}
\]
therefore applies to them. Using (8), scaling of planar area, (7), and the symmetry of \(D,E\) in \(D_0,D_2\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_L^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
&\ge\left|\frac{K_0^u+K_2^u}{2}\right|\\
&\ge\frac{(\sqrt{|K_0^u|}+\sqrt{|K_2^u|})^2}{4}\\
&\ge\frac{(\sqrt{D_0}+\sqrt{D_2})^2}{4}
=\frac{(\sqrt D+\sqrt E)^2}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(u\) was arbitrary, (9) holds in every direction appearing in (5).

Keep this arbitrary \(u\), and now fix an arbitrary linked allocation \(c_0,c_2\in\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
c_0+c_2=2\langle u,z\rangle
=\langle u,x_0\rangle+\langle u,x_2\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(c_0\ge\langle u,x_0\rangle\), then the first cap contains \(x_0\), and therefore
\[
G_{A_0}^u(c_0)+G_{A_2}^u(c_2)
\ge G_{A_0}^u(c_0)\ge D_0\ge E.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(c_0<\langle u,x_0\rangle\), equation (10) gives
\(c_2>\langle u,x_2\rangle\), so the second cap contains \(x_2\), and
\[
G_{A_0}^u(c_0)+G_{A_2}^u(c_2)
\ge G_{A_2}^u(c_2)\ge D_2\ge E.
\tag{12}
\]
The equality case \(c_0=\langle u,x_0\rangle\) is included in (11). Thus (11) and (12) exhaust every allocation in (5), including any minimizing allocation and every possible switch between the two cases. Consequently,
\[
\inf_{\substack{c_0+c_2=2\langle u,z\rangle}}
\bigl(G_{A_0}^u(c_0)+G_{A_2}^u(c_2)\bigr)\ge E
\tag{13}
\]
for every \(u\in\mathbb S^1\).

Substituting the direction-uniform bounds (9) and (13) into (5) yields
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge E+\frac{(\sqrt D+\sqrt E)^2}{4}.
\tag{14}
\]
Since \(z\in L\), the attained maximum over \(L\) is at least the left side of (14), proving the claimed bound.

It remains to verify the cutoff exactly. Without dividing by either depth,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E+\frac{(\sqrt D+\sqrt E)^2}{4}-D
&=\frac{5E+2\sqrt{DE}-3D}{4}\\
&=\frac{(5\sqrt E-3\sqrt D)(\sqrt E+\sqrt D)}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
If \(E\ge9D/25\), then \(5\sqrt E\ge3\sqrt D\), so both factors in the last expression are nonnegative. Thus (14) is at least \(D\). At the endpoint \(E=9D/25\), the first factor in (15) is exactly zero, so equality in this scalar comparison is included. The proof used no division by \(D\) or \(E\). Although (2) shows that zero depths cannot occur for the present two-dimensional bodies, the same calculation also covers the degenerate zero case: \(D=0\) forces \(E=0\), while \(E=0<D\) does not satisfy \(E\ge9D/25\).
