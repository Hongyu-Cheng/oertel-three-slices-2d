## Statement

Assume a hypothetical strict-low sequence in the pinch setting of accepted
fact `f882a944b9fbef55`, normalized after reflecting the height coordinate
if necessary by
\[
|A_{0,n}|=1,\qquad |A_{2,n}|=r_n\longrightarrow0,\qquad
|A_{1,n}|=s_n,\qquad 1-r_n<s_n<1+r_n.
\]
For an arbitrary normalized minimal certificate from accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, write
\[
H_{i,n}
=\{(h,w):f_{i,n}(w)+\sigma_{i,n}(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad i=1,2,3,
\]
and define
\[
p_{i,n}
=\left|A_{1,n}\cap\{f_{i,n}\le0\}\right|,
\]
\[
C_{0i,n}
=A_{0,n}\cap\{f_{i,n}-\sigma_{i,n}\le0\},
\qquad
C_{2i,n}
=A_{2,n}\cap\{f_{i,n}+\sigma_{i,n}\le0\},
\]
\[
e_{i,n}=|C_{0i,n}|+|C_{2i,n}|,
\qquad
t_n=\frac{2(1+r_n+s_n)}9.
\]
Thus strict lowness is
\[
p_{i,n}+e_{i,n}<t_n
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), every point of \(A_{2,n}\) belongs to at
least two of the three actual closed caps \(C_{2i,n}\).  Consequently
\[
\boxed{\ \sum_{i=1}^3e_{i,n}\ge2r_n.\ }
\tag{2}
\]
This conclusion holds uniformly in both alternatives of
`f882a944b9fbef55`: the small endpoint may converge to one point or to a
nondegenerate compact segment.  Moreover, (1), (2), and the strict residual
lower bound from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` imply
\[
\boxed{\
\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_{i=1}^3p_{i,n}
>\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\ }
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

Suppose first that a subsequence has been chosen on which the compactification
of accepted fact `f882a944b9fbef55` holds.  At the end we remove this
subsequence assumption.

### 1. Moving coordinates and the exact paired thresholds

Apply the common planar affine normalization and the accepted height shear.
Write the transformed slice bodies as \(K_{j,n}\).  If \(\kappa_n>0\) is
the absolute determinant of the common planar linear part, then, after a
further subsequence,
\[
\kappa_n\longrightarrow\kappa>0,
\]
\[
|K_{0,n}|=\kappa_n,\qquad
|K_{1,n}|=\kappa_ns_n,\qquad
|K_{2,n}|=\kappa_nr_n,
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
K_{0,n}\longrightarrow T_0,\qquad
K_{1,n}\longrightarrow T_1,\qquad
K_{2,n}\longrightarrow L
\tag{5}
\]
in Hausdorff distance.  Here \(T_0,T_1\) are nondegenerate triangles of
area \(\kappa\), while \(L\) is either one point or one nondegenerate compact
segment, and
\[
\frac{T_0+L}{2}\subseteq T_1.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) be the affine barycentric coordinates of
\(T_1\).

Write the transformed \(i\)-th full halfspace as
\[
\{(h,w):g_{i,n}(w)+\tau_{i,n}(h-1)\le0\}.
\]
Accepted fact `f882a944b9fbef55` also permits a permutation of the three
rows for each \(n\) after which the oriented middle cutting lines converge
to the three centroidal vertex-cap lines.  Choose affine coordinates
\(\ell_{i,n}\) for these oriented lines such that
\[
K_{1,n}\cap\{g_{i,n}\le0\}
=K_{1,n}\cap\{\ell_{i,n}\ge c_{i,n}\},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\ell_{i,n}\longrightarrow\beta_i
\quad\hbox{uniformly on the common bounding ball},\qquad
c_{i,n}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\tag{8}
\]
Such coordinates are obtained by positively rescaling the oriented line
normal so that its linear part converges to that of \(\beta_i\), then fixing
its value at the centroid of \(T_1\) to be \(1/3\).  Positive rescaling does
not change any halfspace.

Because its middle section is (7), there is a number
\(\lambda_{i,n}>0\) such that
\[
g_{i,n}(w)=\lambda_{i,n}
\bigl(c_{i,n}-\ell_{i,n}(w)\bigr).
\]
Therefore its actual transformed endpoint caps are
\[
C'_{0i,n}
=K_{0,n}\cap\{\ell_{i,n}\ge x_{i,n}\},
\qquad
x_{i,n}=c_{i,n}-\frac{\tau_{i,n}}{\lambda_{i,n}},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
C'_{2i,n}
=K_{2,n}\cap\{\ell_{i,n}\ge y_{i,n}\},
\qquad
y_{i,n}=c_{i,n}+\frac{\tau_{i,n}}{\lambda_{i,n}}.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular, the actual finite slope gives the exact paired-threshold
identity
\[
x_{i,n}+y_{i,n}=2c_{i,n}.
\tag{11}
\]
The height shear causes no change in (11): after the shear a full affine
functional still has equal and opposite height-\(0\) and height-\(2\)
displacements from its height-\(1\) level.

### 2. Vanishing endpoint mass pins the large-endpoint thresholds

The transformed middle cap in row \(i\) has area tending to
\(4\kappa/9\), by the line and body convergence in
`f882a944b9fbef55`.  The transformed target satisfies
\[
\kappa_nt_n\longrightarrow\frac{4\kappa}{9}.
\]
Multiplying (1) by \(\kappa_n\) therefore gives
\[
|C'_{0i,n}|+|C'_{2i,n}|\longrightarrow0
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{12}
\]

Set
\[
M_i=\max_{u\in T_0}\beta_i(u).
\tag{13}
\]
We claim
\[
x_{i,n}\ge M_i-o(1).
\tag{14}
\]
If not, then for some \(\varepsilon>0\) and a further subsequence,
\[
x_{i,n}\le M_i-\varepsilon.
\]
The affine function \(\beta_i\) is nonconstant on the two-dimensional
triangle \(T_0\), so
\[
T_0\cap\{\beta_i>M_i-\varepsilon/2\}
\]
has positive area and contains a compact positive-area subset of
\(\operatorname{int}T_0\).  Hausdorff convergence of the convex bodies in
(5) puts that compact subset inside \(K_{0,n}\) for all large \(n\), while
uniform convergence in (8) puts it inside
\(\{\ell_{i,n}\ge x_{i,n}\}\).  This contradicts
\(|C'_{0i,n}|\to0\) in (12), and proves (14).  The argument also covers a
maximizing edge: it only uses a threshold strictly below the maximum.

Combining (8), (11), and (14) gives the upper threshold bound
\[
y_{i,n}\le\frac23-M_i+o(1).
\tag{15}
\]

### 3. Exact barycentric area comparison

Fix \(v\in L\).  By (6),
\[
P_v:=\frac{T_0+v}{2}\subseteq T_1.
\]
This is a translate of the homothetic copy \(T_0/2\), so
\[
|P_v|=\frac{\kappa}{4}.
\tag{16}
\]

For two distinct indices \(j,k\), define
\[
R_{jk}
=T_1\cap\{\beta_j\le1/3,\ \beta_k\le1/3\}.
\]
In the \((\beta_j,\beta_k)\)-coordinate simplex, \(T_1\) is
\[
\{(a,b):a\ge0,\ b\ge0,\ a+b\le1\},
\]
of coordinate area \(1/2\), whereas \(R_{jk}\) is the entire square
\([0,1/3]^2\), of coordinate area \(1/9\).  Hence
\[
|R_{jk}|=\frac{2\kappa}{9}<\frac{\kappa}{4}=|P_v|.
\tag{17}
\]

We now prove that at least two indices \(i\) satisfy
\[
M_i+\beta_i(v)>\frac23.
\tag{18}
\]
If at most one did, there would be two distinct indices \(j,k\) for which
\[
M_j+\beta_j(v)\le\frac23,\qquad
M_k+\beta_k(v)\le\frac23.
\]
For every \(u\in T_0\), these inequalities would give
\[
\beta_j\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\le\frac13,\qquad
\beta_k\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\le\frac13.
\]
Thus \(P_v\subseteq R_{jk}\), contradicting (16)-(17).  This proves (18),
including all equality cases.

### 4. A uniform margin on a point or the entire segment

For \(v\in L\), let \(q(v)\) be the second largest of the three numbers
\[
M_i+\beta_i(v)-\frac23,\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]
The second-largest operation on three real numbers is continuous, so
\(q\) is continuous on \(L\).  Equation (18) says \(q(v)>0\) for every
\(v\in L\).  Compactness therefore gives
\[
\eta:=\min_{v\in L}q(v)>0.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(L\) is a point, this is the strict second-largest margin at that point.
If \(L\) is a nondegenerate segment, (19) is uniform on its interior and at
both endpoints.

Let \(v_n\in K_{2,n}\) be arbitrary.  Hausdorff convergence supplies
\(v\in L\) with \(|v_n-v|\to0\), uniformly in the choice of \(v_n\).
For at least two indices \(i\), (19) gives
\[
M_i+\beta_i(v)-\frac23\ge\eta.
\]
Uniform convergence in (8), the affine continuity of \(\beta_i\), and
(15) imply, uniformly for \(v_n\in K_{2,n}\),
\[
\ell_{i,n}(v_n)-y_{i,n}
\ge
M_i+\beta_i(v)-\frac23-o(1).
\tag{20}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the right side is at least \(\eta/2\)
for those two indices.  Hence every \(v_n\in K_{2,n}\) lies in the
interior of at least two of the actual closed caps \(C'_{2i,n}\).

Integrating this pointwise multiplicity statement yields
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3|C'_{2i,n}|
=\int_{K_{2,n}}\sum_{i=1}^3
\mathbf 1_{C'_{2i,n}}(v)\,dv
\ge2|K_{2,n}|
=2\kappa_nr_n.
\tag{21}
\]
The strict margin \(\eta/2\) is stronger than needed for the closed caps.
Thus a moving point on a cutting line, a support threshold attained on a
vertex or edge, and either endpoint of a limiting segment create no
boundary loss.

The affine normalization maps the original endpoint caps bijectively to
the caps in (9)-(10) and multiplies every planar area by \(\kappa_n\).
It follows from (21) that every original point of \(A_{2,n}\) belongs to
at least two \(C_{2i,n}\), and
\[
\sum_i e_{i,n}
\ge\sum_i|C_{2i,n}|
=\frac1{\kappa_n}\sum_i|C'_{2i,n}|
\ge2r_n.
\]
This proves (2) on the compactified subsequence.

If the pointwise double-cover conclusion failed for infinitely many
indices of the original sequence, apply `f882a944b9fbef55` to that
subsequence.  A further subsequence has either a point limit or a
nondegenerate-segment limit, and the argument above makes every one of its
sufficiently late indices satisfy the double cover, a contradiction.
Therefore the conclusion holds for all sufficiently large \(n\) in the
original sequence.

### 5. Strict-low middle starvation

Summing (1) and applying (2) gives
\[
\sum_i p_{i,n}<3t_n-\sum_i e_{i,n}
\le3t_n-2r_n.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_i p_{i,n}
&>
\frac{4s_n}{3}-3t_n+2r_n\\
&=
\frac{2(s_n-1+2r_n)}3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives the strict residual inequality
\(s_n>1-r_n\).  Substitution into (22) proves
\[
\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_i p_{i,n}
>\frac{2r_n}{3},
\]
which is (3) with the full signed first-order constant.
