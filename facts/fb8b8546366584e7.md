## Statement

Let \(f_j,f_k,f_\ell\) be nonconstant affine functions on
\(\mathbb R^2\) whose linear parts are pairwise nonparallel.  Put
\[
M=\frac{259}{100},\qquad
L_j=L_k=L_\ell=-\frac23,\qquad
\rho_0=\frac{10}{3001},
\]
and
\[
u_j=u_k=\frac1{100},\qquad u_\ell=\frac1{50}.
\]
There is no nonempty two-dimensional compact convex polygon \(A_0\) of
area \(M\) such that, simultaneously for every
\(i\in\{j,k,\ell\}\),
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{f_i\le a\}\right|
=
\min\left\{
M,\,
\max\left\{
0,\,
u_i+\rho_0(a-L_i)
\right\}
\right\}
\qquad(a\in\mathbb R).
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently there is no exact rational pair \(A_0,A_2\), and hence no
compatible middle polygon \(K\), realizing the three cap profiles in (1)
together with any prescribed exact-cell supports, whole-body support
extrema, crossing equalities, and \(W=0\) values.

## Proof

We first prove a general geometric lemma.  Let \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\)
be a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex body, let
\(\varphi\) be a nonconstant affine function, and suppose that
\[
F(a)=|A\cap\{\varphi\le a\}|
\]
is affine with a positive constant derivative on the whole open interval
between the minimum and maximum of \(\varphi\) on \(A\).
Then \(A\) is a parallelogram, and the two faces on which
\(\varphi\) is minimized and maximized are nondegenerate parallel edges.

Indeed, use coordinates \(x=\varphi\) and a Euclidean arclength
coordinate \(y\) along the level lines of \(\varphi\).  If
\([\alpha,\beta]\) is the support interval of \(x\), then for
\(\alpha<x<\beta\) the section of \(A\) is an interval
\[
\{y:(x,y)\in A\}=[r_-(x),r_+(x)].
\]
Convexity of \(A\) makes \(r_-\) convex and \(r_+\) concave.  By the
coarea identity, for almost every \(x\),
\[
F'(x)
=
\frac{\mathcal H^1(A\cap\{\varphi=x\})}
{\|\nabla\varphi\|}.
\tag{2}
\]
The section length is continuous on the interior of the support
interval, so the almost-everywhere identity and the constant value of
\(F'\) imply that
\[
r_+(x)-r_-(x)=c
\tag{3}
\]
for one constant \(c>0\) and every \(\alpha<x<\beta\).  Equation (3)
gives \(r_+=r_-+c\).  Since \(r_+\) is concave, \(r_-\) is concave as
well as convex, hence affine.  Therefore \(r_+\) is affine with the
same slope.  Taking closures at \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) gives
\[
A=\{(x,y):\alpha\le x\le\beta,\,
r_-(x)\le y\le r_-(x)+c\},
\]
which is a parallelogram.  Its two edges at \(x=\alpha,\beta\) are
parallel to the level lines of \(\varphi\).  This proves the lemma.

Now suppose that an \(A_0\) satisfying (1) exists.  Since
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad \rho_0>0,
\]
the nonconstant interval of the right-hand side of (1) is exactly
\[
\left(
L_i-\frac{u_i}{\rho_0},\,
L_i+\frac{M-u_i}{\rho_0}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Outside its closure the right-hand side is respectively \(0\) and
\(M\).  Hence (4) is the full open support interval of \(f_i\) on
\(A_0\), and the derivative there is the positive constant
\(\rho_0\).  Applying the lemma to \(f_i\) shows that \(A_0\) is a
parallelogram having edges parallel to the level lines of \(f_i\).

Apply this conclusion for \(i=j,k,\ell\).  Pairwise nonparallel linear
parts mean that the three level-line directions
\[
\{v:\nabla f_j\mathbin{\cdot}v=0\},\qquad
\{v:\nabla f_k\mathbin{\cdot}v=0\},\qquad
\{v:\nabla f_\ell\mathbin{\cdot}v=0\}
\]
are three distinct one-dimensional directions.  Thus the same
parallelogram \(A_0\) would have to possess edges in three distinct
directions.  A nondegenerate parallelogram has exactly two edge
directions, a contradiction.  This proves the nonexistence assertion.

If one additionally assigns the whole-body support values
\[
\min_{A_0^{XY}}(X+2Y)=0,\qquad
\max_{A_2^{uv}}(u+2v)=\frac32,
\]
then the positive direction \((1,2)\) formally gives
\[
\max_{A_2^{uv}}(u+2v)
-\min_{A_0^{XY}}(X+2Y)
=\frac32<2=2\min(1,2).
\]
Accepted fact `331355cde8383f52` would convert this inequality into
absence of the strict \(R_{j\ell}\) midpoint chamber if common
two-dimensional endpoint bodies with those support values existed.
The contradiction above shows that the proposed three cap profiles
cannot belong to such an \(A_0\).  Therefore the formal support-gap
witness does not extend to a joint convex-geometric realization, and
there is no \(A_2\) or \(K\) left to construct.
