## Proof

We first prove a division-free quadrant lemma.  Let \(C\) be a compact convex polygon in affine coordinates \((T,R)\), with coordinate area equal to \(\Delta\) times physical area.  Suppose
\[
C\cap\{R=0\}=[q_-,q]\times\{0\},
\qquad q\ge0,
\]
and \((p,-r)\in C\), where \(r\ge0\).  Let \(E=C\cap\{T\ge0,\ R\ge0\}\), and let its physical area be \(D\).

Assume first \(r>0\).  For \((T,R)\in E\) with \(R>0\), the segment from \((p,-r)\) to \((T,R)\) meets \(R=0\) at
\[
\left(\frac{rT+Rp}{r+R},0\right).
\]
This point belongs to the full section, so
\[
rT+(p-q)R\le rq.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(R=0\), the same inequality follows from \(T\le q\).  If \(p>q\), (1) contains \(E\) in the triangle
\[
T\ge0,\qquad R\ge0,\qquad
rT+(p-q)R\le rq,
\]
whose coordinate area is
\[
\frac{rq^2}{2(p-q)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
2\Delta D(p-q)\le rq^2.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(p=q\) and \(r>0\), (1) is the strip \(T\le q\), and (2) is automatic.  If \(p<q\), the left side of (2) is nonpositive, so (2) is automatic.  If \(r=0\) and \(p>q\), the point \((p,0)\) contradicts the fact that \(q\) is the right endpoint of the full section.  If \(r=0\) and \(p\le q\), (2) is merely automatic; in particular, \(r=0,p=q\) yields only \(0\le0\) and no strip containment.  Thus (2) is valid on every order, tie, and zero stratum without dividing by \(r\), \(q\), or \(p-q\).

For \(E_{j\ell}\), use \((T,R)=(X,Y)\).  Up to null unsupported cells and cutting lines, its physical area is \(b\).  Its full \(R=0\) section has right endpoint
\[
q=\beta+H=\delta-\tau.
\]
The two endpoints of the remaining diagonal chord are
\[
(p,-r)=(\delta+t,-t),
\qquad t=\gamma,\ \gamma+K.
\]
Substitution in (2) gives respectively (Tb-) and (Tb+).

If \(\tau>0\), the lower endpoint row is the stronger of the two whenever it is active.  Indeed, for \(t\ge\gamma\),
\[
\gamma(t+\tau)\le t(\gamma+\tau).
\]
If \(\tau=0\), every positive \(t\) gives the same bound \(2\Delta b\le\delta^2\).  Since \(\gamma+K>0\), (Tb+) retains this bound even at \(\gamma=\tau=0\).  If \(\gamma=0<\tau\), the lower endpoint is \((\delta,0)\), whereas the right endpoint of the full horizontal chord is \(\delta-\tau<\delta\); this is impossible, exactly as encoded by (Tb-).

For \(E_{k\ell}\), use
\[
(T,R)=(p_k,-p_\ell)=(\delta-X-Y,-Y).
\]
This affine change has determinant magnitude one.  Up to null unsupported cells and cutting lines, the positive quadrant has physical area \(c\).  Its full \(R=0\) section is
\[
T\in[\tau,\delta-\beta],
\]
so \(q=\delta-\beta\).  The two endpoints of the remaining \(X=0\) chord become
\[
(p,-r)=(\delta-s,-s),
\qquad s=\alpha,\ \alpha+J.
\]
Substitution in (2) gives respectively (Tc-) and (Tc+).

When \(\alpha\le s<\beta\), the lower endpoint row is the stronger active row because
\[
\frac{\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}
\le\frac{s}{\beta-s},
\]
which follows by cross multiplication from \(s\ge\alpha\).  If \(s=\beta>0\), then \(r=s>0\) and \(p=q\), so (1) is the strip \(T\le q\).  At \(\alpha=\beta=0\), instead \(r=0,p=q=\delta\), and the lemma gives only the tautology \(0\le0\).  If \(s>\beta\), the polynomial row is automatic because its left side is nonpositive.

For each endpoint used in the first two targets, a positive endpoint parameter makes exactly one of its two target coordinates negative; a zero parameter puts the endpoint on that axis and is already covered by the zero case of the lemma.  Hence there is at most one external-axis choice, and swapping the axes produces no additional inequality.

It remains to enumerate the third cyclic target.  In \((T,R)=(p_j,p_k)\), the two endpoints of the remaining \(p_\ell=0\) chord are
\[
(\beta,\delta-\beta),\qquad
(\beta+H,\delta-\beta-H)=(\beta+H,\tau).
\]
Both coordinates are nonnegative.  Swapping \(T\) and \(R\) preserves that property.  Hence neither endpoint has the form \((p,-r)\) with \(r>0\), and the quadrant lemma supplies no triangle row for \(E_{jk}\).  This proves that the four displayed rows are the complete cyclic enumeration under the stated endpoint-chord definition.  The proof deliberately says nothing about using arbitrary exterior supported-cell points after other axis choices.
