## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon.  Let three
pairwise nonparallel lines bound a triangle \(\mathcal T\), choose the side
of each line containing \(\mathcal T\) as its central halfplane \(H_i^-\),
and let \(H_i^+\) be the complementary outer halfplane.  Suppose
\[
C_0=K\cap H_1^-\cap H_2^-\cap H_3^-,
\qquad
C_i=K\cap H_i^+\quad(i=1,2,3)
\]
have positive areas
\[
q=|C_0|,\qquad x_i=|C_i|,
\]
and the interiors of \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) are pairwise disjoint.  Put
\[
D=\frac q{|\mathcal T|},\qquad
r_i=\frac{x_i}{q},\qquad R=r_1+r_2+r_3.
\]
Thus \(0<D\leq1\).

For \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\), let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), and define
\[
A_k=\frac{1+R}{r_k}=\frac{q+x_1+x_2+x_3}{x_k},
\qquad
c_k=2\sqrt{r_i r_j}=\frac{2\sqrt{x_i x_j}}q,
\]
\[
d_{1k}=\frac{4A_kc_k^2}{(A_k+c_k^2)^2}.
\]
For \(t\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
\operatorname{clip}(t)=\min\{1,\max\{0,t\}\},
\]
and put
\[
d_{2k}
=1-\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}
\right)^2,
\qquad
d_0=\min\left\{
1,\frac3{r_1^{-1}+r_2^{-1}+r_3^{-1}}
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
D\geq\delta(r_1,r_2,r_3),
\qquad
\delta
=\min\{d_0,d_{11},d_{12},d_{13},d_{21},d_{22},d_{23}\}.
\tag{1}
\]

At \(r_1=r_2=r_3=1/2\), one has
\[
d_0=\frac12,\qquad
d_{1k}=\frac59,\qquad
d_{2k}=1-\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right)^2
=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}.
\]
Hence (1) gives \(D\geq1/2\).  Since the last two displayed values are
strictly larger than \(1/2\), continuity shows that in some neighborhood of
\((r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1/2,1/2,1/2)\), the minimum in (1) is \(d_0\), so the
lower bound there is
\[
D\geq
\frac3{r_1^{-1}+r_2^{-1}+r_3^{-1}}
=\frac{3}{q(x_1^{-1}+x_2^{-1}+x_3^{-1})}.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, the explicit rational neighborhood
\[
\frac9{20}\leq r_i\leq\frac{11}{20}
\quad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{2a}
\]
forces
\[
D\geq\frac25.
\tag{2b}
\]

## Proof

Affinely map \(\mathcal T\) to the barycentric simplex
\[
\Delta=\{\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1:\alpha_i\geq0\},
\]
using \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) as planar coordinates.  Its coordinate area is
\(1/2\).  Area ratios are affine invariant, so the coordinate areas of the
four cells are
\[
|C_0|=\frac D2,\qquad |C_i|=\frac{Dr_i}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

First suppose that no endpoints of chords belonging to different cutting
lines coincide.  Apply the finite boundary reduction in accepted fact
`b7cb6541c1d790bc`.  It preserves the cutting lines, all chord endpoints,
and the four areas in (3), and it leaves at most one unmarked vertex on each
of the six boundary arcs.  We work with the reduced polygon.

Every chord endpoint lies on a side of \(\Delta\).  Indeed, if an endpoint
on \(\alpha_i=0\) had \(\alpha_j<0\), a sufficiently small relative
neighborhood inside \(K\), on the side \(\alpha_i<0\), would give a
positive-area intersection of caps \(i\) and \(j\).  In the present
noncoincident case the six endpoint parameters are therefore positive.
Write
\[
a=t_{12},\quad b=t_{21},\quad
c=t_{23},\quad d=t_{32},\quad
e=t_{31},\quad f=t_{13},
\]
where, if \(k\) is the missing index,
\[
p_{ij}=(1-t_{ij})e_k+t_{ij}e_j.
\]
The three open side gaps are
\[
L_1=1-a-f,\qquad L_2=1-b-c,\qquad L_3=1-d-e.
\tag{4}
\]

The convex hull of the six chord endpoints is obtained from \(\Delta\) by
deleting corner triangles of doubled areas \(ab,cd,ef\).  It is contained
in \(C_0\).  Consequently, with
\[
S=ab+cd+ef,
\]
equation (3) gives
\[
D\geq1-S.
\tag{5}
\]
For fixed
\[
s_1=a+f=1-L_1,\quad
s_2=b+c=1-L_2,\quad
s_3=d+e=1-L_3,
\]
the expression \(S\) is affine in each of \(a,b,d\) separately after
substituting \(f=s_1-a\), \(c=s_2-b\), and \(e=s_3-d\).
Its maximum on the resulting box occurs at a vertex, where its value is
either zero or one of \(s_1s_2,s_2s_3,s_3s_1\).  Hence
\[
S\leq\max\{s_1s_2,s_2s_3,s_3s_1\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Relabel the cuts so that \(L_3=\max_iL_i\).  Then \(s_3\leq s_1,s_2\), so
(5)-(6) imply
\[
D\geq1-(1-L_1)(1-L_2)
=L_1+L_2-L_1L_2.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
L_1\leq D,\qquad L_2\leq D,
\qquad
(1-L_1)(1-L_2)\geq1-D.
\tag{8}
\]

The reduced positive cap \(i\) is a triangle.  Write its outer vertex as
\[
u_1=(-h_1,A,B),\quad
u_2=(C,-h_2,E),\quad
u_3=(F,G,-h_3),
\tag{9}
\]
where all displayed letters are positive and
\[
A+B=1+h_1,\quad C+E=1+h_2,\quad F+G=1+h_3.
\]
The base of cap \(i\) occupies the fraction \(L_i\) of its side of
\(\Delta\).  Comparing its triangular area with (3) gives
\[
h_i=\frac{Dr_i}{L_i}.
\tag{10}
\]

Convexity at the two edges adjacent to each cap vertex gives
\[
b(A-a)\geq ah_1,\qquad e(B-f)\geq fh_1,
\]
\[
a(C-b)\geq bh_2,\qquad d(E-c)\geq ch_2,
\]
\[
c(G-d)\geq dh_3,\qquad f(F-e)\geq eh_3.
\tag{11}
\]
For example, the first expression in (11) is the supporting determinant of
the directed edge \(u_1p_{12}\), evaluated at \(p_{21}\).  Adding the two
inequalities belonging to cap \(i\), then using (4), (9), and (10), yields
\[
\frac ab+\frac fe\leq1+\frac{L_1^2}{Dr_1},
\quad
\frac ba+\frac cd\leq1+\frac{L_2^2}{Dr_2},
\quad
\frac dc+\frac ef\leq1+\frac{L_3^2}{Dr_3}.
\]
The three reciprocal-pair inequalities give
\[
3D\leq\frac{L_1^2}{r_1}
       +\frac{L_2^2}{r_2}
       +\frac{L_3^2}{r_3}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(L_3<D\), then (8) and (12) imply
\[
3D<D^2(r_1^{-1}+r_2^{-1}+r_3^{-1}).
\]
Therefore
\[
D<d_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_3\geq D.
\tag{13}
\]

We next use the triangle of the three cap vertices.  Along each segment
joining two \(u_i\)'s, the zero of the first negative barycentric coordinate
must occur no later than the zero of the second one.  Otherwise an open
subsegment, and hence a positive-area relative neighborhood in \(K\), would
belong to two caps.  Thus
\[
AC\geq h_1h_2,\qquad
EG\geq h_2h_3,\qquad
FB\geq h_3h_1.
\tag{14}
\]
Let \(U=\operatorname{conv}\{u_1,u_2,u_3\}\).  Direct expansion of its
barycentric determinant gives
\[
2|U|
=h_1GE+h_3AC+h_2FB+CGB+FAE-h_1h_2h_3.
\]
Using (14) in the first and third terms, and using AM--GM in the two mixed
terms, gives
\[
2|U|\geq
h_3\left(\sqrt{AC}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}\right)^2.
\tag{15}
\]
The coordinate area of the whole polygon is
\[
|K|=\frac D2(1+R).
\]
Since \(U\subseteq K\), equations (10) and (15) imply
\[
\sqrt{AC}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}
\leq\sqrt{\frac{(1+R)L_3}{r_3}}
=\sqrt{A_3L_3}.
\tag{16}
\]

The first inequalities for caps 1 and 2 in (11) also give
\[
A\geq\frac{a(b+h_1)}b,\qquad
C\geq\frac{b(a+h_2)}a.
\]
Therefore
\[
AC\geq(a+h_2)(b+h_1)
\geq\left(\sqrt{ab}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}\right)^2,
\tag{17}
\]
where the last difference is
\((\sqrt{ah_1}-\sqrt{bh_2})^2\).
Furthermore, Cauchy--Schwarz and (8) give
\[
\sqrt{L_1L_2}
\leq1-\sqrt{(1-L_1)(1-L_2)}
\leq1-\sqrt{1-D}.
\]
It follows from (10) that
\[
\sqrt{h_1h_2}
\geq
\sqrt{r_1r_2}\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-D}\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]
Finally, (5) gives \(S\geq1-D\), while \(c,f<1\) gives
\[
cd+ef<d+e=1-L_3.
\]
Thus
\[
ab>L_3-D.
\tag{19}
\]

Assume \(0<D<\delta\).  Then \(D<d_0\), so (13) applies.  Combining
(16)-(19), and recalling \(c_3=2\sqrt{r_1r_2}\), gives the necessary strict
inequality
\[
F_3(D,L_3)
:=\sqrt{L_3-D}
  +c_3\bigl(1+\sqrt{1-D}\bigr)
  -\sqrt{A_3L_3}
<0.
\tag{20}
\]
For fixed \(D\), the derivative with respect to \(L>D\) is
\[
\frac1{2\sqrt{L-D}}-\frac{\sqrt{A_3}}{2\sqrt L}.
\]
It changes sign at most once, from positive to negative.  Hence the minimum
of \(F_3(D,L)\) on \(D\leq L\leq1\) is attained at \(L=D\) or \(L=1\).

We show that both endpoint values are positive.  Put
\(s=\sqrt{1-D}\), \(A=A_3\), and \(c=c_3\).  Since \(D<d_{13}\),
\[
s>
\frac{|A-c^2|}{A+c^2},
\]
using
\[
1-d_{13}=\frac{(A-c^2)^2}{(A+c^2)^2}.
\]
If \(A\geq c^2\), this is equivalent to
\[
c(1+s)>\sqrt{AD};
\]
if \(A<c^2\), the same inequality holds automatically.  Therefore
\[
F_3(D,D)=c(1+s)-\sqrt{AD}>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Also \(D<d_{23}\) implies
\[
s>
\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{\sqrt A-c}{1+c}\right).
\]
Whenever the clipped quantity is zero the desired inequality is immediate;
whenever it lies strictly between zero and one, the displayed inequality is
exactly
\[
c+(1+c)s>\sqrt A.
\]
The remaining clipped value one would give \(d_{23}=0\), incompatible with
\(0<D<\delta\).  Hence
\[
F_3(D,1)=c+(1+c)s-\sqrt A>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (21)-(22) contradict (20) and the endpoint-minimum property.
The choice of the largest \(L_k\) was arbitrary, and \(\delta\) includes
both thresholds for every possible \(k\).  This proves (1) when all chord
endpoints belonging to different lines are distinct.

We record a direct rational audit of (2a)-(2b).  Suppose (2a) holds and
\(D<2/5\).  Then
\[
\frac3{r_1^{-1}+r_2^{-1}+r_3^{-1}}
\geq\frac9{20}>\frac25,
\]
so (13) gives \(L_3\geq D\), whichever gap is largest.  Put
\(s=\sqrt{1-D}\).  Then \(s>\sqrt{3/5}>3/4\), while
\[
c_3\geq\frac9{10},
\qquad
A_3
=1+\frac{1+r_1+r_2}{r_3}
\leq\frac{17}{3}.
\]
Consequently
\[
F_3(D,D)
>\frac{63}{40}-\sqrt{\frac{34}{15}}>0,
\tag{23}
\]
where the last inequality follows after squaring from
\(3969\cdot15>34\cdot1600\).

For the other endpoint, it is enough to use \(s>3/4\).  Define
\[
G(r_1,r_2,r_3)
=\left(\frac34+\frac74c_3\right)^2-A_3.
\]
On the box (2a), \(G\) is increasing in every variable.  Indeed, for
\(i=1,2\),
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial r_i}
\left(\frac34+\frac74c_3\right)^2
\geq
2\left(\frac{93}{40}\right)
 \left(\frac{63}{40}\right)
>\frac{20}{9}
\geq\frac{\partial A_3}{\partial r_i},
\]
where
\(\partial c_3/\partial r_i=\sqrt{r_j/r_i}\geq9/10\).
Also \(\partial G/\partial r_3>0\), because \(c_3\) is independent of
\(r_3\) and \(A_3=1+(1+r_1+r_2)/r_3\) decreases with \(r_3\).
The minimum is therefore at
\(r_1=r_2=r_3=9/20\), where
\[
c_3=\frac9{10},\qquad A_3=\frac{47}{9},
\qquad
G=\left(\frac{93}{40}\right)^2-\frac{47}{9}>0
\]
because \(8649\cdot9>47\cdot1600\).  Hence
\[
F_3(D,1)
> \frac34+\frac74c_3-\sqrt{A_3}>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (23)-(24) again contradict (20), proving (2b) in the generic
stratum.  On this box \(A_k>c_k^2\) and
\(0<(\sqrt{A_k}-c_k)/(1+c_k)<1\).  Thus \(d_{1k}\) and \(d_{2k}\) are
exactly the zero thresholds of the two decreasing endpoint expressions.
Equations (23)-(24), for every possible largest gap \(k\), also show
\(\delta(r_1,r_2,r_3)\geq2/5\) throughout the closed box (2a).

It remains to remove that genericity assumption.  Two chord endpoints from
different lines can coincide only at a vertex \(e_k\) of \(\Delta\).
At each such boundary point, the two incident chord directions enter the
tangent cone of \(K\).  There is a supporting affine functional that
increases strictly along both directions: use an interior normal when
\(e_k\) is a vertex, and the normal to the supporting edge when \(e_k\)
lies in the relative interior of an edge.  Intersect \(K\) with the
parallel halfplane whose boundary is moved a positive distance into \(K\).
For sufficiently small distances, the new boundary meets the two incident
chord segments in two distinct points, preserves positive area in every
cell, and cannot create an overlap because the new polygon is a subset of
\(K\).  Performing the finitely many truncations with generic small
distances produces polygons \(K_\varepsilon\) having no shared chord
endpoints and converging to \(K\) in area as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

For these polygons,
\[
q_\varepsilon\to q,\qquad
x_{i,\varepsilon}\to x_i,\qquad
D_\varepsilon=\frac{q_\varepsilon}{|\mathcal T|}\to D.
\]
The function \(\delta\) in (1) is continuous for positive
\((r_1,r_2,r_3)\).  Applying the already proved generic bound to
\(K_\varepsilon\) and passing to the limit proves (1) on every coincident
endpoint stratum.  The same limit proves (2b), including boundary points of
the closed box (2a).  The specialization and the local formula (2) were
computed in the statement.
