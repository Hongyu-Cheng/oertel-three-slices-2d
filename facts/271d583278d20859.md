## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\frac14\Delta .
\tag{1}
\]
These are rational polygons and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac58\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{2}
\]
Their areas and target are
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac1{32}\right),
\qquad
T=\frac{33}{32},\qquad t=\frac{11}{48}.
\tag{3}
\]
For the positively homogeneous extension of the exact threshold, take
\[
p=(1,3),\qquad r=(-1,3),\qquad p+r=(0,6).
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_t(p)&=3-\sqrt{\frac{29}{8}},\\
R_t(r)&=3-\frac{\sqrt{29}}2,\\
R_t(p+r)&=6-\frac{\sqrt{87}}2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
and hence
\[
R_t(p)+R_t(r)-R_t(p+r)
=\frac{\sqrt{29}}2
\left(\sqrt3-1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the claimed superadditivity is false.

## Proof

For a nonzero vector \(v\), write
\[
\widehat Q_v(u)=\|v\|Q^\Delta_{v/\|v\|}(u).
\]
Equivalently, \(\widehat Q_v(u)\) is the threshold \(c\) for which the
lower \(v\)-cap of \(\Delta\) has area \(u\).  Positive rescaling of the
direction scales every cap threshold by the same factor, so the allocation
formula in accepted facts `17ca54faa05ad878` and `65512ded3a06d971`
computes \(R_t(v)\) directly with these unnormalized quantiles.

If the three vertex values of \(v\) on \(\Delta\) are
\(x\le y\le z\), direct similar-triangle area calculation gives
\[
\widehat Q_v(u)=
\begin{cases}
x+\sqrt{2u(y-x)(z-x)},
&0\le u\le\dfrac{y-x}{2(z-x)},\\[6pt]
z-\sqrt{(1-2u)(z-x)(z-y)},
&\dfrac{y-x}{2(z-x)}\le u\le\dfrac12.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Repeated endpoint values are covered by the corresponding surviving
branch.  For \(\frac14\Delta\), the quantile is
\[
\widehat Q^{\,A_2}_v(u)=\frac14\widehat Q_v(16u).
\tag{8}
\]

Compatibility follows from (2) and accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`.
The residual ratios are
\[
M=\frac12,\qquad m=\frac1{32},\qquad
\frac mM=\frac1{16},\qquad \frac{a_1}{M}=1.
\]
Since \(1/16<9/25\), the strict residual interval in accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e` is
\[
1-\frac1{16}<1<1+\frac1{16}.
\]
Thus (1) lies in the full strict residual area strip, not merely on one of
its boundaries.

The feasible middle-allocation interval is
\[
[L,U]=[t-m,t]=\left[\frac{19}{96},\frac{11}{48}\right].
\tag{9}
\]
At its left endpoint the total outer demand is the positive,
capacity-active value \(m=1/32\).  Define the positively rescaled endpoint
convolution
\[
\widehat E_v(m)=
\max_{0\le u\le m}
\left\{\widehat Q_v(u)
+\frac14\widehat Q_v\!\left(\frac12-16u\right)\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Formula (7) gives the following exact maxima:
\[
\widehat E_{(1,3)}(m)=\frac34,\qquad
\widehat E_{(-1,3)}(m)=-\frac14,\qquad
\widehat E_{(0,6)}(m)=\frac32.
\tag{11}
\]
Here is a direct audit of (11), including the individual endpoint
allocation \(u=0\).  For \(v=(1,3)\), the maximand in (10) is
\[
\begin{cases}
\dfrac34+(\sqrt6-2\sqrt3)\sqrt u,
&0\le u\le\dfrac1{48},\\[5pt]
\sqrt{6u}+\dfrac14\sqrt{3-96u},
&\dfrac1{48}\le u\le\dfrac1{32}.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]
The first branch is strictly below \(3/4\) for \(u>0\).  On the second
branch, putting \(x=\sqrt{6u}\) gives
\[
x+\frac14\sqrt{3-16x^2},\qquad x^2\ge\frac18.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
1-\frac{4x}{\sqrt{3-16x^2}}<0,
\]
and its left-endpoint value is
\(1/(2\sqrt2)+1/4<3/4\).  Thus the unique maximum is the
\(u=0\) value \(3/4\).

For \(v=(-1,3)\), the maximand is
\[
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac14+(2\sqrt2-2\sqrt6)\sqrt u,
&0\le u\le\dfrac3{128},\\[5pt]
-\dfrac54+\sqrt{8u}+\dfrac14\sqrt{4-128u},
&\dfrac3{128}\le u\le\dfrac1{32}.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
The first branch is strictly below \(-1/4\) for \(u>0\).  In the second,
putting \(x=\sqrt{8u}\) gives \(x^2\ge3/16\), and the nonconstant part
\[
x+\frac14\sqrt{4-16x^2}
\]
has derivative \(1-4x/\sqrt{4-16x^2}<0\).  Its left-endpoint
value makes (13) equal to \(-1+\sqrt3/4<-1/4\).  Hence the unique maximum
is again at \(u=0\), with value \(-1/4\).

For \(v=(0,6)\), (10) is
\[
\frac{15}{2}-6\sqrt{1-2u}-6\sqrt{2u}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(0<u\le1/32\),
\(\sqrt{1-2u}+\sqrt{2u}>1\), so (14) is strictly below its
\(u=0\) value \(3/2\).  This proves (11) from the original cap areas.

At \(a=L=19/96\), one has \(1-2L=29/48\).  Formula (7) therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_{(1,3)}(L)&=3-\sqrt{\frac{29}{8}},\\
\widehat Q_{(-1,3)}(L)&=3-\frac{\sqrt{29}}2,\\
\widehat Q_{(0,6)}(L)&=6-\frac{\sqrt{87}}2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Each value in (15) is strictly larger than one half of its corresponding
value in (11): the three comparisons are respectively against
\(3/8,-1/8,3/4\).  Indeed, the first and third values exceed \(1\), while
the second is positive.  The exact optimizer classification in accepted
fact `65512ded3a06d971` now puts all three directions at the unique left
endpoint \(a=L\), and the actual value there is the middle branch because
\(L>0\).  This proves (5).

Finally,
\[
\sqrt3>1+\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]
because squaring reduces this to \(3/2>\sqrt2\), and then to
\(9/4>2\).  Substitution into (6) proves the strict violation.

ZERO-DEMAND AND DEGENERACY AUDIT
The total outer demand at the optimizer is \(m>0\), so the finite
convolution in (10), not the convention \(E_v(0)=-\infty\), is used.
The maximizing endpoint split \(u=0\) in (12)-(14) is different: it is an
individual zero allocation and correctly uses the finite support value
\(\widehat Q_v(0)\), exactly as required by accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878`.  The competing endpoint \(a=U=t\) has total outer
demand zero and is covered by the actual \(-\infty\) convention in
accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971`; the strict left-endpoint comparisons
above still make \(L\) the unique optimizer.

The vectors \(p,r\) in (4) are linearly independent, \(p+r\ne0\), and none
is antipodal to another.  Thus (6) is not a zero-vector, antipodal, or
collinear artifact.

For an explicit antipodal check, the same calculation for \(v=(0,-6)\)
gives
\[
\widehat E_v(m)=-\frac{15}{2}+\frac3{\sqrt2},\qquad
R_t(v)=-6+\frac{\sqrt{57}}2.
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, the endpoint maximand is
\[
-\frac{15}{2}+6\sqrt{2u}+\frac32\sqrt{1-32u}.
\]
It is strictly concave and its derivative vanishes at \(u=1/64\), which
gives the displayed value of \(\widehat E_v(m)\).  Its left-allocation
comparison is strict because
\(\sqrt{57}>15/2\) and \(3/\sqrt2<3\).  Hence
\[
R_t(0,6)+R_t(0,-6)
=\frac{\sqrt{57}-\sqrt{87}}2<0.
\tag{17}
\]
The zero-sum positive-spanning completion of (4) also obeys the weaker
inequality strictly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_t(1,3)+R_t(-1,3)+R_t(0,-6)
&=\frac12\left(
\sqrt{57}-\sqrt{29}\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)\right)<0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
because \(1+1/\sqrt2>\sqrt2\), so the subtracted term exceeds
\(\sqrt{58}>\sqrt{57}\).

More generally, accepted fact `23dcab2387927bc4` proves that
\(g=(1/3,1/3)\) belongs to the true depth region \(D_t\) for (1).
The exact profile identity in accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` then gives
\[
R_t(v)\le v\cdot g\qquad(v\in\mathbb R^2).
\]
Therefore every zero-sum family for this instance, including all zero,
antipodal, and collinear cases, satisfies
\[
\sum_i R_t(v_i)\le \sum_i v_i\cdot g=0.
\tag{19}
\]
This confirms that the counterexample concerns only superadditivity.

FIRST UNSUPPORTED STEP
Nonemptiness of all exact profile halfplanes does not make their raw
directional thresholds the lower support function of their intersection.
Some profile inequalities are redundant.  Therefore one cannot infer
superadditivity of \(R_t\) from compatibility or from the existence of a
true depth point.

NEXT TEST
Discard full superadditivity and attack only the numerical
positive-spanning inequality from accepted fact `9328166a552ec875`.
Any exact obstruction must violate that inequality itself.  By
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, such an obstruction would empty the true depth region,
so it cannot be obtained merely by exposing redundant directional profile
constraints as in (6).
