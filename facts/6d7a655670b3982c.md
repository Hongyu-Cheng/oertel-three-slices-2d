## Statement

Use the three polygons, target \(q\), point \(z_*\), mass
\(\mu_n(u,d)\), and capacity ceiling \(C(n)\) from accepted fact
`dd848df2b7dc1a3c`.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,1/5),(3/5,1/2),(1/10,13/25)\},\\
A_1&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3/2,0),(8/5,1/5),(1/2,1),(0,4/5)\},\\
A_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(1,3/5),(0,1)\},
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
q=\frac{2521}{4500},\qquad z_*=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right),
\]
\[
C(n)=\sup\{u:\exists d,\ \mu_n(u,d)<q\}.
\]
Let
\[
\alpha=\frac{1396+\sqrt{683191}}{1125},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{1304+\sqrt{434791}}{1125}.
\]

Then every real \(t\le\beta\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
C((1,t))<\frac{1+t}{2}=(1,t)\cdot z_*.
}
\tag{1}
\]
More quantitatively, if
\[
G_t(u):=\min_d\mu_{(1,t)}(u,d),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
G_t\left(\frac{1+t}{2}\right)>q+\frac1{20}
\qquad(t\le\beta).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, let
\[
\lambda_1,\lambda_2>0,\qquad
t_1,t_2<\alpha,
\]
\[
\bar t:=
\frac{\lambda_1t_1+\lambda_2t_2}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}
>\beta,
\]
and define
\[
n_1=\lambda_1(1,t_1),\qquad
n_2=\lambda_2(1,t_2),\qquad
n_3=-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(1,\bar t).
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(n_1+n_2+n_3=0\) and
\[
\boxed{
C(n_1)+C(n_2)+C(n_3)<0.
}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, no strict-low constants \(u_i<C(n_i)\) for a triple
(3) can have \(u_1+u_2+u_3>0\).

## Proof

Put
\[
L_t(x,y)=x+ty
\]
and define the lower-cap functions
\[
F_j(s;t)=|A_j\cap\{L_t\le s\}|
\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\]
At the center score
\[
u_*=\frac{1+t}{2},
\]
the exact minimum mass is
\[
G_t(u_*)=
F_1(u_*;t)
+\min_x\bigl(F_0(x;t)+F_2(1+t-x;t)\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]

For a triangle of area \(B\) whose ordered vertex scores are
\[
\rho_0<\rho_1<\rho_2,
\]
its exact lower-cap function is
\[
\tau_{B;\rho_0,\rho_1,\rho_2}(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le\rho_0,\\
\displaystyle
\frac{B(s-\rho_0)^2}
{(\rho_1-\rho_0)(\rho_2-\rho_0)},
&\rho_0\le s\le\rho_1,\\
\displaystyle
B\left(
1-\frac{(\rho_2-s)^2}
{(\rho_2-\rho_0)(\rho_2-\rho_1)}
\right),
&\rho_1\le s\le\rho_2,\\
B,&s\ge\rho_2.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Triangulating each endpoint polygon from the origin gives areas
\[
\frac{19}{100},\quad\frac{131}{1000}
\]
for \(A_0\), and
\[
\frac35,\quad\frac12
\]
for \(A_2\).  Formula (6), applied to those four triangles, is an
exact five-chamber cap formula for each endpoint polygon once the
four vertex scores are ordered.

Write the endpoint scores as
\[
p_1=1+\frac t5,\qquad
p_2=\frac35+\frac t2,\qquad
p_3=\frac1{10}+\frac{13t}{25}
\tag{7}
\]
on \(A_0\), and
\[
\zeta_1=2,\qquad
\zeta_2=1+\frac{3t}{5},\qquad
\zeta_3=t
\tag{8}
\]
on \(A_2\).  The middle-cap area
\[
A(t):=F_1((1+t)/2;t)
\]
has the four relevant exact pieces
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_1(t)&=\frac{119t^2-128t+55}{40t(8t-11)},\\
a_2(t)&=\frac{20t^2+72t-99}{20(8t-11)},\\
a_3(t)&=\frac{5t^2+18t+45}{20(2t+5)},\\
a_4(t)&=\frac{(t+1)^2}{8t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Every endpoint or middle score-order change on \(t\le\beta\) occurs
at one of
\[
-5,\quad-\frac53,\quad-\frac65,\quad-1,\quad
-\frac5{26},\quad0,\quad\frac43,\quad\frac53.
\]
Thus there are exactly nine nondegenerate parameter chambers.  Their
complete score ledger is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
t\text{-range}&A_0\text{ score order}&A_2\text{ score order}&A(t)\\ \hline
(-\infty,-5)&p_3<p_2<p_1<0&\zeta_3<\zeta_2<0<\zeta_1&a_1\\
(-5,-5/3)&p_3<p_2<0<p_1&\zeta_3<\zeta_2<0<\zeta_1&a_1\\
(-5/3,-6/5)&p_3<p_2<0<p_1&\zeta_3<0<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_1\\
(-6/5,-1)&p_3<0<p_2<p_1&\zeta_3<0<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_1\\
(-1,-5/26)&p_3<0<p_2<p_1&\zeta_3<0<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_2\\
(-5/26,0)&0<p_3<p_2<p_1&\zeta_3<0<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_2\\
(0,4/3)&0<p_3<p_2<p_1&0<\zeta_3<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_3\\
(4/3,5/3)&0<p_3<p_1<p_2&0<\zeta_3<\zeta_2<\zeta_1&a_3\\
(5/3,\beta]&0<p_3<p_1<p_2&0<\zeta_3<\zeta_1<\zeta_2&a_4.
\end{array}
\tag{10}
\]
Each row of (10), together with the two endpoint triangulations and
(6), specifies every endpoint cap chamber in (5); no directional or
allocation chamber remains implicit.

For each of the nine rows, exact `Piecewise` expansion using (6)
followed by real quantifier elimination tested
\[
\exists(t,x)\quad
\left[
\begin{array}{l}
t\text{ lies in the selected row of (10)},\\
x\text{ lies in any endpoint chamber generated by (6)},\\
1+t-x\text{ lies in any endpoint chamber generated by (6)},\\
F_0(x;t)+F_2(1+t-x;t)+A(t)
\le q+\dfrac1{20}
\end{array}
\right].
\tag{11}
\]
The nine exact results, in the order of (10), were
\[
(\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},
\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},
\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False}).
\tag{12}
\]
The last query includes its algebraic endpoint \(t=\beta\).

At the eight rational boundaries where adjacent score chambers meet,
SageMath over \(\mathbb Q^2\) independently enumerated every endpoint
breakpoint and every stationary point of the active quadratic.  It
returned:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
t&\min_x(F_0+F_2)&A(t)&G_t(u_*)-q\\ \hline
-5&14467/52000&367/1020&618923/7956000\\
-5/3&321/1000&539/1460&85381/657000\\
-6/5&321/1000&9499/24720&268907/1854000\\
-1&321/1000&151/380&27043/171000\\
-5/26&321/1000&9473/21190&3963493/19071000\\

4/3&321/1000&701/1380&55631/207000\\
5/3&321/1000&8/15&2647/9000
\end{array}
\tag{13}
\]
Every last-column entry is strictly larger than \(1/20\).
Equations (10)-(13) prove the uniform bound (2) on all
\(t\le\beta\).

For each fixed \(t\), polygon cap areas are continuous in their
thresholds.  Their endpoint infimal convolution is continuous and
nondecreasing in \(u\): on a compact \(u\)-interval its minimizers may
be restricted to a common compact allocation interval because all cap
functions are constant on their tails.  Hence \(G_t(u)\) is continuous
and nondecreasing.  The strict excess in (2) persists on a left
neighborhood of \(u_*=(1+t)/2\), so the strict-low capacity ceiling
lies strictly below \(u_*\).  This proves (1).

It remains to prove (4).  Capacity is positively homogeneous:
\[
C(\rho n)=\rho C(n)\qquad(\rho>0),
\tag{14}
\]
because scaling \(n,u,d\) by \(\rho\) leaves every halfspace section
unchanged.

For each \(i=1,2\), either \(t_i\le\beta\), in which case (1) gives
\[
C((1,t_i))-\frac{1+t_i}{2}<0,
\tag{15}
\]
or \(\beta<t_i<\alpha\), in which case accepted fact
`dd848df2b7dc1a3c` gives the same strict inequality.  Since
\(\bar t>\beta\), that accepted fact also gives
\[
C(-(1,\bar t))+\frac{1+\bar t}{2}<0.
\tag{16}
\]
By (14), (15), and (16),
\[
C(n_i)-n_i\cdot z_*<0\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
The normals in (3) sum to zero, so their scores at \(z_*\) sum to
zero.  Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3C(n_i)
=
\sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(C(n_i)-n_i\cdot z_*\bigr)
<0,
\]
which proves (4).

The nine quantified queries in (11), including all of their exact
endpoint `Piecewise` branches, were run with local Wolfram Kernel
\(14.3.0\) using exact `Resolve` over the reals.  All returned the
literal Boolean `False`; none timed out.  The eight rational boundary
audits in (13) were independently rerun with SageMath \(10.8\), using
`Polyhedron(..., base_ring=QQ)`, exact breakpoint enumeration, and
exact stationary points.
