## Statement

Let \(\mathcal R_n\) be a residual counterexample sequence satisfying all
hypotheses of accepted fact `d1177b21864bbcc6`, normalized by
\[
q_n=1,\qquad
x_{1,n}=x_{2,n}=x_{3,n}=\frac5{21},\qquad
m_n=\frac{12}{7},\qquad
D_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]
Put \(A_{2,n}\) in the John position of that fact:
\[
B\subseteq\widetilde A_{2,n}\subseteq2B.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose any \(a_n\in\widetilde A_{0,n}\), put \(v_n=a_n/2\), and apply
the slice-preserving affine shear
\[
(h,z)\longmapsto(h,z-(2-h)v_n).
\tag{3}
\]
Write
\[
\widehat A_{0,n}=\widetilde A_{0,n}-2v_n,\qquad
\widehat K_n=\widetilde K_n-v_n.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
0\in\widehat A_{0,n},\qquad
\frac B2\subseteq\widehat K_n.
\tag{5}
\]
For every unit vector \(u\),
\[
w_{\widehat K_n}(u)
\geq
\frac{
w_{\widehat A_{0,n}}(u)+w_{\widetilde A_{2,n}}(u)
}{2}
\geq
1+\frac12w_{\widehat A_{0,n}}(u).
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\mathcal V\) denotes planar mixed area, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
4|\widehat K_n|
&\geq
|\widehat A_{0,n}+\widetilde A_{2,n}|\\
&=
|\widehat A_{0,n}|+|\widetilde A_{2,n}|
+2\mathcal V(\widehat A_{0,n},\widetilde A_{2,n})\\
&\geq
|\widehat A_{0,n}|+|\widetilde A_{2,n}|
+2\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover,
\[
|\widehat K_n|\geq
\frac14\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_n).
\tag{8}
\]
Suppose that the two gauged diameter sequences are not both bounded.
Choose indices \(n_j\) such that
\[
\max\left\{
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_{n_j}),
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n_j})
\right\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Equation (6) implies that the middle diameter is at least half this
maximum, so
\(\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_{n_j})\to\infty\).  Reindex this
subsequence by \(n\).  On the resulting genuine escape subsequence,
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_n)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
k_n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
p_{\varnothing,n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{9}
\]

Put \(p_n=p_{\varnothing,n}\), and retain the usual middle marginals
\(c_{i,n}\), total middle doubleton mass \(d_{R,n}\), and central middle
mass \(r_n=r_{123,n}\).  On the reindexed genuine escape subsequence
fixed above,
\[
\frac{M_n}{p_n}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{k_n}{p_n}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{\eta_n}{p_n}\longrightarrow1,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\frac{c_{i,n}}{p_n}\longrightarrow\frac49
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\qquad
\frac{d_{R,n}}{p_n}
+2\frac{r_n}{p_n}
\longrightarrow\frac13.
\tag{11}
\]
The accepted relative estimate remains valid at every stage,
\[
\frac{\eta_n}{p_n}\geq c_{\mathrm{rel}}>0,
\tag{12}
\]
and (10) sharpens it on escape.

If, along a further subsequence,
\[
\rho=\lim_n\frac{r_n}{p_n},
\tag{13}
\]
then the strengthened cyclic-singleton inequality in accepted fact
`e1361027c1dda6fa` forces
\[
0\leq\rho\leq
\rho_*:=
\frac{74-12\sqrt{30}}{289}.
\tag{14}
\]

There is the following strict geometric exclusion.  After the shear (3),
write
\[
\lambda_{i,n}=-f_{i,n},\qquad
\lambda_{1,n}+\lambda_{2,n}+\lambda_{3,n}=1,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
\Delta_n=\{\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n},\lambda_{3,n}\geq0\}.
\]
Accepted fact `e40abbed940a40ab` identifies \(\Delta_n\) with the
central middle triangle and gives, after division by the common John area
multiplier,
\[
|\Delta_n|=r_n.
\tag{16}
\]
On the same reindexed genuine escape subsequence, suppose that there are
\(a_n>0\) such that
\[
\widehat K_n
\subseteq
K_{a_n,n}:=
\{\lambda_{i,n}\geq-a_n,\ i=1,2,3\},
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\liminf_n
\theta_n>\frac23,
\qquad
\theta_n:=
\frac{k_n}{r_n(1+3a_n)^2}.
\tag{18}
\]
No such genuine escape subsequence exists.  In particular, no reindexed
genuine escape subsequence can satisfy the exact case
\[
\widehat K_n=K_{a_n,n}
\tag{19}
\]
for every \(n\).

More generally, (17) alone gives the exact reflected-support estimate
\[
\boxed{\qquad
p_n\leq6a_n^2r_n.
\qquad}
\tag{20}
\]
Thus (18) is a uniform high-occupancy condition, not an eccentricity
bound.

## Proof

The shear (3) leaves \(A_{2,n}\) fixed and sends the compatibility
inclusion to
\[
\frac{\widehat A_{0,n}+\widetilde A_{2,n}}2
\subseteq\widehat K_n.
\tag{21}
\]
The choice \(2v_n=a_n\) gives
\(0\in\widehat A_{0,n}\).  Combining this with (2) and (21) gives
\[
\frac B2=\frac{\{0\}+B}{2}\subseteq\widehat K_n,
\]
which proves (5).  Width is additive under Minkowski sum and monotone
under inclusion.  Since \(w_B(u)=2\), (6) follows from (21).

For (7), area scales by four under the midpoint dilation, so (21) gives
\[
4|\widehat K_n|
\geq|\widehat A_{0,n}+\widetilde A_{2,n}|.
\]
Choose a diameter segment \(I_n\subseteq\widehat A_{0,n}\).  Mixed area
is monotone in both arguments, and (2) gives
\[
\mathcal V(\widehat A_{0,n},\widetilde A_{2,n})
\geq\mathcal V(I_n,B)
=\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n}).
\]
Indeed, \(I_n+B\) is the radius-one stadium, whose area is
\(\pi+2\operatorname{diam}(I_n)\), so the last mixed area is exactly
\(\operatorname{diam}(I_n)\).  This proves (7).

To prove (8), put
\[
R_n=\sup\{|z|:z\in\widehat K_n\}.
\]
For a point \(z_n\in\widehat K_n\) with
\(|z_n|\) arbitrarily close to \(R_n\), the body contains the triangle
with apex \(z_n\) and base the unit-diameter chord of \(B/2\)
perpendicular to \(z_n\).  This triangle has area
\(|z_n|/2\).  Hence
\[
|\widehat K_n|\geq\frac{R_n}{2}.
\]
Because \(0\in B/2\subseteq\widehat K_n\),
\(\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_n)\leq2R_n\), proving (8).
Now suppose that the maximum of the two gauged diameter sequences is
unbounded.  Choose \(n_j\) so that this maximum tends to infinity.  If
\(\widehat A_{0,n_j}\) realizes that maximum, choose a unit direction
realizing the width
\(\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n_j})\).  Equation (6) then gives
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_{n_j})
\geq
\frac12\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n_j}),
\]
whereas if \(\widehat K_{n_j}\) realizes the maximum there is nothing
to prove.  Thus, for every \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_{n_j})
\geq
\frac12
\max\left\{
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_{n_j}),
\operatorname{diam}(\widehat A_{0,n_j})
\right\},
\]
and the middle diameter tends to infinity.  Reindex this subsequence by
\(n\).  This is exactly the genuine escape subsequence fixed before
(9).

Let \(q'_n\) be the transformed central \(Q\)-cell area.  Accepted fact
`d1177b21864bbcc6` gives
\[
\frac{7\pi}{12}\leq q'_n\leq\frac{7\pi}{3}.
\tag{22}
\]
All transformed slice and cell areas equal \(q'_n\) times their
\(q_n=1\) values.  Thus (8), (22), and
\(\operatorname{diam}(\widehat K_n)\to\infty\) on the reindexed
subsequence imply \(k_n\to\infty\).

We now derive the scalar calibration.  Write
\[
s_{P,n}=\sum_i p_{\{i\},n},\qquad
d_{P,n}=\sum_{i<j}p_{\{i,j\},n}.
\]
The canonical tails give
\[
0\leq u_{i,n}\leq\frac5{21}.
\tag{23}
\]
Since
\[
\sum_i u_{i,n}=s_{P,n}+2d_{P,n},
\qquad
M_n=p_n+s_{P,n}+d_{P,n},
\]
equation (23) gives
\[
0\leq M_n-p_n\leq\frac57.
\tag{24}
\]
The exact \(W=0\) identity in accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` is
\[
2p_n=\frac{69}{7}+k_n+\eta_n.
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(\eta_n\geq0\) and the preceding paragraph gives
\(k_n\to\infty\), equations (24)-(25) give
\(p_n\to\infty\) and \(M_n/p_n\to1\).

Every residual counterexample fails the large-slice conclusion of
accepted facts `2f9be517307232c7` and `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Therefore
\[
M_n<\frac{12}{7}+k_n,\qquad
k_n<M_n+\frac{12}{7}.
\tag{26}
\]
Divide (26) by \(p_n\) and use (24).  The first inequality gives
\(\liminf k_n/p_n\geq1\), and the second gives
\(\limsup k_n/p_n\leq1\).  This proves the first two limits in (10).
Dividing (25) by \(p_n\) proves the third.  Equation (12) is accepted
fact `4ee2807833cea34e`; the limit just obtained is stronger.

At the fixed ratios,
\[
v_{i,n}=m_n-x_{i,n}=\frac{31}{21}.
\]
The three crossings are
\[
u_{i,n}+\frac{31}{21}+c_{i,n}
=\frac{2(M_n+12/7+k_n)}9.
\tag{27}
\]
Divide (27) by \(p_n\), use (10), (23), and
\(p_n\to\infty\).  This proves the first part of (11).
Moreover,
\[
\eta_n=s_{P,n}+4d_{P,n}+3d_{R,n}+6r_n.
\tag{28}
\]
Equation (23) bounds \(s_{P,n}+4d_{P,n}\), while
\(\eta_n/p_n\to1\).  Dividing (28) by \(3p_n\) proves the second part
of (11).

We record the consequence of the cyclic-singleton inequality.  Pass to a
subsequence on which (13) holds and on which every normalized middle
doubleton mass converges.  Let \(d_{jk,n}\) denote the doubleton cell with
indices \(j,k\), and put
\[
\overline d_{jk}=\lim_n\frac{d_{jk,n}}{p_n}.
\]
The exact cell partition gives
\[
r_{i,n}
=k_n-c_{j,n}-c_{k,n}+d_{jk,n}+r_n.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (10), (11), and (29) yield
\[
\lim_n
\frac{r_{i,n}+2r_n/9}{p_n}
=
\frac{1+11\rho}{9}+\overline d_{jk},
\tag{30}
\]
while
\[
\sum_{j<k}\overline d_{jk}
=\frac13-2\rho.
\tag{31}
\]
Accepted fact `e1361027c1dda6fa` gives
\[
\left(
\sqrt{r_{1,n}+\frac{2r_n}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_{2,n}+\frac{2r_n}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_{3,n}+\frac{2r_n}{9}}
\right)^2
\leq2k_n.
\tag{32}
\]
For a fixed nonnegative sum in (31), concavity of the square root shows
that the left side of the limiting form of (32) is minimized by putting
all doubleton mass into one cell.  Hence
\[
\frac{
\left(
\sqrt{4-7\rho}+2\sqrt{1+11\rho}
\right)^2
}{9}
\leq2.
\tag{33}
\]
Equation (31) first gives \(0\leq\rho\leq1/6\).  Expanding (33), isolating
the nonnegative radical, and squaring gives
\[
289\rho^2-148\rho+4\geq0.
\]
On \(0\leq\rho\leq1/6\), this is exactly
\[
\rho\leq\frac{74-12\sqrt{30}}{289},
\]
which proves (14).

It remains to prove the reflected-support estimate.  Suppress \(n\), and
put
\[
\beta_i=-L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-\sum_iL_i>0.
\tag{34}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_i\beta_i=1+\delta,
\]
and the ambient reflected \(P\)-triangle is
\[
\mathcal T_P=\{\lambda_i\leq\beta_i,\ i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{35}
\]
The central cell \(P_\varnothing\) is contained in the strict interior of
\(\mathcal T_P\).

Fix \(i\).  The positive \(Q\)-double cap
\(Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}\) contains a point \(y\in A_2\) satisfying
\[
\lambda_i(y)<L_i=-\beta_i.
\tag{36}
\]
For every \(x\in P_\varnothing\), compatibility and (17) give
\[
\frac{x+y}{2}\in K\subseteq K_a,
\]
and therefore
\[
\lambda_i(x)+\lambda_i(y)\geq-2a.
\]
Together with (36), this implies the strict support bound
\[
\lambda_i(x)>\beta_i-2a.
\tag{37}
\]
This holds for all three indices.

Use the reflected barycentric coordinates
\[
\mu_i=\frac{\beta_i-\lambda_i}{\delta}.
\tag{38}
\]
On \(\mathcal T_P\), they satisfy
\[
\mu_i\geq0,\qquad
\mu_1+\mu_2+\mu_3=1.
\]
Equations (35), (37), and (38) show that \(P_\varnothing\) maps into
\[
\left\{
\mu_i\geq0,\quad
\sum_i\mu_i=1,\quad
\mu_i<\frac{2a}{\delta}\ (i=1,2,3)
\right\}.
\tag{39}
\]
The whole triangle \(\mathcal T_P\) is a reflected dilation of
\(\Delta\) by factor \(\delta\).  This is the exact ambient-triangle
homothety of accepted fact `e40abbed940a40ab`; hence its area, in the
\(q=1\) normalization, is \(\delta^2r\).

For the unit barycentric simplex, the fraction with
\(\max_i\mu_i\leq s\) is
\[
F(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq s\leq1/3,\\
(3s-1)^2,&1/3\leq s\leq1/2,\\
1-3(1-s)^2,&1/2\leq s\leq1,\\
1,&s\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{40}
\]
Indeed, for \(s\geq1/2\) the three removed vertex triangles are
disjoint and each has relative area \((1-s)^2\).  For
\(1/3\leq s\leq1/2\), inclusion-exclusion adds the three pairwise
overlaps and gives
\[
1-3(1-s)^2+3(1-2s)^2=(3s-1)^2.
\]
Thus (39) gives
\[
p\leq\delta^2rF(2a/\delta).
\tag{41}
\]

The right side of (41) has the following uniform bound:
\[
\delta^2F(2a/\delta)\leq6a^2.
\tag{42}
\]
To check every range, if \(\delta\leq2a\), the left side is at most
\(\delta^2\leq4a^2\).  If \(2a\leq\delta\leq4a\), it equals
\[
\delta^2-3(\delta-2a)^2
=-2\delta^2+12a\delta-12a^2
\leq6a^2,
\]
with equality at \(\delta=3a\).  If
\(4a\leq\delta\leq6a\), it equals
\[
(6a-\delta)^2\leq4a^2.
\]
If \(\delta\geq6a\), it is zero.  Equations (41)-(42) prove (20).

Finally, the homothety calculation recorded in accepted fact
`e1361027c1dda6fa` gives
\[
|K_a|=r(1+3a)^2.
\tag{43}
\]
Using (18), (20), and (43),
\[
\frac{k_n}{p_n}
\geq
\theta_n
\frac{(1+3a_n)^2}{6a_n^2}
>
\frac32\theta_n.
\tag{44}
\]
If \(\liminf\theta_n>2/3\), equation (44) gives
\(\liminf k_n/p_n>1\), contradicting (10).  This proves the asserted
high-occupancy exclusion, including (19).

Accepted fact `22d750674c663f88` identifies every positive-area equality
shape for the alternating-sector inequality as a triangle centered at the
fan concurrency whose sides are parallel to the three fan lines.  In the
coordinates (15), any such triangle has equations
\(\lambda_i\geq c_i\).  Its centroid has \(i\)-th coordinate
\[
c_i+\frac{1-\sum_jc_j}{3}.
\]
Requiring this centroid to be
\((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) forces \(c_1=c_2=c_3=-a\).  Thus these equality shapes
are exactly the triangles \(K_a\), and (19) excludes them on every
reindexed genuine escape subsequence.  No stability claim from near
equality to the occupancy hypothesis (18) is made here.
