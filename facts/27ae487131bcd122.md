## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system and incidence orbit of accepted facts `64760c76cadff481` and `f8e871d10dac1791`, with
\[
0<A<B\leq1,\qquad t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\leq\frac12,
\]
compatible nonsingular endpoint matrices \(U,V\), a nonsingular middle matrix \(L=(\ell_{ij})\) with column sums
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_{ij}=1\qquad(j=1,2,3),
\]
an independent shift \(\sigma\), and all three strict low-row inequalities. Suppose first that all nine entries of \(L\) are nonzero. In the sole surviving chamber of accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915`, after its allowed fixed row and column interchanges,
\[
J_1=\{2\},\qquad J_2=\{2,3\},\qquad J_3=\{1\}.
\]
Then no such \((U,V,L,\sigma)\) exists. Moreover, no such system exists when one or more entries of \(L\) vanish, by accepted fact `6986d9aa136ba907`. Thus the exclusion includes \(B=1\), every zero-middle-entry boundary stratum, and every repeated, constant, or threshold-equality endpoint cap stratum.

## Proof

In the all-nine-nonzero chamber, write the three middle rows as
\[
L_{1*}=(-x,y,-z),\qquad
L_{2*}=(-d,u,v),\qquad
L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),
\]
where all nine displayed letters are positive. Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` gives negative means in rows \(1,2\). Put
\[
r=x+z-y>0,\qquad s=d-u-v>0.
\]
Because the columns of \(L\) sum to one, the row sums total three, and hence
\[
p-a-b=3+r+s. \tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
c_i=\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where \(\mathcal C\) is the literal closed-cap function of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`. Its exact one-positive and two-positive formulas give
\[
c_1=\frac{y^2}{(x+y)(z+y)},\qquad
c_2=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+u)(d+v)},\qquad
c_3=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}. \tag{2}
\]
We first prove the purely middle-triangle inequality
\[
\max\{c_1,c_2\}+c_3\geq\frac89. \tag{3}
\]
Equation (1) gives \(a+b<p\). Therefore
\[
(p+a)(p+b)
\leq\left(p+\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2
<\left(\frac{3p}{2}\right)^2,
\]
so \(c_3>4/9\). The strict third-row inequality gives \(c_3<t\leq1/2\), since both endpoint contributions are nonnegative. Set
\[
C=c_3,\qquad K=\frac89-C,
\qquad D=3-\frac2{\sqrt C},
\qquad G=\frac2{\sqrt K}-3,
\qquad H=\frac{K}{1-2K}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
\frac49<C<\frac12,\qquad
\frac7{18}<K<\frac49,
\]
and \(D,G,H\) are positive.

Suppose, contrary to (3), that \(c_1<K\) and \(c_2<K\). Since \(x+z=y+r\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(x+y)(z+y)
&=2y^2+yr+xz\\
&\leq2y^2+yr+\frac{(y+r)^2}{4}
=\frac{(3y+r)^2}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first formula in (2) consequently yields
\[
c_1\geq\left(\frac{2y}{3y+r}\right)^2.
\]
Because \(2-3\sqrt K>0\), the strict inequality \(c_1<K\) implies
\[
y<\frac{r}{G}. \tag{5}
\]

Put \(P=u+v\), so \(d=P+s\). The second formula in (2) can be written as
\[
c_2=\frac{dP+uv}{d^2+dP+uv}
\geq\frac{dP}{d^2+dP}
=\frac{P}{2P+s}.
\]
Since \(K<1/2\), the strict inequality \(c_2<K\) implies
\[
P<Hs. \tag{6}
\]

The first column sum and the definition of \(r\) give
\[
p=x+d+1=y+r-z+P+s+1.
\]
Combining this identity with (5) and (6) gives
\[
p<\left(1+\frac1G\right)r+(1+H)s+1-z. \tag{7}
\]
We now compare the two coefficients in (7) with \(1/D\). Put
\[
\xi=\sqrt C,\qquad \eta=\sqrt K.
\]
Then \(\xi^2+\eta^2=8/9\), \(2/3<\xi<1/\sqrt2\), and \(\eta<2/3\). Directly,
\[
1+\frac1G=\frac{2(1-\eta)}{2-3\eta},
\qquad
\frac1D=\frac{\xi}{3\xi-2}.
\]
All denominators are positive, and the numerator of their difference is
\[
\xi(2-3\eta)-2(1-\eta)(3\xi-2)
=4-4\xi-4\eta+3\xi\eta
=\frac{(3(\xi+\eta)-4)^2}{6}\geq0.
\]
Hence
\[
1+\frac1G\leq\frac1D. \tag{8}
\]
Also
\[
1+H=\frac{1-K}{1-2K}.
\]
After cross multiplication, the inequality \(1+H\leq1/D\) is equivalent to
\[
K\leq\frac{2(1-\xi)}{2-\xi}. \tag{9}
\]
Here \(K<4/9\), while \(\xi<1/\sqrt2<5/7\) gives
\[
\frac{2(1-\xi)}{2-\xi}>\frac49.
\]
Thus (9) holds, and
\[
1+H\leq\frac1D. \tag{10}
\]
Equations (7), (8), and (10), together with \(z>0\) and \(0<D<3\), now give
\[
p<\frac{r+s}{D}+1-z
<\frac{3+r+s}{D}. \tag{11}
\]

On the other hand, put \(m=3+r+s=p-a-b\). By arithmetic-geometric mean,
\[
(p+a)(p+b)
\leq\left(p+\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2
=\left(\frac{3p-m}{2}\right)^2.
\]
Using the third formula in (2),
\[
\sqrt C\geq\frac{2p}{3p-m}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{m}{p}\leq3-\frac2{\sqrt C}=D,
\]
and hence
\[
p\geq\frac{3+r+s}{D},
\]
contradicting (11). This proves (3).

Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` gives, for the first two rows,
\[
c_1,c_2<\kappa:=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9,
\]
and the strict third-row inequality gives \(c_3<t\). Therefore
\[
\max\{c_1,c_2\}+c_3
<\kappa+t
=\frac{4(1+B)}9
\leq\frac89,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(B\leq1\). This contradicts (3). The inequality remains strict when \(B=1\), so equality is covered literally.

No endpoint projection ordering, distinctness, or sign was used: endpoint caps entered only through their nonnegativity and the accepted strict row bounds. Thus repeated endpoint values, constant projections, and thresholds at support values are already included by the literal cap convention of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.

Finally, suppose some middle entry vanishes. Accepted fact `6986d9aa136ba907` produces arbitrarily small perturbations with the same \(U,V,A,B,t\), the same incidence orbit, all three strict literal row inequalities, and all nine middle entries nonzero in one fixed contained-support chamber after one fixed allowed relabeling. In particular, \(B\leq1\) is unchanged. Each perturbation is impossible by the all-nine-nonzero argument above. Hence no zero-entry boundary system exists either.
