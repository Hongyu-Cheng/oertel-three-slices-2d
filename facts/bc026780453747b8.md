## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact convex bodies with
nonempty interiors. For signs \(\varepsilon,\eta\in\{+,-\}\), use the
corresponding closed coordinate quadrant; its boundary has planar area
zero. Assume the exact Alternative 1 cell areas
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&(+,+)&(-,+)&(+,-)&(-,-)\\ \hline
A_0&97/100&3/200&3/200&0\\
A_2&9/200&1/160&1/160&1/200.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
P=A_0\cap\{x\ge0,y\ge0\},\qquad
Q=A_2\cap\{x\ge0,y\ge0\},
\tag{2}
\]
and assume the high-high constraint
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|<\frac{29}{80}.
\tag{3}
\]

Define the four negative coordinate support depths
\[
a_h=-\min_{(x,y)\in A_h}x,\qquad
b_h=-\min_{(x,y)\in A_h}y
\qquad(h=0,2).
\tag{4}
\]
They are positive because all four mixed cells in (1) have positive
area. Let
\[
w_P=\max_Px-\min_Px,\qquad
h_P=\max_Py-\min_Py.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(\ell_x\) be the maximum length of a horizontal segment contained
in \(Q\), and let \(\ell_y\) be the maximum length of a vertical
segment contained in \(Q\). These numbers are positive.

Define the affine-covariant shape budget
\[
\Gamma
:=
a_0b_0+a_2b_2
+
\frac{12}{25}
\left(
\frac{a_2}{\ell_x}\frac{b_0}{h_P}
+
\frac{a_0}{w_P}\frac{b_2}{\ell_y}
\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
(a_0+a_2)(b_0+b_2)<\Gamma,
\tag{7}
\]
and therefore
\[
\min\{a_0+a_2,b_0+b_2\}<\sqrt{\Gamma}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, the single additional shape hypothesis
\[
\Gamma\le4
\tag{9}
\]
implies
\[
a_0+a_2<2
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
b_0+b_2<2.
\tag{10}
\]
Under (9),
\[
\left|
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap\{x+y\ge-1\}
\right|
\ge
\frac14
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{197}{200}}+\sqrt{\frac{41}{800}}
\right)^2
=
\frac{829+4\sqrt{8077}}{3200}
>
\frac{29}{80}.
\tag{11}
\]

For the triangle family of accepted fact `1ef73dc2fb6792f4`, its
notation and proved estimates imply
\[
a_0b_0<1,\qquad
a_2b_2<\frac1{64},
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\frac{a_0}{w_P}\le1,\qquad
\frac{b_0}{h_P}\le1,\qquad
\frac{a_2}{\ell_x}<\frac{25}{16},\qquad
\frac{b_2}{\ell_y}<\frac{25}{16}.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently
\[
\Gamma<\frac{161}{64},
\qquad
\min\{a_0+a_2,b_0+b_2\}
<
\frac{\sqrt{161}}8
<2.
\tag{14}
\]

## Proof

We first extract everything that follows from the high-high constraint
without any shape hypothesis. Choose a horizontal chord
\(S_x\subseteq Q\) of length \(\ell_x\). Minkowski addition of a
planar convex body and a horizontal segment increases its area by the
segment length times the vertical width:
\[
|P+S_x|=|P|+\ell_xh_P.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, every nonempty horizontal section of \(P\) is an interval,
addition of \(S_x\) increases its length by \(\ell_x\), and integration
over the vertical projection of \(P\) proves (15). Since
\(P+S_x\subseteq P+Q\), while planar scaling in (3) gives
\(|P+Q|<29/20\). Therefore equations (1) and (3) give
\[
\ell_xh_P
\le |P+Q|-|P|
<
\frac{29}{20}-\frac{97}{100}
=\frac{12}{25}.
\tag{16}
\]
The identical vertical-section argument with a vertical chord of
length \(\ell_y\) gives
\[
\ell_yw_P<\frac{12}{25}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus (16)-(17) are universal consequences of the exact cells and
high-high constraint for arbitrary convex bodies.

Factor the two cross terms in the combined-depth product as
\[
a_2b_0
=
\left(\frac{a_2}{\ell_x}\right)
\left(\frac{b_0}{h_P}\right)
(\ell_xh_P),
\]
\[
a_0b_2
=
\left(\frac{a_0}{w_P}\right)
\left(\frac{b_2}{\ell_y}\right)
(w_P\ell_y).
\tag{18}
\]
Substituting (16)-(17) into
\[
(a_0+a_2)(b_0+b_2)
=a_0b_0+a_2b_2+a_2b_0+a_0b_2
\]
proves (7). Both combined depths are positive, so their minimum is no
larger than the square root of their product. The strict inequality in
(7) proves (8), and (9) gives (10).

We next convert (10) to the clipped-area contradiction. Suppose first
that
\[
b_0+b_2<2.
\]
Put
\[
C_{0,x}=A_0\cap\{x\ge0\},\qquad
C_{2,x}=A_2\cap\{x\ge0\}.
\]
Every midpoint of one point from each set has nonnegative
\(x\)-coordinate and has
\[
y\ge-\frac{b_0+b_2}{2}>-1.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{C_{0,x}+C_{2,x}}2
\subseteq
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap\{x+y\ge-1\}.
\tag{19}
\]
The exact cell vector gives
\[
|C_{0,x}|=\frac{97}{100}+\frac3{200}
=\frac{197}{200},
\]
\[
|C_{2,x}|=\frac9{200}+\frac1{160}
=\frac{41}{800}.
\tag{20}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski, in the form recorded by accepted fact
`756b87a16a89ea8f`, applied to (19), proves the first inequality in
(11). If \(a_0+a_2<2\), use instead
\[
C_{0,y}=A_0\cap\{y\ge0\},\qquad
C_{2,y}=A_2\cap\{y\ge0\};
\]
the two areas are again (20), and the same proof applies.

For the last strict numerical comparison in (11),
\[
\frac{829+4\sqrt{8077}}{3200}-\frac{29}{80}
=
\frac{4\sqrt{8077}-331}{3200}>0,
\]
because \(16\cdot8077=129232>109561=331^2\).

It remains to check the specialization. Accepted fact
`1ef73dc2fb6792f4` proves (12), proves the first two inequalities in
(13), and exhibits a horizontal segment of length \(q_x\) and a
vertical segment of length \(q_y\) in \(Q\), with
\[
\frac{a_2}{q_x}<\frac{25}{16},
\qquad
\frac{b_2}{q_y}<\frac{25}{16}.
\]
Since \(\ell_x\ge q_x\) and \(\ell_y\ge q_y\), all of (13) follows.
Substitution into (6) gives
\[
\Gamma
<
1+\frac1{64}
+
\frac{12}{25}
\left(\frac{25}{16}+\frac{25}{16}\right)
=\frac{161}{64}.
\]
Equations (8) and (11) now prove (14) and recover the accepted
triangle conclusion with a strictly stronger depth threshold.

The budget is affine-covariant in the relevant sense. Under every
positive determinant-one diagonal change
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(sx,y/s),
\]
the quantities \(a_h,w_P,\ell_x\) are multiplied by \(s\), while
\(b_h,h_P,\ell_y\) are divided by \(s\). Hence every term in
\(\Gamma\), the product in (7), and the conclusion (8) are invariant.

An individual mixed-cell area cannot by itself supply the missing
depth-to-chord bound. For any \(m,t>0\), the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(-t,0),(0,0),\left(0,\frac{2m}{t}\right)
\right\}
\tag{21}
\]
lies in the closed negative-positive quadrant, has area exactly \(m\),
and has negative \(x\)-depth \(t\). Thus a fixed mixed-cell area permits
arbitrarily large depth while its attaching chord shrinks like
\(1/t\). Consequently any unconditional theorem must obtain the
missing ratio from the simultaneous global convex placement, not from
that cap area alone. Conditions (6) and (9) add precisely the two
same-body depth products and the dimensionless depth-to-width
information needed to turn the universal inequalities (16)-(17) into
the depth alternative.
