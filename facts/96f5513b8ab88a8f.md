## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) be a compact polytope such that
\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
 =(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
where \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
A_1=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Write
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\qquad
g_i=\frac1{a_i}\int_{A_i}z\,dz,
\]
and, for \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y)=
\inf\left\{
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y
\right\}.
\]
Then there exists \(y^*\in S\) such that
\[
h_S(y^*)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

More precisely, a witness may be selected by the following ordered alternatives.

1. If \(a_k\ge T/2\) for some \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), one may take \(y^*=(k,g_k)\).

2. If every \(a_i<T/2\) and
\[
(\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2})^2
 +4\min\{a_0,a_2\}\ge2T,
\]
one may take
\[
y^*=\left(1,\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}\right).
\]

3. If the preceding alternatives do not apply and
\[
2\max\{a_0,a_2\}
\le a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
one may take \(y^*=(1,z)\) for some \(z\in A_1\).

4. If all three preceding criteria fail, one may take \(y^*=(1,g_1)\).

These alternatives supply potentially different witnesses. In particular, the middle-cover point \(z\) is not asserted to equal either \((g_0+g_2)/2\) or \(g_1\).

## Proof

Apply the alternatives in their stated order.

If some \(a_k\ge T/2\), accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` gives
\[
h_S((k,g_k))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

Suppose next that no slice has area at least \(T/2\). Then \(a_i<T/2\) for every \(i\). If
\[
(\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2})^2
 +4\min\{a_0,a_2\}\ge2T,
\]
accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` applies and gives
\[
h_S\left(\left(1,\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}\right)\right)
\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
The proposed point lies in \(S\) because
\[
\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}
\in\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=A_1.
\]

Suppose the first two criteria fail. If
\[
2\max\{a_0,a_2\}
\le a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
accepted fact `87035f4e1344144e` supplies some \(z\in A_1\) satisfying
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

It remains only the case in which all three criteria fail. Put
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
r=\frac mM,\qquad
s=\frac{a_1}{M},
\]
and
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}.
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` places these ratios in the residual domain
\[
0<r<\rho,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),
\]
where
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le9/25,\\[2pt]
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\[2pt]
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

Interchanging the outer labels if necessary, assume \(a_0=M\) and \(a_2=m\). This interchange is induced by the affine isometry
\[
(x,z)\longmapsto(2-x,z),
\]
which bijects closed halfspaces, preserves every slice area, fixes the middle slice, and therefore preserves halfspace depth. Set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
By the minimal-family hypothesis, \(B=A_1\), so \(|B|=a_1\), its centroid is \(g_B=g_1\), and the ratios used in accepted fact `18fa46bbd1c0c9d4` are exactly the residual pair \((r,s)\) above. That fact therefore gives
\[
h_S((1,g_1))
=h_S((1,g_B))
\ge\frac{2(M+m+|B|)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

The four ordered alternatives are exhaustive, so the required witness always exists. Their proofs invoke separate existence statements, and no identification of their witnesses is needed.
