## Statement

Assume the surviving \(N=3\) transformed coordinate-cap model supplied by accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `64760c76cadff481`.  Thus \(q>1\), \(0<A\leq B\), and \(P_0,P_1,P_2\) are nondegenerate triangles of respective areas \(A,1,B\) in
\[
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1+q\right\},\qquad
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1\right\},\qquad
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1-q\right\},
\]
with
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), put
\[
u_i=\frac{|P_0\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|}{A},\qquad
c_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|,\qquad
v_i=\frac{|P_2\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|}{B},
\]
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9,\qquad
M_i=Au_i+c_i+Bv_i,
\]
and assume
\[
M_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Let \(g_j\) be the area centroid of \(P_j\), and define
\[
I_j=\{i:(g_j)_i\geq0\}\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\]
The residual inequalities used below are
\[
A+B>1,\qquad B<1+A.
\]
Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` gives these inequalities, the nonempty disjoint sets \(I_0,I_1\), and their three possible orbits.  Then:

1. One has
\[
I_2\cap I_0=I_2\cap I_1=\varnothing
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{I_2=\varnothing}.
\]
Consequently
\[
v_i<\frac59\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]

2. Suppose, after a simultaneous coordinate permutation, that the singleton-singleton orbit is
\[
I_0=\{1\},\qquad I_1=\{2\}.
\]
Put
\[
h=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},\qquad
d_1=|P_1\cap\{x_1\geq1\}|.
\]
Then
\[
h_1>1,\qquad h_2<0,\qquad h_3<0,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\frac B9<d_1<c_1},
\qquad
\boxed{\frac49<c_2<1-\frac B9},
\qquad
\boxed{c_3<1-\frac B9}.
\]
The column-sum identity on \(P_1\) also gives
\[
\boxed{c_2+c_3\geq1-d_1}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
u_1>\frac49,\qquad
c_1+Bv_1<\frac{2(1+B-A)}9,
\]
\[
Au_2+Bv_2<\frac{2(A+B-1)}9.
\]

Let \(a\leq b\leq c\) be the sorted first-coordinate values at the three vertices of \(P_1\).  Then
\[
a+b+c<0,\qquad a<0,\qquad c>1,
\]
and the exact cap formulas are
\[
d_1=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(c-1)^2}{(c-a)(c-b)},&b\leq1,\\[2mm]
1-\dfrac{(1-a)^2}{(b-a)(c-a)},&b\geq1,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
c_1=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{c^2}{(c-a)(c-b)},&b\leq0,\\[2mm]
1-\dfrac{a^2}{(b-a)(c-a)},&b\geq0.
\end{cases}
\]
The two displayed branches agree at \(b=1\) and \(b=0\), respectively, and include repeated lower or upper vertex values.  In particular,
\[
\frac B9<d_1<c_1<\frac{2(1+B-A)}9.
\]
Accepted fact `291c24bb45bd4946` gives \(A/B<3/8\) for every counterexample, so all of these singleton-orbit restrictions hold throughout the exact remaining ratio range.

## Proof

We first record an elementary triangle centroid-cap consequence of the exact formulas in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.  Let \(T\) be a nondegenerate triangle, let \(f\) be a nonconstant affine functional on it, and subtract a threshold so that the threshold is zero.  Write the sorted vertex values as \(a\leq b\leq c\).  If the centroid value is nonnegative, then
\[
|\{x\in T:f(x)\geq0\}|\geq\frac49|T|,
\]
with strict inequality when the centroid value is positive.

Indeed, the claim is immediate if \(a\geq0\).  If \(a<0\leq b\), put \(x=-a>0\).  The nonnegative centroid condition gives \(b+c\geq x\), and the two-nonnegative exact cap branch gives
\[
\frac{|\{f\geq0\}|}{|T|}
=1-\frac{x^2}{(x+b)(x+c)}
\geq\frac12>\frac49.
\]
If \(b\leq0<c\), put \(x=-a\geq y=-b\geq0\).  The centroid condition gives \(c\geq x+y\), and the unique-positive cap fraction
\[
\frac{c^2}{(c+x)(c+y)}
\]
is increasing in \(c\).  At \(c=x+y\), comparison with \(4/9\) reduces exactly to
\[
9(x+y)^2-4(2x+y)(x+2y)=(x-y)^2\geq0.
\]
If the centroid value is positive, then \(c>x+y\) in this last case, so the inequality is strict.  This proves the claim, including zero and repeated-value boundaries.  Applying it to \(-f\) shows that if the centroid value of \(f\) is strictly negative, then the upper cap \(\{f\geq0\}\) has fraction strictly less than \(5/9\), because an affine coordinate is nonconstant on a nondegenerate triangle and its zero line has area zero.

Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` gives
\[
i\in I_0\Longrightarrow u_i\geq\frac49,\qquad
i\in I_1\Longrightarrow c_i\geq\frac49.
\]
The preceding triangle claim gives
\[
i\in I_2\Longrightarrow v_i\geq\frac49.
\]
If \(i\in I_0\cap I_2\), then
\[
M_i\geq\frac{4(A+B)}9
>t,
\]
because
\[
\frac{4(A+B)}9-t=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9>0.
\]
If \(i\in I_1\cap I_2\), then
\[
M_i\geq\frac{4(1+B)}9
>t,
\]
because
\[
\frac{4(1+B)}9-t=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9>0.
\]
This proves both disjointness assertions.

We now exclude every nonempty \(I_2\), including the only combinatorially possible leftover index in the singleton-singleton orbit.  Since every point of \(P_0\) has coordinate sum \(1+q>0\), its three coordinate-nonnegative caps cover \(P_0\), whence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3u_i\geq1.
\]
The identical column-sum argument on \(P_1\), whose coordinate sum is \(1\), gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3c_i\geq1.
\]
If \(k\in I_2\), then \(v_k\geq4/9\), so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3M_i
=A\sum_i u_i+\sum_i c_i+B\sum_i v_i
\geq A+1+\frac{4B}{9}.
\]
On the other hand, the three strict row inequalities give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3M_i<3t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}3.
\]
The lower bound is strictly larger, since
\[
A+1+\frac{4B}{9}
-\frac{2(1+A+B)}3
=\frac{3(1+A)-2B}{9}
>\frac{1+A}{9}>0,
\]
where \(B<1+A\) was used in the strict inequality.  This contradiction proves \(I_2=\varnothing\).  Thus every coordinate of \(g_2\) is strictly negative, and the triangle claim applied to the lower coordinate halfplane gives \(v_i<5/9\) for every \(i\).

Now assume \(I_0=\{1\}\) and \(I_1=\{2\}\).  Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` gives
\[
(g_0)_1>1+q,\qquad (g_0)_2<0,\qquad(g_0)_3<0,
\]
\[
(g_1)_1<0,\qquad(g_1)_2>1,\qquad(g_1)_3<0.
\]
Since \(I_2=\varnothing\), all three coordinates of \(g_2\) are negative.  Compatibility puts
\[
h=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}\in\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
It also gives \(h_2,h_3<0\), while the two endpoint coordinate sums give
\[
h_1+h_2+h_3
=\frac{(1+q)+(1-q)}2=1.
\]
Hence \(h_1>1\).

It is important here not to identify \(h\) with the centroid of the full Minkowski average, which need not be true.  Instead consider the two genuine translated homothets
\[
Q_2=\frac{g_0+P_2}{2},\qquad
Q_0=\frac{P_0+g_2}{2}.
\]
Both lie in \((P_0+P_2)/2\subseteq P_1\), both have centroid \(h\), and their areas are \(B/4\) and \(A/4\), respectively.  Applying the strict triangle centroid-cap claim to \(Q_2\) and the affine functional \(x_1-1\) gives
\[
|Q_2\cap\{x_1\geq1\}|>\frac49\frac B4=\frac B9.
\]
This cap lies in \(P_1\cap\{x_1\geq1\}\), proving \(d_1>B/9\).  Since \((g_1)_1<0\), \(P_1\) has points with \(x_1<0\); since \(d_1>0\), it has points with \(x_1>1\).  Nondegeneracy and convexity then give positive area in the strip \(0<x_1<1\), so \(d_1<c_1\).

For \(i=2,3\), apply the same strict centroid-cap claim on \(Q_2\) to \(-x_i\).  Since \(h_i<0\),
\[
|Q_2\cap\{x_i\leq0\}|>\frac B9.
\]
The coordinate line has zero area on \(P_1\), so
\[
1-c_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\leq0\}|>\frac B9,
\]
which proves \(c_2,c_3<1-B/9\).  The strict lower bound \(c_2>4/9\) follows from \((g_1)_2>1\) and the triangle claim.

For the column-sum cover, every \(x\in P_1\) satisfies
\[
x_1+x_2+x_3=1.
\]
If \(x_1<1\), then \(x_2+x_3>0\), so \(x_2>0\) or \(x_3>0\).  Up to the zero-area line \(x_1=1\), the set on the left has area \(1-d_1\).  It is covered by the second and third coordinate caps, proving
\[
c_2+c_3\geq1-d_1.
\]

The strict centroid coordinates \((g_0)_1>0\) and \((g_1)_2>0\) give
\[
u_1>\frac49,\qquad c_2>\frac49.
\]
Subtracting these contributions from \(M_1<t\) and \(M_2<t\) gives
\[
c_1+Bv_1
<t-\frac{4A}{9}
=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9,
\]
\[
Au_2+Bv_2
<t-\frac49
=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9.
\]

Finally, let \(a\leq b\leq c\) be the first-coordinate vertex values of \(P_1\).  The centroid condition \((g_1)_1<0\) is \(a+b+c<0\).  The inequality \(d_1>0\) forces \(c>1\), and hence \(a<0\).  Applying the complete exact triangle-cap formula of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` at thresholds \(1\) and \(0\) gives the four displayed branches for \(d_1\) and \(c_1\).  At \(b=1\) and \(b=0\), the adjacent expressions agree; the repeated-value formulas are the same limits and are literal because the triangle is nondegenerate.  Combining \(B/9<d_1<c_1\) with the first row budget gives
\[
\frac B9<d_1<c_1<\frac{2(1+B-A)}9.
\]
Accepted fact `291c24bb45bd4946` supplies the independent global restriction \(A/B<3/8\), completing the stated consequences on the remaining singleton-singleton search domain.
