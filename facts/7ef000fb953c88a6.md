## Statement

For
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-2,0),(1,-8),(8,-1)\},\\
A_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-8,-3),(2,7),(-8,-2)\},\\
A_1&=(A_0+A_2)/2,
\end{aligned}
\]
the point
\[
y_*=(1,c),\qquad c=\left(-1,-\frac15\right),
\]
belongs to \(S\) and satisfies
\[
h_S(y_*)\ge\frac{2147609}{117700}>\frac{655}{36}=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

The nine pairwise half-sums of vertices of \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) have convex hull
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-5,-\frac32\right),
\left(-\frac72,-\frac{11}{2}\right),
(5,3),
\left(0,\frac72\right),
(-5,-1)
\right\},
\]
in the displayed counterclockwise order. Its exact halfspace description is
\[
A_1=\{(u,v):L_i(u,v)\ge0,\ 1\le i\le5\},
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_1&=16u+6v+89,&
L_2&=-u+v+2,\\
L_3&=-u-10v+35,&
L_4&=9u-10v+35,\\
L_5&=u+5.
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(c=(-1,-1/5)\), these five expressions equal
\[
\frac{359}{5},\quad \frac{14}{5},\quad 38,\quad 28,\quad 4,
\]
respectively. Hence \(c\in A_1\), so \(y_*\in S\).

Let
\[
R(u,v)=2c-(u,v)=\left(-2-u,-\frac25-v\right).
\]
The reflected polygons needed below have descriptions
\[
R(A_1)=\{(u,v):M_i(u,v)\ge0,\ 1\le i\le5\},
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_1&=-80u-30v+273,&
M_2&=5u-5v+18,\\
M_3&=u+10v+41,&
M_4&=-9u+10v+21,\\
M_5&=-u+3,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
R(A_0)=\{(u,v):P_i(u,v)\ge0,\ 1\le i\le3\},
\]
where
\[
P_1=-40u-15v-6,\qquad
P_2=5u-5v+53,\qquad
P_3=u+10v+4.
\]
Also,
\[
A_2=\{(u,v):N_i(u,v)\ge0,\ 1\le i\le3\},
\]
where
\[
N_1=-u+v-5,\qquad
N_2=9u-10v+52,\qquad
N_3=u+8.
\]

Define the exact symmetric middle polygon
\[
B_1=A_1\cap R(A_1).
\]
Exact clipping by the ten inequalities above gives the following counterclockwise vertex list:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_1=\operatorname{conv}\biggl\{&
\left(-5,-\frac32\right),
\left(-\frac{46}{11},-\frac{81}{22}\right),
\left(-2,-\frac{39}{10}\right),
(-1,-3),
(3,1),\\
&\left(3,\frac{11}{10}\right),
\left(\frac{24}{11},\frac{361}{110}\right),
\left(0,\frac72\right),
\left(-1,\frac{13}{5}\right),
\left(-5,-\frac75\right)
\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For an exact check, the successive active boundary pairs are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(L_1,L_5),\ (L_1,M_3),\ (M_3,M_4),\ (L_2,M_4),\
(L_2,M_5),\\
(M_1,M_5),\ (L_3,M_1),\ (L_3,L_4),\
(L_4,M_2),\ (L_5,M_2).
\end{gathered}
\]
Direct substitution gives nonnegative values for every remaining inequality. The oriented shoelace sum is
\[
\sum_i\det(v_i,v_{i+1})=\frac{3573}{55},
\]
and therefore
\[
|B_1|=\frac{3573}{110}.
\tag{1}
\]
By construction, \(R(B_1)=B_1\).

Next define
\[
B_2=A_2\cap R(A_0).
\]
Exact clipping gives
\[
B_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{54}{11},\frac1{11}\right),
\left(-\frac{81}{55},\frac{194}{55}\right),
\left(-\frac{168}{107},\frac{2026}{535}\right),
\left(-\frac{28}{5},\frac4{25}\right)
\right\},
\]
again in counterclockwise order. The successive active boundary pairs are
\[
(N_1,P_3),\qquad (N_1,P_1),\qquad
(N_2,P_1),\qquad (N_2,P_3),
\]
and direct substitution verifies the two remaining inequalities \(N_3\ge0\) and \(P_2\ge0\). Its oriented shoelace sum is
\[
\sum_i\det(w_i,w_{i+1})=\frac{118027}{29425},
\]
so
\[
|B_2|=\frac{118027}{58850}.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
B_0=R(B_2).
\]
Since \(B_2\subseteq R(A_0)\), applying \(R\) gives \(B_0\subseteq A_0\), while \(B_2\subseteq A_2\) holds by definition.

Consider
\[
B=(\{0\}\times B_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B_1)\cup(\{2\}\times B_2)
\subseteq S
\]
and let \(\nu\) be two-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to \(B\). Define central inversion about \(y_*\) by
\[
J(x,w)=(2-x,R(w)).
\]
It exchanges \(\{0\}\times B_0\) and \(\{2\}\times B_2\), preserves
\(\{1\}\times B_1\), and preserves planar area. Thus \(J(B)=B\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu(B)
&=|B_1|+2|B_2|\\
&=\frac{3573}{110}
  +2\frac{118027}{58850}\\
&=\frac{2147609}{58850}\\
&=\frac{655}{18}+\frac{27553}{264825}
>\frac{655}{18}.
\tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(K\) be any proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(y_*\). Its image \(J(K)\) is the opposite closed halfspace. Since \(\nu\) is \(J\)-invariant,
\[
\nu(K)=\nu(J(K)).
\]
Moreover,
\[
K\cup J(K)=\mathbb R^3,\qquad
K\cap J(K)=\partial K.
\]
Inclusion-exclusion therefore gives
\[
2\nu(K)
=\nu(B)+\nu(B\cap\partial K)
\ge\nu(B).
\]
Because \(B\subseteq S\), (3) yields
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap K)
\ge\nu(K)
\ge\frac{\nu(B)}2
=\frac{2147609}{117700}
=\frac{655}{36}+\frac{27553}{529650}.
\tag{4}
\]

Finally, for any closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(y_*\), accepted fact
`400272f6de6c67bb` supplies a boundary-through-\(y_*\) halfspace
\(K\subseteq H\). Hence
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)
\ge\mathcal H_2(S\cap K)
\ge\frac{2147609}{117700}
>\frac{655}{36}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves the claim.
