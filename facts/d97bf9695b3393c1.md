## Statement

Use the six rays and sector names of accepted fact `f93a19566af88e9a`:
\[
r_0=(1,0),\quad r_1=(0,1),\quad r_2=(-1,1),\quad
r_3=(-1,0),\quad r_4=(0,-1),\quad r_5=(1,-1),
\]
with sectors \(P,W_x,N_x,O,N_y,W_y\) in cyclic order. Let \(K\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon containing \(0\), let \(S=|K|>0\), and denote its six sector areas by the corresponding capital letters. Suppose
\[
N_x-W_y=N_y-W_x=:e>0,
\qquad
\int_Kx\,dA\ge0,\qquad \int_Ky\,dA\ge0.
\]
Put
\[
C=e+W_x+W_y+O,\qquad d=P+W_x+W_y.
\]
Thus
\[
C=|K\cap\{x\le0\}|=|K\cap\{y\le0\}|,
\qquad e=S-C-d.
\]
If
\[
d\ge\frac{4S}{9},
\]
then
\[
C^2\ge4ed.
\]
The conclusion includes polygons for which \(0\) lies on the boundary and polygons having genuine vertices on fan rays.

## Proof

Uniform dilation about \(0\) multiplies \(S,C,d,e\) by the same factor and the deficit by its square. Normalize
\[
S=1,\qquad
\Phi(K)=C^2-4ed=C^2-4d(1-C-d).
\]
Suppose for contradiction that a strict counterexample \(K_0\) exists. Set
\[
\delta=-\Phi(K_0)>0
\]
and let \(N\) be the number of genuine vertices of \(K_0\).

We first prove that all feasible polygons with at most \(N\) genuine vertices and
\[
\Phi\le-\delta                                                     \tag{1}
\]
have bounded diameter. From (1),
\[
4ed\ge C^2+\delta.
\]
Since \(d\le1\) and \(e=N_x-W_y\le N_x\le C\),
\[
e\ge\frac{\delta}{4},\qquad C\ge\frac{\delta}{4}.                 \tag{2}
\]

Assume instead that a sequence \(K_m\) in this negative sublevel has diameter \(D_m\to\infty\). Choose an oriented diameter direction \(v_m\), pass to a subsequence with \(v_m\to v\), and use the longitudinal coordinate
\[
s=\frac{z\cdot v_m}{D_m}.
\]
The projection interval \(I_m\) has length one and contains \(0\). Let \(w_m(s)\) be the length of the perpendicular chord at longitudinal coordinate \(D_ms\). Convexity makes \(w_m\) concave, and
\[
f_m(s)=D_mw_m(s)
\]
is a nonnegative concave probability density on \(I_m\). If \(M_m=\max f_m\), the two chords from a maximizer to the interval endpoints lie below \(f_m\) and enclose a triangle of area \(M_m/2\). Hence
\[
f_m\le2.                                                         \tag{3}
\]

The rescaled sets \(D_m^{-1}K_m\) have diameter one, contain \(0\), and have area \(D_m^{-2}\). A bounded Hausdorff subsequence converges to a convex set of area zero containing a diameter segment of length one; it is therefore a segment parallel to \(v\). For every linear form \(\ell\) with \(\ell(v)\ne0\), the area fraction of \(\{\ell\le0\}\) consequently converges to either
\[
a:=\lim_m\int_{I_m\cap(-\infty,0)}f_m(s)\,ds
\]
or \(1-a\), according to the sign of \(\ell(v)\). Indeed, away from a shrinking interval about \(s=0\), the sign agrees with that of \(s\ell(v)\), while (3) bounds the mass of the discarded interval.

Apply this to \(x,y,x+y\). If \(v_x,v_y\) have the same nonzero sign, then \(C+d\to1\), so \(e\to0\). If \(v_xv_y<0\) and \(v_x+v_y\ne0\), balance makes the two complementary longitudinal masses equal, hence \(a=1/2\); then \(C,d\to1/2\) and again \(e\to0\). If \(v\) lies on a coordinate fan ray, one coordinate cut can retain transverse information, but balance identifies it with the other coordinate cut, while the \(x+y\) cut is the complementary longitudinal cut. Thus \(C+d\to1\) in this case as well.

The only remaining direction is \(v\parallel(-1,1)\). The \(x\)- and \(y\)-cuts are complementary longitudinal cuts, so balance gives
\[
C\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
After a further subsequence, \(d\) converges to a point of \([4/9,1/2]\), the upper endpoint following from \(e=1-C-d\ge0\). Therefore
\[
\lim\Phi
=\frac14-4d\left(\frac12-d\right)
\ge\frac14-\frac8{81}
=\frac{49}{324}>0.                                               \tag{4}
\]
In every other direction \(e\to0\) and \(\lim\Phi=C^2\ge0\). Both conclusions contradict the uniform bound (1). The fixed-vertex negative sublevel has bounded diameter.

Take its Hausdorff closure while allowing \(0\in K\), rather than requiring \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\). A bounded Hausdorff limit of convex polygons with at most \(N\) vertices has at most \(N\) genuine vertices; collisions and collinearities merely lower that number. Area, first moments, and areas cut by the three fixed lines are continuous under bounded convex Hausdorff convergence. Hence the closed negative sublevel is compact.

No polygon in this compact negative sublevel can have \(0\) on its boundary. Such a polygon lies in a closed halfplane through \(0\), so the directions of its nonzero points lie in a closed semicircle. Balance and \(e>0\) give
\[
N_x=e+W_y>0,\qquad N_y=e+W_x>0.                                  \tag{5}
\]
Thus the semicircle contains an interior direction of \(N_x\), with angle strictly between \(3\pi/4\) and \(\pi\), and an interior direction of \(N_y\), with angle strictly between \(3\pi/2\) and \(7\pi/4\). It must use the arc through \(O\) and cannot contain an interior direction of \(P\). Hence
\[
P=0.
\]
Using \(d=W_x+W_y\) and balance, the total area is
\[
1=2d+2e+O.
\]
Consequently
\[
d+e\le\frac12,\qquad C=1-d-e\ge\frac12.
\]
Since \(d\ge4/9\),
\[
4de\le4d\left(\frac12-d\right)\le\frac8{81},
\]
and therefore
\[
\Phi\ge\frac14-\frac8{81}=\frac{49}{324}>0,
\]
contrary to (1). Thus every member of the compact negative sublevel has \(0\) in its interior.

The continuous function \(\Phi\) now attains its minimum on the sublevel at a polygon \(K_*\) with \(0\in\operatorname{int}K_*\). Delete repeated and collinear entries from a limiting vertex list, leaving its genuine vertices. Strict convexity at each genuine vertex and containment of \(0\) are open conditions in this finite-dimensional vertex parameterization. A nearby feasible polygon with deficit below \(\Phi(K_*)\) would still satisfy (1), so \(K_*\) is a local minimizer of \(\Phi\) among feasible polygons with its genuine-vertex count.

Both centroid inequalities are strict at \(K_*\). The negative-feasible-point argument in accepted fact `70dbfdc8fc4b6f51` does not use fan transversality: if
\[
\int_{K_*}x\,dA=0,
\]
the centroid halfplane bound gives \(C\ge4/9\); the same follows from the \(y\)-moment by balance. On \(C,d\ge4/9\),
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial C}\bigl(C^2-4d(1-C-d)\bigr)=2C+4d>0,
\]
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\bigl(C^2-4d(1-C-d)\bigr)
=4(C+2d-1)>0,
\]
and
\[
\Phi(C,d)\ge\Phi(4/9,4/9)=0.
\]
This contradicts \(\Phi(K_*)<0\). Hence the two moment constraints and the condition \(e>0\) are open at \(K_*\). The only active constraints are area normalization, balance, and possibly \(d=4/9\).

We separate the two fan incidences.

First suppose every fan ray meets the relative interior of a boundary edge of \(K_*\). Then no vertex lies on a fan ray and every crossing is transverse. The polygon is a negative local minimizer in the smooth stratum of accepted fact `70dbfdc8fc4b6f51`. That fact reduces such a minimizer to a triangle whose vertices occupy one of the alternating parity classes
\[
P,N_x,N_y
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
W_x,O,W_y.
\]
For the first class, accepted fact `9fdf961ec71bd241` gives
\[
C^2\ge4ed,
\]
contradicting \(\Phi(K_*)<0\). For the second class, the nonnegative centroid moments and accepted fact `ca6c285cd23ef4dc` give \(e\le0\), contradicting \(e>0\). Thus the minimizer cannot be fan-transverse.

It remains to suppose at least one fan ray meets a genuine vertex of \(K_*\). No edge lies on a fan ray, since its supporting line would contain the interior point \(0\). Sector areas are continuously differentiable at this endpoint stratum: moving a ray vertex from one adjacent sector to the other transfers only a triangle whose base and height are both first order, so the two local polynomial formulas differ by second order.

Use the cyclic sector order
\[
(A,B,C_0,D,E,F)=(O,N_y,W_y,P,W_x,N_x)
\]
and the indicator vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_x&=(1,0,0,0,1,1),\\
h_y&=(1,1,1,0,0,0),\\
h_t&=(0,0,1,1,1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(L\) be the derivative of the six sector areas with respect to arbitrary velocities of the genuine vertices. Accepted fact `2cf161fab8bf9e2b` gives
\[
\ker L^T\cap
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,h_x,h_y,h_t\}
=\{0\},                                                         \tag{6}
\]
and, in particular, says that the pullbacks by \(L\) of
\[
\mathbf1,\qquad h_x-h_y,
\]
and, when \(d=4/9\), \(h_t\), are linearly independent. Thus the ordinary multiplier rule applies on the endpoint stratum.

Put
\[
c=h_x\cdot s=h_y\cdot s=C,\qquad d=h_t\cdot s,
\]
where \(s\) is the six-sector area vector. There are \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R\) and, only if \(d=4/9\), a multiplier \(\mu\ge0\) such that
\[
L^Tq=0,                                                         \tag{7}
\]
where
\[
q=(2c+4d)h_x+
\bigl(4(c+2d-1)-\mu\bigr)h_t
-\alpha\mathbf1-\beta(h_x-h_y).                                 \tag{8}
\]
If \(d>4/9\), take \(\mu=0\). The four vectors
\[
\mathbf1,\ h_x,\ h_y,\ h_t
\]
are linearly independent. In (8), the sum of the \(h_x\)- and \(h_y\)-coefficients is \(2c+4d>0\), so
\[
q\ne0.
\]
But \(q\) belongs to the four-dimensional space in (6), and (6) contradicts (7). Hence no endpoint-stratum negative local minimizer exists.

Both possible fan incidences are impossible. Therefore the assumed strict counterexample \(K_0\) does not exist, and every polygon in the statement satisfies \(C^2\ge4ed\).

The compactness argument was carried out only inside the class of polygons with the fixed vertex bound inherited from an alleged counterexample. It proves the required polygonal globalization and handles collisions and degenerating polygon sequences, but it makes no claim that arbitrary convex bodies can be approximated while preserving the exact constraints.
