## Statement

Write points of \(\mathbb R^3\) as \((x,s,t)\), put
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/3,-1),(1/3,-1),(0,2)\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^2,
\qquad \eta=\frac1{10},
\]
and define
\[
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\eta\Delta,
\]
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup
(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr).
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact polytope satisfying
\[
C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
The three fiber areas and their sum are
\[
|A_0|=|A_1|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\frac1{100},
\qquad T=\frac{201}{100}.
\]

Let
\[
Q=[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]\times\{0\}^2,\qquad K=C+Q,
\]
\[
p=(5/2,0,0),\qquad
\rho=\frac{474}{569},\qquad
D=\rho K+(1-\rho)p.
\]
Then \(p\in K\), \(D\subseteq K\), and the centroid of \(D\) is
\[
y=(1,0,0)\in S.
\]
For the closed halfspace
\[
H=\{(x,s,t):2x+t\ge2\},
\]
one has \(y\in\partial H\) and the exact ratios
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}T=\frac{409}{1809},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(D\cap H)}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(D)}=\frac{9248}{18723}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}T
<
\frac12\,\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(D\cap H)}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(D)}.
\]

For comparison, if \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) is any compact set and
\(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(B\ominus Q)
\le \mathcal H_2(B\cap\Lambda)
\le \operatorname{vol}_3(B+Q),
\]
where \(B\ominus Q=\{z:z+Q\subseteq B\}\). Applied to
\(B=C\cap H\), this gives the valid directions
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3((C\cap H)\ominus Q)
\le\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)
\le\operatorname{vol}_3((C\cap H)+Q).
\]

## Proof

The base of \(\Delta\) has length \(2/3\) and its altitude has length
\(3\), so \(|\Delta|=1\). Its centroid is the average of its vertices,
namely \((0,0)\), and \(0\in\Delta\). For every convex set \(E\) containing
the origin and all \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\),
\[
\alpha E+\beta E=(\alpha+\beta)E.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the inclusion from left to right follows from convexity, and the
reverse inclusion follows by taking the same point of \(E\) in both
summands. Hence
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac{\Delta+\eta\Delta}{2}
=\frac{11}{20}\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{2}
\]

We verify all sections of \(C\), not only the three integer sections.
After collecting terms at equal heights, a point of \(C\) at height \(x\)
is obtained from coefficients \(\lambda_i\ge0\) satisfying
\[
\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1,\qquad
\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=x.
\]
For fixed coefficients, (1) shows that the possible planar components are
\[
(\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\eta\lambda_2)\Delta
=\bigl(1-(1-\eta)\lambda_2\bigr)\Delta.
\tag{3}
\]
These homothetic copies are nested. If \(0\le x\le1\), the feasible range
contains \(\lambda_2=0\), which maximizes the coefficient in (3), so
\[
C_x=\Delta.
\]
If \(1\le x\le2\), the equations and nonnegativity give
\(\lambda_2\ge x-1\), with equality feasible. Therefore
\[
C_x=\left(1-\frac9{10}(x-1)\right)\Delta.
\tag{4}
\]
There are no sections outside \(0\le x\le2\). Equations (3)--(4) prove
that the sections at \(0,1,2\) are exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\), and the
projection \([0,2]\) contains no other integer height. This proves the
claimed identity for \(S\). Scaling planar area by the square of the
linear scale gives
\[
|A_0|=|A_1|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\eta^2=\frac1{100},
\]
and hence \(T=201/100\).

The section of \(K=C+Q\) at height \(x\) is the union of \(C_u\) over
\(u\in[x-\tfrac12,x+\tfrac12]\cap[0,2]\). The sections in (4) are nested,
so
\[
K_x=r(x)\Delta,\qquad
r(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&-\tfrac12\le x\le\tfrac32,\\[1mm]
1-\dfrac9{10}(x-\tfrac32),
&\tfrac32\le x\le\tfrac52.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, \(K_{5/2}=\eta\Delta\) contains \(0\), so \(p\in K\).
Convexity then gives \(D\subseteq K\).

Every planar section in (5) has centroid \(0\). Its area is \(r(x)^2\),
and therefore
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(K)
=2+\int_0^1(1-\tfrac9{10}u)^2\,du
=2+\frac{37}{100}
=\frac{237}{100}.
\tag{6}
\]
The first-coordinate moment is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_Kx\,d(x,s,t)
&=\int_{-1/2}^{3/2}x\,dx
+\int_0^1(\tfrac32+u)(1-\tfrac9{10}u)^2\,du\\
&=1+\frac{263}{400}
=\frac{663}{400}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the centroid of \(K\) is \((c,0,0)\), where
\[
c=\frac{663/400}{237/100}=\frac{221}{316}.
\tag{8}
\]
Centroids commute with an invertible affine map by a direct change of
variables. Hence the centroid of \(D=\rho K+(1-\rho)p\) is
\[
\rho(c,0,0)+(1-\rho)p.
\]
Its first coordinate is
\[
\frac{474}{569}\frac{221}{316}
+\frac{95}{569}\frac52
=\frac{663+475}{2\cdot569}=1.
\tag{9}
\]
This proves that the centroid is \(y=(1,0,0)\). Since \(0\in A_1\), one
also has \(y\in S\).

We next compute the mixed cap. On the slice \(x=0\), the inequality
defining \(H\) is \(t\ge2\), so \(H\) meets \(\Delta\) only in its top
vertex and contributes area zero. On the slice \(x=1\), it is \(t\ge0\).
The resulting vertex triangle is similar to \(\Delta\) with linear ratio
\(2/3\), hence has area \(4/9\). On the slice \(x=2\), it is \(t\ge-2\).
The \(t\)-coordinates of \(\eta\Delta\) range from \(-1/10\) to \(1/5\),
so this entire slice lies in \(H\) and contributes \(1/100\). Consequently
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)=\frac49+\frac1{100}
=\frac{409}{900},
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}T
=\frac{409}{1809}.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(F:K\to D\) be \(F(z)=\rho z+(1-\rho)p\). For
\(L(x,s,t)=2x+t\), one has
\[
L(F(z))=\rho L(z)+5(1-\rho).
\]
Thus \(F(z)\in H\) exactly when
\[
L(z)\ge5-\frac3\rho.
\]
Using \(\rho=474/569\) and (8),
\[
5-\frac3\rho
=5-\frac{569}{158}
=\frac{221}{158}=2c.
\tag{11}
\]
It follows that
\[
D\cap H=F(K\cap H'),
\qquad
H'=\{(x,s,t):2x+t\ge2c\}.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(-1/2\le x\le3/2\), the section \(K_x\) is \(\Delta\), and the
section of \(H'\) is \(t\ge2(c-x)\). The cap area is therefore zero for
\(x\le c-1\), equals
\[
\left(\frac{2-2(c-x)}3\right)^2
=\frac{4(x+1-c)^2}{9}
\]
for \(c-1\le x\le c+1/2\), and equals \(1\) for
\(x\ge c+1/2\). The breakpoints lie inside the constant part because
\[
-\frac12<c-1<c+\frac12<\frac32.
\]
For \(3/2\le x\le5/2\), the threshold satisfies
\[
2(c-x)\le2c-3=-\frac{253}{158}<-1.
\]
Every section there is \(r(x)\Delta\) with \(0<r(x)\le1\), whose minimum
\(t\)-coordinate is \(-r(x)\ge-1\). Hence the entire tapered part lies
in \(H'\). Its volume is the tail integral \(37/100\) from (6). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_3(K\cap H')
&=\int_{c-1}^{c+1/2}
\frac{4(x+1-c)^2}{9}\,dx
+\int_{c+1/2}^{3/2}1\,dx+\frac{37}{100}\\
&=\frac12+(1-c)+\frac{37}{100}
=\frac{2312}{1975}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The map \(F\) multiplies every three-dimensional volume by \(\rho^3\).
Equations (6), (12), and (13) thus give
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(D\cap H)}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(D)}
=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(K\cap H')}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(K)}
=\frac{2312/1975}{237/100}
=\frac{9248}{18723}.
\tag{14}
\]
Finally,
\[
409\cdot18723=7657707
<8364816=4624\cdot1809.
\]
Combining this with (10) and (14) proves
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}T
=\frac{409}{1809}
<
\frac{4624}{18723}
=\frac12\,\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(D\cap H)}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(D)}.
\tag{15}
\]

It remains to justify the valid comparison directions. For a compact
\(B\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), write
\[
B_i=\{w\in\mathbb R^2:(i,w)\in B\}
\]
and form the cell union
\[
U_B=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb Z}
\bigl((i+[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12])\times B_i\bigr).
\]
Only finitely many \(B_i\) are nonempty. The cell interiors at distinct
integer heights are disjoint, so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(U_B)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}|B_i|
=\mathcal H_2(B\cap\Lambda).
\tag{16}
\]
If \((x,w)\in B\ominus Q\), choose an integer \(i\) with
\(|x-i|\le1/2\). Then \((i,w)\in B\), so \((x,w)\in U_B\). Conversely,
if \((x,w)\in U_B\), then \((x,w)=(i,w)+(x-i,0,0)\) for some
\((i,w)\in B\) and \((x-i,0,0)\in Q\), so \((x,w)\in B+Q\).
Consequently
\[
B\ominus Q\subseteq U_B\subseteq B+Q.
\]
Taking volumes and using (16) proves
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(B\ominus Q)
\le\mathcal H_2(B\cap\Lambda)
\le\operatorname{vol}_3(B+Q).
\]
For \(B=C\cap H\), one has
\[
B\cap\Lambda=S\cap H,
\]
which gives the displayed erosion and thickening bounds in the statement.
