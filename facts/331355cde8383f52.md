## Statement

Use the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  Let \(N=\{j,k,\ell\}\), put
\[
L_j=L_k=L_\ell=-\frac56,
\qquad
f_j=X-\frac56,\qquad
f_\ell=Y-\frac56,\qquad
f_k=\frac23-X-Y,
\]
and define
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{21}{71},\frac{43}{142}\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac7{10}\right),
\left(\frac45,\frac35\right),
\left(\frac{29}{19},-\frac3{133}\right)
\right\}.
\]
In the same \((X,Y)\)-plane define
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{523}{75},\frac{20}{3}\right),
\left(-\frac{1109}{150},\frac{20}{3}\right),
\left(\frac{361}{150},-\frac13\right),
\left(\frac{212}{75},-\frac13\right)
\right\},
\qquad
K=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Then the positive-area endpoint cells are exactly
\[
P_j,P_\varnothing,P_\ell,P_{k\ell},
\qquad
Q_j,Q_{jk},Q_N,Q_{k\ell},
\]
and the positive-area middle cells of \(K\) are exactly
\[
R_j,R_{jk},R_N,R_{k\ell}.
\]
All twelve displayed cells have positive area.

For the \(j\ell\)-row and the notation of accepted fact
`b431dbefa8a4aec7`, take the nondegenerate separator
\[
a=1,\qquad d=\frac75.
\]
Its data are
\[
|B_{j\ell}|=\frac{27}{50},\qquad
\chi_{j\ell}=\frac{41}{25},\qquad
\lambda_{0j}=\frac14,\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}=\frac1{10},
\]
\[
\lambda_{2j}=\frac3{10},\qquad
\lambda_{2\ell}=\frac{21}{50}.
\]
Both \(Q\)-traces are therefore retained, the separator is one of the
nondegenerate rows contemplated in accepted fact
`decc0d9db44fe9e5`, and
\[
\Phi_{j\ell}=\frac{773}{2800}
<\frac{27}{50}=|B_{j\ell}|.
\]
Moreover the third-direction traces satisfy
\[
\lambda_{0k}=\frac1{46}<\frac{21}{20}=\lambda_{2k}.
\]
Thus all three directions satisfy the strict first trace alternative of
accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc`.  Nevertheless
\[
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap R_{j\ell}\right|=0.
\]

More generally, write
\[
X=p_j,\qquad Y=p_\ell,\qquad
u=-q_j,\qquad v=-q_\ell,
\]
and let \(A_0^{XY}\) and \(A_2^{uv}\) be the two endpoint bodies in
these affine coordinates.  Put
\[
E=A_2^{uv}-A_0^{XY}.
\]
For two-dimensional endpoint bodies, the midpoint body has zero area
in the strict \(R_{j\ell}\)-chamber if and only if there are
\(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), not both zero, such that
\[
\max_{(u,v)\in A_2^{uv}}(\alpha u+\beta v)
-\min_{(X,Y)\in A_0^{XY}}(\alpha X+\beta Y)
\le 2\min(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{1}
\]
When the full central common-order middle support is positive, both
coefficients in (1) must be positive.  Hence the exact additional
condition needed to force positive \(R_{j\ell}\) midpoint area is the
strict negation of (1) for every positive support direction.  The
row-area inequality \(|B_{j\ell}|>\Phi_{j\ell}\) does not imply that
condition.

## Proof

The affine functions sum to \(-1\), their gradients are pairwise
nonparallel, and
\[
L_j+L_k+L_\ell=-\frac52<-1.
\]
Thus
\[
p_j=X,\qquad p_\ell=Y,\qquad
p_k=\frac32-X-Y.
\tag{2}
\]
The first polygon is the exact rational \(A_0\) of accepted fact
`dfae5c73d3b1782a`.  That fact proves that its only positive \(P\)-cells
are \(P_j,P_\varnothing,P_\ell,P_{k\ell}\), all positive, and that
\[
B_{j\ell}
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(0,\frac14\right),
\left(0,\frac12\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac7{10}\right),
\left(\frac45,\frac35\right),
\left(\frac32,0\right),
\left(\frac75,0\right)
\right\},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
|B_{j\ell}|=\frac{27}{50},\qquad
\lambda_{0j}=\frac14,\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}=\frac1{10}.
\tag{4}
\]
The same fact gives the \(p_k=0\) chord endpoints
\[
\left(\frac32,0\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{35}{23},-\frac1{46}\right).
\]
Its Euclidean length is \(\sqrt2/46\), while
\(\|\nabla f_k\|=\sqrt2\) and the common determinant magnitude is
\(1\).  Hence
\[
\lambda_{0k}=\frac1{46}.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(A_2\), use the affine coordinates
\[
u=-q_j=\frac53-X,\qquad
v=-q_\ell=\frac53-Y,\qquad
w=-q_k=\frac72-u-v.
\tag{6}
\]
The four displayed vertices of \(A_2\) become
\[
\left(\frac{216}{25},-5\right),\quad
\left(\frac{453}{50},-5\right),\quad
\left(-\frac{37}{50},2\right),\quad
\left(-\frac{29}{25},2\right).
\]
Equivalently, \(A_2^{uv}\) is the parallelogram
\[
-5\le v\le2,\qquad
\frac{41}{25}\le u+\frac75v\le\frac{103}{50}.
\tag{7}
\]
For a fixed value
\[
h=u+\frac75v,
\]
one has
\[
u=h-\frac75v,\qquad
u+v=h-\frac25v.
\tag{8}
\]
As \(v\) increases from \(-5\) to \(2\), the three cutting lines are
met in the order
\[
w=0
\quad\text{at}\quad
v=\frac52\left(h-\frac72\right)\in
\left[-\frac{93}{20},-\frac{18}{5}\right],
\]
\[
v=0,
\qquad
u=0
\quad\text{at}\quad
v=\frac57h\in
\left[\frac{41}{35},\frac{103}{70}\right].
\tag{9}
\]
All three crossing values lie strictly between \(-5\) and \(2\), in
the displayed order.  Therefore (7), up to its null cutting lines, is
partitioned into exactly the four positive chambers
\[
Q_j,\qquad Q_{jk},\qquad Q_N,\qquad Q_{k\ell}.
\tag{10}
\]

The two axis sections of \(\overline{Q_N}\) are
\[
A_2^{uv}\cap\{u=0\}
=\{0\}\times
\left[\frac{41}{35},\frac{103}{70}\right],
\]
\[
A_2^{uv}\cap\{v=0\}
=\left[\frac{41}{25},\frac{103}{50}\right]\times\{0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Their lengths are \(3/10\) and \(21/50\), proving the two stated
\(A_2\)-traces.  On \(w=0\), equations (7) and (8) give a normalized
chord length
\[
\frac{(103/50)-(41/25)}{(7/5)-1}
=\frac{21}{20},
\tag{12}
\]
which proves the third stated trace.  Equations (4), (5), (11), and
(12) give strict trace inequalities in all three directions, exactly
the strict first alternative required by accepted fact
`eddda25c734d34bc`.

On the six vertices in (3), the values of \(X+\frac75Y\) are
\[
\frac7{20},\quad
\frac7{10},\quad
\frac{32}{25},\quad
\frac{41}{25},\quad
\frac32,\quad
\frac75.
\]
Thus
\[
X+\frac75Y\le\frac{41}{25}
\quad\text{on }B_{j\ell}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equation (7) gives
\[
u+\frac75v\ge\frac{41}{25}
\quad\text{on }\overline{Q_N}.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence \(a=1,d=7/5\) is an actual nondegenerate \(j\ell\)-row
separator in the one common plane, not an independently chosen row
model.  Its support value is \(\chi_{j\ell}=41/25\).  Using the
definition of \(\Phi\) from accepted fact `b431dbefa8a4aec7` and (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{j\ell}
&=\frac12\left(
\frac{41}{25}\frac14+
\frac{41}{25}\frac57\frac1{10}+
\frac1{40}\right)\\
&=\frac{773}{2800}
<\frac{1512}{2800}
=\frac{27}{50}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
This proves strict bulging with retained traces and a nondegenerate
separator, the two row properties automatically supplied in the
general setting by accepted fact `decc0d9db44fe9e5`.

It remains to determine the whole midpoint support.  For
\((X,Y)\in A_0\), direct evaluation at the four vertices gives
\[
\min_{A_0}\left(X+\frac75Y\right)=\frac{91}{710},
\qquad
\max_{A_0}\left(X+\frac75Y\right)=\frac{41}{25}.
\tag{16}
\]
For a midpoint of \((X,Y)\in A_0\) and a point of \(A_2\) having
coordinates \((u,v)\), put
\[
e_1=u-X,\qquad e_2=v-Y.
\]
Equations (7) and (16) give
\[
0\le e_1+\frac75e_2
\le\frac{103}{50}-\frac{91}{710}
=\frac{3429}{1775}<2.
\tag{17}
\]
Affinity and (2), (6) give
\[
2f_j=-e_1,\qquad
2f_\ell=-e_2,\qquad
2f_k=e_1+e_2-2
\tag{18}
\]
at that midpoint.

The strict chamber \(R_k\) would have \(e_1<0,e_2<0\), contradicting
the lower bound in (17).  The strict chamber \(R_\ell\) would have
\[
e_1<0,\qquad e_2>0,\qquad e_1+e_2>2,
\]
and therefore
\[
e_1+\frac75e_2
=(e_1+e_2)+\frac25e_2>2,
\]
contradicting the upper bound in (17).  The strict chamber
\(R_{j\ell}\) would have
\[
e_1>0,\qquad e_2>0,\qquad e_1+e_2>2,
\]
and gives the same contradiction.  Thus no unsupported strict middle
chamber meets \(K\).

All four supported middle chambers do occur with positive area.  The
point
\[
x=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)
\]
is interior to \(A_0\): its consecutive edge determinants in the
displayed vertex order are
\[
-\frac{141}{710},\quad
-\frac2{25},\quad
-\frac{69}{266},\quad
-\frac{1229}{1988}.
\]
The following four points, written in \((u,v)\)-coordinates, are
interior to \(A_2\), since each has \(h=17/10\) and
\(-5<v<2\):
\[
\left(\frac{167}{20},-\frac{19}{4}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{31}{10},-1\right),\quad
\left(\frac{43}{50},\frac35\right),\quad
\left(-\frac25,\frac32\right).
\]
Subtracting \(x\) gives respectively
\[
\left(\frac{157}{20},-\frac{21}{4}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{13}{5},-\frac32\right),\quad
\left(\frac9{25},\frac1{10}\right),\quad
\left(-\frac9{10},1\right).
\tag{19}
\]
Their coordinate sums are
\[
\frac{13}{5},\qquad
\frac{11}{10},\qquad
\frac{23}{50},\qquad
\frac1{10}.
\]
By (18), the four corresponding interior midpoints lie strictly in
\[
R_j,\qquad R_{jk},\qquad R_N,\qquad R_{k\ell},
\]
respectively.  Since sums of interior endpoint points are interior to
the Minkowski sum, each of these four cells has positive area.
Together with the three exclusions above, this proves the exact middle
support.  The cutting lines themselves have planar area zero, as in
accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  In particular,
\[
|K\cap R_{j\ell}|=0
\]
despite (15).

Finally, we prove the exact support-gap criterion (1).  Under the
invertible affine map from the midpoint plane to the
\((e_1,e_2)\)-plane given by (18), the midpoint body maps onto
\[
E=A_2^{uv}-A_0^{XY}.
\]
The strict \(R_{j\ell}\)-chamber maps onto the open convex set
\[
C=\{(e_1,e_2):e_1>0,\ e_2>0,\ e_1+e_2>2\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Because \(E\) is a two-dimensional compact convex body, its
intersection with the open set \(C\) is nonempty if and only if that
intersection has positive area.  Hence zero forbidden midpoint area is
equivalent to \(E\cap C=\varnothing\).

Weak separation of the compact convex set \(E\) from the open convex
set \(C\) gives a nonzero functional
\((\alpha,\beta)\) with
\[
\max_E(\alpha e_1+\beta e_2)
\le\inf_C(\alpha e_1+\beta e_2).
\tag{21}
\]
Both coefficients are nonnegative, since a negative coefficient makes
the right side of (21) equal to \(-\infty\).  Conversely, every
nonnegative nonzero coefficient pair satisfies
\[
\inf_C(\alpha e_1+\beta e_2)
=2\min(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{22}
\]
The left side of (21) is
\[
\max_{A_2^{uv}}(\alpha u+\beta v)
-\min_{A_0^{XY}}(\alpha X+\beta Y).
\]
Thus (21)-(22) are exactly (1), and either direction of this argument
also proves sufficiency.  In a full central common-order support,
positive \(R_N\) area supplies points of \(E\) with both coordinates
positive, so neither coefficient in (1) can vanish.

For the displayed counterexample, the direction
\[
(\alpha,\beta)=\left(1,\frac75\right)
\]
has exact support gap
\[
\frac{103}{50}-\frac{91}{710}
=\frac{3429}{1775}
<2=2\min\left(1,\frac75\right).
\]
This is the quantitative reason its forbidden midpoint chamber is
empty.  Strict bulging (15) leaves this full-body support gap
uncontrolled.  No scalar crossing, canonical-tail, finite-interior, or
\(W=0\) completion is asserted; any exclusion using those additional
hypotheses must derive a strict violation of (1) from them rather than
from row bulging alone.
