## Statement

Assume the residual normalization
\[
a_2=xa_0,\qquad a_1=ya_0,\qquad
0<x\leq\frac9{25},\qquad 1-x<y<1+x,
\]
where \(A_0,A_1\) are nondegenerate triangles, \(A_0\) is the larger endpoint, and \(A_2\) is an arbitrary compatible two-dimensional convex body.  Let \(g_1\) be the centroid of \(A_1\), let \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) be the affine barycentric coordinate functions of \(A_0\) on the whole plane, and put
\[
K:=2A_1\ominus A_0
:=\{v\in\mathbb R^2:A_0+v\subseteq 2A_1\},
\qquad
\rho:=\sqrt{\frac{1+x-y}{18}}.
\]
If
\[
\Gamma(A_0,A_1):=
\min_{\substack{v\in K\\1\leq i\leq3}}
\alpha_i(2g_1-v)
\geq \rho,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,g_1))\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]
This conclusion holds for every compatible \(A_2\) of the prescribed area; condition (1) involves only the two triangles \(A_0,A_1\).

The strict version of (1) is nonempty on a genuinely open set.  More precisely, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac23,-\frac13\right),
\left(-\frac13,\frac23\right),
\left(-\frac13,-\frac13\right)
\right\},
\qquad
B=\frac1{13}
\begin{pmatrix}
-12&-5\\
5&-12
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
For every
\[
(x,y)\in\mathcal R:=
\left\{(x,y):0<x<\frac1{100},\quad
1-x<y<1-\frac x2\right\},
\tag{4}
\]
put \(b=\sqrt y\), \(A_0=\Delta\), and \(A_1=bB\Delta\).  Then \(a_1/a_0=y\), condition (1) is strict, and (2) holds at \((1,0)\) for every compatible \(A_2\) with \(|A_2|=x|\Delta|\).  Such \(A_2\) exist for every pair in (4).  Every displayed pair has no edge of \(A_0\) parallel to an edge of \(A_1\), and every displayed pair has a neighborhood in the full labeled-triangle parameter space on which (1) remains strict.

## Proof

We first prove a planar triangle estimate.  Let \(D\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(A\), with affine barycentric coordinates \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\).  If \(p\in D\) satisfies
\[
\beta_i(p)\geq r\quad(i=1,2,3),
\qquad 0\leq r\leq\frac13,
\tag{5}
\]
then
\[
d_D(p)\geq4r^2A.
\tag{6}
\]

It suffices to consider a line through \(p\).  Let the line cut off vertex \(i\), let \(j,k\) be the other two indices, and put
\[
a=\beta_j(p),\qquad b=\beta_k(p),\qquad
c=\beta_i(p)=1-a-b.
\]
If the line meets the two edges from vertex \(i\) at fractions \(s,t\in(0,1]\), measured from \(i\), then its isolated triangular side has relative area \(st\), and
\[
\frac as+\frac bt=1.
\tag{7}
\]
Writing \(X=a/s\), one has
\[
a\leq X\leq1-b,\qquad
st=\frac{ab}{X(1-X)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Since \(X(1-X)\leq1/4\), (5) gives
\[
st\geq4ab\geq4r^2.
\tag{9}
\]
The concavity of \(X(1-X)\) on \([a,1-b]\) also gives
\[
st\leq
\max\left\{\frac b{1-a},\frac a{1-b}\right\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
1-st\geq
\min\left\{\frac c{1-a},\frac c{1-b}\right\}
\geq\frac r{1-r}\geq4r^2.
\tag{10}
\]
For the middle inequality, \(c\geq r\), while
\(1-a=c+b\leq1-r\) and \(1-b=c+a\leq1-r\).
The last inequality follows from \(4r(1-r)\leq8/9<1\) for
\(0<r\leq1/3\); the case \(r=0\) is immediate.  Thus both closed sides of every line through \(p\) have area at least \(4r^2A\), proving (6).  The same calculation includes a line through a vertex by allowing \(s=1\) or \(t=1\).

At the centroid \(g_D\), all three barycentric coordinates equal \(1/3\).  Applying (6) with \(r=1/3\), and using the vertex cap
\(\{\beta_i\geq1/3\}\), whose relative area is \((2/3)^2\), yields the exact identity
\[
d_D(g_D)=\frac49A.
\tag{11}
\]

We now prove the routing criterion.  Define
\[
\eta:=\frac{2a_0(1+x-y)}9=4\rho^2a_0.
\tag{12}
\]
The residual lower bound \(y>1-x\) gives
\[
0<1+x-y<2x\leq\frac{18}{25},
\]
so \(\rho<1/5<1/3\), and the triangle estimate applies.  Condition (1) and (6) imply
\[
2g_1-K\subseteq D_{A_0}(\eta).
\tag{13}
\]

Compatibility gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Equivalently, every \(v\in A_2\) satisfies \(A_0+v\subseteq2A_1\), so
\[
A_2\subseteq K.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (13) and (14) give
\[
2g_1-A_2\subseteq D_{A_0}(\eta).
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{\eta}{a_0}
=\frac{2(1+x-y)}9
<\frac{4x}{9}<x,
\tag{16}
\]
so \(\eta<a_2\).

Apply the reflected-depth routing bound of accepted fact
`996c3218c6534822` at \(z=g_1\), using its route
\(2g_1-A_2\subseteq D_{A_0}(\eta)\).  Equations (11), (15), and (16) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{A_1}(g_1)+\min\{a_2,\eta\}
&=\frac{4a_1}{9}+\eta\\
&=\frac{4ya_0}{9}
  +\frac{2a_0(1+x-y)}9\\
&=\frac{2a_0(1+x+y)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The residual inequalities also put all three areas in the strict balanced regime:
\[
1<x+y,\qquad y<1+x,\qquad x<1+y.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence the two horizontal terms in the same accepted bound exceed
\(T/2>2T/9\).  This proves (2).

It remains to verify the claimed open subregion exactly.  The barycentric coordinates of \(\Delta\) in (3) are
\[
\alpha_1(z)=z_1+\frac13,\qquad
\alpha_2(z)=z_2+\frac13,\qquad
\alpha_3(z)=\frac13-z_1-z_2.
\tag{19}
\]
The matrix \(B\) has determinant \(1\), satisfies \(B^{-1}=B^{\mathsf T}\), and fixes the origin.  Thus \(A_1=bB\Delta\) has centroid \(0\) and area
\[
|A_1|=b^2|\Delta|=y|\Delta|.
\tag{20}
\]
Let
\[
\lambda_i(w)=\alpha_i(B^{-1}w/b)
\]
be the barycentric coordinate functions of \(A_1\).  Direct evaluation at the three vertices of \(\Delta\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_1:=\min_{\Delta}\lambda_1
  &=\frac13-\frac{29}{39b},\\
m_2:=\min_{\Delta}\lambda_2
  &=\frac13-\frac{19}{39b},\\
m_3:=\min_{\Delta}\lambda_3
  &=\frac13-\frac{24}{39b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
For example, the linear parts of
\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) attain their minima at the first, second, and third displayed vertices of \(\Delta\), with respective values
\(-29/(39b),-19/(39b),-24/(39b)\).

Because \(\lambda_i(0)=1/3\), a point \(z\) belongs to \(2A_1\) exactly when
\(\lambda_i(z)\geq-1/3\) for all \(i\), and
\[
\lambda_i(a+v)=\lambda_i(a)+\lambda_i(v)-\frac13.
\]
It follows that
\[
K=\{v:\lambda_i(v)\geq-m_i,\ i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{22}
\]
The unused barycentric mass in (22) is
\[
s:=1+m_1+m_2+m_3
=2-\frac{24}{13b}
=\frac{2(13b-12)}{13b}.
\tag{23}
\]
For (4), one has
\[
\frac{99}{100}<b<1,
\tag{24}
\]
because \(1-x<y<1\) and \(x<1/100\).  In particular \(s>0\), and \(K\) is a nondegenerate triangle.  Let \(q_i\) be its vertex whose \(A_1\)-barycentric coordinates are
\[
\lambda_j(q_i)=-m_j+s\,\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substituting (21) into (25), then applying \(bB\), gives the following exact table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\alpha_1(-q_i)&\alpha_2(-q_i)&\alpha_3(-q_i)\\ \hline
i=1&
\dfrac{38b}{39}-\dfrac{84}{169}&
-\dfrac{44b}{39}+\dfrac{204}{169}&
\dfrac{2b}{13}+\dfrac{49}{169}\\[3mm]
i=2&
-\dfrac{4b}{39}+\dfrac{84}{169}&
\dfrac{58b}{39}-\dfrac{204}{169}&
-\dfrac{18b}{13}+\dfrac{289}{169}\\[3mm]
i=3&
-\dfrac{34b}{39}+\dfrac{204}{169}&
-\dfrac{14b}{39}+\dfrac{84}{169}&
\dfrac{16b}{13}-\dfrac{119}{169}.
\end{array}
\tag{26}
\]
Every entry is affine in \(b\).  Checking the appropriate endpoint of
\([99/100,1]\) in each of the nine entries shows that their lower bounds are
\[
\frac{3951}{8450},\ \frac{40}{507},\ \frac{3737}{8450},\
\frac{200}{507},\ \frac{2241}{8450},\ \frac{55}{169},\
\frac{170}{507},\ \frac{70}{507},\ \frac{2173}{4225}.
\tag{27}
\]
Their minimum is \(40/507\).  Since (24) is strict and the relevant endpoint for that minimum is \(b=1\), (26) gives
\[
\min_{\substack{v\in K\\1\leq i\leq3}}\alpha_i(-v)
>\frac{40}{507}
>\frac1{30}.
\tag{28}
\]
Here the minimum over \(K\) occurs at a vertex because each \(\alpha_i\) is affine.

For the area band (4),
\[
\rho^2=\frac{1+x-y}{18}
<\frac{2x}{18}
<\frac1{900}.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus \(\rho<1/30\).  Since \(g_1=0\), equations (28) and (29) prove the strict form of (1).

The triangle \(K\) is a homothetic copy of \(A_1\) in its barycentric coordinates, with linear ratio \(s\).  Equations (20) and (23) give
\[
\frac{|K|}{|\Delta|}
=s^2b^2
=\left(2b-\frac{24}{13}\right)^2.
\tag{30}
\]
By (24),
\[
2b-\frac{24}{13}
>\frac{87}{650},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{|K|}{|\Delta|}
>\frac{7569}{422500}
=\frac1{100}+\frac{836}{105625}
>\frac1{100}>x.
\tag{31}
\]
Let \(k\) be the centroid of \(K\) and put
\[
\theta=\sqrt{\frac{x|\Delta|}{|K|}}\in(0,1),\qquad
A_2=k+\theta(K-k).
\tag{32}
\]
Then \(A_2\) is a nondegenerate triangle, \(A_2\subseteq K\), and
\(|A_2|=x|\Delta|\).  By the definition of \(K\),
\((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\).  Therefore the convex hull of the three displayed slices is a compact polytope with exactly these integer-height sections.  This proves that every area pair in (4) has at least one compatible \(A_2\), while the routing proof above applies to every compatible \(A_2\), not only (32).

For completeness, this is a genuine open relative-position class.  Near the displayed family, \(K\) remains nondegenerate.  Its three facet normals and offsets depend continuously on the vertices of \(A_0,A_1\), so its three vertices do as well.  The barycentric functions and \(g_1\) also depend continuously on those vertices.  Therefore \(\Gamma(A_0,A_1)\) is continuous there.  The strict margins (28)-(29), the strict area inequalities (4), and the strict capacity inequality (31) persist under sufficiently small independent perturbations of both triangles.

Finally, the class is not one of the already closed special cases.  The residual description in accepted fact `d1498a01819203fa` shows that (4) avoids every large-slice case, and
\[
x+y<1+\frac x2<2
\]
avoids the hybrid regime of accepted facts `452b737aaf90a400` and
`d1498a01819203fa`.  The aligned edge-frame budget in accepted fact
`2e7881b9f943653e` would require
\[
1-y\leq\frac x2,
\]
whereas (4) gives the strict reverse inequality.

The edge directions of \(\Delta\) may be represented by
\[
(1,0),\qquad(0,1),\qquad(1,-1).
\]
Their images under \(B\) are proportional to
\[
(-12,5),\qquad(-5,-12),\qquad(-7,17),
\]
none of which is parallel to any original edge direction.  Thus the pair is not aligned and cannot satisfy the homothetic hypotheses of accepted facts
`aef70a60593c53a2` or `ea0056993c3a877d`.  It also cannot satisfy
\(A_1=(A_0+A_2)/2\), the hypothesis of accepted fact
`96f5513b8ab88a8f`: every edge direction of \(A_0\) occurs in the Minkowski sum \(A_0+A_2\), while \(2A_1\) has only the three nonparallel directions displayed above.  The proof instead uses the exact reflected-depth route of
`996c3218c6534822`, consistently with the middle profile in accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240`.
