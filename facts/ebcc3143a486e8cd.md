## Statement

Use the normalized affine-endpoint systems \(\mathcal S_{\eta,\mathrm F,\mathrm I,\sigma}\) of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781` and the reduced word automaton of accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b`. Thus
\[
\eta\in\{0,1,2\},\qquad
\sigma_P\in\mathcal A,\quad
\sigma_Q\in\mathcal B,\quad
\sigma_R\in\mathcal C.
\]
For a four-letter word \(w=w_1w_2w_3w_4\), call \(w_1w_2\) its first half and \(w_3w_4\) its second half.

Every feasible system \(\mathcal S_{\eta,\mathrm F,\mathrm I,\sigma}\) satisfies, for each of the two halves, all of the following:

1. the corresponding half of \(\sigma_P\) is not \(\mathrm{PP}\);

2. the corresponding half of \(\sigma_R\) is neither \(\mathrm{NN}\) nor \(\mathrm{PP}\);

3. if the corresponding half of \(\sigma_Q\) is \(\mathrm{PP}\), then the corresponding half of \(\sigma_P\) is either \(\mathrm{NN}\) or \(\mathrm{PN}\).

Consequently, deleting every reduced full/interior system that violates this filter preserves the feasibility union. Exactly
\[
100806
\]
of the \(157344\) reduced full/interior systems are deleted, leaving exactly
\[
56538.
\]
Combined with accepted fact `b23633f36a9edcd1`, the complete affine list then has exactly
\[
266442
\]
systems remaining.

## Proof

Fix a feasible system. All normalized variables and identities below are those of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781`. In particular,
\[
m=q^2,\qquad 9t=2(1+m+k),
\]
and, because the \(Q\)-tail is full,
\[
b=m.
\]

We first derive row-wide inequalities without using the endpoint words.

Since
\[
\ell_P=-\delta,\qquad
\ell_Q\ge D=\delta+2,
\]
every \(p\in P\) and \(q'\in Q\) satisfy
\[
\frac{p_x+q'_x}{2}\ge1.
\]
Midpoint inclusion therefore gives
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R\cap\{x\ge1\}.
\tag{1}
\]

We use the following elementary planar area inequality:
\[
\left|\frac{K+L}{2}\right|
\ge
\left(\frac{\sqrt{|K|}+\sqrt{|L|}}2\right)^2
\tag{2}
\]
for positive-area convex bodies \(K,L\).

For completeness, let \(f(x)\) and \(g(y)\) be the lengths of their vertical slices, and put
\[
A=\int f=|K|,\qquad B=\int g=|L|.
\]
Let \(T\) be the increasing transport defined by
\[
\frac1B\int_{-\infty}^{T(x)}g
=
\frac1A\int_{-\infty}^{x}f.
\]
On the interiors of the projection intervals,
\[
T'(x)=\frac{Bf(x)}{Ag(T(x))}
\]
almost everywhere. At
\[
z(x)=\frac{x+T(x)}2,
\]
the vertical slice of \((K+L)/2\) contains the average of the two corresponding slice intervals, hence has length at least
\[
\frac{f(x)+g(T(x))}{2}.
\]
Since \(z'=(1+T')/2\), change of variables gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\frac{K+L}{2}\right|
&\ge
\frac14\int
 \bigl(f+g(T)\bigr)(1+T')\,dx\\
&=
\frac14\left(
 A+B+\int\bigl(fT'+g(T)\bigr)\,dx
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover,
\[
fT'+g(T)
=
\frac{Bf^2}{Ag(T)}+g(T)
\ge2f\sqrt{\frac BA}.
\]
Its integral is at least \(2\sqrt{AB}\), proving (2). Vanishing endpoint slices are handled by restricting to the open projection intervals and taking endpoint limits.

The raw areas of \(P,Q\) are \(\Lambda,\Lambda q^2\). Applying (2) to (1), whose right-hand side has normalized area \(d\), yields
\[
d\ge\frac{(1+q)^2}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]

The exact overlap identity in accepted fact `b046e3066136e781` is
\[
\Omega=2t-b-d,\qquad \Omega\ge0.
\]
Using \(b=m=q^2\),
\[
d\le2t-m=\frac{4-5q^2+4k}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3) and (4) gives
\[
16k\ge29q^2+18q-7.
\tag{5}
\]

The residual-strip inequality
\[
5q^2+2q-5<0
\]
and \(q>0\) imply \(q<1\). Therefore (5) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
9(t-m)
&=2+2k-7q^2\\
&\ge
2+\frac{29q^2+18q-7}{8}-7q^2\\
&=
\frac{9(1+2q-3q^2)}8\\
&=
\frac{9(1-q)(3q+1)}8>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
m<t.
\tag{6}
\]

We also have
\[
t<k.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, this is equivalent to
\[
7k>2(1+m).
\]
If \(\eta=0\), then \(q\le3/5\) and \(k>1-q^2\), so
\[
7k-2(1+q^2)
>
5-9q^2
\ge\frac{44}{25}>0.
\]
If \(\eta=1\) or \(2\), then \(q>3/5\) and
\[
2k>3q^2+2q-1.
\]
Thus
\[
7k-2(1+q^2)
>
\frac{17q^2+14q-11}{2}
>
\frac{44}{25}>0,
\]
where the last inequality follows because the numerator is increasing for \(q>0\) and equals \(88/25\) at \(q=3/5\).

Finally,
\[
t<1.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(\eta=0\), \(q\le3/5\), while for \(\eta=1\), \(2q^2\le1\). In both cases
\[
k<1+q^2\le2-q^2.
\]
For \(\eta=2\), \(k<2-q^2\) is explicit. Hence always
\[
1+m+k<3,\qquad t<\frac23<1.
\]

Let
\[
\alpha_E=|E\cap\{y\le0\}|/\Lambda,\qquad
\beta_E=|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|/\Lambda.
\]
The crossing and tail constraints of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781` give
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t,
\]
\[
\alpha_R+m\ge t,\qquad
\beta_R+m\ge t.
\]
All caps are nonnegative. By (6) and (7),
\[
0<t-m\le\alpha_R,\beta_R\le t<k.
\tag{9}
\]
Also, by (8),
\[
\alpha_P,\beta_P\le t<1.
\tag{10}
\]

We now use the endpoint words.

For the first half of a word, \(\mathrm{NN}\) forces the \(y\le0\) cap to be zero, while \(\mathrm{PP}\) forces it to equal the whole body area. Indeed, \(\mathrm{NN}\) makes \(-L_E\le0\), so \(L_E\ge0\); \(\mathrm{PP}\) makes \(-U_E\ge0\), so \(U_E\le0\). For the second half, the same statements hold for the \(x+y\ge0\) cap: \(\mathrm{NN}\) makes \(U_E+x\le0\), while \(\mathrm{PP}\) makes \(L_E+x\ge0\).

Thus (10) excludes a \(\mathrm{PP}\) half in \(\sigma_P\), proving rule 1. Equation (9) excludes both \(\mathrm{NN}\) and \(\mathrm{PP}\) halves in \(\sigma_R\), proving rule 2.

Suppose the first half of \(\sigma_Q\) is \(\mathrm{PP}\). Then
\[
\alpha_Q=m.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_R=t-m.
\]
Together with \(\alpha_R\ge t-m\) and \(\alpha_P\ge0\), this forces
\[
\alpha_P=0.
\tag{11}
\]
A positive-area convex body with zero \(y\le0\) cap lies in \(y\ge0\). Hence \(-L_P\) and \(-U_P\) are nonpositive at both endpoints. Positive area makes \(-U_P\) nonzero somewhere, so its label is \(\mathrm N\); the label of \(-L_P\) is \(\mathrm N\), unless it is identically zero, in which case the boundary convention of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781` labels it \(\mathrm P\). Therefore the first half of \(\sigma_P\) is \(\mathrm{NN}\) or \(\mathrm{PN}\).

The identical argument with the halfplane \(x+y\ge0\) shows that a \(\mathrm{PP}\) second half of \(\sigma_Q\) forces \(\beta_P=0\), and hence the second half of \(\sigma_P\) is \(\mathrm{NN}\) or \(\mathrm{PN}\). This proves rule 3.

It remains to count the retained systems from the tables in accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b`.

First impose rule 1 on \(\mathcal A\). The prefix-\(\mathrm{PP}\) row has \(3\) words. A suffix \(\mathrm{PP}\) occurs in the \(\mathrm{NN}\), \(\mathrm{XN}\), and \(\mathrm{XX}\) rows, also giving \(3\) words. These sets are disjoint because \(\mathrm{PPPP}\notin\mathcal A\). Thus \(48\) of the \(54\) words remain. Among them, \(4\) are \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only, \(6\) are \(\pi\)-fixed, and \(2\) are both fixed and \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only.

Next impose rule 2 on \(\mathcal C\). Prefixes \(\mathrm{NN}\) and \(\mathrm{PP}\) contribute \(3+9=12\) words. Suffixes \(\mathrm{NN}\) and \(\mathrm{PP}\) contribute \(3+9=12\). Their intersection consists of
\[
\mathrm{NNPP},\qquad \mathrm{PPNN},\qquad \mathrm{PPPP}.
\]
Hence \(21\) words are deleted and \(33\) remain. Exactly one remaining word, \(\mathrm{PNPN}\), is \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only. Of the six fixed words, only \(\mathrm{PPPP}\) is deleted, so \(5\) fixed words remain, exactly one of them \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only.

Now impose rule 3 on the \(48\) retained \(P\)-words and all \(36\) \(Q\)-words. For one fixed half, there are \(9\) \(Q\)-words with that half equal to \(\mathrm{PP}\). There are \(34\) retained \(P\)-words whose corresponding half is outside \(\{\mathrm{NN},\mathrm{PN}\}\). Thus each half forbids
\[
34\cdot9=306
\]
pairs.

The intersection of the two forbidden sets has \(Q\)-word \(\mathrm{PPPP}\). The eligible retained \(P\)-words with both halves outside \(\{\mathrm{NN},\mathrm{PN}\}\) are counted from the \(\mathrm{PX},\mathrm{PY},\mathrm{XN},\mathrm{XX},\mathrm{YN},\mathrm{YY}\) rows as
\[
4+2+6+6+4+2=24.
\]
Therefore rule 3 deletes
\[
306+306-24=588
\]
of the \(48\cdot36\) pairs, leaving
\[
1140.
\tag{12}
\]
None of the \(588\) deleted pairs is \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only, so \(4\cdot6=24\) such pairs remain. Among fixed pairs, rule 3 deletes the four pairs having \(Q\)-word \(\mathrm{PPPP}\) and \(P\)-word
\[
\mathrm{PXPX},\quad
\mathrm{XNXN},\quad
\mathrm{XXXX},\quad
\mathrm{YNYN}.
\]
Thus \(6\cdot4-4=20\) fixed pairs remain, including all
\[
2\cdot2=4
\]
fixed \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only pairs.

Combining (12) with the \(33\) retained \(R\)-words gives
\[
1140\cdot33=37620
\]
label vectors. Of these,
\[
24\cdot1=24
\]
are \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only, so the mixed-vector count is
\[
37620-24=37596.
\]
There are
\[
20\cdot5=100
\]
fixed vectors, of which
\[
4\cdot1=4
\]
are \(\{\mathrm N,\mathrm P\}\)-only. Hence the fixed mixed-vector count is \(96\).

All three filtering rules are invariant under the half-swap involution \(\pi\). The lexicographic rule in accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b` therefore retains exactly
\[
\frac{37596+96}{2}=18846
\]
systems for each \(\eta\). The original full/interior count is \(52448\) per \(\eta\), so the filter deletes
\[
3(52448-18846)=100806
\]
systems and leaves
\[
3\cdot18846=56538.
\]
Every deleted system was proved infeasible, while every retained system is literally an original system, so the feasibility union is unchanged.

Finally, accepted fact `b23633f36a9edcd1` deletes all \(104784\) full/full systems. Therefore the complete affine list now has
\[
472032-104784-100806=266442
\]
systems.
