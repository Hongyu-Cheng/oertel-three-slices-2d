## Statement

Let
\[
E=\{(s_1,s_2,s_3)\in\mathbb R^3:s_1+s_2+s_3=1\},
\]
equipped with a fixed positive constant multiple of planar Lebesgue measure. Let \(P,K\subset E\) be nondegenerate triangles and let \(L\subset E\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body such that
\[
|P|=A,\qquad |K|=\mu,\qquad |L|=A-\mu,
\qquad 0<\mu<A.
\]
Let \(g_P,g_K\) be the centroids of \(P,K\), put
\[
z=\frac{g_P+g_K}{2},
\]
and assume
\[
z_1,z_2<0<z_3.
\]
For \(\tau\in\mathbb R^3\), define
\[
\ell_i=|L\cap\{s_i>0\}|,
\qquad
p_i=|P\cap\{s_i>\tau_i\}|,
\qquad
k_i=|K\cap\{s_i>-\tau_i\}|
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Assume the row-\(3\) support-contact conditions
\[
\tau_3=\max_{x\in P}x_3,
\qquad
-\tau_3<\min_{x\in K}x_3,
\]
and assume all three paired-cap rows are strict:
\[
\ell_i+p_i+k_i<\frac{4A}{9}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
If \(c=(c_1,c_2,c_3)\) is the centroid of \(L\), then
\[
c_3<0.
\]
Consequently, some \(j\in\{1,2\}\) has \(c_j>0\). For every \(j\in\{1,2\}\) with \(c_j>0\),
\[
\ell_j\ge\frac{4(A-\mu)}9,
\qquad
p_j+k_j<\frac{4\mu}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact 0d7c62845adeb53c proves the planar centroid halfplane bound needed here: if \(C\) is a compact two-dimensional convex body with centroid \(g\), then every closed planar halfplane containing \(g\) and having \(g\) on its boundary cuts from \(C\) an area of at least \(4|C|/9\).

Apply this bound to \(L\). For each \(i\), the closed halfplane
\[
Q_i=\{s\in E:s_i\ge c_i\}
\]
has boundary through the centroid \(c\), and therefore
\[
|L\cap Q_i|\ge\frac{4|L|}{9}.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(c_i>0\), then \(Q_i\subseteq\{s_i>0\}\). If \(c_i=0\), the difference between \(Q_i=\{s_i\ge0\}\) and \(\{s_i>0\}\) lies in the line \(\{s_i=0\}\subset E\), which has planar area zero. Thus, for every \(i\) with \(c_i\ge0\),
\[
\ell_i\ge\frac{4|L|}{9}
=\frac{4(A-\mu)}9.
\tag{2}
\]

The strict contact convention determines the endpoint caps in row \(3\) exactly. Since
\[
\tau_3=\max_{x\in P}x_3,
\]
the strict cap \(P\cap\{s_3>\tau_3\}\) is empty, so \(p_3=0\). Since
\[
-\tau_3<\min_{x\in K}x_3,
\]
every point of \(K\) satisfies \(s_3>-\tau_3\), so \(k_3=|K|=\mu\). Strictness of row \(3\) now gives
\[
\ell_3+\mu<\frac{4A}{9},
\]
and hence
\[
\ell_3<\frac{4A}{9}-\mu
=\frac{4(A-\mu)}9-\frac{5\mu}{9}
<\frac{4(A-\mu)}9.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(c_3\ge0\), equation (2) with \(i=3\) contradicts (3). Therefore
\[
c_3<0.
\tag{4}
\]

Because \(L\subset E\), its centroid also belongs to \(E\), so
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3=1.
\]
Together with (4), this yields
\[
c_1+c_2=1-c_3>1.
\]
In particular, some \(j\in\{1,2\}\) has \(c_j>0\).

Fix any \(j\in\{1,2\}\) with \(c_j>0\). Equation (2) gives
\[
\ell_j\ge\frac{4(A-\mu)}9.
\tag{5}
\]
Strictness of row \(j\), followed by (5), gives
\[
p_j+k_j
<\frac{4A}{9}-\ell_j
\le\frac{4A}{9}-\frac{4(A-\mu)}9
=\frac{4\mu}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (4), (5), and (6) prove all claimed conclusions. No statement about the other negative row follows from this argument.
