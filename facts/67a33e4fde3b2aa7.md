## Statement

Use the notation introduced in accepted fact `48df266dc82dd70d`, but not its conclusion restricted to \(\alpha\leq 10^{-5}\).  Explicitly, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/100\\[2pt]1/100&1+1/10000\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B=g+M(\Delta-g),
\]
and let \(Q\) have cyclic vertices
\[
\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
Set
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=B,\qquad A_2=Q,
\qquad
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Moreover, with
\[
p=|Q|=\frac{27}{16000},\qquad
T=\frac{16027}{16000},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000},
\]
one has the exact identity
\[
h_S((1,g))=\frac29+p
=t+\frac{21}{16000}>t.
\]
Consequently no collection of closed halfspaces of \(S\)-mass strictly below \(t\), even a collection of arbitrary cardinality, can cover \(\{1\}\times B\) by its interiors.  In particular the middle-slice requirement in accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` is impossible for this fixed triple.

## Proof

The determinant of \(M\) is
\[
1\left(1+\frac1{10000}\right)-\frac1{10000}=1.
\]
Thus \(B\) is a triangle of area \(1/2\) and centroid \(g\).  Direct shoelace evaluation on the displayed vertices of \(Q\), equivalently the area calculation recorded in accepted fact `a58dc34569c34d62` at its parameter \(1/20\), gives
\[
|Q|=\frac12\cdot\frac{27}{8000}=\frac{27}{16000}=p.
\]
This proves the displayed values of \(T\) and \(t\).

We first audit compatibility without using the small-shear conclusion of accepted fact `48df266dc82dd70d`.  Since
\[
M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}10001/10000&-1/100\\-1/100&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the barycentric coordinates of \((x,y)\) relative to \(B\) are
\[
X=\frac{10001}{10000}x-\frac1{100}y+\frac{33}{10000},
\qquad
Y=-\frac1{100}x+y+\frac1{300},
\qquad Z=1-X-Y.
\]
Thus \(B=\{X\geq0,Y\geq0,Z\geq0\}\).  Because these functions are affine, their minima on each polygon occur at vertices.  Exact substitution gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\min X&\min Y&\min Z\\ \hline
\Delta&-67/10000&-1/150&49/15000\\
Q&10561/200000&151/3000&119051/300000.
\end{array}
\]
For \(d\in\Delta\) and \(q\in Q\), affinity therefore gives
\[
X\!\left(\frac{d+q}{2}\right)\geq\frac{9221}{400000}>0,
\quad
Y\!\left(\frac{d+q}{2}\right)\geq\frac{131}{6000}>0,
\quad
Z\!\left(\frac{d+q}{2}\right)\geq\frac{120031}{600000}>0.
\]
Hence \((\Delta+Q)/2\subseteq B\), proving exact midpoint compatibility.

We next compute the one geometric error term needed below.  On \(\Delta\), the inequality \(Z\geq0\) is automatic by the preceding table.  The cut \(Y<0\) removes the triangle with vertices
\[
(1,0),\qquad \left(\frac13,0\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{301}{303},\frac2{303}\right),
\]
whose area is \(2/909\).  The cut \(X<0\) removes the triangle with vertices
\[
(0,1),\qquad \left(0,\frac{33}{100}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{67}{10101},\frac{10034}{10101}\right),
\]
whose area is \(4489/2020200\).  These triangles are disjoint because
\[
X+Y=\frac{9901}{10000}x+\frac{99}{100}y+\frac{199}{30000}>0
\quad\text{on }\Delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
\delta:=|\Delta\setminus B|
=\frac2{909}+\frac{4489}{2020200}
=\frac{2706967}{612120600}<\frac1{200}.
\tag{1}
\]

We recall and verify the exact planar cap estimates used in accepted fact `a58dc34569c34d62`.  Let \(\ell\) be a nonzero linear functional with \(\ell(g)=0\).  After relabeling the vertices of \(\Delta\), its ordered vertex values are
\[
-A,\qquad A-D,\qquad D,
\]
where \(A,D>0\) and \(1/2\leq\rho:=D/A\leq2\).  If \(1/2\leq\rho\leq1\), the relative area of \(\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}\) is
\[
1-\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}\geq\frac12.
\]
If \(1\leq\rho\leq2\), the relative areas of the caps at levels \(0\) and \(A\) are respectively
\[
\frac{\rho^2}{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)},
\qquad
\frac{(\rho-1)^2}{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{\rho^2+(\rho-1)^2}{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}-\frac{12}{25}
=\frac{26(\rho-31/26)^2+1/26}{25(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}
\geq\frac1{5850}.
\]
Here the last inequality uses \((1+\rho)(2\rho-1)\leq9\) for
\(1\leq\rho\leq2\).
Multiplying relative areas by \(|\Delta|=1/2\), and noting that \(1/4>6/25+1/11700\) in the first range, proves
\[
|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|+|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq A\}|
\geq\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}.
\tag{2}
\]
The same formulas give the centroid-cap estimate
\[
|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|\geq\frac29;
\tag{3}
\]
in the second range the relative area minus \(4/9\) is exactly
\[
\frac{(\rho-2)^2}{9(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}\geq0,
\]
while the first range gives the stronger relative bound \(1/2\).  Pulling a halfplane through \(g\) back from \(B\) by the determinant-one map \(M\) shows from (3) that every such halfplane cuts from \(B\) area at least \(2/9\).

Let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing \((1,g)\).  The whole space has mass \(T\) and is harmless.  For a proper \(H\), if its boundary does not contain \((1,g)\), translate the boundary parallel toward that point until it does.  The resulting halfspace is contained in \(H\), so it is enough to bound proper halfspaces through \((1,g)\).  If the planar part of the normal is nonzero, such a halfspace can be written
\[
H=\{(h,z):\ell(z)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\},
\qquad \ell(g)=0,
\tag{4}
\]
with \(\ell\neq0\).

Suppose first that the height-two section of (4) contains all of \(Q\).  Its middle section is a halfplane through the centroid of \(B\), so (3) and \(|Q|=p\) give
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\geq\frac29+p
=t+\frac{21}{16000}.
\tag{5}
\]

Otherwise there is \(q\in Q\) with \(\ell(q)+\beta<0\).  Every displayed vertex of \(Q\) belongs to \(\Delta\), so \(Q\subseteq\Delta\).  With \(-A=\min_\Delta\ell\), it follows that
\[
\beta<-\ell(q)\leq A.
\]
The height-zero section of (4) therefore contains \(\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq A\}\).  Its middle section is \(B\cap\{\ell\geq0\}\), and
\[
|B\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|
\geq|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|-\delta.
\]
Dropping the nonnegative height-two contribution, then using (1) and (2), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)
&\geq\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}-\delta\\
&>\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}-\frac1{200}
=t+\frac{11689}{936000}
>t+\frac{21}{16000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
If the planar part of the normal is zero, the two proper halfspaces through height one have masses \(1\) and \(1/2+p\), both larger than the right-hand side of (5).  Equations (5) and (6), together with the initial translation, prove
\[
h_S((1,g))\geq\frac29+p.
\tag{7}
\]

For the reverse inequality, set
\[
\ell_*(x,y)=y-\frac{x}{100}-\frac{33}{100}
\]
and consider the rational closed halfspace
\[
H_*=\left\{(h,x,y):
\ell_*(x,y)+\frac{17}{25}(h-1)\geq0\right\}.
\]
At height zero, \(\max_\Delta\ell_*=67/100<17/25\), so the section is empty.  At height one, \(\ell_*=Y-1/3\), and the section \(Y\geq1/3\) cuts from \(B\) the affine image of the corner triangle \(\{(X,Y)\in\Delta:Y\geq1/3\}\), of area
\[
\frac12\left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac29.
\]
At height two, exact evaluation on the four vertices gives
\[
\min_Q\ell_*=-\frac{283}{1000}>-\frac{17}{25},
\]
so the section contains all of \(Q\).  Hence \((1,g)\in H_*\) and
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_*)=\frac29+p.
\]
Together with (7), this proves the claimed exact depth.

Finally, \(g\in\operatorname{int}B\).  If interiors of strict-low halfspaces covered \(\{1\}\times B\), one of those interiors would contain \((1,g)\), whereas every closed halfspace containing \((1,g)\) has mass at least \(t+21/16000>t\).  This proves the asserted no-cover conclusion, and accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` identifies it as the exact obstruction to completing the fixed triple's nine-halfspace counterexample certificate.
