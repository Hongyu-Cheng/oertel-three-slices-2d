## Statement

Assume the all-nine-nonzero contained-support chamber of accepted fact 89f1aef98e9b8915, with the full residual-area and incidence constraints. Thus
\[
0<A<B,
\qquad t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\leq\frac12,
\qquad B<1+A,
\]
the columns of \(L\) sum to one, the first two middle and small-endpoint centroid coordinates are respectively negative and nonnegative, and all three literal row masses are strictly below \(t\). After the fixed allowed relabeling, write
\[
L_{1*}=(-\xi,y,-\zeta),\qquad
L_{2*}=(-D,u,v),\qquad
g=L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),
\]
where all displayed quantities are positive, and put
\[
\rho=\xi+\zeta-y>0,qquad
\tau=D-u-v>0,
\qquad
d=\max\{a,b\},\quad e=\min\{a,b\}.
\]
Define, for both \(d>e\) and \(d=e\),
\[
h:=\frac{d-e}{p+d}.
\]
Thus \(h=0\) when \(d=e\). In the notation of accepted fact 83ae76262528a9f8, let
\[
c=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)},
\qquad
\eta=t-c,
\]
\[
\mu_\eta=A\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta A}\right),
\qquad
\nu_\eta=B\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta B}\right),
\]
and let the actual row-minimum parameter be
\[
x=1-\sum_i\min_jU_{ij}\in[\sqrt A,2-\sqrt B].
\]
If
\[
\frac{\mu_\eta}{x^2}\ge h,
\qquad
\frac{\nu_\eta}{(2-x)^2}\ge h,
\tag{LL}
\]
then
\[
A\mathcal C(gU;-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C(gV;\sigma_3)\ge\eta.
\]
Consequently the third literal row mass is at least \(c+\eta=t\), contrary to its strict row inequality. The conclusion includes \(d=e\), equality in either condition in (LL), both determinant signs, every endpoint cap-order boundary, every row-minimum attainment pattern, and every value of \(\sigma_3\).

## Proof

Write
\[
\delta=B-1.
\]
The full residual-area inequalities give
\[
0<\delta<A,
\qquad A+\delta\le\frac14,
\tag{1}
\]
the second inequality being equivalent to \(t\le1/2\). Put
\[
c_i=\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)\qquad(i=1,2).
\]

We first retain the constraints imposed by the first two strict rows. If three affine vertex values have nonnegative arithmetic mean, their closed upper zero-cap has area fraction at least \(4/9\). Indeed, in the only extremal case there is one positive value \(P\) and two negative magnitudes \(r_1,r_2\), with \(P\ge r_1+r_2\), and the exact corner-cap fraction is
\[
\frac{P^2}{(P+r_1)(P+r_2)}
\ge
\frac{P^2}{(P+(r_1+r_2)/2)^2}
\ge\frac49.
\]
If at least two values are nonnegative, the negative complement has fraction at most \(1/2\), so the same conclusion follows. This includes zero, repeated, and constant values under the literal closed-cap convention.

The small-endpoint centroid coordinates in rows \(1,2\) are nonnegative. Hence their small-endpoint cap fractions are at least \(4/9\), while all large-endpoint contributions are nonnegative. The first two strict row inequalities therefore imply
\[
c_1,c_2<\kappa:=t-\frac{4A}{9}
=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9
=\frac49-\frac{2(A-\delta)}9
<\frac49.
\tag{2}
\]

The exact middle one-positive and two-positive formulas are
\[
c_1=\frac{y^2}{(\xi+y)(\zeta+y)},
\qquad
c_2=1-\frac{D^2}{(D+u)(D+v)}.
\tag{3}
\]
From \(c_2<4/9\),
\[
5(D+u)(D+v)<9D^2.
\]
Using \(u+v=D-\tau\), this gives
\[
5D\tau>D^2+5uv,
\qquad
\tau>\frac D5,
\qquad
v<\frac{4D}{5}.
\tag{4}
\]
The third column sum is \(-\zeta+v-b=1\). Since \(b>0\), (4) gives
\[
D>\frac54(\zeta+1).
\tag{5}
\]

From \(c_1<4/9\) and \(y=\xi+\zeta-\rho\), exact expansion gives
\[
(\xi-\zeta)^2<\rho(6y+\rho).
\tag{6}
\]
We claim
\[
\xi<\frac32\zeta+11\rho.
\tag{7}
\]
This is immediate if \(\xi\le\zeta\). Otherwise set
\[
F(X)=(X-\zeta)^2-\rho(6X+6\zeta-5\rho).
\]
At \(X=3\zeta/2+11\rho\),
\[
F(X)=\frac{\zeta^2}{4}-4\zeta\rho+60\rho^2>0,
\]
because its discriminant as a quadratic in \(\zeta\) is
\(16\rho^2-60\rho^2<0\). Moreover
\(F'(X)=2(X-\zeta)-6\rho>0\) from that point onward. Thus
\(\xi\ge3\zeta/2+11\rho\) would contradict (6), proving (7).

The first column sum gives \(p=1+\xi+D\), while summing the three row sums gives
\[
p-a-b=3+\rho+\tau.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (4), (5), and (7) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
11(3+\rho+\tau)
&>33+11\rho+\frac{11D}{5}\\
&>1+\xi+D=p.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, after using (7), the excess of the middle expression over \(p\) is greater than
\[
32+\frac65D-\frac32\zeta
>32+\frac32(\zeta+1)-\frac32\zeta>0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\lambda:=\frac{p-a-b}{p}>\frac1{11}.
\tag{9}
\]

Put
\[
P=\frac d p,
\qquad E=\frac e p.
\]
Then \(P\ge E>0\), (9) gives
\[
P+E<\frac{10}{11},
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
c=\frac1{(1+P)(1+E)}.
\tag{11}
\]
By arithmetic-geometric mean and (10),
\[
c>\left(1+\frac5{11}\right)^{-2}=\frac{121}{256}.
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(c\), write \(S=P+E\). Since
\[
PE=\frac1c-1-S,
\]
the larger root
\[
P=\frac{S+\sqrt{(S+2)^2-4/c}}2
\]
is increasing in \(S\). Thus (10) and (11) imply
\[
q:=\frac d{p+d}=\frac P{1+P}<Q(c),
\qquad
Q(c):=\frac{11-16c+\sqrt{c(256c-121)}}{11}.
\tag{13}
\]
To verify the expression for \(Q(c)\), at the limiting value \(S=10/11\) one has
\[
c=\frac{11(1-q)^2}{21-32q};
\]
the larger root of this quadratic equation is exactly \(Q(c)\). Also \(c<t\le1/2\), so every radical in (13) is real on \([121/256,1/2]\).

We next prove the parameter-uniform allocation estimate
\[
\frac{\mu_\eta}{x}+\frac{\nu_\eta}{2-x}\ge2Q(c).
\tag{14}
\]
Set \(z=\sqrt\eta\). From (1), (12), and
\[
c+z^2=t=\frac49+\frac{2(A+\delta)}9\le\frac12,
\]
we have
\[
0\le z^2\le\frac7{256}.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(\alpha\ge1/2\), direct squaring shows
\[
0\le r\le\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{1-r}\ge1-\alpha r.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(A>(A+\delta)/2=[9(c+z^2)-4]/4\), (12) and (15) give
\[
\frac{z^2}{A}<\frac{7}{32}
<\left(\frac{192}{361}\right)^2,
\qquad
\frac z{\sqrt A}<\frac{192}{361}.
\]
Also \(B>1\) gives
\[
\frac z{\sqrt B}<\frac{\sqrt7}{16}<\frac{64}{289}.
\]
Apply (16) with \(\alpha=19/32\) to the first ratio and with \(\alpha=17/32\) to the second. Since
\[
\mu_\eta=A-\sqrt{A\eta},
\qquad
\nu_\eta=B-\sqrt{B\eta},
\]
we obtain
\[
\sqrt{\mu_\eta}+\sqrt{\nu_\eta}
\ge\sqrt A+\sqrt B-\frac98z.
\tag{17}
\]
For fixed \(A+\delta\), the function
\(\sqrt A+\sqrt{1+\delta}\) is increasing in \(A\) on
\(A>\delta\). Hence, writing \(w=c+z^2\), (17) gives
\[
\sqrt{\mu_\eta}+\sqrt{\nu_\eta}
\ge R(c,z):=
\frac12\sqrt{9w-4}+\frac32\sqrt w-\frac98z.
\tag{18}
\]

We now verify exactly that
\[
\frac{R(c,z)^2}{2}\ge2Q(c)
\tag{19}
\]
on
\[
\frac{121}{256}\le c\le\frac12,
\qquad
0\le z\le\sqrt{\frac12-c}.
\tag{20}
\]
The derivative with respect to \(z\) is
\[
R_z=\frac{9z}{2\sqrt{9w-4}}+
\frac{3z}{2\sqrt w}-\frac98,
\tag{21}
\]
and it is strictly increasing.

If \(247/512\le c\le1/2\), (21) is nonpositive even at the right endpoint, so the minimum is attained at
\(z=\sqrt{1/2-c}\). Put \(Y=8\sqrt2z\in[0,3/2]\). Then
\[
\frac{R^2}{2}=\left(1-\frac{9Y}{128}\right)^2,
\qquad
c=\frac12-\frac{Y^2}{128}.
\]
After isolating the only radical in (19), the positive left side is
\[
H(Y)=\frac{81920-25344Y-3205Y^2}{32768}>0,
\]
and squaring is equivalent to
\[
128H(Y)^2-(64-Y^2)(7-2Y^2)
=-\frac{11}{8388608}P_4(Y)\ge0,
\tag{22}
\]
where
\[
P_4(Y)=591381Y^4-14768640Y^3-113606656Y^2
+377487360Y-268435456.
\]
In the degree-four Bernstein basis, the coefficients of \(P_4\) on \([0,4/3]\) are
\[
\left(-268435456,-142606336,-\frac{1361838080}{27},
-\frac{6152192}{9},-\frac{2020096}{9}\right),
\]
and on \([4/3,3/2]\) they are
\[
\left(-\frac{2020096}{9},-\frac{1503584}{9},
-\frac{23757860}{27},-\frac{4752931}{2},-\frac{74714971}{16}\right).
\]
Every coefficient is negative, proving (22).

If \(121/256\le c\le247/512\), the minimum in (21) is its unique critical point, including the right endpoint at the upper boundary. Put
\[
\theta=\sqrt{\frac{9w-4}{w}}.
\]
Equation \(R_z=0\) gives
\[
z=\frac{3\theta\sqrt w}{4(\theta+3)},
\]
and direct substitution gives
\[
c=\frac{(\theta+12)(7\theta+12)}
{4(3-\theta)(\theta+3)^3},
\qquad
\frac{R^2}{2}
=\frac{(16\theta^2+69\theta+144)^2}
{512(3-\theta)(\theta+3)^3}.
\tag{23}
\]
The first expression in (23) is strictly increasing; its derivative is
\[
\frac{\theta(7\theta^2+123\theta+63)}
{2(\theta-3)^2(\theta+3)^4}>0.
\]
Its value at \(7/8\) is \(121/256-106327/129650432\), while \(w\le1/2\) gives \(\theta\le1\). Thus
\[
\frac78<\theta\le1.
\tag{24}
\]
Let
\[
N_4=14080\theta^4+91872\theta^3+131731\theta^2
+3552\theta-94464.
\]
The quantity obtained by isolating the radical in (19) is
\[
K(\theta)=\frac{11}{2}\frac{R^2}{2}-11+16c
=\frac{N_4}{1024(3-\theta)(\theta+3)^3}.
\]
Its degree-four Bernstein coefficients on \([7/8,1]\) are
\[
\left(\frac{5075267}{64},\frac{12081853}{128},
\frac{42477091}{384},\frac{2048861}{16},146771\right),
\]
so \(K(\theta)>0\). Squaring gives
\[
K(\theta)^2-c(256c-121)
=\frac{11P_8(\theta)}
{1048576(\theta-3)^2(\theta+3)^6},
\tag{25}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_8(\theta)={}&18022400\theta^8+235192320\theta^7
+1084361216\theta^6+1811593152\theta^5\\
&-755860005\theta^4-4944144960\theta^3
-2808940032\theta^2\\
&+2615721984\theta+2818768896.
\end{aligned}
\]
Its degree-eight Bernstein coefficients on \([7/8,1]\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(&2932977954619/4096,
5067325637829/8192,
30046337879441/57344,\\
&24839170701325/57344,
19907257462335/57344,
1914203368493/7168,\\
&24837619991/128,
2071283157/16,
74714971).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every coefficient is positive, so (25), hence (19), holds. This completes the exact check on (20).

Cauchy--Schwarz, (18), and (19) now give
\[
\frac{\mu_\eta}{x}+\frac{\nu_\eta}{2-x}
\ge
\frac{(\sqrt{\mu_\eta}+\sqrt{\nu_\eta})^2}{2}
\ge2Q(c),
\]
which proves (14) for the actual \(x\), including both endpoints of its allowed interval.

It remains to use the branch formulas and the correct per-instance consequence of the accepted envelope proof. For \(d>e\), accepted fact 83ae76262528a9f8 defines
\[
\psi(w)=
\begin{cases}
-d+\sqrt{w(d-e)(p+d)},&0\le w\le h,\\
(p+d)w-d,&h\le w\le1,
\end{cases}
\]
and the two formulas agree at \(w=h\). For \(d=e\), it defines
\[
\psi(w)=(p+d)w-d
\qquad(0\le w\le1);
\]
our definition \(h=0\) makes both inequalities in (LL) meaningful and places this entire boundary in the linear case. Hence, in every case covered by (LL),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_\eta(x)
&=x\psi\!\left(\frac{\mu_\eta}{x^2}\right)
+(2-x)\psi\!\left(\frac{\nu_\eta}{(2-x)^2}\right)\\
&=(p+d)\left(\frac{\mu_\eta}{x}
+\frac{\nu_\eta}{2-x}\right)-2d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (13), (14), and (26) give
\[
G_\eta(x)\ge2(p+d)Q(c)-2d>0.
\tag{27}
\]

We do not infer \(\Gamma_\eta\ge0\) from (27). Instead we use the per-instance contrapositive proved in equations (38)--(41) of accepted fact 83ae76262528a9f8, which we restate with the present aliases. Let
\[
\ell_i=\min_jU_{ij},
\qquad
U=\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+xX,
\qquad
V=-\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+(2-x)Y,
\]
be its actual row-minimum normalization, and put
\[
\Delta_3=g\ell+\sigma_3,
\qquad
r_*=-\frac{\Delta_3}{x},
\qquad
z_* =\frac{\Delta_3}{2-x}.
\]
Then
\[
xr_*+(2-x)z_*=0.
\tag{28}
\]
Let
\[
\rho_X=\mathcal C(gX;r_*),
\qquad
\rho_Y=\mathcal C(gY;z_*).
\]
These are exactly the two third-row endpoint cap fractions. If their weighted contribution were strictly below \(\eta\),
\[
A\rho_X+B\rho_Y<\eta,
\tag{29}
\]
then the contained-triangle estimate and the exact inverse-capacity formula in that accepted proof give, strictly,
\[
r_*>\psi\!\left(\frac{\mu_\eta}{x^2}\right),
\qquad
z_*>\psi\!\left(\frac{\nu_\eta}{(2-x)^2}\right).
\tag{30}
\]
Multiplying (30) by \(x\) and \(2-x\), and using (28), yields
\[
0=xr_*+(2-x)z_*>G_\eta(x).
\tag{31}
\]
Thus the accepted proof establishes the per-instance implication
\[
A\mathcal C(gU;-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C(gV;\sigma_3)<\eta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
G_\eta(x)<0
\tag{32}
\]
for the actual \(x\). This implication does not involve \(\Gamma_\eta\). Equation (27) contradicts the conclusion of (32), so the third endpoint contribution is at least \(\eta\). Adding the middle contribution \(c=t-\eta\) contradicts the strict third row.

The normalization and contrapositive in accepted fact 83ae76262528a9f8 use absolute determinants, literal closed caps, actual row minima, and an arbitrary shift. Therefore no determinant sign, cap-order cell, minimum-attaining column, or shift was chosen, and all boundary cases asserted in the statement, including \(d=e\) and equality in (LL), are covered.
