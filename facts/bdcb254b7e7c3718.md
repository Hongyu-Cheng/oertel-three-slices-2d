## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(1,1)\},
\]
and
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(1,1/2),(1/2,1),(0,1/2)\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_S\bigl(1,(1/2,1/2)\bigr)=\frac12>\frac7{18}=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Consequently, no collection of halfspaces of mass less than \(7/18\) can cover the middle slice in its interiors. In particular, the at-most-nine-halfspace certificate characterized by accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` does not exist for this family.

## Proof

Put
\[
z_*=\bigl(1,(1/2,1/2)\bigr)
\]
and define
\[
B_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,1),(1/2,1/2)\}\subseteq A_0,
\]
\[
B_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(1/2,0),(1,1/2),(1/2,1),(0,1/2)\}\subseteq A_1,
\]
and
\[
B_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,1),(1,0),(1/2,1/2)\}\subseteq A_2.
\]
Their exact areas are
\[
|B_0|=\frac14,\qquad |B_1|=\frac12,\qquad |B_2|=\frac14.
\]
Thus the two-dimensional measure restricted to
\[
B=(\{0\}\times B_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B_1)\cup(\{2\}\times B_2)
\]
has total mass \(1\).

Consider the central inversion
\[
J(r,u,v)=(2-r,1-u,1-v).
\]
It fixes \(z_*\), preserves planar area, exchanges \(\{0\}\times B_0\) with \(\{2\}\times B_2\), and preserves \(\{1\}\times B_1\). Hence \(J(B)=B\).

Let \(K\) be any proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(z_*\), and let \(\nu\) denote the measure restricted to \(B\). Since \(J\) is central inversion about \(z_*\), \(J(K)\) is the opposite closed halfspace. Therefore
\[
K\cup J(K)=\mathbb R^3,\qquad K\cap J(K)=\partial K.
\]
The symmetry \(J(B)=B\) gives \(\nu(K)=\nu(J(K))\). Inclusion-exclusion now yields
\[
2\nu(K)
=\nu(K)+\nu(J(K))
=\nu(B)+\nu(B\cap\partial K)
=1+\nu(B\cap\partial K)\ge1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mu(K)\ge\nu(K)\ge\frac12.
\]

Let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing \(z_*\). Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` gives a closed halfspace \(K\subseteq H\) whose boundary contains \(z_*\). Hence
\[
\mu(H)\ge\mu(K)\ge\frac12,
\]
and therefore \(h_S(z_*)\ge1/2\).

For the reverse inequality, take
\[
H_*=\{(r,u,v):v\ge1/2\}.
\]
Its three sections have exact areas
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{v\ge1/2\}\right|=\frac18,
\]
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{v\ge1/2\}\right|=\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\left|A_2\cap\{v\ge1/2\}\right|=\frac18.
\]
Thus \(\mu(H_*)=1/2\), proving
\[
h_S(z_*)=\frac12.
\]

Finally,
\[
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|
=\frac12+\frac34+\frac12
=\frac74,
\qquad
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac7{18}<\frac12.
\]
No halfspace of mass less than \(7/18\) contains \(z_*\). Hence no finite collection of such halfspaces can cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\) in its interiors. In particular, the middle-slice condition in accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` fails, so no complete at-most-nine-halfspace certificate exists for this fixed family.
