## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
\beta_1(x,y)=x,\quad
\beta_2(x,y)=y,\quad
\beta_3(x,y)=1-x-y.
\]
Take
\[
A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_0=\frac{19}{10}\Delta,\qquad
A_2=\frac1{10}\Delta.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
f_1=49-50\beta_1,\qquad
f_2=-50\beta_2,\qquad
f_3=-50\beta_3
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
s_1=-45,\qquad s_2=-95,\qquad s_3=-50.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\).  The three proper closed halfspaces
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\leq0\}
\tag{4}
\]
form a normalized strict edge-frame certificate.  Their middle thresholds
are
\[
(b_1,b_2,b_3)=\left(\frac{49}{50},0,0\right),
\tag{5}
\]
their endpoint thresholds in the same barycentric coordinates are
\[
(r_1,r_2,r_3)
=\left(\frac{47}{25},\frac{19}{10},1\right)
\quad\text{at height }0,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
(q_1,q_2,q_3)
=\left(\frac2{25},-\frac{19}{10},-1\right)
\quad\text{at height }2.
\tag{7}
\]
The endpoint contribution vector is
\[
(e_1,e_2,e_3)
=\left(\frac1{2500},\frac1{200},\frac1{200}\right).
\tag{8}
\]
All three full halfspace masses are strictly below \(2T/9\), but the
registered payment fails.

Here is the valid replacement.  In any configuration from the registered
statement, write
\[
B=b_1+b_2+b_3,\qquad
\delta=1-B>0,\qquad
\sigma=s_1+s_2+s_3,
\tag{9}
\]
and let \(a_0,a_2\) denote the two endpoint areas without ordering them.
Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3e_i\geq \mathcal L(\sigma),
\tag{10}
\]
where
\[
\mathcal L(\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
a_0+a_2,
&-1\leq\sigma\leq1,\\[1mm]
a_2+
\left(a_0-a_1\delta^2(1+\sigma)^2\right)_+,
&\sigma<-1,\\[1mm]
a_0+
\left(a_2-a_1\delta^2(\sigma-1)^2\right)_+,
&\sigma>1,
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
and \(t_+=\max\{t,0\}\).

Consequently, accepted fact `79125fb2149eb8c2` gives
\[
\max_i\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
\geq \frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac{\mathcal L(\sigma)}3.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the \(2T/9\) target follows whenever
\[
\mathcal L(\sigma)\geq
\frac23(M+m-a_1).
\tag{13}
\]
Condition (13) always holds on the nonempty open slope interval
\(-1<\sigma<1\).

More explicitly, suppose \(A_0\) is the larger endpoint, so
\(a_0=M,a_2=m\), and suppose the lower-pinch inequalities
\[
\frac m2<M-a_1<m
\tag{14}
\]
hold.  For \(\sigma<-1\), the replacement closes the open region
\[
\delta^2(1+\sigma)^2
<
\frac{M+m+2a_1}{3a_1}.
\tag{15}
\]
The symmetric statement, with \(\sigma>1\), holds when \(A_2\) is the
larger endpoint.  These regions include a one-sided open neighborhood of
\(\sigma=-1\), respectively \(\sigma=1\).

The constants in (11) cannot be increased in general.  For an exact
equality example, keep \(A_1=\Delta\) and take
\[
A_0=A_2=P
:=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(0,0),\left(\frac14,0\right),
\left(0,\frac23\right)\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Let
\[
(b_1,b_2,b_3)
=\left(\frac{13}{50},\frac{27}{40},0\right),
\qquad
\delta=\frac{13}{200},
\tag{17}
\]
and use endpoint thresholds
\[
(r_1,r_2,r_3)
=\left(\frac{13}{50},\frac{27}{40},-2\right),
\qquad
(q_1,q_2,q_3)
=\left(\frac{13}{50},\frac{27}{40},2\right).
\tag{18}
\]
These thresholds come from
\[
s_1=s_2=0,\qquad s_3=\frac{400}{13}.
\tag{19}
\]
The first two caps have zero area on both endpoints, the height-\(0\)
third cap is all of \(P\), and the height-\(2\) third cap has zero area.
Hence
\[
\sum_i e_i=|P|=\frac1{12}.
\tag{20}
\]
For (19), the last line of (11) also equals \(a_0=1/12\), so equality
holds.  This sharpness example is a common-frame example, not a low-mass
residual certificate.

## Proof

First verify the counterexample.  Minkowski addition of positive
homothetic copies of the same triangle gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac{(19/10+1/10)\Delta}{2}
=\Delta=A_1.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the endpoint triple is compatible, and accepted fact
`8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` supplies a compact three-slice polytope with exactly
these integer sections.

The functions in (2) satisfy
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3
=49-50(\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3)
=-1.
\tag{22}
\]
At height \(1\), the inequalities \(f_i\leq0\) are exactly
\(\beta_i\geq b_i\), with (5).  If a point lay outside all three open
middle sections, it would satisfy
\[
\beta_i\leq b_i\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
and hence
\[
1=\sum_i\beta_i\leq\sum_i b_i=\frac{49}{50},
\]
a contradiction.  Therefore the middle interiors cover the whole plane.
The cuts are edge-parallel vertex caps, including the endpoint value
\(b_i=0\) explicitly allowed by accepted fact
`79125fb2149eb8c2`.

Substitution of \(h=0\) and \(h=2\) in (4) gives (6) and (7).  Direct
triangle similarity gives the three middle cap areas
\[
\left(\frac1{5000},\frac12,\frac12\right),
\tag{23}
\]
the height-\(0\) endpoint cap areas
\[
\left(\frac1{5000},0,0\right),
\tag{24}
\]
and the height-\(2\) endpoint cap areas
\[
\left(\frac1{5000},\frac1{200},\frac1{200}\right).
\tag{25}
\]
For example, the first caps in both homothetic endpoint triangles have
linear side length \(1/50\), so each has area
\((1/50)^2/2=1/5000\).  The second and third height-\(0\) caps are,
respectively, a vertex and a vertex, while the corresponding height-\(2\)
thresholds lie below the whole small triangle.

Equations (24)-(25) prove (8) and
\[
\sum_i e_i
=\frac1{2500}+\frac1{200}+\frac1{200}
=\frac{13}{1250}.
\tag{26}
\]
The slice areas and target are
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)
=\left(\frac{361}{200},\frac12,\frac1{200}\right),
\qquad
T=\frac{231}{100},
\qquad
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{77}{150}.
\tag{27}
\]
From (23)-(25), the three full masses are
\[
\left(\frac3{5000},\frac{101}{200},\frac{101}{200}\right),
\tag{28}
\]
and every entry in (28) is strictly below \(77/150\).  Finally,
\[
\frac23(M+m-a_1)
=\frac23\left(
\frac{361}{200}+\frac1{200}-\frac12\right)
=\frac{131}{150},
\tag{29}
\]
which, together with (26), proves the strict failure.

Notice that
\[
M=\frac{361}{200}>
\frac12+\frac1{200}=a_1+m.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus this exact counterexample does not satisfy the omitted residual
balance.  It refutes the registered literal implication, but not the
corrected implication obtained by adding all residual hypotheses.

We next prove the replacement.  Since the \(i\)-th middle halfplane is
the edge-parallel cap \(\{\beta_i\geq b_i\}\), there is a positive
constant \(\lambda_i\) such that
\[
f_i=\lambda_i(b_i-\beta_i).
\tag{31}
\]
The only linear dependence among the three barycentric linear parts is
their sum.  The identity \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\) therefore forces
\[
\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3.
\]
Taking constant terms in (31) then gives
\[
f_i=\frac{b_i-\beta_i}{\delta}.
\tag{32}
\]
Consequently the actual endpoint thresholds are
\[
r_i=b_i-\delta s_i,\qquad
q_i=b_i+\delta s_i.
\tag{33}
\]
Their sums are
\[
R:=\sum_i r_i
=1-\delta(1+\sigma),
\qquad
Q:=\sum_i q_i
=1+\delta(\sigma-1).
\tag{34}
\]

Let
\[
x_i=\left|A_0\cap\{\beta_i\geq r_i\}\right|,
\qquad
y_i=\left|A_2\cap\{\beta_i\geq q_i\}\right|.
\tag{35}
\]
Then \(e_i=x_i+y_i\).  These are the actual paired endpoint caps, not
independently assigned bonuses.  Accepted fact `0b5d84877f51b7c3`
already gives the universal consequence
\[
\sum_i(x_i+y_i)\geq\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\tag{36}
\]
The common barycentric frame gives the sharper routed estimate below.

If \(R\leq1\), the three closed halfplanes
\(\{\beta_i\geq r_i\}\) cover the whole plane.  Indeed, a point outside
all three would satisfy \(\beta_i<r_i\) for every \(i\), and summing would
give \(1<R\leq1\).  Hence
\[
\sum_i x_i\geq a_0.
\tag{37}
\]
The same argument gives
\[
\sum_i y_i\geq a_2
\quad\text{whenever }Q\leq1.
\tag{38}
\]
For \(-1\leq\sigma\leq1\), both inequalities apply by (34), proving the
first line of (11).

Suppose \(\sigma<-1\).  Then \(Q<1<R\), so (38) gives
\(\sum_i y_i\geq a_2\).  The part of \(A_0\) outside all three height-\(0\)
caps is contained in
\[
\mathcal T_R=\{w:\beta_i(w)\leq r_i\ (i=1,2,3)\}.
\tag{39}
\]
The coordinates
\[
\nu_i(w)=\frac{r_i-\beta_i(w)}{R-1}
\tag{40}
\]
are nonnegative on \(\mathcal T_R\) and sum to \(1\).  Thus
\(\mathcal T_R\) is a reflected homothetic copy of \(A_1\), with linear
ratio \(R-1\), and
\[
|\mathcal T_R|=a_1(R-1)^2
=a_1\delta^2(1+\sigma)^2.
\tag{41}
\]
The union of the three height-\(0\) caps has area at most
\(\sum_i x_i\), so (39)-(41) give
\[
\sum_i x_i
\geq
\left(a_0-a_1\delta^2(1+\sigma)^2\right)_+.
\tag{42}
\]
Adding (38) proves the second line of (11).  The case \(\sigma>1\) is
identical after exchanging heights \(0\) and \(2\), and proves the third
line.

Now apply accepted fact `79125fb2149eb8c2` to the same indexed middle
caps and the actual bonuses \(e_i\).  This proves (12).  Subtracting
\(2T/9\) from its right side shows that (13) is exactly sufficient:
\[
\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac{\mathcal L(\sigma)}3
-\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}9
=
\frac{3\mathcal L(\sigma)-2(M+m-a_1)}9.
\tag{43}
\]
For \(-1<\sigma<1\), one has
\(\mathcal L(\sigma)=M+m\), so (13) is strict.  This gives the asserted
nonempty lower-pinch closure and strictly extends the area-only regime
recorded in accepted fact `2e7881b9f943653e` on this slope interval.

Under (14), if \(A_0\) is the larger endpoint and \(\sigma<-1\), then
\[
\frac{M+m+2a_1}{3a_1}<\frac{M}{a_1}
\]
because \(2(M-a_1)>m\).  Hence the positive part in the second line of
(11) is active under (15), and direct rearrangement gives
\[
m+M-a_1\delta^2(1+\sigma)^2
>
\frac23(M+m-a_1).
\]
This proves (15); the other orientation is symmetric.

Finally, for (16)-(19), the middle thresholds sum to \(187/200<1\), so
the middle interiors cover the plane.  The first two endpoint thresholds
strictly exceed the corresponding coordinate maxima \(1/4\) and \(2/3\)
on both copies of \(P\), so those four cap areas vanish.  Since
\(-2<\beta_3\leq1<2\) on \(P\), the height-\(0\) third cap is all of
\(P\) and the height-\(2\) third cap is empty.  Thus (20) holds.  Also
\[
\delta(\sigma-1)
=\frac{13}{200}\left(\frac{400}{13}-1\right)
=\frac{387}{200},
\]
so
\[
a_2-a_1\delta^2(\sigma-1)^2
=\frac1{12}-\frac12\left(\frac{387}{200}\right)^2<0.
\]
The last line of (11) is therefore exactly \(a_0=1/12\), proving
sharpness.

The corrected residual all-slope assertion, with
\[
M<a_1+m,\qquad a_1<M+m,
\]
and all three strict low-mass conditions retained, is not decided by
this packet.  The counterexample proves that those omitted residual
hypotheses cannot be dropped, while (11)-(15) isolate the remaining
one-sided extreme-slope branch.
