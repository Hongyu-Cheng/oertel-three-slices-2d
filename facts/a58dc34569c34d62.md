## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\tag{1}
\]
and let
\[
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2\subseteq\Delta
\tag{2}
\]
where \(A_2\) is any nonempty two-dimensional compact convex body.  Put
\[
p=|A_2|,\qquad T=1+p,\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
If
\[
0<p\leq\frac2{25},
\tag{4}
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,g))
\geq
t+\min\left\{\frac{7p}{9},\frac1{11700}\right\}
>t.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently no collection of strict-low halfspaces, even of arbitrary
cardinality, can cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\) by its interiors.

Here is a rational one-parameter family satisfying all the requested
geometric conditions.  For rational
\[
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac1{20},
\tag{6}
\]
let \(A_2=Q_\varepsilon\), where the cyclic vertices of
\(Q_\varepsilon\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0&=\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\\
q_1&=\left(\frac1{20}+10\varepsilon,
           \frac1{20}+\varepsilon^2\right),\\
q_2&=\left(\frac1{20}+9\varepsilon,
           \frac1{20}+3\varepsilon^2\right),\\
q_3&=\left(\frac1{20}+\varepsilon,
           \frac1{20}+\varepsilon^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This is an off-center, non-centrally-symmetric, transverse thin
quadrilateral contained in \(\operatorname{int}\Delta\), and
\[
p=|Q_\varepsilon|=\frac{27}{2}\varepsilon^3
\leq\frac{27}{16000}<\frac2{25}.
\tag{8}
\]
Compatibility is automatic:
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{9}
\]

The two endpoint slices nevertheless have exact independent strict-low
covers.  Define
\[
d=\frac p6,\qquad
c=\frac23+d,\qquad
q=\frac13-d.
\tag{10}
\]
At height \(0\), use
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{01}&=\{(h,x,y):h+x+y\leq c\},\\
H_{02}&=\{(h,x,y):h-x\leq-q\},\\
H_{03}&=\{(h,x,y):h-y\leq-q\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Their interiors cover \(\{0\}\times\Delta\), and every row has exact mass
\[
\mu(H_{0r})=\frac{c^2}{2}
=\frac29+\frac p9+\frac{p^2}{72}
<t,
\qquad
t-\mu(H_{0r})=\frac{p(8-p)}{72}>0.
\tag{12}
\]
At height \(2\), the single halfspace
\[
H_{21}=\{(h,x,y):-h\leq-3/2\}
\tag{13}
\]
has interior containing the entire target slice, exact mass
\[
\mu(H_{21})=p<t,
\qquad
t-\mu(H_{21})=\frac{2-7p}{9}>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the exact independent-cover status of the family is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{target slice}&\text{strict-low interior-cover status}\\ \hline
0&\text{feasible with the three rational rows in (11)}\\
1&\text{infeasible even with arbitrarily many rows, by (5)}\\
2&\text{feasible with the one rational row in (13)}.
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]
By accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f`, the middle infeasibility is exactly
the missing component of a nine-halfspace counterexample certificate.

## Proof

We first prove the all-direction bound (5).  Let \(H\) be any closed
halfspace containing \((1,g)\).  Translating its boundary parallel toward
\((1,g)\) gives a halfspace contained in \(H\) whose boundary contains
\((1,g)\).  It is therefore enough to bound boundary-through-\((1,g)\)
halfspaces.

Suppose first that the planar normal is nonzero.  After changing its sign
if necessary, write
\[
H=\{(h,z):\ell(z)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\},
\qquad
\ell(g)=0,
\tag{16}
\]
where \(\ell(z)=u\cdot(z-g)\) for some nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^2\).
Let its ordered values on the three vertices of \(\Delta\) be
\[
-A,\qquad A-B,\qquad B,
\tag{17}
\]
where \(A,B>0\).  These values sum to zero because \(\ell(g)=0\).
Ordering the middle value gives
\[
\frac12\leq\rho:=\frac BA\leq2.
\tag{18}
\]

We need one exact planar cap calculation.  Areas below are stated as
fractions of \(|\Delta|=1/2\).  If \(1/2\leq\rho\leq1\), then the side
\(\{\ell\geq0\}\) contains the two vertices with nonnegative functional
values.  The excluded one-vertex triangle has relative area
\[
\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|}{|\Delta|}
=1-\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}
\geq\frac12.
\tag{19}
\]

If \(1\leq\rho\leq2\), both \(\{\ell\geq0\}\) and
\(\{\ell\geq A\}\) are caps at the vertex with value \(B\).  Similar
triangles give
\[
\frac{|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|}{|\Delta|}
=\frac{\rho^2}{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
\frac{|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq A\}|}{|\Delta|}
=\frac{(\rho-1)^2}{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Their sum exceeds \(12/25\) by an exact uniform margin.  Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{\rho^2+(\rho-1)^2}
{(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}-\frac{12}{25}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{26\rho^2-62\rho+37}
{25(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
while
\[
26\rho^2-62\rho+37
=26\left(\rho-\frac{31}{26}\right)^2+\frac1{26}
\geq\frac1{26}
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)\leq9.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (19)-(24) imply the uniform two-cap estimate
\[
|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|
+|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq A\}|
\geq\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(\rho\leq1\), this follows already from (19), since
\(1/4>6/25+1/11700\).  For \(\rho\geq1\), it follows by multiplying
(22) by \(|\Delta|=1/2\).

The same formulas also give the usual centroid cap bound, here without
invoking any external result:
\[
|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|\geq\frac29.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(\rho\leq1\), equation (19) is stronger.  For
\(1\leq\rho\leq2\), subtracting \(4/9\) from the relative area in (20)
gives
\[
\frac{(\rho-2)^2}
{9(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}\geq0.
\tag{27}
\]

We now apply the cap estimates to (16).  If the height-\(2\) section of
\(H\) contains all of \(A_2\), then (26) gives
\[
\mu(H)\geq\frac29+p
=t+\frac{7p}{9}.
\tag{28}
\]
Otherwise choose \(z_2\in A_2\) outside that closed section.  Equation
(16) gives
\[
\ell(z_2)+\beta<0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\beta<-\ell(z_2).
\tag{29}
\]
Because \(z_2\in A_2\subseteq\Delta\) and the minimum vertex value of
\(\ell\) is \(-A\),
\[
\ell(z_2)\geq-A,
\qquad
\beta<A.
\tag{30}
\]
The height-\(0\) section \(\{\ell\geq\beta\}\) therefore contains
\(\{\ell\geq A\}\).  Combining it with the height-\(1\) section
\(\{\ell\geq0\}\), equation (25) yields
\[
\mu(H)\geq\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}.
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(p\leq2/25\),
\[
t=\frac{2(1+p)}9\leq\frac6{25},
\tag{32}
\]
so (31) is at least \(t+1/11700\).

If the planar normal is zero, the two proper halfspaces through height
\(1\) have masses \(1\) and \(1/2+p\), both larger than \(t\).
The whole-space halfspace, if allowed, has the still larger mass \(T\).
Equations (28), (31), and (32) prove the uniform bound (5).  In
particular, no strict-low halfspace contains \((1,g)\), so no family of
their interiors covers the middle slice.

We next audit the explicit family.  Before applying the anisotropic scaling
\((x,y)\mapsto(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon^2y)\), the four displacement
vectors in (7) are
\[
(0,0),\qquad(10,1),\qquad(9,3),\qquad(1,1).
\tag{33}
\]
The successive edge determinants are
\[
21,\qquad18,\qquad6,\qquad9,
\tag{34}
\]
so the cyclic list is a strictly convex quadrilateral.  Shoelace area of
the unscaled list is \(27/2\); the anisotropic map has determinant
\(\varepsilon^3\), proving (8).

The two opposite diagonal sums of the displacement list are
\[
(9,3)\neq(11,2),
\tag{35}
\]
so \(Q_\varepsilon\) is not centrally symmetric.  Its edge directions
after scaling are
\[
(10\varepsilon,\varepsilon^2),\quad
(-\varepsilon,2\varepsilon^2),\quad
(-8\varepsilon,-2\varepsilon^2),\quad
(-\varepsilon,-\varepsilon^2).
\tag{36}
\]
For (6), both coordinates of each vector are nonzero and none has
coordinate sum zero.  Thus no edge is parallel to any of the three edge
directions of \(\Delta\).

The horizontal and vertical projection lengths of \(Q_\varepsilon\) are
\(10\varepsilon\) and \(3\varepsilon^2\), so its aspect ratio is at most
\[
\frac{3\varepsilon^2}{10\varepsilon}
\leq\frac3{200}.
\tag{37}
\]
Every coordinate in (7) is positive, and every vertex satisfies
\[
x+y\leq
\frac1{10}+10\varepsilon+3\varepsilon^2
\leq\frac{243}{400}<1.
\tag{38}
\]
Hence \(Q_\varepsilon\subset\operatorname{int}\Delta\).  Moreover,
\[
y\leq\frac1{20}+3\varepsilon^2
\leq\frac{23}{400}<\frac13,
\tag{39}
\]
so the entire quadrilateral, and therefore its centroid, is separated
from \(g\).  Convexity of \(\Delta\) now proves the midpoint containment
(9).  This audits compatibility directly.

It remains to audit the two endpoint covers.  From (4) and (10),
\[
0<q<c<1.
\tag{40}
\]
At height \(0\), the closed complement of the three interiors in (11)
would satisfy
\[
x+y\geq c,\qquad x\leq q,\qquad y\leq q.
\tag{41}
\]
But
\[
x+y\leq2q=\frac23-2d
<\frac23+d=c,
\tag{42}
\]
so the complement is empty.  At heights \(1\) and \(2\), every section of
every halfspace in (11) is empty: the first would require
\(x+y\leq c-1<0\), while the second and third would require
\(x\geq1+q>1\) or \(y\geq1+q>1\).  Thus each row receives mass only from
\(A_0\).  Its planar section is a corner triangle with leg ratio \(c\),
which proves the exact mass and margin in (12).

Finally, (13) has empty sections at heights \(0\) and \(1\), while its
interior contains the full height-\(2\) slice.  This proves its exact mass
\(p\), and (4) gives the strict margin in (14).  Therefore (15) audits all
three independent slicewise optimizations exactly, with the middle
centroid inequality (5) as the first obstruction.
