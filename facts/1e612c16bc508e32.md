## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compatible nondegenerate
triangles in the full strict residual strip of accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e`. Use the areas
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9},
\]
and the exact profile and weighted-threshold notation of accepted facts
`17ca54faa05ad878` and `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.

Let \(g_i\) be the centroid of \(A_i\), put
\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},
\tag{1}
\]
and let \(\beta\) be the smallest barycentric coordinate of \(z\) in
\(A_1\). Compatibility puts \(z\in A_1\). If
\[
\boxed{(18\beta^2-1)a_1\ge M-m,}
\tag{2}
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, for every noncollinear positive-spanning triple
\[
q_1,q_2,q_3\in\mathbb S^1,\qquad
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell=0,
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_{q_\ell}(t)\le0.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the registered implication \(W>0\Rightarrow(\mathrm{CB})\)
holds on the class (2), because its antecedent never occurs. This does not
assert the full triangular CB implication when (2) fails.

The class is genuinely new and open. In particular, take
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac23,-\frac13\right),
\left(-\frac13,\frac23\right),
\left(-\frac13,-\frac13\right)
\right\},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac34,-\frac7{24}\right),
\left(-\frac14,\frac7{12}\right),
\left(-\frac12,-\frac7{24}\right)
\right\},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac2{35},\frac3{10}\right),
\left(-\frac{13}{70},-\frac3{20}\right),
\left(\frac{17}{70},-\frac3{20}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
This rational triple is strictly compatible, lies strictly in the residual
strip, satisfies (2) strictly, and lies strictly outside the area class of
accepted fact `a6808da3e0b737ca`. All these strict properties persist on a
full Euclidean neighborhood of its nine vertices.

## Proof

We first prove the planar estimate needed for the middle triangle. Let
\(A\) be a triangle of area \(a\), and let \(x\) have positive
barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\). Put
\[
\beta=\min_i\lambda_i.
\]
Every closed planar halfspace containing \(x\) cuts from \(A\) an area at
least
\[
4\beta^2a.
\tag{8}
\]

It is enough to consider a boundary line through \(x\). Suppose one of its
two pieces is the triangle at vertex \(v_i\). Write the intersections with
the two incident sides as
\[
(1-s)v_i+sv_j,\qquad (1-u)v_i+uv_k,
\qquad 0<s,u\le1.
\]
Since \(x\) lies on the segment between these intersection points, the
positive numbers
\[
X=\frac{\lambda_j}{s},\qquad
Y=\frac{\lambda_k}{u}
\]
satisfy \(X+Y=1\). The isolated triangular piece has relative area
\[
su=\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{XY}
\ge4\lambda_j\lambda_k
\ge4\beta^2.
\tag{9}
\]

For the other piece, \(s,u\le1\) gives
\[
\lambda_j\le X\le1-\lambda_k.
\]
The minimum of \(X(1-X)\) on this interval occurs at an endpoint. Hence
\[
su\le
\max\left\{
\frac{\lambda_k}{1-\lambda_j},
\frac{\lambda_j}{1-\lambda_k}
\right\},
\]
and therefore
\[
1-su\ge
\min\left\{
\frac{\lambda_i}{1-\lambda_j},
\frac{\lambda_i}{1-\lambda_k}
\right\}
\ge\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}
\ge4\beta^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The last inequality is \(1\ge4\beta(1-\beta)\). Both closed sides of every
line through \(x\) therefore satisfy (8). A line through a vertex follows
by a limiting argument, proving the planar estimate.

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), write
\[
r_q=q\cdot z,
\]
and use the cap functions \(F_{i,q}\) of accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878`. For every actual allocation \(c\in\mathbb R\),
\[
F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(2r_q-c)\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, if \(c\ge q\cdot g_0\), the first cap contains the centroid of
\(A_0\), so the triangle-centroid estimate proved in accepted fact
`43e70f89f224c5d7` gives
\[
F_{0,q}(c)\ge\frac{4a_0}{9}\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\]
If \(c<q\cdot g_0\), then (1) gives
\[
2r_q-c=q\cdot g_0+q\cdot g_2-c>q\cdot g_2.
\]
The second cap contains the centroid of \(A_2\), and hence
\[
F_{2,q}(2r_q-c)\ge\frac{4a_2}{9}\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\]
This proves (11) for every finite allocation, including support
endpoints. Taking the actual full endpoint infimal convolution gives
\[
I_q(2r_q):=
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(2r_q-c)\right)
\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus neither a fixed allocation nor either support-extreme routing has
replaced the full profile.

The planar estimate (8) in \(A_1\) gives
\[
F_{1,q}(r_q)\ge4\beta^2a_1.
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (12) and (13),
\[
\Phi_q(r_q)
=F_{1,q}(r_q)+I_q(2r_q)
\ge4\beta^2a_1+\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{14}
\]
Condition (2) is exactly
\[
4\beta^2a_1+\frac{4m}{9}
\ge\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}=t.
\tag{15}
\]

Equation (14) also directly controls all three-dimensional halfspaces.
Shrink a nonhorizontal halfspace containing \((1,z)\) parallel to itself
until its boundary contains \((1,z)\). Its three slice contributions are
\[
F_{0,q}(r_q+\lambda)+F_{1,q}(r_q)+F_{2,q}(r_q-\lambda)
\]
for some \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Equation (11),
with \(c=r_q+\lambda\), bounds this sum from below by the right side of
(14). A horizontal halfspace containing \((1,z)\) contributes at least
\(a_0+a_1\) or \(a_1+a_2\). The full strict residual strip in accepted
fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives \(a_i<T/2\) for all \(i\), so both horizontal
masses exceed \(T/2>2T/9\). This proves (3). Compatibility gives
\(z\in A_1\), and accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` supplies the admissible
compact three-slice polytope.

Accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` defines \(\rho_q(t)\) as the first
\(r\) for which \(\Phi_q(r)\ge t\). Equations (14)-(15) imply
\[
\rho_q(t)\le q\cdot z.
\tag{16}
\]
For a positive-spanning triple, using the notation and relation from
accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`,
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_{q_\ell}(t)
\le
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell\cdot z
=0.
\]
This proves (4). In particular, the antecedent \(W>0\) is impossible on
the class (2), regardless of the unique outer allocations or the complete
actual contact sets.

It remains to verify the rational open-class calibration. The centroids of
all three triangles (5)-(7) are \(0\). The first two triangles are those
of accepted fact `74e60bee4b401289`, and direct determinants, or the area
calculation there followed by the rational scaling used in (7), give
\[
M=\frac12,\qquad a_1=\frac{35}{64},\qquad
m=\frac{27}{280}.
\tag{17}
\]

Compatibility is strict. Accepted fact `74e60bee4b401289` computes
\[
K=2A_1\ominus A_0
=\left\{(x,y):
\frac78x+y\le\frac{17}{48},\
{}-\frac78x+\frac14y\le\frac{13}{48},\
{}-\frac54y\le\frac5{16}
\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
For the three vertices in (7), the respective slack triples in (18) are
\[
\left(\frac5{48},\frac7{48},\frac{11}{16}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac23,\frac7{48},\frac18\right),\qquad
\left(\frac7{24},\frac{25}{48},\frac18\right).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus \(A_2\subset\operatorname{int}K\), equivalently
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subset\operatorname{int}A_1.
\]
Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` therefore realizes these slices by a
compact polytope.

The normalized areas are
\[
r=\frac{m}{M}=\frac{27}{140}<\frac9{25},
\qquad
s=\frac{a_1}{M}=\frac{35}{32},
\]
and
\[
s-(1-r)=\frac{321}{1120}>0,\qquad
(1+r)-s=\frac{111}{1120}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` therefore places the triple strictly in
its residual strip.

The triple is strictly outside the area class proved in accepted fact
`a6808da3e0b737ca`. In its normalized notation,
\[
\delta=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9=\frac{37}{1680},
\qquad
r-\delta=\frac{41}{240},
\]
but
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{41}{240}}\right)^2
<\frac{175}{72}
=\frac{20s}{9}.
\tag{21}
\]
For an exact audit, subtract \(281/240\) from the right side and square
the remaining positive quantities:
\[
\frac{41}{60}<\frac{907^2}{720^2},
\]
because \(354240<822649\).

Here \(z=0\) is the centroid of \(A_1\), so \(\beta=1/3\), and (2) has
the strict margin
\[
(18\beta^2-1)a_1-(M-m)
=\frac{35}{64}-\frac{113}{280}
=\frac{321}{2240}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
The corresponding depth margin in (15) is
\[
\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac{4m}{9}-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{107}{3360}>0.
\tag{23}
\]

All compatibility slacks in (19), residual slacks in (20), the strict
reverse area inequality in (21), and the new criterion margin in (22)
depend continuously on the nine vertices. Hence a full Euclidean
neighborhood of (5)-(7) consists of compatible nondegenerate triangular
triples that remain in the residual strip, remain outside the area class
of accepted fact `a6808da3e0b737ca`, and satisfy (2). Independent small
vertex perturbations in this neighborhood destroy the displayed centroid
alignment and all homotheties. The pointwise proof (11)-(16), not
continuity alone, proves the result on every such perturbed triple.

This candidate closes exactly the class (2). It does not derive the
piecewise-quadratic max-plus contact inequality when (2) fails, does not
prove full triangular CB, and gives no residual triangular counterexample.
