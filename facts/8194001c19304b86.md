## Statement

Use the notation of accepted fact `a49f2b26cc468e4c`, and set
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(1/2,1/4)\},
\]
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3/2,0),(0,5/4)\},
\qquad
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Then \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\), \(D=q(1)=4/9\), and, for
\[
z=\left(\frac12,\frac5{12}\right),
\]
every proper closed halfspace \(H\subset\mathbb R^3\) containing \((1,z)\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge\frac49.
\]
Consequently \(h_S((1,z))\ge4/9\), \(M(1)\ge D\), and there do not exist three proper closed halfspaces, each of total three-slice mass strictly below \(4/9\), whose height-one trace interiors cover \(A_1\).

## Proof

We first record the elementary triangle cap calculation used below. Let \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{V_0,V_1,V_2\}\) have area \(A\), and let \(p\) have barycentric coordinates \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\). A line through \(p\) that leaves \(V_i\) alone on one side meets \(V_iV_j\) and \(V_iV_k\) at fractions \(r,t\) from \(V_i\). Writing \(p=\theta P+(1-\theta)Q\) for the two intersection points gives
\[
r=\frac{\lambda_j}{\theta},\qquad
t=\frac{\lambda_k}{1-\theta},\qquad
\lambda_j\le\theta\le1-\lambda_k.
\]
The one-vertex side therefore has area
\[
A\,rt=A\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{\theta(1-\theta)},
\tag{1}
\]
and the other side has the complementary area.

At the centroid, all three barycentric coordinates are \(1/3\). In (1), \(\theta\in[1/3,2/3]\), so the one-vertex area fraction lies in \([4/9,1/2]\), while the complementary fraction lies in \([1/2,5/9]\). Thus the centroid has planar halfplane depth \(4A/9\). Conversely, every point of a triangle has a barycentric coordinate at least \(1/3\); the cap through that point parallel to the opposite edge has area at most \(4A/9\). Hence the maximal planar halfplane depth of a triangle is exactly \(4A/9\).

We also need three noncentral points of \(A_0\). If the barycentric coordinates are a permutation of
\[
\left(\frac56,\frac1{12},\frac1{12}\right),
\tag{2}
\]
then (1) gives depth \(A/36\). Indeed, when the isolated vertex has coordinate \(5/6\), the one-vertex fraction ranges from \(1/36\) to \(1/11\), and its complement is larger. When the isolated vertex has coordinate \(1/12\), the one-vertex fraction ranges from \(1/2\) to \(10/11\), and the complementary fraction ranges from \(1/11\) to \(1/2\). Thus every side through the point has area at least \(A/36\), with equality in the first case at \(\theta=1/2\).

The three triangle areas are
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\frac1{16},\qquad |A_1|=\frac{15}{16}.
\tag{3}
\]
Since the centroid of \(A_2\) is
\[
c_2=\left(\frac13,\frac1{12}\right),
\tag{4}
\]
the preceding calculation gives
\[
d_{A_2}(c_2)=\frac49|A_2|=\frac1{36}.
\tag{5}
\]
It also gives
\[
\max_{A_0}d_{A_0}=\frac49,\qquad
\max_{A_2}d_{A_2}=\frac1{36},
\]
and hence \(D=4/9\). Equation (3) gives \(q(1)=2(1+15/16+1/16)/9=4/9\).

For completeness, \(A_1=\{(x,y):x,y\ge0,\ (2/3)x+(4/5)y\le1\}\). For \(\ell(x,y)=(2/3)x+(4/5)y\),
\[
\max_{A_0}\ell=\frac43,\qquad
\max_{A_2}\ell=\frac8{15}.
\]
Every point of \((A_0+A_2)/2\) has nonnegative coordinates and \(\ell\)-value at most
\[
\frac12\left(\frac43+\frac8{15}\right)=\frac{14}{15}<1.
\]
Thus \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\), as required for the deformation setting of `a49f2b26cc468e4c`.

We now prove the asserted depth bound at the centroid \(z=(1/2,5/12)\) of \(A_1\). A proper closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\) can be translated parallel toward that point until its boundary contains \((1,z)\); this only decreases every slice cap. It therefore suffices to consider
\[
H=\{(r,p): n\cdot p+a(r-1)\le b\},
\qquad b=n\cdot z,
\tag{6}
\]
where \((n,a)\ne(0,0)\). Its three planar caps are
\[
C_0=A_0\cap\{n\cdot p\le b+a\},\quad
C_1=A_1\cap\{n\cdot p\le b\},\quad
C_2=A_2\cap\{n\cdot p\le b-a\}.
\tag{7}
\]

There is a direction-independent lower bound on the two outer caps. Reflection about \(z\) gives
\[
E=A_0\cap(2z-A_2)
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac12,\frac7{12}\right),
\left(\frac12,\frac34\right),
\left(\frac23,\frac23\right)
\right\},
\]
so
\[
|E|=\frac12\cdot\frac16\cdot\frac16=\frac1{72}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(p\in E\), put \(q=2z-p\in A_2\). Since \(n\cdot p+n\cdot q=2b=(b+a)+(b-a)\), at least one of \(p\in C_0\) and \(q\in C_2\) holds. The reflection is area preserving, so integration over \(E\) yields
\[
|C_0|+|C_2|\ge |E|=\frac1{72}.
\tag{9}
\]

If \(n=0\), then \(C_1=A_1\), and the desired bound follows immediately. Assume \(n\ne0\), and write the vertices of \(A_1\) as
\[
V_0=(0,0),\qquad V_1=(3/2,0),\qquad V_2=(0,5/4).
\]
Put \(\rho_i=n\cdot(V_i-z)\). Since \(z=(V_0+V_1+V_2)/3\), one has \(\rho_0+\rho_1+\rho_2=0\). If the side \(C_1\) contains at least two vertices, the centroid cap calculation above gives
\[
|C_1|\ge\frac12|A_1|=\frac{15}{32}>\frac49.
\tag{10}
\]
It remains to treat the case in which \(C_1\) contains exactly one vertex. Normalize the negative centered projection of that vertex to \(-1\). The other two centered projections are \(s\) and \(1-s\) for some \(s\in[0,1]\). The trace line cuts the two incident edges at fractions \(1/(1+s)\) and \(1/(2-s)\), so
\[
|C_1|=\frac{15}{16(1+s)(2-s)}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(s\notin[1/5,4/5]\), then \((1+s)(2-s)\le54/25\), and (9) and (11) give
\[
|C_0|+|C_1|+|C_2|
\ge\frac{125}{288}+\frac4{288}
=\frac{129}{288}>\frac49.
\tag{12}
\]
Hence suppose \(s\in[1/5,4/5]\). Equation (11) gives
\[
|C_1|\ge\frac5{12}.
\tag{13}
\]
We show in every isolated-vertex case that
\[
|C_0|+|C_2|\ge\frac1{36}.
\tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
p_*=2z-c_2=\left(\frac23,\frac34\right).
\tag{15}
\]
The following three points of \(A_0\) have barycentric coordinates given by permutations of (2), so each has planar depth \(1/36\):
\[
p_0^{(0)}=\left(\frac16,\frac1{12}\right),\qquad
p_0^{(1)}=\left(\frac53,\frac1{12}\right),\qquad
p_0^{(2)}=\left(\frac16,\frac56\right).
\tag{16}
\]
If \(V_0\) is isolated, take \(\rho_0=-1,\rho_1=s,\rho_2=1-s\). Then
\[
n=\left(\frac{2(1+s)}3,\frac{4(2-s)}5\right),
\qquad
n\cdot(p_*-p_0^{(0)})=\frac{7-s}{5}\ge0.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(V_1\) is isolated, take \(\rho_1=-1,\rho_0=s,\rho_2=1-s\). Then
\[
n=\left(-\frac{2(1+s)}3,\frac{4(1-2s)}5\right),
\qquad
n\cdot(p_*-p_0^{(1)})=\frac{2(3-s)}5\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(V_2\) is isolated, take \(\rho_2=-1,\rho_0=s,\rho_1=1-s\). Then
\[
n=\left(\frac{2(1-2s)}3,-\frac{4(1+s)}5\right),
\qquad
n\cdot(p_*-p_0^{(2)})=\frac{2-3s}{5}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (19) is nonnegative when \(s\le2/3\).

In each nonnegative case, \(n\cdot(p_0^{(i)}+c_2)\le2n\cdot z=2b\). Since the two outer thresholds in (7) sum to \(2b\), at least one of
\[
n\cdot p_0^{(i)}\le b+a,\qquad n\cdot c_2\le b-a
\]
holds. The corresponding outer cap contains a point of planar depth \(1/36\), by (5) and (16). This proves (14) for (17), (18), and for (19) when \(s\le2/3\).

It remains only to prove (14) in the \(V_2\)-isolated case when \(s\ge1/2\), which covers the rest of \(s\in[1/5,4/5]\). Here (19) gives
\[
n=\left(\frac{2(1-2s)}3,-\frac{4(1+s)}5\right),\qquad b=-s.
\tag{20}
\]
The ordered projections of the \(A_0\) vertices have minimum
\[
m_0=-\frac{4(1+s)}5,
\]
next value \(-4(2s-1)/3\), and maximum \(0\). The two projection gaps from the minimum are
\[
\delta_0=\frac{4(8-7s)}{15},\qquad
\Delta_0=\frac{4(s+1)}5.
\tag{21}
\]
The ordered projections of the \(A_2\) vertices have minimum
\[
m_2=\frac{2-13s}{15},
\]
next value \((1-2s)/3\), and maximum \(0\), with gaps
\[
\delta_2=\frac{s+1}{5},\qquad
\Delta_2=\frac{13s-2}{15}.
\tag{22}
\]
Set
\[
x=b+a-m_0,\qquad y=b-a-m_2.
\]
Then
\[
x+y=\frac{2-s}{3}=:L.
\tag{23}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that \(|C_0|+|C_2|<1/36\). First \(x,y>0\). If \(x\le0\), then \(y\ge L\ge\delta_2\), so monotonicity and the \(A_2\) cap area at its middle projection give
\[
|C_2|\ge\frac1{16}\frac{\delta_2}{\Delta_2}
=\frac{3(s+1)}{16(13s-2)}
\ge\frac9{224}>\frac1{36}.
\]
If \(y\le0\), then \(x\ge L<\delta_0\), and the first triangular cap formula gives
\[
|C_0|\ge
\frac{L^2}{\delta_0\Delta_0}
=\frac{25(2-s)^2}{48(8-7s)(s+1)}
\ge\frac19>\frac1{36}.
\]
For the last inequality, use \(2-s\ge6/5\) and \((8-7s)(s+1)\le27/4\) on \(s\in[1/2,4/5]\).

Moreover, both thresholds occur before the middle projection values in (21) and (22). Indeed, the cap areas at those values are respectively
\[
\frac{\delta_0}{\Delta_0}
=\frac{8-7s}{3(s+1)}\ge\frac49,
\qquad
\frac1{16}\frac{\delta_2}{\Delta_2}
=\frac{3(s+1)}{16(13s-2)}
\ge\frac9{224}>\frac1{36}.
\]
Thus the assumption that each outer cap has area below \(1/36\) puts both caps in their first quadratic pieces. Equations (21) and (22) give
\[
|C_0|+|C_2|
=\frac{75}{16(s+1)}
\left(
\frac{x^2}{8-7s}+\frac{y^2}{13s-2}
\right).
\]
By Cauchy-Schwarz and (23),
\[
|C_0|+|C_2|
\ge
\frac{75}{16(s+1)}
\frac{L^2}{(8-7s)+(13s-2)}
=\frac{25(2-s)^2}{288(s+1)^2}
\ge\frac{25}{648}>\frac1{36},
\]
because \((2-s)/(s+1)\ge2/3\) for \(s\le4/5\). This contradiction completes the proof of (14).

Combining (13) and (14) gives
\[
|C_0|+|C_1|+|C_2|
\ge\frac1{36}+\frac5{12}
=\frac49.
\tag{24}
\]
Together, (10), (12), and (24) cover every trace direction and every height slope \(a\). Thus every proper closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\) has total three-slice mass at least \(4/9\), proving \(h_S((1,z))\ge4/9\).

Finally, if three proper closed halfspaces of mass strictly below \(4/9\) had height-one trace interiors covering \(A_1\), one trace interior would contain \(z\). Its three-dimensional halfspace would then contain \((1,z)\), contradicting the established depth bound. Therefore the requested three-halfspace cover does not exist.
