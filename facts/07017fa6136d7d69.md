## Statement

Let \(D\) be a nondegenerate triangle of physical area \(\Delta\), and use barycentric coordinates on \(D\). For real parameters \(a,b,c,d,e,f\), let \(H(a,b,c,d,e,f)\) be the possibly degenerate cyclic polygon with vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_0&=(1-f,f,0),&x_1&=(a,1-a,0),\\
x_2&=(0,1-b,b),&x_3&=(0,c,1-c),\\
x_4&=(d,0,1-d),&x_5&=(1-e,0,e).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(\ell_a,\ell_b,\ell_c,\ell_d,\ell_e,\ell_f,s_1,s_2,s_3\in\mathbb Q\) be arbitrary, and impose
\[
\begin{gathered}
a,b,c,d,e,f\geq0,\qquad
a\geq\ell_a,\ b\geq\ell_b,\ c\geq\ell_c,\ d\geq\ell_d,\ e\geq\ell_e,\ f\geq\ell_f,\\
s_3\leq a+f\leq1,\qquad
s_1\leq b+c\leq1,\qquad
s_2\leq d+e\leq1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Define the effective geometric lower bounds
\[
A=\max\{0,\ell_a\},\quad B=\max\{0,\ell_b\},\quad
C=\max\{0,\ell_c\},\quad D_0=\max\{0,\ell_d\},\quad
E=\max\{0,\ell_e\},\quad F=\max\{0,\ell_f\},
\tag{2}
\]
and the effective target sums
\[
t_3=\max\{s_3,A+F\},\qquad
t_1=\max\{s_1,B+C\},\qquad
t_2=\max\{s_2,D_0+E\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The bound polytope (1) is nonempty if and only if
\[
t_1\leq1,\qquad t_2\leq1,\qquad t_3\leq1.
\tag{4}
\]
Equivalently, all three effective lower-bound pair sums and all three requested lower sums are at most \(1\).

Assume (4). There is a loss minimizer with
\[
a+f=t_3,\qquad b+c=t_1,\qquad d+e=t_2.
\tag{5}
\]
For a specified pair, its requested constraint is itself tight, for example \(a+f=s_3\), if and only if \(s_3\geq A+F\); if \(s_3<A+F\), that equality is infeasible and the constraint is redundant. The analogous criteria are \(s_1\geq B+C\) and \(s_2\geq D_0+E\). Equality in these comparisons is allowed.

Put
\[
a_0=A,\quad a_1=t_3-F,\qquad
b_0=B,\quad b_1=t_1-C,\qquad
d_0=D_0,\quad d_1=t_2-E
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\Gamma(u,v,w)=uv+(t_1-v)w+(t_2-w)(t_3-u).
\tag{7}
\]
Then the exact minimum relative area loss is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m=\min\{&
\Gamma(a_0,b_0,d_0),\Gamma(a_0,b_0,d_1),
\Gamma(a_0,b_1,d_0),\Gamma(a_0,b_1,d_1),\\
&\Gamma(a_1,b_0,d_0),\Gamma(a_1,b_0,d_1),
\Gamma(a_1,b_1,d_0),\Gamma(a_1,b_1,d_1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the exact maximum physical area in this closed fiber is
\[
\max |H|=\Delta(1-m).
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficient \(m\) and all feasibility and corner data in (2)-(8) are obtained by exact rational arithmetic and finite rational comparisons; (9) then multiplies by the physical scale \(\Delta\). Formula (8) remains valid when any interval in (6) collapses, when any parameter is zero, and when \(t_i=1\), which forces the corresponding support edge to have zero length. Points of (1) whose pair sum is \(1\) even when \(t_i<1\) are also included and are covered by the tightening argument.

For the numerical lower bounds, sums, and support-triangle scale in accepted fact `faa6f23d89f50659`, formula (8) gives
\[
m=
\frac{40\,351\,140\,305\,732\,756\,252\,537}
     {52\,732\,819\,270\,053\,605\,182\,500}
\]
and, with \(\Delta=9801/20000\),
\[
\max |H|=
\frac{12\,381\,678\,964\,320\,848\,929\,963}
     {107\,607\,018\,202\,333\,650\,000\,000}.
\tag{10}
\]
The certificate applies exactly to fibers with the displayed cyclic support-edge parametrization and constraints (1). It makes no assertion that a different cyclic order or a different support-incidence type admits the same parametrization.

## Proof

The nonnegativity constraints and the six stated lower bounds are equivalent to the effective bounds (2). If (1) has a feasible point, then
\[
a+f\geq\max\{s_3,A+F\}=t_3,
\]
while \(a+f\leq1\), so \(t_3\leq1\). The same argument gives \(t_1,t_2\leq1\). Conversely, if (4) holds, then
\[
(a,f)=(A,t_3-A),\qquad
(b,c)=(B,t_1-B),\qquad
(d,e)=(D_0,t_2-D_0)
\]
satisfy every constraint: for instance \(t_3-A\geq F\) follows from \(t_3\geq A+F\). This proves the exact feasibility test, including negative raw lower bounds and negative or redundant \(s_i\).

The relative loss is
\[
L(a,b,c,d,e,f)=ab+cd+ef.
\tag{11}
\]
It is coordinatewise nondecreasing on the nonnegative orthant. More explicitly, suppose \(a+f>t_3\), put \(r=a+f-t_3\), and choose
\[
\rho_a=\min\{r,a-A\},\qquad \rho_f=r-\rho_a.
\]
Since
\[
r\leq(a-A)+(f-F),
\]
we have \(0\leq\rho_a\leq a-A\) and \(0\leq\rho_f\leq f-F\). Replacing \(a,f\) by \(a-\rho_a,f-\rho_f\) preserves feasibility, makes their sum \(t_3\), and changes the loss by
\[
L(a-\rho_a,b,c,d,e,f-\rho_f)-L(a,b,c,d,e,f)
=-\rho_a b-\rho_f e\leq0.
\]
The identical construction for \(b,c\) decreases the loss by a quantity of the form \(\rho_b a+\rho_c d\), and that for \(d,e\) decreases it by a quantity of the form \(\rho_d c+\rho_e f\). Applying the three reductions successively proves (5), with no strict-positivity assumption. Hence zero partners and every tie are included.

The equality \(a+f=s_3\) is compatible with the lower bounds exactly when \(s_3\geq A+F\); under feasibility it is then the effective equality in (5). If \(s_3<A+F\), every feasible point has \(a+f\geq A+F>s_3\), so the requested equality is impossible. This proves the three stated tightness criteria.

On the equality face (5), eliminate
\[
f=t_3-a,\qquad c=t_1-b,\qquad e=t_2-d.
\]
The remaining feasible set is exactly the box
\[
[a_0,a_1]\times[b_0,b_1]\times[d_0,d_1],
\tag{12}
\]
because, for example, \(a\geq A\) and \(f\geq F\) are exactly \(a_0\leq a\leq a_1\). Its intervals are nonempty by (3). Substitution in (11) gives
\[
\Phi(a,b,d)=ab+(t_1-b)d+(t_2-d)(t_3-a)=\Gamma(a,b,d).
\tag{13}
\]
This polynomial is affine in each variable separately. If all three intervals in (12) have positive length, repeated one-dimensional affine interpolation expresses \(\Phi(a,b,d)\) as
\[
\sum_{i,j,k\in\{0,1\}}
\lambda_i(a)\mu_j(b)\nu_k(d)\Phi(a_i,b_j,d_k),
\tag{14}
\]
where the eight coefficients are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). Therefore every box value is at least the least corner value, and a corner realizing that least value is feasible. If an interval collapses, omit that interpolation step; (8) merely lists duplicate corners. Thus (8) is exact in every collapsed, zero-parameter, and slope-tie case.

The two vertices adjacent to the second barycentric corner cut from \(D\) a triangle of area \(\Delta ab\); the analogous corner triangles have areas \(\Delta cd\) and \(\Delta ef\). The upper sum constraints in (1) give the stated cyclic order, so these corner triangles have disjoint interiors. Consequently, including coincident vertices and collinear limits,
\[
|H|=\Delta(1-ab-cd-ef)=\Delta(1-L).
\tag{15}
\]
Combining (8), (11), and (15) proves (9).

For the data recorded in accepted fact `faa6f23d89f50659`, each requested sum exceeds its corresponding effective lower-bound sum, so \(t_i=s_i\), and (6) is exactly the three-variable box recorded there. Its exact margins give
\[
s_2-b_1-d_1>0,\qquad a_1-d_1>0,\qquad
a_1-b_0+s_1-s_3>0.
\]
Thus (13) first decreases to \(a=a_1\), then increases with \(b\) and with \(d\), selecting the corner \((a_1,b_0,d_0)\). Direct substitution gives the value of \(m\) and the scaled area in (10). The prescribed area in that predecessor exceeds (10) by
\[
\frac{11\,664\,845\,540\,778\,202\,767\,703}
     {122\,979\,449\,374\,095\,600\,000\,000}>0,
\]
so the general formula reproduces its numerical contradiction. This check uses only the fixed incidence type stated here and does not extend the conclusion to any other terminal code.
