## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes in the exact residual area domain, with
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,
\qquad
k=|K|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9<M.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions whose linear parts are pairwise nonparallel and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(j=1,2,3\), put
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|,
\]
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j+|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
     +|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\qquad
I_j=\{a:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Assume
\[
c_j+m\ge t,\qquad I_j\ne\varnothing,\qquad
L_j=\inf I_j
\quad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{4}
\]

Relabel the strict-empty direction as \(q\), write
\[
\{i,\ell,q\}=\{1,2,3\},
\qquad
\alpha_q=\min_{A_0}f_q,
\qquad
L_q<\alpha_q,
\tag{5}
\]
and set
\[
\Delta=\left|\det(\nabla f_i,\nabla f_\ell)\right|>0,
\qquad
D=-1-\alpha_q-L_i-L_\ell.
\tag{6}
\]
Suppose \(D>0\) and that the large endpoint is the maximal residual-wedge triangle
\[
A_0=
\{x\in\mathbb R^2:
f_i(x)\ge L_i,\
f_\ell(x)\ge L_\ell,\
f_q(x)\ge\alpha_q\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Then rows \(i\) and \(\ell\) cannot both satisfy (4). Equivalently, no canonical finite-boundary configuration in the exact residual area domain can have the form (5)-(7).

More precisely, if rows \(i\) and \(\ell\) were both genuine lower crossings, then
\[
m\ge M=\frac{D^2}{2\Delta}.
\tag{8}
\]
But accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives \(m<M\) throughout the exact residual area domain, so (8) is impossible.

This family lies strictly inside the complementary regime requested here. Indeed, (7) gives
\[
M=\frac{D^2}{2\Delta},
\]
and therefore
\[
D^2-2\Delta(M-2m)=4\Delta m>0.
\tag{9}
\]
It also automatically has the strict-low level sum
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3
=-1-D-(\alpha_q-L_q)<-1.
\tag{10}
\]

In canonical rational coordinates, the excluded family is completely explicit. Take
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad
f_2(x,y)=y,\qquad
f_3(x,y)=-1-x-y,
\tag{11}
\]
choose arbitrary rational numbers
\[
D>0,\qquad L_2,L_3\in\mathbb Q,\qquad g>0,
\]
and put
\[
\alpha=-1-D-L_2-L_3,\qquad L_1=\alpha-g,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
A_0=
\operatorname{conv}\{
(\alpha,L_2),\
(\alpha+D,L_2),\
(\alpha,L_2+D)
\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(q=1\), \(i=2\), \(\ell=3\), \(\Delta=1\),
\[
|A_0|=\frac{D^2}{2},
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3=-1-D-g<-1,
\tag{14}
\]
and (9) holds for every \(m>0\). Nevertheless, no rational or irrational choice of \(A_2,K\) can complete (13) to three finite lower crossings in the exact residual domain.

## Proof

We do not use the pending support-wedge packet. Everything below follows directly from the displayed family, elementary polygon sections, and the two accepted predecessors declared above.

Define the affine coordinate map
\[
\Phi(x)=
\bigl(f_i(x)-L_i,\ f_\ell(x)-L_\ell\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
Its absolute Jacobian is \(\Delta\). By (2) and (6),
\[
f_q(x)-\alpha_q
=D-\bigl(f_i(x)-L_i\bigr)
   -\bigl(f_\ell(x)-L_\ell\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently, (7) is carried bijectively by \(\Phi\) onto
\[
T_D=\{(a,b):a\ge0,\ b\ge0,\ a+b\le D\}.
\tag{17}
\]
This proves
\[
M=|A_0|=\frac{|T_D|}{\Delta}
=\frac{D^2}{2\Delta}.
\tag{18}
\]
It also shows that the row-\(i\) and row-\(\ell\) lower caps at \(L_i,L_\ell\) have area zero: they are the two supporting sides \(a=0\) and \(b=0\).

Let
\[
Q=\Phi(A_2).
\]
Then \(Q\) is a nonempty two-dimensional convex polygon and
\[
|Q|=\Delta m.
\tag{19}
\]
For a first-coordinate value \(a\), let
\[
\lambda_i(a)
=\left|\{b:(a,b)\in Q\}\right|
\]
be the length of the vertical section, and define analogously the horizontal-section length
\[
\lambda_\ell(b)
=\left|\{a:(a,b)\in Q\}\right|.
\tag{20}
\]
For a polygon these section lengths are continuous and piecewise affine on the interior of their projection intervals. At an endpoint, their one-sided interior limits equal the length of the corresponding supporting face, possibly zero. This follows directly because the upper and lower endpoints of a section lie on fixed polygon edges between consecutive projected vertex values and hence extend affinely to each endpoint.

Consider row \(i\). In the first coordinate of (15), its \(A_2\)-cap at level \(L_i\) is cut at
\[
\tau_i=-2L_i,
\tag{21}
\]
because
\[
f_i\le-L_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f_i-L_i\le-2L_i.
\]
The \(A_0\)-cap is empty in area at \(L_i\). Accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`, applied using (1), (3), and (4), gives the exact crossing equality at \(L_i\) and says that the \(A_2\)-cap is never empty. There are exactly two possibilities relevant here:

1. If \(c_i+m>t\), the \(A_2\)-cap is proper, so \(\tau_i\) lies in the interior of the first-coordinate projection of \(Q\).

2. If \(c_i+m=t\), the empty \(A_0\)-cap forces the \(A_2\)-cap to be full and the same accepted fact gives support contact. Thus \(\tau_i\) is the upper first-coordinate support of \(Q\).

In the proper case define
\[
\lambda_i^*=\lambda_i(\tau_i).
\]
In the full case define \(\lambda_i^*\) as the left interior limit of \(\lambda_i(a)\) at \(\tau_i\). By the polygon observation above, \(Q\) contains on the line \(a=\tau_i\) a closed vertical segment of length \(\lambda_i^*\).

For \(0<\varepsilon<D\), (17) gives the exact \(A_0\)-cap increment
\[
\left|A_0\cap
\{f_i\le L_i+\varepsilon\}\right|
=\frac1\Delta
\int_0^\varepsilon(D-a)\,da
=\frac{D\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2/2}{\Delta}.
\tag{22}
\]
On the other hand, increasing the row level from \(L_i\) to \(L_i+\varepsilon\) lowers the first-coordinate threshold of the \(A_2\)-cap from \(\tau_i\) to \(\tau_i-\varepsilon\). Hence its area change is
\[
-\frac1\Delta
\int_{\tau_i-\varepsilon}^{\tau_i}
\lambda_i(a)\,da.
\tag{23}
\]

Because \(L_i=\inf I_i\) and \(I_i\) is nonempty and open, there is a sequence
\[
\varepsilon_n\downarrow0
\quad\text{with}\quad
\mu_i(L_i+\varepsilon_n)<t=\mu_i(L_i),
\tag{24}
\]
where the equality is supplied by accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`. Combining (22)-(24), dividing by \(\varepsilon_n\), and taking the limit gives
\[
D-\lambda_i^*\le0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\lambda_i^*\ge D.
\tag{25}
\]
The argument includes the full-cap support-face case: if that face were a point or had length below \(D\), the mass would initially increase, contradicting (24).

Applying the same argument with the two coordinates interchanged gives a closed horizontal segment in \(Q\), on the line
\[
b=\tau_\ell:=-2L_\ell,
\]
whose length \(\lambda_\ell^*\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_\ell^*\ge D.
\tag{26}
\]

We now use both nonparallel rows simultaneously. The following elementary segment bound is exact:

> If a convex set in the \((a,b)\)-plane contains a vertical segment of length \(u\) and a horizontal segment of length \(v\), then its area is at least \(uv/2\).

To prove it, let \(P\) be the intersection of the two lines carrying the segments. If \(P\) belongs to both segments, their convex hull has the two segments as perpendicular-coordinate diagonals and has area \(uv/2\), including endpoint intersections. If \(P\) does not belong to the vertical segment, that segment lies strictly on one side of the horizontal line. One of its endpoints has distance at least \(u\) from the horizontal line, and the triangle formed by this endpoint and the full horizontal segment has area at least \(uv/2\). If \(P\) belongs to the vertical segment but not the horizontal segment, interchange the two roles. In every case the stated triangle or quadrilateral lies in the convex set.

Apply this bound to the two segments supplied by (25)-(26). Convexity of \(Q\) gives
\[
|Q|
\ge\frac{\lambda_i^*\lambda_\ell^*}{2}
\ge\frac{D^2}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]
Using (18)-(19),
\[
\Delta m=|Q|
\ge\frac{D^2}{2}
=\Delta M,
\]
and hence
\[
m\ge M.
\tag{28}
\]

It remains to compare (28) with the registered residual hypotheses. Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
0<\frac mM<
\rho:=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}.
\tag{29}
\]
Moreover \(\rho<1\), since
\[
27-2\sqrt{26}<25
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1<\sqrt{26}.
\]
Thus (29) gives \(m<M\), contradicting (28). This proves that no \(A_2\), and therefore no compatible completion \(K\), realizes the maximal-wedge triangle family.

For completeness, (10) follows without any crossing input. Equation (6) gives
\[
L_i+L_\ell=-1-\alpha_q-D,
\]
so
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3
=L_q+L_i+L_\ell
=-1-D-(\alpha_q-L_q)<-1.
\]
Equations (18) and \(m>0\) give (9). Hence the excluded rational family is genuinely in the complementary wedge regime and already has the required strict level sum; its first failure is the simultaneous one-sided crossing geometry in the two remaining directions, not the earlier scalar wedge clearance.
