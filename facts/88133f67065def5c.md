## Statement

Assume the surviving \(N=3\) coordinate-sign normal form of accepted fact
`09393c6edf1fc4e2` and the exact residual area domain of accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e`. Thus
\[
q>1,\qquad 0<m\le M,\qquad 1+m<2M,
\tag{1}
\]
and the strict residual area-triangle inequalities are
\[
M-m<1<M+m,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
M<1+m,\quad 1<m+M.
\tag{1a}
\]
There are nondegenerate triangles
\[
P_0\subset\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1+q\right\},\qquad
P_1\subset\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1\right\},\qquad
P_2\subset\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1-q\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
satisfying
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\tag{3}
\]
Normalize planar area by \(|P_1|\), so
\[
|P_0|=m,\qquad |P_1|=1,\qquad |P_2|=M.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(J\subseteq N:=\{1,2,3\}\), define the coordinate sign cells
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{0,J}
&=P_0\cap
\{x_i\ge0\ (i\in J),\ x_i<0\ (i\notin J)\},\\
P_{1,J}
&=P_1\cap
\{x_i\ge0\ (i\in J),\ x_i<0\ (i\notin J)\},\\
P_{2,J}
&=P_2\cap
\{x_i\ge0\ (i\in J),\ x_i<0\ (i\notin J)\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
and write
\[
p_J=|P_{0,J}|,\qquad r_J=|P_{1,J}|,\qquad z_J=|P_{2,J}|.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
b_i=\sum_{J\ni i}r_J,\qquad
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{J\ni i}z_J,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
E_0=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}(|J|-1)p_J,\qquad
E_1=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}(|J|-1)r_J,\qquad
E_2=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}(|J|-1)z_J.
\tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
T=1+m+M,\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{9}
\]
and suppose all three coordinate-cap rows are strict:
\[
b_i+u_i+v_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{10}
\]
Then:

1. The all-negative top cell satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{\quad
   z_\varnothing>\frac{T}{3}+E_0+E_1+E_2.
   \quad}
   \tag{11}
   \]

2. For every \(J,H\subseteq N\) with \(p_Jz_H>0\),
   \[
   \boxed{\quad
   \frac14\left(\sqrt{p_J}+\sqrt{z_H}\right)^2
   \le
   \sum_{\varnothing\ne K\subseteq J\cup H}r_K.
   \quad}
   \tag{12}
   \]

3. Let \(g_1\) be the centroid of \(P_1\). There is an index \(k\) such
   that \(x_k(g_1)>0\), and its middle cap satisfies
   \[
   b_k>\frac49.
   \tag{13}
   \]
   With
   \[
   d=t-\frac49=\frac{2(m+M-1)}9,
   \tag{14}
   \]
   every strict system satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{\quad
   \frac14\left(\sqrt{m-d}+\sqrt M\right)^2<\frac59.
   \quad}
   \tag{15}
   \]
   Consequently no strict \(N=3\) system exists whenever
   \[
   \boxed{\quad
   \left(
   \sqrt{m-\frac{2(m+M-1)}9}+\sqrt M
   \right)^2\ge\frac{20}{9}.
   \quad}
   \tag{16}
   \]

## Proof

We first derive the area inequalities used later without relying on an
undefined inherited strip. In accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, the
residual variables are
\[
r=\frac{m}{M},\qquad s=\frac{a_1}{M}.
\]
The present normalization has \(a_1=|P_1|=1\), so
\[
r=\frac{m}{M},\qquad s=\frac1M.
\tag{17}
\]
The exact raw residual system in that accepted fact contains
\[
1-r<s<1+r.
\tag{18}
\]
Multiplying (18) by \(M>0\) and using (17) gives
\[
M-m<1<M+m.
\tag{19}
\]
The left inequality in (19) is \(M<1+m\), while the right inequality is
\(1<m+M\). This proves every assertion in (1a) directly from the cited
accepted residual-domain fact.

We next fix the boundary convention. On each plane in (2), every coordinate
function is nonconstant on a nondegenerate triangle. Indeed, a
two-dimensional triangle contained both in \(\sum_i x_i=c\) and in
\(x_k=c'\) would lie in the intersection of two distinct affine planes,
which is a line. Thus every coordinate cutting line has planar area zero.
The cells in (5) form area-disjoint partitions, and replacing any strict
negative inequality by its weak closure does not change a cell area.

The positive coordinate sums of \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) imply
\[
p_\varnothing=r_\varnothing=0.
\tag{20}
\]
For the bottom triangle,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^3u_i
&=\sum_{J\subseteq N}|J|p_J\\
&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}p_J
 +\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}(|J|-1)p_J
=m+E_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
The same calculation gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3b_i=1+E_1.
\tag{22}
\]
For the top triangle, its all-negative cell need not vanish, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^3v_i
&=\sum_{J\subseteq N}|J|z_J\\
&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}z_J
 +\sum_{\varnothing\ne J}(|J|-1)z_J\\
&=M-z_\varnothing+E_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Summing (10) and using (21)-(23) yields
\[
T+E_0+E_1+E_2-z_\varnothing
<3t=\frac{2T}{3}.
\]
Rearranging proves (11), including its strict sign.

We next prove every cellwise transport inequality. Fix \(J,H\subseteq N\)
with \(p_Jz_H>0\). Let
\[
\overline P_{0,J}
=P_0\cap
\{x_i\ge0\ (i\in J),\ x_i\le0\ (i\notin J)\},
\tag{24}
\]
\[
\overline P_{2,H}
=P_2\cap
\{x_i\ge0\ (i\in H),\ x_i\le0\ (i\notin H)\}.
\tag{25}
\]
These are compact convex bodies of positive area, and the zero-area cutting
lines give
\[
|\overline P_{0,J}|=p_J,\qquad
|\overline P_{2,H}|=z_H.
\tag{26}
\]
Take \(x\in\overline P_{0,J}\), \(y\in\overline P_{2,H}\), and
\[
w=\frac{x+y}{2}.
\]
Compatibility (3) puts \(w\) in \(P_1\). If \(i\notin J\cup H\), then
\[
x_i\le0,\qquad y_i\le0,\qquad w_i\le0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\overline P_{0,J}+\overline P_{2,H}}2
\subseteq
R_{J\cup H}:=
P_1\cap\{x_i\le0:i\notin J\cup H\}.
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(p_J>0\), equation (20) shows \(J\ne\varnothing\), so
\(J\cup H\ne\varnothing\). Since the coordinate sum on \(P_1\) is one,
every point of \(P_1\) has at least one nonnegative coordinate. Up to the
cutting lines,
\[
R_{J\cup H}
=
\bigcup_{\varnothing\ne K\subseteq J\cup H}P_{1,K},
\]
an area-disjoint union. Hence
\[
|R_{J\cup H}|
=\sum_{\varnothing\ne K\subseteq J\cup H}r_K.
\tag{28}
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski applied to the two positive-area convex bodies in
(24)-(25), followed by (27)-(28), gives
\[
\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_J}+\sqrt{z_H}\right)^2
\le
\left|
\frac{\overline P_{0,J}+\overline P_{2,H}}2
\right|
\le
\sum_{\varnothing\ne K\subseteq J\cup H}r_K.
\]
This proves (12). Notice that the proof fixes one pair \((J,H)\). It makes
no claim that the midpoint images for two different pairs are disjoint.

It remains to prove the centroid-row deletion bound. Since
\[
x_1(g_1)+x_2(g_1)+x_3(g_1)=1,
\]
some index \(k\) satisfies
\[
x_k(g_1)\ge\frac13>0.
\tag{29}
\]
We verify the exact triangle cap bound needed at this row. Let a nonconstant
affine functional on a triangle have sorted vertex values \(a\le b\le c\),
and normalize them to
\[
0,\qquad s,\qquad1,
\qquad
s=\frac{b-a}{c-a}\in[0,1].
\]
The normalized value at the centroid is \((1+s)/3\). If \(s\le1/2\), the
upper cap through the centroid has relative area
\[
\frac{(2-s)^2}{9(1-s)}\ge\frac49,
\tag{30}
\]
because \((2-s)^2-4(1-s)=s^2\). If \(s\ge1/2\), its relative area is
\[
1-\frac{(1+s)^2}{9s}\ge\frac49,
\tag{31}
\]
because
\[
5s-(1+s)^2=-s^2+3s-1>0
\qquad(1/2\le s\le1).
\]
At \(s=0\), equation (30) gives equality \(4/9\); all repeated-value
strata are therefore included.

Apply this calculation to the coordinate functional \(x_k\) on \(P_1\).
The cap \(\{x_k\ge x_k(g_1)\}\) has area at least \(4/9\). By (29), it is
strictly contained in \(\{x_k\ge0\}\). The intervening nonempty strip has
positive area because \(x_k\) is nonconstant. Therefore
\[
b_k=|P_1\cap\{x_k\ge0\}|>\frac49,
\]
which proves (13).

We now use the explicitly derived residual inequalities (19). Define \(d\)
by (14). The right inequality \(1<m+M\) in (19) gives
\[
d=\frac{2(m+M-1)}9>0.
\tag{32}
\]
The left inequality \(M-m<1\), equivalently \(M<1+m\), gives
\[
m+M-1<2m,
\]
and therefore
\[
d<\frac{4m}{9}<m.
\tag{33}
\]
This proves
\[
0<d<m
\tag{34}
\]
before any later use of that fact.

The low-row inequality (10), together with (13), gives
\[
u_k+v_k<t-b_k<d.
\tag{35}
\]
In particular, using \(u_k,v_k\ge0\), (34), and \(m\le M\),
\[
m-u_k>0,\qquad M-v_k>0.
\tag{36}
\]

Define the closed complementary caps
\[
D_0=P_0\cap\{x_k\le0\},\qquad
D_2=P_2\cap\{x_k\le0\},\qquad
D_1=P_1\cap\{x_k\le0\}.
\tag{37}
\]
They are compact and convex. Cutting-line nullity and (36) give
\[
|D_0|=m-u_k>0,\qquad
|D_2|=M-v_k>0,\qquad
|D_1|=1-b_k.
\tag{38}
\]
For \(x\in D_0\) and \(y\in D_2\), compatibility gives
\((x+y)/2\in P_1\), while its \(k\)-th coordinate is nonpositive. Hence
\[
\frac{D_0+D_2}{2}\subseteq D_1.
\tag{39}
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski now yields
\[
1-b_k
\ge
\frac14\left(\sqrt{m-u_k}+\sqrt{M-v_k}\right)^2.
\tag{40}
\]

We minimize the last radical sum exactly under (35). Put
\[
e=u_k+v_k<d.
\]
By (34), \(e<m\). For fixed \(e\), the function
\[
f(u)=\sqrt{m-u}+\sqrt{M-e+u},
\qquad 0\le u\le e,
\tag{41}
\]
is concave, so its minimum occurs at an endpoint. Since \(M\ge m\),
\[
\sqrt m-\sqrt{m-e}
\ge
\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-e},
\]
and hence
\[
\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
\le
\sqrt m+\sqrt{M-e}.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus the smaller endpoint value in (41) is
\(\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M\). Because \(e<d\),
\[
\sqrt{m-u_k}+\sqrt{M-v_k}
\ge\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
>\sqrt{m-d}+\sqrt M.
\tag{43}
\]
Combining (13), (40), and (43) proves
\[
\frac14\left(\sqrt{m-d}+\sqrt M\right)^2
<1-b_k<\frac59,
\]
which is (15). If the left side of (16) is at least \(20/9\), then the
left side of (15) is at least \(5/9\), a contradiction. This proves (16).

STRATA AND STRICTNESS AUDIT
All sign cells use one fixed disjoint convention in (5). Their closures are
used only to apply Brunn-Minkowski, and cutting lines have zero area on every
nondegenerate triangle. Formula (12) is asserted only when both endpoint
cells have positive area, so both closed cells are genuine
two-dimensional convex bodies. The centroid calculation includes distinct,
repeated-low, and repeated-high vertex-value strata. The exact residual
inequalities are stated in (1a), derived from accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e` in (17)-(19), and used explicitly in (32)-(34) before
\(d<m\) enters the complement and minimization arguments. Strictness in
(11) comes from the three strict row masses; strictness in (13) comes from
the positive centroid offset and a positive-area strip; strictness in (15)
comes from \(u_k+v_k<d\). No step adds or otherwise treats midpoint images
from different cell pairs as disjoint.
