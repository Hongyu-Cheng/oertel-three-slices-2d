## Statement

Assume the canonical root setting of accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`, normalize
\[
M=|A_0|=1,\qquad r=|A_2|,\qquad s=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9,
\]
and assume the root left-tail Regime A of accepted fact `ab2620c83e116de2`.  Thus, for one index \(j\),
\[
c_j+r<t,\qquad
\delta:=2t-s+c_j\geq r,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
0<r<\frac13,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\begin{cases}
1-r<s<1+r,&0<r\leq1/5,\\
1-r<s<2-4r,&1/5<r<1/3.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(i,j,\ell\) be distinct.  Choose actual low shifts \(a_i,a_\ell\) as in accepted fact `c0b5fee36666b707`, so
\[
u_h:=|A_0\cap\{f_h\leq a_h\}|,\qquad
v_h:=|A_2\cap\{f_h\leq-a_h\}|,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
c_h+u_h+v_h<t\qquad(h=i,\ell).
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
P=A_0\cap\{f_i>a_i,\ f_\ell>a_\ell\},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
Q=A_2\cap\{f_i>-a_i,\ f_\ell>-a_\ell\},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
R=A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-a_i,\ f_\ell\leq-a_\ell\},
\tag{8}
\]
and put
\[
p=|P|,\qquad q=|Q|,\qquad \omega=|R|.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
0<\delta<\frac{2-s-r}{3}<\frac13,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
v_i+v_\ell=r-q+\omega,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
p>1-\delta+r-q+\omega>\frac23.
\tag{12}
\]
Moreover, exactly the following geometric alternatives apply.

1. If \(q>0\), then
\[
4c_j>1-\delta+r+\omega
\tag{13}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\quad c_j>\frac t2+\frac{\omega}{5}.\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, (1) and (14) force
\[
\boxed{\quad 8r+\frac{9\omega}{5}<1+s.\quad}
\tag{15}
\]

2. If \(q=0\) and \(\omega=0\), then, up to interchanging \(i\) and \(\ell\),
\[
v_i=r,\qquad v_\ell=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\quad c_i+r<t,\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
so a second root left tail is forced.

3. If \(q=0\) and \(\omega>0\), then
\[
0<\omega<\delta-r,
\tag{18}
\]
and the true midpoint body gives the aggregate middle charge
\[
\boxed{\quad
c_1+c_2+c_3
>
\frac14
\left(
\sqrt{\,1-\delta+r+\omega\,}+\sqrt{\omega}
\right)^2.
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
Also every individual middle cap obeys
\[
c_h<t-\omega\qquad(h=1,2,3),
\tag{20}
\]
so this alternative has the exact necessary scalar consequence
\[
\frac14
\left(
\sqrt{\,1-\delta+r+\omega\,}+\sqrt{\omega}
\right)^2
<3(t-\omega).
\tag{21}
\]

Thus, if the two small-endpoint complementary high caps in (7) cover \(A_2\) up to a null set, then \(\omega=0\): positive simultaneous complement gives (14), while zero simultaneous complement gives the tail propagation (17).  If they do not cover \(A_2\) up to a null set, then \(\omega>0\), and either (14) holds or the extra midpoint charge (19) holds.

## Proof

Accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194` gives nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\tag{22}
\]
as well as the compatibility inclusion
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{23}
\]
The same compatibility inclusion, and the planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality used below, are recorded in accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8`.

First derive the scalar bounds which will be used without losing the actual shifts.  Since the middle caps cover \(K\), equation (22) gives
\[
k-c_j\leq c_i+c_\ell.
\tag{24}
\]
Adding the two strict low-mass inequalities in (5), and then using (24), gives
\[
u_i+u_\ell+v_i+v_\ell
<2t-c_i-c_\ell
\leq2t-s+c_j
=\delta.
\tag{25}
\]
This is the actual-shift version of the estimate in accepted fact `c0b5fee36666b707`.

Accepted fact `ab2620c83e116de2` writes
\[
\delta-r=x-\alpha,\qquad
x<\beta,\qquad
\beta-\alpha=\frac{2-s-4r}{3},
\]
where \(x=c_j\).  Therefore
\[
\delta
<
r+\frac{2-s-4r}{3}
=\frac{2-s-r}{3}.
\tag{26}
\]
Since (3) gives \(s+r>1\), equation (26) proves (10).

All affine cutting lines have planar area zero.  Inclusion-exclusion on \(A_2\), with the two closed low caps from (8) and their strict complements, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_i+v_\ell
&=
\left|
\{f_i\leq-a_i\}\cup\{f_\ell\leq-a_\ell\}
\right|
+\left|
\{f_i\leq-a_i,\ f_\ell\leq-a_\ell\}
\right|\\
&=(r-q)+\omega,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (11).  Similarly,
\[
p
=
1-
\left|
\{f_i\leq a_i\}\cup\{f_\ell\leq a_\ell\}
\right|
\geq1-u_i-u_\ell.
\tag{27}
\]
Combining (25), (11), and (27) yields
\[
p>1-\delta+r-q+\omega.
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(0\leq q\leq r\), equations (10) and (28) prove (12).

Suppose first that \(q>0\).  The closures of \(P\) and \(Q\) are compact two-dimensional convex bodies of areas \(p\) and \(q\).  For \(x_0\in\overline P\) and \(x_2\in\overline Q\), one has
\[
f_i(x_0)\geq a_i,\qquad f_i(x_2)\geq-a_i,
\]
and the analogous two inequalities for \(\ell\).  Hence
\[
f_i\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)\geq0,\qquad
f_\ell\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)\geq0.
\]
Equations (22)-(23) therefore give
\[
\frac{\overline P+\overline Q}{2}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_j\leq-1\}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_j\leq0\}.
\tag{29}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski, (28), and (29) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
4c_j
&\geq(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\\
&\geq p+q\\
&>1-\delta+r+\omega,
\end{aligned}
\]
proving (13).  Since
\[
1-\delta+r
=1+r+s-2t-c_j
=\frac52t-c_j,
\tag{30}
\]
equation (13) gives
\[
5c_j>\frac52t+\omega,
\]
which is (14).  Combining (14) with the strict tail in (1) gives
\[
r+\frac{\omega}{5}<\frac t2=\frac{1+r+s}{9},
\]
equivalent to (15).

Now suppose \(q=\omega=0\).  Consider the affine map
\[
\Phi:A_2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,\qquad
\Phi(z)=\bigl(f_i(z)+a_i,\ f_\ell(z)+a_\ell\bigr).
\tag{31}
\]
The two linear parts are nonparallel by accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`, so \(\Phi\) is an affine isomorphism from the ambient plane to \(\mathbb R^2\).  Thus \(\Phi(A_2)\) is a two-dimensional compact convex body with connected interior.

The condition \(q=0\) says that its intersection with the open first quadrant has zero area, hence is empty in the interior.  The condition \(\omega=0\) says the same for the open third quadrant.  Therefore the connected open set \(\operatorname{int}\Phi(A_2)\) lies entirely in one of the two remaining open quadrants.  Its closure lies in the closure of that quadrant.  Up to interchanging \(i\) and \(\ell\), this gives
\[
f_i+a_i\leq0,\qquad f_\ell+a_\ell\geq0
\quad\hbox{on }A_2.
\tag{32}
\]
The two zero lines have planar area zero, so (32) proves (16).  The \(i\)-th strict low-mass inequality in (5) then gives
\[
c_i+r\leq c_i+u_i+r<t,
\]
which proves (17).

Finally suppose \(q=0<\omega\).  Equation (11) becomes
\[
v_i+v_\ell=r+\omega.
\]
Together with (25) and nonnegativity of \(u_i+u_\ell\), this proves (18).  The closures of \(P\) and \(R\) are compact two-dimensional convex bodies, of areas \(p\) and \(\omega\).  Compatibility gives the true midpoint body
\[
D:=\frac{\overline P+\overline R}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{33}
\]
Equation (22) implies that every point of \(D\) belongs to at least one of the three middle caps \(K\cap\{f_h\leq0\}\).  Therefore
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\geq|D|.
\tag{34}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski, (28) with \(q=0\), and (33)-(34) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1+c_2+c_3
&\geq\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt\omega)^2\\
&>
\frac14
\left(
\sqrt{\,1-\delta+r+\omega\,}+\sqrt\omega
\right)^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (19).

The set \(R\) is contained in both small-endpoint low caps, so
\[
v_i\geq\omega,\qquad v_\ell\geq\omega.
\]
Their strict low-mass inequalities give \(c_i,c_\ell<t-\omega\).  Also \(\omega\leq r\), and the root tail in (1) gives
\[
c_j<t-r\leq t-\omega.
\]
This proves (20).  Summing (20) and comparing with (19) proves (21), completing the trichotomy.
