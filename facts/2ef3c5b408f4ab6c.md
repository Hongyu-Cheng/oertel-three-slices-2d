## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies with
\[
M=|A_0|>m=|A_2|>0.
\]
For every nonzero linear form \(\lambda\), define
\[
q(\lambda)=
\inf\{s:|A_0\cap\{\lambda\le s\}|\ge m\},
\qquad
\beta(\lambda)=\max_{a\in A_2}\lambda(a),
\]
and put
\[
c(\lambda)=q(\lambda)+\beta(\lambda).
\]
Then \(q\), \(\beta\), and \(c\) are continuous on the space of
nonzero linear forms.

Suppose no translate \(z-A_2\) is contained in \(D_m(A_0)\).  There
exist three pairwise nonparallel nonzero linear forms satisfying
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=0
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3c(\lambda_i)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
The forms can be chosen arbitrarily close to a positive zero-sum
certificate supplied by accepted fact `9aa8eaca3b60ecf3`.
Consequently, a common positive scaling preserves pairwise
nonparallelism and makes the sum in (2) exceed \(2\).

## Proof

We first prove continuity of the cap quantile.  Fix a nonzero linear
form \(\lambda\), and define
\[
F_\lambda(s)=|A_0\cap\{\lambda\le s\}|.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(s_n\to s\), the corresponding indicator functions converge
pointwise away from the line \(\{\lambda=s\}\), which has planar
measure zero.  Dominated convergence shows that \(F_\lambda\) is
continuous.  If
\[
\alpha_\lambda=\min_{A_0}\lambda,
\qquad
\delta_\lambda=\max_{A_0}\lambda,
\]
then the lower support cap has area zero and the upper cap has area
\(M\).  Since \(0<m<M\), the infimum defining \(q(\lambda)\) is
attained and
\[
F_\lambda(q(\lambda))=m.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, attainment follows because
\(\{s:F_\lambda(s)\ge m\}\) is a nonempty closed upper set bounded
below.  If the value in (4) were greater than \(m\), continuity would
give a smaller threshold with cap area at least \(m\).

Let \(\lambda,\mu\) be any two nonzero forms and set
\[
\varepsilon_0=
\sup_{x\in A_0}|(\lambda-\mu)(x)|.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(x\in A_0\) and \(\lambda(x)\le q(\lambda)\), then
\[
\mu(x)\le q(\lambda)+\varepsilon_0.
\]
By (4), the \(\lambda\)-cap on the left has area \(m\).  Therefore
\[
q(\mu)\le q(\lambda)+\varepsilon_0.
\]
Interchanging \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) gives
\[
\boxed{
|q(\lambda)-q(\mu)|
\le
\sup_{x\in A_0}|(\lambda-\mu)(x)|.
}
\tag{6}
\]
When \(\mu\to\lambda\) in the two-dimensional dual space, compactness
of \(A_0\) makes the right side of (6) tend to zero.  Thus \(q\) is
continuous at every nonzero form.  No assertion at the zero form is
needed.

The support term satisfies the analogous estimate
\[
\boxed{
|\beta(\lambda)-\beta(\mu)|
\le
\sup_{a\in A_2}|(\lambda-\mu)(a)|.
}
\tag{7}
\]
For example,
\[
\beta(\lambda)
\le\beta(\mu)+\sup_{a\in A_2}|(\lambda-\mu)(a)|,
\]
and the reverse inequality follows by interchanging the forms.
Compactness of \(A_2\) now proves continuity of \(\beta\).  Equations
(6)-(7) prove continuity of \(c=q+\beta\) on the nonzero forms,
including directions exposing positive-length support faces.

Assume that no translate \(z-A_2\) lies in \(D_m(A_0)\).  Accepted fact
`9aa8eaca3b60ecf3` supplies nonzero forms
\(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3\) such that
\[
\nu_1+\nu_2+\nu_3=0,
\qquad
S:=c(\nu_1)+c(\nu_2)+c(\nu_3)>0.
\tag{8}
\]
We perturb this certificate explicitly.  Choose a linear form
\(\eta\) not parallel to \(\nu_1\), and for real \(\varepsilon\) put
\[
\lambda_1(\varepsilon)=\nu_1,\qquad
\lambda_2(\varepsilon)=\nu_2+\varepsilon\eta,\qquad
\lambda_3(\varepsilon)=\nu_3-\varepsilon\eta.
\tag{9}
\]
The identity in (8) gives
\[
\lambda_1(\varepsilon)+
\lambda_2(\varepsilon)+
\lambda_3(\varepsilon)=0
\tag{10}
\]
for every \(\varepsilon\).

Identify the dual space with \(\mathbb R^2\), and write
\([\,\cdot,\cdot\,]\) for its determinant.  Since
\([\nu_1,\eta]\ne0\),
\[
[\lambda_1(\varepsilon),\lambda_2(\varepsilon)]
=
[\nu_1,\nu_2]+\varepsilon[\nu_1,\eta].
\tag{11}
\]
If \(\nu_1,\nu_2\) are independent, the right side remains nonzero for
all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).  If they are parallel, it is
nonzero for every \(\varepsilon\ne0\).  Thus there are arbitrarily
small nonzero \(\varepsilon\) for which
\(\lambda_1(\varepsilon)\) and \(\lambda_2(\varepsilon)\) are
independent.

For a zero-sum triple, independence of the first two forms implies all
three forms are nonzero and pairwise nonparallel.  Indeed,
\(\lambda_3=-\lambda_1-\lambda_2\).  If \(\lambda_3\) were zero or
parallel to either \(\lambda_1\) or \(\lambda_2\), the first two forms
would be parallel.  This also shows that an initial nonzero zero-sum
triple has only two possibilities: it is already pairwise
nonparallel, or every pair is parallel.  Hence (9)-(11) cover every
initially parallel or repeated-direction case.

Because \(\nu_2,\nu_3\ne0\), all three arguments of \(c\) in (9) stay
nonzero for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).  The continuity proved
above gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&c(\lambda_1(\varepsilon))
+c(\lambda_2(\varepsilon))
+c(\lambda_3(\varepsilon))\\
&\hspace{35mm}\longrightarrow
c(\nu_1)+c(\nu_2)+c(\nu_3)=S>0
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
as \(\varepsilon\to0\).  Choose a sufficiently small nonzero
\(\varepsilon\) for which (11) is nonzero and the sum in (12) is
positive.  The resulting triple is arbitrarily close to the original
one and satisfies (1)-(2).

For clarity, consider directly the two-direction degeneration in the
proof of accepted fact `9aa8eaca3b60ecf3`.  Before padding, it has
opposite nonzero forms \(\mu,-\mu\) with
\[
c(\mu)+c(-\mu)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Its padded triple is
\[
\nu_1=\mu,\qquad
\nu_2=\nu_3=-\frac12\mu.
\]
Choose \(\eta\) not parallel to \(\mu\), and set
\[
\lambda_1=\mu,\qquad
\lambda_2=-\frac12\mu+\varepsilon\eta,\qquad
\lambda_3=-\frac12\mu-\varepsilon\eta.
\tag{14}
\]
These forms sum to zero.  If
\(\Delta=[\mu,\eta]\ne0\), then
\[
[\lambda_1,\lambda_2]=\varepsilon\Delta,\qquad
[\lambda_1,\lambda_3]=-\varepsilon\Delta,\qquad
[\lambda_2,\lambda_3]=\varepsilon\Delta.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus they are pairwise nonparallel for every
\(\varepsilon\ne0\).  Positive homogeneity from accepted fact
`9aa8eaca3b60ecf3` gives
\[
c(\mu)+2c\left(-\frac12\mu\right)
=c(\mu)+c(-\mu)>0.
\tag{16}
\]
By continuity, the perturbed sum for (14) remains positive for all
sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\).  This proves explicitly
that a two-opposite-direction Farkas certificate is not an obstruction
to a genuinely two-dimensional survivor.

Finally, let \(S'>0\) be the sum for the pairwise nonparallel triple
just constructed.  For every \(t>0\), positive homogeneity gives
\[
c(t\lambda_i)=tc(\lambda_i).
\]
Choose \(t>2/S'\).  The forms \(t\lambda_i\) remain nonzero,
zero-sum, and pairwise nonparallel, while
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3c(t\lambda_i)=tS'>2.
\]
This proves the final assertion.
