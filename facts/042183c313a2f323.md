## Statement

Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a compact polytope satisfying
\(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)\), where each \(A_i\) is a compact two-dimensional convex polygon. Retain \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(t=2T/9\), and the halfspace depth \(h_S\) of the locked problem.

Suppose there are \(z\in\operatorname{int}A_1\), pairwise nonparallel unit vectors \(q_1,q_2,q_3\), real slopes \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\), and positive weights \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
d&=h_S((1,z))=\max_{w\in A_1}h_S((1,w)),\\
\sum_{j=1}^3\alpha_j&=1,
\qquad \sum_{j=1}^3\alpha_jq_j=0,\\
\widehat H_j&=\{(h,w):q_j\cdot(w-z)+\lambda_j(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad \mathcal H_2(S\cap\widehat H_j)=d
\quad(j=1,2,3).
\end{aligned}
\]

Assume that \(z\) is the midpoint of every full middle chord
\(L_{1j}=A_1\cap\{w:q_j\cdot(w-z)=0\}\). Then there exists \(y\in S\) such that \(h_S(y)\ge2T/9\).

These are the attained active-cut objects of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`, with the additional full-middle-chord bisection hypothesis. The theorem allows positive selected outer lengths and partial endpoint caps. In particular, if a triple of slices is a counterexample to the locked problem, then every attained balanced active triple supplied by that accepted fact has at least one full middle chord whose midpoint differs from \(z\). For such a row, its full middle chord length \(b_j\) and its simultaneous selected outer length \(\ell_j\), with precisely that fact's selection convention, satisfy \(0<\ell_j<b_j\).

## Proof

Assume, for contradiction, that every point of \(S\) has depth below \(t\). In particular the attained value satisfies \(d<t\). The necessary residual inequalities in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` and the counterexample consequence of accepted fact `291c24bb45bd4946` give

\[
0<m<\frac{3M}{8},
\qquad M-m<a_1<M+m.
\tag{1}
\]

Reflect the height coordinate by \(h\mapsto2-h\), if necessary, so that \(a_0=M\) and \(a_2=m\). This exchanges the endpoint bodies and replaces every slope \(\lambda_j\) by \(-\lambda_j\); the point \(z\), the normals \(q_j\), the weights \(\alpha_j\), and all full middle chords retain their meanings. Reflection preserves depth and every hypothesis. Use the reflected objects below with the same notation.

Normalize the active planes by
\(g_j(w)=\alpha_jq_j\cdot(w-z)\) and
\(\sigma_j=\alpha_j\lambda_j\). Thus
\(\widehat H_j=\{(h,w):g_j(w)+\sigma_j(h-1)\le0\}\)
and \(\sum_jg_j=0\). This is exactly the concurrent normalization in accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`; shifted covering forms whose sum is \(-1\) are different objects and are not used here.

For each row let \(e_j\) be its actual total endpoint payment, namely

\[
e_j=
\left|A_0\cap\{g_j-\sigma_j\le0\}\right|
+\left|A_2\cap\{g_j+\sigma_j\le0\}\right|.
\tag{2}
\]

Put \(Q_j=\{g_j\le0\}\). Since row \(j\) has mass \(d\), its middle cap area is \(|A_1\cap Q_j|=d-e_j\). All these cap areas are planar areas. They are distinct from the canonical chord lengths \(b_j\) and selected lengths \(\ell_j\) of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`.

The three active cuts cover the whole middle plane, because their defining forms there sum to zero. They must cover the small endpoint plane as well. Indeed, suppose \(\sum_j\sigma_j\ge0\). At height zero the sum of the defining forms is then \(-\sum_j\sigma_j\le0\), so those cuts also cover the whole large endpoint plane. Consequently \(3d\ge M+a_1\). But (1) gives
\(M+a_1>2M-m>2m\), hence
\(M+a_1>2(M+m+a_1)/3=3t\). This contradicts \(d<t\). We therefore have \(\sum_j\sigma_j<0\). At height two the forms sum to this negative constant, proving that the three small-endpoint caps cover \(A_2\). If \(E=e_1+e_2+e_3\), it follows that \(E\ge m\).

Every individual payment also satisfies \(0\le e_j\le m\). For the upper bound, keep \(q_j\) and \(z\) fixed and replace the height slope by a sufficiently negative finite number. Compactness makes the large-endpoint contribution exactly zero and the small-endpoint contribution exactly \(m\): the height-zero inequality is \(q_j\cdot(w-z)\le\lambda\), and the height-two inequality is \(q_j\cdot(w-z)\le-\lambda\). The middle cap is unchanged. The resulting halfspace still contains \((1,z)\), whose depth is \(d\), so its mass \((d-e_j)+m\) is at least \(d\). This proves \(e_j\le m\). No limiting slope or section differentiability has been assumed. We have established

\[
0\le e_j\le m\quad(j=1,2,3),
\qquad E\ge m.
\tag{3}
\]

Relabel the rows so that \(e_1\le e_2\le e_3\), and put \(p=e_1+e_2\). For the concurrent three-halfplane fan in \(A_1\), let \(s_j\) be the area belonging only to \(Q_j\), and let \(D_j\) be the area belonging to the other two halfplanes and not to \(Q_j\). These are the singleton and doubleton areas of the planar assertion in accepted fact `d6ab13d2d8067def`. The notation \(D_j\) denotes a sector area and is distinct from the attained depth \(d\). Away from the three cutting lines, every point belongs to exactly one or exactly two halfplanes. The lines have planar area zero. Thus, for \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
d-e_i&=s_i+D_j+D_k,\\
s_i-D_i
&=a_1-(d-e_j)-(d-e_k)
=a_1-2d+E-e_i,\\
\sum_{i=1}^3D_i&=3d-E-a_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The planar individual-sector inequality proved in accepted fact `d6ab13d2d8067def` applies: \(A_1\) is a convex polygon, \(z\) is interior, the three lines are pairwise nonparallel with positively balanced forms, and all three full chords are bisected by \(z\). That planar assertion has no outer-section hypothesis. It gives
\(D_i\ge\min_j(s_j-D_j)=a_1-2d+p\) for each \(i\). This inequality remains valid if its right side is nonpositive. Summing it and using (4) gives
\(3d-E-a_1\ge3(a_1-2d+p)\), or
\(9d\ge4a_1+4p+e_3\). Therefore \(d<t\) and (1) imply

\[
4p+e_3
\le9d-4a_1
<2(M+m-a_1)
<4m.
\tag{5}
\]

Since \(e_3\ge0\), (5) implies \(p<m\). Define \(n=m-p\). Equations (3) and (5) imply \(0<n\le m\) and \(e_3\ge n\). Applying the same individual-sector inequality to \(D_1,D_2\) in the first identity of (4) also yields

\[
s_3=d-e_3-D_1-D_2
\le5d-2a_1-2p-e_3.
\tag{6}
\]

We now trim both endpoint bodies using the closed complements of rows 1 and 2. Let \(x\) be the sum of their two large-endpoint cap areas and \(y\) the sum of their two small-endpoint cap areas. Explicitly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=\sum_{j=1}^2\left|A_0\cap\{g_j-\sigma_j\le0\}\right|,\\
y&=\sum_{j=1}^2\left|A_2\cap\{g_j+\sigma_j\le0\}\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(x,y\ge0\) and \(x+y=p\). Define the compact convex polygons

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_0&=A_0\cap\{g_1-\sigma_1\ge0\}
             \cap\{g_2-\sigma_2\ge0\},\\
K_2&=A_2\cap\{g_1+\sigma_1\ge0\}
             \cap\{g_2+\sigma_2\ge0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The union bound for the removed parts gives
\(|K_0|\ge M-x>0\) and \(|K_2|\ge m-y>0\). Positivity follows from \(x,y\le p<m\le M\). These estimates include overlapping caps and all supporting or nontransverse outer cuts, because every boundary in (7) is an affine line of zero planar area.

Let \(\widetilde B=(K_0+K_2)/2\) be the midpoint sum of the trimmed bodies. Convexity of \(C\) gives \(\widetilde B\subseteq(A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\). If \(u\in K_0\) and \(v\in K_2\), then for \(j=1,2\),
\(g_j(u)\ge\sigma_j\) and \(g_j(v)\ge-\sigma_j\). Since \(g_j\) is affine,
\(g_j((u+v)/2)\ge0\). Thus
\(\widetilde B\subseteq A_1\cap\{g_1\ge0,g_2\ge0\}\), the closure of singleton sector 3, and \(|\widetilde B|\le s_3\).

To obtain the area lower bound using the declared accepted inputs, apply the necessity direction of accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` to \(K_0,\widetilde B,K_2\). They are exactly the three integer-height sections of the convex hull of \(\{0\}\times K_0\) and \(\{2\}\times K_2\). This is a compact polytope contained between heights zero and two. Its endpoint sections are \(K_0,K_2\), and its height-one section is exactly their midpoint sum \(\widetilde B\); all three sections are two-dimensional convex polygons. Therefore that accepted area-compatibility inequality gives

\[
s_3\ge|\widetilde B|
\ge\frac{(\sqrt{|K_0|}+\sqrt{|K_2|})^2}{4}
\ge\frac{(\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-y})^2}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]

Here \(m-y=n+x\) and \(0\le x\le p=m-n\). In particular,
\((M-x)(n+x)-Mn=x(M-n-x)\ge0\), since \(M-n-x\ge M-m\ge0\). The two radicands in (8) have sum \(M+n\), so expanding the square proves

\[
(\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{n+x})^2
\ge(\sqrt M+\sqrt n)^2,
\qquad
s_3\ge\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt n)^2}{4}.
\tag{9}
\]

On the other hand, (6), \(d<t\), and \(a_1>M-m\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_3
&\le5d-2a_1-2p-e_3\\
&<\frac{10M+10m-8a_1}{9}-2p-e_3\\
&<\frac{2M}{9}+2(m-p)-e_3\\
&=\frac{2M}{9}+2n-e_3
\le\frac{2M}{9}+n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (9)-(10) and multiplying by 36 yields
\(M+18\sqrt{Mn}<27n\). But (1) and \(0<n\le m\) give
\(n<3M/8<4M/9\). Because \(M,n>0\), this implies
\(27n<18\sqrt{Mn}\), a contradiction. The assumed global failure is impossible, proving the theorem.

Finally consider any genuine counterexample. Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` supplies the strict area hypotheses needed for accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`, and the counterexample has no middle point of depth at least \(t\). Hence that accepted active-cut reduction supplies an attained balanced triple. The theorem just proved applies to any such triple if all three full middle chords are bisected, so every such triple has an off-center full middle chord. For that row, accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346` gives \(0\le\ell_j\le b_j\) and
\(\|z-c_{1j}\|\le\ell_j(b_j-\ell_j)/(b_j+2\ell_j)\), where \(c_{1j}\) is the full middle chord midpoint and \(b_j>0\) is its length. The left side is positive, forcing \(0<\ell_j<b_j\). This uses the full transverse chords and the simultaneous supporting-edge selections exactly as that accepted fact specifies. In particular, neither the proof of the theorem nor its endpoint trimming has restricted the payments (2) to zero or full slice areas.
