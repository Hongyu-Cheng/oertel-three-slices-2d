## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and work in the affine plane
\[
\Pi
=
\left\{
(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb R^3:
z_1+z_2+z_3=1
\right\},
\]
with planar area normalized so that
\[
\Delta
=
\{z\in\Pi:z_i\geq0\ (i=1,2,3)\}
\]
has area \(1/2\). Fix \(d>1\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^3\) with
\[
b_1+b_2+b_3=1-d,
\]
and put
\[
A_2=\Delta,
\qquad
A_0=b+d\Delta
=
\{b+dy:y\in\Delta\}.
\]
Thus \(A_0,A_2\) are two-dimensional compact convex polytopes and
\[
M:=|A_0|=\frac{d^2}{2},
\qquad
m:=|A_2|=\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, when \(d\ne1\),
\[
A_0=z_\ast+d(A_2-z_\ast),
\qquad
z_\ast=\frac{b}{1-d}.
\]

Let
\[
f_i(z)=\alpha(z_i-\rho_i)
\qquad(i\in N),
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
\alpha>0,
\qquad
\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3=1+\frac1\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
and the three linear parts are pairwise nonparallel on \(\Pi\). Let
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3<-1.
\]

For every \(J,H,G\subseteq N\), use the exact weak/strict convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` and define
\[
P_J
=
A_0\cap
\{f_j\leq L_j\ (j\in J),\
f_j>L_j\ (j\notin J)\},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
Q_H
=
A_2\cap
\{f_j\leq-L_j\ (j\in H),\
f_j>-L_j\ (j\notin H)\},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
R_G
=
K\cap
\{f_j\leq0\ (j\in G),\
f_j>0\ (j\notin G)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(K\subseteq\Pi\) is a two-dimensional compact convex polytope satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{7}
\]
Write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,
\qquad
q_H=|Q_H|,
\qquad
r_G=|R_G|,
\qquad
k:=|K|.
\tag{8}
\]
The exact cells partition their source polytopes up to their assigned null cutting lines, so
\[
M=\sum_Jp_J,
\qquad
m=\sum_Hq_H,
\qquad
k=\sum_Gr_G.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(i\in N\), define the three marginals
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,
\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,
\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,
\tag{10}
\]
and put
\[
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,
\qquad
E_R=r_{\{1,2\}}+r_{\{1,3\}}+r_{\{2,3\}}+2r_N,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\tag{12}
\]
Also define
\[
W
=
\sum_{J\subseteq N}(3|J|-2)p_J
+
\sum_{H\subseteq N}(3|H|-2)q_H
+
\sum_{G\subseteq N}(3|G|-2)r_G.
\tag{13}
\]

Assume the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{N,\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\},
\tag{14}
\]
so
\[
q:=q_N>0,
\qquad
x_i:=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,
\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3.
\tag{15}
\]
Assume
\[
r_{\{1\}}r_{\{2\}}r_{\{3\}}r_N>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Assume the three crossing equalities explicitly:
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{17}
\]
and assume
\[
W=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Assume the two canonical tails for every \(i\):
\[
c_i+m\geq h,
\qquad
c_i+M\geq h.
\tag{19}
\]
Assume all three strict no-half inequalities:
\[
M<m+k,
\qquad
m<M+k,
\qquad
k<M+m.
\tag{20}
\]
Only the first inequality in (20) is used below.

Define the two parallel endpoint cut levels
\[
\tau_i=\rho_i+\frac{L_i}{\alpha},
\qquad
\lambda_i=\rho_i-\frac{L_i}{\alpha}.
\tag{21}
\]
Assume the one-cut caps
\[
P_i^+
:=
A_0\cap\{f_i>L_i\}
=
A_0\cap\{z_i>\tau_i\},
\tag{22}
\]
\[
C_i
:=
Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}
\]
are proper and positive. Put
\[
\pi_i=d+b_i-\tau_i\in(0,d),
\qquad
\sigma_i=1-\lambda_i\in(0,1).
\tag{23}
\]
Under the support condition (14), the one-cut \(Q\)-cap
\[
A_2\cap\{f_i>-L_i\}
=
A_2\cap\{z_i>\lambda_i\}
\]
has area \(x_i=|C_i|\), since every other exact \(Q\)-cell omitting \(i\) has area zero.

Then no configuration satisfying (1)-(23) exists. In particular, no such configuration exists in the separated-scale range
\[
\frac{M}{m}=d^2>3.
\]

## Proof

Because \(A_0=b+d\Delta\), its maximum \(z_i\)-coordinate is \(d+b_i\). Hence \(P_i^+\) is a corner triangle of linear scale \(\pi_i\). Similarly, the one-cut \(Q\)-cap in (23) is a corner triangle of \(\Delta\) with linear scale \(\sigma_i\). Therefore
\[
|P_i^+|=\frac{\pi_i^2}{2},
\qquad
x_i=\frac{\sigma_i^2}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
The cutting lines have area zero. Since \(u_i\) is the area of
\[
A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\},
\]
equation (24) gives
\[
u_i=M-|P_i^+|
=\frac{d^2-\pi_i^2}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
The support condition (14) and (15) give
\[
q
=m-x_1-x_2-x_3
=
\frac{
1-\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2-\sigma_3^2
}{2}.
\tag{26}
\]
Consequently,
\[
U+q
=
\frac{
3d^2+1
-\sum_i\pi_i^2
-\sum_i\sigma_i^2
}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]

We first derive the linear cap-scale constraint. Equation (23) gives
\[
\tau_i=d+b_i-\pi_i,
\qquad
\lambda_i=1-\sigma_i.
\]
Write
\[
\Pi_1=\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_3,
\qquad
\Sigma_1=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3.
\]
Using \(\sum_i b_i=1-d\), one obtains
\[
\sum_i\tau_i=1+2d-\Pi_1,
\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_i=3-\Sigma_1.
\tag{28}
\]
By (21),
\[
\rho_i=\frac{\tau_i+\lambda_i}{2},
\]
so (28) yields
\[
\sum_i\rho_i
=
2+d-\frac{\Pi_1+\Sigma_1}{2}.
\tag{29}
\]
On the other hand, (3) gives
\[
\sum_i\rho_i=1+\frac1\alpha>1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Pi_1+\Sigma_1<2d+2.
\tag{30}
\]

We next derive the required summed crossing identity directly from the definitions. Under (14),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i v_i
&=
\sum_{H\subseteq N}|H|q_H\\
&=
3q+2(x_1+x_2+x_3)\\
&=
2m+q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Because \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), the exact cell \(R_\varnothing\) is empty. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i c_i
&=
\sum_{G\subseteq N}|G|r_G\\
&=
k+
r_{\{1,2\}}+r_{\{1,3\}}+r_{\{2,3\}}
+2r_N\\
&=
k+E_R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Summing the three explicit equalities (17), then using (12), (31), and (32), gives
\[
U+2m+q+k+E_R
=3h
=\frac{2(M+m+k)}3.
\]
Multiplication by \(3\) and rearrangement give the exact identity
\[
2M=4m+k+3(U+q+E_R).
\tag{33}
\]
This derivation uses neither the tails (19) nor the separate assumption \(W=0\).

Write
\[
e^2=2k.
\tag{34}
\]
Substitute \(M=d^2/2\), \(m=1/2\), and (27) into (33). Since (16) gives
\[
E_R\geq2r_N>0,
\]
the resulting equality gives the strict estimate
\[
3\left(
\sum_i\pi_i^2+\sum_i\sigma_i^2
\right)
>
7d^2+7+e^2.
\tag{35}
\]
The first strict no-half inequality in (20) is
\[
\frac{d^2}{2}
<
\frac12+\frac{e^2}{2},
\]
so
\[
e^2>d^2-1.
\tag{36}
\]
Equations (35)-(36) imply
\[
3\left(
\sum_i\pi_i^2+\sum_i\sigma_i^2
\right)
>
8d^2+6.
\tag{37}
\]

The proper-cap bounds in (23) give
\[
\sum_i\pi_i^2
<
d\Pi_1,
\qquad
\sum_i\sigma_i^2
<
\Sigma_1.
\tag{38}
\]
Because \(d>1\), equations (30) and (38) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\pi_i^2+\sum_i\sigma_i^2
&<
d\Pi_1+\Sigma_1\\
&=
d(\Pi_1+\Sigma_1)
-(d-1)\Sigma_1\\
&<
2d^2+2d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
Combining (37) and (39) would force
\[
8d^2+6<6d^2+6d,
\]
and therefore
\[
d^2-3d+3<0.
\tag{40}
\]
This is impossible because
\[
d^2-3d+3
=
\left(d-\frac32\right)^2+\frac34
>0.
\]
The contradiction proves the statement.

The proof did not restrict the shape of \(K\) beyond the polytope hypothesis, the exact middle-cell definitions, and the first no-half inequality in (20). It therefore rules out every compatible convex-polytope enlargement of the midpoint body within this endpoint ansatz. It makes no claim for nontriangular endpoints or for cutting directions not parallel to the corresponding triangle sides.
