## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,20/11),(11/10,0)\},
\]
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
A_2=\frac1{10}A_0,
\]
and let
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Put
\[
\mathsf n_x=6544\cdot10^{12}+128136395483,
\qquad
\mathsf n_y=8419\cdot10^{12}+448874945401,
\]
\[
z_x=\frac{\mathsf n_x}{10^{16}},
\qquad
z_y=\frac{\mathsf n_y}{25\cdot10^{15}},
\qquad z=(z_x,z_y).
\]
Then \(z\in\operatorname{int}(A_1)\) and
\[
h_S((1,z))
=
\frac{224892939209839241053870813888823}
{500000000000000000000000000000000}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
h_S((1,z))-\frac{67}{150}
=
\frac{4678817629517723161612441666469}
{1500000000000000000000000000000000}
>0.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

Write the barycentric coordinates in \(A_1\), relative respectively to
\[
O=(0,0),\qquad R=(2,0),\qquad T=(0,1),
\]
as
\[
\Lambda_O(x,y)=1-\frac x2-y,\qquad
\Lambda_R(x,y)=\frac x2,\qquad
\Lambda_T(x,y)=y.
\]
At \(z\), these are
\[
\lambda_O=\frac{33601563818240981}{10^{17}},\qquad
\lambda_R=\frac{\mathsf n_x}{2\cdot10^{16}},\qquad
\lambda_T=\frac{\mathsf n_y}{25\cdot10^{15}}.
\tag{3}
\]
They are positive, sum to \(1\), and are all strictly below \(337/1000\). Thus \(z\in\operatorname{int}(A_1)\).

We first record an elementary triangle-cap calculation. Let an interior point of a triangle have barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_i,\lambda_j,\lambda_k\). Suppose a halfplane through the point contains exactly vertex \(i\). If its boundary meets the two sides from \(i\) at relative side parameters \(a,b\), then
\[
\frac{\lambda_j}{a}+\frac{\lambda_k}{b}=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\text{cap area}}{\text{triangle area}}=ab.
\tag{4}
\]
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality in (4) gives
\[
ab\ge4\lambda_j\lambda_k.
\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, equality is attained at \(a=2\lambda_j\), \(b=2\lambda_k\).

The largest possible value of \(ab\) under (4) and \(0<a,b\le1\) is
\[
\max\left\{
\frac{\lambda_j}{1-\lambda_k},
\frac{\lambda_k}{1-\lambda_j}
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, after writing \(b=\lambda_k a/(a-\lambda_j)\), the only interior critical point is the minimum from (5), so the maximum is at \(a=1\) or \(b=1\). Since every coordinate in (3) is below \(337/1000\), equation (6) is below \(337/663\). Therefore any halfplane through \(z\) containing at least two vertices of \(A_1\) has middle area greater than
\[
1-\frac{337}{663}=\frac{326}{663}
>\frac{67}{150},
\qquad
\frac{326}{663}-\frac{67}{150}=\frac{1493}{33150}.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to handle halfplanes whose middle cap contains exactly one vertex. We now reduce the endpoint contribution to finitely many exact chambers.

Every non-slice-parallel halfspace whose boundary contains \((1,z)\) has the form
\[
\sigma h+px+qy\le \sigma+pz_x+qz_y,
\qquad (p,q)\ne(0,0).
\tag{8}
\]
Put
\[
X=\frac{11}{10}p,\qquad Y=\frac{20}{11}q,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{20}{11},\qquad \beta=\frac{11}{20},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\rho=\frac{10}{11}z_x
=\frac{\mathsf n_x}{11\cdot10^{15}},
\qquad
\tau=\frac{11}{20}z_y
=\frac{92613937624399411}{500000000000000000}.
\tag{10}
\]
The values of \(px+qy\) at the vertices of \(A_0\) are \(0,X,Y\); at the vertices \(O,R,T\) of \(A_1\) they are
\[
0,\qquad \alpha X,\qquad \beta Y.
\tag{11}
\]
The middle threshold is
\[
t_1=pz_x+qz_y=\rho X+\tau Y.
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(C_{X,Y}(s)\) be the area of the cap of \(A_0\) at threshold \(s\). Since \(|A_0|=1\), this is also its area fraction. Because \(A_2=A_0/10\), if
\[
a=t_1+\sigma,\qquad b=10(t_1-\sigma)=20t_1-10a,
\tag{13}
\]
then the total endpoint mass of (8) is exactly
\[
C_{X,Y}(a)+\frac1{100}C_{X,Y}(b).
\tag{14}
\]

For three distinct vertex values \(\ell<m<u\), the exact cap function of a unit-area triangle is
\[
C_{\ell,m,u}(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le\ell,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(s-\ell)^2}{(m-\ell)(u-\ell)},&\ell<s\le m,\\[2mm]
1-\dfrac{(u-s)^2}{(u-\ell)(u-m)},&m<s\le u,\\[2mm]
1,&s>u.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]
This follows by similarity of the lower or upper triangular cap.

For completeness, the angular parameter requested in the subgoal can be made rational. If
\[
u=\tan(\theta/2),
\]
then, after multiplication by the positive factor \(1+u^2\), the planar normal is
\[
(p,q)=(1-u^2,2u).
\tag{16}
\]
The omitted direction \(u=\infty\) is \((p,q)=(-1,0)\) and is covered below by the \(R\)-vertex case. In the two nontrivial one-vertex cones, it is shorter to use the following rational projective ratios derived from (16):
\[
\frac{Y}{X}=\frac{400u}{121(1-u^2)},
\qquad
\frac{X}{-Y}=-\frac{121(1-u^2)}{400u}.
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose first that the middle cap contains only \(O\). Equations (11)-(12) give
\[
0<t_1<\alpha X,\qquad 0<t_1<\beta Y.
\]
Thus \(X,Y>0\). Normalize \(X=1\) and put \(v=Y\). The exact angular chamber is
\[
v_O^-\le v\le v_O^+,
\qquad
v_O^-=\frac{\rho}{\beta-\tau},
\qquad
v_O^+=\frac{\alpha-\rho}{\tau},
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
1<v_O^-<v_O^+.
\tag{19}
\]
The endpoint vertex scores are \(0,1,v\), so (15) becomes
\[
g_O(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le0,\\
s^2/v,&0<s\le1,\\
1-(v-s)^2/(v(v-1)),&1<s\le v,\\
1,&s>v.
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]
Here \(t_1=\rho+\tau v\), and (13) gives
\[
b=20(\rho+\tau v)-10a.
\tag{21}
\]

Suppose next that the middle cap contains only \(T\). Then
\[
\beta Y<t_1<0,\qquad t_1<\alpha X.
\]
Thus \(Y<0\). Normalize \(Y=-1\), put \(v=X\), and obtain
\[
v_T^-\le v\le v_T^+,
\qquad
v_T^-=-\frac{\tau}{\alpha-\rho},
\qquad
v_T^+=\frac{\tau}{\rho}.
\tag{22}
\]
The exact bounds are
\[
-1<v_T^-<0<v_T^+<1,
\qquad
v_T^->\frac{\tau-\beta}{\rho}.
\tag{23}
\]
For \(v<0\), the endpoint scores are \(-1,v,0\); for \(v>0\), they are \(-1,0,v\). Equation (15) gives
\[
g_-(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le-1,\\
(s+1)^2/(v+1),&-1<s\le v,\\
1-s^2/(-v),&v<s\le0,\\
1,&s>0,
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
\[
g_+(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le-1,\\
(s+1)^2/(v+1),&-1<s\le0,\\
1-(v-s)^2/(v(v+1)),&0<s\le v,\\
1,&s>v.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]
At \(v=0\), the repeated-score limit is
\[
g_0(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\le-1,\\
(s+1)^2,&-1<s\le0,\\
1,&s>0.
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]
Now \(t_1=\rho v-\tau\), so
\[
b=20(\rho v-\tau)-10a.
\tag{27}
\]

Equations (20), (24), (25), and (26) give respectively \(16,16,16,\) and \(9\) height-slope chamber pairs for \(a,b\), hence \(57\) exact chambers. The following closed exact Wolfram Language audit checks every chamber, including all threshold boundaries. `Piecewise` has the displayed final value \(1\), all constants are rational, and every expected parameter is quantified.


This was run with local Wolfram kernel 14.3.0 using exact arithmetic. Its complete output was
\[

\{\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False},\mathrm{False}\}\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The four `False` values prove from (14) that every \(O\)-only or \(T\)-only middle cap pays endpoint mass at least \(1/100\), for every real height slope \(\sigma\). The reduction is exhaustive: (18) is the full \(O\)-only angular cone, while (22)-(26) are the full \(T\)-only cone and its sole repeated-score direction.

We can now finish the depth bound. If the middle cap contains only \(O\), equations (5) and (28) give mass at least
\[
4\lambda_R\lambda_T+\frac1{100}.
\]
If it contains only \(T\), they give mass at least
\[
4\lambda_O\lambda_R+\frac1{100}.
\]
If it contains only \(R\), the nonnegative endpoint mass and (5) give mass at least
\[
4\lambda_O\lambda_T.
\]
The three exact strict margins over \(67/150\) are, respectively,
\[
\frac{1543856826420479141937704071049}
{375000000000000000000000000000000},
\tag{29}
\]
\[
\frac{4678817629517723161612441666469}
{1500000000000000000000000000000000},
\tag{30}
\]
and
\[
\frac{11219946057685360529302707035143}
{1875000000000000000000000000000000}.
\tag{31}
\]
These lower bounds are all at least the value claimed in (1). More explicitly,
\[
\left(4\lambda_R\lambda_T+\frac1{100}\right)
-\left(4\lambda_O\lambda_R+\frac1{100}\right)
=
\frac{498869892054731135379458205909}
{500000000000000000000000000000000}>0,
\tag{32}
\]
\[
4\lambda_O\lambda_T
-\left(4\lambda_O\lambda_R+\frac1{100}\right)
=
\frac{7161898694384275436382873269409}
{2500000000000000000000000000000000}>0,
\tag{33}
\]
and
\[
\frac{326}{663}
-\left(4\lambda_O\lambda_R+\frac1{100}\right)
=
\frac{13895981303876583181283650391710351}
{331500000000000000000000000000000000}>0.
\tag{34}
\]
Caps containing at least two vertices were already handled by (7). Directions for which the boundary passes through a vertex follow by continuity from the closed chambers above.

If \(p=q=0\), the halfspace is slice-parallel. The two possible masses through height \(1\) are
\[
|A_0|+|A_1|=2,\qquad |A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{101}{100},
\]
so these also exceed \(67/150\). Any halfspace containing \((1,z)\) can be translated toward that point until its boundary contains the point, without increasing its mass. The preceding cases therefore prove the lower bound in (1).

Finally, equality in (1) is attained. In the middle triangle take the cap containing \(T\) whose boundary is
\[
\frac{\Lambda_O(x,y)}{2\lambda_O}
+\frac{\Lambda_R(x,y)}{2\lambda_R}=1.
\tag{35}
\]
It passes through \(z\), and (4) gives its middle area
\[
4\lambda_O\lambda_R.
\]
Use its planar normal in (8) and choose \(\sigma\) sufficiently negative. Since the endpoint triangles are compact, the slice-\(0\) cap is then empty and the slice-\(2\) cap is all of \(A_2\). The total mass is exactly
\[
4\lambda_O\lambda_R+\frac1{100}
=
\frac{224892939209839241053870813888823}
{500000000000000000000000000000000}.
\]
This proves (1), and subtraction gives (2). A halfspace of mass below \(67/150\) cannot contain \((1,z)\), so no collection of interiors of such halfspaces can cover all three slices.
