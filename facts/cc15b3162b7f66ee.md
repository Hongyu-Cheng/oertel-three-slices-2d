## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\tfrac12\Delta,
\]
and define
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A_0)\cup
(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr)\subseteq\mathbb R^3.
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact polytope, each \(A_i\) is a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex body, and
\[
C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Writing \(a_i=|A_i|\) and \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), the strict area-triangle inequalities
\[
a_i<a_j+a_k\qquad(\{i,j,k\}=\{0,1,2\})
\]
hold.

For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), \(r\in\mathbb R\), and \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), define the closed-halfplane cap area
\[
F_{i,q}(r)
=
\left|A_i\cap\{w\in\mathbb R^2:q\cdot w\le r\}\right|
\]
and define
\[
\Phi_q(r)
=
F_{1,q}(r)
+
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)\bigr).
\]
For this triple there is no finite Borel measure \(\nu\), supported on \(A_1\), satisfying both
\[
\nu(A_1)\ge\frac{2T}{3}
\]
and
\[
\nu(\{w\in\mathbb R^2:q\cdot w\le r\})\le\Phi_q(r)
\qquad(q\in\mathbb S^1,\ r\in\mathbb R).
\]
The obstruction is the single profile constraint \(q=(1,0)\), \(r=2\).

## Proof

The sets \(A_0=A_1=\Delta\) and \(A_2=\tfrac12\Delta\) are triangles with nonempty interiors. Hence they are nonempty two-dimensional compact convex bodies. Their defining vertices form a finite set in each of the three planes of heights \(0,1,2\), so \(C\), the convex hull of their finitely many lifted vertices, is a compact polytope.

We next prove the exact integer-slice identity. The first coordinate of every point of \(C\) lies in \([0,2]\), so the only possible integer first coordinates are \(0,1,2\). A convex combination with first coordinate \(0\) can use only points of \(\{0\}\times A_0\), and a convex combination with first coordinate \(2\) can use only points of \(\{2\}\times A_2\). Thus the sections at heights \(0\) and \(2\) are exactly \(A_0\) and \(A_2\).

Since \(\Delta\) is convex and contains \(0\),
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=
\frac{\Delta+\frac12\Delta}{2}
=
\frac34\Delta
\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\]
For the remaining section, group any convex representation of a point of \(C\) at height \(1\) by the three heights. Its grouped weights \(\theta_0,\theta_1,\theta_2\) obey
\[
\theta_0+\theta_1+\theta_2=1,\qquad
\theta_1+2\theta_2=1,
\]
so \(\theta_0=\theta_2=s\) and \(\theta_1=1-2s\). Its planar component has the form
\[
(1-2s)w_1+2s\,\frac{w_0+w_2}{2},
\qquad w_i\in A_i.
\]
The midpoint belongs to \(A_1\) by the preceding inclusion, and the displayed point belongs to \(A_1\) by convexity. Conversely, every point of \(A_1\) occurs in \(C\) at height \(1\) by definition. The height-\(1\) section is therefore exactly \(A_1\). This proves
\[
C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
so all geometric hypotheses of the locked problem hold.

The triangle \(\Delta\) has area \(1\), while \(\tfrac12\Delta\) has area \(1/4\). Therefore
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)=(1,1,\tfrac14),\qquad T=\tfrac94.
\]
The three strict area-triangle inequalities are, explicitly,
\[
1<1+\tfrac14,\qquad
1<1+\tfrac14,\qquad
\tfrac14<1+1.
\]

It remains to evaluate one closed-halfplane profile. Take \(q=(1,0)\) and \(r=2\), and write the first planar coordinate as \(x\). Since \(A_1\subseteq\{x\le2\}\),
\[
F_{1,q}(2)=|A_1|=1.
\]
If \(\lambda\ge0\), then \(2+\lambda\ge2\), the maximum first coordinate of \(A_0\). Hence
\[
F_{0,q}(2+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(2-\lambda)\ge F_{0,q}(2+\lambda)=1.
\]
If \(\lambda<0\), then \(2-\lambda>2\), while the maximum first coordinate of \(A_2=\tfrac12\Delta\) is \(1\). Hence
\[
F_{0,q}(2+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(2-\lambda)\ge F_{2,q}(2-\lambda)=\tfrac14.
\]
For \(\lambda=-3\), the two thresholds are \(-1\) and \(5\). The first closed cap misses \(A_0\), while the second contains all of \(A_2\), so the sum is exactly \(1/4\). Consequently,
\[
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_{0,q}(2+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(2-\lambda)\bigr)
=\tfrac14
\]
and
\[
\Phi_q(2)=1+\tfrac14=\tfrac54.
\]
The closed-boundary convention is literal throughout: at every threshold used above the asserted polygon is wholly contained in, or wholly disjoint from, the relevant closed halfplane.

Now let \(\nu\) be any finite Borel measure supported on \(A_1\). Since \(A_1\subseteq\{x\le2\}\),
\[
\nu(\{x\le2\})=\nu(A_1).
\]
Thus the single required profile inequality at \(q=(1,0)\), \(r=2\) would imply
\[
\nu(A_1)\le\Phi_q(2)=\tfrac54.
\]
The required total-mass inequality would instead imply
\[
\nu(A_1)\ge\frac{2T}{3}
=\frac23\cdot\frac94
=\frac32.
\]
Since \(5/4<3/2\), no such measure exists. In finite-dual language, assign multiplier \(1\) to the single halfplane constraint \((q,r)=((1,0),2)\). On measures supported on \(A_1\), its left side is exactly the total-mass objective, while its right side is \(5/4\), strictly below the required \(3/2\).

This proves failure only of the auxiliary measure assertion. It neither constructs a point of mixed-integer depth below \(2T/9\) nor contradicts the locked centerpoint theorem.
