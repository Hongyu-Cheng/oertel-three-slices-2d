## Statement

Fix \(x,\ell,b>0\) with
\[
x+\ell+b=1,
\qquad
\lambda=x+\ell,
\]
and assume
\[
x<t_1<t_2\leq\lambda.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
D=\frac{16\ell-9(b+\ell)^2}{2},
\qquad
K_0=\frac{5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2}{2},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
a_*(t)=\frac{-t^2+2t+9x^2-2x}{2},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
k_*(t)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(t-x)(16\ell-5t+11x-6)}2,
&x\leq t\leq\lambda,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{8\ell^2+3t^2-6t-3x^2+6x}2,
&\lambda\leq t\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
There do not exist \(h\geq0\) and \(d_1,d_2>0\) satisfying
\[
h+d_1+d_2<\frac{\ell}{x},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\ell+(1-x)h+(t_1-x)d_1+(t_2-x)d_2\geq b,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
a_*(1)h+a_*(t_1)d_1+a_*(t_2)d_2<D,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
K_0+k_*(1)h+k_*(t_1)d_1+k_*(t_2)d_2<0.
\tag{8}
\]
The same infeasibility holds on the closure with
\(d_i\geq0\), \(x\leq t_1\leq t_2\leq\lambda\), by the same
pointwise dual argument.  Coincident atoms may also be combined.
In particular, both \(t_i=x\) and the weak boundary
\(t_2=\lambda\) are included.

## Proof

The coefficient \(a_*(t)\) is strictly positive for
\(x\leq t\leq1\).  Indeed, the numerator in (3) has value \(8x^2\)
at \(t=x\) and derivative \(2(1-t)\geq0\).  Also
\[
2K_0
=5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2
=(b+\ell)^2+4(b-\ell)^2>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus (7) forces \(D>0\).

We prove the following pointwise dual inequalities:
\[
K_0a_*(t)+Dk_*(t)\geq0
\qquad(x\leq t\leq\lambda),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
K_0a_*(1)+Dk_*(1)\geq0.
\tag{11}
\]
Once these are known, divide (10)--(11) by \(D>0\), multiply by
\(d_1,d_2,h\), and add.  Equations (7), (10), and (11) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&K_0+k_*(1)h+k_*(t_1)d_1+k_*(t_2)d_2\\
&\quad\geq
K_0-\frac{K_0}{D}
\bigl(a_*(1)h+a_*(t_1)d_1+a_*(t_2)d_2\bigr)>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
contradicting (8).  Notice that (5)--(6) are not used in (12).

It remains to certify (10)--(11).  Put
\[
L=b+\ell=1-x,\qquad
z=\frac{\ell}{L},\qquad
\varepsilon=16z-9L.
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(0<z<1\), and \(D>0\) is exactly
\[
\varepsilon>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover \(0<L<1\), so
\[
\max\{0,16z-9\}<\varepsilon<16z,
\qquad
L=\frac{16z-\varepsilon}{9}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(t=x+s\), \(0\leq s\leq\ell\), the left side of (10) is a
quadratic in \(s\).  Write its quadratic Bernstein expansion on this
interval as
\[
B_0(1-r)^2+2B_1r(1-r)+B_2r^2,
\qquad
r=\frac{s}{\ell}.
\tag{16}
\]
Direct coefficient comparison gives
\[
B_0=2x^2(5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2),
\qquad
B_1=2P_B,
\qquad
B_2=2P_0,
\tag{17}
\]
while
\[
K_0a_*(1)+Dk_*(1)=2P_1.
\tag{18}
\]
Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_B={}&5b^2-10b^3+5b^4-6b\ell+2b^2\ell+8b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-4b\ell^2-b^2\ell^2-4b\ell^3,\\
P_0={}&5b^2-10b^3+5b^4-6b\ell+2b^2\ell+12b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-10b\ell^2+5b^2\ell^2,\\
P_1={}&5b^2-10b^3+9b^4-6b\ell-4b^2\ell+18b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-10b\ell^2+9b^2\ell^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The following normalized formulas are exact:
\[
Q_B=\frac{81P_B}{L^2},
\qquad
Q_0=\frac{81P_0}{L^2},
\qquad
Q_1=\frac{81P_1}{L^2}.
\tag{19}
\]
They will show
\[
P_B>0,\qquad P_0>0,\qquad P_1\geq0.
\tag{20}
\]

First,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1={}&9\varepsilon^2(1-z)^2
{}+18\varepsilon(1-z)(5-24z+24z^2)\\
&+9(5-16z+16z^2)(9-32z+32z^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
With \(q(z)=5-24z+24z^2\), exact completion of the square gives
\[
\frac{Q_1}{9}
=\bigl((1-z)\varepsilon+q(z)\bigr)^2
{}+4(2z-1)^2(5+4z-4z^2)\geq0,
\tag{21}
\]
because \(5+4z-4z^2=5+4z(1-z)>0\).  Hence \(P_1\geq0\).

Next,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0={}&\varepsilon^2(1-z)^2(5+2z-2z^2)\\
&+2\varepsilon(1-z)
(45-179z+147z^2+64z^3-32z^4)\\
&+(5-16z+16z^2)
(81-288z+256z^2+64z^3-32z^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Its leading coefficient as a quadratic in \(\varepsilon\) is
positive, and its discriminant factors as
\[
\operatorname{disc}_{\varepsilon}Q_0
=324(z-1)^3z(40-153z+153z^2)<0.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, \(0<z<1\), while
\[
40-153z+153z^2>0
\]
because its leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is
\[
153^2-4\cdot153\cdot40=-1071<0.
\]
Therefore \(Q_0>0\), and \(P_0>0\).

Finally define
\[
F(z)=45-179z+184z^2+32z^3,
\]
\[
G(z)=81-288z+256z^2+32z^3.
\tag{24}
\]
The exact expression for the remaining Bernstein coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_B={}&(1-z)(5-7z-2z^2)\varepsilon^2
{}+2(1-z)F(z)\varepsilon\\
&+(5-16z+16z^2)G(z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Both \(F\) and \(G\) are positive on \(0<z<1\).  For an exact check,
put \(y=2z-1\).  Then
\[
F=4y^3+58y^2+\frac{29}{2}y+\frac{11}{2},
\]
\[
G=4y^3+76y^2-4y+5.
\tag{26}
\]
When \(0\leq y<1\), \(F>0\), and
\[
76y^2-4y+5>0
\]
because its discriminant is \(-1504\); hence \(G>0\).  When
\(-1<y\leq0\), put \(u=-y\).  Since \(0\leq u<1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F
&=-4u^3+58u^2-\frac{29}{2}u+\frac{11}{2}\\
&\geq54u^2-\frac{29}{2}u+\frac{11}{2}>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
where the last quadratic has discriminant \(-3911/4\), and
\[
G=-4u^3+76u^2+4u+5
\geq72u^2+4u+5>0.
\tag{28}
\]

Let
\[
z_0=\frac{-7+\sqrt{89}}4.
\]
The function \(5-7z-2z^2\) decreases on \(z>0\), vanishes at
\(z_0\), and has value \(55/128>0\) at \(z=9/16\).  Hence
\[
\frac9{16}<z_0<1.
\tag{29}
\]
If \(0<z\leq z_0\), differentiation of (25) gives
\[
\frac{\partial Q_B}{\partial\varepsilon}
=2(1-z)\bigl((5-7z-2z^2)\varepsilon+F(z)\bigr)>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q_B(\varepsilon)>Q_B(0)
=(5-16z+16z^2)G(z)>0,
\tag{30}
\]
where
\[
5-16z+16z^2=1+16\left(z-\frac12\right)^2>0.
\]

If \(z_0<z<1\), the quadratic (25) is concave in
\(\varepsilon\).  By (15) and (29), its admissible interval is
\[
16z-9<\varepsilon<16z.
\]
The two endpoint values factor as
\[
Q_B(16z)=81(5-16z+16z^2)>0,
\]
\[
Q_B(16z-9)=81z(2z-1)(9z-4)>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Concavity places \(Q_B\) above the chord joining these positive
endpoint values.  Thus \(Q_B>0\), and \(P_B>0\).

Equations (17), (20), and the nonnegativity of the Bernstein basis in
(16) prove (10) on the full closed interval
\(x\leq t\leq\lambda\).  Equations (18), (20) prove (11).  This
includes \(t=\lambda\) exactly, as well as the formal boundary
\(t=x\).

If \(t_1=t_2\), the two curvature weights combine into one atom; if a
weight is zero, only one atom remains.  Accepted fact
`cff96cfcc631176f` supplies the same strict conclusion on those
one-kink faces.  Accepted fact `644583f5590ae85e` shows that the
finite-curvature localization preserves \(A\), \(K\), and all
clipping data, so the present chamber certificate applies exactly to
the left-left two-kink outputs of that localization.  The strict
contradiction (12) completes the subgoal.
