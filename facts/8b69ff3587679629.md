## Statement

Assume the complete Alternative 1 hypotheses of accepted facts `bc026780453747b8` and `92a1b537d7e7876c`.  Normalize the full positive axis traces of \(P\) to
\[
[a,A]\times\{0\},\qquad \{0\}\times[b,B],
\qquad A=a+1,\quad B=b+1,
\]
and let \(D>0\) be the product of the two physical trace lengths.  Thus
\[
|P|=\frac pD,\qquad p=\frac{97}{100},
\]
and each normalized mixed cap of \(A_0\) has area
\[
\frac cD,\qquad c=\frac3{200}.
\]

There are finite positive numbers \(\sigma,\rho\) for which the full normalized \(A_0\) body obeys the division-free supporting inequalities
\[
y+\sigma(x-a)\geq0,\qquad
x+\rho(y-b)\geq0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The first support passes through \((a,0)\), and the second passes through \((0,b)\).  An actual normalized trace has either \(a,b>0\), or \(a=b=0\).  In the first case,
\[
b\geq\sigma a,\qquad a\geq\rho b,\qquad \sigma\rho\leq1. \tag{2}
\]

Define the exact strongest outer coefficients without assuming that a maximizing point exists:
\[
\alpha=\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb R:
u-A\leq\lambda v\ \hbox{for every }(u,v)\in P\},
\]
\[
\beta=\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb R:
v-B\leq\lambda u\ \hbox{for every }(u,v)\in P\}.          \tag{3}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\alpha=\sup_{\substack{(u,v)\in P\\v>0}}\frac{u-A}{v},
\qquad
\beta=\sup_{\substack{(u,v)\in P\\u>0}}\frac{v-B}{u}.     \tag{4}
\]
Both numbers are finite.  Put
\[
q_x=\alpha+\frac1\sigma,\qquad
q_y=\beta+\frac1\rho.                                    \tag{5}
\]
If \(q_x>0\), the normalized lower cap is contained in a triangle of area \(1/(2q_x)\), and hence
\[
D\geq2cq_x=\frac3{100}q_x.                               \tag{6}
\]
If \(q_x=0\), the two relevant lines are parallel and cut out an unbounded strip of width one.  If \(q_x<0\), they cut out an expanding unbounded wedge.  Thus (1) and (3) alone give no finite lower-cap capacity in those two cases.  The coordinate-swapped statement is exact: if \(q_y>0\), then
\[
D\geq2cq_y=\frac3{100}q_y,                               \tag{7}
\]
whereas \(q_y=0\) and \(q_y<0\) give respectively an unbounded strip and an expanding wedge.  In particular, a zero outer coefficient causes no singularity: \(\alpha=0\) gives lower-cap capacity \(\sigma/2\), and \(\beta=0\) gives left-cap capacity \(\rho/2\).

Let
\[
E=|\operatorname{conv}(0,P)|-|P|.
\]
If \(a,b>0\) and \(\sigma\rho<1\), define
\[
\Phi(a,b,\sigma,\rho)
=
\frac{\sigma a^2+\rho b^2-2\sigma\rho ab}
     {2(1-\sigma\rho)}
=
\frac{ab}{2}
-
\frac{(b-\sigma a)(a-\rho b)}
     {2(1-\sigma\rho)}.                                  \tag{8}
\]
Then the sharp support-line defect bound is
\[
E\geq\Phi(a,b,\sigma,\rho).                              \tag{9}
\]
If \(a,b>0\) and \(\sigma\rho=1\), (2) forces
\[
b=\sigma a,\qquad a=\rho b;
\]
the two support lines coincide with the line through the inner trace endpoints, and the sharp bound is
\[
E\geq\frac{ab}{2}.                                       \tag{10}
\]
If \(a=b=0\), the southwest trace triangle is degenerate and the sharp bound is \(E\geq0\), regardless of whether the two chosen support lines coincide.  Exactly one zero offset is impossible for an actual normalized trace.

Accepted fact `92a1b537d7e7876c`, together with \(|P|=p/D\) from accepted fact `bc026780453747b8`, gives
\[
DE\leq k,\qquad
k=\frac9{200r_-^2}-p
=\frac{65+29\sqrt{65}}{400},
\qquad
r_-=\frac{87-3\sqrt{65}}{388}.                           \tag{11}
\]

Choose a rightmost point
\[
R=(W,Y)\in P,\qquad W=\max_{(u,v)\in P}u,
\]
and a topmost point
\[
T=(X,H)\in P,\qquad H=\max_{(u,v)\in P}v.
\]
Assume that \(R\ne T\) and that the fully inward order holds:
\[
0\leq X\leq A\leq W,\qquad
0\leq Y\leq B\leq H.                                    \tag{12}
\]
The four trace endpoints and \(R,T\) all belong to \(P\).  Since \(P\) is convex,
\[
K:=\operatorname{conv}\{(a,0),(A,0),R,T,(0,B),(0,b)\}
\subseteq P.                                             \tag{13}
\]
Since \(P\) lies in the positive quadrant and \(W,H\) are its coordinate maxima,
\[
P\subseteq[0,W]\times[0,H].                              \tag{14}
\]
Full-trace membership also gives the two zero-level implications
\[
Y=0\Longrightarrow W=A,\qquad
X=0\Longrightarrow H=B.                                 \tag{14a}
\]

The support-polygon area is
\[
t_K:=|K|=\frac{AY+WH-XY+XB-ab}{2}.                       \tag{15}
\]
Equation (13) gives
\[
Dt_K\leq p.                                              \tag{16}
\]
Put
\[
\alpha_K=\sup_{\substack{(u,v)\in K\\v>0}}\frac{u-A}{v},
\qquad
\beta_K=\sup_{\substack{(u,v)\in K\\u>0}}\frac{v-B}{u}.
\]
Equations (3)-(4) and (13) give
\[
\alpha\geq\alpha_K,\qquad \beta\geq\beta_K.               \tag{17}
\]

The exact maximum horizontal chord \(M_x=m_x(K)\) is
\[
M_x=W\quad\hbox{if }b\leq Y,                              \tag{18}
\]
whereas, if \(Y<b\), then \(b>0\), \(H>Y\), and
\[
G_x=W-\frac{a(b-Y)}b,\qquad
J_x=W-\frac{(W-X)(b-Y)}{H-Y},
\qquad
M_x=\max\{G_x,J_x\}.                                     \tag{19}
\]
The exact maximum vertical chord \(M_y=m_y(K)\) is
\[
M_y=H\quad\hbox{if }a\leq X,                              \tag{20}
\]
whereas, if \(X<a\), then \(a>0\), \(W>X\), and
\[
G_y=H-\frac{b(a-X)}a,\qquad
J_y=H-\frac{(H-Y)(a-X)}{W-X},
\qquad
M_y=\max\{G_y,J_y\}.                                     \tag{21}
\]
Equations (18)-(21) include \(Y=b\), \(X=a\), \(Y=0<b\), \(X=0<a\), both maximum ties, coincident outer trace endpoints, and nonunique support faces.
Moreover, (13) and the definitions of \(W,H\) give
\[
m_x(P)\geq M_x,\qquad m_y(P)\geq M_y,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{W}{m_x(P)}+\frac{H}{m_y(P)}
\leq
\frac W{M_x}+\frac H{M_y}.                              \tag{21a}
\]

Treating \(D,E,\alpha,\beta,a,b,W,H,X,Y,\sigma,\rho\) as scalar variables, consider the relaxation consisting of (2), (5), the applicable branches of (6)-(10), (11)-(12), (15)-(21), and
\[
E\geq t_K+\frac{ab}{2}-\frac pD,\qquad
D\left(t_K+\frac{ab}{2}\right)\leq\frac9{200r_-^2},       \tag{22}
\]
does not imply
\[
\frac W{M_x}+\frac H{M_y}\leq\frac{5000}{291}.            \tag{23}
\]
Indeed, the following tuple is an exact rational obstruction:
\[
a=b=8,\quad A=B=W=H=9,\quad X=Y=0,
\]
\[
\sigma=\rho=\frac12,\quad
\alpha=\beta=-1,\quad
D=\frac3{100},\quad
E=\frac{64}{3}.                                          \tag{24}
\]
It lies in the strictly finite branch \(q_x=q_y=1\), satisfies every inequality in this scalar relaxation, and gives
\[
M_x=M_y=1,\qquad
\frac W{M_x}+\frac H{M_y}=18>\frac{5000}{291}.            \tag{25}
\]

Every actual positive cell also satisfies the additional necessary envelope inequality
\[
\frac pD+E
=|\operatorname{conv}(0,P)|
\leq
\left|
\{(x,y)\in[0,W]\times[0,H]:
x-A\leq\alpha y,\ y-B\leq\beta x\}
\right|.                                                  \tag{26}
\]
For (24), the set on the right of (26) is the triangle
\[
\{x,y\geq0:x+y\leq9\}
\]
of area \(81/2\), whereas the left side is \(161/3\).  Thus (26) excludes the particular scalar tuple (24).  This exclusion alone does not prove that the scalar relaxation augmented by (26) implies (23).

## Proof

The zero southwest cell in accepted fact `bc026780453747b8` and convexity imply that \(A_0\) has no point in the open southwest quadrant.  Otherwise, mixing such a point with an interior point produces an open southwest subset of \(A_0\), contradicting zero southwest area.  If \(b=0<a\), then \((0,0)\) lies in the body, so the segment from \((0,0)\) to \((a,0)\), followed by \([a,A]\times\{0\}\), makes the full horizontal trace \([0,A]\times\{0\}\) of length \(a+1>1\).  If \(a=0<b\), the coordinate-swapped argument makes the full vertical trace \(\{0\}\times[0,B]\) of length \(b+1>1\).  Hence exactly one zero offset is impossible.

At the boundary point \((a,0)\), choose a supporting line and orient its normal so that the body is in its nonnegative halfplane.  The point \((A,0)\) makes the first normal coordinate nonnegative.  If that coordinate were zero, then the point \((0,b)\) would orient the other coordinate nonnegatively, while the positive lower mixed cell would contradict the resulting horizontal support.  Hence the first coordinate is positive.  The point \((0,b)\), together with \(a,b>0\), then makes the second coordinate positive.  Dividing the normal by its second coordinate gives the first inequality in (1) with \(0<\sigma<\infty\).  The coordinate-swapped argument at \((0,b)\) gives the second inequality with \(0<\rho<\infty\).

When \(a=b=0\), a supporting line at the common origin exists.  The two positive axis traces make both normal coordinates nonnegative, and the two positive mixed cells rule out either being zero.  The same line may be used for both roles, or two different supporting lines may be chosen when the support cone is nontrivial.  No division by \(a\) or \(b\) is involved.  Substitution of \((0,b)\) and \((a,0)\) into (1) gives (2).  Multiplication gives \(\sigma\rho\leq1\).  If equality holds, both inequalities in (2) must be equalities, proving the incidence assertion preceding (10).

Take a lower-cap point \((x,y)\) with \(y<0\), and take \((u,v)\in P\) with \(v>0\).  Their segment meets the \(x\)-axis at
\[
\left(\frac{vx-yu}{v-y},0\right).
\]
This point lies in the full trace \([a,A]\times\{0\}\), so its abscissa is at most \(A\).  Since \(v-y>0\), this is exactly
\[
v(x-A)\leq y(u-A),
\qquad
x-A\leq\frac{u-A}{v}\,y.                                 \tag{27}
\]
A fixed lower-cap point bounds all ratios in (4) from above after division by the negative number \(y\), proving that \(\alpha\) is finite.  Taking the supremum in (27), with \(y<0\), gives
\[
x-A\leq\alpha y.                                         \tag{28}
\]
At depth \(t=-y\geq0\), the first inner support in (1) and (28) give
\[
a+\frac t\sigma\leq x\leq A-\alpha t.                    \tag{29}
\]
The interval in (29) has length \(1-q_xt\).  If \(q_x>0\), it vanishes at \(t=1/q_x\), and integration gives
\[
\int_0^{1/q_x}(1-q_xt)\,dt=\frac1{2q_x}.
\]
This proves (6).  If \(q_x=0\), (29) is a constant-width unbounded strip; if \(q_x<0\), its width increases with \(t\).  This proves all lower-cap cases, including \(\alpha=0\).

For a left-cap point \((x,y)\) with \(x<0\), and a positive-cell point \((u,v)\) with \(u>0\), the \(y\)-axis intercept of their segment is
\[
\left(0,\frac{uy-xv}{u-x}\right).
\]
It is at most \(B\), so
\[
u(y-B)\leq x(v-B).
\]
Taking the strongest coefficient gives
\[
y-B\leq\beta x.
\]
Writing \(t=-x\geq0\), the second inner support in (1) gives the vertical interval
\[
b+\frac t\rho\leq y\leq B-\beta t,
\]
of length \(1-q_yt\).  The preceding integration proves (7) and all its parallel, diverging, and zero-coefficient cases.

For the defect calculation, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,0),(0,b)\}.
\]
The two trace endpoints belong to \(P\), so \(\Delta\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(0,P)\).  Equation (1) implies
\[
P\cap\Delta\subseteq
\Delta\cap\{y+\sigma(x-a)\geq0\}
\cap\{x+\rho(y-b)\geq0\}.                                \tag{30}
\]
When \(\sigma\rho<1\), the two support lines meet at
\[
S=
\left(
\frac{\rho(b-\sigma a)}{1-\sigma\rho},
\frac{\sigma(a-\rho b)}{1-\sigma\rho}
\right).                                                  \tag{31}
\]
Conditions (2) put \(S\) in \(\Delta\).  The allowed part on the right of (30) is the triangle with vertices \((a,0),(0,b),S\).  Its area is
\[
\frac{(b-\sigma a)(a-\rho b)}
     {2(1-\sigma\rho)}.
\]
Subtracting this from \(|\Delta|=ab/2\) proves (8)-(9).  The bound is sharp because equality is attained when the southwest part of \(P\) is exactly that triangle.  When \(\sigma\rho=1\), the support lines coincide with the trace-endpoint chord, so the allowed part of \(\Delta\) has area zero and (10) follows.  The case \(a=b=0\) is immediate from \(|\Delta|=0\).

Accepted fact `92a1b537d7e7876c` gives
\[
D|\operatorname{conv}(0,P)|\leq\frac9{200r_-^2}.
\]
Subtracting \(D|P|=p\), supplied by accepted fact `bc026780453747b8`, proves (11).  Rationalizing
\[
\frac1{r_-}=\frac{29+\sqrt{65}}6
\]
gives the displayed exact value of \(k\).

All six generators of \(K\) belong to \(P\), so convexity proves (13).  The definitions of the coordinate maxima and positivity of \(P\) prove (14).  If \(Y=0\), then \(R=(W,0)\in P\) belongs to the full horizontal trace, so \(W\leq A\); (12) gives the reverse inequality and hence \(W=A\).  The coordinate-swapped argument proves \(X=0\Rightarrow H=B\), establishing (14a).

In the displayed cyclic order, the six consecutive edge cross products are
\[
Y,\quad
(W-A)(H-Y)+Y(W-X),\quad
(W-X)(H-B)+X(H-Y),\quad
X,\quad a,\quad b.
\]
They are all nonnegative under (12), so after deleting coincident generators the displayed list is the boundary order of \(K\); zero cross products are precisely the allowed collinear support faces.  Shoelace now gives (15), while (13) and (14) justify every later use of \(K\subseteq P\) and of the bounding rectangle.  In particular, (16) follows from \(K\subseteq P\), and (17) follows by taking the suprema over the subset \(K\).

Horizontal section length in a polygon is affine between consecutive vertex levels.  First suppose \(b\leq Y\).  Because \(Y\leq B\), the point \((0,Y)\) lies on the vertical trace segment, while \(R=(W,Y)\) belongs to \(K\).  Their segment is a horizontal chord of length \(W\).  No horizontal chord can exceed the coordinate width \(W\), proving (18).

Now suppose \(Y<b\).  Then \(b>0\) and the ordered levels are
\[
0\leq Y<b<B\leq H.
\]
If \(Y=0\), (14a) gives \(W=A\).  Thus the section at \(y=0\) has length \(A-a=1\), and the formula below gives \(G_x=W-a=1\).  If \(Y>0\), the section at \(y=0\) is exactly the trace base \([(a,0),(A,0)]\), again of length one.

At level \(y=Y\), interpolation on the southwest edge gives the length
\[
G_x=W-a\frac{b-Y}{b}.
\]
At level \(y=b\), interpolation on the edge from \(R\) to \(T\) gives
\[
J_x=W-(W-X)\frac{b-Y}{H-Y}.
\]
The level-zero length is at most \(G_x\): this is equality in the separately treated case \(Y=0\), and for \(Y>0\),
\[
G_x\geq A-a\frac{b-Y}{b}
=1+\frac{aY}{b}\geq1.
\]
The length at \(y=B\) is at most \(J_x\), because the right endpoint moves weakly left along the edge from \(R\) to \(T\) as the level increases from \(b\) to \(B\).  If \(H>B\), the top level is the point \(T\).  If \(H=B\), the top support face has length \(X\leq J_x\).  Piecewise affinity now proves (19), including \(G_x=J_x\).

The coordinate-swapped proof gives (20)-(21).  In its strict branch \(X<a\), one first treats \(X=0\): equation (14a) gives \(H=B\), so the section at \(x=0\) has length one and \(G_y=H-b=1\).  If \(X>0\), the section at \(x=0\) is the unit vertical trace and
\[
G_y\geq B-b\frac{a-X}{a}
=1+\frac{bX}{a}\geq1.
\]
The same interpolation and monotonicity argument proves (21).  In particular, if \(W>A\), the rightmost level is the point \(R\); if \(W=A\), the right support face has length \(Y\leq J_y\).  Thus \(Y=0\), \(X=0\), all level-order ties, and all denominator-degenerate first branches are covered.

Every horizontal or vertical chord of \(K\) is a chord of \(P\) by (13), so \(m_x(P)\geq M_x\) and \(m_y(P)\geq M_y\).  Because \(R\) and \(T\) realize the coordinate maxima, \(K\) and \(P\) have the same widths \(W,H\).  This proves (21a).

The area of \(\operatorname{conv}(0,K)\) is \(t_K+ab/2\).  Since
\[
\operatorname{conv}(0,K)\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(0,P),
\]
subtracting \(|P|=p/D\) proves the first inequality in (22), and the anchor from accepted fact `92a1b537d7e7876c` proves the second.

It remains to verify the scalar obstruction.  For (24), (2) reads
\[
8\geq4,\qquad 8\geq4,
\]
and \(\sigma\rho=1/4<1\).  Formula (8) gives
\[
\Phi
=
\frac{(1/2)64+(1/2)64-2(1/4)64}
     {2(3/4)}
=\frac{64}{3}
=E.                                                       \tag{32}
\]
The support polygon is
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(8,0),(9,0),(0,9),(0,8)\}.
\]
Every point \((u,v)\in K\) with \(v>0\) satisfies \(u+v\leq9\), with equality on the outer edge.  Hence
\[
\alpha_K=\sup_{v>0}\frac{u-9}{v}=-1.
\]
The coordinate-swapped supremum is also \(\beta_K=-1\), so (24) satisfies (17) at equality.  Thus \(q_x=q_y=1\), and
\[
\frac cD=\frac{3/200}{3/100}=\frac12
=\frac1{2q_x}=\frac1{2q_y}.                              \tag{33}
\]
Both finite cap inequalities are attained.

Equation (15) gives \(t_K=17/2\), so
\[
Dt_K=\frac{51}{200}<\frac{97}{100}=p.                    \tag{34}
\]
Also
\[
t_K+\frac{ab}{2}-\frac pD
=\frac{81}{2}-\frac{97}{3}
=\frac{49}{6}
<\frac{64}{3}=E.                                         \tag{35}
\]
The defect anchor is
\[
DE=\frac3{100}\frac{64}{3}=\frac{16}{25}
<
\frac{65+29\sqrt{65}}{400}=k.                            \tag{36}
\]
Indeed,
\[
29^2\cdot65-191^2=18{,}184>0.
\]
The second inequality in (22) also holds:
\[
D\left(t_K+\frac{ab}{2}\right)
=\frac3{100}\frac{81}{2}
=\frac{243}{200}
<
\frac{453+29\sqrt{65}}{400}
=\frac9{200r_-^2}.                                       \tag{37}
\]

Here \(Y=0<b\) and \(X=0<a\).  The separate zero-level arguments above give \(W=A\) and \(H=B\), and (19) and (21) give
\[
G_x=J_x=G_y=J_y=1.
\]
Thus all four section-order comparisons are ties, \(M_x=M_y=1\), and (25) follows because
\[
18\cdot291=5{,}238>5{,}000.
\]

Finally, (3) implies
\[
P\subseteq
\{(x,y):x-A\leq\alpha y,\ y-B\leq\beta x\}.
\]
The origin satisfies both outer inequalities.  Combining this fact with (14), and using convexity of the intersection of the four halfplanes, gives (26).  For the scalar tuple (24), \(\alpha=\beta=-1\), so the right side of (26) is the triangle \(x,y\geq0,\ x+y\leq9\), of area \(81/2\).  But
\[
\frac pD+E=\frac{97}{3}+\frac{64}{3}=\frac{161}{3}
>\frac{81}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, intersecting that outer triangle with the two inner halfplanes removes the sharp area \(64/3\), leaving only
\[
\frac{81}{2}-\frac{64}{3}=\frac{115}{6}
<\frac{97}{3}=\frac pD.
\]
Therefore the additional necessary envelope condition excludes (24).  This proves only the exclusion of this rational point, not sufficiency of the augmented system for (23).
