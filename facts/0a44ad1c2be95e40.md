## Statement

Assume the complete hypotheses of the accepted critical-ratio ledger
`6e4bb19f9157027f`, normalized by
\[
q=q_{123}=1,\qquad
|C_i|=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}=x:=\frac5{21}
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Thus
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad p=|P|=p_\varnothing>0,
\qquad
B_i=\frac{P+C_i}{2},
\]
\[
s_R=\sum_i r_i,\qquad
d_R=\sum_{i<j}r_{ij},\qquad
r=r_{123}>0,\qquad
k=s_R+d_R+r,
\]
\[
w_P=s_P+4d_P,\qquad
\eta=w_P+3d_R+6r,
\]
and, with
\[
\mathcal U=B_1\cup B_2\cup B_3,\qquad
I=\sum_{i<j}|B_i\cap B_j|,
\]
\[
\alpha=s_R+d_R-|\mathcal U|,\qquad
\beta=s_R-I,\qquad
\Xi=\alpha+\beta.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), put
\[
o_k=|B_i\cap B_j|.
\tag{2}
\]
Let
\[
S_Q=C_1+C_2+C_3
\]
and use the planar mixed area
\[
\mathcal V(A,B)=\frac{|A+B|-|A|-|B|}{2}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal A(p)=\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt{\frac5{21}}\right)^2
\tag{3}
\]
and the common-core shape charge
\[
\mathcal Q=
\frac12\left(
\mathcal V(P,S_Q)-\sqrt{p|S_Q|}
\right)
+
\frac{\sqrt p}{2}
\left(
\sqrt{|S_Q|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Then every term in (1) and (4) is nonnegative, and the exact
overlap/coverage identities are
\[
\boxed{\quad
2s_R+d_R=\mathcal A(p)+\mathcal Q+\Xi,
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\boxed{\quad
d_R+I
=
\mathcal A(p)+\mathcal Q-s_R+\alpha
\geq
\mathcal A(p)+\mathcal Q-s_R.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, the cap-shape excess \(\mathcal Q\) cannot be subtracted
by increasing the pairwise overlaps.

The overlaps themselves satisfy the exact quantitative charge
\[
\boxed{\quad
\left(
\sqrt{o_1+\frac{2r}{9}}
+\sqrt{o_2+\frac{2r}{9}}
+\sqrt{o_3+\frac{2r}{9}}
\right)^2
\leq2k.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
I\leq
2k+\frac{2r}{3}-\frac{8}{3}\sqrt{kr}.
\tag{8}
\]
There is also a support-level convexity charge:
\[
o_j o_k>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_{jk}>0
\qquad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}),
\tag{9}
\]
where the two overlaps on the left are precisely the two pairwise
overlaps contained in \(B_i\).  Since all three singleton cells are
positive,
\[
\boxed{\quad \Xi+d_R>0.\quad}
\tag{10}
\]

The equality and near-equality alternatives in (5) are exact.
Equality
\[
2s_R+d_R=\mathcal A(p)
\tag{11}
\]
holds if and only if all of the following hold:

1. \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) are translates of one another;
2. \(P\) is homothetic to the \(C_i\);
3. up to cutting-line null sets, \(\mathcal U\) covers every singleton
   and doubleton middle cell, and
   \(B_i\cap B_j\) covers the corresponding remaining singleton cell.

More quantitatively, if the left side of (5) minus \(\mathcal A(p)\)
is at most \(\varepsilon\), then
\[
0\leq\alpha,\beta,\Xi\leq\varepsilon,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
0\leq
\mathcal V(P,S_Q)-\sqrt{p|S_Q|}
\leq2\varepsilon,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
0\leq
\sqrt{|S_Q|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}
\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\sqrt p}.
\tag{14}
\]

Now retain \(W=0\) and the strict positive-middle defect from accepted
fact `0009896ebff12f31`.  Put
\[
H(p)=
4p-\frac{150}{7}
-\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt{\frac5{21}}\right)^2
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
p_*=
\left(
\frac{\sqrt{105}+2\sqrt{13790}}{91}
\right)^2
=7.2549488905\ldots.
\tag{16}
\]
Then every hypothetical realization satisfies the exact strict budget
\[
\boxed{\quad
0\leq
\mathcal Q+\Xi+d_R+2r
<
H(p),
\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
and hence
\[
p>p_*.
\tag{18}
\]

Suppose a sequence of hypothetical realizations has \(p\to p_*\).
Then
\[
\mathcal Q,\Xi,d_R,r\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\eta,w_P\longrightarrow\frac67,
\tag{19}
\]
\[
k\longrightarrow
k_*:=2p_*-\frac{75}{7}>0,
\qquad
s_R,I\longrightarrow k_*.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (12)--(14) hold with \(\varepsilon\to0\).  If a subsequence of
the normalized overlap vectors converges,
\[
\frac{(o_1,o_2,o_3)}I\longrightarrow(y_1,y_2,y_3),
\]
then
\[
y_i\geq0,\qquad
y_1+y_2+y_3=1,\qquad
\left(\sqrt{y_1}+\sqrt{y_2}+\sqrt{y_3}\right)^2\leq2.
\tag{21}
\]
In particular,
\[
\max_i y_i\geq\frac12,
\tag{22}
\]
and equality in (22) is possible in (21) only for a permutation of
\((1/2,1/2,0)\).  Thus equal or nearly equal sharing among the three
singleton overlaps is excluded on this boundary.

Finally, let
\[
\Delta_0=4k-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2,\qquad
m=\frac{12}{7},
\qquad
D=\frac1{|\mathcal T_Q|},
\]
where \(D\) is the determinant parameter of accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`.  Every hypothetical critical-ratio counterexample
satisfies
\[
\frac{\Delta_0}{m}>
\frac{293-8\sqrt{183}}{49}
=3.7709797129\ldots,
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
D<
\frac{2401}{
\left(
\sqrt{84}+\sqrt{18888-96\sqrt{183}}
\right)^2
}
=0.1194266603\ldots.
\quad}
\tag{24}
\]
All non-strict area identities, inequalities, and near-equality conclusions
include the eight endpoint-coincidence strata, collinear and redundant
boundary labels, and admissible limiting coincidences of cutting lines.
The strict support conclusions (9)--(10) hold for every admissible member,
without a claimed uniform modulus across a degenerating line arrangement.

## Proof

Accepted fact `6e4bb19f9157027f` proves
\[
2s_R+d_R=\sum_i|B_i|+\Xi
\tag{25}
\]
with \(\alpha,\beta,\Xi\geq0\).  For positive-area planar convex bodies,
Minkowski area is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial.  Merging the
counterclockwise edge-vector lists gives
\[
|aA+bB|
=a^2|A|+2ab\,\mathcal V(A,B)+b^2|B|
\]
and shows additivity of mixed area in either argument.  Hence
\[
\mathcal V(P,S_Q)=\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i).
\tag{26}
\]
Also
\[
|B_i|
=\frac14|P+C_i|
=\frac14(p+x)+\frac12\mathcal V(P,C_i).
\]
Summing and using (26) gives
\[
\sum_i|B_i|
=\frac34(p+x)+\frac12\mathcal V(P,S_Q).
\tag{27}
\]
Subtracting (3) from (27), then adding and subtracting
\(\sqrt{p|S_Q|}/2\), gives exactly
\[
\sum_i|B_i|-\mathcal A(p)=\mathcal Q.
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (25) and (28) prove (5).

Planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
\mathcal V(P,S_Q)\geq\sqrt{p|S_Q|}
\]
and, iterated over the three equal-area caps,
\[
\sqrt{|S_Q|}
\geq\sqrt{|C_1|}+\sqrt{|C_2|}+\sqrt{|C_3|}
=3\sqrt x.
\]
Thus both summands in (4) are nonnegative.  The equality
characterization supplied in accepted fact `b8e22f6a4b26ea94` says that
equality for positive-area convex bodies occurs exactly for homothetic
bodies.  Equality in the iterated inequality therefore makes the three
equal-area caps translates.  Then \(S_Q\) is a translate of a dilation
of any one cap, so equality in the first term of (4) is exactly the
homothety of \(P\) with the caps.  This proves the two shape clauses of
the equality classification.

The incidence statement in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, as used in
the accepted ledger, gives
\[
\mathcal U\subseteq
\bigcup_{1\leq|G|\leq2}\overline{R_G},
\qquad
B_i\cap B_j\subseteq\overline{R_{\{k\}}}
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=N)
\tag{29}
\]
up to cutting-line null sets.  Therefore
\[
\sum_i|B_i|=|\mathcal U|+I.
\tag{30}
\]
Substitute (28) and
\(|\mathcal U|=s_R+d_R-\alpha\) in (30).  This gives the exact identity
in (6).  Moreover, \(\alpha=0\) is exactly almost-everywhere coverage of
the noncentral middle cells.  Since \(o_i\leq r_i\) and
\[
\beta=\sum_i(r_i-o_i),
\]
\(\beta=0\) is exactly almost-everywhere coverage of each singleton by
its corresponding pairwise intersection.  This proves the coverage
clause of (11), and the converse follows by reversing the same
identities.  Since (5) is the sum of the three nonnegative quantities
\(\alpha+\beta\) and the two terms in (4), bounds (12)--(14) follow
term by term.

Accepted fact `e1361027c1dda6fa` applies because the three singleton
cells are positive and gives
\[
\left(
\sqrt{r_1+\frac{2r}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_2+\frac{2r}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_3+\frac{2r}{9}}
\right)^2\leq2k.
\]
Monotonicity and \(o_i\leq r_i\) prove (7).  Put \(a=2r/9\).
For \(u,v\geq0\),
\[
\sqrt{u+a}+\sqrt{v+a}
\geq\sqrt{u+v+a}+\sqrt a,
\tag{31}
\]
because after squaring positive sides the remaining inequality is
\((u+a)(v+a)\geq a(u+v+a)\), namely \(uv\geq0\).
Applying (31) twice yields
\[
\sum_i\sqrt{o_i+a}
\geq\sqrt{I+a}+2\sqrt a.
\]
Combine this with (7), isolate the first square root, and square the
nonnegative sides:
\[
I\leq2k+3a-4\sqrt{2ka}.
\]
Substitution of \(a=2r/9\) proves (8).

We prove (9) directly from convexity.  Fix distinct \(i,j,k\) and assume
\[
o_j>0,\qquad o_k>0.
\]
By (29), positive area and the nullity of the cutting lines supply
points
\[
x\in\operatorname{int}(B_i)\cap R_{\{k\}},
\qquad
y\in\operatorname{int}(B_i)\cap R_{\{j\}}
\]
with all three sign inequalities strict.  Along \([x,y]\), one has
\(f_i>0\).  At \(x\),
\[
f_j>0,\qquad f_k<0,
\]
and at \(y\),
\[
f_j<0,\qquad f_k>0.
\]
Throughout the segment,
\[
f_j+f_k=-1-f_i<0.
\]
Hence the zero of \(f_j\) occurs strictly before the zero of \(f_k\),
and on the nonempty open subsegment between them
\[
f_j<0,\qquad f_k<0,\qquad f_i>0.
\]
Small disks around \(x\) and \(y\) remain in \(B_i\) and in their strict
singleton chambers.  Their convex hull contains a positive-area
neighborhood of this open subsegment, contained in the strict
doubleton chamber \(R_{\{j,k\}}\).  Thus \(r_{jk}>0\), proving (9).

If \(\Xi=0\), then \(\beta=0\), so \(o_i=r_i>0\) for every \(i\).
Applying (9) cyclically makes all three doubleton areas positive, and
therefore \(d_R>0\).  If \(\Xi>0\), (10) is immediate.  This proves the
strict support charge in every case.

We next derive the boundary budget.  The \(W=0\) elimination in accepted
fact `6e4bb19f9157027f` gives
\[
2s_R+d_R
=4p-\frac{138}{7}-\frac{7\eta}{3}+\frac{w_P}{3}.
\tag{32}
\]
Using
\[
\eta=w_P+3d_R+6r
\]
in (32), and then using (5), gives the exact identity
\[
\mathcal Q+\Xi+d_R+2r
=
4p-\frac{138}{7}-\mathcal A(p)-2\eta.
\tag{33}
\]
Accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` computes the critical-ratio value
\(\epsilon=6/7\) and proves
\[
\eta>\frac67.
\]
Equations (15) and (33) prove (17).

The inequality \(H(p)>0\) can be solved exactly.  Put \(t=\sqrt p\).
After multiplying by \(28\), it is
\[
91t^2-2\sqrt{105}\,t-605>0.
\]
Its negative root is irrelevant, and its positive root is
\[
\frac{\sqrt{105}+2\sqrt{13790}}{91}.
\]
This proves (16)--(18).

If \(p\to p_*\), then \(H(p)\to0\), so (17) and nonnegativity force
\[
\mathcal Q,\Xi,d_R,r\to0.
\]
Equation (33) gives \(\eta\to6/7\), and
\(\eta=w_P+3d_R+6r\) gives \(w_P\to6/7\).
The \(W=0\) identity from accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7`,
\[
2p=\frac{69}{7}+k+\eta,
\tag{34}
\]
and \(k=s_R+d_R+r\) prove (20).  Since
\(\beta=s_R-I\leq\Xi\), one also has \(I\to k_*\).
The termwise conclusions (12)--(14) follow from the already proved
decomposition.

The normalized overlap vectors lie in the compact simplex.  On any
convergent subsequence, divide (7) by \(I\) and use
\[
I,k\to k_*,\qquad r\to0.
\]
This proves (21).  If every \(y_i\leq1/2\), the concave function
\(\sum_i\sqrt{y_i}\) attains its minimum on that truncated simplex at
an extreme point.  The extreme points are the permutations of
\((1/2,1/2,0)\), where the square of the sum is \(2\).  Strict
concavity gives equality only at those points.  This proves (22) and
its equality statement.

It remains to prove the determinant calibration.  Accepted fact
`084bf2f472bf29c7` gives
\[
\sqrt p\geq
\frac{
\sqrt m+\sqrt{120m+84+28\eta}
}{7}.
\]
Here \(m=12/7\) and \(\eta>6/7\), so
\[
\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}}
\geq\sqrt{\frac p m}
>
\frac{1+\sqrt{183}}7.
\tag{35}
\]
The outer-centroid argument in accepted fact `b8e22f6a4b26ea94`
shows that a member of the canonical counterexample branch must satisfy
\[
M\leq m+k.
\tag{36}
\]
Writing \(z=\sqrt{M/m}\), equations (36) and the definition of
\(\Delta_0\) give
\[
\frac{\Delta_0}{m}
\geq3z^2-2z-5.
\]
The right side is increasing on the range in (35).  Substitution of
\((1+\sqrt{183})/7\) proves the strict inequality (23).

Finally, the strengthened scale bridge in accepted fact
`b8e22f6a4b26ea94` gives
\[
\frac1D
\geq
\left(
2\sqrt r+
\frac{
\sqrt m+
\sqrt{120m+84+28\eta+7\Delta_0}
}{7}
\right)^2.
\tag{37}
\]
Drop the strictly positive first term and use
\(\eta>6/7\) and (23).  The second radicand is then strictly larger
than
\[
\frac{18888-96\sqrt{183}}{49},
\]
while \(m=84/49\).  Solving (37) for \(D\) proves (24).

All bodies used above are closures of positive-area exact cells, so
changing weak and strict assignments adds only subsets of cutting
lines.  Equations (25)--(37) use areas, Minkowski sums, convexity, and
continuous radicals, with no division by an endpoint or side-gap
parameter.  The eight endpoint-pair strata and all coincident,
collinear, or redundant labels in accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8` are therefore included.  The non-strict inequalities
also pass to admissible coincident-line limits; the strict support
implication (9) is asserted for each positive-area admissible member and
no uniform modulus across a degenerating line arrangement is claimed.
