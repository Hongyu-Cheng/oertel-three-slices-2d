## Statement

Use the canonical common-plane notation of accepted fact
`a2a9387275a2c97c`.  Put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-\sum_{r=1}^3L_r>0,
\]
and let
\[
\Delta=|\det(\nabla f_i,\nabla f_r)|>0
\]
for either \(r\ne i\); the magnitude is independent of that choice.
Fix one direction \(i\).  Let
\[
b=|P_0|>0,\qquad
u_i=|A_0\cap\{p_i\le0\}|>0,
\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\{q_i\le0\}|,
\]
\[
m=|A_2|,\qquad
C_i=|K\cap\{f_i\le0\}|.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume the crossing and canonical left tail
\[
C_i+u_i+v_i=t,\qquad C_i+m\ge t.
\tag{2}
\]

For \(s\) in the corresponding support intervals, define the coordinate
chord profiles
\[
W_0(s)=
\Delta\,
\frac{\mathcal H^1(A_0\cap\{p_i=s\})}
     {\|\nabla f_i\|},
\qquad
W_2(s)=
\Delta\,
\frac{\mathcal H^1(A_2\cap\{q_i=s\})}
     {\|\nabla f_i\|}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let
\[
\lambda_{0i}=W_0(0+),\qquad
\lambda_{2i}=W_2(0-),
\tag{4}
\]
and let
\[
g_i=W'_{0,+}(0),\qquad h_i=W'_{2,-}(0)
\tag{5}
\]
be the one-sided slopes of the affine chord pieces adjacent to zero.
At polygonal breakpoints these are the literal one-sided slopes.
Assume that this is a genuine lower crossing in the sense of accepted
fact `eddda25c734d34bc`, so exactly one of
\[
\lambda_{0i}<\lambda_{2i},
\tag{6a}
\]
\[
\lambda_{0i}=\lambda_{2i},
\qquad g_i+h_i<0
\tag{6b}
\]
holds.

Then
\[
\lambda_{2i}>\lambda_{0i}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\Delta u_i
\bigl(\Delta b-\lambda_{0i}\delta\bigr)_+
<
\frac{\lambda_{0i}^2\delta^2}{4}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently, using \(u_i,b>0\),
\[
\lambda_{2i}>\lambda_{0i}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\lambda_{0i}\delta>
2\Delta\bigl(\sqrt{u_i(u_i+b)}-u_i\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus trace equality forces strict slack in the accepted sharp chord
inequality `6a9cc7c11e208bba`.

For the full central common-order support
\[
P_j,P_0,P_\ell,P_{k\ell},\qquad
Q_j,Q_{jk},Q_N,Q_{k\ell},
\tag{9}
\]
write the endpoint cell areas as
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &P_j&P_0&P_\ell&P_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &a&b&c&d
\end{array},
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &Q_j&Q_{jk}&Q_N&Q_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &e&g_0&g&s .
\end{array}
\tag{10}
\]
Then (8) specializes to
\[
\lambda_{2j}>\lambda_{0j}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\lambda_{0j}\delta>
2\Delta\bigl(\sqrt{a(a+b)}-a\bigr),
\tag{11j}
\]
\[
\lambda_{2k}>\lambda_{0k}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\lambda_{0k}\delta>
2\Delta\bigl(\sqrt{d(d+b)}-d\bigr),
\tag{11k}
\]
\[
\lambda_{2\ell}>\lambda_{0\ell}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\lambda_{0\ell}\delta>
2\Delta\bigl(
\sqrt{(c+d)(b+c+d)}-(c+d)
\bigr).
\tag{11l}
\]

In particular, consider the scalar/profile data
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
P&a&b&c&d\\ \hline
 &1&49/2&2&1
\end{array},
\quad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
Q&e&g_0&g&s\\ \hline
 &3&1&1&1
\end{array},
\quad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
R&x&y&z&w\\ \hline
 &1&1&1&3
\end{array},
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\delta=100,\qquad\Delta=1,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\lambda_{0j}=\lambda_{2j}
=\lambda_{0k}=\lambda_{2k}
=\frac{\sqrt{51/2}-1}{50},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\lambda_{0\ell}=\lambda_{2\ell}
=\frac{\sqrt{165/2}-3}{50}.
\tag{15}
\]
These data violate (11j), (11k), and (11l), each with equality in both
alternatives.  Hence no convex polygons \(A_0,A_2\) can realize this
profile calibration at genuine lower crossings satisfying the canonical
tails.  No separator or middle-body construction can repair it.

## Proof

The profiles in (3) are nonnegative, concave, and piecewise affine on
their support intervals.  Coarea in these coordinates gives
\[
\int_I W_0(s)\,ds
=\Delta|A_0\cap\{p_i\in I\}|,
\qquad
\int_I W_2(s)\,ds
=\Delta|A_2\cap\{q_i\in I\}|.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(P_0\subseteq\{0<p_i<\delta\}\),
\[
\Delta b\le\int_0^\delta W_0(s)\,ds.
\tag{17}
\]
The crossing and tail in (2) give the opposite-side area comparison
\[
m-v_i\ge u_i.
\tag{18}
\]

Accepted fact `6a9cc7c11e208bba`, or the following concavity argument,
gives
\[
\Delta u_i
\bigl(\Delta b-\lambda_{0i}\delta\bigr)_+
\le
\frac{\lambda_{0i}^2\delta^2}{4}.
\tag{19}
\]
For completeness, suppose the positive part in (19) is nonzero.  The
right slope \(g_i\) is a supergradient of \(W_0\), so
\[
W_0(s)\le\lambda_{0i}+g_i s.
\]
Using this on \(0<s<\delta\) in (17) gives
\[
g_i\ge
\frac{2(\Delta b-\lambda_{0i}\delta)}{\delta^2}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
On the negative side, nonnegativity and the same supporting line confine
the support to \(s\ge-\lambda_{0i}/g_i\), whence
\[
\Delta u_i
\le\int_{-\lambda_{0i}/g_i}^0
(\lambda_{0i}+g_i s)\,ds
=\frac{\lambda_{0i}^2}{2g_i}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (20)-(21) prove (19).  The remaining regimes, including a
zero trace or a support contact, follow by the same limiting argument;
with \(u_i,b>0\), equality in (19) is necessarily in the positive-part
regime.

Assume for contradiction that neither alternative in (7) holds.  The
genuine-crossing alternatives (6) then force
\[
\lambda_{0i}=\lambda_{2i}=:\lambda>0,
\tag{22}
\]
and equality holds in (19).  Put
\[
A=\Delta u_i,\qquad B=\Delta b.
\]
Equality gives \(B>\lambda\delta\) and
\[
A(B-\lambda\delta)=\frac{\lambda^2\delta^2}{4}.
\tag{23}
\]
Every inequality in (20)-(21) must therefore be equality.  In
particular,
\[
g_i=
\frac{2(B-\lambda\delta)}{\delta^2}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2A}>0.
\tag{24}
\]
This is the equality-case slope forced by the actual \(A_0\) profile,
not an independently assignable parameter.

By (6b), the left slope of the actual \(A_2\) profile satisfies
\[
h_i<-g_i<0.
\tag{25}
\]
The left slope \(h_i\) is a supergradient of the concave function
\(W_2\).  Hence on the positive side
\[
W_2(s)\le\lambda+h_i s.
\]
Nonnegativity confines that side of the support to
\(0\le s\le-\lambda/h_i\), and (16) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(m-v_i)
&\le\int_0^{-\lambda/h_i}(\lambda+h_i s)\,ds\\
&=\frac{\lambda^2}{-2h_i}
<\frac{\lambda^2}{2g_i}
=A
=\Delta u_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus \(m-v_i<u_i\), contradicting (18).  This proves the strict
disjunction (7).

To obtain (8), put \(r=\lambda_{0i}\delta\).  The non-strict inequality
(19) has positive root
\[
r_*=2\Delta\bigl(\sqrt{u_i(u_i+b)}-u_i\bigr).
\]
For \(u_i,b>0\), one has \(0<r_*<\Delta b\).  Strictness in (19) is
therefore equivalent to \(r>r_*\).  This proves (8), and substitution
of the cap areas in (9)-(10) proves (11j)-(11l).

Finally, the totals in (12) are
\[
M=\frac{57}{2},\qquad m=6,\qquad\kappa=6,\qquad t=9.
\]
The three cap triples are
\[
(u_j,v_j,C_j)=(1,5,3),\qquad
(u_k,v_k,C_k)=(1,3,5),\qquad
(u_\ell,v_\ell,C_\ell)=(3,2,4).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus every crossing equals \(t\), and the left-tail quantities are
\[
C_j+m=9,\qquad C_k+m=11,\qquad C_\ell+m=10.
\]
Moreover,
\[
m-v_j=s=1=u_j,\qquad
m-v_k=e=3>u_k=1,
\]
\[
m-v_\ell=e+g_0=4>u_\ell=c+d=3.
\tag{28}
\]
The traces (14)-(15) equal exactly the three roots in (11j)-(11l),
while every trace pair is equal.  Each direction would therefore force
the strict reverse inequality \(m-v_i<u_i\) from (26), contradicting
(28).  This independently rules out the proposed calibration and
completes the proof.
