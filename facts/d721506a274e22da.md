## Statement

First assume only the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane
hypotheses, exact-cell convention, \(M\geq m\), crossings, canonical
tails, \(W=0\), all-double positive \(Q\)-support, and
\(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\).  Normalize
\[
q=q_{123}=1,\qquad
x_1=x_2=x_3=\frac5{21},\qquad
m=\frac{12}{7}.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the notation of accepted fact `6e4bb19f9157027f`:
\[
p=p_\varnothing,\qquad
w=w_P=s_P+4d_P,\qquad
\eta=w+3d_R+6r_{123},
\]
\[
\Xi\geq0,\qquad
\mathcal V_i=\mathcal V(P,C_i),\qquad |C_i|=\frac5{21}.
\]
Define
\[
\Lambda(p)
=
\frac{1839}{28}-\frac{39p}{4}
+\frac92\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\kappa
=
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal V_i
-3\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}}
\geq0.
\tag{3}
\]

For every full realization satisfying these base hypotheses, the
midpoint-ledger target of accepted fact `6e4bb19f9157027f`,
\[
\frac32\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal V_i+3\Xi
\geq
\frac{39p}{4}+w-\frac{1839}{28},
\tag{4}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac32\kappa+3\Xi\geq w-\Lambda(p).
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the baseline bounds \(\kappa\geq0\), \(\Xi\geq0\) prove (4)
throughout
\[
w\leq\Lambda(p).
\tag{6}
\]
This is the largest region that these two nonnegativity bounds alone can
certify: when \(w>\Lambda(p)\), their formal extremal values
\(\kappa=\Xi=0\) do not imply (5).

Let \(c_*>0\) be the constant of accepted fact
`af9178be3aa6202a`.  Every full realization under the base hypotheses
satisfies the universal scalar restrictions
\[
0\leq w\leq\frac{10}{7},\qquad
\eta>w,\qquad
\eta>\frac67,\qquad
\eta\geq c_*,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
7\eta\leq w-\Lambda(p)+6.
\tag{8}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\max\left\{w,\frac67\right\}<\eta
\leq\frac{w-\Lambda(p)+6}{7},
\qquad
\eta\geq c_*,
\qquad
0\leq w\leq\frac{10}{7}.
\tag{9}
\]
The strict and weak lower bounds on \(\eta\) in (9) remain separate when
\(c_*>6/7\).

Still without imposing any residual-counterexample condition, the exact
ledger identity is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac32\kappa+3\Xi
=
w-\Lambda(p)-(7\eta-6).
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
Hence (8) is precisely the nonnegativity consequence of (10), and the
amount required in (5) exceeds the correction recorded in (10) by
exactly
\[
7\eta-6>0.
\tag{11}
\]

There is also a universal cap-shape formulation.  Put
\[
\omega_C
=
\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}
-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}
\geq0.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
\kappa\geq\sqrt p\,\omega_C.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently each of
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Xi\geq\frac{w-\Lambda(p)}3
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
\omega_C\geq
\frac{2\bigl(w-\Lambda(p)\bigr)}{3\sqrt p}
\quad}
\tag{15}
\]
is sufficient for (4).  The exact tradeoff supplied by the same
cap-shape lower-bound method is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\omega_C+\frac{2\Xi}{\sqrt p}
\geq
\frac{2\bigl(w-\Lambda(p)\bigr)}{3\sqrt p}.
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
If the actual mixed-area correction \(\kappa\), rather than the lower
bound (13), is retained, (5) is the exact necessary-and-sufficient
tradeoff.

Now impose the additional hypothesis that the realization remains in
the canonical residual counterexample branch.  By the outer-centroid
alternative proved in accepted fact `b8e22f6a4b26ea94`, this residual
hypothesis supplies
\[
\boxed{\quad M\leq m+k.\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
Only under this added residual hypothesis, define
\[
D=\frac1{|\mathcal T_Q|},\qquad
\Delta_0
=
4k-\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{\frac{12}{7}}\right)^2.
\]
Then every residual realization satisfies (7)-(8) together with
\[
p\geq\frac{57}{7}+\eta+\frac w4,
\tag{18}
\]
\[
\Delta_0>
\frac{129-12\sqrt{21}}7,
\qquad
0<D<\frac19.
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, only on this residual branch,
\[
p>9,\qquad
\Lambda(p)<0,
\tag{20}
\]
and therefore
\[
w-\Lambda(p)
>
\frac{309}{14}-\frac92\sqrt{\frac{15}{7}}
>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the baseline closure region (6) contains no scalar point in the
canonical residual counterexample branch.  Assertions (17)-(21) are not
claimed for a full realization already closed by the outer-centroid
alternative.

## Proof

We begin with consequences valid for every full realization under the
base hypotheses.  At (1), accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` computes
\[
\eta>\frac67.
\tag{22}
\]
Also
\[
\eta=w+3d_R+6r_{123}>w
\tag{23}
\]
because \(d_R\geq0\) and \(r_{123}>0\).  Accepted fact
`af9178be3aa6202a` gives \(\eta\geq c_*\).  The effective lower bound in
accepted fact `9b267b01d2864206` is weaker than (22): its definition
gives
\[
c_{\rm eff}\leq\frac{7L}{16}<\frac67,
\]
since \(L<1\).

For the tail projection, write
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=m-x_i.
\]
The \(i\)-th crossing and left tail give
\[
h=u_i+v_i+c_i\leq c_i+m,
\]
so
\[
u_i\leq m-v_i=x_i=\frac5{21}.
\]
Consequently
\[
s_P+2d_P=\sum_i u_i\leq\frac57.
\]
Since
\[
w=s_P+4d_P\leq2(s_P+2d_P),
\]
this proves \(0\leq w\leq10/7\), and hence all of (7).

The universal planar mixed-area bound gives
\[
\mathcal V_i\geq
\sqrt{|P||C_i|}
=
\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}},
\]
which proves (3).  Substituting
\[
\sum_i\mathcal V_i
=
3\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}}+\kappa
\]
into (4), and moving the baseline term to the right, proves the exact
equivalence (5), because
\[
\frac{39p}{4}+w-\frac{1839}{28}
-
\frac92\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}}
=w-\Lambda(p).
\]
The region (6), and its maximality relative to only
\(\kappa,\Xi\geq0\), follow immediately.

Accepted fact `6e4bb19f9157027f` gives, for every base realization, the
exact identity
\[
7\eta
=
\frac{39p}{4}+w-\frac{1671}{28}
-\frac32\sum_i\mathcal V_i-3\Xi.
\tag{24}
\]
Substitution of (3) into (24) proves (10).  Nonnegativity of its left
side gives (8).  At the scalar level, this is the fixed-ratio form of the
overlap-corrected multiplicity inequality in accepted fact
`2bb72698f0248a65`; identity (24) records its two geometric slacks
separately.  Combining (8) with (22)-(23) proves (9).  Comparing (5) and
(10) proves (11).

For the cap-shape lower bound, the planar area polynomial
\[
|aK+bL|
=
a^2|K|+2ab\,\mathcal V(K,L)+b^2|L|
\qquad(a,b\geq0)
\]
shows that mixed area is additive in either Minkowski argument.  This
can be checked directly by merging the counterclockwise edge-vector
lists and expanding the shoelace determinant.  Therefore
\[
\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i)
=
\mathcal V(P,C_1+C_2+C_3).
\tag{25}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski, first for the three caps and then for \(P\) and
their sum, gives
\[
\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}
\geq
\sum_i\sqrt{|C_i|}
=3\sqrt{\frac5{21}},
\]
\[
\mathcal V(P,C_1+C_2+C_3)
\geq
\sqrt{p\,|C_1+C_2+C_3|}.
\]
Using (12) in (25) proves
\[
\sum_i\mathcal V_i
\geq
3\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}}+\sqrt p\,\omega_C,
\]
which is (13).  Substituting this lower bound into (5) proves (16);
(14) and (15) are its two one-term specializations.  Everything through
this point used only the base full-realization hypotheses.

We now impose the residual-counterexample hypothesis (17).  Accepted
fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` gives the exact \(W=0\) identity
\[
2p=\frac{69}{7}+k+\eta,
\qquad
k=2p-\frac{69}{7}-\eta.
\tag{26}
\]
Moreover
\[
M=p+s_P+d_P
\geq
p+\frac{s_P+4d_P}{4}
=p+\frac w4.
\tag{27}
\]
Combining (17), (26), and (27) gives
\[
p+\frac w4
\leq
\frac{12}{7}+2p-\frac{69}{7}-\eta,
\]
which is exactly (18).  With (22), it gives
\[
p>9+\frac w4\geq9.
\tag{28}
\]
This proves the first assertion in (20), only under (17).

The quantity \(\Delta_0\) is the total \(\mathrm F0\) deficit from
accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3`.  The residual inequality (17) gives
\(k\geq M-m\), and hence
\[
\Delta_0
\geq
3M-5m-2\sqrt{Mm}.
\tag{29}
\]
By (27)-(28),
\[
\frac{M}{m}
\geq
\frac{p+w/4}{12/7}
>
\frac9{12/7}
=\frac{21}{4}.
\]
The function \(3t-5-2\sqrt t\) is strictly increasing for \(t>1/9\).
Dividing (29) by \(m=12/7\) therefore proves
\[
\Delta_0
>
\frac{12}{7}\left(\frac{43}{4}-\sqrt{21}\right)
=
\frac{129-12\sqrt{21}}7,
\]
which is the first assertion in (19).  This sharpens the
\(\Delta_0\geq3m\) residual bound already recorded in accepted fact
`b8e22f6a4b26ea94`.

The strengthened scale bridge in accepted fact
`b8e22f6a4b26ea94`, again only after retaining this residual
\(\Delta_0\), is
\[
\frac1D
\geq
\left(
2\sqrt{r_{123}}
+
\frac{
\sqrt{12/7}
+
\sqrt{2028/7+28\eta+7\Delta_0}
}{7}
\right)^2.
\tag{30}
\]
By (22) and the first part of (19), the second radicand in (30) is
strictly larger than
\[
\frac{2028}{7}+24+129-12\sqrt{21}
=
\frac{3099}{7}-12\sqrt{21}
=
\left(21-\sqrt{\frac{12}{7}}\right)^2.
\tag{31}
\]
The last square root is positive.  Dropping only the positive
\(2\sqrt{r_{123}}\) term from (30), and using (31), gives
\[
\frac1D
>
\left(
\frac{\sqrt{12/7}+21-\sqrt{12/7}}7
\right)^2
=9.
\]
Thus \(0<D<1/9\), proving the second assertion in (19).  This improves
the residual \(D\)-upper bound in accepted fact `9b267b01d2864206`.

Finally,
\[
\Lambda'(p)
=
-\frac{39}{4}
+\frac94\sqrt{\frac5{21p}}<0
\qquad(p\geq9),
\]
and
\[
\Lambda(9)
=
-\frac{309}{14}
+\frac92\sqrt{\frac{15}{7}}
<0.
\tag{32}
\]
The last comparison is equivalent, after squaring positive sides, to
\(945<10609\).  Equations (28) and (32) prove the second assertion in
(20) and the uniform residual bound (21).

For comparison with the fixed-ratio determinant terms displayed in
accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31`,
\[
A_k=\frac{36}{5},\qquad c_k=\frac{10}{21},
\]
and exact substitution gives
\[
d_{1k}=\frac{496125}{4190209}>\frac19,
\]
where \(9\cdot496125=4465125>4190209\).  However
\[
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}>1
\]
because \(\sqrt{36/5}>41/21\).  Hence \(d_{2k}=0\), so the accepted
minimum \(\delta\) is still zero.  Thus the residual bound \(D<1/9\)
does not itself close the high-clip branch.  The conditional rows of
accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3` may further shrink the residual scalar
outer region, but they do not supply the unproved cap-shape lower bounds
(14)-(16).  This completes the exact scalar elimination with the
full-realization and residual-counterexample conclusions separated.
