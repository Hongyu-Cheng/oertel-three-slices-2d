## Statement

Assume the exact Regime A strip of accepted fact `ab2620c83e116de2`
and Alternative 3 of accepted fact `879a3c45b5f50670`. More explicitly,
let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex
body of area \(s\) and centroid \(g\). Let \(f_i,f_j,f_\ell\) be
nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_i+f_j+f_\ell=-1.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
c_h=\left|K\cap\{f_h\le0\}\right|
\qquad(h=i,j,\ell),
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume
\[
\begin{cases}
0<r\le1/5,\quad 1-r<s<1+r,\\
\text{or}\\
1/5<r<1/3,\quad 1-r<s<2-4r,
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
\[
c_j+r<t,
\tag{4}
\]
and that there are nonnegative \(u_i,u_\ell,v_i,v_\ell\) and
\(\omega>0\) such that
\[
c_i+u_i+v_i<t,\qquad
c_\ell+u_\ell+v_\ell<t,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
v_i+v_\ell=r+\omega.
\tag{6}
\]
Then these hypotheses are inconsistent. Consequently Alternative 3
\(q=0<\omega\) cannot occur anywhere in the full Regime A strip,
including \(r=1/5\) and all cap-empty or cap-full boundary cases.

## Proof

We use the planar centroid halfspace inequality established in accepted
fact `6e68b44550cac3c8`: if a closed halfplane contains the centroid of
a planar convex body of area \(s\), then its intersection with the body
has area at least \(4s/9\).

The strip (3) implies
\[
s>1-r,\qquad s<1+r,\qquad 0<r<\frac13.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, in the second line of (3), \(2-4r<1+r\) because \(r>1/5\).
Using (2) and (7),
\[
\frac{4s}{9}-(t-r)
=\frac{2s+7r-2}{9}
>\frac{5r}{9}>0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
t-\frac{4s}{9}
=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Also \(t<s\): by \(s>1-r\) and \(r<1/3\),
\[
7s>7(1-r)>2(1+r).
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (4) and (8) give
\[
c_j<\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(f_j(g)\le0\), then the \(j\)-cap is a closed halfplane section
containing \(g\), so the centroid halfspace inequality contradicts
(11). Hence
\[
f_j(g)>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Equation (1) and (12) imply
\[
f_i(g)+f_\ell(g)<-1.
\]
At least one of these two values is nonpositive. Interchange \(i,\ell\)
if necessary and assume
\[
f_i(g)\le0.
\tag{13}
\]
The centroid halfspace inequality gives
\[
c_i\ge\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{14}
\]
The first inequality in (5) gives \(c_i<t\).

Define
\[
F(x)=2\sqrt{sx}-2x.
\]
On \([4s/9,s]\),
\[
F'(x)=\sqrt{\frac{s}{x}}-2\le-\frac12,
\]
so \(F\) is strictly decreasing there. A direct substitution of
\(t=2(1+r+s)/9\) gives
\[
4st-(3t-r)^2
=
\frac{
4(s-1+r)(s+1+2r)+r(8-9r)
}{9}>0
\tag{15}
\]
by (7). Since \(3t-r=(2+2s-r)/3>0\), equation (15) yields
\[
F(t)=2\sqrt{st}-2t>t-r>c_j.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (10), (14), and \(c_i<t\) place \(c_i\) in
\([4s/9,t)\), and therefore
\[
F(c_i)>F(t)>c_j.
\tag{17}
\]

If \(f_\ell(g)\le0\), the centroid halfspace inequality gives
\[
c_\ell\ge\frac{4s}{9}\ge F(c_i),
\tag{18}
\]
where the last inequality uses the monotonicity of \(F\) and
\(F(4s/9)=4s/9\). If \(f_\ell(g)>0\), apply accepted theorem
`0688f679297c8baa` with
\[
(f_1,f_2,f_3)=(f_\ell,f_j,f_i).
\]
Its hypotheses hold by (1), (12), and the present case
\(f_\ell(g)>0\), so
\[
\max\{c_\ell,c_j\}\ge F(c_i).
\]
Equation (17) rules out \(c_j\) as the maximizing term, and again
\[
c_\ell\ge F(c_i).
\tag{19}
\]

The function
\[
x+F(x)=2\sqrt{sx}-x
\]
is increasing for \(0<x<s\), because its derivative is
\(\sqrt{s/x}-1>0\). Equations (10), (14), and (18)-(19) therefore give
\[
c_i+c_\ell
\ge c_i+F(c_i)
\ge\frac{4s}{9}+F\left(\frac{4s}{9}\right)
=\frac{8s}{9}.
\tag{20}
\]

Finally add the two strict inequalities in (5), use nonnegativity of
\(u_i,u_\ell\), and substitute (6):
\[
c_i+c_\ell+r+\omega<2t.
\]
Together with (20),
\[
r+\omega
<2t-\frac{8s}{9}
=\frac{4(1+r-s)}9
<\frac{8r}{9}
<r,
\tag{21}
\]
where the penultimate inequality is strict by \(s>1-r\). This is
impossible because \(\omega>0\). Thus Alternative 3 is excluded
throughout (3). No step assumes that any \(u_h\) or \(v_h\) is positive,
so the cap-empty and cap-full boundaries are included.
