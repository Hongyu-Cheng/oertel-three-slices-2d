## Statement

Normalize
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and, for \(z\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
\mathcal C(z;\tau)
=\left|\left\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\geq\tau\right\}\right|.
\]
Let \(U,V,L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and
\(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)^\top\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top L=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\det U\det V\ne0,
\]
\[
\min_jU_{ij}+\min_jV_{ij}\geq0
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Put
\[
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|,\qquad
\rho=\frac{\min\{A,B\}}{\max\{A,B\}},\qquad
\eta=\frac1{\max\{A,B\}},
\]
and assume the strict residual strip of accepted fact
64760c76cadff481:
\[
0<\rho<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},\qquad
L_0(\rho)<\eta<U_0(\rho),
\]
where
\[
L_0(\rho)=
\begin{cases}
1-\rho,&0<\rho\leq9/25,\\
(3\rho+2\sqrt\rho-1)/2,&9/25<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U_0(\rho)=
\begin{cases}
1+\rho,&0<\rho\leq1/2,\\
2-\rho,&1/2<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
Suppose there are permutation matrices \(R,P\) such that, after the
lossless relabeling
\[
L\longmapsto RLP,\qquad
U\longmapsto P^\top U,\qquad
V\longmapsto P^\top V,\qquad
\sigma\longmapsto R\sigma,
\]
the middle matrix has the form
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
\xi_1&-a\xi_1&-b\xi_1\\
-c\xi_2&\xi_2&-d\xi_2\\
-e\xi_3&-f\xi_3&\xi_3
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,a,b,c,d,e,f>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the three strict inequalities
\[
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
<\frac{2(1+A+B)}9
\qquad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{2}
\]
cannot all hold. Equivalently, at least one row in (2) is at least
\(2(1+A+B)/9\).

## Proof

Assume for contradiction that all three inequalities in (2) are strict.
Accepted fact 09393c6edf1fc4e2 applies to the exact \(N=3\) system of
accepted fact 64760c76cadff481. After possibly interchanging the two
endpoints and replacing \(\sigma\) by \(-\sigma\), it gives
\[
m=\min\{A,B\}=A,\qquad M=\max\{A,B\}=B,\qquad
q:=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3>1,\qquad 1+m<2M,
\tag{3}
\]
and nondegenerate triangles
\[
P_0\subset\left\{\sum_i x_i=1+q\right\},\qquad
P_1\subset\left\{\sum_i x_i=1\right\},\qquad
P_2\subset\left\{\sum_i x_i=1-q\right\}
\tag{4}
\]
such that
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,\qquad
|P_0|=m,\quad |P_1|=1,\quad |P_2|=M.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), define the normalized coordinate-cap areas
\[
u_i=|P_0\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|,\qquad
B_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|,\qquad
v_i=|P_2\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|.
\tag{6}
\]
The same accepted fact identifies (2) exactly with
\[
u_i+B_i+v_i<t,\qquad
T:=1+m+M,\qquad t:=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]

We first rederive the two residual area inequalities that will make every
later radical and strict comparison literal. In the present notation
\(\rho=m/M\) and \(\eta=1/M\). The displayed formula for \(L_0\)
satisfies \(L_0(\rho)\geq1-\rho\). Indeed this is equality for
\(\rho\leq9/25\), while for \(\rho>9/25\) the difference is
\[
\frac{5\rho+2\sqrt\rho-3}{2}>0,
\]
because it vanishes at \(9/25\) and is strictly increasing. Similarly,
\(U_0(\rho)\leq1+\rho\): this is equality for
\(\rho\leq1/2\), and for \(\rho\geq1/2\) it is
\(2-\rho\leq1+\rho\). Hence the strict residual strip gives
\[
1-\rho<\eta<1+\rho,
\]
or, after multiplication by \(M>0\),
\[
M-m<1<M+m.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular,
\[
M<1+m,\qquad m+M>1.
\tag{9}
\]

We next match the middle stratum to the accepted selector. Let
\[
\kappa=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a&b\\ c&0&d\\ e&f&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The column-sum equation for (1) is
\[
(I-\kappa)^\top
\begin{pmatrix}\xi_1\\\xi_2\\\xi_3\end{pmatrix}
=\mathbf1.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus
\(\kappa^\top\xi=\xi-\mathbf1<\xi\) coordinatewise. In the weighted
maximum norm with weights \(\xi_i>0\), this gives an induced norm
strictly below one, so the spectral radius of \(\kappa\) is below one.
For every \(0\leq r\leq1\), the matrix \(I-r\kappa\) is therefore
nonsingular. Continuity of its determinant from \(\det I=1\) gives
\[
\delta:=\det(I-\kappa)
=1-ac-be-df-ade-bcf>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Under the affine parametrization \(P_1=L\Delta\) and the normalization
\(|P_1|=1\), positive row factors do not change a zero-threshold cap.
Consequently the \(B_i\) in (6), and the singleton middle sign-cell
areas
\[
R_k=
\left|P_1\cap
\{x_k\geq0,\ x_i<0\text{ for }i\ne k\}\right|,
\tag{12}
\]
are exactly the quantities \(B_i,R_k\) of accepted fact
c5fe776a8c0535b9. In particular all three \(R_k\) are positive.

We now derive the residual mass quantities. Put
\[
S=B_1+B_2+B_3,\qquad
G=3t-S-m=\frac{2+2M-m}{3}-S.
\tag{13}
\]
Every coordinate function is nonconstant on every triangle in (4), since
a nondegenerate triangle cannot lie in the intersection of its
coordinate-sum plane with a coordinate plane. Hence all coordinate
cutting lines have area zero. The positive coordinate sum on \(P_0\)
implies that its three coordinate caps cover it, so
\(\sum_i u_i\geq m\). Summing (7) and using \(v_i\geq0\) gives
\[
3t>\sum_i(u_i+B_i+v_i)\geq m+S.
\]
Therefore every strict system necessarily has
\[
G>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(G\leq0\), including \(G=0\), is already an exclusion gate.

Let
\[
z_\varnothing=
\left|P_2\cap\{x_1<0,x_2<0,x_3<0\}\right|.
\]
The union of the three top coordinate caps has area
\(M-z_\varnothing\), and hence
\(\sum_i v_i\geq M-z_\varnothing\). Another use of the strict sum
in (7) gives
\[
z_\varnothing>m+M+S-3t.
\]
Define
\[
Z:=m+M+S-3t=M-G
=\frac{T}{3}+S-1.
\tag{15}
\]
Here \(E_1:=S-1\) is exactly the middle cap-multiplicity excess, since
the middle coordinate caps cover \(P_1\), and (15) is equivalently
\(Z=T/3+E_1\). We have proved the strict lower bound
\[
z_\varnothing>Z.
\tag{16}
\]

For completeness, we next rederive the independent area-gate
alternative. Set
\[
d=t-\frac49=\frac{2(m+M-1)}9.
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (8)-(9) imply
\[
0<d<\frac{4m}{9}<m.
\tag{18}
\]
Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(P_1\). Since
\(\sum_i x_i(g)=1\), some index \(j\) satisfies
\(x_j(g)\geq1/3>0\). We record the exact centroid cap calculation,
including repeated vertex values. Normalize the three sorted values of
the nonconstant affine function \(x_j\) on \(P_1\) to \(0,s,1\), with
\(0\leq s\leq1\). Its normalized centroid value is
\((1+s)/3\). The cap formulas in accepted fact
64760c76cadff481 give the cap fraction above the centroid as
\[
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(2-s)^2}{9(1-s)},&0\leq s\leq1/2,\\[2mm]
1-\dfrac{(1+s)^2}{9s},&1/2\leq s\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
The first expression is at least \(4/9\), since
\((2-s)^2-4(1-s)=s^2\). The second is at least \(4/9\), since
\(-s^2+3s-1>0\) on \([1/2,1]\). Formula (19) includes
the repeated-low equality \(s=0\) and the repeated-high endpoint
\(s=1\). Since \(x_j(g)>0\), the zero-threshold coordinate cap
strictly contains this centroid-level cap by a positive-area strip.
Therefore
\[
B_j>\frac49.
\tag{20}
\]
The \(j\)-th strict row in (7) gives
\[
u_j+v_j<t-B_j<d.
\tag{21}
\]
The complementary convex caps
\[
D_0=P_0\cap\{x_j\leq0\},\qquad
D_2=P_2\cap\{x_j\leq0\},\qquad
D_1=P_1\cap\{x_j\leq0\}
\]
have areas \(m-u_j,M-v_j,1-B_j\). Equations (18) and (21) show
that the first two have positive area. Compatibility gives
\((D_0+D_2)/2\subseteq D_1\), so planar Brunn--Minkowski yields
\[
1-B_j\geq
\frac14\left(\sqrt{m-u_j}+\sqrt{M-v_j}\right)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(e=u_j+v_j<d\). For fixed \(e\), the function
\[
r\longmapsto\sqrt{m-r}+\sqrt{M-e+r},
\qquad 0\leq r\leq e,
\]
is concave, so its minimum is at an endpoint. Because \(m\leq M\),
\[
\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
\leq\sqrt m+\sqrt{M-e}.
\]
Consequently (18) and \(e<d\) give
\[
\sqrt{m-u_j}+\sqrt{M-v_j}
\geq\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
>\sqrt{m-d}+\sqrt M.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (20), (22), and (23) proves the strict necessary area gate
\[
\left(\sqrt{m-\frac{2(m+M-1)}9}+\sqrt M\right)^2
<\frac{20}{9}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the reverse weak inequality, including equality, excludes a strict
system.

We finally derive the singleton Brunn--Minkowski gate used by the
selector. To avoid collision with the triangles \(P_0,P_1,P_2\), put
\[
\Pi_k:=S-B_k;
\]
thus \(\Pi_k\) is exactly the quantity denoted \(P_k\) in accepted fact
c5fe776a8c0535b9. Define
\[
s_k:=m-2t+\Pi_k=t-G-B_k.
\tag{25}
\]
Let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), and let
\[
p_k=
\left|P_0\cap
\{x_k\geq0,\ x_i<0,\ x_j<0\}\right|
\]
be the bottom singleton-cell area. Up to null coordinate lines, its
complement in \(P_0\) is the union of the bottom \(i\)- and \(j\)-caps.
Hence
\[
p_k=m-\left|P_0\cap
\bigl(\{x_i\geq0\}\cup\{x_j\geq0\}\bigr)\right|
\geq m-u_i-u_j.
\]
Adding the strict rows \(i,j\) in (7), and discarding their nonnegative
top contributions, gives
\[
u_i+u_j<2t-B_i-B_j=2t-\Pi_k.
\]
Therefore
\[
p_k>s_k.
\tag{26}
\]
If \(s_k>0\) and \(Z>0\), the closures of this bottom singleton cell
and the top all-negative cell are positive-area convex bodies. The
midpoint of any two of their points lies in \(P_1\) by (5), and its
\(i\)- and \(j\)-coordinates are nonpositive. Since its coordinate sum
is one, its \(k\)-coordinate is positive. Their midpoint set is
therefore contained in the closure of the middle singleton cell (12).
Planar Brunn--Minkowski, followed by (16) and (26), gives the strict
necessary singleton gate
\[
4R_k
\geq\left(\sqrt{p_k}+\sqrt{z_\varnothing}\right)^2
>\left(\sqrt{s_k}+\sqrt Z\right)^2.
\tag{27}
\]

We now apply the selector. Accepted fact c5fe776a8c0535b9, using
(11), supplies an index \(k\) such that
\[
\Pi_k\geq\theta(S),\qquad
4R_k\leq S-\frac13,
\tag{28}
\]
where
\[
\theta(S)=
\begin{cases}
8/9,&S\leq4/3,\\
(4-S)/3,&S\geq4/3.
\end{cases}
\tag{29}
\]
We verify that the radicals in (27) are positive without discarding any
equality case. From (9) and (25),
\[
s_k
=\Pi_k+\frac{5m-4-4M}{9}
>\Pi_k-\frac89+\frac m9.
\tag{30}
\]
If \(S\leq4/3\), equations (28)-(30) give
\(s_k>m/9>0\), even when \(\Pi_k=8/9\). If
\(S\geq4/3\), equations (13)-(14) and \(M<1+m\) give
\[
S<\frac{2+2M-m}{3}<\frac{4+m}{3},
\qquad m>3S-4.
\tag{31}
\]
Consequently
\[
s_k>
\frac{4-S}{3}-\frac89+\frac m9
=\frac{m+4-3S}{9}>0.
\tag{32}
\]
At \(S=4/3\), both branches of (29) equal \(8/9\), so either
argument applies. Thus equality in either the branch condition or the
first selector inequality causes no loss.

The positivity of \(R_k\), already included in accepted fact
c5fe776a8c0535b9, and the second inequality in (28) imply
\(S-1/3>0\). Equations (9) and (15) give
\[
Z-\left(S-\frac13\right)
=\frac{m+M-1}{3}>0.
\tag{33}
\]
Hence \(Z>0\), so the necessary singleton gate (27) applies to this
selected \(k\). But (27), (28), (32), and (33) give the impossible
strict chain
\[
4R_k
>\left(\sqrt{s_k}+\sqrt Z\right)^2
>Z
>S-\frac13
\geq4R_k.
\tag{34}
\]
The strict middle inequalities supply the first strict cell lower bounds,
the residual strip supplies the strict comparison in (33), and
\(s_k>0\) supplies the strict comparison with \(Z\). Therefore (34)
also covers equality \(4R_k=S-1/3\). This contradiction proves the
claimed exclusion of the entire stratum (1).

The accepted dependency closure used here does not turn this stratum
statement into the full three-row exclusion. Accepted fact
64760c76cadff481 retains all cap ordering and multiplicity strata,
accepted fact 09393c6edf1fc4e2 supplies the common-plane reduction but
does not restrict every middle row to (1), and accepted fact
c5fe776a8c0535b9 assumes \(a,b,c,d,e,f>0\) and \(\delta>0\).
Thus no cited accepted predecessor covers every complementary zero-entry,
repeated-positive-vertex-incidence, or two-positive-vertex case, and
the conclusion stops at the exact distinct-vertex stratum.
