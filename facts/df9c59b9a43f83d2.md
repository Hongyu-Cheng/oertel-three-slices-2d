## Statement

Use spatial coordinates \((x,u,v)\), with integer-height coordinate \(x\). Set
\[
\varepsilon=\frac1{200},\qquad
\Delta=\{(u,v):u\ge0,\ v\ge0,\ u+v\le1\},\qquad
K=\{(u,v):u\ge0,\ v\ge0,\ 2/3\le u+v\le5/6\}.
\]
Consider the two triples
\[
(A_0^{\mathrm I},A_1^{\mathrm I},A_2^{\mathrm I})
=(\Delta,\Delta,\varepsilon\Delta),\qquad
(A_0^{\mathrm {II}},A_1^{\mathrm {II}},A_2^{\mathrm {II}})
=(2K,\Delta,2\varepsilon K).
\]
For \(j\in\{\mathrm I,\mathrm {II}\}\), let \(C^j\) be the convex hull of \(\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i^j)\), and let \(S^j=C^j\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)\). Each \(C^j\) is a compact rational polytope with exactly the displayed nonempty two-dimensional integer-height sections. The two ordered area triples agree:
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac1{80000}\right),
\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2=\frac{80001}{80000}.
\]
Both triples lie in the full strict residual strip of accepted fact 15bb2c1680a0254e. More precisely, with that fact's notation \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(r=m/M\), and \(s=a_1/M\), they satisfy \(r=1/40000<9/25\) and \(1-r<s=1<1+r\).

Let \(g_i^j\) denote the centroid of \(A_i^j\). Reuse the segment notation of accepted fact e50325b2598082f2, adding the family superscript:
\[
z_0^j=\frac{g_0^j+g_2^j}{2},\qquad
\delta^j=g_1^j-z_0^j,\qquad
z_t^j=z_0^j+t\delta^j\quad(0\le t\le1).
\]
The coefficient used here is explicitly \(\beta=1-t\), so
\[
z_{1-\beta}^j=(1-\beta)g_1^j+\beta\frac{g_0^j+g_2^j}{2}.
\]
Every such point belongs to \(A_1^j\). Write \(h_{S^j}(y)=\inf_{H\ni y}\mathcal H_2(S^j\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed halfspaces. With \(\beta_0=9/200000\), the following strict failures hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{S^{\mathrm I}}\bigl((1,z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm I})\bigr)
&<\frac{2T}{9}
&&\text{for every }\beta\in[\beta_0,1],\\
h_{S^{\mathrm {II}}}\bigl((1,z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm {II}})\bigr)
&<\frac{2T}{9}
&&\text{for every }\beta\in[0,\beta_0].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, there is no function \(b:(0,\infty)^3\to[0,1]\) for which choosing \(\beta=b(a_0,a_1,a_2)\) makes this centroid-segment selector attain depth at least \(2T/9\) for every compatible three-slice polytope in the full strict residual strip.

## Proof

For any convex planar set \(B\), convexity gives \(B+\varepsilon B=(1+\varepsilon)B\): division by \(1+\varepsilon\) turns a point of the Minkowski sum into a convex combination of two points of \(B\), and using the same point twice proves the reverse inclusion. Therefore the midpoint bodies of the two triples are
\[
\frac{A_0^{\mathrm I}+A_2^{\mathrm I}}2
=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\Delta\subseteq\Delta,\qquad
\frac{A_0^{\mathrm {II}}+A_2^{\mathrm {II}}}2
=(1+\varepsilon)K\subseteq\Delta.
\]
For the second containment, both coordinates are nonnegative and their sum is at most \((1+\varepsilon)5/6=67/80<1\). The first containment follows from \((1+\varepsilon)/2=201/400<1\).

To verify the actual integer sections, group a convex combination defining a point of either \(C^j\) by its three heights, with weights \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ge0\) summing to one. At height one, \(\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=1\), hence \(\lambda_0=\lambda_2\). Its planar component has the form
\[
\lambda_1p_1+2\lambda_0\frac{p_0+p_2}{2},
\qquad p_i\in A_i^j,\qquad \lambda_1+2\lambda_0=1.
\]
The midpoint containments just proved place this point in \(A_1^j\). The reverse inclusion follows from the definition of the convex hull. At height zero or two, only points of that height can receive positive weight, so those sections are exactly \(A_0^j\) and \(A_2^j\). All heights lie in \([0,2]\); thus there are no other integer-height sections. All defining vertices are rational, and every displayed planar polygon has positive area, proving the polytope and dimension assertions.

For the area and centroid of \(K\), use \(w=u+v\) and \(0\le u\le w\). The change from \((u,w)\) to \((u,v)\) has absolute Jacobian one, so
\[
|K|=\int_{2/3}^{5/6}w\,dw=\frac18,\qquad
\frac{1}{|K|}\int_K(u+v)\,du\,dv
=8\int_{2/3}^{5/6}w^2\,dw=\frac{61}{81}.
\]
Symmetry under interchanging \(u,v\) then gives the centroid \(g_K=(61/162,61/162)\). The centroid of \(\Delta\) is \(g=(1/3,1/3)\). Area scales by the square of the dilation factor, so both triples have areas \((1/2,1/2,\varepsilon^2/2)\), as asserted. In particular,
\[
\frac{2T/9}{|\Delta|}
=\frac{2(2+\varepsilon^2)}9
=\frac{8889}{20000}.
\]
For residuality, accepted fact 15bb2c1680a0254e gives the exact first-branch bounds \(L(r)=1-r\), \(U(r)=1+r\) when \(0<r\le9/25\). Here \(r=1/40000\), \(s=1\), and both bounds are strict. Also
\[
0<r<\frac9{25}<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(\sqrt{26}<9\). Hence both compatible geometric triples belong to the full strict residual strip, with its original normalization unchanged.

Centroids commute with positive dilations, so family I has \((g_0^{\mathrm I},g_1^{\mathrm I},g_2^{\mathrm I})=(g,g,\varepsilon g)\), and family II has \((g_0^{\mathrm {II}},g_1^{\mathrm {II}},g_2^{\mathrm {II}})=(2g_K,g,2\varepsilon g_K)\). These centroids lie in their respective polygons. The midpoint containments place each \(z_0^j\) in \(\Delta\); convexity then puts the entire segment from \(z_0^j\) to \(g_1^j\) in \(\Delta=A_1^j\).

For family I, let \(q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)\) be the coordinate sum of \(z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm I}\). Direct substitution gives
\[
q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)=\frac{2-\beta(1-\varepsilon)}3,\qquad
H_\beta^{\mathrm I}=\{(x,u,v):u+v\le q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)x\}.
\]
This is exactly the halfspace of accepted fact e50325b2598082f2 under the alias \(t=1-\beta\), \(q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)=\gamma_{1-\beta}\). In particular \((1,z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm I})\) lies on its boundary. Its slice mass can also be checked directly. For \(0\le\beta\le1\),
\[
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{3}\le q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)\le\frac23.
\]
The height-zero intersection is only the origin and has planar area zero. The middle intersection is \(q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)\Delta\), of area \(q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)^2/2\). At height two, the threshold is \(2q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)\ge2(1+\varepsilon)/3>\varepsilon\), so all of \(\varepsilon\Delta\) is included. Thus
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S^{\mathrm I}\cap H_\beta^{\mathrm I})}{|\Delta|}
=q_{\mathrm I}(\beta)^2+\varepsilon^2.
\]
The function \(q_{\mathrm I}\) is positive and decreasing on \([0,1]\). At \(\beta_0=9/200000\), exact arithmetic gives
\[
q_{\mathrm I}(\beta_0)=\frac{79998209}{120000000},\qquad
\frac{8889}{20000}
-\left(q_{\mathrm I}(\beta_0)^2+\frac1{40000}\right)
=\frac{6556792319}{14400000000000000}>0.
\]
Consequently, for every \(\beta\ge\beta_0\), this admissible halfspace has mass strictly below \(2T/9\), proving the first depth failure.

For family II, the midpoint centroid is \(z_0^{\mathrm {II}}=(1+\varepsilon)g_K\), whose coordinate sum is \((1+\varepsilon)61/81=4087/5400\). Hence the coordinate sum of \(z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm {II}}\) is
\[
q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)
=\frac23+\beta\left(\frac{4087}{5400}-\frac23\right)
=\frac23+\frac{487}{5400}\beta.
\]
It is increasing in \(\beta\). For \(0\le\beta\le\beta_0\),
\[
\frac23\le q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)
\le q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta_0)
=\frac{26666829}{40000000}<1.
\]
Use the closed halfspace
\[
H_\beta^{\mathrm {II}}
=\left\{(x,u,v):u+v\le
\frac43+\left(q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)-\frac43\right)x\right\}.
\]
At height one its boundary contains \(z_{1-\beta}^{\mathrm {II}}\), and its middle intersection is \(q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)\Delta\). At height zero, the threshold \(4/3\) equals the minimum coordinate sum on \(2K\), so the intersection is its lower exposed line segment, of planar area zero. At height two, the threshold satisfies
\[
2q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)-\frac43
\le2q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta_0)-\frac43
=\frac{487}{60000000}
<\frac1{150}
=\min_{(u,v)\in2\varepsilon K}(u+v).
\]
Thus the height-two intersection is empty. The total mass therefore obeys
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S^{\mathrm {II}}\cap H_\beta^{\mathrm {II}})}{|\Delta|}
=q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta)^2
\le q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta_0)^2,
\]
and the exact comparison is
\[
\frac{8889}{20000}-q_{\mathrm {II}}(\beta_0)^2
=\frac{231084759}{16\cdot10^{14}}>0.
\]
This proves the second depth failure for every \(\beta\in[0,\beta_0]\), including its endpoints.

Finally, any proposed function \(b\) must choose the same number \(\beta=b(1/2,1/2,1/80000)\) for both triples. If \(\beta\ge\beta_0\), family I fails; if \(\beta\le\beta_0\), family II fails. These two cases cover \([0,1]\). Therefore no such function can provide the universal depth guarantee, even on these two triples within the full strict residual strip. The argument compares a common coefficient across different geometries and does not assert that either polytope lacks some point of depth at least \(2T/9\).
