## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon. Let
\[
\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3
\]
be pairwise nonparallel affine lines, and choose for each line a central closed halfplane \(H_i^-\) and its complementary outer closed halfplane \(H_i^+\). Assume:

1. each \(\ell_i\) meets the interior of \(K\), so \(K\cap\ell_i\) is a nondegenerate chord;
2. the six chord endpoints are distinct;
3. the three outer caps
\[
K\cap H_i^+
\]
have pairwise disjoint interiors; and
4. after inserting the six chord endpoints as marked boundary vertices, each of the six open boundary arcs between consecutive marks lies in one fixed closed sign chamber, alternating between a cap chamber
\[
H_i^+\cap H_j^-\cap H_k^-
\]
and the central chamber
\[
H_1^-\cap H_2^-\cap H_3^-.
\]

Then a finite sequence of parallel vertex slides and collinear deletions produces a convex polygon \(K'\) with the following properties:

1. the six marked chord endpoints and the three cutting lines are unchanged;
2. every marked-to-marked boundary arc remains in its original closed sign chamber;
3. every such arc has at most one unmarked vertex;
4. the signed area between every arc and its fixed endpoint chord is unchanged; and
5. the areas of the three outer caps and the central remainder are unchanged.

In particular, \(K'\) has at most twelve vertices.

## Proof

Orient the boundary counterclockwise and insert the six marked chord endpoints into the vertex list. We first record the elementary line-intersection fact that resolves the only new issue beyond the parallel slide in accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031`.

Let \(C\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon and let a line \(L\) meet both open halfplanes in interior points of \(C\). Then
\[
C\cap L
\]
is a nondegenerate closed segment, its two endpoints are the only points of
\[
\partial C\cap L,
\tag{1}
\]
and the relative interior of that segment lies in \(\operatorname{int}C\). Indeed, choose interior points on the two sides of \(L\). Their joining segment crosses \(L\) at an interior point of \(C\). Hence \(L\) meets \(\operatorname{int}C\), and the standard relative-interior identity for the intersection of a convex set with an affine line gives
\[
\operatorname{relint}(C\cap L)=\operatorname{int}C\cap L.
\]
This proves (1).

Now fix one marked-to-marked arc whose closed sign chamber is \(S\). Suppose that the arc contains at least two consecutive unmarked vertices \(u,v\), and let \(a\) be the vertex immediately preceding \(u\). The parallel-slide calculation used in the proof of accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031` moves \(u\) on the line
\[
u+t(v-a).
\tag{2}
\]
The two shoelace terms involving \(u\) are unchanged, because
\[
\det(a,u+t(v-a))+\det(u+t(v-a),v)
\]
differs from
\[
\det(a,u)+\det(u,v)
\]
by
\[
t\bigl(\det(a,v-a)+\det(v-a,v)\bigr)
=t\bigl(\det(a,v)-\det(a,v)\bigr)
=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031` also gives that the admissible positions in (2) preserving convexity form an interval and that, at one endpoint of the appropriate direction, \(u,v\), and the vertex following \(v\) become collinear. At that endpoint \(v\) can be deleted.

It remains to prove that the motion can reach this deleting endpoint without leaving \(S\). Follow the chosen slide from \(t=0\), and suppose that at or before the collinearity endpoint the moving vertex first meets a cutting line \(\ell_i\). Up to that first time, \(u(t)\) stays in the same open sign chamber, all other vertices are fixed, and the ordered polygon remains convex. In particular, the two fixed marked endpoints of the chord on \(\ell_i\) remain boundary intersections. The positive areas on both sides of \(\ell_i\), which the slide has not changed, ensure that the line still meets the polygon interior.

At the first contact, the limiting polygon is convex and still has interior points on both sides of \(\ell_i\). By (1), its boundary has exactly the two fixed marked points on \(\ell_i\). Therefore the new contact \(u(t)\) cannot be a third distinct boundary point. There are only two cases:

1. \(u(t)\) collides with one of the two fixed marked endpoints. Convexity forces the boundary chain between two occurrences of the same extreme point to be degenerate. Keep the fixed marked occurrence and delete the coincident unmarked occurrence, together with any intervening zero-length or collinear unmarked vertices. The number of unmarked vertices decreases.
2. \(u(t)\) is distinct from both fixed marks. This contradicts (1), so this case is impossible.

Thus no moving vertex crosses a cutting line before either the standard collinearity deletion or a marked-endpoint collision. The entire swept set
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{a,v,u(0),u(t)\}
\]
therefore lies in the same closed convex chamber \(S\). The polygon changes nowhere outside that set. Equation (3) preserves total signed area, so the area of the one affected chamber is unchanged, while every other chamber is untouched. Equivalently, the signed area between the current marked-to-marked arc and its fixed endpoint chord is preserved. Deleting a collinear vertex or merging a coincident unmarked vertex changes no area.

Perform this operation whenever an arc has at least two unmarked vertices. Each operation leaves all six marks fixed, keeps every arc in its assigned closed chamber, preserves convexity and all four cell areas, and strictly decreases the total number of unmarked vertices. Hence the process terminates after finitely many operations. At termination each of the six arcs has at most one unmarked vertex, so together with the six marked chord endpoints the resulting polygon has at most twelve vertices.

The statement assumes the generic six-distinct-chord configuration needed for the finite parameterization. Collinear vertices present initially can simply be deleted first. Coincidence or tangency degeneracies are obtained as limits of the same construction, but no such limiting extension is needed for the stated conclusion.
