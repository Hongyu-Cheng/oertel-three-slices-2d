## Statement

Assume the \(N=3\) exact barycentric system of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`. Put
\[
q=\sigma _1+\sigma _2+\sigma _3,\qquad
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9,
\]
and
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Then:

1. If \(-1\le q\le1\), then
   \(\sum_iM_i\ge1+A+B\).
2. If \(q>1\), then \(\sum_iM_i\ge1+A\), and the three strict
   inequalities \(M_i<t\) are impossible if \(1+A\ge2B\).
3. If \(q<-1\), then \(\sum_iM_i\ge1+B\), and the three strict
   inequalities \(M_i<t\) are impossible if \(1+B\ge2A\).
4. Every remaining strict system can, after possibly interchanging
   \(U,A\) with \(V,B\) and replacing every \(\sigma_i\) by
   \(-\sigma_i\), be written
   \[
   B=M=\max\{A,B\},\qquad
   A=m=\min\{A,B\},\qquad
   q>1,\qquad
   1+m<2M.
   \]
   In this remaining system, \(\det L\ne0\). With
   \(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)^\top\), the invertible map
   \[
   \Phi(h,z)=\bigl(h,Lz+(1-h)\sigma\bigr)
   \]
   sends the three triangles to
   \[
   P_0=LU\Delta+\sigma,\qquad
   P_1=L\Delta,\qquad
   P_2=LV\Delta-\sigma.
   \]
   They lie respectively in
   \[
   \left\{\sum_i x_i=1+q\right\},\qquad
   \left\{\sum_i x_i=1\right\},\qquad
   \left\{\sum_i x_i=1-q\right\},
   \]
   satisfy \((P_0+P_2)/2\subseteq P_1\), have area ratio
   \(A:1:B\), and, after normalization by \(|P_1|\), the sum of
   their three caps \(x_i\ge0\) is exactly \(M_i\) for each
   \(i=1,2,3\).

## Proof

We begin with the threshold-sum cover principle. Suppose
\(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r z^{(i)}=\mathbf1^\top
\tag{1}
\]
coordinatewise, and suppose \(\tau_1+\cdots+\tau_r\le1\). Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\mathcal C(z^{(i)};\tau_i)\ge1.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, if some \(\lambda\in\Delta\) lay outside every closed cap, then
\[
z^{(i)}\cdot\lambda<\tau_i\qquad(i=1,\ldots,r).
\]
Summing and using (1) would give
\[
1=\mathbf1^\top\lambda
<\sum_i\tau_i\le1,
\]
a contradiction. Thus the closed caps cover \(\Delta\), and (2)
follows. This pointwise proof includes constant rows, repeated values,
and equality in the threshold sum.

The column-sum identities for \(L,U,V\) give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3L_{i*}=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3(LU)_{i*}=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3(LV)_{i*}=\mathbf1^\top.
\tag{3}
\]
Applying (2) to the middle rows with all thresholds zero gives
\[
\sum_i\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)\ge1.
\tag{4}
\]
For the \(U\)-rows, the thresholds are \(-\sigma_i\), whose sum is
\(-q\). Hence \(q\ge-1\) gives
\[
\sum_i\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)\ge1.
\tag{5}
\]
For the \(V\)-rows, the thresholds are \(\sigma_i\), whose sum is
\(q\). Hence \(q\le1\) gives
\[
\sum_i\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)-(6) prove
\[
\sum_iM_i\ge
\begin{cases}
1+A+B,&-1\le q\le1,\\
1+A,&q>1,\\
1+B,&q<-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

If every row were strict, then
\[
\sum_iM_i<3t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}3.
\tag{8}
\]
In the central range, (7) directly contradicts (8). If \(q>1\), then
\[
1+A\ge\frac{2(1+A+B)}3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1+A\ge2B,
\tag{9}
\]
so (7) again contradicts (8) whenever the condition in (9) holds.
The identical calculation with \(A,B\) interchanged proves the
\(q<-1\) assertion.

It follows that a remaining strict system must satisfy one of
\[
q>1,\quad 1+A<2B,
\tag{10}
\]
or
\[
q<-1,\quad 1+B<2A.
\tag{11}
\]
Interchanging the endpoint data by
\[
(U,A,V,B,\sigma)\longmapsto(V,B,U,A,-\sigma)
\tag{12}
\]
preserves compatibility, the residual strip, and every \(M_i\), while
replacing \(q\) by \(-q\). Thus (11) becomes (10), and it is enough to
consider (10).

Write
\[
m=\min\{A,B\},\qquad M=\max\{A,B\},\qquad
R=\frac mM,\qquad S=\frac1M.
\tag{13}
\]
We claim that (10) forces \(B=M\). Otherwise \(A=M\) and \(B=m\), so
\(1+M<2m\). Dividing by \(M\) gives
\[
S<2R-1,
\tag{14}
\]
and in particular \(R>1/2\). On this range the strict lower residual
bound is
\[
S>L_0(R)=\frac{3R+2\sqrt R-1}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]
But
\[
L_0(R)-(2R-1)
=\frac{1-R+2\sqrt R}{2}>0
\tag{16}
\]
because the residual strip has \(0<R<1\). Equations (14)-(16)
contradict one another. Therefore \(B=M\), \(A=m\), and (10) is exactly
\[
q>1,\qquad 1+m<2M.
\tag{17}
\]

We next exclude singular \(L\). We first note an elementary numerical
consequence of the residual strip which will handle the one-row
degeneracy:
\[
t<1.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, throughout the residual interval,
\[
L_0(R)\ge\frac{16}{25}.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(R\le9/25\), this is \(1-R\ge16/25\). For \(R>9/25\), the second
branch of \(L_0\) is strictly increasing and equals \(16/25\) at
\(R=9/25\). Also
\[
R<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}<\frac{17}{25}.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(S>16/25\), so \(M<25/16\), and (20) gives
\[
m=RM<\frac{17}{16}.
\]
Consequently
\[
1+A+B=1+m+M<\frac{29}{8},
\qquad
t<\frac{29}{36}<1,
\]
which proves (18).

Suppose now that \(\det L=0\). Choose a nonzero column vector
\(c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
c^\top L=0.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(\mathbf1^\top L=\mathbf1^\top\), every vector
\[
w=\mathbf1+s c
\tag{22}
\]
satisfies \(w^\top L=\mathbf1^\top\). Starting at
\(\mathbf1=(1,1,1)^\top\) and moving in one of the two directions
\(\pm c\), stop at the first boundary point of the nonnegative
orthant. This gives a nonzero \(w\ge0\) with at least one zero
coordinate and
\[
\sum_iw_iL_{i*}=\mathbf1^\top.
\tag{23}
\]

If exactly two coordinates \(w_i,w_j\) are positive, define a two-row
system by
\[
\widehat L_{1*}=w_iL_{i*},\qquad
\widehat L_{2*}=w_jL_{j*},\qquad
\widehat\sigma_1=w_i\sigma_i,\qquad
\widehat\sigma_2=w_j\sigma_j.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation (23) gives the required column sums. For every \(a>0\),
\[
\mathcal C(az;a\tau)=\mathcal C(z;\tau),
\tag{25}
\]
so the two row masses of (24) are exactly \(M_i\) and \(M_j\). Both
are strictly below \(t\), contradicting the exact two-row exclusion in
accepted fact `a75a8435d7e8176f`.

The only other possibility is that exactly one coordinate \(w_i\) is
positive. Then (23) says
\[
w_iL_{i*}=\mathbf1^\top.
\]
Hence \(L_{i*}\cdot\lambda=1/w_i>0\) for every
\(\lambda\in\Delta\), and the middle cap in row \(i\) is full:
\[
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)=1.
\]
Therefore \(M_i\ge1>t\) by (18), again contradicting strictness.
This proves
\[
\det L\ne0
\tag{26}
\]
in every genuinely three-row survivor.

It remains to give the promised literal coordinate-sign model. Put
\(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)^\top\) and define
\[
\Phi(h,z)=\bigl(h,Lz+(1-h)\sigma\bigr).
\tag{27}
\]
By (26), this is invertible, with inverse
\[
\Phi^{-1}(h,x)
=\bigl(h,L^{-1}(x-(1-h)\sigma)\bigr).
\tag{28}
\]
For the original triangles \(A_0=U\Delta\), \(A_1=\Delta\), and
\(A_2=V\Delta\), their images are
\[
P_0=LU\Delta+\sigma,\qquad
P_1=L\Delta,\qquad
P_2=LV\Delta-\sigma.
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(\mathbf1^\top L=\mathbf1^\top\), every \(z\) in an original
triangle has coordinate sum one, and hence
\[
\mathbf1^\top\bigl(Lz+(1-h)\sigma\bigr)
=1+(1-h)q.
\tag{30}
\]
This proves the three asserted coordinate-sum planes. Nonzero
\(\det L,\det U,\det V\) also shows that all three images are
nondegenerate triangles.

The endpoint translations cancel at the middle height, so
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}
=L\left(\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right)
\subseteq LA_1=P_1.
\tag{31}
\]
Thus compatibility is preserved exactly. Moreover,
\(\mathbf1^\top L=\mathbf1^\top\) makes the sum-zero plane invariant,
and the induced map on the one-dimensional quotient is the identity.
Therefore the planar area multiplier of \(L\) on every slice is
\[
D=|\det L|.
\tag{32}
\]
Translations do not change area, so
\[
|P_0|=DA,\qquad |P_1|=D,\qquad |P_2|=DB.
\tag{33}
\]

Finally, the \(i\)-th coordinate of the second component of (27) is
\[
(Lz)_i+(1-h)\sigma_i
=(Lz)_i-\sigma_i(h-1).
\tag{34}
\]
Thus the original row-\(i\) halfspace is sent literally to
\(\{x_i\ge0\}\). Using \(z=U\lambda,\lambda,V\lambda\) at the three
heights gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P_0\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|
&=DA\,\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i),\\
|P_1\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|
&=D\,\mathcal C(L_{i*};0),\\
|P_2\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|
&=DB\,\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Their sum is \(DM_i\); division by \(|P_1|=D\) gives exactly \(M_i\).
Because (27)-(28) are inverse affine maps, no compatibility, area, cap,
boundary, or degeneracy information is lost in this rewriting.

In the reduced branch, \(A=m\) is the bottom area and \(B=M\) is the
top area. Since \(q>1\), the bottom plane has coordinate sum
\(1+q>0\), so its three coordinate-nonnegative caps cover \(P_0\).
The top plane has coordinate sum \(1-q<0\), so it has no analogous
forced cover. Hence the small endpoint is exactly on the covered side
and the large endpoint is on the only uncovered side, as claimed.
