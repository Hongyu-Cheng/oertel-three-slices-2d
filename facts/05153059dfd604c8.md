## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\).  Assume the common affine-plane and exact-cell
hypotheses of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`: the nonconstant affine
functions \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) have pairwise nonparallel linear parts,
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,
\]
and the nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes
\(A_0,A_2,K\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) satisfy
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
For \(J\subseteq N\), set
\[
P_J=A_0\cap
\{f_i\leq L_i\ (i\in J),\ f_i>L_i\ (i\notin J)\},
\]
\[
Q_J=A_2\cap
\{f_i\leq-L_i\ (i\in J),\ f_i>-L_i\ (i\notin J)\},
\]
\[
R_J=K\cap
\{f_i\leq0\ (i\in J),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin J)\},
\]
and write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,\qquad q_J=|Q_J|,\qquad r_J=|R_J|.
\]
Put
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad \kappa=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{J\ni i}q_J,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{J\ni i}r_J,
\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9.
\]
Assume the finite-interior conditions
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad 0<c_i<\kappa
\qquad(i\in N),
\]
the three crossings and the canonical tails
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h,\qquad
c_i+m\geq h,\qquad c_i+M\geq h
\qquad(i\in N),
\]
and
\[
W:=
\sum_{J\subseteq N}(3|J|-2)p_J+
\sum_{J\subseteq N}(3|J|-2)q_J+
\sum_{J\subseteq N}(3|J|-2)r_J=0.
\]
Assume that the positive \(Q\)-support is the relabeled single-double
branch from accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`,
\[
\{J:q_J>0\}=\{123,1,2,12\}.
\]
Write
\[
q=q_{123},\qquad a=q_1,\qquad b=q_2,\qquad
\gamma=q_{12},\qquad p=p_\varnothing,
\]
so that
\[
q,a,b,\gamma>0,\qquad m=q+a+b+\gamma,
\]
and put
\[
\Delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0.
\]
Then
\[
\frac pm>
\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Delta>2.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` gives
\[
P_{123}=\varnothing,\qquad Q_\varnothing=\varnothing,\qquad
R_\varnothing=\varnothing,
\tag{1}
\]
and every cutting line has planar area zero.

We first derive \(p>0\), before invoking any bridge theorem.  In the
functional \(W\), an empty-index cell has coefficient \(-2\), a
singleton has coefficient \(1\), a doubleton has coefficient \(4\),
and a triple has coefficient \(7\).  By (1), \(P_\varnothing\) is the
only cell that can have a negative contribution.  The prescribed
positive \(Q\)-support contributes
\[
a+b+4\gamma+7q>0.
\]
Thus \(W=0\) implies
\[
2p\geq a+b+4\gamma+7q>0,
\tag{2}
\]
and hence
\[
p>0.
\tag{3}
\]

We next audit the weighted crossing ledger.  Put
\[
D_i=u_i+v_i+c_i.
\]
The crossings give \(D_1=D_2=D_3=h\), and therefore
\[
2D_1-D_2-D_3=0.
\tag{4}
\]
For a cell indexed by \(J\), its coefficient in
\(W+2D_1-D_2-D_3\) is
\[
\omega(J)=
3|J|-2+2\mathbf 1_{\{1\in J\}}
-\mathbf 1_{\{2\in J\}}
-\mathbf 1_{\{3\in J\}}.
\tag{5}
\]
Every coefficient is displayed in the following table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
J&\varnothing&1&2&3&12&13&23&123\\ \hline
\omega(J)&-2&3&0&0&5&5&2&7.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]
The same row applies separately to the \(P,Q,R\) families.  Using
(1) and the exact \(Q\)-support, the identity
\(W+2D_1-D_2-D_3=0\) is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&-2p+3a+5\gamma+7q
{}+3r_1+5r_{12}+7r_{123}+E_1,\\
E_1={}&
3p_1+5p_{12}+5p_{13}+2p_{23}
+5r_{13}+2r_{23}\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, no omitted exact cell in (7) has a negative
coefficient.  The positive cell \(q_2=b\), as well as
\(p_2,p_3,r_2,r_3\), has coefficient zero in this ledger.

We record the two geometric lower bounds charged by (7).  The weak
closure of the midpoint inclusion
\[
\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_1}{2}\subseteq R_1
\]
has no area discrepancy: all changed boundaries lie in cutting lines.
More explicitly, if \(x\in\overline{P_\varnothing}\) and
\(y\in\overline{Q_1}\), then the two coordinates \(f_2,f_3\) of
\((x+y)/2\) are nonnegative, and their sum identity makes its
\(f_1\)-coordinate negative.  Compatibility puts the midpoint in
\(K\).  The weak target chamber differs from \(R_1\) only on the
lines \(f_2=0\) and \(f_3=0\).  Applying the accepted planar
Brunn--Minkowski theorem in fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` to the two
closures, whose areas are \(p\) and \(a\), gives
\[
r_1\geq
\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt a)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

To apply the quantitative bridge, define
\[
\mathcal T_P=\{x:f_i(x)>L_i\ (i=1,2,3)\},\qquad
\Theta=|\mathcal T_P|,\qquad
\alpha=\frac p\Theta.
\]
The affine sign map is an isomorphism by accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`.  Since \(\Delta>0\), \(\mathcal T_P\) is a
finite positive-area triangle.  Also
\(P_\varnothing\subseteq\mathcal T_P\), so (3) gives
\[
0<\alpha\leq1.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(y_1,y_2\) be the area centroids of \(Q_1,Q_2\), put
\(\eta_i=f_i+L_i\), and define
\[
A_*=\frac{\eta_1(y_2)-\eta_1(y_1)}{\Delta},\qquad
B_*=\frac{\eta_2(y_1)-\eta_2(y_2)}{\Delta},
\]
\[
\Lambda=\max\{A_*,B_*\},\qquad
\lambda=\min\{A_*,B_*\}.
\]
The hypotheses needed by accepted fact `f1fcc678305bbd1c` have now
all been derived: (3) gives \(p>0\), and the exact support gives
\(a=|Q_1|>0\) and \(b=|Q_2|>0\).  That fact gives
\[
A_*,B_*>1
\tag{10}
\]
and, for
\[
\Gamma_{12}=
\left|
\operatorname{conv}\left(
\frac{P_\varnothing+\overline{Q_1}}2
\cup
\frac{P_\varnothing+\overline{Q_2}}2
\right)\cap R_{12}
\right|,
\]
the strict estimate
\[
\Gamma_{12}>
\frac{p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})}.
\tag{11}
\]
Since the set measured by \(\Gamma_{12}\) is contained in \(R_{12}\),
\[
r_{12}\geq\Gamma_{12}>
\frac{p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})}.
\tag{12}
\]

Substituting (8) and (12) in the exact ledger (7), and using
\(E_1\geq0\), yields the strict inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
2p>{}&
3a+\frac34(\sqrt p+\sqrt a)^2
+5\gamma+7q+7r_{123}\\
&+
\frac{5p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The strict sign comes from (12).

Interchanging indices \(1\) and \(2\) in (4)-(8) gives the coefficient
row
\[
(-2,0,3,0,5,2,5,7)
\]
in the same index order as (6).  The \(Q\)-terms retained by this
second ledger are \(3b+5\gamma+7q\), the target terms are
\(3r_2+5r_{12}+7r_{123}\), and all omitted coefficients are
nonnegative.  The same bridge (12) is charged to \(r_{12}\), while
Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
r_2\geq\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt b)^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
2p>{}&
3b+\frac34(\sqrt p+\sqrt b)^2
+5\gamma+7q+7r_{123}\\
&+
\frac{5p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
This completes the exact-cell coefficient audit for both weighted
crossings.

We now solve the remaining area optimization.  This is only a
homogeneous division by the positive number \(m\), not a geometric
change of coordinates.  Set
\[
x=\frac pm,\qquad
\widehat a=\frac am,\qquad
\widehat b=\frac bm,\qquad
\widehat\gamma=\frac\gamma m,\qquad
\widehat q=\frac qm.
\]
Then all four hatted source masses are positive and
\[
\widehat a+\widehat b+\widehat\gamma+\widehat q=1.
\tag{15}
\]
Dropping the nonnegative \(r_{123}\) and bridge terms from
(13)-(14), dividing by \(m\), and expanding the squares gives
\[
2x>
\frac{3x}{4}+\frac{15\widehat a}{4}
+\frac32\sqrt{x\widehat a}
+5\widehat\gamma+7\widehat q,
\tag{16}
\]
\[
2x>
\frac{3x}{4}+\frac{15\widehat b}{4}
+\frac32\sqrt{x\widehat b}
+5\widehat\gamma+7\widehat q.
\tag{17}
\]

For a proof of the symmetric reduction, fix
\[
s=\widehat a+\widehat b\in(0,1)
\]
and define
\[
\phi_x(t)=\frac{15t}{4}+\frac32\sqrt{xt}
\qquad(t\geq0).
\]
Because \(x>0\), \(\phi_x\) is strictly increasing.  Therefore the
larger right-hand side in (16)-(17) is
\[
\frac{3x}{4}+5(1-s)+2\widehat q+
\phi_x\bigl(\max\{\widehat a,\widehat b\}\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]
For fixed \(s\),
\[
\max\{\widehat a,\widehat b\}\geq\frac s2,
\]
with equality if and only if
\(\widehat a=\widehat b=s/2\).  Thus symmetry is forced by the
minimax problem, rather than assumed.  Moreover
\[
5\widehat\gamma+7\widehat q
=5(1-s)+2\widehat q,
\]
so its infimum for fixed \(s\) is obtained in the closed relaxation
at \(\widehat q=0\), with
\(\widehat\gamma=1-s\).  In the actual positive support
\(\widehat q>0\).  Hence (18) is strictly larger than
\[
G_x(s)=
\frac{3x}{4}+5-\frac{25s}{8}
+\frac32\sqrt{\frac{xs}{2}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (16)-(18) imply
\[
2x>G_x(s).
\tag{20}
\]

For \(x>0\), \(G_x\) is continuous on \([0,1]\) and strictly concave
on \((0,1)\), since its only nonlinear term is a positive multiple
of \(\sqrt{s}\).  A concave function on an interval is bounded below
by the smaller endpoint value, so
\[
G_x(s)\geq\min\{G_x(0),G_x(1)\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Both endpoints are
\[
G_x(0)=\frac{3x}{4}+5,
\tag{22}
\]
\[
G_x(1)=
\frac{3x}{4}+\frac{15}{8}
+\frac32\sqrt{\frac x2}.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus (20)-(21) imply that at least one of
\(2x>G_x(0)\) and \(2x>G_x(1)\) holds.

At the first endpoint,
\[
2x>\frac{3x}{4}+5
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x>4.
\tag{24}
\]
At the second endpoint, put \(y=\sqrt{2x}>0\).  Then
\[
2x>
\frac{3x}{4}+\frac{15}{8}
+\frac32\sqrt{\frac x2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
5y^2-6y-15>0.
\tag{25}
\]
The roots of the polynomial in (25) are
\[
\frac{3-2\sqrt{21}}5<0,
\qquad
\frac{3+2\sqrt{21}}5>0.
\]
Since \(y>0\), (25) is equivalent to
\[
y>\frac{3+2\sqrt{21}}5,
\]
and therefore to
\[
x>
\frac12\left(\frac{3+2\sqrt{21}}5\right)^2
=\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}.
\tag{26}
\]
Also
\[
\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}<4
\]
because \(\sqrt{21}<5\).  Hence either endpoint alternative
(24)-(25) implies (26).

This also identifies every equality pattern of the closed
optimization.  For fixed \(s\), the minimizing split is
\(\widehat a=\widehat b=s/2\), and the minimizing allocation of the
remaining mass is \(\widehat q=0\),
\(\widehat\gamma=1-s\).  The endpoint \(s=0\) gives the threshold
\(x=4\).  The endpoint \(s=1\) gives the smaller threshold in (26)
and the limiting tuple
\[
(\widehat a,\widehat b,\widehat\gamma,\widehat q)
=\left(\frac12,\frac12,0,0\right).
\]
Neither endpoint belongs to the positive single-double domain.
Together with the strict inequalities (13)-(14), this verifies that
the conclusion is \(x>\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}\), not a weak
inequality.

It remains to extract the offset bound.  From (9)-(10),
\[
\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\geq1,
\qquad
1+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\leq2.
\tag{27}
\]
Keeping only the \(p\)-part of the Brunn--Minkowski term and the
quantitative bridge term in (13), and using (27), gives
\[
2p>
\frac{3p}{4}+\frac{5p}{2\Delta}.
\tag{28}
\]
Division by \(p>0\), already proved in (3), yields
\[
\frac54>\frac{5}{2\Delta}.
\]
Since \(\Delta>0\), this is equivalent to
\[
\Delta>2.
\tag{29}
\]
The endpoint \(\alpha=1\) causes no limiting issue: the denominator
in (11) is then \(1\).  For every \(0<\alpha\leq1\), (27) holds, and
the strict sign in (28) excludes \(\Delta=2\) as well as every
smaller positive value.  This proves both claims.
