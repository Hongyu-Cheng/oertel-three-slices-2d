## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, let
\[
A_1=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad
a_i=|A_i|\quad(i=0,2),
\]
and let
\[
H=\{(j,z):\ell(j,z)\le0\},
\qquad
\ell(j,z)=\langle \xi,z\rangle+\alpha j+\beta,
\]
where \(\xi\ne0\). For independent uniform points \(Z_i\in A_i\), define
\[
U_0=\ell(0,Z_0),\qquad U_2=\ell(2,Z_2).
\]
Let \(p_i\) be the probability density of \(U_i\), with support
\[
[\underline u_i,\overline u_i],
\]
and put
\[
\mu_i=\mathbb E U_i,\qquad
\delta_i=\mu_i-\underline u_i,
\]
\[
L_i=\frac{\overline u_i-\underline u_i}{\delta_i},
\qquad
s_i=-\frac{\underline u_i}{\delta_i},
\qquad
m_i=3-L_i
\quad(i=0,2),
\]
and
\[
\gamma=\frac{\delta_2}{\delta_0},
\qquad
c=s_0+\gamma s_2.
\]
Then
\[
\delta_i>0,\qquad \frac32\le L_i\le3,
\]
and
\[
h_i(x)=\delta_i p_i(\underline u_i+\delta_i x)
\]
is a nonnegative concave probability density on \([0,L_i]\) with mean \(1\). The pairs
\[
(s_i,L_i-s_i)
\]
are the normalized signed support clearances, since
\[
(\underline u_i,\overline u_i)
=
\bigl(-\delta_i s_i,\ \delta_i(L_i-s_i)\bigr).
\]

For \(L>0\) and \(0\le\tau\le L\), define
\[
h_{L,\tau}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2(L-x)}{L^2},&\tau=0,\ 0\le x\le L,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2x}{L^2},&\tau=L,\ 0\le x\le L,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2x}{L\tau},&0<\tau<L,\ 0\le x\le\tau,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2(L-x)}{L(L-\tau)},&0<\tau<L,\ \tau\le x\le L,\\[4pt]
0,&x\notin[0,L],
\end{cases}
\]
and its cumulative distribution
\[
P_{L,\tau}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t h_{L,\tau}(x)\,dx.
\]
Explicitly,
\[
P_{L,\tau}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\le0,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{t^2}{L\tau},&0<t\le\tau,\ \tau>0,\\[7pt]
1-\dfrac{(L-t)^2}{L(L-\tau)},&\tau\le t<L,\ \tau<L,\\[7pt]
1,&t\ge L.
\end{cases}
\]
The two interior expressions agree at \(t=\tau\); for \(\tau=0\) only the third interior branch is used, and for \(\tau=L\) only the second is used.

Accepted fact `c415ce7703455561` gives unique probability measures \(\nu_i\) on \([0,L_i]\) such that
\[
h_i(x)=\int h_{L_i,\tau}(x)\,\nu_i(d\tau),
\qquad
\int\tau\,\nu_i(d\tau)=m_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Define the tent-pair affine-slack kernel
\[
K(\tau,\sigma)
=
\int_0^{L_0}
h_{L_0,\tau}(x)\,
P_{L_2,\sigma}\!\left(\frac{c-x}{\gamma}\right)\,dx.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a continuous, explicitly piecewise-rational function of its parameters.

Let
\[
B_i=\{z\in A_i:\ell(i,z)\le0\},
\qquad
b_i=|B_i|,
\qquad
\rho_i=\frac{b_i}{a_i}.
\]
For the one-halfspace quantities from accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`, namely
\[
D=\{(z_0,z_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
\ell(0,z_0)+\ell(2,z_2)<0\},
\qquad d=|D|,
\]
and
\[
E=
\left|
\left((B_0\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times B_2)\right)\setminus D
\right|,
\]
one has the exact full-distribution formulas
\[
\frac d{a_0a_2}
=
\iint K(\tau,\sigma)\,\nu_0(d\tau)\nu_2(d\sigma),
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\rho_i
=
\int P_{L_i,\tau}(s_i)\,\nu_i(d\tau),
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\frac E{a_0a_2}
=
\rho_0+\rho_2-\rho_0\rho_2
-
\iint K(\tau,\sigma)\,\nu_0(d\tau)\nu_2(d\sigma).
\tag{5}
\]

For feasible data \(L_i,s_i,\rho_i\), let \(\mathcal V_i\) be the set of all probability measures
\[
\eta=\sum_{j=1}^k w_j\delta_{\tau_j},
\qquad 1\le k\le3,
\]
such that
\[
w_j\ge0,\qquad
0\le\tau_j\le L_i,\qquad
\sum_jw_j=1,
\]
\[
\sum_jw_j\tau_j=m_i,
\qquad
\sum_jw_jP_{L_i,\tau_j}(s_i)=\rho_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the exact sharp cap-conditioned extremal formula is
\[
\overline{\mathcal D}
=
\max_{\eta_0\in\mathcal V_0,\ \eta_2\in\mathcal V_2}
\iint K(\tau,\sigma)\,\eta_0(d\tau)\eta_2(d\sigma),
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\underline{\mathcal E}
=
\rho_0+\rho_2-\rho_0\rho_2-\overline{\mathcal D}.
\tag{8}
\]
Among all pairs of nonnegative concave projection densities having the fixed parameters
\[
L_0,L_2,s_0,s_2,\gamma,\rho_0,\rho_2,
\]
the infimum of \(E/(a_0a_2)\) equals \(\underline{\mathcal E}\), and it is attained by two densities that are each mixtures of at most three tents. Thus every such halfspace satisfies
\[
E\ge a_0a_2\,\underline{\mathcal E}.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently, suppose \(N\le3\) strict low-mass halfspaces cover the middle slice as in accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`. For every halfspace with nonzero planar normal, form \(\underline{\mathcal E}_r\) from (7)-(8). If a planar normal vanishes, its outer slacks \(X_r,Y_r\) are constant and set instead
\[
\underline{\mathcal E}_r
=
\mathbf 1_{\{X_r\le0\ \mathrm{or}\ Y_r\le0\}}
-
\mathbf 1_{\{X_r+Y_r<0\}},
\]
which is exactly \(E_r/(a_0a_2)\). Then
\[
\sum_{r=1}^N E_r
\ge
a_0a_2\sum_{r=1}^N\underline{\mathcal E}_r.
\tag{10}
\]
Writing
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
every such cover must therefore satisfy
\[
\sum_{r=1}^N
\left(
b_{r0}b_{r2}
+
a_0a_2\underline{\mathcal E}_r
\right)
<
\frac{M(2M-m-a_1)}3.
\tag{11}
\]
The reverse weak inequality rules out the cover.

## Proof

By the projection-density concavity in accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f`, the section-length density of each nonconstant projection is concave on its support. Dividing by \(a_i\) and applying the positive affine change of variable
\[
u=\underline u_i+\delta_i x
\]
shows that \(h_i\) is a nonnegative concave probability density on \([0,L_i]\). Since the centroid projection lies strictly between the two support values,
\[
\underline u_i<\mu_i<\overline u_i,
\]
so \(\delta_i>0\). Moreover,
\[
\int_0^{L_i}xh_i(x)\,dx
=
\frac{\mu_i-\underline u_i}{\delta_i}
=1.
\]
Accepted fact `c415ce7703455561` now proves \(3/2\le L_i\le3\), the unique representation (1), and
\[
\int\tau\,d\nu_i=3-L_i=m_i.
\]

Write
\[
U_0=\delta_0(x-s_0),
\qquad
U_2=\delta_2(y-s_2)
\]
in the two normalized coordinates. Therefore
\[
U_0+U_2<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x+\gamma y<c.
\tag{12}
\]
For a pair of tents with peaks \(\tau,\sigma\), conditioning on \(x\) gives
\[
\Pr(x+\gamma y<c)
=
\int_0^{L_0}
h_{L_0,\tau}(x)
P_{L_2,\sigma}\!\left(\frac{c-x}{\gamma}\right)\,dx
=
K(\tau,\sigma).
\]
The strict boundary in (12) has product measure zero. Applying Tonelli to the two tent mixtures proves (3).

Similarly,
\[
U_i\le0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_i\le s_i,
\]
so the normalized outer cap area is
\[
\rho_i
=
\int_0^{s_i}h_i(x)\,dx
=
\int P_{L_i,\tau}(s_i)\,\nu_i(d\tau),
\]
with the same formula valid when \(s_i\notin[0,L_i]\). This proves (4).

If \(x>s_0\) and \(y>s_2\), then
\[
x+\gamma y>s_0+\gamma s_2=c.
\]
Thus the event in (12) is contained, up to null boundaries, in
\[
\{x\le s_0\}\cup\{y\le s_2\}.
\]
Independence gives the latter event probability
\[
\rho_0+\rho_2-\rho_0\rho_2.
\]
Subtracting (3) proves (5), exactly matching the defect definition in accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`.

It remains to prove the sharp finite reduction. The tent densities are uniformly bounded by \(2/L\) and depend pointwise almost everywhere continuously on their peak. Dominated convergence therefore gives \(L^1\)-continuity in the peak. It follows from (2) that \(K\) is continuous on
\[
[0,L_0]\times[0,L_2].
\]

Let \(\mathcal P_i\) denote all probability measures on \([0,L_i]\) satisfying the two moment constraints in (6), without a support bound. Fix \(\eta_2\in\mathcal P_2\) and define
\[
\Phi_0(\tau)=\int K(\tau,\sigma)\,\eta_2(d\sigma).
\]
This is continuous. Consider the compact curve
\[
\tau\longmapsto
\left(
\tau,\,
P_{L_0,\tau}(s_0),\,
\Phi_0(\tau)
\right)
\subseteq\mathbb R^3.
\]
For any \(\eta_0\in\mathcal P_0\), the vector
\[
\left(
m_0,\,
\rho_0,\,
\int\Phi_0\,d\eta_0
\right)
\]
lies in the convex hull of this curve. The point with largest third coordinate above the fixed first two coordinates is a boundary point of that convex hull. It lies in a supporting face of affine dimension at most two, so it is a convex combination of at most three points of the curve. Hence the maximum over \(\mathcal P_0\) is attained by some \(\eta_0'\in\mathcal V_0\).

Starting with any pair in \(\mathcal P_0\times\mathcal P_2\), first make this replacement on the first coordinate without decreasing the double integral. With the resulting \(\eta_0'\) fixed, apply the same argument to the second coordinate and obtain \(\eta_2'\in\mathcal V_2\), again without decreasing the value. Thus
\[
\sup_{\eta_0\in\mathcal P_0,\ \eta_2\in\mathcal P_2}
\iint K\,d\eta_0d\eta_2
=
\sup_{\eta_0\in\mathcal V_0,\ \eta_2\in\mathcal V_2}
\iint K\,d\eta_0d\eta_2.
\]
Measures in \(\mathcal V_i\) are parameterized by three ordered peak locations and three simplex weights subject to closed constraints. This is a compact parameter set, so continuity of \(K\) turns the last supremum into the maximum in (7). Since the cap-union probability is fixed, (5) proves (8) and (9).

The bound is genuinely sharp within the polytopal minimal family. Given maximizing measures \(\eta_i\), let
\[
h_i(x)=\int h_{L_i,\tau}(x)\,\eta_i(d\tau).
\]
These are piecewise-linear concave densities. For arbitrary prescribed positive areas \(a_i\), define
\[
A_i^\star
=
\left\{
\left(
\delta_i(x-s_i),\,
\frac{a_i}{\delta_i}y
\right):
0\le x\le L_i,\quad
|y|\le\frac{h_i(x)}2
\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Each \(A_i^\star\) is a two-dimensional convex polygon of area \(a_i\). For the halfspace whose planar inequality is first coordinate \(\le0\), its normalized signed projection density is exactly \(h_i\), its signed support clearances and cap fraction are the prescribed ones, and its defect attains (8). Taking
\[
A_1^\star=\frac{A_0^\star+A_2^\star}{2}
\]
places the construction in the required minimal family. Hence (8) is an attained extremum, not a relaxation.

As a calibration, consider the two equal-cap examples in accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`. In both, the two normalized projection densities are
\[
h_0=h_2=\frac12\mathbf 1_{[0,2]},
\qquad
L_0=L_2=2,
\qquad
s_0=s_2=\frac25,
\qquad
\rho_0=\rho_2=\frac15.
\]
For the first example,
\[
\delta_0=\delta_2=\frac12,
\qquad
\gamma=1,
\qquad
c=\frac45.
\]
Thus the normalized pair is uniform on \([0,2]^2\), and
\[
\frac d{a_0a_2}
=
\frac14\left|\{(x,y)\in[0,2]^2:x+y<4/5\}\right|
=
\frac2{25}.
\]
For the second example,
\[
\delta_0=1,\qquad
\delta_2=\frac12,\qquad
\gamma=\frac12,\qquad
c=\frac35,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac {d'}{a_0a_2}
=
\frac14\left|\{(x,y)\in[0,2]^2:x+y/2<3/5\}\right|
=
\frac9{100}.
\]
The common cap-union probability is
\[
\frac15+\frac15-\frac1{25}=\frac9{25},
\]
so (5) gives
\[
E=\frac7{25},
\qquad
E'=\frac{27}{100}.
\]
Hence the kernel detects the relative signed support scale \(\gamma\) that zero-threshold cap areas omit.

For a cover, applying (9) to each nonconstant halfspace and the displayed exact indicator formula to each constant-slack halfspace proves (10). Accepted fact `a5309a9087686166` gives the strict necessary inequality
\[
\sum_{r=1}^N(b_{r0}b_{r2}+E_r)
<
\frac{M(2M-m-a_1)}3.
\]
Substitution of (10) proves (11).
