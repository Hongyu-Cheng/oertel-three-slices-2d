## Statement

Use the exact cells of accepted fact 0e51a47a9add571f. The following
two-dimensional rational polytopal model has the three-weight, rank-two,
finite-scale line type of accepted fact 27839a03c483c90b and the minimal
proper \(Q\)-support \(\{23\}\) from accepted fact ed22242313aecf82.

In coordinates \((x,y)\), set
\[
 f_1=-1-x-y,\qquad f_2=x,\qquad f_3=y,
 \qquad (L_1,L_2,L_3)=(-21,0,0).
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
 A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(20,0),(0,20)\},
\tag{2}
\]
and parameterize
\[
 A_2=
 \{(-11-s-t,-11-s+t):0\le s\le\tfrac1{10},\
                                  |t|\le11\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
 \mathcal T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1)\},
\qquad
 B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 K=\operatorname{conv}\left(
 B\cup\mathcal T\cup\{(\tfrac65,\tfrac65)\}\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\tag{6}
\]
the positive-area \(P\)-support is \(\{\varnothing\}\), the positive-area
\(Q\)-support is \(\{23\}\), and the centroids satisfy
\[
 g_0\in P_\varnothing,\qquad g_2\in Q_{23}.
\tag{7}
\]
All seven nonempty ambient middle sign cells have positive area in \(K\).
Moreover,
\[
 M=200,\qquad m=\frac{22}{5},\qquad k=\frac{989}{5},
\tag{8}
\]
so
\[
 M>3m,\qquad
 \max\{M,m,k\}<\frac{M+m+k}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]

This proper model is the Hausdorff limit of full all-double models. For
\(0<\varepsilon<2/25\), define
\[
 A_2^{(\varepsilon)}
 =
 \{(-11-s-t,-11-s+t):-\varepsilon\le s\le\tfrac1{10},\
                                      |t|\le11\},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
 K^{(\varepsilon)}
 =
 \operatorname{conv}\left(
 \frac{A_0+A_2^{(\varepsilon)}}2
 \cup\mathcal T\cup\{(\tfrac65,\tfrac65)\}\right).
\tag{11}
\]
For every such \(\varepsilon\), the positive-area \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
 \{123,13,12,23\},
\tag{12}
\]
with
\[
 x_1=\frac{22}{5},\qquad
 x_2=x_3=\varepsilon^2,\qquad
 q=44\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^2,
\tag{13}
\]
and \(x_1>5|A_2^{(\varepsilon)}|/9\). Also
\[
 A_2^{(\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow A_2,\qquad
 K^{(\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow K
\tag{14}
\]
in Hausdorff distance. For all sufficiently small positive
\(\varepsilon\), every middle sign cell still has positive area and the
strict no-half inequalities in (9) still hold.

Nevertheless, the transverse quantities of accepted fact
1ba5106642a3b8bd for the limiting dominant deep body satisfy
\[
 \theta_2=\theta_3=\frac{442}{841},
\qquad
 \theta_2^2+\theta_3^2
 =\frac{390728}{707281}<1.
\tag{15}
\]
The bridge quantity of accepted fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 satisfies
\[
 r_{123}=\frac12,\qquad
 \eta_1=\frac1{10},\qquad
 \lambda_1=\frac1{40},
\tag{16}
\]
whereas its endpoint slack is
\[
 \frac{5x_1}{36}
 +\frac{\sqrt{p_\varnothing x_1}}6
 =
 \frac{11}{18}+\frac{2\sqrt{55}}3
 >\frac1{40}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus neither branch of the proposed transverse-or-bridge disjunction
holds, and the geometry itself is compatible.

The first missing global constraint is visible exactly. In the limit
\[
 u_1=v_1=0,\qquad
 c_1=\left|K\cap\{f_1\le0\}\right|=\frac{357}{10},
\tag{18}
\]
but
\[
 h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9=\frac{4022}{45},
\qquad
 u_1+v_1+c_1-h=-\frac{4831}{90}.
\tag{19}
\]
Hence the model violates the first canonical crossing. No claim is made
that it is an actual residual counterexample.

## Proof

The functions in (1) are nonconstant, have pairwise nonparallel gradients,
and satisfy
\[
 f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad L_1+L_2+L_3=-21<-1.
\tag{20}
\]
To audit the finite-scale type directly, put
\[
 \lambda_i=-f_i,\qquad
 (s_1,s_2,s_3)=(\sqrt2,1,1),\qquad S=\sqrt2+2,
\]
\[
 \phi_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{s_i},\qquad
 \alpha_i=\frac{s_i}{S},\qquad
 \sigma=\frac1S.
\tag{21}
\]
Then all \(\alpha_i\) are positive, every \(\nabla\phi_i\) has unit norm,
their gradients span \(\mathbb R^2\), and
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i\phi_i
 =\frac{\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3}{S}
 =\sigma>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus this is exactly the rank-two finite-scale identity, not a parallel or
fan degeneration. The three \(Q\)-threshold lines are
\[
 x+y=-22,\qquad x=0,\qquad y=0,
\tag{23}
\]
so they are pairwise nonparallel and nonconcurrent.

The triangle (2) lies in the closure of \(P_\varnothing\), and its
boundary on the three threshold lines has area zero. Therefore
\[
 p_\varnothing=|A_0|=200,\qquad u_1=u_2=u_3=0.
\tag{24}
\]
For a point of (3),
\[
 f_1+L_1=2s,\qquad f_2=-11-s-t,\qquad f_3=-11-s+t.
\tag{25}
\]
Hence its interior lies in \(Q_{23}\), and all other limiting \(Q\)-cells
have zero area. The parameter map in (3) has absolute Jacobian \(2\), so
\[
 |A_2|=2\cdot\frac1{10}\cdot22=\frac{22}{5}.
\tag{26}
\]
At \(s=0\), \(A_2\) contains the full threshold edge
\[
 \{x+y=-22,\ x\le0,\ y\le0\},
\tag{27}
\]
including its two vertices \((0,-22)\) and \((-22,0)\). Thus the missing
cells have not been made null by separating \(A_2\) from their threshold
lines.

The centroid computations are
\[
 g_0=(\tfrac{20}{3},\tfrac{20}{3}),\qquad
 g_2=(-\tfrac{221}{20},-\tfrac{221}{20}).
\tag{28}
\]
They obey all three strict inequalities for \(P_\varnothing\), while
\[
 f_1(g_2)=\frac{211}{10}>21=-L_1,\qquad
 f_2(g_2)=f_3(g_2)=-\frac{221}{20}<0,
\tag{29}
\]
which proves (7).

For the approximation audit, on \(A_2^{(\varepsilon)}\) put
\[
 q_1=f_1+L_1=2s,\qquad
 q_2=f_2=-11-s-t,\qquad
 q_3=f_3=-11-s+t.
\tag{30}
\]
When \(s>0\), both \(q_2,q_3\) are negative, giving \(Q_{23}\). When
\(-\varepsilon<s<0\), the three \(t\)-intervals
\[
 [-11,-11-s),\qquad
 [-11-s,11+s],\qquad
 (11+s,11]
\tag{31}
\]
give \(Q_{13},Q_{123},Q_{12}\), respectively. Since
\(q_2+q_3=-22-2s<0\), no cell with both \(q_2,q_3>0\) occurs. Integrating
the constant Jacobian \(2\) over these regions gives exactly (13):
\[
 |Q_{13}|=|Q_{12}|
 =2\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}(-s)\,ds=\varepsilon^2,
\]
\[
 |Q_{123}|
 =2\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}(22+2s)\,ds
 =44\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^2.
\tag{32}
\]
Also
\[
 |A_2^{(\varepsilon)}|
 =\frac{22}{5}+44\varepsilon,
\]
and \(x_1>5|A_2^{(\varepsilon)}|/9\) is equivalent to
\(\varepsilon<2/25\). Equations (10)--(11) give (14) directly.

By definition, \(B\subseteq K\), proving midpoint inclusion. A direct
Minkowski-sum vertex enumeration gives the ordered extreme vertices of
\(B\):
\[
 (-\tfrac{221}{20},-\tfrac1{20}),\
 (-\tfrac1{20},-\tfrac{221}{20}),\
 (\tfrac{199}{20},-\tfrac{221}{20}),\
 (10,-11),\
 (-11,10),\
 (-\tfrac{221}{20},\tfrac{199}{20}).
\tag{33}
\]
After adjoining \(\mathcal T\) and \((6/5,6/5)\), the ordered extreme
vertices of \(K\) are
\[
 (-\tfrac{221}{20},-\tfrac1{20}),\
 (-\tfrac1{20},-\tfrac{221}{20}),\
 (\tfrac{199}{20},-\tfrac{221}{20}),\
 (10,-11),\
 (\tfrac65,\tfrac65),\
 (-11,10),\
 (-\tfrac{221}{20},\tfrac{199}{20}).
\tag{34}
\]
The shoelace formula applied to (34) gives
\[
 |K|=\frac{989}{5}.
\tag{35}
\]
Together with (24) and (26), this proves (8). Their sum is
\[
 M+m+k=\frac{2011}{5},
\tag{36}
\]
and comparison with \(2011/10\) proves both assertions in (9).
Hausdorff continuity of convex hull and planar area makes these strict
inequalities persist for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\).

The polygon \(K\) meets every middle chamber in positive area. The apex
\((6/5,6/5)\) lies strictly in \(R_1\); the strict-sign vertices of \(B\)
near \((199/20,-221/20)\) and
\((-221/20,199/20)\) give \(R_3\) and \(R_2\); an interior point of \(B\)
obtained from the two endpoint centroids lies strictly in \(R_{23}\).
The points
\[
 (1,-1),\qquad(-1,1)
\tag{37}
\]
lie in the interior of
\(\operatorname{conv}\{(10,-11),(6/5,6/5),(-11,10)\}\)
and lie strictly in \(R_{13},R_{12}\), respectively. Finally,
\(\operatorname{int}\mathcal T\subset R_{123}\). All these incidences are
strict, so they persist in \(K^{(\varepsilon)}\) for small
\(\varepsilon\). Since the entire ambient \(R_{123}\)-chamber is exactly
\(\mathcal T\), its inclusion in \(K\) also gives
\[
 r_{123}=|\mathcal T|=\frac12.
\tag{38}
\]

It remains to audit the two failed stability alternatives. Let
\[
 \psi=f_1+L_1.
\]
On \(A_2\), equation (25) gives \(\psi=2s\), so
\[
 \eta_1=\psi(g_2)=\frac1{10},\qquad
 C_1^\sharp
 =\{(-11-s-t,-11-s+t):
       \tfrac1{20}\le s\le\tfrac1{10},\ |t|\le11\}.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(i=2,3\), the support intervals from accepted fact
1ba5106642a3b8bd are therefore
\[
 a_i^-=0,\qquad a_i^+=20,
\]
\[
 b_i^-=\frac1{20},\qquad b_i^+=\frac{221}{10}.
\tag{40}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Omega_i
 =20+\left(\frac{221}{10}-\frac1{20}\right)
 =\frac{841}{20},
\]
\[
 \theta_i
 =\frac{b_i^+-a_i^-}{\Omega_i}
 =\frac{442}{841}.
\tag{41}
\]
Squaring proves (15). Equations (38)--(39) give
\[
 \lambda_1=\frac{r_{123}\eta_1}{2}=\frac1{40}.
\tag{42}
\]
Using \(p_\varnothing=200\) and \(x_1=22/5\) in the endpoint-slack formula
of accepted fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 gives exactly (17). This proves that
both proposed alternatives fail in a model with full threshold contact
and an explicit full-support approximation.

Finally, all vertices in (34) except \((6/5,6/5)\) lie on or below the
line \(x+y=-1\); the two vertices on that line are \((10,-11)\) and
\((-11,10)\). Hence
\[
 K\cap\{f_1\le0\}
 =
 \operatorname{conv}\{(10,-11),(\tfrac65,\tfrac65),(-11,10)\}.
\tag{43}
\]
The determinant formula gives its area as \(357/10\). Equations
(24)--(25) give \(u_1=v_1=0\) in the limit, while (36) gives
\(h=4022/45\). Their exact difference is (19). Thus this construction
pinpoints, rather than assumes away, the first additional global condition
that any successful closure of the proper supports must exploit.
