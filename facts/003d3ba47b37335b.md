## Statement

Work in centered coordinates \((x,y)\) in which the two crossing cap halfplanes are
\[
\mathcal C_2=\{(x,y):y\le x\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_3=\{(x,y):y\ge -x\},
\]
and the middle contact cap halfplane is
\[
\mathcal C_1=\{(x,y):x\le1\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be two-dimensional convex bodies, and put
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad
\tau=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
For \(i=2,3\), define
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\mathcal C_i|,
\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\mathcal C_i|,
\qquad
c_i=|K\cap\mathcal C_i|,
\]
and define
\[
c_1=|K\cap\mathcal C_1|.
\]
Fix \(\delta>0\) and define the canonical \(A_2\)-contact cap area in these centered coordinates by
\[
v_1=\left|A_2\cap\{(x,y):x\le2+\delta\}\right|.
\]
Assume
\[
A_0\subseteq\{(x,y):x\le-|y|\},
\qquad
A_2\subseteq\{(x,y):x\ge|y|\},
\]
and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(L,0),(B,H),(B,-H)\},
\qquad
L<0<1<B<H.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume the two exact crossing equalities
\[
c_2+u_2+v_2=\tau,
\qquad
c_3+u_3+v_3=\tau,
\]
the canonical contact lower bound
\[
c_1+m\ge\tau,
\]
and the smallest-contact condition
\[
c_1+v_1
\le
\min\{c_2+u_2+v_2,\ c_3+u_3+v_3\}.
\]
Then
\[
M>m+k.
\]
In particular, the lower chamber \(M<m+k\) is impossible.

## Proof

The two boundary lines of each diagonal cone have planar area zero.  The endpoint cone containments therefore give
\[
u_2=u_3=0,
\qquad
v_2=v_3=m.
\]
Reflection of \(K\) in the \(x\)-axis exchanges \(\mathcal C_2\) and \(\mathcal C_3\), so \(c_2=c_3\).  Define
\[
p=\frac{c_2}{k}=\frac{c_3}{k},
\qquad
q=\frac{c_1}{k},
\qquad
\mu=\frac Mk,
\qquad
\nu=\frac mk.
\]
The crossing equalities now read \(\tau=c_2+m\).  Since \(\tau=2(M+m+k)/9\),
\[
\frac{\tau}{k}=p+\nu,
\qquad
\mu=\frac{9p+7\nu-2}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
M<m+k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu<\nu+1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
9p+5\nu<4.
\tag{3}
\]

The contact lower bound and the crossing equality give
\[
c_1+m\ge\tau=c_2+m,
\]
hence \(q\ge p\).  The smallest-contact condition gives
\[
q+\frac{v_1}{k}\le p+\nu.
\]
Since \(v_1\ge0\),
\[
\nu\ge q-p.
\tag{4}
\]

It remains to use the exact geometry of (1).  Write
\[
W=B-L,\qquad
\rho=\frac{1-L}{W},\qquad
z=1-\rho=\frac{B-1}{W},
\qquad
a=\frac BW,\qquad
h=\frac HW.
\]
The inequalities in (1) imply
\[
0<z<1,\qquad
a=z+\frac1W>z,\qquad
h>0.
\]
The line \(x=1\) cuts from the vertex \((L,0)\) a triangle similar to \(K\), with linear ratio \((1-L)/(B-L)=\rho\).  Thus \(q=\rho^2\).

For the lower crossing cap, the line \(y=x\) cuts the vertical edge from \((B,-H)\) to \((B,H)\) in the ratio
\[
\frac{B+H}{2H}
\]
measured from \((B,-H)\), and it cuts the edge from \((B,-H)\) to \((L,0)\) in the ratio
\[
\frac{B+H}{B+H-L}
\]
measured from \((B,-H)\).  Hence its area ratio is the product of these two edge ratios.  Therefore
\[
q=\rho^2=(1-z)^2,
\qquad
p=\frac{(B+H)^2}{2H(B+H-L)}
=\frac{(a+h)^2}{2h(1+h)}.
\tag{5}
\]

For fixed \(z\in(0,1)\), direct differentiation gives
\[
\frac{(h+z)^2}{2h(1+h)}
\ge
\begin{cases}
2z(1-z),&0<z<1/2,\\[2mm]
1/2,&1/2\le z<1.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, if \(z<1/2\), the unique critical point is
\[
h=\frac{z}{1-2z},
\]
where the value is \(2z(1-z)\); if \(z\ge1/2\), the function decreases to \(1/2\) as \(h\to\infty\).  Since \(a>z\), equation (5) is strictly larger than the left side of (6).

If \(z\ge1/2\), then \(p>1/2\), whereas
\[
q=(1-z)^2\le\frac14,
\]
contradicting \(q\ge p\).  Hence \(z<1/2\).  Now (5), (6), and \(q\ge p\) give
\[
(1-z)^2=q\ge p>2z(1-z),
\]
so \(z<1/3\).  Consequently,
\[
4p+5q
>
8z(1-z)+5(1-z)^2
=5-2z-3z^2
>4.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (4) and (7) yields
\[
9p+5\nu
\ge
9p+5(q-p)
=4p+5q
>4.
\]
Using (2),
\[
\mu-(\nu+1)=\frac{9p+5\nu-4}{2}>0.
\]
Thus \(M>m+k\), as claimed.  In particular, (3) cannot hold.
