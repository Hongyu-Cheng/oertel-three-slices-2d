## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\). Let \(q_1,q_2,q_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on \(\mathbb R^2\), with pairwise nonparallel zero lines, such that
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-\sigma
\]
for some \(\sigma>0\). Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional convex polytope, and for \(J\subseteq N\) define
\[
Q_J=Q\cap\{q_i\le0\ (i\in J),\ q_i>0\ (i\notin J)\}.
\]

Fix \(j\in N\), and write \(N\setminus\{j\}=\{k,\ell\}\). If
\[
|Q_{\{j\}}|>0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|Q_{\{k,\ell\}}|>0,
\]
then
\[
|Q_{\{j,k\}}|+|Q_{\{j,\ell\}}|+|Q_N|>0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
|Q_{\{k\}}|+|Q_{\{\ell\}}|
+|Q_{\{j,k\}}|+|Q_{\{j,\ell\}}|+|Q_N|>0.
\]

More precisely, one may choose \(x\in\operatorname{int}Q\) and \(y\in\operatorname{int}Q\) with strict sign patterns
\[
q_j(x)<0,\quad q_k(x)>0,\quad q_\ell(x)>0,
\]
\[
q_j(y)>0,\quad q_k(y)<0,\quad q_\ell(y)<0.
\]
For \(z(t)=(1-t)x+ty\), let \(\tau_i\in(0,1)\) be the unique zero of \(q_i(z(t))\). Every chamber occurring on a nonempty open interval in one of the following exhaustive paths has positive area:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{order of crossing times}&\text{open-interval chamber path}\\ \hline
\tau_k<\tau_j<\tau_\ell
&\{j\}\to\{j,k\}\to\{k\}\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_\ell<\tau_j<\tau_k
&\{j\}\to\{j,\ell\}\to\{\ell\}\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_k<\tau_\ell<\tau_j
&\{j\}\to\{j,k\}\to N\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_\ell<\tau_k<\tau_j
&\{j\}\to\{j,\ell\}\to N\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_k<\tau_j=\tau_\ell
&\{j\}\to\{j,k\}\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_\ell<\tau_j=\tau_k
&\{j\}\to\{j,\ell\}\to\{k,\ell\}\\
\tau_k=\tau_\ell<\tau_j
&\{j\}\to N\to\{k,\ell\}.
\end{array}
\]

## Proof

Each zero set \(q_i^{-1}(0)\) is a line and hence has planar area zero. The boundary of the two-dimensional polytope \(Q\) also has planar area zero. Therefore the positive-area hypothesis on \(Q_{\{j\}}\) permits a choice
\[
x\in Q_{\{j\}}\setminus
\left(\partial Q\cup\bigcup_{i\in N}q_i^{-1}(0)\right),
\]
and the positive-area hypothesis on \(Q_{\{k,\ell\}}\) permits an analogous choice of \(y\). Thus \(x,y\in\operatorname{int}Q\), and their signs are all strict as stated.

Put \(z(t)=(1-t)x+ty\). For each \(i\), the function \(t\mapsto q_i(z(t))\) is affine. It has opposite strict signs at \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), so it has a unique zero \(\tau_i\in(0,1)\). Moreover, \(q_j(z(t))\) is negative before \(\tau_j\) and positive after it, whereas \(q_k(z(t))\) and \(q_\ell(z(t))\) are positive before their respective zeros and negative afterward.

We claim
\[
\tau_j>\min\{\tau_k,\tau_\ell\}.
\]
If \(\tau_j<\min\{\tau_k,\tau_\ell\}\), then immediately after \(\tau_j\) all three coordinates are positive, contradicting
\[
q_j(z(t))+q_k(z(t))+q_\ell(z(t))=-\sigma<0.
\]
If \(\tau_j=\tau_k<\tau_\ell\), then at their common zero
\[
q_\ell(z(\tau_j))=-\sigma<0,
\]
whereas \(\tau_j<\tau_\ell\) requires \(q_\ell(z(\tau_j))>0\), again a contradiction. The case \(\tau_j=\tau_\ell<\tau_k\) is symmetric, and equality of all three crossing times would give \(0=-\sigma\). This proves the claim.

The claim leaves exactly the seven orders displayed in the statement. Reading the signs between consecutive crossing times gives the corresponding chamber paths. For example, if
\(\tau_k<\tau_j<\tau_\ell\), the successive strict negative-coordinate sets are
\[
\{j\},\quad \{j,k\},\quad \{k\},\quad \{k,\ell\}.
\]
The other strict orders follow identically. If \(\tau_k=\tau_\ell<\tau_j\), both \(q_k\) and \(q_\ell\) become negative while \(q_j\) remains negative, so the segment traverses \(Q_N\) on the nonempty interval \((\tau_k,\tau_j)\).

It remains to justify that traversing a chamber on an open parameter interval forces positive area, rather than merely a segment inside that chamber. Let \(t_0\in(0,1)\) lie strictly between consecutive crossing times, and let \(G\) be the strict negative-coordinate set at \(z(t_0)\). Since \(x\in\operatorname{int}Q\), there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq Q\). Convexity gives
\[
B\bigl(z(t_0),(1-t_0)\varepsilon\bigr)
=(1-t_0)B(x,\varepsilon)+t_0y
\subseteq Q.
\]
All \(q_i(z(t_0))\) are nonzero. By continuity, a smaller open ball about \(z(t_0)\) has the same strict sign pattern. That ball is contained in \(Q_G\), so \(|Q_G|>0\). Hence every intermediate chamber listed in the table has positive area.

In the first, third, and fifth paths, \(Q_{\{j,k\}}\) has positive area. In the second, fourth, and sixth paths, \(Q_{\{j,\ell\}}\) has positive area. In the seventh path, \(Q_N\) has positive area. Thus
\[
|Q_{\{j,k\}}|+|Q_{\{j,\ell\}}|+|Q_N|>0,
\]
which implies the asserted positive total area in all five remaining non-forced cells.

Finally, all boundary contacts are harmless. A single-coordinate crossing is one point of the segment on a zero line. In the cases
\(\tau_k<\tau_j=\tau_\ell\) and
\(\tau_\ell<\tau_j=\tau_k\), the simultaneous contact satisfies two zero equalities and the remaining coordinate equals \(-\sigma\); under the \(\le0\) convention that isolated point belongs to \(Q_N\), but no positive area of \(Q_N\) is inferred from it. When \(\tau_k=\tau_\ell<\tau_j\), the same boundary point is followed by the nonempty open interval in \(Q_N\), which is why \(Q_N\) then has positive area. The zero lines have planar area zero, so changing which adjacent cell receives such boundary points does not affect any area conclusion.
