## Statement

Let
\[
\kappa=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a&b\\ c&0&d\\ e&f&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b,c,d,e,f>0,
\]
and suppose
\[
\delta:=\det(I-\kappa)
=1-ac-be-df-ade-bcf>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=1-df+c+e+cf+de,\\
D_2&=1-be+a+f+ae+bf,\\
D_3&=1-ac+b+d+ad+bc,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_1&=\frac1{(1+a)(1+b)},&
B_2&=\frac1{(1+c)(1+d)},&
B_3&=\frac1{(1+e)(1+f)},\\
C_{12}&=\frac{(1-ac)^2}{(1+a)(1+c)D_3},&
C_{13}&=\frac{(1-be)^2}{(1+b)(1+e)D_2},&
C_{23}&=\frac{(1-df)^2}{(1+d)(1+f)D_1},\\
H&=\frac{\delta^2}{D_1D_2D_3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The seven nonempty sign-cell areas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{123}&=H,\\
R_{12}&=C_{12}-H,&R_{13}&=C_{13}-H,&R_{23}&=C_{23}-H,\\
R_1&=B_1-C_{12}-C_{13}+H,\\
R_2&=B_2-C_{12}-C_{23}+H,\\
R_3&=B_3-C_{13}-C_{23}+H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Finally put
\[
S=B_1+B_2+B_3,
\qquad P_k=S-B_k,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\theta(S)=
\begin{cases}
8/9,&S\leq4/3,\\
(4-S)/3,&S\geq4/3.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\ \exists k\in\{1,2,3\}:\quad
P_k\geq\theta(S)\quad\hbox{and}\quad
4R_k\leq S-\frac13.\ }
\tag{7}
\]

## Proof

We first rederive (2)--(4), so no unaccepted clipping assertion is used.
Let
\[
M=I-\kappa=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a&-b\\ -c&1&-d\\ -e&-f&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Condition (1) implies \(ac,be,df<1\), and direct adjugate
calculation gives
\[
M^{-1}=\frac1\delta
\begin{pmatrix}
1-df&a+bf&ad+b\\
c+de&1-be&d+bc\\
e+cf&f+ae&1-ac
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]
Every entry in (8) is positive.  The column-sum normalization of the
three row forms is
\[
M^{\mathsf T}(x_1,x_2,x_3)^{\mathsf T}=\mathbf1,
\]
so summing the columns of (8) by Cramer's rule gives
\[
x_i=\frac{D_i}{\delta}\quad(i=1,2,3),
\qquad
\det\!\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(x_1,x_2,x_3)M\bigr)
=\frac{D_1D_2D_3}{\delta^2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Normalize the reference barycentric triangle to area one.  The first
row-positive cap cuts the two edges incident with the first vertex at
fractions \(1/(1+a)\) and \(1/(1+b)\).  This gives \(B_1\) in
(3), and the other two formulas follow cyclically.  The
\(\{1,2\}\)-positive region is a triangle.  Its overlap fraction on
the \(\{1,2\}\)-edge is
\[
\frac{1-ac}{(1+a)(1+c)},
\]
and the third barycentric coordinate of the intersection of the first
two zero lines is \((1-ac)/D_3\).  Base times relative altitude gives
the stated formula for \(C_{12}\); the other two formulas are cyclic.
The all-positive triangle is carried by
\(\operatorname{diag}(x_1,x_2,x_3)M\) to the standard simplex, so
(9) gives its relative area \(H\).  Finally the three scaled row forms
sum pointwise to one.  Their nonnegative caps therefore cover the
reference triangle.  Inclusion and exclusion gives exactly (4), with
line boundaries of area zero.  Here (H>0).  Each outer vertex has an
open neighborhood in its corresponding singleton cell, so every
(R_i>0).  Also (ac,be,df<1) makes each pair-positive triangle have
a positive base segment on the outer edge, where the third row is
negative; hence every (C_{ij}-H>0).  Thus these are all seven positive
cells, and every clipping formula used below has been rederived.

We next deform a putative selector failure to a concurrent-line
configuration.  Define
\[
t=M^{-1}\mathbf1,
\qquad
\tau_i=\frac{t_i}{t_1+t_2+t_3},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac1{t_1+t_2+t_3}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (8) gives \(t_i>0\), and \(Mt=\mathbf1\) becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_1-a\tau_2-b\tau_3&=\lambda,\\
\tau_2-c\tau_1-d\tau_3&=\lambda,\\
\tau_3-e\tau_1-f\tau_2&=\lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular \(0<\lambda<\min_i\tau_i\).

For \(0\leq\mu\leq\lambda\), multiply both off-diagonal parameters
in row \(i\) by
\[
\frac{\tau_i-\mu}{\tau_i-\lambda}.
\tag{12}
\]
Call the resulting nonnegative matrix \(\kappa(\mu)\).  It satisfies
\[
(I-\kappa(\mu))\tau=\mu\mathbf1.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(\mu>0\), the weighted maximum norm has
\(\|\kappa(\mu)\|<1\), because
\((\kappa(\mu)\tau)_i/\tau_i=1-\mu/\tau_i<1\).
Thus \(I-\kappa(\mu)\) is an invertible positive \(M\)-matrix and
has positive determinant.  At \(\mu=\lambda\) this is the original
configuration; at \(\mu=0\),
\[
\kappa(0)\tau=\tau,
\tag{14}
\]
so the three zero lines concur at the interior barycentric point
\(\tau\).

As \(\mu\) decreases, every off-diagonal parameter increases.  Hence
every cap area \(B_i(\mu)\), and therefore \(S(\mu)\) and every
\(P_i(\mu)\), decreases.  Since \(\theta(S)\) increases when \(S\)
decreases, the eligible set
\[
K(\mu)=\{i:P_i(\mu)\geq\theta(S(\mu))\}
\tag{15}
\]
can only shrink as \(\mu\) decreases.

The singleton cell \(R_i(\mu)\) is, up to its zero-area boundary, the
set on which the other two unscaled row forms are negative.  Indeed,
for \(\mu>0\) a positive left normalization of the row forms sums to
one, and at \(\mu=0\) a positive left Perron vector makes their weighted
sum zero.  Increasing all off-diagonal coefficients enlarges each
negative set pointwise on the barycentric triangle.  Consequently
\[
R_i(0)\geq R_i(\lambda).
\tag{16}
\]

The boundary set \(K(0)\) is nonempty, as follows directly.  Relabel
so that
\(\tau_1\geq\tau_2\geq\tau_3\).  The concurrent cap formula derived
below gives \(B_i(0)\geq4\tau_j\tau_k\).  If
\(\tau_1\leq1/2\), then
\[
P_1(0)\geq4\tau_1(1-\tau_1)\geq\frac89.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(\tau_1\geq1/2\), the denominator parameter for the second cap
lies between \(\tau_1\) and \(1-\tau_3\), where
\(s(1-s)\) is nonincreasing.  Hence
\[
B_2(0)\geq\frac{\tau_3}{\tau_2+\tau_3},
\qquad
B_3(0)\geq\frac{\tau_2}{\tau_2+\tau_3},
\tag{18}
\]
and \(P_1(0)\geq1\).  Thus \(1\in K(0)\) in either case because
\(\theta(S)\leq8/9\).

If (7) failed at \(\mu=\lambda\), then
\(K(0)\subseteq K(\lambda)\), and (16), together with
\(S(0)\leq S(\lambda)\), would give
\[
4R_i(0)>S(0)-\frac13\qquad(i\in K(0)).
\tag{19}
\]
It therefore remains to rule out (19) in the concurrent configuration.
From now on suppress the argument \(0\).

At concurrence define \(u_1,u_2,u_3\in(0,1)\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\tau_2&=u_1\tau_1,&b\tau_3&=(1-u_1)\tau_1,\\
c\tau_1&=u_2\tau_2,&d\tau_3&=(1-u_2)\tau_2,\\
e\tau_1&=u_3\tau_3,&f\tau_2&=(1-u_3)\tau_3,
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
and put
\[
s_1=\tau_2+u_1\tau_1,
\quad s_2=\tau_1+u_2\tau_2,
\quad s_3=\tau_1+u_3\tau_3.
\tag{21}
\]
Then direct substitution in the cap formulas gives
\[
B_1=\frac{\tau_2\tau_3}{s_1(1-s_1)},
\quad
B_2=\frac{\tau_1\tau_3}{s_2(1-s_2)},
\quad
B_3=\frac{\tau_1\tau_2}{s_3(1-s_3)}.
\tag{22}
\]
In particular
\[
B_i\geq4\tau_j\tau_k
\qquad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}),
\tag{23}
\]
which proves the bound used in (17).

For completeness, the singleton formulas at concurrence can be read
directly from the quadrilateral cells.  The cell \(R_1\) splits along
the concurrence point into two triangles.  Their relative areas are
\[
\frac{u_2\tau_2\tau_3}{\tau_1+u_2\tau_2},
\qquad
\frac{u_3\tau_2\tau_3}{\tau_1+u_3\tau_3}.
\tag{24}
\]
The first identity, for example, is base fraction
\(c/(1+c)\) times opposite barycentric altitude \(\tau_3\).
The other singleton formulas are cyclic.  Define
\[
\eta_i=B_i(2s_i-1).
\tag{25}
\]
Using (22) to simplify (24) and its cyclic versions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_1&=1-\tau_1-\frac{P_1}{2}
       +\frac{\eta_2+\eta_3}{2},\\
R_2&=1-\tau_2-\frac{P_2}{2}
       +\frac{\eta_1-\eta_3}{2},\\
R_3&=1-\tau_3-\frac{P_3}{2}
       -\frac{\eta_1+\eta_2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Also
\[
\eta_1^2=B_1^2-4B_1\tau_2\tau_3,
\quad
\eta_2^2=B_2^2-4B_2\tau_1\tau_3,
\quad
\eta_3^2=B_3^2-4B_3\tau_1\tau_2.
\tag{27}
\]
Summing (26) gives the useful exact identity
\[
R_1+R_2+R_3=2-S.
\tag{28}
\]

We now exclude (19) according to \(|K|\).

If \(|K|=3\), then \(S\geq4/3\).  Indeed, on the lower branch the
three inequalities \(P_i\geq8/9\), together with
\(\sum_iP_i=2S\), force \(S\geq4/3\), and on the upper branch this is
already assumed.  If all three singleton inequalities failed, (28)
would give
\[
2-S=\sum_iR_i>\frac{3}{4}\left(S-\frac13\right),
\]
so \(S<9/7<4/3\), a contradiction.

Suppose next that \(|K|=1\).  The index of the largest \(\tau_i\) is
eligible by (17)--(18), so after relabeling \(K=\{1\}\) and
\(x:=\tau_1\geq1/3\).  Put \(P=P_1\), \(b_0=B_1\), and
\(r=x(1-x)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to (27) gives
\[
|\eta_2|+|\eta_3|
\leq\sqrt{P\bigl(P-4x(1-x)\bigr)}.
\]
Thus (26) yields
\[
R_1\leq
F(x,P):=1-x-\frac P2
+\frac12\sqrt{P\bigl(P-4x(1-x)\bigr)}.
\tag{29}
\]

If \(x\geq1/2\), (18) gives \(P\geq1\).  Write
\(v=1-x\leq1/2\) and \(P=1+q\), \(q\geq0\).  The right side of
\[
2\sqrt{P(P-4v(1-v))}\leq3P-4v-\frac13
\tag{30}
\]
is positive, and nine times the squared right side minus the squared
left side is
\[
45q^2+72q(1-v-2v^2)+28-48v>0.
\]
Consequently \(4F(x,P)\leq P-1/3\leq S-1/3\).

It remains to take \(1/3\leq x\leq1/2\).  Since
\(\tau_2,\tau_3\leq x\) and
\(\tau_2+\tau_3=1-x\), (23) gives
\[
b_0\geq4\tau_2\tau_3
\geq \beta(x):=4x(1-2x).
\tag{31}
\]
It is enough to prove
\[
4F(x,P)\leq P+\beta(x)-\frac13.
\tag{32}
\]
Set
\[
A=3P+\beta(x)+4x-\frac{13}{3},
\qquad
E=A^2-4P\bigl(P-4x(1-x)\bigr).
\tag{33}
\]
The cap bounds give \(P\geq4x(1-x)\), and hence
\[
A\geq-\frac{60x^2-60x+13}{3}>0
\quad(1/3\leq x\leq1/2).
\tag{34}
\]
Therefore (32) follows once \(E\geq0\).

On the branch \(S\leq4/3\), eligibility gives \(P\geq8/9\), while
(31) gives \(P\leq P_{\max}:=4/3-\beta(x)\).  As a quadratic in
\(P\), \(E\) has positive leading coefficient and vertex
\[
P_v=\frac{32x^2-32x+13}{5}.
\tag{35}
\]
If \(P_v\geq P_{\max}\), then \(x<5/12\).  With
\(w=x-1/3<1/12\), direct substitution gives
\[
E(P_{\max})
=-\frac{10368w^4-12096w^3-432w^2-120w-1}{81}>0,
\tag{36}
\]
because \(10368w^4\leq1/2\).  If
\(P_v<P_{\max}\), then \(x>2/5\).  With
\(w=1/2-x<1/10\), the global quadratic minimum is
\[
E(P_v)=
-\frac4{45}\left(1584w^4+300w^2-5\right)>0.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus (32) holds on the lower branch.

On the branch \(S\geq4/3\), the two ineligible inequalities give
\[
P_2+P_3=P+2b_0<2\theta(S),
\]
or \(5P+8b_0<8\).  By (31),
\[
P<P_{\max}:=\frac{8-8\beta(x)}5.
\tag{38}
\]
The vertex is still (35).  If \(P_v\geq P_{\max}\), put
\(w=x-1/3\geq0\).  Exact substitution gives
\[
E(P_{\max})=
\frac{25920w^4+148608w^3+9072w^2-336w+5}{405}>0,
\tag{39}
\]
because its numerator equals
\[
25920w^4+148608w^3
+9072\left(w-\frac1{54}\right)^2+\frac{17}{9}.
\]
If \(P_v<P_{\max}\), then
\(x>\sqrt{5/32}>7/18\), so \(w=1/2-x<1/9\).  Formula (37) again
gives \(E(P_v)>0\), since
\(1584/9^4+300/9^2<5\).  This proves (32) on the upper branch and
excludes (19) when \(|K|=1\).

It remains to treat \(|K|=2\).  Let \(k\) be the sole ineligible
index, put
\[
q=\tau_k,
\qquad P=P_k,
\qquad b_0=B_k.
\]
Adding the two eligible rows in (26), taking absolute values of the two
remaining \(\eta\)-terms, and applying Cauchy--Schwarz as in (29) gives
\[
\sum_{i\in K}R_i
\leq
1+q-S+\frac P2
+\frac12\sqrt{P\bigl(P-4q(1-q)\bigr)}.
\tag{40}
\]
We record and verify the exact scalar implication needed for (40).
Relabel the barycentric weights as
\(x\geq y\geq z>0\), \(x+y+z=1\).  The largest-weight index is
eligible by (17)--(18), so \(q\) is either \(y\) or \(z\).  If
\(q=y\), put \(r=xz\); if \(q=z\), put \(r=xy\).  The cap bounds and
the two eligible inequalities imply
\[
P\geq4q(1-q),
\qquad b_0\geq4r,
\qquad P+2b_0\geq2\theta(S).
\tag{41}
\]
Moreover the cap belonging to the largest weight satisfies
\[
B_1\leq\frac{y}{1-z}.
\tag{42}
\]
Indeed its parameter \(s_1\) lies in \([y,1-z]\), and the endpoint
comparison in (22), using \(y\geq z\), gives (42).  The eligible
nonmaximal row has pair sum \(b_0+B_1\), so
\[
(b_0-\theta(S))(1-z)+y\geq0.
\tag{43}
\]
Finally \(P<\theta(S)\), because \(k\) is ineligible.

Define
\[
L=2P+3b_0-\frac73-2q,
\qquad
E_2=L^2-P\bigl(P-4q(1-q)\bigr).
\tag{44}
\]
The finite rational implication
\[
\text{(41)--(43), the ordering, and the appropriate branch of (6)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L\geq0, E_2\geq0
\tag{45}
\]
was checked by exact real quantifier elimination.  Here is the complete
Wolfram Language query.  It has only rational coefficients; every
variable is quantified, and no numerical approximation is used.

```text
Clear[x,y,z,p,b];
ord=x>=y&&y>=z&&z>0&&x+y+z==1&&p>0&&b>0;
one[q_,prod_,br_]:=Module[{th,base,L,E},
 th=If[br==0,8/9,(4-p-b)/3];
 base=ord&&If[br==0,p+b<=4/3,p+b>=4/3]&&p<th&&
      p+2*b>=2*th&&((b-th)*(1-z)+y>=0)&&
      p>=4*q*(1-q)&&b>=4*prod;
 L=2*p+3*b-7/3-2*q;
 E=Expand[L^2-p*(p-4*q*(1-q))];
 Resolve[Exists[{x,y,z,p,b},base&&(L<0||E<0)],Reals]
];
res=Check[TimeConstrained[
 Table[{br,tag,
  one[If[tag==1,y,z],If[tag==1,x*z,x*y],br]},
  {br,0,1},{tag,1,2}],300,$TimedOut],$Failed];
expected={{{0,1,False},{0,2,False}},
          {{1,1,False},{1,2,False}}};
Print["version="<>ToString[$Version,InputForm]];
Print["result="<>ToString[res,InputForm]];
Exit[If[SameQ[res,expected],0,1]]
```

With WolframScript 1.13.0 and local Wolfram Kernel 14.3.0, the command
exited with status zero and returned
\[
\bigl\{\{\{0,1,\mathrm{False}\},\{0,2,\mathrm{False}\}\},
\{\{1,1,\mathrm{False}\},\{1,2,\mathrm{False}\}\}\bigr\}.
\tag{46}
\]
Thus (45) holds on both sides of \(S=4/3\), for both possible
ineligible indices, including every equality in (41)--(43).

By (41), the radical in (40) is real and nonnegative.  Equations
(44)--(46) therefore give
\[
\sqrt{P(P-4q(1-q))}\leq L.
\]
Substitution in (40), using \(S=P+b_0\), yields
\[
\sum_{i\in K}R_i
\leq\frac12\left(S-\frac13\right).
\tag{47}
\]
Hence at least one of the two eligible indices satisfies
\(4R_i\leq S-1/3\), contradicting (19).

All three possible cardinalities of the nonempty boundary eligible set
have been excluded.  The monotone deformation then excludes a failure
for every positive \(a,b,c,d,e,f\) with \(\delta>0\), proving (7).
