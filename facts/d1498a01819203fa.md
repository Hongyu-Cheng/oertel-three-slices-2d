## Statement

In the locked problem, put
\[
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
N=a_1+m,\qquad T=N+M.
\]
Let \(X_0,X_2\) be independent and uniform on \(A_0,A_2\), let
\(\widehat\mu\) be the probability law of
\((X_0+X_2)/2\), and define the finite planar measure
\[
\nu(B)=|A_1\cap B|+m\widehat\mu(B).
\]
For \(z\in A_1\), let
\[
d_\nu(z)=\inf\{\nu(Q):Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2
\text{ is a closed halfplane containing }z\}.
\]
Then every tilted closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\) has slice measure
at least \(d_\nu(z)\).  There is a point \(z_*\in A_1\) such that
\[
d_\nu(z_*)\ge\frac N3.
\]
Consequently,
\[
2M\le a_1+m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_S((1,z_*))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{1}
\]
Equality \(2M=a_1+m\) is included.

Normalize the endpoint areas by
\[
x=\frac mM,\qquad y=\frac{a_1}{M}.
\]
After applying the accepted large-slice lemma, the hybrid implication
\((1)\), and the accepted exact compatibility condition on areas, the
area pairs not covered by either depth lemma are exactly
\[
\boxed{
0<x<1,\qquad
y\ge\frac{(1+\sqrt x)^2}{4},\qquad
y>1-x,\qquad
y<1+x,\qquad
y<2-x.}
\tag{2}
\]
Equivalently, including every weak and strict boundary,
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
0<x\le 9/25:
 &1-x<y<1+x,\\[1mm]
9/25<x\le1/2:
 &\dfrac{(1+\sqrt x)^2}{4}\le y<1+x,\\[3mm]
1/2<x<1:
 &\dfrac{(1+\sqrt x)^2}{4}\le y<2-x.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
This is exact only as an area-level residual region: every pair in
\((2)\) is realized by some compatible triple, but other geometric facts
may still cover parts of it.

The part of the hybrid regime not already covered by the large-slice lemma
is exactly
\[
\frac12<x\le1,\qquad 2-x\le y<1+x.
\tag{4}
\]
For the accepted sharpness family
\(A_0=A_1=\Delta,\ A_2=\varepsilon\Delta\), one has
\((x,y)=(\varepsilon^2,1)\).  Every \(0<\varepsilon<1\) lies in the
residual region \((2)\), while \(\varepsilon=1\) lies on the included
hybrid boundary \(x+y=2\).  Thus the hybrid lemma does not remove the
asymptotically sharp sequence \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

## Proof

For \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), write
\[
G_i^u(c)=
\left|A_i\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le c\}\right|.
\]
Fix \(z\in A_1\), put \(t=\langle u,z\rangle\), and fix
\(\lambda\in\mathbb R\).  The tilted halfspace through \((1,z)\) has
mass
\[
G_0^u(t+\lambda)+G_1^u(t)+G_2^u(t-\lambda)
\tag{5}
\]
by the exact normal form in accepted fact `9e512193da02119e`.

Set \(U=\langle u,X_0\rangle\) and
\(V=\langle u,X_2\rangle\).  With closed events,
\[
\{U+V\le2t\}
\subseteq
\{U\le t+\lambda\}\cup\{V\le t-\lambda\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, if both inequalities on the right fail, then
\(U>t+\lambda\) and \(V>t-\lambda\), whence \(U+V>2t\).
Thus no equality case is discarded.  The union bound and the uniform
marginals give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\mu(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le t\})
&\le
\frac{G_0^u(t+\lambda)}{a_0}
+\frac{G_2^u(t-\lambda)}{a_2},\\
m\widehat\mu(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le t\})
&\le
\frac m{a_0}G_0^u(t+\lambda)
+\frac m{a_2}G_2^u(t-\lambda)\\
&\le G_0^u(t+\lambda)+G_2^u(t-\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The last line is exactly where the unequal-area normalization is checked:
\(m/a_0\le1\) and \(m/a_2\le1\), with equality for the smaller endpoint
and possibly for both.

For
\[
Q_u(z)=\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le\langle u,z\rangle\},
\]
adding the middle term in \((7)\) yields, for every \(u,\lambda\),
\[
G_0^u(t+\lambda)+G_1^u(t)+G_2^u(t-\lambda)
\ge \nu(Q_u(z))\ge d_\nu(z).
\tag{8}
\]
Taking first the infimum in \(\lambda\) and then in \(u\) preserves the
direction of \((8)\).  Every nonhorizontal halfspace containing
\((1,z)\) can also have its boundary translated parallel toward
\((1,z)\); the resulting boundary-through-\((1,z)\) halfspace is a
subset of the original.  Hence \((8)\) lower-bounds all tilted
halfspaces, not just those initially written with boundary through the
point.

All boundary conventions are literal.  The events in \((6)\) are closed.
Moreover, \(\nu\) gives zero mass to every affine line.  The Lebesgue
part clearly does.  For the midpoint part, fix a line \(L\).  Conditional
on \(X_0=p\), the event \((X_0+X_2)/2\in L\) requires
\(X_2\in2L-p\), a planar-null affine line.  Fubini therefore gives
\(\widehat\mu(L)=0\).  None of the preceding inequalities needs this
null-boundary observation, but it confirms that there is no hidden atom
when closed caps are compared.

We next prove the required planar centerpoint and its location.  Accepted
fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore both summands of \(\nu\) are supported on the compact convex
set \(A_1\), and \(\nu(\mathbb R^2)=N\).

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the family of closed halfplanes \(H\) satisfying
\(\nu(H)>2N/3\).  Any three members have intersection of positive
\(\nu\)-measure, because
\[
\nu(H_1\cap H_2\cap H_3)
\ge N-\sum_{j=1}^3\nu(H_j^c)>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(\nu\) is supported on \(A_1\), the three compact convex sets
\(A_1\cap H_j\) intersect.

For completeness, the finite planar Helly step used here follows from
Radon's argument.  If a minimal finite family of convex sets had empty
intersection while every three intersected, it would have at least four
members.  For each of four members choose a point lying in all the other
members.  Four planar points have an affine dependence whose positive
and negative coefficients, after normalization, give a common point of
the convex hulls of two disjoint subfamilies.  For each original convex
set, at least one of these two subfamilies omits its exceptional point,
so the common point belongs to that convex set.  It also belongs to every
remaining set, which contains all four points.  This contradicts
minimality.  Hence every finite subfamily of
\(\{A_1\cap H:H\in\mathcal H\}\) intersects.  Compactness of \(A_1\)
then gives
\[
z_*\in A_1\cap\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal H}H.
\tag{11}
\]

If a closed halfplane
\(Q=\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le c\}\) containing \(z_*\) had
\(\nu(Q)<N/3\), its open complement would have mass \(>2N/3\).
The closed halfplanes
\[
H_n=\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\ge c+1/n\}
\]
increase to \(Q^c\).  Continuity from below would make some \(H_n\) a
member of \(\mathcal H\), while \(z_*\notin H_n\), contradicting
\((11)\).  Thus every closed halfplane containing \(z_*\) has mass at
least \(N/3\), proving
\[
d_\nu(z_*)\ge N/3.
\tag{12}
\]
This argument remains valid even for measures with boundary atoms, and
it also proves directly that the centerpoint lies in \(A_1\).

It remains to pass from tilted to full mixed-integer depth.  The two
slice-parallel closed halfspaces through \((1,z_*)\) are
\(x\le1\) and \(x\ge1\).  Because they are closed, both contain the
entire middle slice, and their masses are
\[
a_0+a_1,\qquad a_1+a_2.
\tag{13}
\]
Their minimum is \(a_1+m=N\).  A slice-parallel halfspace containing
\((1,z_*)\) but with a more distant boundary contains one of these two,
so its mass is no smaller.  Combining \((8)\), \((12)\), and \((13)\)
shows that every closed halfspace containing \((1,z_*)\) has mass at
least \(N/3\).  If \(N\ge2M\), then
\[
\frac N3-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{N-2M}{9}\ge0,
\tag{14}
\]
which proves \((1)\).  When \(N=2M\), equality holds in \((14)\);
there is no strictness gap.

We finally derive and audit the residual area band.  Since all areas are
positive and \(m\le M\), initially \(0<x\le1\) and \(y>0\).
Accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` gives the exact compatibility condition
\[
y\ge b(x):=\frac{(1+\sqrt x)^2}{4}.
\tag{15}
\]
The accepted large-slice lemma `2f9be517307232c7` covers equality as
well as strict inequality.  In normalized variables, a largest endpoint
has area at least \(T/2\) exactly when
\[
x+y\le1,
\tag{16}
\]
and the middle slice has area at least \(T/2\) exactly when
\[
y\ge1+x.
\tag{17}
\]
The smaller endpoint cannot be at least \(T/2\), since it would require
\(x\ge1+y\).  Therefore failure of every large-slice case is exactly
\[
x+y>1,\qquad y<1+x.
\tag{18}
\]
The hybrid condition is \(x+y\ge2\), including equality, so failure of
the hybrid case is exactly \(x+y<2\).  Combining this with \((15)\) and
\((18)\) gives \((2)\).  At \(x=1\), compatibility gives \(y\ge1\)
while hybrid failure gives \(y<1\), so the residual has \(x<1\).

To obtain \((3)\), observe that
\[
b(x)-(1-x)
=\frac{(5\sqrt x-3)(\sqrt x+1)}4,
\tag{19}
\]
so the active lower boundary changes at \(x=9/25\).  The two strict
upper boundaries \(1+x\) and \(2-x\) cross at \(x=1/2\).  At
\(x=9/25\), the compatibility and large-slice boundaries coincide, but
the large-slice equality is already covered, so the residual inequality
there is strict.  At \(x=1/2\), both upper boundaries coincide and are
strict.  This proves every endpoint convention in \((3)\).

Exactness of the area region follows from the sufficiency direction of
accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8`: every positive triple satisfying
\((15)\), after multiplying by any \(M>0\), is realized by compatible
two-dimensional convex-polytopal sections.  Thus no additional
area-only restriction follows from compatibility.

Inside the hybrid region, avoiding the already accepted middle
large-slice regime requires
\[
2-x\le y<1+x.
\]
This interval is nonempty exactly when \(x>1/2\), giving \((4)\).
Compatibility is automatic on this strip because
\[
2-x-b(x)=\frac{(1-\sqrt x)(5\sqrt x+7)}4\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]
All remaining hybrid points are already covered by the middle
large-slice case; the largest-endpoint large-slice case is disjoint from
the hybrid condition.

Finally, accepted fact `43e70f89f224c5d7` has normalized areas
\((x,y)=(\varepsilon^2,1)\).  Direct substitution gives all four strict
residual inequalities for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), while compatibility is
weakly satisfied.  At \(\varepsilon=1\), \(x+y=2\), so the weak hybrid
condition applies.  This proves the stated overlap and sharpness claims
without treating the consultation as evidence.
