## Statement

Let \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on
an affine plane such that
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) be compact two-dimensional convex polygons satisfying
\[
|A_0|=\frac14,\qquad |A_1|=1,\qquad
|A_2|=\frac{81}{100},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(s_1,s_2,s_3\in\mathbb R\) satisfy
\[
s_1+s_2+s_3>1.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\), define
\[
C_{0j}=A_0\cap\{\lambda_j\ge-s_j\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(I\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), define the exact lower-label cell
\[
P_I=\left\{z\in A_0:
\{j:z\in C_{0j}\}=I\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Assume
\[
|P_{\{1\}}|=\frac9{100},\qquad
|P_{\{2\}}|=\frac1{25},\qquad
|P_{\{1,3\}}|=\frac3{500},\qquad
|P_{\{2,3\}}|=\frac{57}{500},
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
|P_I|=0
\quad\text{for every }
I\notin\bigl\{\{1\},\{2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\bigr\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Define the upper caps
\[
C_{2j}=A_2\cap\{\lambda_j\ge s_j\}
\tag{9}
\]
and assume
\[
|C_{21}|=|C_{22}|=|C_{23}|=0.
\tag{10}
\]
Finally, define the middle caps and their areas by
\[
B_j=A_1\cap\{\lambda_j\ge0\},
\qquad b_j=|B_j|,
\tag{11}
\]
and assume the three strict row capacities
\[
b_1+\frac{12}{125}<\frac{103}{225},\qquad
b_2+\frac{77}{500}<\frac{103}{225},\qquad
b_3+\frac3{25}<\frac{103}{225}.
\tag{12}
\]

No tuple
\[
(A_0,A_1,A_2,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,s_1,s_2,s_3)
\]
satisfies (1)--(12). Consequently, for every tuple satisfying all the
displayed hypotheses, one has
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3 b_j-1\ge\frac1{300}.
\tag{13}
\]

## Proof

Assume, for contradiction, that a full tuple satisfying (1)--(12)
exists. Put
\[
h_j=\lambda_j+s_j
\qquad(j=1,2,3)
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
c=h_1+h_2+h_3
=1+s_1+s_2+s_3>2,
\tag{15}
\]
where (15) uses (1) and (4). By (5), membership in \(C_{0j}\) is
exactly the weak inequality \(h_j\ge0\).

Let \(U=\operatorname{int}A_0\). By the hypothesis preceding (2),
\(A_0\) is a two-dimensional convex polygon. Hence \(U\) is a nonempty
open convex set, and \(\partial A_0\) has planar area zero. By the
initial nonconstancy hypothesis and (14), each \(h_j\) is nonconstant,
so its zero set
\[
L_j=\{h_j=0\}
\tag{16}
\]
is a line of planar area zero.

The support condition (8) contains no label having both \(1\) and \(2\),
and no label having neither \(1\) nor \(2\). It follows that
\[
U\cap\{h_1>0,\ h_2>0\}=\varnothing,
\qquad
U\cap\{h_1<0,\ h_2<0\}=\varnothing.
\tag{17}
\]
To justify both equalities without any generic-position assumption,
suppose one displayed set were nonempty. It would be open. Away from
the null line \(L_3\), it is a finite union of exact cells \(P_I\)
whose labels are excluded by (8), so it has area zero by (8) and the
nullity of \(L_3\). A nonempty open subset of the affine plane has
positive area, a contradiction. Thus
\[
h_1(z)h_2(z)\le0
\qquad(z\in U).
\tag{18}
\]

The first two positive masses in (7), together with the nullity of
\(\partial A_0\cup L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3\), give points \(p,q\in U\)
outside all three cutting lines such that
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &h_1&h_2&h_3\\ \hline
p&>0&<0&<0\\
q&<0&>0&<0.
\end{array}
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, \(h_1\) takes both signs on the convex open set \(U\),
so \(L_1\cap U\) contains a nondegenerate line segment.

For every \(z\in L_1\cap U\), one has \(h_2(z)=0\). Indeed, if
\(h_2(z)>0\), continuity and the fact that the nonconstant affine
function \(h_1\) takes both signs in every neighborhood of a point of
\(L_1\) produce a nearby point with \(h_1>0,h_2>0\), contrary to
(17). If \(h_2(z)<0\), the same argument on the other side of \(L_1\)
produces \(h_1<0,h_2<0\), also contrary to (17).

Thus \(h_2\) vanishes on a nondegenerate segment of \(L_1\). Since
\(h_2\) is a nonconstant affine function, its zero line equals \(L_1\).
Two nonconstant affine functions with the same zero line are nonzero
scalar multiples. Their opposite signs at \(p\) show that the scalar is
negative, so for some \(\alpha>0\),
\[
h_2=-\alpha h_1.
\tag{20}
\]
This also handles a possible simultaneous crossing of the first two
cutting lines: the missing positive-area chambers force the two lines
to coincide, rather than permitting a jump through an isolated
intersection point.

Equations (15) and (20) give
\[
h_3=c-h_1-h_2
=c+(\alpha-1)h_1.
\tag{21}
\]
At \(p\), (19) gives \(h_1(p)>0\) and \(h_3(p)<0\). Since \(c>0\),
(21) forces
\[
\alpha-1<0.
\tag{22}
\]
At \(q\), (19) gives \(h_1(q)<0\) and \(h_3(q)<0\). The same equation
instead forces
\[
\alpha-1>0.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (22) and (23) contradict each other. Therefore no
\(A_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,s_1,s_2,s_3\) can satisfy the
lower-arrangement conditions (1), (4)--(8).

We now bridge this contradiction to every remaining hypothesis in the
statement. A full tuple satisfying (1)--(12) would in particular
contain the just-excluded lower tuple. The additional area requirements
in (2), the convex compatibility inclusion (3), the upper-cap
requirements (9)--(10), and the middle-cap definitions and strict row
capacities (11)--(12) only impose further conditions on that same
tuple; none can create an \(A_0\) satisfying the contradictory
lower-arrangement conditions. Hence the set of full realizations of
(1)--(12) is empty.

Statement (13) is a universal assertion over precisely that empty set
of full realizations. It therefore holds vacuously, proving the claimed
fixed-calibration conclusion.
