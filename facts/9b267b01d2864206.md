## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a two-dimensional compact convex body and let \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) be closed halfspaces.  For \(I\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), define the exact membership cell
\[
E_I
=
K\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}H_i
\cap\bigcap_{i\notin I}(\mathbb R^2\setminus H_i).
\]
For every \(s\) with \(0<s\le1/3\), there is a number \(\omega(s)>0\), depending only on \(s\), such that
\[
|E_{\{i\}}|\ge s|K|\quad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{1}
\]
implies
\[
\sum_{|I|\ne1}|E_I|\ge\omega(s)|K|.
\tag{2}
\]
The same \(\omega(s)\) applies when two or three boundary lines are parallel, approach coincidence, coincide with the same orientation, or coincide with opposite orientations.

Now let \(\mathcal F\) be the full \(W=0\) all-double common-plane class of accepted fact `af9178be3aa6202a`, and use its notation
\[
\eta=s_P+4d_P+3d_R+6r_{123}.
\]
Define
\[
L=\frac{20\sqrt{30}-27}{289},
\qquad
s_0=\frac{L}{12},
\qquad
\omega_0=\omega(s_0),
\tag{3}
\]
\[
c_{\mathrm{eff}}
=
\min\left\{
\frac{7L}{16},\,
\frac{21\omega_0}{8}
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(L,s_0,\omega_0,c_{\mathrm{eff}}>0\), and every realization in \(\mathcal F\) satisfies
\[
\frac{\eta}{q}\ge c_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, the constant \(c_*\) of accepted fact `af9178be3aa6202a` obeys
\[
c_*\ge c_{\mathrm{eff}}>0.
\tag{6}
\]

More explicitly, put
\[
\lambda_i=\frac{x_i}{q},\qquad
R=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3,\qquad
D=\frac{q}{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
Every realization in \(\mathcal F\) satisfies the effective strict scale bound
\[
D<
\widehat\tau_c(R)
:=
\frac{49}{
\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+
\sqrt{204+120R+28c_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\right)^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
At
\[
\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac5{21},
\]
one has \(R=5/7\) and
\[
D<\widehat\tau_c(5/7)<\frac7{48}.
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

We first construct the modulus.  All area ratios in (1)-(2) are invariant under invertible affine maps.  Choose a diameter segment of \(K\).  Send its endpoints to \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\), and scale the perpendicular coordinate so that the maximum absolute distance of the transformed body from the diameter line is \(1\).  Every point projects between the endpoints of a diameter, so the transformed body lies in
\[
R_0=[0,1]\times[-1,1].
\]
It contains a triangle with base \([(0,0),(1,0)]\) and height \(1\).  Hence its area lies in \([1/2,2]\).

Each boundary line \(\partial H_i\) crosses the interior of \(K\): the positive-area cell \(E_{\{i\}}\) lies on its \(H_i\)-side, while either other singleton cell lies on its complementary side.  Therefore every transformed boundary line meets \(R_0\).  Write an oriented transformed halfspace as
\[
\{x:n\cdot x\le a\},
\qquad |n|=1.
\]
The normal \(n\) lies on the compact unit circle, and the condition that the boundary meets \(R_0\) puts \(a\) in the fixed compact interval \([-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]\).

Let \(\mathcal C_s\) be the class of tuples consisting of a compact convex \(K\subseteq R_0\) with \(1/2\le|K|\le2\), together with three halfspaces in the preceding compact parametrization, satisfying (1).  This class is compact.  Indeed, support functions of convex subsets of \(R_0\) are uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz, so every sequence has a uniformly convergent subsequence whose limit is the support function of a compact convex set.  The area lower bound persists, so the limit is two-dimensional.  The three unit normals and offsets also have convergent subsequences.

Under these convergences, every exact-cell area converges.  Away from the boundary of the limiting convex body and the three limiting lines, membership in the body and in all halfspaces eventually stabilizes.  Those four boundaries have planar area zero, so dominated convergence inside the fixed rectangle proves the claim.  Thus \(\mathcal C_s\) is closed, and
\[
(K,H_1,H_2,H_3)
\longmapsto
\frac{\sum_{|I|\ne1}|E_I|}{|K|}
\]
is continuous on it.

If \(\mathcal C_s\) is empty, set \(\omega(s)=1\).  Otherwise, let \(\omega(s)\) be the minimum of the last continuous function on \(\mathcal C_s\).  This minimum is positive.  If it were zero, put \(U=\operatorname{int}K\) at a minimizing tuple.  Every component of
\[
U\setminus
\left(\partial H_1\cup\partial H_2\cup\partial H_3\right)
\]
would have membership count exactly one, because a component with another count is a nonempty open subset of the forbidden union.

Every line \(\partial H_i\) crosses \(U\).  If one of these lines were distinct from both other boundary lines, choose a point of its intersection with \(U\) away from its at most two intersections with the other lines.  In a sufficiently small disk around that point, crossing only \(\partial H_i\) changes the membership count by one.  The count therefore cannot equal one on both sides.  Hence each boundary line must coincide with another, which for three lines forces all three to coincide.  If \(j\) of the three halfspaces select one side of the common line, the membership counts on the two open sides are \(j\) and \(3-j\).  They cannot both equal one.  This contradiction proves \(\omega(s)>0\), including all parallel and coincident-line limits, and proves (2).

We next feed this modulus into the exact scalar normalization.  Scale a realization in \(\mathcal F\) so that \(q=1\), and abbreviate
\[
p=p_\varnothing,\qquad
r=r_{123},\qquad
e=\frac{\eta}{p},
\]
\[
z=\frac1p,\qquad
a=\frac mp,\qquad
t=\frac kp,\qquad
\xi_i=\frac{x_i}{p},\qquad
y_i=\frac{r_i}{p}.
\tag{9}
\]
The exact \(W=0\) identity in accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` becomes
\[
2=4a+3z+t+e.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(m>1\), this identity gives \(p>7/2\).

Put
\[
\sigma=\frac{s_P+d_P}{p}.
\]
The \(\mathrm{F0}\) inequality used in accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` gives
\[
\left(\sqrt{1+\sigma}+\sqrt a\right)^2\le4t,
\]
and hence
\[
(1+\sqrt a)^2\le4t.
\tag{11}
\]
Equation (10) also gives \(t\le2-4a\).  With \(u=\sqrt a\), (11) therefore implies
\[
17u^2+2u-7\le0,
\qquad
0\le u\le
u_0:=\frac{2\sqrt{30}-1}{17}.
\tag{12}
\]

Let
\[
C=z+t-a.
\]
Solving (10) for \(z\), then using (11), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
C
&=\frac{2-7a+2t-e}{3}\\
&\ge
\frac{5+2u-13u^2}{6}-\frac e3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The quadratic in (13) is concave in \(u\), so its minimum on \([0,u_0]\) is attained at an endpoint.  Its value at \(u=0\) is \(5/6\).  Using
\[
17u_0^2+2u_0-7=0
\]
at the other endpoint gives
\[
\frac{5+2u_0-13u_0^2}{6}
=
\frac{10u_0-1}{17}
=
\frac{20\sqrt{30}-27}{289}
=L.
\]
Thus
\[
C\ge L-\frac e3.
\tag{14}
\]

In the all-double support,
\[
v_i=m-x_i.
\]
Moreover
\[
0\le u_i\le s_P+d_P\le\eta,
\qquad
0\le c_i-r_i\le d_R+r\le\frac{\eta}{3}.
\tag{15}
\]
Divide the \(i\)-th crossing equality by \(p\).  Since
\[
\frac{h}{p}
=
\frac{2(1+\sigma+a+t)}9
\ge
\frac{2(1+a+t)}9,
\]
equations (15), \(\xi_i\ge0\), and (10) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_i
&\ge
\frac{2+2t-7a}{9}-\frac{4e}{3}\\
&=
\frac C3-\frac{11e}{9}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (14) and (16) yields
\[
y_i\ge\frac L3-\frac{4e}{3}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose first that
\[
\eta\le\frac{7L}{16}.
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(p>7/2\), one has \(e<2\eta/7\le L/8\).  Therefore (17) gives
\[
y_i\ge\frac L6.
\]
Also \(t\le2\), so
\[
\frac{r_i}{k}=\frac{y_i}{t}\ge\frac{L}{12}=s_0
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{19}
\]

Apply (2) to the middle body \(K\) and the three halfspaces \(f_i\le0\).  Under the canonical exact-cell convention used in accepted fact `444fb905d0cc5122`, the membership-count-zero cell is empty, the count-two cells have total area \(d_R\), and the count-three cell has area \(r\).  Equations (19) and (2) give
\[
d_R+r\ge\omega_0 k.
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently
\[
\eta
\ge3(d_R+r)
\ge3\omega_0k.
\tag{21}
\]
By (11), \(t\ge1/4\), while \(p>7/2\).  Hence \(k=pt>7/8\), and (21) gives
\[
\eta>\frac{21\omega_0}{8}.
\tag{22}
\]

If (18) fails, then \(\eta>7L/16\).  If it holds, (22) applies.  In both cases,
\[
\eta\ge
\min\left\{
\frac{7L}{16},\,
\frac{21\omega_0}{8}
\right\}
=c_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\]
Undoing the normalization \(q=1\) proves (5).  By the definition of \(c_*\) in accepted fact `af9178be3aa6202a`, equation (6) follows.

Finally, put
\[
\rho=\frac{r_{123}}q,\qquad
\mu=\frac{\eta}{q}.
\]
Apply the exact scalar bridge of accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` with \(|\mathcal T_Q|=q/D\), divide by \(q\), and use \(m/q=1+R\).  It gives
\[
\frac1D
\ge
\left(
2\sqrt\rho+
\frac{
\sqrt{1+R}+
\sqrt{204+120R+28\mu}
}{7}
\right)^2.
\]
Here \(\rho>0\) and \(\mu\ge c_{\mathrm{eff}}\), so (7) follows strictly.

At the critical ratio, \(R=5/7\), and
\[
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{12}{7}}+
\sqrt{\frac{2028}{7}}
\right)^2
=336,
\]
because \(\sqrt{12\cdot2028}=156\).  The positive term \(28c_{\mathrm{eff}}\) makes the denominator in (7) strictly larger, whereas \(49/336=7/48\).  This proves (8).
