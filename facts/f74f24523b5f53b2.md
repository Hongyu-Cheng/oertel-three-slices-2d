## Statement

Assume the strict \(N=3\) matrix system of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`, in the surviving orientation of accepted fact
`09393c6edf1fc4e2`.  Thus
\[
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|=\max\{A,B\},\qquad
q=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3>1.
\]
Put
\[
r=\frac AB,\qquad S=\frac1B,
\]
and define
\[
L_0(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le 9/25,\\[1mm]
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
U_0(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\[1mm]
2-r,&1/2<r,
\end{cases}
\qquad
S_c(r)=
\left(\frac{2\sqrt5+\sqrt{14r-2}}6\right)^2.
\]
Then
\[
1-\frac A2<B<1+A,\qquad 0<r<\frac12,
\tag{1}
\]
and, more precisely,
\[
\begin{cases}
0<r\le1/5,\qquad
L_0(r)<S<\min\{U_0(r),\,1+r/2\},\\[1mm]
1/5<r<1/2,\qquad
\max\{L_0(r),S_c(r)\}<S<
\min\{U_0(r),\,1+r/2\}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

There is also the following lossless parametrization of the endpoint
matrices.  Let
\[
\ell_i=\min_j U_{ij},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s=\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3,
\tag{3}
\]
and set
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{1-s},
\qquad
Y=\frac{V+\ell\mathbf1^\top}{1+s}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
-1<s<1,
\tag{5}
\]
\(X,Y\) are entrywise nonnegative, their columns sum to one, and
\[
U=\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1-s)X,\qquad
V=-\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1+s)Y,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\det U=(1-s)^2\det X,\qquad
\det V=(1+s)^2\det Y.
\tag{7}
\]
Conversely, if \(-1<s<1\), \(\mathbf1^\top\ell=s\), and \(X,Y\) are
entrywise nonnegative column-stochastic matrices, then (6) has column
sums one and satisfies all three compatibility inequalities
\[
\min_jU_{ij}+\min_jV_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
Formula (7) remains valid, so both determinant signs occur in this
parametrization.

Finally, fix any \(\delta>0\) and put
\[
R=\delta L,\qquad \tau=\delta\sigma.
\tag{9}
\]
Then the column sums of \(R\) equal \(\delta\),
\[
\det R\ne0,\qquad
\tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3>\delta,
\tag{10}
\]
and the three row masses are exactly
\[
\mathcal C(R_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((RU)_{i*};-\tau_i)
+B\mathcal C((RV)_{i*};\tau_i)
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{11}
\]
Conversely, any \(R,\tau,\delta\) satisfying the column-sum condition
and (10) gives \(L=R/\delta\), \(\sigma=\tau/\delta\), with
\(\det L\ne0\), \(q>1\), and the same row masses.  If the data are
rational and \(\delta\) is positive rational, all transformations above
preserve rationality.

## Proof

By accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`, a strict feasible matrix system
reconstructs a genuine counterexample.  Accepted fact
`09393c6edf1fc4e2` permits the displayed orientation with \(B\ge A\)
and \(q>1\).

Normalize the middle area to one.  Accepted fact `776f5d2aa96794fd`
says that \(1\ge B+A/2\) is sufficient for the desired depth
conclusion.  Its contrapositive for the reconstructed counterexample is
\[
1<B+\frac A2.
\tag{12}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
S<1+\frac r2.
\tag{13}
\]
The strict residual area-triangle inequality gives \(B<1+A\), proving
the two inequalities involving \(A,B\) in (1).

Accepted fact `6f36d5017fd780b5` says that every counterexample lies
in exactly one of
\[
0<r\le1/5,\qquad L_0(r)<S<U_0(r),
\tag{14}
\]
or
\[
1/5<r<r_0,\qquad S_c(r)<S<U_0(r),
\qquad
r_0=\frac{163-12\sqrt{51}}{125}.
\tag{15}
\]
Combining (13) with (14) already gives the first line of (2).
For the second branch, suppose \(r\ge1/2\).  Expanding \(S_c\) gives
\[
S_c(r)-\left(1+\frac r2\right)
=
\frac{2\sqrt{70r-10}-9-2r}{18}.
\tag{16}
\]
Both sides of
\[
2\sqrt{70r-10}\ge9+2r
\tag{17}
\]
are nonnegative on \(r\ge1/2\), and squaring is reversible there.
Moreover,
\[
4(70r-10)-(9+2r)^2
=-4\left(r-\frac12\right)
\left(r-\frac{121}{2}\right)\ge0
\tag{18}
\]
for \(1/2\le r<r_0<1\); here \(r_0<1\) follows directly from
\(12\sqrt{51}>38\).  Hence (16) is nonnegative, so (15) gives
\[
S>S_c(r)\ge1+\frac r2,
\]
contradicting (13).  Therefore \(r<1/2\).  Intersecting (13)-(15)
now gives the second line of (2).  Since \(r=A/B<1/2\), also \(B>2A\).

We next prove the endpoint parametrization using only the matrix
conditions in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.  Let
\[
n_i=\min_jV_{ij}.
\]
Compatibility gives
\[
\ell_i+n_i\ge0\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{19}
\]
Because each column of \(U\) sums to one,
\(\sum_i\ell_i\le1\).  Equality would force every entry in every
column to equal its row minimum, so all columns of \(U\) would be
identical, contrary to \(\det U\ne0\).  Thus \(s<1\).  The identical
argument gives \(\sum_i n_i<1\).  Summing (19) yields
\[
s\ge-\sum_i n_i>-1,
\]
which proves (5).

The numerator defining \(X\) is entrywise nonnegative.  Equation (19)
also gives \(V_{ij}+\ell_i\ge n_i+\ell_i\ge0\), so the numerator
defining \(Y\) is entrywise nonnegative.  Their column sums are
respectively \(1-s\) and \(1+s\), proving that \(X,Y\) are
column-stochastic and proving (6).

Since \(\mathbf1^\top X=\mathbf1^\top\),
\[
U=\bigl((1-s)I+\ell\mathbf1^\top\bigr)X.
\]
The matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det\bigl((1-s)I+\ell\mathbf1^\top\bigr)
=(1-s)^3\left(1+\frac{s}{1-s}\right)
=(1-s)^2.
\]
This proves the first identity in (7).  Similarly,
\[
V=\bigl((1+s)I-\ell\mathbf1^\top\bigr)Y
\]
and
\[
\det\bigl((1+s)I-\ell\mathbf1^\top\bigr)
=(1+s)^3\left(1-\frac{s}{1+s}\right)
=(1+s)^2,
\]
proving the second identity.  Conversely, (6) has column sums one,
and positivity of \(1-s,1+s\) gives
\[
\min_jU_{ij}\ge\ell_i,\qquad
\min_jV_{ij}\ge-\ell_i,
\]
which proves (8).  This also proves that the parametrization does not
impose a determinant-sign restriction.

For the last assertion, (9) gives column sums \(\delta\),
\(\det R=\delta^3\det L\ne0\), and
\[
\sum_i\tau_i=\delta q>\delta.
\]
For every \(c>0\), the exact cap function in accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` satisfies
\[
\mathcal C(cz;c\alpha)=\mathcal C(z;\alpha).
\tag{20}
\]
Apply (20) with \(c=\delta\) to each of the three terms in each row.
This proves (11).  Division by \(\delta\) proves the converse, and all
formulas use only rational arithmetic when the stated data are rational.
