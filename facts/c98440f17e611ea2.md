## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) and \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) satisfy the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses and the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. In particular, \(K\) is a nonempty two-dimensional convex polytope, the \(f_i\) are nonconstant affine functions,
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
and, for \(G\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
R_G=K\cap\{f_j\leq0\ (j\in G),\ f_j>0\ (j\notin G)\},
\qquad r_G=|R_G|.
\]
Write \(M=|A_0|\), \(m=|A_2|\), and \(k=|K|\). With the notation of accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`, let
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G.
\]
Assume \(M\geq m\), the canonical tails, crossings and \(W=0\), all-double positive \(Q\)-support, \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\), and the fixed normalization from accepted fact `d721506a274e22da`,
\[
q=q_{123}=1,\qquad x_i=\frac5{21},\qquad
m=\frac{12}{7},\qquad
v_i=m-x_i=\frac{31}{21}\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Suppose, finally, that the realization remains in the residual canonical counterexample branch. The outer-centroid alternative of accepted fact `b8e22f6a4b26ea94`, with its residual formulation in accepted fact `d721506a274e22da`, then gives
\[
M\leq m+k.
\]

For
\[
K_i=K\cap\{f_i\geq0\}\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
each \(K_i\) is a two-dimensional convex body,
\[
\operatorname{int}K_1\cap\operatorname{int}K_2\cap\operatorname{int}K_3
=\varnothing,
\qquad
|K_i|=k-c_i,
\]
and Scott's planar union theorem yields
\[
M+m-k\geq\frac{93}{14}.
\]
This contradicts the residual inequality \(M\leq m+k\). Therefore no realization satisfying the stated hypotheses remains in the residual canonical counterexample branch. The conclusion does not assert that a full \(W=0\) realization already closed by the outer-centroid witness is itself impossible.

## Proof

Fix \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), and choose \(j\neq i\). The hypothesis \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\) gives \(r_j>0\). Because \(i\notin\{j\}\), the exact-cell convention gives \(f_i>0\) throughout \(R_{\{j\}}\). Hence
\[
R_{\{j\}}\subseteq K_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
|K_i|\geq r_j>0.
\]
The set \(K_i\) is the intersection of the compact convex set \(K\) with a closed halfplane. It is therefore compact and convex, and its positive planar area implies nonempty interior. Thus \(K_i\) is a two-dimensional convex body.

In fact, the three \(K_i\) have no common point. If \(z\in K_1\cap K_2\cap K_3\), then \(f_i(z)\geq0\) for all \(i\), contradicting
\[
f_1(z)+f_2(z)+f_3(z)=-1.
\]
Their interiors consequently have no common point.

For the area identity, the exact cells with \(i\in G\) partition \(K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\), so
\[
\left|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|
=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G=c_i.
\]
The two sets \(K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\) and \(K_i=K\cap\{f_i\geq0\}\) cover \(K\), and their intersection lies in the cutting line \(\{f_i=0\}\). Since \(f_i\) is nonconstant, this line has planar area zero. Therefore
\[
|K_i|=k-c_i.
\]
This also audits the apparent reversal between the weak inequality in \(K_i\) and the weak inequality assigned to indexed exact cells: their only overlap is the null cutting line.

All cell areas are nonnegative, so \(u_i\geq0\). The crossing equality and the fixed value of \(v_i\) give
\[
c_i=h-u_i-v_i\leq h-\frac{31}{21}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(i\),
\[
|K_i|=k-c_i\geq k-h+\frac{31}{21}.
\]

Scott's theorem applies to \(K_1,K_2,K_3\), and their union is contained in \(K\). Hence
\[
k\geq|K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3|
\geq\frac95\min_i|K_i|
\geq\frac95\left(k-h+\frac{31}{21}\right).
\]
Multiplying by \(5\) and rearranging gives
\[
9h\geq4k+\frac{93}{7}.
\]
Since \(9h=2(M+m+k)\), this is equivalent to
\[
M+m-k\geq\frac{93}{14}.
\]
On the residual branch,
\[
M+m-k\leq2m=\frac{24}{7}=\frac{48}{14},
\]
which is impossible because \(93/14>48/14\). Thus the residual canonical counterexample branch is empty. If \(M>m+k\), the residual implication is unavailable because the accepted outer-centroid witness has already closed that realization; no nonexistence claim for such a full realization is made here.

## External sources

P. R. Scott, "On the union of convex bodies with no interior point in common", Mathematika 37 (1990), 245-250, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300012961 . The theorem used states that if \(K_1,\ldots,K_{d+1}\) are \(d\)-dimensional convex bodies whose interiors have no common point, then
\[
\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{d+1}K_i\right|
\geq
\frac{(d+1)^d}{(d+1)^d-d^d}\min_i|K_i|.
\]
For \(d=2\), the coefficient is \(9/(9-4)=9/5\). The proof above verifies that the three \(K_i\) are two-dimensional planar convex bodies and that their interiors have empty common intersection, so every hypothesis of Scott's theorem is satisfied.
