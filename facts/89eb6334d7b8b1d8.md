## Statement

Use the exact objects of accepted fact 9eb5170d42a00426, explicitly
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
and the quadrilateral \(Q\) with cyclic vertices
\[
q_0=\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
q_1=\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
q_2=\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
q_3=\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
Let \(e_1=(1,0)\). For every real \(r\geq0\), set
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=\Delta-re_1,\qquad A_2=Q.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\leq r\leq\frac{159}{800}.
\]
For every compatible \(r\), put
\[
S_r=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),\qquad
T=\frac{16027}{16000},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000},
\]
and let \(m_r=g-re_1\), the centroid of \(A_1\). Then
\[
h_{S_r}((1,m_r))
\geq \frac29+\frac{27}{16000}
=t+\frac{21}{16000}>t.
\]
Consequently, for every compatible \(r\), no collection of closed halfspaces of \(S_r\)-mass strictly below \(t\), even of arbitrary cardinality, covers \(\{1\}\times A_1\) by its interiors. In particular, the middle-slice condition of accepted fact 8f5f00ddd4f22d2f fails uniformly on the entire compatible translated-middle family.

## Proof

The translated triangle has the facet description
\[
\Delta-re_1=\{(x,y):x\geq-r,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1-r\}.
\]
The extrema of the three corresponding affine functions on a Minkowski average are the averages of their extrema on the two summands. From the displayed vertices of \(Q\),
\[
\min_Qx=\min_Qy=\frac1{20},
\qquad
\max_Q(x+y)=q_{1,x}+q_{1,y}=\frac{241}{400}.
\]
Hence every point of \((\Delta+Q)/2\) satisfies
\[
x\geq\frac1{40}\geq-r,
\qquad y\geq\frac1{40}>0,
\qquad x+y\leq\frac{1+241/400}{2}=\frac{641}{800}.
\]
Thus \(0\leq r\leq159/800\) implies compatibility. Conversely, the point
\[
\frac{(1,0)+q_1}{2}\in\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}
\]
has coordinate sum \(641/800\), so it does not belong to \(\Delta-re_1\) when \(r>159/800\). This proves the exact interval.

Accepted fact 9eb5170d42a00426 gives \(|Q|=27/16000\); translation preserves \(|\Delta|=1/2\). Therefore
\[
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=1+\frac{27}{16000}=\frac{16027}{16000},
\]
and the asserted value of \(t\) follows.

Fix a compatible \(r\), and consider a closed halfspace \(H\) containing \((1,m_r)\). The whole space, if allowed as a halfspace, has mass \(T\) and satisfies the claimed bound. Otherwise \(H\) is proper. If its boundary does not contain \((1,m_r)\), translate the boundary parallel toward that point until it does. The resulting closed halfspace is contained in \(H\), so it is enough to prove the lower bound for proper boundary-through-\((1,m_r)\) halfspaces.

First suppose the planar part of the normal is nonzero. There are \(u=(u_1,u_2)\neq0\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R\) such that, on the three supporting planes, the halfspace is described by
\[
H=\{(h,z):L(z+re_1)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\},
\qquad
L(w)=u\cdot(w-g).
\]
At height \(1\), writing \(w=z+re_1\in\Delta\), its contribution is
\[
|\Delta\cap\{L\geq0\}|.
\]
The centroid-cap estimate proved in accepted fact a58dc34569c34d62 gives
\[
|\Delta\cap\{L\geq0\}|\geq\frac29.
\]

If the height-\(2\) section contains all of \(Q\), then
\[
\mathcal H_2(S_r\cap H)
\geq\frac29+|Q|
=\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}.
\]

Suppose instead that the height-\(2\) section does not contain all of \(Q\). Choose \(q\in Q\) outside that closed section. Then
\[
L(q+re_1)+\beta<0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\beta-ru_1<-L(q+2re_1).
\tag{1}
\]
For every compatible \(r\), one has the exact containment
\[
Q+2re_1\subseteq\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, all points \(q\in Q\) have nonnegative coordinates, and
\[
(q_x+2r)+q_y
\leq\frac{241}{400}+2\frac{159}{800}=1.
\]
Let
\[
-A=\min_{w\in\Delta}L(w),
\]
so \(A>0\). By (2), \(L(q+2re_1)\geq-A\), and (1) yields
\[
\beta-ru_1<A.
\tag{3}
\]
At height \(0\), the section of \(H\) on \(A_0=\Delta\) is
\[
\Delta\cap\{L\geq\beta-ru_1\}.
\]
By (3), this section contains \(\Delta\cap\{L\geq A\}\). The exact two-cap estimate proved in accepted fact a58dc34569c34d62 now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_2(S_r\cap H)
&\geq
|\Delta\cap\{L\geq0\}|
+|\Delta\cap\{L\geq A\}|\\
&\geq\frac6{25}+\frac1{11700}
=\frac{2809}{11700}\\
&=\left(\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}\right)
+\frac{30281}{1872000}
>\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If the planar part of the normal vanishes, a proper boundary-through-height-\(1\) halfspace contains either the height-\(1\) and height-\(2\) slices, of mass \(1/2+27/16000\), or the height-\(0\) and height-\(1\) slices, of mass \(1\). Each is larger than \(2/9+27/16000\). Thus every closed halfspace containing \((1,m_r)\) has mass at least
\[
\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}
=\frac{16027}{72000}+\frac{21}{16000}
=t+\frac{21}{16000}.
\]
Taking the infimum proves the depth bound.

Finally, \(m_r\) is in the interior of the translated triangle \(A_1\). Any interior cover of \(\{1\}\times A_1\) contains \((1,m_r)\) in the interior of one of its halfspaces, but every closed halfspace containing this point has mass strictly greater than \(t\). Hence no strict-low interior cover of the middle slice exists. Accepted fact 8f5f00ddd4f22d2f then excludes the direct three-slice strict-low cover certificate throughout the compatible family.
