## Statement

Let
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\]
be the three integer-height sections of a compact polytope, where the \(A_i\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes. Write
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9},
\]
and suppose
\[
h_S(y)<t\qquad\text{for every }y\in S.
\tag{1}
\]

After possibly applying the height reflection
\[
(h,w)\longmapsto(2-h,w)
\tag{2}
\]
to the entire configuration, there are exactly three proper closed halfspaces
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad i=1,2,3,
\tag{3}
\]
with all of the following properties:

\[
\mu(H_i)<t\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\{1\}\times A_1
\subseteq
\bigcup_{i=1}^3\operatorname{int}H_i,
\tag{5}
\]
and no proper subfamily of the three interiors covers the middle slice. The affine functions \(f_i\) are nonconstant, their linear parts are pairwise nonparallel, and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{6}
\]
The complementary planar halfplanes
\[
P_i=\{w:f_i(w)\ge0\}
\]
satisfy
\[
P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3=\varnothing.
\tag{7}
\]
Finally,
\[
s_1+s_2+s_3<-1,
\tag{8}
\]
and, in the orientation selected in (2),
\[
|A_0|>|A_2|.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus one may write
\[
M=|A_0|>|A_2|=m.
\]

## Proof

By accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f`, hypothesis (1), applied only to the middle slice, gives proper closed halfspaces
\[
G_r=\{\ell_r\le0\},
\qquad 1\le r\le q\le3,
\]
such that
\[
\mu(G_r)<t
\]
and their interiors cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\). Choose an inclusion-minimal such subfamily. We first prove that \(q=3\).

Put
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
k=a_1.
\]
Temporarily interchange the two endpoint heights if needed so that
\[
|A_0|=M,\qquad |A_2|=m.
\]
For the \(r\)-th halfspace, use the notation
\[
B_{rj}=\{w\in A_j:(j,w)\in G_r\},
\qquad b_{rj}=|B_{rj}|.
\]
Accepted fact `a5309a9087686166` gives nonnegative quantities \(d_r\) satisfying
\[
Mm\le\sum_{r=1}^q d_r
\]
and
\[
d_r
\le
mb_{r0}+Mb_{r2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
Mm
\le
\sum_{r=1}^q\bigl(mb_{r0}+Mb_{r2}\bigr)
\le
M\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2}),
\]
so
\[
m\le\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2}).
\tag{10}
\]
The middle interiors cover \(A_1\), and each open middle section is contained in \(B_{r1}\). Hence
\[
k\le\sum_{r=1}^q b_{r1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Adding (10) and (11) gives
\[
m+k
\le
\sum_{r=1}^q\mu(G_r)
<
qt.
\tag{12}
\]

Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` implies from (1) that every slice area is strictly below \(T/2\). In particular,
\[
M<\frac T2,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
M<m+k
\]
and
\[
T=M+m+k<2(m+k).
\tag{13}
\]
If \(q\le2\), then (12)-(13) yield
\[
m+k<2t=\frac{4T}{9}<\frac89(m+k),
\]
a contradiction. Thus
\[
q=3.
\tag{14}
\]
The same argument shows that no strict-low-mass cover of the middle slice by one or two proper halfspaces exists, not merely that the chosen cover has three members.

Return to the three halfspaces in (14), and identify the middle plane with \(\mathbb R^2\). Put
\[
Q_i=\{w:\ell_i(1,w)\ge0\}.
\]
The interior cover is equivalent to
\[
A_1\cap Q_1\cap Q_2\cap Q_3=\varnothing.
\tag{15}
\]
Suppose \(Q_1\cap Q_2\cap Q_3\ne\varnothing\). Apply the planar Helly argument proved in accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` to the four convex sets
\[
A_1,Q_1,Q_2,Q_3.
\]
By (15), some subfamily of at most three has empty intersection. The subfamily cannot consist only of \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\), because their intersection was assumed nonempty. It therefore consists of \(A_1\) and at most two of the \(Q_i\). The corresponding one or two original halfspace interiors cover \(A_1\), contradicting the conclusion after (14). Hence
\[
Q_1\cap Q_2\cap Q_3=\varnothing.
\tag{16}
\]
Equivalently, the three open middle sections cover all of \(\mathbb R^2\).

Write
\[
\ell_i(h,w)=g_i(w)+\alpha_i(h-1),
\qquad
g_i(w)=n_i\cdot w+b_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Every \(g_i\) is nonconstant. Indeed, if \(g_i<0\) is constant, the single middle section of \(G_i\) is the whole plane, contradicting (14). If \(g_i\ge0\) is constant, the middle interior of \(G_i\) is empty and the member is redundant, contradicting inclusion-minimality. Thus
\[
n_i\ne0\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover, every pair of the closed halfplanes
\[
Q_i=\{w:n_i\cdot w+b_i\ge0\}
\]
has nonempty intersection. Otherwise the complementary two open halfplanes would cover the whole plane and hence \(A_1\), again contradicting (14).

We now prove and apply the needed affine Farkas alternative. Let \(N\) be the \(3\times2\) matrix whose \(i\)-th row is \(n_i^\mathsf T\), and let \(b=(b_1,b_2,b_3)\). The system
\[
Nw\ge-b
\tag{19}
\]
is infeasible by (16). Consider the polyhedral cone
\[
\mathcal C=\{Nw-z:w\in\mathbb R^2,\ z\in\mathbb R_+^3\}.
\]
The system (19) is feasible exactly when \(-b\in\mathcal C\). Since it is infeasible, separation of the point \(-b\) from the closed polyhedral cone \(\mathcal C\) gives a nonzero vector \(\lambda\) such that
\[
\lambda\cdot(-b)>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda\cdot x\le0\quad(x\in\mathcal C).
\tag{20}
\]
Both \(Nw\) and \(-Nw\) belong to \(\mathcal C\), so (20) gives
\[
\lambda^\mathsf TN=0.
\]
Also \(-z\in\mathcal C\) for every \(z\in\mathbb R_+^3\), so (20) gives
\[
\lambda\ge0.
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i n_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i b_i<0.
\tag{21}
\]

In fact,
\[
\lambda_i>0\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{22}
\]
If, for example, \(\lambda_3=0\), then (21) would give
\[
\lambda_1g_1(w)+\lambda_2g_2(w)
=\lambda_1b_1+\lambda_2b_2<0
\qquad(w\in\mathbb R^2).
\]
No point could satisfy both \(g_1\ge0\) and \(g_2\ge0\), contrary to the pairwise feasibility proved above. The other cases are identical.

The three vectors \(n_i\) are pairwise nonparallel. If one pair were parallel, the positive dependence in (21) would make all three vectors collinear. After choosing a nonzero common normal \(e\), every inequality \(g_i\ge0\) would then be a closed halfline constraint on the single coordinate \(e\cdot w\). A finite family of closed halflines on \(\mathbb R\) whose every pair intersects has nonempty total intersection: the largest lower endpoint does not exceed the smallest upper endpoint because the corresponding lower and upper halflines intersect. This would contradict (16). Hence the linear parts are pairwise nonparallel.

Set
\[
c=-\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i b_i>0,
\qquad
f_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{c}g_i,
\qquad
s_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{c}\alpha_i.
\tag{23}
\]
By (22), multiplying the \(i\)-th defining inequality by \(\lambda_i/c\) does not change its halfspace or its interior. Thus the resulting halfspaces are still proper, satisfy the same strict mass bounds, and form the same inclusion-minimal middle cover. Equations (21) and (23) give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3 f_i=-1.
\]
They also preserve (16), which is now exactly (7).

Put
\[
\Sigma=s_1+s_2+s_3.
\]
At heights \(0,1,2\), the sums of the three defining planar functions in (3) are respectively
\[
-1-\Sigma,\qquad -1,\qquad -1+\Sigma.
\tag{24}
\]
Suppose \(-1\le\Sigma\le1\). Each sum in (24) is nonpositive. Therefore, at every point of each slice, at least one of the three corresponding closed inequalities holds. The three slice sections of the halfspaces cover \(A_0,A_1,A_2\). Hence
\[
T
\le
\sum_{i=1}^3\mu(H_i)
<
3t
=\frac{2T}{3},
\]
a contradiction. It follows that
\[
|\Sigma|>1.
\tag{25}
\]

If \(\Sigma>1\), apply the height reflection (2) to the entire polytope, the three slices, and the three halfspaces. This affine isometry preserves planar areas, halfspace masses, depth, properness, and the middle slice. In (3), it replaces every \(s_i\) by \(-s_i\), so it replaces \(\Sigma\) by \(-\Sigma\). Thus, after at most this reflection,
\[
\Sigma<-1,
\]
which proves (8).

It remains to prove the endpoint ordering in this orientation. By (24), at height \(2\) the sum of the three defining functions is
\[
-1+\Sigma<0.
\]
Thus the three height-\(2\) sections cover \(A_2\). The three middle sections also cover \(A_1\). Consequently,
\[
|A_1|+|A_2|
\le
\sum_{i=1}^3\mu(H_i)
<
3t
=\frac{2T}{3}.
\]
Writing
\[
x=|A_0|,\qquad y=|A_2|,\qquad k=|A_1|,
\]
this strict inequality rearranges to
\[
k+y<2x.
\tag{26}
\]

Compatibility of the three slices and accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` give
\[
4k\ge(\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y})^2.
\tag{27}
\]
Suppose \(x\le y\), and put \(r=\sqrt{x/y}\in(0,1]\). From (27),
\[
k+y
\ge
\left(\frac{(1+r)^2}{4}+1\right)y.
\]
But
\[
\frac{(1+r)^2}{4}+1-2r^2
=
\frac{(1-r)(7r+5)}4
\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
k+y\ge2r^2y=2x,
\]
contradicting (26). Hence
\[
|A_0|>|A_2|.
\]
Renaming these two areas as \(M\) and \(m\) proves (9) and completes the lemma.
