## Statement

Let \(\mathscr R\) be the class of full common-plane residual-counterexample realizations satisfying the complete canonical finite-interior and exact-cell conventions, \(M\geq m\), the three crossings, the canonical tails, \(W=0\), all-double positive \(Q\)-support, \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\), the no-half-slice residual inequalities, and
\[
q=q_{123}=1,\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac5{21},\qquad
m=\frac{12}{7}.
\]
Use the notation of accepted facts `0a44ad1c2be95e40` and `d721506a274e22da`:
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\quad p=|P|,\quad
C_i=\overline{Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}},\quad
S_Q=C_1+C_2+C_3,
\]
\[
\omega_C=\sqrt{|S_Q|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}},\qquad
\kappa=\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i)-3\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}},
\]
\[
\mathcal Q=
\frac12\left(\mathcal V(P,S_Q)-\sqrt{p|S_Q|}\right)
+\frac{\sqrt p}{2}\omega_C,
\]
\[
\mathcal A(p)=\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt{\frac5{21}}\right)^2,
\qquad
\Lambda(p)=\frac{1839}{28}-\frac{39p}{4}
+\frac92\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}},
\]
and let \(\Xi,s_R,d_R,w,\eta,D\) have the meanings in those accepted facts.

There is a universal constant \(\omega_0>0\) such that every
\(\mathcal R\in\mathscr R\) satisfies
\[
\omega_C\geq\omega_0,
\qquad
\mathcal Q=\frac{\kappa}{2}
\geq\frac{\sqrt p}{2}\omega_C
>\frac32\omega_0.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathcal Q+\Xi=2s_R+d_R-\mathcal A(p),
\qquad
\Xi+d_R>0.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(\Xi=0\), then, up to cutting-line null sets,
\[
B_1\cup B_2\cup B_3
=\bigcup_{1\leq |G|\leq2}R_G,
\qquad
B_i\cap B_j=R_{\{k\}}
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}),
\tag{3}
\]
and all three doubleton middle cells have positive area.  Thus every
member, and in particular every attained negative minimizer of the exact
ledger deficit
\[
F=\frac32\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i)+3\Xi
-\left(\frac{39p}{4}+w-\frac{1839}{28}\right),
\tag{4}
\]
has either positive coverage slack \(\Xi>0\), or the exact full-coverage
configuration (3) with \(d_R>0\), while always having the uniformly
positive cap-shape charge (1).  It also satisfies
\[
F=3(\mathcal Q+\Xi)-\bigl(w-\Lambda(p)\bigr)
=6-7\eta<0,
\tag{5}
\]
and hence
\[
w-\Lambda(p)>3(\mathcal Q+\Xi)>\frac92\omega_0.
\tag{6}
\]

Finally, if two realizations in \(\mathscr R\) have the same values of
\((p,w,s_R,d_R)\), then they have the same value of
\(\mathcal Q+\Xi\) and the same value of \(F\).  Therefore every feasible
shape variation preserving these four aggregates is an exact flat direction
of \(F\); its first-order stationarity gives no cap- or coverage-equality
condition.

## Proof

Accepted fact `0435696483192d31` gives a universal \(D_0>0\) such that
\[
D\geq D_0
\tag{7}
\]
throughout \(\mathscr R\).  Accepted fact `d721506a274e22da` gives
\[
D<\frac19,\qquad p>9.
\tag{8}
\]
We first prove the asserted uniform cap gap.

Suppose, to the contrary, that there is a sequence
\(\mathcal R_n\in\mathscr R\) with \(\omega_{C,n}\to0\).  Apply to
\(A_{2,n}\) the common planar John normalization used in accepted fact
`3ff81f067c99b5ad`, and write \(J_n>0\) for its area multiplier.  In the
normalized plane,
\[
B_2\subseteq A_{2,n}'\subseteq2B_2.
\tag{9}
\]
The physical area of \(A_{2,n}\) is
\[
1+3\frac5{21}=\frac{12}{7}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\pi\leq |A_{2,n}'|=\frac{12J_n}{7}\leq4\pi,
\qquad
\frac{7\pi}{12}\leq J_n\leq\frac{7\pi}{3}.
\tag{10}
\]
The normalized central and cap areas are respectively
\[
J_n,\qquad \frac{5J_n}{21}.
\tag{11}
\]
The determinant parameter is affine invariant, since accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8` gives
\[
D_n=\frac{q_n'}{|\mathcal T_{Q,n}'|}
=\frac{J_n}{|\mathcal T_{Q,n}'|}.
\tag{12}
\]
Also, Minkowski sums and planar areas scale by \(J_n\), so the normalized
cap deficit is
\[
\omega_{C,n}'=\sqrt{J_n}\,\omega_{C,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]

We now take a limit using only bounded support functions and bounded line
parameters.  The support functions of the bodies in (9) are uniformly
bounded and uniformly Lipschitz on the unit circle.  A diagonal subsequence
on a countable dense set of directions, followed by the common Lipschitz
bound, converges uniformly to the support function of a convex body
\(A_2'\) satisfying
\[
B_2\subseteq A_2'\subseteq2B_2.
\tag{14}
\]
After a further subsequence, \(J_n\to J\in[7\pi/12,7\pi/3]\) and
\(D_n\to D\in[D_0,1/9]\).

Orient the \(i\)-th normalized cutting line so that its central halfplane
is
\[
H_{i,n}^+=\{z:\nu_{i,n}\mathbin{\cdot}z\geq t_{i,n}\},
\qquad |\nu_{i,n}|=1.
\]
Each line crosses \(A_{2,n}'\), so (9) gives \(|t_{i,n}|\leq2\).
Pass to a subsequence on which every \((\nu_{i,n},t_{i,n})\) converges.
Intersections of a Hausdorff-convergent convex body with convergent
halfplanes converge away from their null boundary lines.  Hence the limiting
central cell and caps have areas
\[
J,\qquad \frac{5J}{21},\frac{5J}{21},\frac{5J}{21},
\tag{15}
\]
and the limiting cap interiors are pairwise disjoint.

The limiting line triple is nondegenerate.  If two limiting outer
halfplanes had the same boundary and orientation, their positive-area caps
would have overlapping interiors.  With opposite orientation, the central
cell in (15) would be contained in the common line and have zero area.  If
two distinct limiting lines were parallel with the same outer orientation,
one positive-area cap would be contained in the other.  With opposite outer
orientations, their central halfplanes form a strip, and the triangle cut
out together with the third halfplane becomes unbounded.  The approximating
triangle areas would then tend to infinity.  This contradicts (7), (10),
and (12), which give
\[
|\mathcal T_{Q,n}'|
=\frac{J_n}{D_n}
\leq\frac{7\pi}{3D_0}.
\tag{16}
\]
The only possible bounded-area near-parallel collapse would make the strip
width tend to zero, but that again contradicts the positive central area in
(15).  Thus the three limiting lines are pairwise nonparallel and bound a
proper triangle.  Their vertices and area converge, and (12) gives
\[
D=\frac{J}{|\mathcal T_Q'|}\leq\frac19.
\tag{17}
\]

Hausdorff continuity of Minkowski addition and planar area, together with
(13), now gives
\[
\sqrt{|C_1'+C_2'+C_3'|}
=3\sqrt{\frac{5J}{21}}.
\tag{18}
\]
Rescale the limiting plane by an affine map of area multiplier \(1/J\).
The central area becomes \(1\), the three cap areas become \(5/21\), and
\(D\) is unchanged.  Equality (18) is the original-cap equality considered
in accepted fact `d0a445c133525448`.

For completeness, the equality classification used there remains valid
for this Hausdorff limit even if the limiting body is not initially
polygonal.  Equality in the iterated planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality
makes the three positive-area caps translates of one convex body \(B\).
Their three nonparallel cutting chords give three distinct base segments of
\(B\).  Applying the support argument of `d0a445c133525448` to each base
normal makes the two translation differences linearly independent.  If the
boundary of \(B\) had any nontrivial arc outside those three base segments,
choose a supporting point in the relative interior of that arc.  In each
translated cap this point lies strictly beyond the corresponding cutting
line, so the cap and \(A_2'\) have the same support there.  The resulting
support equality would make the nonzero supporting normal orthogonal to
both independent translation differences, an impossibility.  Thus \(B\)
is exactly the triangle with those three base sides.  The remaining
calculation in `d0a445c133525448`, which uses only convex containment and
area, gives
\[
D\geq D_{\mathrm{tr}}
=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
>\frac19.
\tag{19}
\]
This contradicts (17).  Therefore a universal \(\omega_0>0\) exists.

Mixed-area additivity gives
\(\mathcal V(P,S_Q)=\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i)\).  Directly simplifying the
definition of \(\mathcal Q\) yields
\[
\mathcal Q
=\frac12\sum_i\mathcal V(P,C_i)
-\frac32\sqrt{\frac{5p}{21}}
=\frac{\kappa}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
The first summand in the defining decomposition of \(\mathcal Q\) is
nonnegative by planar Brunn--Minkowski, so
\[
\mathcal Q\geq\frac{\sqrt p}{2}\omega_C.
\]
Equations (1) now follow from \(p>9\), (8), and the uniform cap gap.

Accepted fact `0a44ad1c2be95e40` gives the exact coverage identity
\[
2s_R+d_R=\mathcal A(p)+\mathcal Q+\Xi
\tag{21}
\]
and the strict support charge
\[
\Xi+d_R>0.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves (2).  If \(\Xi=0\), both nonnegative coverage slacks
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) in that accepted fact vanish.  Their equality
classification gives (3); its support implication then makes every
doubleton middle cell positive.  This proves the coverage dichotomy.

Finally, substitute (20) into the exact ledger identity of accepted fact
`d721506a274e22da`:
\[
\frac32\kappa+3\Xi
=w-\Lambda(p)-(7\eta-6).
\]
This proves (5), and the accepted strict inequality \(\eta>6/7\), together
with (1), proves (6).

Equation (21) also proves the flat-fiber assertion without taking a
derivative: fixing \((p,s_R,d_R)\) fixes \(\mathcal Q+\Xi\), and then
fixing \(w\) fixes
\[
F=3(\mathcal Q+\Xi)-\bigl(w-\Lambda(p)\bigr).
\]
Thus an area-preserving shape variation on such a fiber merely transfers
charge between \(\mathcal Q\) and \(\Xi\).  First-order optimality along
that variation is the identity \(0=0\), so it cannot trigger the cap
equality classification or the coverage equality classification.
