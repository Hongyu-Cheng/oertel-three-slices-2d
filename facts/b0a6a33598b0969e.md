## Statement

Use the normalized coordinates and cap conventions of accepted fact `4ec05d1da924da91` and the residual area domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. Let
\[
\delta,w,r>0,\qquad D=\delta+2,
\]
and let
\[
P=\{(x,y):-\delta\le x\le0,\ 0\le y\le h_P(x)\},
\]
\[
Q=\{(x,y):D\le x\le D+w,\ -h_Q(x)\le y\le0\},
\]
\[
R=\{(x,y):0\le x\le r,\ -a(x)\le y\le c(x)\},
\]
where \(h_P,h_Q,a,c\) are nonnegative affine functions. Assume that \(P,Q,R\) have positive area and
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R.
\]
Put
\[
M=|P|,\qquad m=|Q|,\qquad
A=\int_0^r a(x)\,dx,\qquad
C=\int_0^r c(x)\,dx,\qquad
k=A+C,
\]
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\qquad M\ge m.
\]
With
\[
\mu=\frac mM,\qquad \sigma=\frac kM,\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
assume
\[
0<\mu<\rho,\qquad L(\mu)<\sigma<U(\mu),
\]
where
\[
L(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1-\mu,&0<\mu\le 9/25,\\
(3\mu+2\sqrt\mu-1)/2,&9/25<\mu<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1+\mu,&0<\mu\le1/2,\\
2-\mu,&1/2<\mu<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(E\in\{P,Q,R\}\), define
\[
\alpha_E=|E\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
\beta_E=|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|.
\]
Assume
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t,
\]
\[
\alpha_R+m\ge t,\qquad \beta_R+m\ge t.
\]
Define
\[
b=|Q\cap\{x\ge D\}|,\qquad
d=|R\cap\{x\ge1\}|,
\]
and assume
\[
b>0,\qquad k-d+m\ge t.
\]
Then
\[
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]
In fact, no \(P,Q,R\) satisfy all the hypotheses preceding the definitions of \(b,d\).

## Proof

For an affine function on an interval of width \(\ell>0\) whose endpoint values are \(u,v\), define
\[
\Phi_\ell(u,v)=\int_0^\ell [f(s)]_+\,ds.
\]
Exact integration gives
\[
\Phi_\ell(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\ v\le0,\\
\dfrac{\ell}{2}(u+v),&u\ge0,\ v\ge0,\\
\dfrac{\ell v^2}{2(v-u)},&u<0<v,\\
\dfrac{\ell u^2}{2(u-v)},&v<0<u.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
The formulas agree when an endpoint is zero, so (1) covers all tied thresholds and zero endpoint heights.

Write
\[
p_-=h_P(-\delta),\qquad p_+=h_P(0),
\]
\[
q_-=h_Q(D),\qquad q_+=h_Q(D+w),
\]
\[
a_-=a(0),\qquad a_+=a(r),
\qquad
c_-=c(0),\qquad c_+=c(r).
\]
Then
\[
M=\frac{\delta}{2}(p_-+p_+),\qquad
m=\frac w2(q_-+q_+),
\]
\[
A=\frac r2(a_-+a_+),\qquad
C=\frac r2(c_-+c_+).
\tag{2}
\]

The lower crossing caps are
\[
\alpha_P=0,\qquad \alpha_Q=m,\qquad \alpha_R=A.
\tag{3}
\]
For the other crossing, set
\[
B=\Phi_\delta(p_--\delta,p_+),
\]
\[
\Gamma=\Phi_w(q_--D,q_+-D-w),
\]
\[
G=\Phi_r(a_-,a_+-r).
\tag{4}
\]
At each \(x\in[-\delta,0]\), the vertical length of \(P\cap\{x+y\ge0\}\) is \([h_P(x)+x]_+\). At each \(x\in[D,D+w]\), the part of \(Q\) outside \(\{x+y\ge0\}\) has length \([h_Q(x)-x]_+\). At each \(x\in[0,r]\), the part of \(R\) outside \(\{x+y\ge0\}\) has length \([a(x)-x]_+\). Consequently the exact endpoint formulas are
\[
\beta_P=B,\qquad
\beta_Q=m-\Gamma,\qquad
\beta_R=A+C-G.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
0\le \Gamma\le m,\qquad 0\le G\le A.
\tag{6}
\]

By (3), the first crossing equality is
\[
t=m+A.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (7) with the definition of \(t\) gives
\[
2(M+C)=7(m+A),
\qquad
C=\frac72(m+A)-M.
\tag{8}
\]
Using (5) in the second crossing equality and then using (7) gives
\[
B+(m-\Gamma)+(A+C-G)=m+A,
\]
hence
\[
B+C=\Gamma+G.
\tag{9}
\]
The upper crossing-tail inequality gives
\[
(A+C-G)+m\ge m+A,
\]
so
\[
C\ge G.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9) and (10) yield
\[
\Gamma\ge B.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus both crossing equalities have entered jointly.

First suppose \(\Gamma>0\). Since \(h_Q(x)-x\) is affine, (1) implies that for some
\[
\xi\in\{D,D+w\}
\]
one has
\[
h_Q(\xi)>\xi.
\tag{12}
\]
The points \((-\delta,0),(0,0)\) belong to \(P\), and
\[
(\xi,-h_Q(\xi))\in Q.
\]
Their two midpoints with the latter point belong to \(R\). Therefore, with
\[
x_1=\frac{\xi-\delta}{2},\qquad
x_2=\frac{\xi}{2},\qquad
H=\frac{h_Q(\xi)}2,
\]
midpoint inclusion gives
\[
a(x_1)\ge H,\qquad a(x_2)\ge H.
\tag{13}
\]
Moreover,
\[
0<x_1<x_2\le r,\qquad
x_2-x_1=\frac\delta2,\qquad
H>\frac{\xi}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed \(x_1\ge1\), and the midpoint of \((0,0)\in P\) and \((D+w,0)\in Q\) shows
\[
r\ge\frac{D+w}{2}\ge x_2.
\tag{15}
\]

If \(a\) is nondecreasing, then (13)-(14) give
\[
A\ge\int_{x_1}^r a(x)\,dx
\ge H(r-x_1)
\ge H(x_2-x_1)
>\frac{\xi\delta}{4}
\ge\frac{D\delta}{4}
>\frac{\delta^2}{5}.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(a\) is nonincreasing, then
\[
A\ge\int_0^{x_2}a(x)\,dx
\ge Hx_2
>\frac{\xi^2}{4}
\ge\frac{D^2}{4}
>\frac{\delta^2}{5}.
\tag{17}
\]
A constant affine function is covered by either case. Thus every affine slope ordering gives
\[
A>\frac{\delta^2}{5}.
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (8)-(9) give
\[
M-B=\frac72(m+A)-\Gamma-G.
\tag{19}
\]
Using (6) and (18),
\[
M-B\ge\frac52(m+A)
>\frac52A
>\frac{\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
On the other hand, sectionwise,
\[
M-B
=\int_{-\delta}^0
\min\{h_P(x),-x\}\,dx
\le\int_{-\delta}^0(-x)\,dx
=\frac{\delta^2}{2},
\tag{21}
\]
contradicting (20).

It remains to cover \(\Gamma=0\), including all endpoint-zero chambers not covered by (12). By (11), \(B=0\), and (9)-(10) give
\[
C=G\le A.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(k=A+C>0\), (22) implies \(A>0\). From (8) and \(C\le A\),
\[
7(m+A)=2(M+C)\le2(M+A),
\]
so
\[
5A+7m\le2M.
\tag{23}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mu=\frac mM<\frac27<\frac9{25}.
\tag{24}
\]
The residual lower bound from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` is therefore
\[
\frac{k}{M}>1-\mu,
\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
k>M-m.
\tag{25}
\]
But (22) gives \(k=A+C\le2A\), so
\[
2A>M-m.
\tag{26}
\]
Multiplying (26) by \(5/2\) and combining it with (23) yields
\[
\frac52(M-m)<5A\le2M-7m.
\]
After multiplication by \(2\), this says
\[
5M-5m<4M-14m,
\]
or
\[
M+9m<0,
\]
contrary to \(M,m>0\).

Thus neither \(\Gamma>0\) nor \(\Gamma=0\) is possible. Hence the joint hypotheses have no realization. For completeness, (15) gives \(r>1\), while \(Q\subseteq\{x\ge D\}\), so the contact tails have the unambiguous formulas
\[
b=m,\qquad
d=\int_1^r(a(x)+c(x))\,dx.
\]
The desired inequality follows.
