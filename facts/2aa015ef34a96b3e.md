## Statement

Fix
\[
A=\frac18,\qquad B=\frac{11249}{10000},\qquad
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-32877&46362&-17003\\
-67122&2&53678\\
100000&-46363&-36674
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1}
\]
the second exact calibration of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98`.  Put
\[
t=\frac{22499}{45000},\qquad
c_3=\mathcal C(100000,-46363,-36674;0)
=\frac{5000000000}{10002008331},
\]
\[
\eta=t-c_3
=\frac{3909493241}{50010041655000}.
\tag{2}
\]
There do not exist endpoint matrices \(U,V\) and allocations
\(\sigma\) satisfying the compatible endpoint parametrization of
accepted fact `f74f24523b5f53b2`,
\[
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\]
for which the third strict row inequality of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` holds.  More explicitly, for every
\[
-1<s<1,\qquad \mathbf1^\top\ell=s,
\]
and every pair of entrywise nonnegative column-stochastic matrices
\(X,Y\) satisfying
\[
U=\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1-s)X,\qquad
V=-\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1+s)Y,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
(1-s)^2|\det X|=A,\qquad
(1+s)^2|\det Y|=B,
\tag{4}
\]
and for every \(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\), one has
\[
A\mathcal C((LU)_{3*};-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{3*};\sigma_3)
\geq\eta.
\tag{5}
\]
This includes both determinant signs, all asymmetric \(X,Y\), and all
zero-entry boundary strata.  Consequently the full third-row mass is
at least \(c_3+\eta=t\), so the fixed calibration (1) cannot be
completed to a strict rational or real matrix witness.

## Proof

Write
\[
g=(p,-a,-b)=(100000,-46363,-36674),
\qquad p=100000,\quad a=46363,\quad b=36674.
\tag{6}
\]
For the parametrization (3), put
\[
d=L\ell+\sigma.
\]
Then the two endpoint rows in the third cap are exactly
\[
(LU)_{3*}+\sigma_3\mathbf1^\top
=d_3\mathbf1^\top+(1-s)gX,
\]
\[
(LV)_{3*}-\sigma_3\mathbf1^\top
=(1+s)gY-d_3\mathbf1^\top.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\ell\) and \(\sigma\) enter the third row only through the one
number \(d_3\).

We first prove an elementary fixed-area triangle-cap bound.  Let
\(T\) be a nondegenerate triangle, let \(f\) be a nonconstant affine
functional on \(T\), and let
\[
\rho=\frac{|T\cap\{f\geq z\}|}{|T|}.
\]
Then \(T\cap\{f\leq z\}\) contains a triangle of area at least
\[
(1-\sqrt\rho)|T|.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, if the upper cap has one positive vertex, let
\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in[0,1]\) be its two edge ratios from that
vertex.  The exact one-positive formula of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` gives
\(\rho=\lambda_1\lambda_2\).  The lower part contains a triangle with
the opposite edge as base and area ratio
\(1-\min\{\lambda_1,\lambda_2\}\), which is at least
\(1-\sqrt\rho\).  If the upper cap has two positive vertices, its
lower complement is itself a triangle of area ratio \(1-\rho\), and
\(1-\rho\geq1-\sqrt\rho\).  Empty, full, repeated-value, and threshold
equality cases follow literally from the closed-cap formulas in
`64760c76cadff481`.  This proves (8), including all boundary strata.

We next compute an exact capacity in the standard triangle
\(\Delta\), whose area is normalized to one.  For \(z\in\mathbb R\),
put
\[
K_z=\{\lambda\in\Delta:g\lambda\leq z\}
\]
and let \(m(z)\) be the maximum normalized area of a triangle
contained in \(K_z\).  Then
\[
m(z)=
\begin{cases}
0,&z\leq-a,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(z+a)^2}{(a-b)(p+a)},&-a\leq z\leq-b,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{z+a}{p+a},&-b\leq z\leq p,\\[3mm]
1,&z\geq p.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
The first, second, and fourth branches are immediate: before the first
vertex \(K_z\) is empty, between the two negative vertex values it is
the similar corner triangle at the \(-a\) vertex, and after the last
vertex it is all of \(\Delta\).

For \(-b\leq z\leq p\), \(K_z\) is the quadrilateral with the two
negative vertices and the two intersections on the edges from the
\(p\) vertex.  Put
\[
r_a=\frac{z+a}{p+a},\qquad
r_b=\frac{z+b}{p+b}.
\]
A maximum-area triangle in a convex polygon may be chosen with all
three vertices among the polygon vertices: with two vertices fixed,
area is maximized by moving the third to a polygon vertex, and this
may be repeated for all three vertices.  The four vertex-triangle
areas in this quadrilateral are
\[
r_a,\qquad r_b,\qquad r_a(1-r_b),\qquad r_b(1-r_a).
\]
Since \(0\leq r_b\leq r_a\leq1\), their maximum is \(r_a\).  This
proves the third branch of (9).

The joining capacity is
\[
m(-b)=\frac{a-b}{p+a}=\frac{9689}{146363}<\frac19,
\tag{10}
\]
where the exact margin is
\[
\frac19-\frac{9689}{146363}
=\frac{59162}{1317267}>0.
\]
It follows from (9)-(10) that whenever \(w>1/9\) and \(m(z)>w\),
\[
z>(p+a)w-a.
\tag{11}
\]

We now apply these facts to arbitrary data (3)-(4).  Let
\[
\alpha=|\det X|=\frac{A}{(1-s)^2},\qquad
\beta=|\det Y|=\frac{B}{(1+s)^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The matrices \(X,Y\) map \(\Delta\) to arbitrary, possibly
orientation-reversing, subtriangles \(T_X=X\Delta\) and
\(T_Y=Y\Delta\) of normalized areas \(\alpha,\beta\).  Their
determinant signs therefore play no role below.

Assume for a contradiction that the left side of (5) is strictly less
than \(\eta\).  Define
\[
\rho_X=\mathcal C\left(gX;-\frac{d_3}{1-s}\right),
\qquad
\rho_Y=\mathcal C\left(gY;\frac{d_3}{1+s}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (7) and positive homogeneity of the exact cap formula give
\[
A\rho_X+B\rho_Y<\eta,
\]
and hence
\[
\rho_X<\frac{\eta}{A},\qquad
\rho_Y<\frac{\eta}{B}.
\tag{14}
\]
The following two exact estimates will be used:
\[
\frac{\eta}{A}<\frac1{81},
\qquad
\frac1{81}-\frac{\eta}{A}
=\frac{219799490902}{18753765620625}>0,
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\frac{\eta}{B}<\frac1{10000},
\qquad
\frac1{10000}-\frac{\eta}{B}
=\frac{34322726895419}{1125125917154190000}>0.
\tag{16}
\]

Because \(Y\) is column-stochastic, \(Y\Delta\subseteq\Delta\), so
\(\beta\leq1\).  Since \(B>1\), (12) gives \(s>0\), and therefore
\(\alpha>A=1/8\).  By (8), (14), and (15), the lower halfplane
corresponding to \(T_X\) contains a triangle of area strictly greater
than
\[
\alpha\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{A}}\right)
>\frac18\cdot\frac89=\frac19.
\tag{17}
\]
Also \(s<1\), so \(\beta>B/4>1/4\).  Equations (8), (14), and (16)
give a lower-halfplane triangle in \(T_Y\) of area strictly greater
than
\[
\beta\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{B}}\right)
>\frac14\cdot\frac{99}{100}>\frac19.
\tag{18}
\]

Set
\[
r=-\frac{d_3}{1-s},\qquad
z=\frac{d_3}{1+s}.
\tag{19}
\]
The two thresholds satisfy the exact allocation identity
\[
(1-s)r+(1+s)z=0.
\tag{20}
\]
By (8), (11), and (17),
\[
r>(p+a)\alpha
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{A}}\right)-a.
\tag{21}
\]
Similarly, (18) gives
\[
z>(p+a)\beta
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{B}}\right)-a.
\tag{22}
\]
Define
\[
u=A\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{A}}\right),
\qquad
v=B\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{B}}\right).
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (15)-(16) imply the simple strict bounds
\[
u>\frac19,\qquad v>1.
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed, the first follows from \(A=1/8\) and
\(\sqrt{\eta/A}<1/9\); the second follows from
\(\sqrt{\eta/B}<1/100\) and
\[
\frac{99B}{100}=\frac{1113651}{1000000}>1.
\]

Multiply (21) by \(1-s\), multiply (22) by \(1+s\), add, and use
(12), (20), and (23).  This gives
\[
0>
(p+a)\left(\frac{u}{1-s}+\frac{v}{1+s}\right)-2a.
\tag{25}
\]
On the other hand, Cauchy--Schwarz and (24) give
\[
\frac{u}{1-s}+\frac{v}{1+s}
\geq
\frac{(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2}{(1-s)+(1+s)}
>\frac{(1/3+1)^2}{2}
=\frac89.
\tag{26}
\]
Substituting \(p=100000\) and \(a=46363\), the right side of (25) is
therefore strictly greater than
\[
(p+a)\frac89-2a
=\frac{336370}{9}>0,
\]
contradicting (25).  Thus (5) holds for every permitted
\(s,\ell,X,Y,\sigma\).  Adding \(c_3\) and using (2) excludes the
strict third row, completing the claimed endpoint obstruction.
