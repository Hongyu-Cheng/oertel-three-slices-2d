## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Write \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and let
\[
c_i=\frac1{a_i}\int_{A_i}w\,dw
\]
be the planar centroid of \(A_i\). Define
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\qquad
\mu(E)=\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|\{w\in A_i:(i,w)\in E\}\right|,
\]
and
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu(S\cap H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]

Assume at least one of the following conditions:

1. \(c_1=(c_0+c_2)/2\);
2. \(\max\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}\ge T/2\).

Then \(S\) is the exact mixed-integer part of a compact convex polytope and
\[
\max_{y\in S}h_S(y)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
More precisely, under condition 2, every \(j\) with \(a_j\ge T/2\) satisfies
\[
h_S((j,c_j))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Under condition 1, if no slice has area at least \(T/2\), then
\[
h_S((1,c_1))>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` applied to the assumed midpoint inclusion gives a compact convex polytope whose integer-height sections are exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\).

We use the planar case of Grünbaum's centroid inequality:
\[
|K\cap Q|\ge\frac49|K|
\tag{G}
\]
whenever \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a convex body and \(Q\) is a closed halfplane containing the centroid of \(K\). The exact source and applicability check appear below.

First observe that for every \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\),
\[
h_S((j,c_j))\ge\frac49a_j.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` permits restriction to halfspaces whose boundary contains \((j,c_j)\). For such a halfspace, its section on height \(j\) is either all of \(A_j\), when the planar component of its normal vanishes, or the intersection of \(A_j\) with a planar halfplane whose boundary contains \(c_j\). In the latter case (G) gives area at least \(4a_j/9\). All other slice contributions are nonnegative, proving (1).

If condition 2 holds and \(a_j\ge T/2\), then (1) gives
\[
h_S((j,c_j))
\ge\frac49a_j
\ge\frac49\frac T2
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

It remains to assume condition 1 and that \(a_i<T/2\) for all \(i\). By accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, consider an arbitrary closed halfspace whose boundary contains \((1,c_1)\). After choosing its side, write it as
\[
H=\{(t,w):\ell(w-c_1)+v(t-1)\ge0\},
\]
where \(\ell:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) is linear, \(v\in\mathbb R\), and \((\ell,v)\ne(0,0)\).

Set
\[
\sigma=\ell(c_2-c_1)+v.
\]
The defining expression of \(H\) at \((2,c_2)\) equals \(\sigma\). Since
\[
c_0-c_1=-(c_2-c_1),
\]
its value at \((0,c_0)\) equals
\[
\ell(c_0-c_1)-v=-\sigma.
\]
Thus \(H\) contains at least one of \((0,c_0)\) and \((2,c_2)\). Let \(k\in\{0,2\}\) be an index whose centroid point is contained in \(H\).

The middle-slice section of \(H\) has area at least \(4a_1/9\), by the same argument as in (1). The height-\(k\) section is either all of \(A_k\) or a planar halfplane section containing \(c_k\), so (G) gives area at least \(4a_k/9\). Consequently,
\[
\mu(S\cap H)\ge\frac49(a_1+a_k)
\ge\frac49\min\{a_1+a_0,a_1+a_2\}.
\]
Because both outer areas are strictly less than \(T/2\),
\[
a_1+a_0=T-a_2>\frac T2,
\qquad
a_1+a_2=T-a_0>\frac T2.
\]
Hence every such \(H\) satisfies the uniform bound
\[
\mu(S\cap H)
\ge\frac49\min\{a_1+a_0,a_1+a_2\}
>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Taking the infimum proves
\[
h_S((1,c_1))>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

To verify strict enlargement, every triple in accepted fact `a4fa27298af87644` satisfies the present hypotheses: its slice centroids are \(p_i\), with \(p_1=(p_0+p_2)/2\), and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=p_1+\frac{\lambda_0+\lambda_2}{2}\Delta
\subseteq p_1+\lambda_1\Delta=A_1.
\]
The present theorem also contains triples excluded there. For example, take
\[
A_0=A_2=[-1,1]\times[-1,1],
\qquad
A_1=[-1,3]\times[-1,2].
\]
Then
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=A_0\subseteq A_1,
\]
the areas are \((4,12,4)\), and \(T=20\). Their centroids are
\[
c_0=c_2=(0,0),
\qquad
c_1=(1,1/2),
\]
so affine-center alignment fails. Moreover, \(A_1\) is not homothetic to \(A_0\), since their side-length ratios differ. Condition 2 holds, and the proof gives
\[
h_S((1,c_1))\ge\frac49\cdot12=\frac{16}{3}
>\frac{40}{9}=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, Theorem 2 and its stronger centroid statement in the proof, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257.

Exact theorem used: if \(K\subset\mathbb R^n\) is a convex body with centroid \(G_K\), then every closed halfspace \(Q\) containing \(G_K\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(K\cap Q)
\ge
\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n\operatorname{vol}_n(K).
\]
For \(n=2\), the constant is \(4/9\). Each \(A_i\) above is a compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^2\) with nonempty interior, and \(c_i\) is its Lebesgue centroid, so all hypotheses apply directly.
