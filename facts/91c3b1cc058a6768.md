## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},
\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and, for \(z\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\mathcal C(z;\tau)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\ge\tau\}\right|.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(n\), let \(U_n,V_n,R_n\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\),
\(\tau_n\in\mathbb R^3\), and \(\delta_n>0\) satisfy
\[
\det U_n\ne0,\qquad \det V_n\ne0,\qquad \det R_n\ne0,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
A_n:=|\det U_n|\le B_n:=|\det V_n|,
\qquad
r_n:=\frac{A_n}{B_n},
\qquad
S_n:=\frac1{B_n},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
0<r_n<\frac38,\qquad
1-r_n<S_n<1+\frac{r_n}{2},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(R_n)_{ij}=\delta_n
\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{5}
\]
These are the area and cover conditions supplied, after the surviving
orientation and removal of finitely many terms, by accepted facts
`09393c6edf1fc4e2`, `291c24bb45bd4946`, and
`f74f24523b5f53b2`. Define
\[
t_n=\frac{2(1+A_n+B_n)}9
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{i,n}
={}&\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)\\
&+A_n\mathcal C((R_nU_n)_{i*};-\tau_{i,n})
+B_n\mathcal C((R_nV_n)_{i*};\tau_{i,n})
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Assume
\[
r_n\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_n:=
\frac{\delta_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
Put
\[
\widehat R_n=\frac{R_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F},
\qquad
P=3I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top.
\tag{9}
\]
Then \(t_n\to4/9\), and the following assertions hold.

1. If, along a subsequence, \(\widehat R_n\to R^0\) and
   \(\operatorname{rank}R^0=1\), then
   \[
   \liminf_n\max_{1\le i\le3}(M_{i,n}-t_n)\ge\frac1{18}
   \tag{10}
   \]
   along that subsequence.

2. Suppose
   \[
   \limsup_n\max_{1\le i\le3}(M_{i,n}-t_n)\le0.
   \tag{11}
   \]
   Every cluster point of \(\widehat R_n\) then has rank two and is of
   the form
   \[
   R^0=\frac1{\sqrt{18}}\,\Pi P
   \tag{12}
   \]
   for a \(3\times3\) permutation matrix \(\Pi\). In particular,
   \[
   \operatorname{dist}_F
   \left(
   \widehat R_n,
   \left\{\frac1{\sqrt{18}}\Pi P:
   \Pi\text{ is a permutation matrix}\right\}
   \right)\longrightarrow0,
   \tag{13}
   \]
   and
   \[
   \mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)\longrightarrow\frac49
   \qquad(i=1,2,3).
   \tag{14}
   \]

3. Under (11), every large-end cap vanishes:
   \[
   \mathcal C((R_nV_n)_{i*};\tau_{i,n})
   \longrightarrow0
   \qquad(i=1,2,3).
   \tag{15}
   \]
   This conclusion includes all repeated-projection and support-contact
   strata in the cap formula.

More generally, before imposing the Frobenius normalization in (9), the
rank-two equality class in (12) is exactly
\[
\{\alpha\Pi P:\alpha>0,\ \Pi\text{ a permutation matrix}\}.
\tag{16}
\]

## Proof

From (3)-(4) and \(r_n\to0\),
\[
S_n\to1,\qquad
A_n=\frac{r_n}{S_n}\to0,\qquad
B_n=\frac1{S_n}\to1.
\tag{17}
\]
Equation (6) therefore gives \(t_n\to4/9\). The restriction
\(r_n<3/8\) in (4) is the exact global exclusion supplied by accepted
fact `291c24bb45bd4946`; the sharper small-\(r\) bounds in (4) are the
first branch of accepted fact `f74f24523b5f53b2`.

Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives the complete cap formulas. We
first record two consequences, including the boundary cases needed
below.

First, for every \(c>0\),
\[
\mathcal C(cz;c\tau)=\mathcal C(z;\tau).
\tag{18}
\]
Second, the map
\[
z\longmapsto\mathcal C(z;0)
\tag{19}
\]
is continuous on \(\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}\). Indeed, if \(z\) is
nonconstant on \(\Delta\), its zero set is a line of planar area zero,
so dominated convergence applies to the indicators of
\(\{z^{(k)}\cdot\lambda\ge0\}\). If \(z\) is a nonzero constant row,
all sufficiently close rows have the same strict sign on \(\Delta\),
and their cap fractions are identically zero or one. Thus repeated
vertex values, a zero value on an edge, and nonzero constant rows cause
no discontinuity. Only the zero row is excluded from (19).

We next prove the exact centroid-cap lemma, including its equality
case. Let \(z=(a,b,c)\ne0\), sorted so that \(a\le b\le c\), and assume
\[
a+b+c\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)\ge\frac49,
\tag{21}
\]
with equality if and only if
\[
(a,b,c)=(-u,-u,2u)
\qquad\text{for some }u>0.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(a\ge0\), the cap is full. The case \(c\le0\) is incompatible with
(20) for a nonzero row. Suppose first that
\(a\le b\le0<c\). Write
\[
u=-a\ge v=-b\ge0,\qquad c=u+v+w,\qquad w\ge0.
\]
The one-positive-vertex branch of the cap formula gives
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)=\frac{c^2}{(c+u)(c+v)}.
\]
The desired cross-multiplied numerator is
\[
\begin{aligned}
9c^2-4(c+u)(c+v)
&=(u-v)^2+6(u+v)w+5w^2\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Equality forces \(w=0\) and \(u=v>0\), which is (22).
If instead \(a=-u<0\le b\le c\), then (20) gives \(b+c\ge u\), and
\[
(u+b)(u+c)-2u^2
=u(b+c-u)+bc\ge0.
\tag{24}
\]
The two-positive-vertex branch gives
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)
=1-\frac{u^2}{(u+b)(u+c)}
\ge\frac12>\frac49.
\tag{25}
\]
This proves (21)-(22), including all repeated-value cases.

By (5), (8), and (9),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(\widehat R_n)_{i*}
=\varepsilon_n\mathbf1^\top\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
\]
The unit Frobenius sphere is compact, so every subsequence of
\(\widehat R_n\) has a further convergent subsequence. Every cluster
point \(R^0\) satisfies
\[
\lVert R^0\rVert_F=1,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3R^0_{i*}=0.
\tag{27}
\]
Hence \(R^0\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}R^0\le2\).

Suppose first that \(\operatorname{rank}R^0=1\). Its nonzero rows have
the form \(c_i z\) for one nonzero row \(z\). Their sum is zero by
(27), so at least one nonzero \(c_i\) is positive and another is
negative. If \(z\) is nonconstant, the two corresponding oppositely
oriented caps partition \(\Delta\) up to the zero line, and their
fractions sum to one. If \(z\) is a nonzero constant row, one of those
caps is the whole triangle. In both cases,
\[
\max_i\mathcal C(R^0_{i*};0)\ge\frac12.
\tag{28}
\]
The two rows used in this argument are nonzero, so (18)-(19) give
\[
\liminf_n\max_i\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)\ge\frac12
\tag{29}
\]
along the selected subsequence. All three terms in (7) are
nonnegative. Combining (17), (29), and \(t_n\to4/9\) proves (10).
This also handles a third row tending to zero: continuity is used only
for the two nonzero opposite limiting rows.

Now assume (11), and fix any cluster point \(R^0\). Equation (10)
excludes rank one, so \(R^0\) has rank two. No row of \(R^0\) is zero,
because (27) would then make the other two rows opposite and the rank
at most one. From (7), nonnegativity, (11), and \(t_n\to4/9\),
\[
\limsup_n\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)\le\frac49
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{30}
\]
Using (18)-(19) at the three nonzero limiting rows gives
\[
\mathcal C(R^0_{i*};0)\le\frac49
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{31}
\]

Let
\[
\mu_i=\frac13\sum_{j=1}^3R^0_{ij}.
\]
Equation (27) gives \(\sum_i\mu_i=0\). If some \(\mu_i>0\), the
centroid-cap lemma is strict and contradicts (31). Hence
\(\mu_i\le0\) for every \(i\), and their zero sum forces
\[
\mu_1=\mu_2=\mu_3=0.
\tag{32}
\]
Applying the equality case (22) row by row, there are
\(\alpha_i>0\) and \(\pi(i)\in\{1,2,3\}\) such that
\[
R^0_{i*}
=\alpha_i\bigl(3e_{\pi(i)}^\top-\mathbf1^\top\bigr).
\tag{33}
\]
Put
\[
D_j=\sum_{\pi(i)=j}\alpha_i,\qquad
D=\sum_i\alpha_i.
\]
Summing (33) over the rows and using (27) yields
\[
3D_j-D=0\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{34}
\]
Thus every \(D_j>0\). Since there are exactly three positive
\(\alpha_i\), the map \(\pi\) is a permutation, and (34) gives
\[
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha_3.
\tag{35}
\]
Therefore \(R^0=\alpha\Pi P\) for a positive \(\alpha\) and a
permutation matrix \(\Pi\). Since
\[
\lVert P\rVert_F^2=3\cdot2^2+6\cdot1^2=18
\]
and \(\lVert R^0\rVert_F=1\), necessarily
\(\alpha=1/\sqrt{18}\). This proves (12), and it also proves the
unnormalized equality classification (16).

Every cluster point belongs to the finite set in (13). Compactness now
proves (13): otherwise a subsequence at a fixed positive distance from
that set would have a cluster point outside it. Every row of every
matrix in that finite set is a positive multiple of a permutation of
\((2,-1,-1)\), whose cap fraction is \(4/9\) by the exact formula in
accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`. Continuity and the same subsequence
argument prove (14).

It remains to prove the large-end assertion without assuming any
convergence of \(V_n\) or \(\tau_n\). Put
\[
g_n=\max_i(M_{i,n}-t_n).
\]
For every fixed \(i\), (7) and nonnegativity give
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le
B_n\mathcal C((R_nV_n)_{i*};\tau_{i,n})\\
&\le
M_{i,n}-\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)\\
&\le
t_n+g_n-\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
By (11), (14), and \(t_n\to4/9\), the limsup of the last expression is
at most zero. Its left side is nonnegative, so it converges to zero.
Since \(B_n\to1\), this proves (15). The argument uses only cap
fractions and therefore also covers repeated maxima, support edges,
and constant endpoint projection rows exactly. This completes all
three assertions.
