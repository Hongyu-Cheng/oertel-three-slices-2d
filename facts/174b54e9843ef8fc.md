## Statement

Assume the canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, and the actual dominant all-double counterexample hypotheses of accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1`.  Thus
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
T=M+m+k,
\]
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,
\qquad m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\]
\[
U=\sum_{i=1}^3u_i,\qquad
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123},
\]
and the dominant index is labeled so that
\[
x_1>\frac{5m}{9},\qquad
u_1<x_1-\frac{5m}{9},\qquad
x_2,x_3<\frac{4m}{9}.
\]
Let \(g_0,g_2\) be the centroids of \(A_0,A_2\), and put
\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
M>3m+3(U+q+E_R)>3m,                                      \tag{1}
\]
\[
u_i\leq U<
\frac{M-3m}{3}-q-E_R<\frac M3
\qquad(i=1,2,3).                                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{4M}{9}-u_i>
\frac M9+m+q+E_R,
\qquad
M-u_i>
\frac{2M}{3}+m+q+E_R.                                   \tag{3}
\]
At the dominant cut there is also the exact \(A_2\)-cap deficit
\[
\frac{4m}{9}-(m-x_1)
=x_1-\frac{5m}{9}>u_1.                                  \tag{4}
\]

For every \(i\),
\[
f_i(g_0)>L_i,                                             \tag{5}
\]
so \(g_0\in P_0\).  Accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` gives
\[
f_1(g_2)>-L_1,\qquad
f_2(g_2)<-L_2,\qquad
f_3(g_2)<-L_3,
\]
so \(g_2\in Q_{23}\).  Consequently
\[
z\in\operatorname{int}K,\qquad f_1(z)>0,\qquad
z\in R_2\cup R_3\cup R_{23}.                             \tag{6}
\]

Write
\[
w_{0,i}=\max_{A_0}f_i-\min_{A_0}f_i,\qquad
w_{2,i}=\max_{A_2}f_i-\min_{A_2}f_i.
\]
These widths are positive.  The support-width centroid bound of accepted fact `f1096f0628017701` gives
\[
\frac{f_i(g_0)-L_i}{w_{0,i}}
\geq
\frac{4M-9u_i}{12M}
>
\frac{M+9m+9q+9E_R}{12M}
\qquad(i=1,2,3),                                         \tag{7}
\]
where the same conclusion holds when \(u_i=0\).  At the dominant \(A_2\) cut,
\[
\frac{f_1(g_2)+L_1}{w_{2,1}}
\geq
\frac{9x_1-5m}{12m}.                                     \tag{8}
\]
Thus the strict middle-cap localization has the quantitative form
\[
f_1(z)
\geq
\frac{w_{0,1}(4M-9u_1)}{24M}
+
\frac{w_{2,1}(9x_1-5m)}{24m}
>0.                                                       \tag{9}
\]
For \(i=2,3\), the same accepted support-width bound, applied to
\(-f_i-L_i\) on \(A_2\), gives
\[
\frac{-f_i(g_2)-L_i}{w_{2,i}}
\geq
\frac{4m-9x_i}{12m}>0.                                   \tag{10}
\]
Equations (7) and (10) give positive lower bounds on the two opposing terms in \(2f_i(z)\), but do not compare them.

The numerical sufficient condition in accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` is strictly false here.  Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&2T-\left((\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m\right)\\
&\qquad
=M-3m+2k-2\sqrt{Mm}\\
&\qquad
>(\sqrt M-\sqrt m)^2+6(U+q+E_R)>0.                       \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the accepted balanced-middle bridge cannot certify depth at \((1,z)\) anywhere in this branch.  The large-slice criterion of accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` also cannot apply in an actual counterexample, since its contrapositive forces
\[
M,m,k<\frac T2.                                          \tag{12}
\]

Finally, \(z\) need not be the centroid of
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
For example, with
\[
A_0^\ast=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
A_2^\ast=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(1,1)\},
\]
the midpoint of the two centroids is \((1/2,1/3)\), whereas the centroid of \((A_0^\ast+A_2^\ast)/2\) is \((1/2,7/18)\).  Hence neither \(B\subseteq K\) nor (6) justifies applying Grünbaum's centroid bound to a halfplane of \(B\) or \(K\) through \(z\).

## Proof

In the all-double case, accepted fact `0819231d0af83197` gives the exact identity
\[
E_R=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}-(U+m+q).
\]
With
\[
A=U+q+E_R,
\]
this is
\[
2M=4m+k+3A.                                               \tag{13}
\]
Accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` gives
\[
k>2m+3q+3U+3E_R=2m+3A.                                  \tag{14}
\]
Substituting (14) into (13) yields
\[
2M>6m+6A,
\]
which is (1).  Since \(A=U+q+E_R\), (1) also gives (2).

By the exact-cell partition,
\[
u_i=\left|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}\right|.
\]
Subtracting the upper bound in (2) from \(4M/9\) and from \(M\) proves (3).  Equation (4) is an algebraic rewriting of the dominant inequalities in accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1`.

Suppose \(f_i(g_0)\leq L_i\) for some \(i\).  Then the closed halfplane section
\[
A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}
\]
contains the centroid \(g_0\).  The planar Grünbaum inequality recorded in accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` would give \(u_i\geq4M/9\), contrary to (3).  This proves (5).  A centroid of a two-dimensional convex body is an interior point, so \(g_0\) lies in the strict exact cell \(P_0\).  The same fact and the strict localization already proved in accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` place \(g_2\) in the interior of \(A_2\) and in \(Q_{23}\).  Hence \(z\) is interior to \((A_0+A_2)/2\), and therefore interior to \(K\).  Affinity gives
\[
2f_1(z)
=\bigl(f_1(g_0)-L_1\bigr)
+\bigl(f_1(g_2)+L_1\bigr)>0.                             \tag{15}
\]
The incidence rule in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, applied to the endpoint types \((P_0,Q_{23})\), gives exactly
\[
z\in R_2\cup R_3\cup R_{23}.
\]
Equivalently, \(f_1(z)>0\), while \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\) forces at least one of \(f_2(z),f_3(z)\) to be negative.  Neither of their individual signs follows from endpoint incidence.

For \(u_i>0\), both open sides of the cut \(f_i=L_i\) meet the interior of \(A_0\): the negative side has positive area, and the positive side contains \(g_0\).  Since \(u_i<4M/9\), accepted fact `f1096f0628017701`, applied to
\[
Q=A_0,\qquad \phi=f_i-L_i,\qquad d=u_i,
\]
proves the first inequality in (7).  If \(u_i=0\), then \(\min_{A_0}(f_i-L_i)\geq0\).  The endpoint tent representation in the proof of that accepted fact shows that the centroid coordinate in any nonconstant affine support direction is at least one third of the support width from its minimum.  Therefore
\[
\frac{f_i(g_0)-L_i}{w_{0,i}}\geq\frac13
=\frac{4M-9u_i}{12M},
\]
so (7) also holds in the zero-cap case.  The strict second inequality in (7) follows from (2).

Equation (8) is the dominant-cut specialization already contained in accepted fact `f1096f0628017701`.  Adding its unnormalized form to the \(i=1\) case of (7), then using (15), proves (9).

For \(i=2,3\), the exact positive side
\[
A_2\cap\{f_i>-L_i\}
\]
has area \(x_i\).  Both sides meet the interior because \(0<x_i<m\), and accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` gives \(x_i<4m/9\).  Apply accepted fact `f1096f0628017701` with
\[
Q=A_2,\qquad \phi=-f_i-L_i,\qquad d=x_i.
\]
This proves (10).  But
\[
2f_i(z)
=\bigl(f_i(g_0)-L_i\bigr)
-\bigl(-f_i(g_2)-L_i\bigr),\qquad i=2,3,                 \tag{16}
\]
and (7), (10) are lower bounds on both positive quantities in (16).  They give no ordering.  Fixing either sign requires an upper bound for one margin or a direct cross-slice comparison between them.

Because \(M\geq m\), the left side of the balanced-middle condition in accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` is
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m.
\]
The first two equalities in (11) are direct expansion.  Inserting (14) gives
\[
M-3m+2k-2\sqrt{Mm}
>
M+m-2\sqrt{Mm}+6A
=(\sqrt M-\sqrt m)^2+6A,
\]
proving (11).  Also, if any of \(M,m,k\) were at least \(T/2\), accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` would construct a point of depth at least \(2T/9\), contrary to the actual-counterexample hypothesis.  This proves (12).

Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` shows that depth at \((1,z)\) requires a lower bound for every tilted three-slice cap through that point.  The membership \(z\in K\cap\{f_1>0\}\) controls only one canonical sign direction and supplies no such all-direction estimate.

For the final centroid calibration, the two displayed triangles have centroids
\[
\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac23,\frac13\right).
\]
Their Minkowski sum is the pentagon with counterclockwise vertices
\[
(0,0),(2,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,1).
\]
The polygon centroid formula gives its centroid as \((1,7/9)\).  Scaling by \(1/2\) gives centroid \((1/2,7/18)\), different from
\[
\frac12\left(
\left(\frac13,\frac13\right)
+
\left(\frac23,\frac13\right)\right)
=\left(\frac12,\frac13\right).
\]
Thus \(z\) is not generally the centroid of the midpoint body, and inclusion of that body in \(K\) does not identify \(z\) with the centroid of \(K\).  This is the first missing comparison needed to turn (6) or (9) into a new halfspace-depth bound.
