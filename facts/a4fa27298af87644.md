## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate compact triangle with
centroid \(0\) and area \(D>0\). Let \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2>0\),
let \(p_0,p_1,p_2\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfy
\(p_1=(p_0+p_2)/2\), and assume
\(\lambda_0+\lambda_2\le2\lambda_1\). Define
\(A_i=p_i+\lambda_i\Delta\) and
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)
\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2.
\]
Equip \(S\) with the measure
\[
\mu(E)=\sum_{i=0}^2\operatorname{area}\{x\in A_i:(i,x)\in E\},
\]
write
\(T=\mu(S)=D(\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)\), and define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu(S\cap H):H\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]
Then \(S\) is the exact mixed-integer part of a compact convex polytope,
and one of the three slice centroids \((i,p_i)\) satisfies
\(h_S((i,p_i))\ge2T/9\).

## Proof

Since \(\Delta\) is convex,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=p_1+\frac{\lambda_0+\lambda_2}{2}\Delta
\subseteq p_1+\lambda_1\Delta=A_1,
\]
where the inclusion uses \(0\in\Delta\) and
\(\lambda_0+\lambda_2\le2\lambda_1\). Thus fact
`8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` gives a compact convex polytope whose
integer-coordinate slices are exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\).

Apply the affine shear
\[
(t,x)\longmapsto
\left(t,x-p_0-\frac t2(p_2-p_0)\right).
\]
It maps the three slice centroids to \((i,0)\), acts by a translation on
every slice \(\{t=i\}\), and maps halfspaces bijectively to halfspaces.
Hence it preserves \(\mu\) and all halfspace depths. It therefore suffices
to treat \(A_i=\lambda_i\Delta\).

Every planar halfplane through the centroid of a triangle contains at least
\(4/9\) of its area. Indeed, if the ordered values of a defining linear
functional at the vertices are \(r_1\le r_2\le r_3\), then
\(r_1+r_2+r_3=0\). When the selected side is the corner at \(r_3\), its
relative area is
\[
\frac{r_3^2}{(r_3-r_1)(r_3-r_2)}
=\frac1{(1+a)(1+b)}
=\frac1{2+ab}\ge\frac49,
\]
where \(a=-r_1/r_3\), \(b=-r_2/r_3\), \(a+b=1\), and
\(ab\le1/4\). In the other case, the selected side is the complement of a
corner of relative area at most \(1/2\), so it also has relative area at
least \(4/9\).

Fact `400272f6de6c67bb` allows the depth at each fixed slice centroid to
be evaluated using only halfspaces whose boundary passes through that
centroid. Consequently,
\[
h_S((0,0))\ge\frac{4D\lambda_0^2}{9},
\qquad
h_S((2,0))\ge\frac{4D\lambda_2^2}{9},
\]
because the corresponding slice section is a planar halfplane through the
triangle centroid, or the whole slice.

If \(\lambda_0^2\ge\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2\), then
\[
h_S((0,0))\ge\frac{4D\lambda_0^2}{9}
\ge\frac{2D(\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
The analogous conclusion holds at \((2,0)\) if
\(\lambda_2^2\ge\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2\).

Suppose neither inequality holds. Consider any
boundary-through-\((1,0)\) halfspace, written as
\[
H=\{(t,x):\ell(x)+v(t-1)\ge0\}.
\]
Its middle-slice section contains at least \(4D\lambda_1^2/9\). If
\(v\ge0\), its top-slice section
\(\{\ell(x)\ge-v\}\cap\lambda_2\Delta\) contains the centroid halfplane
\(\{\ell(x)\ge0\}\cap\lambda_2\Delta\). Hence
\[
\mu(S\cap H)\ge\frac{4D(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)}9
\ge\frac{2T}{9},
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to
\(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2\ge\lambda_0^2\). If \(v\le0\), the
bottom-slice section contains
\(\{\ell(x)\ge0\}\cap\lambda_0\Delta\), and similarly
\[
\mu(S\cap H)\ge\frac{4D(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_0^2)}9
\ge\frac{2T}{9},
\]
using \(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_0^2\ge\lambda_2^2\). Therefore
\(h_S((1,0))\ge2T/9\), completing the proof.
