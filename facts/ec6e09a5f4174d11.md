## Statement

Let \(\Delta\) be a fixed unit-area triangle. For each \(n\), let the interiors of the proper closed halfplanes \(L_{1,n},L_{2,n},L_{3,n}\) form a minimal cover of \(\mathbb R^2\), put
\[
p_{i,n}=|\Delta\cap L_{i,n}|,\qquad
\eta_n=\max_i p_{i,n}-\frac49,
\]
and let \(e_{i,n}\geq0\) and \(E_n=\max_i e_{i,n}\). Assume
\(\eta_n\to0\) and \(E_n\to0\). Then, along the set of indices for which
\[
D_n:=
\frac49+\frac13\sum_i e_{i,n}
-\max_i(p_{i,n}+e_{i,n})
>0,
\]
one has
\[
\frac{D_n}{\eta_n+E_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
In fact, there is \(n_0\) such that every \(n\geq n_0\) with \(D_n>0\) satisfies
\[
0<\frac{D_n}{\eta_n+E_n}\leq51\sqrt{\eta_n}.
\tag{1}
\]
The denominator is never zero: every strict cover as above has
\(\eta_n>0\).

This is the first-order stability companion to the exact edge-frame inequality in accepted fact `79125fb2149eb8c2`. It is compatible with the counterexample in accepted fact `82eb4941d401611c`: that example has a second-order deficit, while its cap displacement and \(\eta_n\) are first order.

## Proof

Write the vertices of \(\Delta\) as \(v_1,v_2,v_3\), and let \(z\) be its centroid.

First we record the elementary centroid cap inequality, including its equality case. If a closed halfplane \(H\) contains \(z\), then
\[
|\Delta\cap H|\geq\frac49.
\tag{2}
\]
If equality holds, then, for exactly one \(i\),
\[
H=\{\beta_i\geq1/3\},
\tag{3}
\]
where \(\beta_i\) is the barycentric coordinate at \(v_i\).

To prove this, suppose first that \(H\) contains only \(v_i\) strictly. Scale an affine defining function \(g\) so that
\[
g(v_i)=1,\qquad g(v_j)=-a,\qquad g(v_k)=-b,
\qquad a,b\geq0.
\]
The condition \(z\in H\) is \(a+b\leq1\), and the two edge fractions of the corner cap are \(1/(1+a)\) and \(1/(1+b)\). Hence
\[
|\Delta\cap H|
=\frac1{(1+a)(1+b)}
\geq\frac1{(1+(a+b)/2)^2}
\geq\frac49.
\]
Equality forces \(a=b=1/2\), which is (3). If \(H\) contains two vertices, apply the same calculation to the complementary one-vertex cap. The condition \(z\in H\) makes the two corresponding nonnegative parameters have sum at least \(1\), so that complementary cap has area at most \(1/2\). Thus \(H\) has area at least \(1/2\). The cases in which a vertex lies on the cutting line follow by allowing \(a=0\) or \(b=0\), and a halfplane containing all three vertices contains all of \(\Delta\). This proves (2) and its equality statement. The same proof shows that if \(z\in\operatorname{int}H\), then
\[
|\Delta\cap H|>\frac49.
\tag{4}
\]

We next prove the qualitative normalization needed below. Consider any sequence of the stated minimal covers for which
\(\max_i p_{i,n}\to4/9\). Since the three interiors cover \(\Delta\),
\[
\sum_i p_{i,n}\geq1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
p_{i,n}\geq1-2\max_jp_{j,n}=\frac19-o(1)
\tag{5}
\]
for every \(i\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), every cap area is therefore bounded away from both \(0\) and \(1\).

Represent each oriented halfplane as
\[
L_{i,n}=\{x:u_{i,n}\cdot x\geq s_{i,n}\},
\qquad |u_{i,n}|=1.
\]
By (5), its boundary line meets the fixed compact triangle. Thus the \(s_{i,n}\) are uniformly bounded. From any subsequence one may extract a further subsequence on which all three pairs \((u_{i,n},s_{i,n})\) converge to proper oriented halfplanes \(L_i^\infty\). Area is continuous under this convergence because a line has planar measure zero, so
\[
|\Delta\cap L_i^\infty|\leq\frac49.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
L_1^\infty\cup L_2^\infty\cup L_3^\infty=\mathbb R^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, a point strictly outside all three limiting closed halfplanes would be outside all three \(L_{i,n}\) for large \(n\), contradicting the cover by their interiors.

By (7), one limiting halfplane contains \(z\). Equations (2) and (6) make it an equality halfplane, so after relabeling it is
\(\{\beta_1\geq1/3\}\). Approach \(z\) through points with
\(\beta_1<1/3\). By (7), one of the other two limiting halfplanes contains infinitely many such points and hence contains \(z\). Equations (2) and (6) make it another equality halfplane, distinct from the first. Relabel it as
\(\{\beta_2\geq1/3\}\). Finally approach \(z\) through points with
\(\beta_1<1/3\) and \(\beta_2<1/3\). These points must lie in the third limiting halfplane, so the third equality case is
\(\{\beta_3\geq1/3\}\).

Every convergent subsequence has therefore reached, up to a permutation, exactly the three halfplanes in (3). It follows that the distance of the original triple from the finite set of their permutations tends to zero. Otherwise a subsequence at positive distance would have a limiting triple just classified. Hence, after a permutation that may depend on \(n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(L_{i,n}\cap\Delta\) is the corner triangle at \(v_i\), its other two vertices lie strictly outside \(L_{i,n}\), and the normalized affine defining functions can be written cyclically as
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &v_1&v_2&v_3\\ \hline
g_1&1&-a_1&-b_1\\
g_2&-b_2&1&-a_2\\
g_3&-a_3&-b_3&1
\end{array}
\qquad(a_i,b_i>0),
\tag{8}
\]
where
\[
a_i\longrightarrow\frac12,\qquad
b_i\longrightarrow\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
This also rules out cutting lines through vertices, parallel limits, and escaping limits.

We now prove an explicit finite-frame estimate. Suppress \(n\), assume (8), and set
\[
t_i=1-a_i-b_i,\qquad d_i=a_i-b_i,\qquad
r=\max_i\{|t_i|,|d_i|\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Assume \(r\leq1/10\). The cap area formula gives
\[
p_i=\frac1{(1+a_i)(1+b_i)}
=\frac4{(3-t_i)^2-d_i^2}
=\frac4{9-k_i},
\qquad
k_i:=6t_i-t_i^2+d_i^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(k\mapsto4/(9-k)\) is increasing, if
\[
\eta=\max_i p_i-\frac49,\qquad K=\max_i k_i,
\]
then
\[
K=\frac{81\eta}{4+9\eta}\leq\frac{81}{4}\eta.
\tag{12}
\]

The minimal strict cover supplies positive Farkas weights
\(\lambda_i>0\) and a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\sum_i\lambda_i g_i(x)=c\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^2).
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, infeasibility of \(g_i(x)\leq0\) gives nonnegative weights with a positive constant sum. A zero weight would give a certificate that the remaining two interiors already cover the plane, contrary to minimality.

Let \(G=(g_i(v_j))_{i,j}\). Then \(G=I-B\) for a nonnegative matrix \(B\), and (13) gives
\[
G^{\mathsf T}\lambda=c\mathbf1>0.
\]
Thus \(B^{\mathsf T}\lambda<\lambda\) coordinatewise. The weighted sup norm gives \(\rho(B)<1\), so \(I-sB\) is nonsingular for \(0\leq s\leq1\). Continuity from \(\det I=1\) yields
\[
\det G>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Direct expansion of the determinant in (8) and (10) gives
\[
4\det G=3(t_1+t_2+t_3)+R,
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
R={}&d_1d_2t_3+d_1d_3t_2+d_2d_3t_1\\
&+d_1(t_2-t_3)+d_2(t_3-t_1)+d_3(t_1-t_2)\\
&+t_1t_2t_3-2(t_1t_2+t_1t_3+t_2t_3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Put
\[
P=\sum_i\max\{t_i,0\},\qquad
N=\sum_i\max\{-t_i,0\},\qquad
S=P+N=\sum_i|t_i|.
\]
Every term in (16) contains a \(t_i\), and the displayed grouping gives
\[
|R|\leq(4r+2r^2)S.
\tag{17}
\]
For example, the six \(d_it_j\) terms contribute at most \(2rS\), the three quadratic \(t_it_j\) terms at most \(2rS\), and the two kinds of cubic terms together at most \(2r^2S\).

If \(t_i>0\), then (11) gives
\[
k_i\geq(6-r)t_i.
\]
Together with \(k_i\leq K\), this implies
\[
P\leq\frac{3K}{6-r}.
\tag{18}
\]
By (14), (15), and (17),
\[
N-P<\frac{|R|}{3}\leq\alpha(P+N),
\qquad
\alpha:=\frac{4r+2r^2}{3}\leq\frac7{50}.
\]
Therefore
\[
S<\frac{2P}{1-\alpha}
\leq\frac{6K}{(6-r)(1-\alpha)}
<\frac65K.
\tag{19}
\]
Also \(\alpha\leq7r/5\), so (14), (15), and (19) give
\[
\sum_i t_i>-\alpha S>-\frac{42}{25}rK.
\tag{20}
\]
Using (11), (19), and (20),
\[
\sum_i k_i
=6\sum_i t_i-\sum_i t_i^2+\sum_i d_i^2
>-\frac{252}{25}rK-rS
>-\frac{282}{25}rK.
\tag{21}
\]

For each \(i\), (11) also gives the exact nonnegative remainder identity
\[
p_i-\frac49
=\frac{4k_i}{81}
+\frac{4k_i^2}{81(9-k_i)}
\geq\frac{4k_i}{81}.
\tag{22}
\]
Summing (22), then using (12) and (21), proves the finite-frame estimate
\[
\frac43-\sum_i p_i
<\frac{282}{25}r\eta.
\tag{23}
\]
The same estimates remove \(r\) from the right side. From (11) and (19),
\[
d_i^2
=k_i-6t_i+t_i^2
\leq K+(6+r)|t_i|
<9K.
\]
Also \(|t_i|\leq S<6K/5\), and \(r\leq1/10\) implies \(K<1\). Hence
\[
r<3\sqrt K\leq\frac{27}{2}\sqrt\eta.
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (23) and (24) gives
\[
\frac43-\sum_i p_i<153\,\eta^{3/2}.
\tag{25}
\]

By (9), the normalized frame radius \(r_n\) in (10) tends to zero. Thus (25) applies for every sufficiently large \(n\). It remains only to insert the bonuses. Since
\[
\max_i(p_{i,n}+e_{i,n})\geq p_{i,n}+e_{i,n}
\]
for every \(i\), summing these three inequalities gives, whenever \(D_n>0\),
\[
3D_n
=\frac43+\sum_i e_{i,n}
-3\max_i(p_{i,n}+e_{i,n})
\leq\frac43-\sum_i p_{i,n}.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (25) and (26) yield
\[
0<D_n<51\,\eta_n^{3/2}.
\]
Since \(\eta_n+E_n\geq\eta_n\), this proves (1).

Finally, the centroid \(z\) belongs to the interior of at least one member of every strict cover. Equation (4) therefore gives
\(\max_i p_{i,n}>4/9\), so \(\eta_n>0\) for every \(n\). Thus no denominator in (1) vanishes. If there are only finitely many positive-numerator indices, the asserted restricted limit is vacuous; otherwise (1) and \(\eta_n\to0\) prove it. The quadratic failure exhibited in accepted fact `82eb4941d401611c` is precisely allowed by (23): the determinant remainder \(R\) is bilinear to first nonzero order in cap offset and shear, whereas the normalized first-order deficit is bounded by \(r_n\to0\). Accepted fact `79125fb2149eb8c2` is recovered at the limiting edge frame.
