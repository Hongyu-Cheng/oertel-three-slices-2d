## Statement

Let \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) be nonconstant affine coordinates
on a plane such that
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1,
\qquad
\Delta=\{\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\geq0\},
\qquad
r=|\Delta|>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(Q,K,P\) be compact convex sets, with \(Q,K\) two-dimensional and
\(P\) of positive area \(p\).  Assume
\[
B_2\subseteq Q\subseteq2B_2,
\qquad
\Delta\subseteq K,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(B_2\) is the Euclidean unit disk.  Let
\(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\in\mathbb R\) satisfy
\[
P\subseteq\{\lambda_i<\beta_i\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For each \(i\), let \(C_i\subseteq Q\) be a compact convex set such that
\[
C_i\subseteq\{\lambda_i\leq-\beta_i\},
\qquad
|C_i|=\frac5{36}|Q|,
\qquad
\frac{P+C_i}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
a_i=-\min_K\lambda_i,\qquad
A=a_1+a_2+a_3,\qquad
k=|K|,
\qquad
\delta_i=-\beta_i-\min_{C_i}\lambda_i,
\tag{5}
\]
and let \(g_i\) be the linear part of \(\lambda_i\).  Then
\(a_i\geq0\), \(\delta_i>0\), and
\[
\lambda_i(x)\geq\beta_i+\delta_i-2a_i
\qquad(x\in P).
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\delta_i\geq\frac{5|Q|}{144}|g_i|
\geq\frac{5\pi}{144}|g_i|
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\qquad}
\tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
u_i=2a_i-\delta_i,
\qquad
s=\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3-1.
\tag{8}
\]
Positive area of \(P\) forces \(u_i>0\) and
\(0<s<u_1+u_2+u_3\).  For \(t_+=\max\{t,0\}\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_u(s)
={}&s_+^2-\sum_i(s-u_i)_+^2
+\sum_{i<j}(s-u_i-u_j)_+^2\\
&-(s-u_1-u_2-u_3)_+^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\qquad p\leq r\,\Phi_u(s).\qquad}
\tag{10}
\]

For any nonnegative vector \(v=(v_1,v_2,v_3)\), write
\[
v_{(1)}\leq v_{(2)}\leq v_{(3)},
\qquad V=v_1+v_2+v_3,
\]
let \(\Phi_v\) denote (9) with \(u\) replaced by \(v\), and define
\[
\Psi(v)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{V^2}{2}-\sum_i v_i^2,
 &v_{(3)}\leq v_{(1)}+v_{(2)},\\[2mm]
2v_{(1)}v_{(2)},
 &v_{(3)}\geq v_{(1)}+v_{(2)}.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
The formulas agree on their common boundary, and
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\max_{t\in\mathbb R}\Phi_v(t)=\Psi(v)
\leq\frac{V^2}{6}.
\qquad}
\tag{12}
\]
Equality in the last inequality holds exactly when
\(v_1=v_2=v_3\), including \(v=0\).  In particular,
\[
p\leq r\,\Psi(u_1,u_2,u_3).
\tag{13}
\]

Define
\[
\bar u_i=2a_i-\frac{5|Q|}{144}|g_i|.
\tag{14}
\]
Then \(0<u_i\leq\bar u_i<2a_i\), and
\[
\boxed{\qquad
p\leq r\,\Psi(\bar u_1,\bar u_2,\bar u_3)
<r\,\Psi(2a_1,2a_2,2a_3).
\qquad}
\tag{15}
\]

Finally, define
\[
\Omega(a_1,a_2,a_3)=\Psi(2a_1,2a_2,2a_3).
\]
After ordering \(a_{(1)}\leq a_{(2)}\leq a_{(3)}\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\Omega(a_1,a_2,a_3)=
\begin{cases}
2A^2-4(a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2),
 &a_{(3)}\leq a_{(1)}+a_{(2)},\\[1mm]
8a_{(1)}a_{(2)},
 &a_{(3)}\geq a_{(1)}+a_{(2)},
\end{cases}\\
\Omega(a_1,a_2,a_3)\leq\frac23A^2,\\
p\geq k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k\leq p<r\,\Omega(a_1,a_2,a_3)
\leq\frac23rA^2.
\end{gathered}}
\tag{16}
\]
The two formulas for \(\Omega\) agree on their common boundary.

## Proof

Fix \(i\), and choose \(q_i\in C_i\) minimizing \(\lambda_i\).  For
every \(x\in P\), (4) and the definition of \(a_i\) give
\[
-a_i
\leq\lambda_i\left(\frac{x+q_i}{2}\right)
=\frac{\lambda_i(x)-\beta_i-\delta_i}{2}.
\]
This proves (6).  Since \(C_i\) has positive area and is contained in
\(\{\lambda_i\leq-\beta_i\}\), its minimum is strictly below
\(-\beta_i\), so \(\delta_i>0\).

The set \(C_i\) lies in the strip between
\[
\lambda_i=-\beta_i-\delta_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda_i=-\beta_i.
\]
This strip has Euclidean width \(\delta_i/|g_i|\).  The orthogonal
projection of \(C_i\) parallel to the strip has length at most
\(\operatorname{diam}(Q)\leq4\).  Hence
\[
|C_i|\leq\frac{4\delta_i}{|g_i|}.
\]
Using \(|C_i|=5|Q|/36\) and \(|Q|\geq\pi\) proves (7).

For \(x\in P\), set
\[
x_i=\beta_i-\lambda_i(x).
\]
Equations (3) and (6) give
\[
0<x_i\leq u_i,
\qquad
x_1+x_2+x_3=s.
\]
Thus positive area forces each \(u_i>0\) and
\(0<s<\sum_i u_i\).

The unrestricted simplex
\[
\{x_i\geq0,\ x_1+x_2+x_3=s\}
\]
has area \(rs^2\), because it is affinely similar to \(\Delta\) with
ratio \(s\).  Apply inclusion-exclusion to the three violations
\(x_i>u_i\).  A single violation has area
\(r(s-u_i)_+^2\); double and triple violations give the remaining
terms in (9).  Therefore the box section in (17) has area
\(r\Phi_u(s)\).  Since it contains \(P\), (10) follows.

It remains to maximize \(\Phi_v\).  Put
\(v_{(1)}\leq v_{(2)}\leq v_{(3)}\) and \(V=\sum_i v_i\).
The map \(x_i\mapsto v_i-x_i\) sends the section at level \(t\) to the
section at level \(V-t\), so
\[
\Phi_v(t)=\Phi_v(V-t).
\]

Suppose first that \(v_{(3)}\geq V/2\).  For
\(0\leq t\leq V/2\), the derivative of \(\Phi_v/2\) is
\[
t-(t-v_{(1)})_+-(t-v_{(2)})_+
+(t-v_{(1)}-v_{(2)})_+.
\]
It is nonnegative and becomes zero at
\(t=v_{(1)}+v_{(2)}\).  By this symmetry, the maximum is attained for every
\[
v_{(1)}+v_{(2)}\leq t\leq v_{(3)},
\]
and direct substitution gives
\[
\max_t\Phi_v(t)=2v_{(1)}v_{(2)}.
\]

Suppose instead that \(v_{(3)}\leq V/2\).  For
\(0\leq t\leq V/2\), no double-violation term is active, and the
derivative of \(\Phi_v/2\) is
\[
t-\sum_i(t-v_i)_+.
\]
It is nonnegative up to \(V/2\).  Hence the same symmetry places the maximum at
\(t=V/2\), where
\[
\max_t\Phi_v(t)=\frac{V^2}{2}-\sum_i v_i^2.
\]
The two cases prove the exact formula in (11)-(12).

If \(V=0\), then \(v=0\), \(\Psi(v)=0=V^2/6\), and the equality
statement is immediate.  Assume \(V>0\).  In the balanced branch,
\[
\Psi(v)
=\frac{V^2}{6}
-\sum_i\left(v_i-\frac V3\right)^2
\leq\frac{V^2}{6},
\]
with equality exactly when all three coordinates are \(V/3\).  In the
dominant branch,
\[
\Psi(v)=2v_{(1)}v_{(2)}
\leq\frac{(v_{(1)}+v_{(2)})^2}{2}
\leq\frac{V^2}{8}
<\frac{V^2}{6}.
\]
This proves all of (12), and (13) follows from (10).

The explicit formula (11) is nondecreasing in every coordinate.  In
the dominant branch its derivatives in the two smaller positive
coordinates are \(2v_{(2)}\) and \(2v_{(1)}\), while its derivative in
the dominant coordinate is zero.  In the balanced branch its derivative
in coordinate \(i\) is \(V-2v_i\geq0\), and at most one of these three
derivatives can vanish.  The formulas are continuous at the branch
boundary.  By (7) and (14),
\[
0<u_i\leq\bar u_i<2a_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Along the segment from \(\bar u\) to \((2a_1,2a_2,2a_3)\), all three
coordinate velocities are positive.  The preceding derivatives show
that the directional derivative is positive wherever it exists; by
continuity across the finitely many ordering and branch boundaries,
\(\Psi(\bar u)<\Psi(2a_1,2a_2,2a_3)\).  Therefore (13) gives (15).

Substitute \(v_i=2a_i\) into (11).  This gives the two displayed
formulas for \(\Omega\).  Applying (12) with \(V=2A\) gives
\(\Omega\leq2A^2/3\).  Finally, if \(p\geq k\), then (15) gives
\[
k\leq p<r\,\Omega(a_1,a_2,a_3),
\]
which completes (16) and the proof.
