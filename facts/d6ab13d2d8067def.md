## Statement

Let \(C\) and its three polygonal slices \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) satisfy the locked problem, and retain \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), and \(t=2T/9\).

Suppose the attained middle-depth maximum admits the actual active-cut and selected-section objects of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`: a point \(z\in\operatorname{int}A_1\), the value \(d=\max_{w\in A_1}h_S((1,w))\), pairwise nonparallel unit vectors \(q_j\), slopes \(\lambda_j\), and positive \(\alpha_j\) with \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\) and \(\sum_j\alpha_jq_j=0\), such that each halfspace
\[
\widehat H_j=\{(h,w):q_j\cdot(w-z)+\lambda_j(h-1)\le0\}
\qquad (j=1,2,3)
\]
has mass \(d\). Suppose its selected outer sections have \(\ell_1=\ell_2=\ell_3=0\), with exactly the section-selection convention and simultaneous moment balance of that accepted fact. In particular, a transverse outer cut uses its full positive-length chord, and zero selected length forces the midpoint of the full middle chord to be \(z\). Then some \(y\in S\) has \(h_S(y)\ge2T/9\).

The following planar inequality is proved as part of the theorem. Let \(K\) be a compact two-dimensional convex polygon, let \(O\in\operatorname{int}K\), and let three pairwise nonparallel lines through \(O\) bound halfplanes \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\). Suppose their defining forms, linear after translation by \(O\) and oriented so each halfplane is the nonpositive side, can be multiplied by positive constants to sum to zero. Suppose \(O\) bisects each full chord of \(K\) on these lines. Let \(s_j\) be the area belonging only to \(Q_j\) and let \(D_j\) be the area of the opposite sector, belonging to the other two halfplanes. Cutting lines have zero area. Then
\[
D_i\ge \min_{j=1,2,3}(s_j-D_j)
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(D_j\) denotes a sector area, and \(d\) remains the attained mixed-integer depth. The planar assertion does not assume that \(O\) is a centroid.

## Proof

We first prove (1), including collinear configurations of the six chord endpoints.

Translate \(O\) to zero. Choose the two boundary rays of the singleton sector indexed by 3 and make an invertible linear coordinate change sending their chord endpoints to \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Because the corresponding full chords are bisected at zero, their opposite endpoints are \((-1,0)\) and \((0,-1)\). The endpoints on the third line can be written as \((\xi,-\eta)\) and \((-\xi,\eta)\), where \(\xi,\eta>0\). In these coordinates the halfplanes are
\[
Q_1=\{x\le0\},\qquad
Q_2=\{y\le0\},\qquad
Q_3=\{\eta x+\xi y\ge0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
All areas in this coordinate calculation change by the same positive factor, so the desired inequality is unaffected.

The six radial endpoints occur in the cyclic order
\[
(1,0),\ (0,1),\ (-\xi,\eta),\ (-1,0),\ (0,-1),\ (\xi,-\eta).
\tag{3}
\]
Each is on the boundary of \(K\). Nonnegative consecutive turning determinants in (3) give
\[
\xi+\eta\ge1,\qquad
\xi-\eta\le1,\qquad
\eta-\xi\le1.
\tag{4}
\]
These conditions can also be checked directly. If \(\xi+\eta<1\), the third endpoint lies in the interior of the diamond with vertices \((\pm1,0),(0,\pm1)\), contrary to its being a radial endpoint. The turns at \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) give the other two inequalities.

Suppose first that equality holds in at least one inequality in (4). Then one point in (3) lies in the relative interior of the segment joining its two neighbors, and its antipodal point has the same property. For example, \(\xi+\eta=1\) places \((-\xi,\eta)\) between \((-1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), while \(\xi-\eta=1\) places \((1,0)\) between \((\xi,-\eta)\) and \((0,1)\). Since this intervening point is on the boundary of \(K\), the line through those three points supports \(K\): any supporting functional at the intervening point must also have equality at both endpoints of the segment. The antipodal segment likewise lies on a supporting line.

The corresponding two boundary arcs are therefore straight. More explicitly, in the cone spanned by either segment's endpoint rays, convexity puts the triangle formed by zero and that segment inside \(K\), while the supporting line puts all of \(K\) in that cone inside the same triangle. Each of these two antipodal cones consists of two consecutive fan sectors. The four sectors include two complete opposite singleton/doubleton pairs; each such pair has equal area by reflection of the two straight-edged triangles. Thus two of the numbers \(s_j-D_j\) are zero. Their minimum is at most zero, and (1) follows from \(D_i\ge0\). This covers every equality case in (4).

It remains to assume all three inequalities in (4) are strict. Consider the halfplanes retained when each doubleton boundary arc is cut off along the segment joining its two radial endpoints. Their intersection is
\[
P=\left\{(x,y):
\begin{aligned}
&x+y\ge-1,\\
&\eta x+(\xi-1)y\le\eta,\\
&(\eta-1)x+\xi y\le\xi
\end{aligned}
\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
We justify both the boundedness of \(P\) and the precise area comparison with \(K\).

The outward normals in (5), in the order of its last two inequalities and then its first, are \((\eta,\xi-1)\), \((\eta-1,\xi)\), and \((-1,-1)\). They have the strictly positive dependence with respective coefficients
\[
1+\xi-\eta,\qquad 1+\eta-\xi,\qquad \xi+\eta-1.
\]
Two of these normals are independent. A recession direction of (5) would have nonpositive pairing with every normal; the positive dependence forces all three pairings to be zero, so that direction must be zero. Thus \(P\) is bounded. It contains the central hexagon which is the convex hull of (3), and zero is interior to that hexagon. Consequently \(P\) is a nondegenerate triangle.

For the area comparison, use the following elementary fact about adjacent radial endpoints \(p\) and \(q\) of a convex body containing zero in its interior. The part of the body strictly beyond the line \(pq\), on the side not containing zero, lies inside the open cone spanned by \(p\) and \(q\). To prove it, use coordinates \(p=(1,0)\), \(q=(0,1)\). If a point \(X=(x,y)\) of the body satisfies \(x+y>1\) and \(x\le0\), the segment from \((1,0)\) to \(X\) meets the positive \(y\)-axis at height \(y/(1-x)>1\), contradicting that \(q\) is the radial endpoint. The case \(y\le0\) is symmetric. Thus any point beyond \(pq\) has \(x,y>0\), as claimed.

Apply this fact to each of the three doubleton endpoint segments in (5). Every singleton sector of \(K\) lies in all three retained halfplanes. Therefore its area in \(P\) is at least its area in \(K\). Conversely, in each doubleton cone, its own inequality in (5) confines \(P\) to the triangle formed by zero and the two corresponding endpoints. That triangle belongs to the central hexagon and hence to \(P\). Thus the doubleton sector of \(P\) is exactly this triangle. It has area at most the corresponding doubleton sector of \(K\).

Write \(s_j(P),D_j(P)\) for the sector areas of \(P\). The preceding argument proves, separately for every \(j\),
\[
s_j(K)\le s_j(P),\qquad
D_j(K)\ge D_j(P),\qquad
s_j(K)-D_j(K)\le s_j(P)-D_j(P).
\tag{6}
\]
The argument has not asserted \(K\subseteq P\). The only parts removed from \(K\) are in doubleton sectors, and the only comparisons needed are exactly (6).

The six original radial endpoints remain the endpoints of the chords of \(P\). Indeed, \(P\) contains their central hexagon; each endpoint lies on one of the three lines in (5), and extension beyond that endpoint on its ray violates that line's inequality. In particular all three chords of \(P\) are bisected by zero. Its three vertices lie respectively in the three singleton sectors. This follows directly from (5) and the strict inequalities (4); for clarity the vertices with these labels are
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_1&=\left(-\frac{2\xi}{1+\xi-\eta},
\frac{\xi+\eta-1}{1+\xi-\eta}\right),\\
V_2&=\left(\frac{\xi+\eta-1}{1+\eta-\xi},
-\frac{2\eta}{1+\eta-\xi}\right),\\
V_3&=\left(\frac{\xi(1+\eta-\xi)}{\xi+\eta-1},
\frac{\eta(1+\xi-\eta)}{\xi+\eta-1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3\) be the barycentric coordinates of zero in this triangle. They are positive and sum to one. The chord bounding the cap at \(V_j\) has endpoints on the two sides incident to \(V_j\). Its midpoint is zero, so its endpoints have the other barycentric coordinates \(2\theta_k\) and \(2\theta_l\). These endpoints lie strictly inside their respective sides. It follows that \(\theta_i<1/2\) for every \(i\). The triangular cap at \(V_j\) consequently has area \(4\theta_k\theta_l|P|\).

The doubleton sector opposite \(V_j\) has its base on the side \(V_kV_l\). The two ends of that base have separation, as a fraction of the side, \(2\theta_k+2\theta_l-1=1-2\theta_j\). Its altitude from zero is \(\theta_j\) times the altitude from \(V_j\). Hence
\[
D_j(P)=\theta_j(1-2\theta_j)|P|.
\tag{8}
\]
In any concurrent three-halfplane fan, the exact sector partition gives
\(s_j-D_j=|P|-|P\cap Q_k|-|P\cap Q_l|\).
Using the cap formula just obtained gives
\[
s_j(P)-D_j(P)
=\bigl(1-4\theta_j(1-\theta_j)\bigr)|P|
=(1-2\theta_j)^2|P|.
\tag{9}
\]
Put \(\varepsilon_j=1-2\theta_j\). These three numbers are positive and sum to one. For every \(i\), equation (8) now yields
\[
D_i(P)
=\frac{|P|}{2}\varepsilon_i
\sum_{j\ne i}\varepsilon_j
\ge |P|\bigl(\min_j\varepsilon_j\bigr)^2
=\min_j\bigl(s_j(P)-D_j(P)\bigr).
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (6) and (10), for each \(i\),
\[
D_i(K)\ge D_i(P)
\ge\min_j\bigl(s_j(P)-D_j(P)\bigr)
\ge\min_j\bigl(s_j(K)-D_j(K)\bigr).
\]
This proves (1) for every convex polygon in the stated planar setting.

We now apply this inequality to the three-slice problem. Suppose, for contradiction, that no point of \(S\) has depth at least \(t\). Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` then gives in particular
\[
0<m\le M,\qquad M-m<a_1<M+m.
\tag{11}
\]
The attained middle maximum satisfies \(d<t\). Moreover (11) implies
\(t=2(M+m+a_1)/9<4(M+m)/9\le8M/9<M\).

By the precise selected-section conclusion of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`, \(\ell_j=0\) rules out a transverse boundary line on either endpoint body, because its selected segment would then be a full positive-length chord. Each endpoint contribution in every active row is therefore either zero or the full endpoint area. A full contribution \(M\) is impossible because the total row mass is \(d<t<M\). Thus each row's total endpoint payment, denoted by \(e_j\), belongs to \(\{0,m\}\). The same accepted fact's moment balance says that each full middle chord is bisected by \(z\).

Set
\[
g_j(w)=\alpha_j q_j\cdot(w-z),\qquad
Q_j=\{w:g_j(w)\le0\},\qquad
c_j=|A_1\cap Q_j|=d-e_j.
\tag{12}
\]
These are the concurrent forms with \(\sum_jg_j=0\); they are not the shifted covering forms with sum \(-1\). The quantity \(c_j\) is a cap area, while \(b_j\) from the active-cut notation remains a chord length.

Let \(B=(A_0+A_2)/2\). Convexity of \(C\) gives \(B\subseteq A_1\). If \(e_j=0\), both endpoint bodies lie in the closed complements of that row's endpoint halfplanes. Indeed, a point on the strictly selected side would, by convexity and two-dimensionality, give a positive-area selected part. Averaging the two complementary endpoint inequalities gives \(g_j\ge0\) on \(B\). If all three payments vanished, \(\sum_jg_j=0\) would put \(B\) in the common zero of the three forms, namely the singleton \(\{z\}\). This is impossible because \(B\) is two-dimensional. There is therefore at least one payment \(m\).

The inequality of accepted fact `851e063a1b9908a3`, applied after translating \(z\) to zero, with body \(A_1-z\) and forms \(x\mapsto g_j(z+x)=\alpha_jq_j\cdot x\), is
\[
5(c_1+c_2+c_3)-6\min_j c_j\ge4a_1.
\tag{13}
\]
In this application its linear forms \(\ell_i\) are the translated \(g_j\) of (12), and its cap areas \(b_i\) are the \(c_j\) of (12), not the canonical chord lengths \(b_j\).

If all three payments are \(m\), (13) gives \(9d\ge4a_1+9m\). By (11),
\(4a_1+9m-2T=2(a_1+m-M)+5m>0\), contradicting \(9d<9t=2T\).

If exactly two payments are \(m\), relabel so the payment vector is \((0,m,m)\). The middle cap vector is \((d,d-m,d-m)\), and (13) gives \(9d\ge4a_1+4m\). Again (11) gives \(4(a_1+m)>2T\), a contradiction.

The remaining pattern, after relabeling, is \((e_1,e_2,e_3)=(0,0,m)\). Its middle cap vector is \((d,d,d-m)\). Let \(s_j\) and \(D_j\) now be the singleton and doubleton sector areas of \(A_1\) for (12). The exact sector partition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_1-D_1&=a_1-2d+m,\\
s_2-D_2&=a_1-2d+m,\\
s_3-D_3&=a_1-2d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
All three middle chords are bisected by \(z\), so (1) applies and gives \(D_1,D_2\ge a_1-2d\). This remains a valid inequality if the right side is nonpositive. Since \(c_3=s_3+D_1+D_2=d-m\), we obtain
\[
s_3\le 5d-2a_1-m.
\tag{15}
\]

The two zero-payment rows put \(B\) in \(\{g_1\ge0,g_2\ge0\}\). Since \(\sum_jg_j=0\), this is the closure of singleton sector 3. Consequently \(|B|\le s_3\) and also \(|B|\le c_3=d-m\).

For the needed lower bound on \(|B|\), apply accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` to the three sections \(A_0,B,A_2\). They are realized by the convex hull of \(\{0\}\times A_0\) and \(\{2\}\times A_2\): its height-one section is exactly \(B\), its endpoint sections are \(A_0,A_2\), and it has no other nonempty integer-height section. All three sections are two-dimensional polygons. The necessity direction of that accepted fact therefore gives
\[
\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{4}
\le |B|
\le\min\{d-m,\ 5d-2a_1-m\}.
\tag{16}
\]

Use \(d<t\) in the first upper bound of (16), and then use \(a_1<M+m\) from (11). After multiplication by 36 and rearrangement this gives
\[
29m+18\sqrt{Mm}<7M.
\tag{17}
\]
Explicitly, the intermediate upper bound is
\(d-m<t-m=(2M+2a_1-7m)/9<(4M-5m)/9\).

Use \(d<t\) in the second upper bound of (16), and then use \(a_1>M-m\). The corresponding calculation gives
\[
M+18\sqrt{Mm}<27m.
\tag{18}
\]
Here the intermediate upper bound is
\(5d-2a_1-m<5t-2a_1-m=(10M+m-8a_1)/9<(2M+9m)/9\).

Set \(x=\sqrt{m/M}>0\). Inequalities (17)-(18) say
\[
29x^2+18x<7,
\qquad
1+18x<27x^2.
\tag{19}
\]
The first implies \(x^2<7/29<4/9\), so \(x<2/3\) and therefore \(27x^2<18x\). This contradicts the second inequality. The one-paid pattern is impossible as well.

Every possible zero-section payment pattern has now been excluded under global failure. Hence a triple satisfying the stated all-zero active-section hypothesis has a point of depth at least \(2T/9\), as claimed.
