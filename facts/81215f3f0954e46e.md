## Statement

Use the common-plane exact-cell convention of accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`.  Put
\[
\pi_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,
\]
and let \(\gamma>0\) be the inverse area Jacobian of the affine
coordinates
\[
x=\pi_1,\qquad y=\pi_2,\qquad
\pi_3=\delta-x-y.
\]
Let \(p_J=|P_J|\) and \(q_J=|Q_J|\).  Suppose
\[
p_{12}p_{13}p_{23}q_1q_3q_{12}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal T_\delta
=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(\delta,0),(0,\delta)\}
\subseteq (\pi_1,\pi_2)(A_0)
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
p_0=\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
\alpha=\frac{p_1}{p_0},\qquad
\beta=\frac{p_2}{p_0}.
\]
If \(\alpha\beta<1\), every such convex endpoint satisfies the exact
profile inequality
\[
\boxed{\quad
p_{12}\le
2p_0\,
\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}
     {(1-\alpha\beta)^2}.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
In the symmetric case \(p_1=p_2=s<p_0\), this becomes
\[
p_{12}\le
2p_0\left(\frac{s}{p_0-s}\right)^2.
\tag{5}
\]

For the proposed table in accepted fact `38b9d85f6c0c57a2`,
\[
p_0=\frac{997}{1000},\qquad
p_1=p_2=p_{12}=\frac1{2000}.
\tag{6}
\]
All six factors in (1) are positive for that table.
Equations (5)-(6) would require
\[
p_{12}\le
\frac{997}{500\cdot1993^2}
<\frac1{2000},
\tag{7}
\]
a contradiction.  Consequently the four exact-cell tables in that
fact have no simultaneous planar realization.  This conclusion
holds for arbitrary convex bodies, so it also holds for every
finite-dimensional polygonal profile ansatz and after adding all
midpoint sup-convolution inequalities.

## Proof

Condition (1) and the barycentric containment proved in accepted fact
`cc8c0bf6093b9357` give (2).  The exact cell \(P_0\) is the interior of
\(\mathcal T_\delta\), up to its three null boundary lines, so (3)
follows.

Let
\[
C=(\pi_1,\pi_2)(A_0).
\]
Take an arbitrary point of the closed \(P_{12}\) chamber of \(C\) and
write it as
\[
z=(-a,-b),\qquad a,b\geq0.
\tag{8}
\]
By (2) and convexity,
\[
\Delta_z
=\operatorname{conv}\{z,(\delta,0),(0,\delta)\}
\subseteq C.
\tag{9}
\]
The part of \(\Delta_z\) in the \(P_1\) chamber contains, up to null
boundaries, the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),(0,\delta),
\left(-\frac{a\delta}{\delta+b},0\right)
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Its coordinate area is
\[
\frac{a\delta^2}{2(\delta+b)}.
\tag{11}
\]
Similarly, the part in the \(P_2\) chamber contains the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),(\delta,0),
\left(0,-\frac{b\delta}{\delta+a}\right)
\right\},
\tag{12}
\]
whose coordinate area is
\[
\frac{b\delta^2}{2(\delta+a)}.
\tag{13}
\]
These two triangles are the minimal chord interpolants between the
section through \(z\) and the two sides of
\(\mathcal T_\delta\).  Equivalently, (11) and (13) are the pointwise
lower envelopes forced by concavity of the corresponding section
profiles.

Multiplying (11) and (13) by \(\gamma\), then using (3), gives
\[
p_1\geq p_0\frac{a}{\delta+b},
\qquad
p_2\geq p_0\frac{b}{\delta+a}.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence
\[
a\leq\alpha(\delta+b),
\qquad
b\leq\beta(\delta+a).
\tag{15}
\]
Substitution in (15), using \(\alpha\beta<1\), yields
\[
a\leq
\frac{\alpha\delta(1+\beta)}{1-\alpha\beta},
\qquad
b\leq
\frac{\beta\delta(1+\alpha)}{1-\alpha\beta}.
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(z\) in (8) was arbitrary, the whole \(P_{12}\) cell lies in
the rectangle determined by the two bounds in (16).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{12}
&\leq
\gamma\delta^2
\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}
     {(1-\alpha\beta)^2}\\
&=
2p_0
\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}
     {(1-\alpha\beta)^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (4).  If \(\alpha=\beta=s/p_0\), cancellation of
\((1+\alpha)^2\) against
\((1-\alpha^2)^2\) gives (5).

For (6),
\[
\alpha=\beta=\frac1{1994}.
\tag{17}
\]
In coordinate area, (16) confines \(P_{12}\) to the square
\[
\left[-\frac{10}{1993},0\right]^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus (5) gives the first inequality in (7).  The exact gap is
\[
\frac1{2000}
-\frac{997}{500\cdot1993^2}
=\frac{3968061}{7944098000}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
This contradicts the requested value of \(p_{12}\), proving
nonrealizability.

SageMath \(10.8\) over \(\mathbb Q\) independently checked
\[
\frac{(1/2000)}{997/1000}=\frac1{1994},
\qquad
\frac{10/1994}{1-1/1994}=\frac{10}{1993},
\]
and the rational identity and strict sign in (19).  No numerical
feasibility decision is used.
