## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(0,0),
\left(\frac32,0\right),\left(0,\frac{121}{100}\right)\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(0,0),
\left(\frac4{13},0\right),\left(0,\frac{13}{20}\right)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\right)
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), write
\[
H_i=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:(i,z)\in H\},
\qquad
\mu(H)=\sum_{i=0}^2|A_i\cap H_i|,
\tag{5}
\]
and define
\[
h_S(p)=\inf\{\mu(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }p\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then \(C\) has exactly the three integer-height sections \(A_0,A_1,A_2\).
Moreover,
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad |A_1|=\frac{363}{400},\qquad
|A_2|=\frac1{10},
\]
\[
T:=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{803}{400},
\qquad
t:=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{803}{1800},
\tag{7}
\]
and, at the middle centroid
\[
g_1=\left(\frac12,\frac{121}{300}\right),
\tag{8}
\]
one has the strict quantitative bound
\[
\boxed{
h_S((1,g_1))
\geq t+\frac1{1600}
=\frac{6433}{14400}>t.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently no family of strict-low halfspaces
\(\{H:\mu(H)<t\}\), of any cardinality, can cover
\(\{1\}\times A_1\) by its interiors.

In contrast, the two endpoint slices have exact independent strict-low
interior covers: three rational halfspaces suffice for
\(\{0\}\times A_0\), and one rational halfspace suffices for
\(\{2\}\times A_2\).

## Proof

The areas in (7) follow directly from the base-height formula.  The exact
midpoint core is
\[
B:=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),\left(\frac{15}{13},0\right),
\left(1,\frac{13}{40}\right),
\left(0,\frac{33}{40}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The nontrivial defining inequality of \(A_1\) is
\[
\frac23x+\frac{100}{121}y\leq1.
\tag{11}
\]
Its values at the three nonzero vertices in (10) are, respectively,
\[
\frac{10}{13},\qquad \frac{679}{726},\qquad \frac{15}{22},
\tag{12}
\]
all below one.  Hence \(B\subseteq A_1\).  The section of (4) at height
one is
\[
\operatorname{conv}(A_1\cup B)=A_1.
\]
The endpoint sections are \(A_0,A_2\), and the height projection of \(C\)
is \([0,2]\).  This proves the asserted compatibility and identifies
\(S\) with the three slices used in (5).

Apply the invertible rational affine map
\[
\Phi(x,y)=\left(\frac23x,\frac{100}{121}y\right),
\qquad
\delta:=\det\Phi=\frac{200}{363}.
\tag{13}
\]
Writing hats for transformed sets, one obtains
\[
\widehat A_1
=\Delta:=\operatorname{conv}\{O=(0,0),X=(1,0),Y=(0,1)\},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\widehat A_0
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
O,\left(\frac43,0\right),\left(0,\frac{100}{121}\right)\right\},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\widehat A_2
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
O,\left(\frac8{39},0\right),\left(0,\frac{65}{121}\right)\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Their endpoint areas and the transformed target are
\[
|\widehat A_0|=\frac{200}{363},\qquad
|\widehat A_2|=\frac{20}{363},\qquad
\widehat t:=\delta t=\frac{73}{297}.
\tag{17}
\]
Put
\[
\widehat S=\{(i,\Phi z):(i,z)\in S\}.
\]
The point \(g_1\) maps to the centroid
\[
c=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right)
\tag{18}
\]
of \(\Delta\).  The affine map
\((h,z)\mapsto(h,\Phi z)\) bijects closed halfspaces and multiplies every
slice area by \(\delta\).  Thus it is enough to prove
\[
h_{\widehat S}((1,c))
\geq\widehat t+\frac1{2904},
\tag{19}
\]
because
\[
\frac{1/2904}{\delta}=\frac1{1600}.
\tag{20}
\]

Fix a closed halfspace \(\widehat H\) containing \((1,c)\).  The
whole-space case is immediate.  Otherwise, move its
boundary parallel toward \((1,c)\) until the boundary passes through that
point.  The resulting halfspace is contained in \(\widehat H\), so its
mass is no larger.  We prove the desired lower bound for this translated
halfspace.

If its planar linear part is zero, then its height-one section is the whole
plane.  This includes the two proper slice-parallel halfspaces through
height one.  Hence its middle
contribution is
\[
|\Delta|=\frac12>\widehat t+\frac1{2904}.
\tag{21}
\]
Assume from now on that the planar linear part is nonzero.

The boundary line of the middle section passes through \(c\).  If the
chosen middle halfplane contains at least two vertices of \(\Delta\), its
area is at least \(1/4\).  Indeed, the complementary one-vertex cap through
the centroid has at most half of the area of \(\Delta\).  Therefore
\[
\mu(\widehat H)\geq\frac14
=\widehat t+\frac5{1188}
>\widehat t+\frac1{2904}.
\tag{22}
\]

It remains to treat a middle cap containing exactly one vertex.  Relabel
the vertices so that the contained vertex is first.  After adding a
constant to its affine score and multiplying by a positive scalar, the
three vertex scores are
\[
(1,0,\lambda),\qquad -1<\lambda<\frac12.
\tag{23}
\]
The score at the centroid, and hence the boundary threshold, is
\((1+\lambda)/3\).  Along the two edges from the contained vertex, the
boundary occurs at fractions
\[
\frac{2-\lambda}{3},
\qquad
\frac{2-\lambda}{3(1-\lambda)}
\tag{24}
\]
from that vertex.  Thus the cap occupies the fraction
\[
f(\lambda)=\frac{(2-\lambda)^2}{9(1-\lambda)}
\tag{25}
\]
of \(\Delta\), and its absolute area is \(f(\lambda)/2\).  Since
\[
f(\lambda)-\frac49
=\frac{\lambda^2}{9(1-\lambda)}\geq0,
\tag{26}
\]
every one-vertex middle cap contributes at least \(2/9\).  Also
\[
\frac12-f(\lambda)
=\frac{(1+\lambda)(1-2\lambda)}
{18(1-\lambda)}\geq0,
\tag{27}
\]
which justifies the two-vertex estimate used in (22), including its
limiting boundary incidences.

Put
\[
e:=\widehat t-\frac29=\frac7{297}.
\tag{28}
\]
We next obtain endpoint contribution at least
\(e+1/2904\) in all but one residual direction cone.

Let \(L\) be the normalized middle score, and put
\[
m_2=\min_{\widehat A_2}L,\qquad
R=2L(c)-m_2.
\tag{29}
\]
Write the halfspace as
\[
L(w)+\alpha(h-1)\geq L(c).
\tag{30}
\]
Its height-zero and height-two score thresholds add to \(2L(c)\).  If its
height-two section contains all of \(\widehat A_2\), then its endpoint
contribution is at least
\[
|\widehat A_2|=\frac{20}{363}
=e+\frac1{2904}+\frac{815}{26136}.
\tag{31}
\]
Otherwise its height-two threshold is greater than \(m_2\), so its
height-zero threshold is strictly below \(R\).  Consequently, any
\(D\subseteq\widehat A_0\) satisfying \(L\geq R\) is contained in the
height-zero section.

Use the \(5/24\)-homothetic vertex corners
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_X&=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac43,0\right),\left(\frac{19}{18},0\right),
\left(\frac{19}{18},\frac{125}{726}\right)\right\},\\
D_Y&=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(0,\frac{100}{121}\right),
\left(0,\frac{475}{726}\right),
\left(\frac5{18},\frac{475}{726}\right)\right\},\\
D_O&=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),\left(\frac5{18},0\right),
\left(0,\frac{125}{726}\right)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Each has area
\[
|D_X|=|D_Y|=|D_O|
=\frac{200}{363}\left(\frac5{24}\right)^2
=\frac{625}{26136}
=e+\frac1{2904}.
\tag{33}
\]

For a cap containing \(X\), take
\[
L_X(u,v)=u+\lambda v.
\tag{34}
\]
If \(0\leq\lambda<1/2\), then \(m_2=0\), and direct evaluation on
\(D_X\) gives
\[
\min_{D_X}L_X-R
=\frac7{18}-\frac23\lambda
\geq\frac1{18}>0.
\tag{35}
\]
If \(-1<\lambda<0\), then \(m_2=65\lambda/121\), and
\[
\min_{D_X}L_X-R
=\frac7{18}+\frac{31}{726}\lambda
\geq\frac{377}{1089}>0.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus \(D_X\) supplies (33) for every \(X\)-cap direction.

For a cap containing \(O\), take
\[
L_O(u,v)=1+(\lambda-1)u-v.
\tag{37}
\]
Throughout \(-1<\lambda<1/2\), the minimum score on
\(\widehat A_2\) is
\[
m_2=\frac{56}{121}.
\]
The two potentially minimizing vertices of \(D_O\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_O(5/18,0)-R
&=\frac1{18}-\frac7{18}\lambda+\frac{56}{121}
\geq\frac{1411}{4356},\\
L_O(0,125/726)-R
&=\frac{601}{726}-\frac{2(1+\lambda)}3
+\frac{56}{121}
\geq\frac{211}{726}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
Hence \(D_O\) supplies (33) for every \(O\)-cap direction.

For a cap containing \(Y\), take
\[
L_Y(u,v)=\lambda u+v.
\tag{39}
\]
When \(-1<\lambda\leq-1/14\), one has
\[
m_2=\frac8{39}\lambda
\]
and
\[
\min_{D_Y}L_Y-R
=-\frac3{242}-\frac{43}{234}\lambda
\geq\frac{289}{396396}>0.
\tag{40}
\]
Thus \(D_Y\) supplies (33) in this range.

Equations (31), (33), and (35)--(40) show that all \(X\)-caps, all
\(O\)-caps, and all \(Y\)-caps with
\(\lambda\leq-1/14\) have endpoint contribution at least
\[
e+\frac1{2904}.
\tag{41}
\]
Combining (41) with (26) gives total mass at least the right side of
(19).

It remains to handle \(Y\)-caps with
\[
-\frac1{14}\leq\lambda<\frac12.
\tag{42}
\]
Let \(r_i\) be the score threshold at height \(i\).  Equation (30) gives
\[
r_0+r_2=\frac{2(1+\lambda)}3.
\tag{43}
\]
The top \(Y\)-scores on the endpoint triangles are
\[
B_0=\frac{100}{121},\qquad
B_2=\frac{65}{121}.
\tag{44}
\]
If an endpoint section is full, its contribution is at least
\(20/363\), so (26) and (31) already give the uniform bound (19).
Suppose neither endpoint section is full.  Define
\[
d_i=(B_i-r_i)_+,
\qquad
W_i=B_i-\min_{\widehat A_i}L_Y.
\tag{45}
\]
The endpoint cap contains the homothetic copy about its top \(Y\)-vertex
with scale \(d_i/W_i\) whenever \(d_i>0\).  Indeed, the minimum score on
that copy is \(B_i-d_i=r_i\).  If \(d_i=0\), the following area bound is
trivial.  Therefore
\[
|\widehat A_i\cap\{L_Y\geq r_i\}|
\geq |\widehat A_i|\frac{d_i^2}{W_i^2}.
\tag{46}
\]
Moreover,
\[
d_0+d_2
\geq C(\lambda):=
B_0+B_2-\frac{2(1+\lambda)}3.
\tag{47}
\]
Weighted Cauchy applied to (46) yields
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{end}}(\widehat H)
\geq
\frac{C(\lambda)^2}
{W_0^2/|\widehat A_0|+W_2^2/|\widehat A_2|}.
\tag{48}
\]

First suppose
\[
-\frac1{14}\leq\lambda\leq0.
\]
Then
\[
C(\lambda)\geq\frac{253}{363}>\frac23,
\tag{49}
\]
and
\[
W_0=\frac{100}{121}-\frac43\lambda<1,\qquad
W_2=\frac{65}{121}-\frac8{39}\lambda<\frac35.
\tag{50}
\]
The two strict width bounds follow at \(\lambda=-1/14\) from
\[
1-\left(\frac{100}{121}+\frac2{21}\right)
=\frac{199}{2541}>0,
\]
\[
\frac35-\left(\frac{65}{121}+\frac4{273}\right)
=\frac{7954}{165165}>0.
\]
Hence the denominator in (48) is less than
\[
\frac{363}{200}
+\frac9{25}\frac{363}{20}
=\frac{8349}{1000}<9.
\]
It follows that
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{end}}(\widehat H)>\frac4{81}
=e+\frac{23}{891}.
\tag{51}
\]
Together with (26), this is strictly above \(\widehat t\).

Now suppose
\[
0\leq\lambda<\frac12.
\]
In this range \(W_0=B_0\) and \(W_2=B_2\), so the right side of (48)
simplifies exactly to
\[
E_+(\lambda)
=\frac{176}{2565}
\left(\lambda-\frac{23}{22}\right)^2.
\tag{52}
\]
On \(0\leq\lambda\leq2/5\), this function is decreasing and
\[
E_+(\lambda)\geq E_+(2/5)
=\frac{20164}{705375}
=e+\frac{3539}{705375}.
\tag{53}
\]
Equations (26) and (53) prove the target on this interval.

On \(2/5\leq\lambda<1/2\), equations (25) and (52) give
\[
\frac{f(\lambda)}2\geq\frac{32}{135},
\qquad
E_+(\lambda)\geq\frac{64}{3135}.
\tag{54}
\]
Their sum exceeds the target by
\[
\frac{32}{135}+\frac{64}{3135}-\frac{73}{297}
=\frac{329}{28215}>0.
\tag{55}
\]
If an endpoint section was full, the earlier alternative has the explicit
margin
\[
\frac29+\frac{20}{363}-\widehat t
=\frac{103}{3267}>0.
\tag{56}
\]
This margin is also greater than \(1/2904\).
For completeness, the margins in (51), (53), (55), (56), and (22),
after subtracting \(1/2904\), are respectively
\[
\frac{1997}{78408},\qquad
\frac{290057}{62073000},\qquad
\frac{28097}{2482920},\qquad
\frac{815}{26136},\qquad
\frac{101}{26136},
\tag{57}
\]
all positive.

Equations (21)--(22), (35)--(41), and (49)--(56) exhaust every
slice-parallel case, every planar direction, and every endpoint allocation.
The smallest uniform margin among these cases is the corner margin in
(33).  Thus (19) holds.  Equations (13) and (20) now give (9).

It remains to verify the asserted endpoint covers.  Put
\[
a=\frac{1997}{6000},\qquad
k=1-a=\frac{4003}{6000}.
\tag{58}
\]
The closed halfspaces
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{00}&=\{(h,x,y):h+x/2+y\leq k\},\\
H_{01}&=\{(h,x,y):h-x/2\leq-a\},\\
H_{02}&=\{(h,x,y):h-y\leq-a\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]
have empty sections on \(A_1\) and \(A_2\).  At height zero their
interiors are the barycentric caps
\[
\lambda_0>a,\qquad \lambda_X>a,\qquad \lambda_Y>a.
\]
They cover \(A_0\), because a point outside all three interiors would
satisfy
\[
1=\lambda_0+\lambda_X+\lambda_Y
\leq3a=\frac{1997}{2000}<1.
\tag{60}
\]
Each row has total mass \(k^2\), and
\[
t-k^2
=\frac{3999}{4000000}>0.
\tag{61}
\]

Finally,
\[
H_{21}=\{(h,x,y):h\geq3/2\}
\tag{62}
\]
has empty height-zero and height-one sections, while its interior contains
the whole height-two slice.  Its mass is \(1/10\), with
\[
t-\frac1{10}=\frac{623}{1800}>0.
\tag{63}
\]
This proves the endpoint-cover assertion and completes the proof.
