## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system and incidence orbit of accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2`, `f8e871d10dac1791`, and `89f1aef98e9b8915`, with \(t\leq 1/2\). Thus, in the notation of `f8e871d10dac1791`, after the allowed initial column choice,
\[
L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),\qquad p,a,b>0,\qquad p-a-b>3,
\]
and
\[
c_3=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}<t,
\qquad
0<c_i<\kappa:=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49
\quad(i=1,2).
\]
Suppose exactly one entry of \(L\) is zero and the other eight entries are nonzero. Up to the allowed interchange of rows \(1,2\) and the allowed interchange of columns \(2,3\), the first two rows of \(L\) occur in exactly one of the following five strata. All displayed letters are strictly positive, and the equations and mean inequalities in the last two columns are part of the stratum.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 &L_{1*}&L_{2*}&\text{column-sum equations}&\text{negative-mean inequalities}\\ \hline
\mathrm A&(-d,x,0)&(-e,-f,y)&d+e=p-1,\ x-f=1+a,\ y=1+b&x<d,\ y<e+f\\
\mathrm B&(-d,x,0)&(-e,y,z)&d+e=p-1,\ x+y=1+a,\ z=1+b&x<d,\ e>y+z\\
\mathrm C&(-d,x,0)&(y,-e,z)&y-d=1-p,\ x-e=1+a,\ z=1+b&x<d,\ e>y+z\\
\mathrm D&(0,x,-d)&(-e,-f,y)&e=p-1,\ x-f=1+a,\ y-d=1+b&x<d,\ y<e+f\\
\mathrm E&(0,x,-d)&(-e,y,z)&e=p-1,\ x+y=1+a,\ z-d=1+b&x<d,\ e>y+z.
\end{array}
\]
The literal middle-cap pairs in these five rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\mathrm A&\displaystyle(c_1,c_2)=\left(\frac{x}{x+d},\frac{y^2}{(y+e)(y+f)}\right)\\[3mm]
\mathrm B&\displaystyle(c_1,c_2)=\left(\frac{x}{x+d},1-\frac{e^2}{(e+y)(e+z)}\right)\\[3mm]
\mathrm C&\displaystyle(c_1,c_2)=\left(\frac{x}{x+d},1-\frac{e^2}{(e+y)(e+z)}\right)\\[3mm]
\mathrm D&\displaystyle(c_1,c_2)=\left(\frac{x}{x+d},\frac{y^2}{(y+e)(y+f)}\right)\\[3mm]
\mathrm E&\displaystyle(c_1,c_2)=\left(\frac{x}{x+d},1-\frac{e^2}{(e+y)(e+z)}\right).
\end{array}
\]
In particular, the boundary-row identity used here is literally
\[
\mathcal C(-d,0,x;0)=\frac{x}{d+x}\qquad(d,x>0).
\]
For every endpoint row, no generic-ordering convention is implicit: the cap is the literal function \(\mathcal C\) of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`. In particular, for sorted values \(\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma\), its repeated-value cases are
\[
\mathcal C(\alpha,\alpha,\gamma;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\leq\alpha,\\
\dfrac{(\gamma-\tau)^2}{(\gamma-\alpha)^2},&\alpha<\tau<\gamma,\\
0,&\tau\geq\gamma,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
\mathcal C(\alpha,\gamma,\gamma;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\leq\alpha,\\
1-\dfrac{(\tau-\alpha)^2}{(\gamma-\alpha)^2},&\alpha<\tau<\gamma,\\
0,&\tau\geq\gamma,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal C(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\leq\alpha,\\
0,&\tau>\alpha.
\end{cases}
\]
At a distinct middle knot \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma\), the literal closed-cap value is
\[
\mathcal C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;\beta)
=\frac{\gamma-\beta}{\gamma-\alpha},
\]
which specializes to the displayed boundary-row identity. With these formulas used in the full row masses
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i),
\]
none of the five strata admits \(M_i<t\) for all \(i=1,2,3\). Hence no full compatible \((U,V,L,\sigma)\) strict counterexample has exactly one zero middle vertex entry.

## Proof

We first derive the finite list. Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` gives the strict sign pattern \(L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b)\), so the unique zero cannot lie in row \(3\). It also gives, for each \(i=1,2\), both \(c_i>0\) and the strict negative row mean
\[
\ell_{i1}+\ell_{i2}+\ell_{i3}<0.
\]
Consequently the row containing the unique zero has one positive and one negative remaining entry, while the other first-row entry vector, all of whose entries are nonzero, has at least one positive and at least one negative entry. The same accepted fact excludes a singleton positive support \(\{1\}\) for either of the first two rows, including the case where one of the other entries is zero.

The column sums of \(L\) give
\[
\ell_{11}+\ell_{21}=1-p<0,
\qquad
\ell_{12}+\ell_{22}=1+a>0,
\qquad
\ell_{13}+\ell_{23}=1+b>0.
\tag{1}
\]
If the zero is in column \(1\), use the row interchange to put it in row \(1\), and use the interchange of columns \(2,3\) to make the positive entry of that row occur in column \(2\). The zero row then has signs \((0,+,-)\). Equation (1) forces the other row to be negative in column \(1\) and positive in column \(3\). Its column-\(2\) sign is either negative or positive, giving exactly strata D and E.

If the zero is in column \(2\) or \(3\), use the column interchange to put it in column \(3\), and use the row interchange to put it in row \(1\). The zero row cannot have singleton support \(\{1\}\), so its signs are \((-,+,0)\). Equation (1) forces the other row to be positive in column \(3\). Since that row must also have a negative entry, its first two signs are exactly
\[
(-,-),\qquad(-,+),\qquad(+,-),
\]
giving A, B, and C. This proves exhaustion.

The five representatives are pairwise inequivalent. Column \(1\) is fixed by the allowed column interchange, so D and E cannot meet A, B, or C. In each of these two groups the number of positive entries in the nonzero row separates D from E and A from B,C when applicable. Finally, in B the positive support of the zero row is contained in the positive support of the nonzero row, whereas in C it is not; this relation is invariant under both allowed interchanges. Thus the list has exactly five orbits.

Writing the signs with positive magnitudes and imposing (1) gives the displayed equations. The displayed mean inequalities are exactly the strict negative row-mean conditions. The cap formulas follow directly from the literal formulas in `64760c76cadff481`: a one-positive row, a two-positive row, and a threshold-hit row respectively satisfy
\[
\mathcal C(-d,x,-e;0)=\frac{x^2}{(x+d)(x+e)},
\]
\[
\mathcal C(-d,x,y;0)=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)},
\]
\[
\mathcal C(-d,0,x;0)
=1-\frac{d^2}{d(d+x)}
=\frac{x}{d+x}.
\tag{2}
\]
Permuting the three arguments does not change \(\mathcal C\), so (2) gives every entry of the cap table. The repeated-value formulas in the statement are also the literal closed-cap formulas from `64760c76cadff481`; in particular, the all-equal case retains the full cap at equality.

We now eliminate the five strata in the full endpoint system. Let the unique zero be \(\ell_{rj}=0\), where \(r\in\{1,2\}\), and let \(s\) be the other element of \(\{1,2\}\). Put
\[
Q=(e_r-e_s)e_j^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\bar\lambda=\frac13(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\mu_0=U\bar\lambda,
\]
and define
\[
L_\varepsilon=L+\varepsilon Q,
\qquad
\sigma_\varepsilon=\sigma-\varepsilon Q\mu_0.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Q=0\), the column sums of \(L_\varepsilon\) remain one and
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma_\varepsilon
=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma=q.
\tag{4}
\]
The matrices \(U,V\), and hence \(A,B,t\), the residual conditions, and the original midpoint compatibility, are unchanged. More explicitly, the perturbed transformed triangles
\[
P_{0,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon U\Delta+\sigma_\varepsilon,\qquad
P_{1,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon\Delta,\qquad
P_{2,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon V\Delta-\sigma_\varepsilon
\]
satisfy
\[
\frac{P_{0,\varepsilon}+P_{2,\varepsilon}}2
=L_\varepsilon\frac{U\Delta+V\Delta}{2}
\subseteq L_\varepsilon\Delta=P_{1,\varepsilon}.
\]
Since `09393c6edf1fc4e2` gives \(\det L\ne0\), also \(\det L_\varepsilon\ne0\) for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). For nonzero sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), the selected zero becomes nonzero and none of the other eight entries crosses zero.

The choice of \(\sigma_\varepsilon\) preserves the entire incidence orbit, including possible equality in the small-endpoint centroid coordinates. Indeed, the transformed endpoint and middle centroids are
\[
g_0=LU\bar\lambda+\sigma,
\qquad
g_1=L\bar\lambda,
\qquad
g_2=LV\bar\lambda-\sigma.
\]
Equation (3) gives
\[
g_{0,\varepsilon}=g_0,
\qquad
g_{1,\varepsilon}=g_1+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}(e_r-e_s),
\]
\[
g_{2,\varepsilon}
=g_2+\varepsilon Q(U\bar\lambda+V\bar\lambda).
\tag{5}
\]
In the incidence orbit of `f8e871d10dac1791`, the first two coordinates of \(g_1\) are strictly negative, its third coordinate is unchanged here, and all three coordinates of \(g_2\) are strictly negative. Thus (5) preserves those signs for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), while \(g_0\) is preserved exactly. The third row of \(L\) and the third coordinate of \(\sigma\) are unchanged. Hence the labeling, \(q>1\), the sets \(I_0,I_1\), and every defining incidence sign remain in the same orbit.

It remains to check that a repeated endpoint value cannot destroy strictness under this perturbation. The literal cap function is upper semicontinuous in all four arguments. To see this without a genericity assumption, if \((z_n,\tau_n)\to(z,\tau)\), then for each \(\lambda\in\Delta\),
\[
\limsup_n
\mathbf1_{\{z_n\cdot\lambda\geq\tau_n\}}
\leq
\mathbf1_{\{z\cdot\lambda\geq\tau\}}.
\]
The reverse Fatou inequality, with the constant integrable majorant \(1\), yields
\[
\limsup_n\mathcal C(z_n;\tau_n)
\leq\mathcal C(z;\tau).
\tag{6}
\]
This includes both repeated values and the constant-at-threshold case in the statement. Every perturbed row mass
\[
M_i(\varepsilon)=
\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon)_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon U)_{i*};-(\sigma_\varepsilon)_i)
+B\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon V)_{i*};(\sigma_\varepsilon)_i)
\]
is therefore upper semicontinuous at \(0\). Since \(M_i(0)<t\) strictly for all three rows, all three inequalities remain strict for every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), on either chosen one-sided perturbation.

Choose the sign of \(\varepsilon\) in (3) according to the following table. The support pair shown is the support pair of the resulting all-nonzero first two rows.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{stratum}&\text{chosen sign}&(J_1(\varepsilon),J_2(\varepsilon))\\ \hline
\mathrm A&\varepsilon<0&(\{2\},\{3\})\\
\mathrm B&\varepsilon>0&(\{2,3\},\{2,3\})\\
\mathrm C&\varepsilon<0&(\{2\},\{1,3\})\\
\mathrm D&\varepsilon<0&(\{2\},\{3\})\\
\mathrm E&\varepsilon>0&(\{1,2\},\{2,3\}).
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
For example, in A the zero at \((1,3)\) is made negative, while in B it is made positive; the compensating change in the other first row is too small to alter its nonzero signs. The same direct reading proves all entries of (7).

Accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915` says that every all-nine-nonzero strict system in this same incidence orbit must, up to precisely the allowed interchanges, have one singleton support contained in the other two-element support. None of the five pairs in (7) belongs to that contained-support orbit: the first and fourth have two distinct singleton supports, the second has equal two-element supports, and the third and fifth have non-contained unequal supports. Thus (3)-(7) turn a hypothetical strict system in each zero stratum into an all-nonzero strict system forbidden by `89f1aef98e9b8915`. This contradiction eliminates A-E and proves the theorem.
