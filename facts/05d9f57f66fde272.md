## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes with
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,
\qquad
k=|K|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9<M.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(j=1,2,3\), put
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|,
\]
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j+|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
     +|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\qquad
I_j=\{a:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Assume
\[
c_j+m\ge t,\qquad
I_j\ne\varnothing,\qquad
L_j=\inf I_j
\quad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{4}
\]
Suppose that row \(q\) is strictly empty on the large endpoint and that the strict-low alternative holds:
\[
L_q<\alpha_q:=\min_{A_0}f_q,
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3<-1.
\tag{5}
\]
Write
\[
\{i,\ell,q\}=\{1,2,3\},
\qquad
\Delta=
\left|\det(\nabla f_i,\nabla f_\ell)\right|>0,
\tag{6}
\]
and use the common affine coordinates
\[
\Phi(x)=(a,b)
=\bigl(f_i(x)-L_i,\ f_\ell(x)-L_\ell\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
Set
\[
P=\Phi(A_0),\qquad Q=\Phi(A_2).
\]
Define the two one-sided \(A_0\) cut-chord lengths
\[
h_i=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\left|\{b:(\varepsilon,b)\in P\}\right|,
\qquad
h_\ell=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\left|\{a:(a,\varepsilon)\in P\}\right|.
\tag{8}
\]
The limits exist for polygons. If a level is strictly below the corresponding \(A_0\)-support, the associated value in (8) is \(0\); at support contact it is the supporting-face length; and for a proper cap it is the ordinary chord length.

Then every canonical finite-boundary configuration satisfies the branch-wide chord product inequality
\[
\boxed{\qquad
h_i h_\ell\le2\Delta m.
\qquad}
\tag{9}
\]
This conclusion includes every allowed \(A_2\)-cap state. For a proper \(A_2\)-cap, the relevant comparison chord is an interior chord. For an empty-full row, accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` puts the full \(A_2\)-cap at upper-support contact, and the comparison chord is the corresponding supporting face, possibly a point.

Now specialize to the shifted-wedge triangle family. Put
\[
D=-1-\alpha_q-L_i-L_\ell>0,
\tag{10}
\]
and suppose that for some \(x,y\ge0\),
\[
P=
\{(a,b):
a\ge-x,\ b\ge-y,\ a+b\le D\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Write
\[
H=D+x+y,\qquad
X=\frac xH,\qquad
Y=\frac yH,\qquad
r=\frac mM.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
M=\frac{H^2}{2\Delta},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
u_i:=|A_0\cap\{f_i\le L_i\}|
=\frac{Hx-x^2/2}{\Delta},
\qquad
u_\ell
=\frac{Hy-y^2/2}{\Delta},
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
h_i=H-x,\qquad h_\ell=H-y.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently the three exact necessary inequalities are
\[
r\ge(1-X)(1-Y),
\qquad
r>2X-X^2,
\qquad
r>2Y-Y^2.
\tag{16}
\]
They imply the shape-independent strict bound
\[
\boxed{\qquad \frac mM>\frac12.\qquad}
\tag{17}
\]

The constant \(1/2\) is sharp for the information in (16): rational \(X,Y\) may approach
\[
1-\frac1{\sqrt2}
\]
and rational \(r\) may approach \(1/2\) from above while all three inequalities remain satisfied. An exact rational calibration with visible slack is
\[
D=4,\qquad x=y=3,\qquad H=10,\qquad
\Delta=1,\qquad M=50,\qquad m=26.
\tag{18}
\]
It has
\[
\frac mM=\frac{13}{25},\qquad
\frac{u_i}{M}=\frac{u_\ell}{M}=\frac{51}{100},
\qquad
\frac{h_i h_\ell}{H^2}=\frac{49}{100}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (16) holds exactly, while accepted fact `7c3b4917f2548179` only requires
\[
\frac mM>
\frac{1-(D/H)^2}{2}
=\frac{21}{50}.
\tag{20}
\]
This calibration is not asserted to have a \(q\)-crossing or a compatible \(K\); it identifies those as the next gates after the branch-wide chord and cap constraints.

## Proof

We first prove (9) without using any unaccepted packet. Keep the affine coordinates (7). Since their absolute Jacobian is \(\Delta\),
\[
|P|=\Delta M,\qquad |Q|=\Delta m.
\tag{21}
\]
For a first-coordinate value \(s\), let
\[
\lambda_{0i}(s)
=\left|\{b:(s,b)\in P\}\right|,
\qquad
\lambda_{2i}(s)
=\left|\{b:(s,b)\in Q\}\right|.
\tag{22}
\]
Define \(\lambda_{0\ell},\lambda_{2\ell}\) analogously using horizontal sections. Section endpoints of a polygon are piecewise affine between consecutive projected vertex values. Hence every section-length function is piecewise affine and continuous on the interior of its projection interval, with finite one-sided limits at its endpoints. At a projection endpoint, the interior limit is exactly the length of the supporting face, possibly zero.

Consider row \(i\). Its \(A_0\)-cap at level \(L_i\) is the part of \(P\) with \(a\le0\). Its \(A_2\)-cap is the part of \(Q\) with
\[
a\le\tau_i,\qquad \tau_i:=-2L_i.
\tag{23}
\]
By accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`, the \(A_0\)-cap is never full and the \(A_2\)-cap is never empty at the lower crossing.

Define
\[
g_i=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\lambda_{2i}(\tau_i-\varepsilon).
\tag{24}
\]
If the \(A_2\)-cap is proper, \(\tau_i\) is interior to its projection interval and \(g_i\) is the ordinary chord length. If it is full, accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` forces the \(A_0\)-cap to be empty and gives
\[
L_i=-\max_{A_2}f_i.
\]
Thus \(\tau_i\) is the upper first-coordinate support of \(Q\), and \(g_i\) is the length of that supporting face. This treats both the proper and empty-full states.

For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the change in the sum of the two endpoint-cap areas when the row level increases from \(L_i\) to \(L_i+\varepsilon\) is
\[
\frac1\Delta
\left(
\int_0^\varepsilon\lambda_{0i}(s)\,ds
-
\int_{\tau_i-\varepsilon}^{\tau_i}
\lambda_{2i}(s)\,ds
\right).
\tag{25}
\]
Accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` gives
\[
\mu_i(L_i)=t.
\]
Because \(L_i=\inf I_i\), there is a sequence
\[
\varepsilon_n\downarrow0
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\mu_i(L_i+\varepsilon_n)<t.
\tag{26}
\]
Divide (25) by \(\varepsilon_n\) and take the limit along this sequence. The definitions (8) and (24) give
\[
h_i-g_i\le0,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
g_i\ge h_i.
\tag{27}
\]
The same argument in row \(\ell\) gives a horizontal chord or supporting face of \(Q\), of length \(g_\ell\), with
\[
g_\ell\ge h_\ell.
\tag{28}
\]

We use the following elementary two-segment lemma. If a convex set in the \((a,b)\)-plane contains a vertical segment of length \(u\) and a horizontal segment of length \(v\), then its area is at least \(uv/2\). Indeed, let \(z\) be the intersection of the two carrying lines. If \(z\) belongs to both segments, their convex hull has area \(uv/2\). If \(z\) misses the vertical segment, one endpoint of that segment has distance at least \(u\) from the horizontal line, so it and the full horizontal segment span a triangle of area at least \(uv/2\). If \(z\) belongs to the vertical segment but misses the horizontal segment, interchange the roles.

The polygon \(Q\) contains the comparison segments of lengths \(g_i,g_\ell\). Its convexity and (27)-(28) therefore give
\[
\Delta m=|Q|
\ge\frac{g_i g_\ell}{2}
\ge\frac{h_i h_\ell}{2}.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (9), including zero supporting faces and both full-cap boundary cases.

We next specialize to (11). The triangle in (11) has vertices
\[
(-x,-y),\qquad
(-x,D+x),\qquad
(D+y,-y),
\]
and side parameter \(H=D+x+y\). Its coordinate area is \(H^2/2\), proving (13). The coordinate area of the cap \(a\le0\) is
\[
\int_{-x}^{0}(D+y-a)\,da
=Hx-\frac{x^2}{2}.
\tag{30}
\]
Division by \(\Delta\) gives the first formula in (14), and the second is symmetric. At \(a=0\), the section interval is
\[
-y\le b\le D,
\]
so its length is \(D+y=H-x\). The horizontal calculation gives \(h_\ell=D+x=H-y\), proving (15).

Accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`, together with the tail inequality in (4), gives at each of the two crossings
\[
u_j<m\qquad(j=i,\ell).
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, if \(v_j=|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-L_j\}|>0\), then the crossing equality and \(c_j+m\ge t\) give
\[
u_j=t-c_j-v_j\le m-v_j<m.
\]
Dividing (14) by (13) yields
\[
\frac{u_i}{M}=2X-X^2,
\qquad
\frac{u_\ell}{M}=2Y-Y^2.
\tag{32}
\]
Similarly, dividing (9), with (13) and (15), by \(H^2\) gives
\[
r\ge(1-X)(1-Y).
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (31)-(33) are exactly (16).

Because \(D>0\),
\[
0\le X<1,\qquad 0\le Y<1,\qquad X+Y<1.
\]
Put
\[
\xi=1-\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]
If \(X\ge\xi\), monotonicity of \(2X-X^2\) on \([0,1]\) gives
\[
r>2X-X^2\ge2\xi-\xi^2=\frac12.
\]
The same conclusion holds if \(Y\ge\xi\). If both \(X<\xi\) and \(Y<\xi\), then
\[
r\ge(1-X)(1-Y)
>
(1-\xi)^2
=\frac12.
\]
This proves (17), with strictness in every case.

To compare with the accepted wedge inequality, note that
\[
\frac DH=1-X-Y>0.
\]
Accepted fact `7c3b4917f2548179` gives
\[
M-\frac{D^2}{2\Delta}<2m.
\]
Using (13) and dividing by \(2M\) gives exactly
\[
r>\frac{1-(D/H)^2}{2},
\]
which is strictly weaker than (17).

Finally, in the rational calibration (18), formulas (13)-(15) give
\[
M=50,\qquad
u_i=u_\ell=\frac{51}{2},\qquad
h_i=h_\ell=7.
\]
Thus \(u_i,u_\ell<m=26\) and
\[
h_i h_\ell=49<52=2m,
\]
while (20) is the exact comparison \(13/25>21/50\). Rational choices \(X,Y\) can approach \(\xi\), and the maximum of the three right sides in (16) then approaches \(1/2\); choosing rational \(r\) just above that maximum proves the stated sharpness for these three constraints.
