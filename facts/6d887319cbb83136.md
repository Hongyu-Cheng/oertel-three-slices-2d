## Statement

Use the common-plane conventions of accepted facts
`2b42506f5de09be1`, `989c9b585d2b42cc`, and
`fa0e57b225178b3b`.  Thus
\[
p_j=X,\qquad p_\ell=Y,\qquad p_k=\delta-X-Y,
\]
coordinate area in \((X,Y)\) is \(\Delta>0\) times physical area,
and the bounded positive endpoint cell
\[
P_0^\circ=\operatorname{int}A_0\cap
\{X>0,\ Y>0,\ X+Y<\delta\}
\]
has physical area \(b\).

For \(A_2\), put
\[
S=\delta+2,\qquad U=-q_k,\qquad V=q_\ell,\qquad
-q_j=S-U+V.
\]
Its two relevant full sections are
\[
V=0,\qquad U\in[u,u+H_2],
\]
\[
q_j=0,\qquad (U,V)=(S-t,-t),
\quad t\in[w_0,w_0+K_2].
\]
Put
\[
\theta=S-u-H_2\ge0.
\]
For \(t\ge0\), define the exact coordinate area
\[
\varphi_\delta(t)
=\frac{\delta^2-(\delta-t)_+^2}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad (r,s)=(f_j,f_\ell).
\]
Then
\[
\left|B\cap\{r\ge0,\ s\ge0\}\right|
\ge
\frac14\max\left\{
\left(b-\frac{\varphi_\delta(w_0)}{\Delta}\right)_+,
\left(b-\frac{\varphi_\delta(\theta)}{\Delta}\right)_+
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every integer \(n\ge1\), one also has
\[
\left|B\cap\{r\ge0,\ s\ge0\}\right|
\ge
\frac{
\bigl(2\Delta b\,n^2-\theta w_0(n+1)^2\bigr)_+
}{
8\Delta n^2(n+1)
}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the supremum of the right side of (3) over
\(n\ge1\) is positive if and only if
\[
2\Delta b>\theta w_0.
\tag{4}
\]
All formulas remain literal when \(b,\theta,w_0\), or a chord length
vanishes.

If a convex middle polygon \(K\) contains \(B\) and its strict cell
\[
R_k=\{r>0,\ s>0,\ f_k<0\}
\]
has area zero, then necessarily
\[
2\Delta b\le\theta w_0,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\Delta b\le\varphi_\delta(w_0),
\qquad
\Delta b\le\varphi_\delta(\theta).
\tag{6}
\]

For the strict rational survivor of accepted fact
`d1a8755c0cc50d14`,
\[
\delta=\Delta=1,\qquad
b=\frac{1622001}{40000000},
\qquad
\theta=\frac{129}{100},
\qquad
w_0=\frac{23}{2000}.
\]
The one-axis bound (2) gives
\[
\left|B\cap\{r\ge0,\ s\ge0\}\right|
\ge\frac{582323}{80000000}>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus that survivor cannot have the prescribed zero \(R_k\)-area.
This is a uniform endpoint-cell proof of the contradiction that
accepted fact `77e4f1bd725f32f9` obtained by clipping the two explicit
decagons.

The lower bound is not automatically positive on the endpoint-cap
region of accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b`.  Its strict rational point
has
\[
\delta=100,\quad \Delta=1,\quad
b=\frac{56501}{400},\quad
\theta=\frac{2029}{20},\quad w_0=50,
\]
and satisfies
\[
2\Delta b\le\theta w_0,\qquad
\Delta b\le\varphi_\delta(w_0),\qquad
\Delta b\le\varphi_\delta(\theta).
\tag{8}
\]
Hence every right side in (2)-(3) is exactly zero there.  The
additional joint invariant missing from the separate endpoint-cap
rows is (5), the dominance-triangle product coupling the \(P_0\) area
to both closest \(A_2\) axis endpoints.  For the later full lens
system, this lemma reduces uniform positivity to excluding its
intersection with (8); the supplied accepted facts do not themselves
give that final parameter elimination.

## Proof

The midpoint constants cancel because
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i.
\]
Thus for an \(A_0\) point with coordinates \((X,Y)\) and an \(A_2\)
point,
\[
2r=p_j+q_j,\qquad 2s=p_\ell+q_\ell.
\tag{9}
\]
This is the parameter form of the midpoint map used in accepted fact
`77e4f1bd725f32f9`.

On the \(A_2\) section \(V=0\), the endpoint \(U=u+H_2\) has
\[
(q_j,q_\ell)=(-\theta,0).
\]
On the section \(q_j=0\), the endpoint \(t=w_0\) has
\[
(q_j,q_\ell)=(0,-w_0).
\]
Both points belong to the compact polygon \(A_2\).  Convexity
therefore gives the whole segment
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}\{(-\theta,0),(0,-w_0)\}\subseteq A_2.
\tag{10}
\]

First prove (2).  The coordinate image of \(P_0^\circ\) lies in
\[
T_\delta=\{(X,Y):X\ge0,\ Y\ge0,\ X+Y\le\delta\}
\]
and has coordinate area \(\Delta b\).  The part of \(T_\delta\) with
\(Y\le t\) has coordinate area exactly
\(\varphi_\delta(t)\).  Hence
\[
\left|
P_0^\circ\cap\{Y\ge w_0\}
\right|_{\rm coord}
\ge
\bigl(\Delta b-\varphi_\delta(w_0)\bigr)_+.
\tag{11}
\]
Adding the point \((q_j,q_\ell)=(0,-w_0)\) and dividing by two maps
this set injectively into \(\{r\ge0,s\ge0\}\).  Coordinate and
physical areas are related by the factor \(\Delta\), and midpoint
scaling multiplies planar area by \(1/4\).  This proves the first term
of (2).  The same argument with
\((q_j,q_\ell)=(-\theta,0)\) and the cap \(X\le\theta\) proves the
second term.

Now fix \(n\ge1\).  For \(i=0,\ldots,n\), convexity and (10) give
\[
q_i=\left(-\frac{i}{n}\theta,
-\left(1-\frac{i}{n}\right)w_0\right)\in A_2.
\tag{12}
\]
Let
\[
E_i=P_0^\circ\cap
\left\{
X\ge\frac{i}{n}\theta,\quad
Y\ge\left(1-\frac{i}{n}\right)w_0
\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
By (9),
\[
\frac{E_i+q_i}{2}
\subseteq B\cap\{r\ge0,s\ge0\},
\]
so
\[
\left|B\cap\{r\ge0,s\ge0\}\right|
\ge\frac{1}{4\Delta}\max_{0\le i\le n}|E_i|_{\rm coord}.
\tag{14}
\]

Assume first \(\theta w_0>0\).  If
\[
\frac{X}{\theta}+\frac{Y}{w_0}>1+\frac1n,
\tag{15}
\]
then \((X,Y)\) belongs to at least one \(E_i\).  Indeed, if
\(X/\theta\ge1\), take \(i=n\); if \(Y/w_0\ge1\), take \(i=0\).
Otherwise the interval
\[
\left[1-\frac{Y}{w_0},\frac{X}{\theta}\right]
\subset(0,1)
\]
has length greater than \(1/n\), so it contains some grid point
\(i/n\).  Thus the complement of \(\bigcup_iE_i\) in
\(P_0^\circ\) is contained in the triangle
\[
\left\{X,Y\ge0:
\frac{X}{\theta}+\frac{Y}{w_0}
\le1+\frac1n\right\},
\]
whose coordinate area is
\[
\frac{\theta w_0}{2}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^2.
\tag{16}
\]
It follows that
\[
\left|\bigcup_{i=0}^nE_i\right|_{\rm coord}
\ge
\left(
\Delta b-
\frac{\theta w_0}{2}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^2
\right)_+.
\tag{17}
\]
Since there are \(n+1\) sets,
\[
\max_i|E_i|_{\rm coord}
\ge\frac1{n+1}
\left|\bigcup_iE_i\right|_{\rm coord}.
\]
Combining this with (14)-(17) gives (3).  If
\(\theta=0\) or \(w_0=0\), one endpoint in (10) is the origin, so
\(P_0^\circ/2\subseteq B\cap\{r\ge0,s\ge0\}\).  Its physical area is
\(b/4\), which is at least the right side of (3).  Hence (3) also
holds on both zero-offset boundaries.

If \(2\Delta b\le\theta w_0\), every numerator in (3) is nonpositive.
If \(2\Delta b>\theta w_0\), then for all sufficiently large integers
\(n\),
\[
2\Delta b\,n^2>\theta w_0(n+1)^2,
\]
so (3) is strictly positive.  This proves (4).  Since
\(f_j+f_k+f_\ell=-1\), every point with \(r,s>0\) has
\(f_k=-1-r-s<0\).  Cutting lines have area zero.  Therefore zero
strict \(R_k\)-area forces every lower bound in (2)-(3) to vanish,
which gives (5)-(6).

For the rational point of accepted fact `d1a8755c0cc50d14`,
\[
\varphi_1\left(\frac{23}{2000}\right)
=\frac{91471}{8000000}.
\]
Substitution in (2) gives
\[
\frac14\left(
\frac{1622001}{40000000}
-\frac{91471}{8000000}
\right)
=\frac{582323}{80000000},
\]
proving (7).  Independently, the \(n=1\) instance of (3) gives
\[
\frac{435201}{320000000}>0.
\]

Finally, at the strict point of accepted fact
`fa0e57b225178b3b`,
\[
\varphi_{100}(50)=3750,\qquad
\varphi_{100}\left(\frac{2029}{20}\right)=5000,
\]
and
\[
2\Delta b=\frac{56501}{200}
<
\frac{10145}{2}=\theta w_0.
\]
This proves (8) and the claimed exact calibration of the remaining
joint obstruction.
