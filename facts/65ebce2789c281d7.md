## Statement

Use the normalized systems and cap notation of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781`, the reduced word automaton of accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b`, and the \(56,274\) full/interior systems surviving accepted facts `ebcc3143a486e8cd` and `e193174c49126e99`.

For every feasible such system,
\[
\sigma_{Q2}\ne\mathrm P\Longrightarrow \beta_R\ge\frac14,
\qquad
\sigma_{Q4}\ne\mathrm P\Longrightarrow \alpha_R\ge\frac14.
\]
Consequently, if \(\eta\in\{1,2\}\), then
\[
\sigma_{Q2}\ne\mathrm P,\qquad
\sigma_{Q4}\ne\mathrm P.
\]
Since \(Q\) lies entirely in \(x>0\), these also imply
\[
\sigma_{Q1}\ne\mathrm N,\qquad
\sigma_{Q3}\ne\mathrm N.
\]

Deleting every current survivor with
\[
\eta\in\{1,2\},
\qquad
\sigma_{Q2}=\mathrm P
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sigma_{Q4}=\mathrm P
\]
preserves the feasibility union. Exactly \(7,248\) systems are deleted, leaving exactly \(49,026\).

## Proof

Write
\[
\alpha_E=\frac{|E\cap\{y\le0\}|}{\Lambda},
\qquad
\beta_E=\frac{|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|}{\Lambda}.
\]
Accepted facts `b046e3066136e781` and `ebcc3143a486e8cd` give
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t,
\]
\[
\alpha_R,\beta_R\ge t-m,
\qquad
t<1,
\]
and
\[
K:=\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R\cap\{x\ge1\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q\le m,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q\le m. \tag{1}
\]

Suppose first that \(\sigma_{Q2}\ne\mathrm P\). Position \(2\) records the endpoint signs of \(-U_Q\). By the exact label convention of accepted fact `b046e3066136e781`, the condition \(\sigma_{Q2}\ne\mathrm P\) means that \(Q\) is not contained in \(\{y\le0\}\), so
\[
\alpha_Q<m.
\]
Also \(\alpha_P\le t<1\). Hence the convex bodies
\[
P^+=P\cap\{y\ge0\},\qquad
Q^+=Q\cap\{y\ge0\}
\]
have positive normalized areas \(1-\alpha_P\) and \(m-\alpha_Q\). Their midpoint sum is contained in \(K\), and every one of its points has \(y\ge0\) and \(x\ge1\). Thus
\[
\frac{P^++Q^+}{2}
\subseteq R\cap\{x+y\ge0\}.
\]
Applying the planar midpoint-area inequality proved in accepted fact `ebcc3143a486e8cd` gives
\[
\beta_R
\ge
\frac{\bigl(\sqrt{1-\alpha_P}
+\sqrt{m-\alpha_Q}\bigr)^2}{4}.
\]
By (1),
\[
(1-\alpha_P)+(m-\alpha_Q)\ge1,
\]
so
\[
\beta_R\ge\frac14. \tag{2}
\]

The symmetric argument starts from \(\sigma_{Q4}\ne\mathrm P\). Then \(\beta_Q<m\), while \(\beta_P<t<1\). The positive-area convex bodies
\[
P^-=P\cap\{x+y\le0\},\qquad
Q^-=Q\cap\{x+y\le0\}
\]
have normalized areas \(1-\beta_P\) and \(m-\beta_Q\). Their midpoint sum lies in \(K\); there \(x\ge1\) and \(x+y\le0\), hence \(y\le0\). Therefore
\[
\frac{P^-+Q^-}{2}
\subseteq R\cap\{y\le0\},
\]
and the same area inequality and (1) yield
\[
\alpha_R
\ge
\frac{\bigl(\sqrt{1-\beta_P}
+\sqrt{m-\beta_Q}\bigr)^2}{4}
\ge\frac14. \tag{3}
\]

Now let \(\eta\in\{1,2\}\). Accepted fact `e193174c49126e99` gives
\[
t-m<\frac{11}{45}<\frac14
\quad(\eta=1),
\qquad
t-m<\frac16<\frac14
\quad(\eta=2), \tag{4}
\]
and has already excluded \(\sigma_Q=\mathrm{PPPP}\).

If \(\sigma_{Q2}=\mathrm P\), then \(\alpha_Q=m\). The first crossing equality and \(\alpha_R\ge t-m\) force
\[
\alpha_P=0,\qquad \alpha_R=t-m. \tag{5}
\]
Simultaneous \(\sigma_{Q4}=\mathrm P\) would give \(\sigma_Q=\mathrm{PPPP}\), using the \(\mathcal B\)-automaton of accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b`, so every current survivor with \(\sigma_{Q2}=\mathrm P\) has \(\sigma_{Q4}\ne\mathrm P\). Equation (3) then contradicts (4) and (5). Hence
\[
\sigma_{Q2}\ne\mathrm P.
\]
The same argument with the two cuts exchanged proves
\[
\sigma_{Q4}\ne\mathrm P.
\]

If \(\sigma_{Q1}=\mathrm N\), position \(1\) forces \(Q\subseteq\{y\ge0\}\). Since the full \(Q\)-tail has \(x\ge D>0\), this forces \(Q\subseteq\{x+y\ge0\}\), and hence \(\sigma_{Q4}=\mathrm P\), already excluded. Likewise, \(\sigma_{Q3}=\mathrm N\) forces \(Q\subseteq\{x+y\le0\}\); because \(x>0\) on \(Q\), this forces \(Q\subseteq\{y\le0\}\), and hence \(\sigma_{Q2}=\mathrm P\). Thus all four stated label exclusions hold.

It remains to count the newly deleted systems. In the \(\mathcal B\)-table of accepted fact `46afc7eb815ffc5b`, exactly \(9\) words have second letter \(\mathrm P\), exactly \(9\) have fourth letter \(\mathrm P\), and their intersection is the single word \(\mathrm{PPPP}\). Accepted fact `e193174c49126e99` has already deleted every \(\mathrm{PPPP}\) pair in branches \(\eta=1,2\). Hence \(16\) \(Q\)-words remain in the new forbidden family.

For each of the \(14\) forbidden words other than
\[
\mathrm{NNPP},\qquad \mathrm{PPNN},
\]
the earlier filter of accepted fact `ebcc3143a486e8cd` allows exactly \(14\) \(P\)-words: \(8\) with the forced half \(\mathrm{NN}\) and \(6\) with it \(\mathrm{PN}\). For \(\mathrm{NNPP}\) and \(\mathrm{PPNN}\), accepted fact `e193174c49126e99` has already removed two of these \(14\) choices, leaving \(12\) each. Thus the new forbidden family contains
\[
14\cdot14+12+12=220 \tag{6}
\]
ordered \((\sigma_P,\sigma_Q)\) pairs.

Exactly \(12\) of these \(220\) pairs use only \(\mathrm N,\mathrm P\). Indeed, the relevant \(\mathrm N,\mathrm P\)-only \(Q\)-words are
\[
\mathrm{NNPP},\quad
\mathrm{PNPP},\quad
\mathrm{PPNN},\quad
\mathrm{PPPN},
\]
and they permit respectively \(2,4,2,4\) \(\mathrm N,\mathrm P\)-only \(P\)-words after the earlier filters.

Accepted fact `ebcc3143a486e8cd` gives \(33\) surviving \(R\)-words, exactly one of which is \(\mathrm N,\mathrm P\)-only. Therefore the number of forbidden mixed twelve-letter vectors is
\[
220\cdot33-12=7248. \tag{7}
\]
The forbidden condition is invariant under the half-swap involution. The four fixed \(\mathcal B\)-words are
\[
\mathrm{PNPN},\quad
\mathrm{PPPP},\quad
\mathrm{PXPX},\quad
\mathrm{PYPY}.
\]
Only \(\mathrm{PPPP}\) has second or fourth letter \(\mathrm P\), and it was already deleted. Hence the new forbidden family has no fixed vector. Burnside’s formula therefore gives
\[
\frac{7248}{2}=3624
\]
newly deleted reduced systems for each of \(\eta=1,2\). The two branches delete
\[
2\cdot3624=7248
\]
systems in total. The branch \(\eta=0\) is unchanged, so the full/interior list now contains
\[
56274-7248=49026
\]
systems.
