## Statement

Assume the affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted
fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Let \(A_2\) be a convex quadrilateral whose four
positive \(Q\)-cells have areas
\[
q_{123}=\frac25|A_2|,
\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15|A_2|.
\]
Put
\[
\ell_i=f_i+L_i,
\qquad
C_i=A_2\cap\{\ell_i\ge0\},
\qquad
\mathcal T_Q=\bigcap_{i=1}^3\{\ell_i\le0\}.
\]
Suppose one of the three positive outer caps strictly contains two adjacent
vertices of \(A_2\), while the other two caps strictly contain one remaining
vertex each. If \(\mathcal T_Q\) is the bounded global central triangle cut
out by the three \(Q\)-lines, then
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|<\frac{77}{5}|A_2|<16|A_2|.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently no area-one quadrilateral of this incidence type can occur in a
realization satisfying the scale conclusion of accepted fact
`b1eaab0ae37367d1`.

## Proof

The exact-cell data first imply the cap geometry used below. Up to the
zero-area cutting lines, \(C_i\) is the union of the exact cells whose index
does not contain \(i\). Among those cells, the only positive one is the
doubleton missing \(i\). Therefore
\[
|C_i|=\frac15|A_2|.
\]
For \(i\ne j\), the relative interiors of \(C_i\) and \(C_j\) cannot meet:
such an intersection would be a nonempty relatively open subset of the
two-dimensional polygon on which both \(\ell_i,\ell_j\) are positive, hence
would have positive area in a singleton or empty-index exact cell. All those
cells have area zero. Thus the three cap interiors are pairwise disjoint.

A cutting line for a positive proper cap cannot coincide with a polygon edge,
since either closed side would then meet the polygon in area zero or in its
full area. It therefore meets the boundary in at most two points. Hence the
vertices strictly contained in any proper halfplane cap form one consecutive
block in cyclic order. Relabeling the quadrilateral and
interchanging the two singleton caps if necessary, the cyclic vertices are
\[
v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,
\]
where \(C_1\) contains \(v_1,v_2\), \(C_2\) contains \(v_3\), and \(C_3\)
contains \(v_4\), all strictly, and no cap strictly contains any other
vertex.

Use barycentric coordinates
\(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) relative to
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_3,v_4\}
\]
and write
\[
v_2=(r,w,-h),
\qquad
r,w,h>0,
\qquad
r+w=1+h.
\]
These signs follow directly from strict convexity: \(v_2\) is on the opposite
side of the diagonal \(v_1v_3\) from \(v_4\), but on the same side as \(v_1\)
of the line \(v_3v_4\) and on the same side as \(v_3\) of the line
\(v_4v_1\). Equivalently, the three barycentric coefficients are the
corresponding signed triangle-area ratios.

The diagonal \(v_1v_3\) partitions \(A_2\) into \(P\) and
\(\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\) with disjoint interiors. The latter
triangle has area \(h|P|\), because its altitude from \(v_2\) to \(v_1v_3\)
is \(h\) times the altitude from \(v_4\). Hence
\[
|A_2|=(1+h)|P|.
\]
Put
\[
p=\frac{1+h}{5}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
|A_2|=5p|P|.
\tag{3}
\]

We now derive all six edge fractions. Let
\[
A_{14}=v_1+a(v_4-v_1),
\qquad
A_{23}=v_2+\kappa(v_3-v_2)
\]
be the two endpoints of the \(C_1\)-chord, where \(a,\kappa\) are measured
from the two strictly capped vertices \(v_1,v_2\). Let
\[
B_{23}=v_3+b(v_2-v_3),
\qquad
B_{34}=v_3+d(v_4-v_3)
\]
be the endpoints of the \(C_2\)-chord, and let
\[
D_{34}=v_4+c(v_3-v_4),
\qquad
D_{14}=v_4+e(v_1-v_4)
\]
be the endpoints of the \(C_3\)-chord. Initially,
\[
a,\kappa,b,d,c,e\in(0,1],
\]
because each line cuts a positive proper cap and may a priori pass through a
non-strictly-contained vertex.

In the affine coordinates \((\alpha_2,\alpha_3)\), the four vertices of
\(C_1\), in cyclic order, are
\[
(0,0),\quad
(w,-h),\quad
((1-\kappa)w+\kappa,-(1-\kappa)h),\quad
(0,a).
\]
The shoelace formula, divided by \(|P|\), gives
\[
\kappa h+a((1-\kappa)w+\kappa)
=aw+\kappa h+a\kappa(1-w).
\]
The cap \(C_2\) is the corner triangle at \(v_3\). Its full corner triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{v_3,v_2,v_4\}\) has area \(r|P|\), so its area is
\(bdr|P|\). Similarly \(C_3\) has area \(ce|P|\). Since every cap has area
\(p|P|\), the three exact area equations are
\[
aw+\kappa h+a\kappa(1-w)=p,
\qquad
bdr=p,
\qquad
ce=p,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\kappa+b\le1,
\qquad
a+e\le1,
\qquad
d+c\le1.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, along \(v_2v_3\) the \(C_1\)-cap occupies the interval of fraction
\(\kappa\) from \(v_2\), while the \(C_2\)-cap occupies the interval of
fraction \(b\) from \(v_3\). If \(\kappa+b>1\), these intervals overlap in a
nontrivial segment, and a one-sided neighborhood inside \(A_2\) has positive
area in both cap interiors. This contradicts their disjointness. The other
two inequalities follow identically on \(v_1v_4\) and \(v_3v_4\).

Because all six fractions are positive, (5) improves every fraction to
strictly less than one. Thus no chord endpoint is a polygon vertex. The
remaining boundary degeneracies are equalities in one or more of the three
sums in (5), when two adjacent chord endpoints coincide at an interior point
of a polygon edge. These are the zero-gap cases retained below.

Define the three boundary gaps
\[
G_{12}=[A_{23},B_{23}],
\qquad
G_{23}=[B_{34},D_{34}],
\qquad
G_{13}=[D_{14},A_{14}],
\tag{5a}
\]
whose relative edge lengths are respectively
\[
g_{12}=1-\kappa-b,
\qquad
g_{23}=1-d-c,
\qquad
g_{13}=1-a-e.
\]
The endpoint order in (5) and convexity show that the three cap chords do
not cross in the interior of \(A_2\). Equivalently, an interior crossing
would create a wedge of positive area lying in both corresponding open cap
halfplanes. Consequently
\[
\mathcal H:=A_2\cap\mathcal T_Q
\]
is the convex central polygon whose boundary, in cyclic order, is
\[
[A_{14},A_{23}],\quad
G_{12},\quad
[B_{23},B_{34}],\quad
G_{23},\quad
[D_{34},D_{14}],\quad
G_{13}.
\]
When all gaps are positive, \(\mathcal H\) is a hexagon. A zero gap merely
identifies the two adjacent listed vertices. Since \(\mathcal H\) is the
exact \(123\)-cell up to cutting-line boundaries,
\[
|\mathcal H|=\frac25|A_2|.
\]

We next justify the global planar partition used at the end of the proof. Let
\[
\Lambda_i=\{\ell_i=0\},
\qquad
X_{ij}=\Lambda_i\cap\Lambda_j.
\]
Because \(\mathcal T_Q\) is a bounded intersection of three halfplanes, the
three lines are distinct and pairwise nonparallel, every halfplane is
nonredundant, and the three \(X_{ij}\) are precisely the vertices of
\(\mathcal T_Q\).

The cyclic boundary order of the convex inclusion
\(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal T_Q\) gives the following exact order on the
three sides of \(\mathcal T_Q\):
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
\Lambda_1:&X_{13},A_{14},A_{23},X_{12},\\
\Lambda_2:&X_{12},B_{23},B_{34},X_{23},\\
\Lambda_3:&X_{23},D_{34},D_{14},X_{13}.
\end{array}
\tag{5b}
\]
Here repeated adjacent entries are allowed only when the corresponding gap
is zero. To see this order without a diagram, traverse the boundary of
\(\mathcal T_Q\) as
\[
X_{12}\longrightarrow X_{23}\longrightarrow X_{13}
\longrightarrow X_{12}.
\]
On each side, its intersection with the full-dimensional convex polygon
\(\mathcal H\) is exactly the cap chord displayed in the boundary list
above. The endpoints of that interval occur in the same order as the two
adjacent sides of \(\mathcal H\), which gives (5b).

This order also locates every pairwise line intersection. Suppose first that
\(g_{ij}>0\). Choose affine coordinates centered at \(X_{ij}\) in which the
two side rays of \(\mathcal T_Q\) on
\(\Lambda_i,\Lambda_j\) are the positive coordinate axes. By (5b), the two
endpoints of \(G_{ij}\) have coordinates \((\lambda,0)\) and \((0,\mu)\)
for some \(\lambda,\mu>0\), and the two adjacent cap-chord segments continue
from these points away from the origin. The line containing the polygon edge
\(G_{ij}\) is
\[
\frac{x}{\lambda}+\frac{y}{\mu}=1.
\]
The adjacent chord segments contain points with coordinates
\((\lambda+\varepsilon,0)\) and \((0,\mu+\varepsilon')\) for some
\(\varepsilon,\varepsilon'>0\). Since \(G_{ij}\) is a supporting side of
the convex polygon \(\mathcal H\), its inward side is therefore
\[
\frac{x}{\lambda}+\frac{y}{\mu}\ge1.
\]
This is also the inward side of the polygon edge of \(A_2\) containing
\(G_{ij}\). Hence \(X_{ij}\) lies strictly outside \(A_2\), across that
edge, and the part of \(\mathcal T_Q\) cut off at this vertex is exactly
\[
E_{ij}=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{X_{ij}\}\cup G_{ij}\bigr).
\]
Indeed, in the displayed coordinates this is the triangle
\[
x\ge0,\qquad y\ge0,\qquad
\frac{x}{\lambda}+\frac{y}{\mu}\le1.
\]

For positive gaps, insert the three noncrossing segments
\[
G_{12},\qquad G_{23},\qquad G_{13}
\]
into \(\mathcal T_Q\). They are sides of the convex polygon \(\mathcal H\),
so their relative interiors are disjoint. By (5b), they join consecutive
pairs of sides of \(\mathcal T_Q\), and the remaining portions of those
sides are, in cyclic order,
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
[X_{12},B_{23}],&[B_{23},B_{34}],&[B_{34},X_{23}],\\
[X_{23},D_{34}],&[D_{34},D_{14}],&[D_{14},X_{13}],\\
[X_{13},A_{14}],&[A_{14},A_{23}],&[A_{23},X_{12}].
\end{array}
\]
Thus these three segments form a planar subdivision of
\(\mathcal T_Q\): its central face is precisely \(\mathcal H\), and its
three corner faces are \(E_{12},E_{23},E_{13}\). Consequently
\[
\mathcal T_Q
=
\mathcal H\ \cup\ E_{12}\ \cup\ E_{23}\ \cup\ E_{13},
\qquad
\text{with pairwise disjoint interiors}.
\tag{5c}
\]
If \(g_{ij}=0\), the two chord lines share the common gap endpoint, so
\(X_{ij}\) is that endpoint and \(E_{ij}\) is a zero-area triangle. Thus
(5c) remains valid after contracting any zero gap to its common endpoint.
This treats one, two, or all three zero gaps directly.

The remaining proof bounds these three exterior triangles.

Set
\[
s=bd.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (2), (4), and \(r+w=1+h\) give
\[
h=5p-1,
\qquad
r=\frac ps,
\qquad
w=p\left(5-\frac1s\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(h,w>0\),
\[
p>\frac15,
\qquad
s>\frac15.
\tag{8}
\]
Also
\[
e=\frac pc,
\qquad
b=\frac sd.
\tag{9}
\]
The edge-order constraints imply
\[
p<c,
\qquad
s<d,
\qquad
p+s<c+d\le1.
\tag{10}
\]

We first record the common inequality behind all three exterior-triangle
bounds. Define
\[
\sigma=p+s-5ps.
\tag{11}
\]
For fixed \(h,w\), let
\[
F(A,K)=Aw+Kh+AK(1-w).
\]
On \(0<A,K<1\),
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial A}=(1-K)w+K>0,
\]
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial K}
=h+A(1-w)
=Ar+h(1-A)>0.
\]
By (5) and (9),
\[
a\le1-\frac pc,
\qquad
\kappa\le1-\frac sd.
\]
Using (4) and substituting these two upper endpoints gives the exact identity
\[
F\left(1-\frac pc,1-\frac sd\right)-p
=\frac{p}{cd}\bigl(\sigma+4cd-c-d\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sigma+4cd-c-d\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(c+d\le1\),
\[
4cd\le(c+d)^2\le c+d,
\]
so (13) implies \(\sigma\ge0\). On the other hand,
\[
1-5\sigma=(5p-1)(5s-1)>0
\]
by (8). Hence
\[
0\le\sigma<\frac15.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (13)-(14) yields
\[
c+d-4cd\le\frac15.
\]
If \(S=c+d\), then \(4cd\le S^2\), and consequently
\[
S^2-S+\frac15\ge0.
\]
Since \(S>p+s>2/5\), the relevant side of this quadratic inequality is
\[
c+d\ge\frac{5+\sqrt5}{10}.
\tag{15}
\]

We will use the following elementary corner-cut formula. In a reference
triangle, suppose two adjacent corner cuts have fractions \(C,D\) and
\(E,F\), with \(D,F\) measured on their shared edge and
\[
g=1-D-F\ge0.
\]
If their two extended cutting lines meet on the far side of that edge, then
the opposite barycentric coordinate of their intersection is
\[
-\frac{g}{D/C+F/E-1}.
\]
The triangle between that intersection and the shared-edge gap therefore has
area, relative to the reference triangle,
\[
\frac{g^2}{D/C+F/E-1}.
\tag{16}
\]
This follows by substituting the two line equations
\[
\frac{\alpha_i}{C}+\frac{\alpha_k}{D}=1,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha_i}{E}+\frac{\alpha_j}{F}=1
\]
and then multiplying the base fraction \(g\) by the relative altitude.
The denominator in (16) is positive because the actual central chamber is
bounded. A zero gap follows by continuity and contributes zero area.

Consider first the exterior triangle at the intersection of the
\(C_2\)- and \(C_3\)-lines, across the edge \(v_3v_4\). Relative to \(P\),
the two corner cuts have fractions
\[
C=\frac{p}{d+bh},
\qquad
D=d,
\qquad
E=\frac pc,
\qquad
F=c.
\]
Thus (16) gives
\[
\frac{|E_{23}|}{|P|}
=
\frac{(1-c-d)^2}
{d(d+bh)/p+c^2/p-1}.
\tag{17}
\]
Using \(bd=s\), \(h=5p-1\), and (11), the denominator is
\[
\frac{c^2+d^2-\sigma}{p}.
\tag{18}
\]
By (14)-(15),
\[
c^2+d^2-\sigma
\ge\frac12\left(\frac{5+\sqrt5}{10}\right)^2-\frac15
=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{20}.
\]
Moreover \(p<c<4/5\), since \(d>s>1/5\). Hence the denominator in (17)
is strictly larger than
\[
\frac{\sqrt5-1}{16}.
\tag{19}
\]
At the same time,
\[
(1-c-d)^2
\le\left(\frac{5-\sqrt5}{10}\right)^2
=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{10}.
\tag{20}
\]
The quantity in (19) is larger than the quantity in (20), because this
comparison is equivalent to \(13\sqrt5>29\). Therefore
\[
|E_{23}|<|P|.
\tag{21}
\]

We next prove the bound used for either exterior triangle adjacent to the
double-vertex cap. The first \(Q\)-line has equation
\[
U\alpha_2+\frac{\alpha_3}{a}=1,
\qquad
U=
\frac{1+(1-\kappa)h/a}{(1-\kappa)w+\kappa}.
\tag{22}
\]
Put
\[
X=aU.
\tag{23}
\]
Eliminating \(w,\kappa\) from (4), (7), and (22) gives
\[
X=
\frac{N}{p(1-4\sigma)},
\tag{24}
\]
where
\[
N=
\sigma a^2
+2s(5p-1)a
+s(5p-1)(4p-1).
\tag{25}
\]
For completeness, the edge constraint \(\kappa+b\le1\) gives a second exact
inequality needed below. Solving (4) for \(\kappa\) gives
\[
1-\kappa
=
\frac{a+4p-1}{\,5p-1+a(1-w)\,}.
\]
Since \(1-\kappa\ge b=s/d\), and since
\[
s(1-w)=s-5ps+p=\sigma,
\]
we obtain
\[
a(d-\sigma)
\ge s(5p-1)-d(4p-1).
\tag{26}
\]
Notice that
\[
d-\sigma=d-s+sw>0.
\tag{27}
\]

We claim
\[
3N\ge(1-4\sigma)(1-d)^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Here is an exact algebraic proof. Normalize
\[
P_0=5p-1,
\qquad
Q_0=5s-1,
\qquad
R_0=5d-1.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (8), (10), and (14) become
\[
P_0,Q_0>0,
\qquad
P_0Q_0\le1,
\qquad
Q_0<R_0<3-P_0.
\tag{30}
\]
Also
\[
\sigma=\frac{1-P_0Q_0}{5}.
\]
Define
\[
J=5P_0Q_0-4P_0R_0+P_0+R_0+1
\tag{31}
\]
and
\[
W=
5(1-P_0Q_0)a^2
+10P_0(Q_0+1)a
+P_0(Q_0+1)(4P_0-1).
\tag{32}
\]
Then (25)-(26) and (28) are respectively equivalent to
\[
25N=W,
\tag{33}
\]
\[
5a(R_0+P_0Q_0)\ge J,
\tag{34}
\]
\[
15W\ge(1+4P_0Q_0)(4-R_0)^2.
\tag{35}
\]
The polynomial \(W\) is increasing in \(a\ge0\).

Suppose first that \(J\le0\), so it is enough to put \(a=0\). Define
\[
g=\frac{4P_0-1}{5},
\qquad
x=\frac{P_0(Q_0+1)}{4P_0-1}.
\]
The inequality \(J\le0\) implies \(g>0\) and
\[
d\ge x.
\]
Also
\[
1-4\sigma=g(4x-1)\ge\frac15.
\]
Since \(d<1-p=(3-g)/4\), one has
\[
g<3-4x.
\]
It follows that
\[
x(1-x)>\frac15.
\tag{36}
\]
At \(a=0\), inequality (35) reduces, using \(d\ge x\), to
\[
3xg\ge(4x-1)(1-x)^2.
\tag{37}
\]
The lower bound \(g\ge1/[5(4x-1)]\) shows that (37) follows from
\[
3x-5(4x-1)^2(1-x)^2>0.
\tag{38}
\]
To verify (38) exactly, put \(z=2x-1\). Equation (36) gives
\(|z|<1/\sqrt5\), and the left side of (38) is
\[
\frac{1-4z+15z^2+20z^3-20z^4}{4}.
\]
For \(z\ge0\), this is positive because
\[
1-4z+15z^2>0,
\qquad
20z^3(1-z)\ge0.
\]
For \(z=-y<0\), the bounds \(y^2<1/5\) give
\[
20y^3<4y,
\qquad
20y^4<4y^2,
\]
so the same expression is larger than \((1+11y^2)/4\). This proves (35)
when \(J\le0\).

Now suppose \(J\ge0\). It is enough to substitute
\[
a_0=\frac{J}{5(R_0+P_0Q_0)}.
\tag{39}
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
15W(a_0)-(1+4P_0Q_0)(4-R_0)^2
=
\frac{1+4P_0Q_0}{(R_0+P_0Q_0)^2}

\tag{40}

where
\[
H=H_0+P_0Q_0A+P_0^2Q_0^2B,
\tag{41}
\]
\[
H_0=
3+6R_0-13R_0^2+8R_0^3-R_0^4
+P_0(6+12R_0-9R_0^2)
+P_0^2(3+6R_0-12R_0^2),
\tag{42}
\]
\[
A=
15P_0^2+30P_0(R_0+1)
-2R_0^3+16R_0^2-2R_0+15,
\tag{43}
\]
\[
B=15P_0-R_0^2+8R_0-1.
\tag{44}
\]
We prove \(H>0\) on (30)-(31).

First, \(H\) is increasing in \(Q_0\). Indeed,
\[
\frac{\partial H}{\partial Q_0}
=P_0A+2P_0^2Q_0B.
\]
For \(0<R_0<3\),
\[
-2R_0^3+16R_0^2-2R_0+15
=2R_0^2(3-R_0)+10R_0^2-2R_0+15
\ge\frac{149}{10},
\]
and
\[
B\ge15P_0-1.
\]
If \(P_0\ge1/15\), both displayed contributions make the derivative positive.
If \(P_0<1/15\), then \(Q_0<R_0<3\), so the possible negative contribution
inside the brackets is larger than \(-2P_0Q_0>-2/5\), while \(A\ge149/10\).
Thus the derivative is positive in all cases.

Put
\[
J_0=1+P_0+R_0-4P_0R_0.
\tag{45}
\]
If \(J_0\ge0\), monotonicity gives
\[
H(P_0,Q_0,R_0)\ge H_0(P_0,R_0).
\]
We now verify \(H_0>0\). Let
\[
C(R)=3+6R-13R^2+8R^3-R^4
\tag{46}
\]
and
\[
G(R)=-16R^4+136R^3-321R^2+211R+59.
\tag{47}
\]
Both are positive on \(0\le R\le3\). One exact check is their Bernstein
expansion on each unit interval: if
\[
F(j+t)=\sum_{i=0}^4\binom4i\beta_i t^i(1-t)^{4-i},
\qquad
0\le t\le1,
\]
then the coefficient lists for \(C\), for \(j=0,1,2\), are
\[
\left(3,\frac92,\frac{23}{6},3,3\right),
\quad
\left(3,3,\frac{23}{6},\frac{13}{2},11\right),
\quad
\left(11,\frac{31}{2},\frac{131}{6},30,39\right),
\tag{48}
\]
and those for \(G\) are
\[
\left(59,\frac{447}{4},111,\frac{363}{4},69\right),
\quad
\left(69,\frac{189}{4},24,\frac{69}{4},29\right),
\quad
\left(29,\frac{163}{4},71,\frac{487}{4},179\right).
\tag{49}
\]
Every coefficient is positive.

As a quadratic in \(P_0\), \(H_0\) has leading coefficient
\[
3+6R_0-12R_0^2.
\]
If this coefficient is nonnegative, then
\[
R_0\le\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}<1,
\]
the linear coefficient \(6+12R_0-9R_0^2\) is positive, and
\[
H_0\ge C(R_0)>0.
\]
If the leading coefficient is negative, \(H_0\) is concave in \(P_0\), so
its minimum on the interval allowed by \(J_0\ge0\) and \(P_0+R_0<3\)
occurs at an endpoint. The endpoint \(P_0=0\) again gives \(C(R_0)>0\).
For the other endpoint put
\[
L(R)=\frac{R+1}{4R-1},
\qquad
u=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
When \(R_0<u\), the endpoint is \(P_0=L(R_0)\), and
\[
H_0(L(R_0),R_0)
=\frac{R_0^2G(R_0)}{(4R_0-1)^2}>0.
\tag{50}
\]
When \(R_0\ge u\), the endpoint is \(P_0=3-R_0\), and
\[
H_0(3-R_0,R_0)
=(4-R_0)(13R_0^3-43R_0^2+21R_0+12)>0.
\tag{51}
\]
For the last sign, the cubic has value \(10-2\sqrt5>0\) at \(u\);
its derivative is positive for \(R_0\ge u>2\), since the derivative has
value \(5\) at \(2\) and is increasing thereafter.

It remains to treat \(J_0<0\). Then \(R_0>1/4\), and \(J\ge0\) forces

\frac{4P_0R_0-P_0-R_0-1}{5P_0}

Monotonicity and direct substitution give

=
\frac{(P_0+1)^2(4R_0-1)}{25}
\left(
60P_0(R_0+1)
-4R_0^3+33R_0^2-87R_0+1
\right).
\tag{52}
\]
The inequality \(J_0<0\) says
\[
P_0>L(R_0).
\]
Together with \(P_0<3-R_0\), this also ensures that the interval is
nonempty. The bracket in (52) is strictly larger than its value at
\(P_0=L(R_0)\), which is
\[
\frac{G(R_0)}{4R_0-1}>0
\]
by (47)-(49). Thus \(H>0\), proving (40), (35), and finally (28).

The exterior triangle at the intersection of the \(C_1\)- and \(C_3\)-lines,

By (53) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality,

Since \(0\le g_{13}\le1\),
