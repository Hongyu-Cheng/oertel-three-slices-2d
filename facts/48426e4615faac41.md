## Statement

Let
\[
v_0=(0,0),\qquad v_1=(2,0),\qquad v_2=(0,1),\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}.
\]
Set
\[
A_0=\frac87\Delta,\qquad A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\frac47\Delta,
\tag{1}
\]
where the three homotheties have center \(v_0\). Write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad a_1=|A_1|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad
T=M+a_1+m,\qquad L=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Let \(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2\) be the barycentric coordinates of
\(\Delta\), and define affine functions by
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &v_0&v_1&v_2\\ \hline
g_1&-28&14&10\\
g_2&-\frac12&-1&-2\\
g_3&\frac{59}{2}&-12&-7.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Put \(f_i=-g_i\), and define
\[
B_i=A_1\cap\{f_i\le0\},\qquad b_i=|B_i|,
\]
\[
E_i=
\left\{(x_0,x_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
f_i\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)<0\right\},
\qquad
N=\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf1_{E_i}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(\Pi_m\) be the common capacitated coupling set of accepted fact
`f347fefedf08f4e2`.

Then (1) is a compatible rational polygonal triple in the strict residual
strip, the rows in (2) are nonconstant and pairwise nonparallel, and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{4}
\]
The middle cap areas are
\[
b_1=\frac{29}{57},\qquad b_2=0,\qquad
b_3=\frac{3481}{6059},
\tag{5}
\]
and \(b_i<L\) for every \(i\). Nevertheless,
\[
\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_m}\int(N-1)\,d\pi
\le\frac{6295}{22344}
<
\frac{834734}{2417541}
=
\frac{2M-m-a_1}{3}
-\left(\sum_i b_i-a_1\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the registered uniform excess-multiplicity inequality is false.

For the same instance, put \(d_i=L-b_i>0\). One has
\[
\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_m}\max_i\frac{\pi(E_i)}{d_i}>1.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, the exact deficit-weighted condition holds, and accepted fact
`f347fefedf08f4e2` gives
\[
b_3+\tau(E_3)>L.
\tag{8}
\]
Hence this counterexample refutes only the uniform surrogate, not the exact
row target or the locked theorem.

## Proof

The reference triangle has area \(1\), so
\[
M=\frac{64}{49},\qquad a_1=1,\qquad m=\frac{16}{49},
\qquad
T=\frac{129}{49},\qquad L=\frac{86}{147}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since the three triangles are homothetic about the same point,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac67\Delta\subsetneq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{10}
\]
This proves compatibility. It also realizes the three slices by a compact
polytope: take the convex hull of their integer-height embeddings. At height
\(1\), every convex combination involving height \(0\) and height \(2\)
uses a point of (10), while \(A_1\) itself is already a generating section.

For the residual-strip audit,
\[
r=\frac mM=\frac14,\qquad s=\frac{a_1}{M}=\frac{49}{64}.
\]
Here \(r<9/25\), and
\[
1-r=\frac34<\frac{49}{64}<\frac54=1+r.
\tag{11}
\]
The remaining raw residual inequalities are strict as well:
\[
\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4}=\frac9{16}<\frac{49}{64},
\qquad
\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}=\frac38
<\frac{49}{64}<\frac74=2-r.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the example lies in the full strict residual strip.

Every column of (2) sums to \(1\), whence
\[
g_1+g_2+g_3=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently the open halfplanes
\(\{f_i<0\}=\{g_i>0\}\) cover the whole plane. In the coordinates of
\(\Delta\),
\[
\nabla g_1=(21,38),\qquad
\nabla g_2=\left(-\frac14,-\frac32\right),\qquad
\nabla g_3=\left(-\frac{83}{4},-\frac{73}{2}\right).
\tag{14}
\]
Each consecutive determinant in (14) equals \(-22\), so the three rows are
nonconstant and pairwise nonparallel.

We next compute the middle cap areas. The negative \(g_1\)-corner at
\(v_0\) has edge ratios
\[
\frac{28}{28+14}=\frac23,\qquad
\frac{28}{28+10}=\frac{14}{19}.
\]
Therefore
\[
b_1=1-\frac23\frac{14}{19}=\frac{29}{57}.
\tag{15}
\]
The second row is strictly negative at all three vertices of \(A_1\), so
\[
b_2=0.
\tag{16}
\]
The positive \(g_3\)-corner at \(v_0\) has edge ratios
\[
\frac{59}{59+24}=\frac{59}{83},\qquad
\frac{59}{59+14}=\frac{59}{73},
\]
which gives
\[
b_3=\frac{59}{83}\frac{59}{73}=\frac{3481}{6059}.
\tag{17}
\]
Using (9),
\[
L-b_1=\frac{71}{931}>0,\qquad
L-b_2=\frac{86}{147}>0,\qquad
L-b_3=\frac{9367}{890673}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
This proves the genuine low-middle-cap hypothesis for all three rows.

We now retain the actual common midpoint sign cells. Every midpoint belongs
to \((6/7)\Delta\subset A_1\). Since \(g_2<0\) throughout \(A_1\),
\[
E_2=\varnothing.
\tag{19}
\]
At every midpoint, (13) gives
\[
g_1+g_3=1-g_2>1.
\]
Hence
\[
E_1\cup E_3=A_0\times A_2,\qquad
N-1=\mathbf1_{E_1\cap E_3}.
\tag{20}
\]
The strict inequalities in the definitions cause no boundary loss in
(19)-(20).

Define one measurable vertex cover of the first midpoint graph by
\[
U=A_0\cap\{g_1\ge19\},\qquad
V=A_2\cap\{g_1\ge-19\}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(x_0\notin U\) and \(x_2\notin V\), then
\[
g_1(x_0)<19,\qquad g_1(x_2)<-19.
\]
Affineness yields
\[
g_1\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)<0,
\]
so
\[
E_1\subseteq(U\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V).
\tag{22}
\]

The \(g_1\)-values at the vertices of \(A_0=(8/7)\Delta\) are
\[
-28,\qquad20,\qquad\frac{108}{7}.
\]
The cap at the vertex of value \(20\), cut at level \(19\), has relative
area
\[
\frac{(20-19)^2}{(20+28)(20-108/7)}
=\frac7{1536}.
\]
Thus
\[
|U|=\frac{64}{49}\frac7{1536}=\frac1{168}.
\tag{23}
\]
The \(g_1\)-values at the vertices of \(A_2=(4/7)\Delta\) are
\[
-28,\qquad-4,\qquad-\frac{44}{7}.
\]
At level \(-19\), the excluded corner at the vertex of value \(-28\) has
relative area
\[
\frac9{24}\frac9{152/7}=\frac{189}{1216}.
\]
Consequently
\[
|V|=\frac{16}{49}\left(1-\frac{189}{1216}\right)
=\frac{1027}{3724}.
\tag{24}
\]

For every \(\pi\in\Pi_m\), equations (20), (22), and the two marginal
constraints give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int(N-1)\,d\pi
&=\pi(E_1\cap E_3)
\le\pi(E_1)\\
&\le\pi_0(U)+\pi_2(V)
\le |U|+|V|
=\frac{6295}{22344}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
This uses one common midpoint graph and one vertex cover. It does not use
product volumes, separate optimal couplings, or endpoint Venn tables.

The proposed right side is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2M-m-a_1}{3}
-\left(\sum_i b_i-a_1\right)
&=\frac37-
\left(\frac{29}{57}+\frac{3481}{6059}-1\right)\\
&=\frac{834734}{2417541}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The strict gap is
\[
\frac{834734}{2417541}-\frac{6295}{22344}
=\frac{8603699}{135382296}>0.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (25)-(27) prove (6) and disprove the uniform transport bound.

It remains to decide the exact deficit-weighted condition on this instance.
Since \(A_2\subset A_0\), the diagonal measure
\[
\pi^\Delta=(x\mapsto(x,x))_\#
\bigl(\mathcal L^2|_{A_2}\bigr)
\tag{28}
\]
belongs to \(\Pi_m\). Its second marginal is \(\mu_2\), and its first
marginal is \(\mu_2\le\mu_0\). The \(g_3\)-values at the vertices of
\(A_2\) are
\[
\frac{59}{2},\qquad\frac{81}{14},\qquad\frac{121}{14},
\]
all positive. Every midpoint under (28) equals its endpoint \(x\in A_2\),
so
\[
\pi^\Delta(E_3)=m=\frac{16}{49}.
\tag{29}
\]
By (18),
\[
d_3=L-b_3=\frac{9367}{890673},
\qquad
m-d_3=\frac{281465}{890673}>0.
\tag{30}
\]
Therefore the same single coupling proves
\[
\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_m}\max_i\frac{\pi(E_i)}{d_i}
\ge
\frac{\pi^\Delta(E_3)}{d_3}>1,
\]
which is (7). Accepted fact `f347fefedf08f4e2` gives
\(\tau(E_3)=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_m}\pi(E_3)=m\), since no coupling has total
mass greater than \(m\). Equation (30) then gives
\[
b_3+\tau(E_3)=b_3+m>L.
\]
This proves (8) and completes the separation between the false uniform
bound and the surviving exact deficit-weighted condition.
