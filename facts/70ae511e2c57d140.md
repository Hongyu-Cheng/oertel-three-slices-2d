## Statement

Use the affine-plane and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Let \(D\ge28\) be an integer, take \((s,t)\) as planar coordinates, and set
\[
f_1(s,t)=\frac{s+t}{2},\qquad
f_2(s,t)=\frac{t-s}{2},\qquad
f_3(s,t)=-1-t,
\]
\[
L_1=L_2=0,\qquad L_3=-D-1.
\]
Thus \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\) and
\(-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)=D\). Put \(C=D+2\), and define the rational triangles
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{D+3}{2},\frac{D-1}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{D+3}{2},\frac{D-1}{2}\right),
(0,D+1)\right\},
\]
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(-2C,-C-2),(2C,-C-2),(0,-C+2)\}.
\]
Their exact cell areas are
\[
p_1=p_2=p_3=1,\qquad
p_\varnothing=\frac{(D+3)^2}{4}-3,
\]
with all other \(P\)-cells null, and
\[
q_1=q_2=D,\qquad q_{123}=2D+4,\qquad q_{12}=4D+12,
\]
with all other \(Q\)-cells null. In particular
\[
M=|A_0|=\frac{(D+3)^2}{4},\qquad
m=|A_2|=8(D+2),
\]
\[
M-m=\frac{D^2-26D-55}{4}>0,
\qquad
\frac{M}{m}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
and the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\(\{123,1,2,12\}\).

For \(i=1,2,3\), define
\[
\Phi_i(a)=
|A_0\cap\{f_i\le a\}|+
|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a\}|.
\]
Then the following direct endpoint property holds:
\[
L_i=\inf\{a:\Phi_i(a)<\Phi_i(L_i)\}.
\tag{A}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\Phi_1(0)=\Phi_2(0)=7D+17<m,\qquad
\Phi_3(-D-1)=2D+5<m.
\tag{B}
\]
Consequently, fix \(i\), let \(c_i\ge0\), and let \(h\) satisfy
\[
0<h<M,\qquad h=c_i+\Phi_i(L_i).
\tag{C}
\]
Then (B) gives \(c_i+m>h\), and \(c_i+M>h\) follows from
\(c_i\ge0\) and \(h<M\), while (A) makes
\[
I_i=\{a:c_i+\Phi_i(a)<h\}
\]
nonempty with \(\inf I_i=L_i\). Thus all the hypotheses of accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` hold with \(P=A_0\), \(Q=A_2\), \(c=c_i\), and threshold \(h\), and \(L_i\) is an actual lower crossing. In particular, any canonical residual realization satisfying the three common equalities \(h=c_i+\Phi_i(L_i)\) and \(0<h<M\) has all three actual lower crossings and strict endpoint tails.

Define the exact midpoint images
\[
U_1=\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_1}{2},\qquad
U_2=\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_2}{2},\qquad
V=\frac{P_3+Q_{123}}2.
\]
Their closures satisfy
\[
\overline{U_1}
=\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_1}}2,\qquad
\overline{U_2}
=\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_2}}2,\qquad
\overline V
=\frac{\overline{P_3}+\overline{Q_{123}}}2.
\tag{D}
\]
Put
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{U_1}\cup\overline{U_2}\cup\overline V)
=\overline{\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2\cup V)}.
\]
The hull \(H\) is the symmetric rational nonagon whose vertices are
\[
\left(-\frac{5D+9}{4},-\frac{D+7}{4}\right),\quad
\left(-\frac{5D+7}{4},-\frac{D+9}{4}\right),\quad
\left(-\frac{2D+5}{2},-2\right),
\]
\[
\left(-\frac{D+2}{2},-\frac12\right),\quad
\left(0,\frac12\right),
\]
together with the reflections in the line \(s=0\) of the first four vertices. Its area is
\[
|H|=\frac{9D^2+74D+113}{16}.
\tag{E}
\]

For \(G\ne\varnothing\), put
\[
\mathscr R_G=
\{f_i\le0\ (i\in G),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin G)\},
\qquad
h_G=|H\cap\mathscr R_G|.
\]
The complete list of nonzero values is
\[
h_1=h_2=\frac{6D^2+29D+9}{24},
\qquad
h_{12}=\frac{D^2+18D+65}{16},
\]
\[
h_3=\frac{D+2}{4(D+4)},
\qquad
h_{13}=h_{23}=
\frac{13D^2+64D+54}{24(D+4)},
\qquad
h_{123}=1.
\tag{F}
\]
Therefore define the nonnegative weighted charge
\[
\mathcal R^\star_H
:=\frac32(h_1+h_2)+5h_{12}
+\frac72(h_{13}+h_{23})+7h_{123}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal R^\star_H
=\frac{51D^3+830D^2+4037D+6216}{48(D+4)},
\]
\[
\mathcal R^\star_H-2p_\varnothing
=\frac{27D^3+590D^2+3533D+6504}{48(D+4)}>0.
\tag{G}
\]

If \(K\) is any convex body satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
then \(H\subseteq K\). For
\(r_G=|K\cap\mathscr R_G|\), define
\[
\mathcal R^\star_K
=\frac32(r_1+r_2)+5r_{12}
+\frac72(r_{13}+r_{23})+7r_{123}.
\]
Hence \(k=|K|\ge|H|\), while
\[
|H|-(M+m)
=\frac{5D^2-78D-179}{16}>0.
\tag{H}
\]
The polynomial in (H) is already positive for every integer \(D\ge18\), hence in particular throughout the present \(D\ge28\) family. Thus no such \(K\) can also satisfy the residual no-half inequality \(k<M+m\). Independently, since all \(R^\star\)-coefficients are nonnegative and cutting-line boundaries are null, every compatible \(K\) satisfies
\(\mathcal R^\star_K\ge\mathcal R^\star_H>2p_\varnothing\).

## Proof

Put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i.
\]
At the three displayed vertices of \(A_0\), the triples
\((p_1,p_2,p_3)\) are respectively
\[
\left(-1,\frac{D+1}{2},\frac{D+1}{2}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{D+1}{2},-1,\frac{D+1}{2}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{D+1}{2},\frac{D+1}{2},-1\right).
\]
The area of \(A_0\) is \((D+3)^2/4\). Each lower cap \(p_i\le0\) is a corner triangle obtained by the linear ratio \(2/(D+3)\) on both incident edges, and hence has area
\[
\frac{(D+3)^2}{4}
\left(\frac{2}{D+3}\right)^2=1.
\]
For \(D>1\), two such corner caps are disjoint up to their null boundaries. This proves all asserted \(P\)-cell areas.

For \(A_2\), put \(q_i=f_i+L_i\). At its three displayed vertices, \(q_3\) takes the values \(2,2,-2\); the line \(q_3=0\) cuts off the top triangle \(Q_{123}\). Direct intersection with the three lines \(q_i=0\), followed by the triangle-area formula, gives
\[
|Q_1|=|Q_2|=D,\qquad
|Q_{123}|=2C,\qquad
|A_2|=8C.
\]
The remaining positive chamber is \(Q_{12}\), of area
\[
8C-2D-2C=4D+12.
\]
The three vertex signs are respectively \(1,2,123\), and the region between them has type \(12\); convexity and the three cutting lines show that no other \(Q\)-cell has positive area. This proves the support and area claims. Finally,
\[
4M-4m=D^2-26D-55>0
\]
for \(D\ge28\), so the area ordering required in accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` is available on the entire stated range.

We next prove (A), rather than inferring it from a derivative at the proposed level. By symmetry it suffices to treat rows \(1\) and \(3\).

For row \(1\), the \(f_1\)-values on the vertices of \(A_0\) are
\(-1,(D+1)/2,(D+1)/2\). Hence
\[
|A_0\cap\{f_1\le a\}|=(a+1)^2
\qquad(-1\le a\le0).
\]
On \(A_2\), the unique maximum of \(f_1\) is \(D/2\), and the other two vertex values are \(-D/2\) and \(-(3D+8)/2\). For
\(-D/2\le b\le D/2\), the upper corner above \(f_1=b\) has area
\[
\frac{(D-2b)^2}{D}.
\]
Taking \(b=-a\), one obtains, for \(-1\le a\le0\),
\[
\Phi_1(a)-\Phi_1(0)
=\left(1-\frac4D\right)a^2-2a\ge0.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(-D/2\le a\le-1\), the \(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap only increases as \(a\) decreases. At \(a=-1\), its excess over \(\Phi_1(0)\) is
\[
3-\frac4D>0.
\]
For \(a\le-D/2\), the \(A_2\)-cap is all of \(A_2\), and
\[
m-\Phi_1(0)=D-1>0.
\]
Thus \(\Phi_1(a)\ge\Phi_1(0)\) for every \(a\le0\). On the other hand, the same quadratic formula gives
\[
\Phi_1(a)-\Phi_1(0)<0
\quad\text{for}\quad
0<a<\frac{2D}{D-4}.
\]
Therefore \(0=\inf\{a:\Phi_1(a)<\Phi_1(0)\}\). Reflection in \(s=0\) proves the identical assertion in row \(2\).

For row \(3\), write \(a=L_3+\varepsilon\). The \(p_3\)-values on \(A_0\) are again \(-1,(D+1)/2,(D+1)/2\), while the \(q_3\)-values on \(A_2\) are \(-2,2,2\). Thus, for
\(-1\le\varepsilon\le1\),
\[
\Phi_3(L_3+\varepsilon)-\Phi_3(L_3)
=-2(C-1)\varepsilon+\frac{C+2}{2}\varepsilon^2.
\tag{2}
\]
This is nonnegative for \(-1\le\varepsilon\le0\). For
\(\varepsilon\le-1\), the \(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap increases as \(\varepsilon\) decreases; its value at \(\varepsilon=-1\) already exceeds the value at \(0\) by \((5C-2)/2\). Hence
\[
\Phi_3(a)\ge\Phi_3(L_3)\qquad(a\le L_3).
\]
Since \(C>2\), the right side of (2) is strictly negative for every
\[
0<\varepsilon<1.
\]
This proved local interval is sufficient to give
\[
L_3=\inf\{a:\Phi_3(a)<\Phi_3(L_3)\}.
\]
The displayed cell areas give
\[
\Phi_1(0)=\Phi_2(0)=1+(m-D)=7D+17,
\qquad
\Phi_3(L_3)=1+2C=2D+5,
\]
which proves (B). Now let \(c_i,h\) satisfy (C). The functions are continuous, \(I_i\ne\varnothing\) by the strict right-hand intervals just proved, \(c_i+m>h\) by (B), \(M\ge m\) by \(D\ge28\), and \(f_i\) is nonconstant. Together with \(c_i\ge0\) and \(0<h<M\), these are precisely the remaining hypotheses needed to apply accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`. This proves the stated actual-crossing implication without extending that predecessor beyond its scope.

It remains to compute the joint midpoint hull. The closures of the source cells have the following vertices:
\[
\overline{P_\varnothing}
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\pm\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-1}{2}\right),
\left(\pm\frac{D+1}{2},\frac{D+1}{2}\right),
(\pm1,D)\right\},
\]
\[
\overline{P_3}=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,D),(0,D+1),(1,D)\},
\]
\[
\overline{Q_1}
=\operatorname{conv}\{(-2C,-C-2),(-C-2,-C-2),(-C,-C)\},
\]
\[
\overline{Q_2}=\rho(\overline{Q_1}),\qquad
\overline{Q_{123}}
=\operatorname{conv}\{(-C,-C),(0,-C+2),(C,-C)\},
\]
where \(\rho(s,t)=(-s,t)\). Each exact cell is a bounded convex polygon with possibly some boundary edges omitted. Therefore its closure is the displayed closed polygon, and for any two such cells \(E,F\),
\[
\overline{E+F}=\overline E+\overline F.
\]
This proves (D). The differences \(\overline{U_1}\setminus U_1\), \(\overline{U_2}\setminus U_2\), and \(\overline V\setminus V\) lie in the boundaries of their closed midpoint polygons, hence are finite unions of line segments and have planar area zero.

Adding the closed polygons and dividing by two gives
\[
\overline{U_1}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{5D+9}{4},-\frac{D+7}{4}\right),
\left(-\frac{5D+7}{4},-\frac{D+9}{4}\right),
\left(-\frac{2D+5}{2},-2\right),
\right.
\]
\[
\left.
\left(-\frac{D+3}{2},-1\right),
\left(-\frac{D+1}{2},-1\right),
\left(-\frac{D+9}{4},-\frac{D+9}{4}\right),
\left(-\frac{D+3}{4},-\frac{D+3}{4}\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
\overline{U_2}=\rho(\overline{U_1}),
\]
and
\[
\overline V=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{D+3}{2},-1\right),
\left(-\frac C2,-\frac12\right),
\left(0,\frac12\right),
\left(\frac C2,-\frac12\right),
\left(\frac{D+3}{2},-1\right)
\right\}.
\]
On the right boundary from bottom to top, the successive slopes
\(ds/dt\) of the claimed nonagon are
\[
1,\quad -1,\quad-\frac{D+3}{3},\quad-\frac{D+2}{2},
\]
which are strictly decreasing. Substitution of the \(t\)-coordinates of every omitted vertex in the displayed lists for \(\overline{U_1},\overline{U_2},\overline V\) gives \(|s|\le w(t)\), where \(w\) is the affine interpolation of these boundary vertices written below. Thus pruning the nonextreme points gives exactly the nonagon listed in the statement.

For an independent area and cell audit, its horizontal section at height \(t\) is \([-w(t),w(t)]\). Put
\[
t_0=-\frac{D+9}{4},\qquad
t_1=-\frac{D+7}{4}.
\]
The nonzero section range is \(t_0\le t\le1/2\), and the vertex list gives
\[
w(t)=
\begin{cases}
t+\dfrac{3D+8}{2},&t_0\le t\le t_1,\\[1mm]
-t+\dfrac{2D+1}{2},&t_1\le t\le-2,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{2D+3-2(D+3)t}{6},&-2\le t\le-\dfrac12,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{(D+2)(1-2t)}4,&-\dfrac12\le t\le\dfrac12.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
Integrating \(2w(t)\) gives (E).

The sign-cell cuts in these coordinates are especially simple. For \(t<-1\), the central interval \(t\le s\le-t\) is \(R_{12}\), with the two side intervals in \(R_1,R_2\). For \(-1\le t<0\), the same central interval is \(R_{123}\), with side intervals in \(R_{13},R_{23}\). For \(t\ge0\), the central interval \(-t<s<t\) is \(R_3\), with side intervals in \(R_{13},R_{23}\). The cutting lines themselves have area zero, so the weak/strict assignments do not affect any of the following integrals. One checks from (3) that \(w(t)>-t\) on \([t_0,0]\). On \([0,1/2]\), the unique solution of \(w(t)=t\) is
\[
\tau=\frac{D+2}{2(D+4)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
h_1=h_2=\int_{t_0}^{-1}(w(t)+t)\,dt,
\qquad
h_{12}=\int_{t_0}^{-1}(-2t)\,dt,
\]
\[
h_{123}=\int_{-1}^{0}(-2t)\,dt,
\]
\[
h_{13}=h_{23}
=\int_{-1}^{0}(w(t)+t)\,dt+
\int_0^\tau(w(t)-t)\,dt,
\]
\[
h_3=\int_0^\tau2t\,dt+\int_\tau^{1/2}2w(t)\,dt.
\]
Substitution of the four affine branches of \(w\) gives exactly (F), and their sum is (E). Substitution in the weighted definition gives (G).

We finally separate exact-cell incidence from the null closure boundaries. Accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` gives the literal inclusions
\[
U_1\subseteq R_1,\qquad U_2\subseteq R_2
\]
for the exact half-open source cells. It does not assert these inclusions for \(\overline{U_1},\overline{U_2}\). Instead,
\[
|\overline{U_1}\setminus U_1|
=|\overline{U_2}\setminus U_2|=0,
\]
and any points reassigned from \(R_1\) or \(R_2\) lie on the cutting lines \(f_j=0\); hence all area and weighted-charge calculations above are unchanged. Directly from the signs of \(P_3,Q_{123}\),
\[
V\subseteq\{f_3\le0\},
\]
again with only null boundary changes after closure.

Every exact image \(U_1,U_2,V\) lies in \((A_0+A_2)/2\). If \(K\) is compatible, compact, and convex, it contains their convex hull and therefore also its closure \(H\). This proves \(H\subseteq K\) without using any false closure-cell inclusion. The exact subtraction
\[
|H|-(M+m)=\frac{5D^2-78D-179}{16}
\]
is positive at \(D=18\) and strictly increasing thereafter. It proves (H), the no-half contradiction, and the lemma.
