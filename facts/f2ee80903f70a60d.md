## Statement

Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1/2)\},\\
A_1&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/2,1/2),(0,0),(3/4,0),
                         (1/2,1/4),(-1/2,3/4)\},\\
A_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(-1,1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Use strict target \(11/48\).  For \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), put
\[
u_\lambda=(1,\lambda)
\]
and define
\[
M_\lambda(c)=\inf_{k\in\mathbb R}\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|A_i\cap
\{w:u_\lambda\cdot w\leq c+k(i-1)\}\right|,
\qquad
R_1(\lambda)=\sup\{c:M_\lambda(c)<11/48\}.
\]
Let the two fixed-direction endpoints be
\[
R_2=-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12},\qquad
R_3=-\frac56+\frac{\sqrt{26}}{12}.
\]
Then
\[
R_1(\lambda)\leq\frac14
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1)
\]
and
\[
R_1(\lambda)+R_2+\lambda R_3
\leq\frac{\sqrt{78}-9}{12}<0
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1).
\]
Consequently, for every \(0<\lambda\leq1\), no three halfspaces with
height-one trace normals
\[
(1,\lambda),\qquad(-1,0),\qquad(0,-1),
\]
each of total three-slice mass strictly below \(11/48\), have trace
interiors covering \(\mathbb R^2\).  The same complement-feasibility
conclusion holds at the parallel limit \(\lambda=0\).

## Proof

For \(i=0,1,2\), write
\[
F_{i,\lambda}(t)
=\left|A_i\cap\{(x,y):x+\lambda y\leq t\}\right|.
\]
We first compute the middle contribution at \(t=1/4\).  In
counterclockwise order, denote the vertices of \(A_1\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
p&=(-1/2,1/2),&q&=(0,0),&r&=(3/4,0),\\
s&=(1/2,1/4),&v&=(-1/2,3/4).
\end{aligned}
\]
Their \(x+\lambda y\) values are
\[
\frac{\lambda-1}{2},\qquad 0,\qquad\frac34,\qquad
\frac{\lambda+2}{4},\qquad\frac{3\lambda-2}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]
Direct pairwise comparison shows that the only support-order ties on
\([0,1]\) occur at \(\lambda=0\), when \(p\) and \(v\) tie, at
\(\lambda=2/3\), when \(q\) and \(v\) tie, and at \(\lambda=1\), when
\(p,q\) and \(r,s\) tie in their respective pairs.  For every
\(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), however, \(p,q,v\) lie in
\(\{x+\lambda y\leq1/4\}\), with \(v\) on the cutting line only at
\(\lambda=1\), while \(r,s\) lie strictly outside.

The cutting line meets \([q,r]\) at
\[
i=(1/4,0)
\]
and meets the edge \([s,v]\), whose supporting line is \(x+2y=1\), at
\[
j_\lambda=
\left(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2(2-\lambda)},
      \frac{3}{4(2-\lambda)}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
At \(\lambda=1\), this second point is \(v\), so the same formula includes
the clipping degeneration.  The excluded part of \(A_1\) is the
quadrilateral
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{i,r,s,j_\lambda\}.
\]
Writing \(j_\lambda=(x_\lambda,y_\lambda)\), the shoelace formula gives
\[
2\left|\operatorname{conv}\{i,r,s,j_\lambda\}\right|
=\frac3{16}+\frac{y_\lambda-x_\lambda}{4}
=\frac{7+\lambda}{16(2-\lambda)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}\{i,r,s,j_\lambda\}\right|
=\frac{7+\lambda}{32(2-\lambda)}.
\]
Since \(|A_1|=13/32\),
\[
F_{1,\lambda}(1/4)
=\frac{13}{32}-\frac{7+\lambda}{32(2-\lambda)}
=\frac{19-14\lambda}{32(2-\lambda)}
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus all middle-body support-order changes and both endpoint degeneracies
have been included.

It remains to minimize the two outer contributions exactly.  Put
\[
G_\lambda=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,\lambda}(a)+F_{2,\lambda}(1/2-a)\right).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(0<\lambda<1\), exact triangle clipping gives
\[
F_{0,\lambda}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\leq0,\\
\dfrac{t^2}{2\lambda},&0\leq t\leq\lambda/2,\\
\dfrac18-\dfrac{(1/2-t)^2}{2(1-\lambda)},
   &\lambda/2\leq t\leq1/2,\\
\dfrac18,&t\geq1/2,
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
F_{2,\lambda}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\leq\lambda-1,\\
\dfrac{(t+1-\lambda)^2}{2(1-\lambda)(2-\lambda)},
   &\lambda-1\leq t\leq0,\\
\dfrac12-\dfrac{(1-t)^2}{2(2-\lambda)},
   &0\leq t\leq1,\\
\dfrac12,&t\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Each formula is the area of a vertex triangle or its complement.  Adjacent
pieces agree at every breakpoint, so (5) and (6) also include all clipping
boundaries.

For the expression in (4), the complete ordered breakpoint list is
\[
-\frac12<0<\frac{\lambda}{2}<\frac12<\frac32-\lambda.
\tag{7}
\]
Substitution of (5) and (6) gives the following value on all six closed
pieces, with the common boundary values understood:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{interval for }a&
F_{0,\lambda}(a)+F_{2,\lambda}(1/2-a)\\ \hline
(-\infty,-1/2)&\dfrac12\\[2pt]
(-1/2,0)&
\dfrac12-\dfrac{(a+1/2)^2}{2(2-\lambda)}\\[5pt]
(0,\lambda/2)&
\dfrac12+\dfrac{a^2}{2\lambda}
-\dfrac{(a+1/2)^2}{2(2-\lambda)}\\[5pt]
(\lambda/2,1/2)&
\dfrac58-\dfrac{(1/2-a)^2}{2(1-\lambda)}
-\dfrac{(a+1/2)^2}{2(2-\lambda)}\\[5pt]
(1/2,3/2-\lambda)&
\dfrac18+
\dfrac{(3/2-\lambda-a)^2}{2(1-\lambda)(2-\lambda)}\\[5pt]
(3/2-\lambda,\infty)&\dfrac18.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
This list contains every support and clipping cell for
\(0<\lambda<1\).

The first two rows of (8) are strictly larger than \(1/8\).  On the third
row, the quadratic is strictly convex.  If \(0<\lambda\leq1/2\), its
minimum is attained at
\[
a=\frac{\lambda}{4(1-\lambda)}
\]
and equals
\[
\frac12-\frac{1}{16(1-\lambda)}\geq\frac38.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(1/2\leq\lambda<1\), the unconstrained minimizer lies at or to the
right of \(\lambda/2\), so the minimum on this row is attained at
\(a=\lambda/2\).  Its value is
\[
B(\lambda)=
\frac{7-4\lambda-2\lambda^2}{8(2-\lambda)},
\qquad
B(\lambda)-\frac18
=\frac{(1-\lambda)(2\lambda+5)}{8(2-\lambda)}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
The fourth row is concave in \(a\), so its minimum occurs at an endpoint.
The left endpoint has value \(B(\lambda)>1/8\), while the right endpoint
has value
\[
\frac18+\frac{1-\lambda}{2(2-\lambda)}>\frac18.
\tag{11}
\]
The fifth row is at least \(1/8\), and the last row is identically
\(1/8\).  Therefore
\[
G_\lambda=\frac18
\qquad(0<\lambda<1).
\tag{12}
\]

For completeness, the coalesced breakpoint regimes at both ends are
checked directly.  At the parallel boundary \(\lambda=0\),
\[
F_{0,0}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\leq0,\\
t/2-t^2/2,&0\leq t\leq1/2,\\
1/8,&t\geq1/2,
\end{cases}
\]
while
\[
F_{2,0}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\leq-1,\\
(t+1)^2/4,&-1\leq t\leq0,\\
1/2-(1-t)^2/4,&0\leq t\leq1,\\
1/2,&t\geq1.
\end{cases}
\]
The breakpoints in \(a\) are \(-1/2,0,1/2,3/2\).  On
\((-\infty,-1/2]\) the sum in (4) is \(1/2\).  On
\([-1/2,0]\) the sum in (4) is
\[
\frac12-\frac{(a+1/2)^2}{4}\geq\frac7{16}.
\]
On \([0,1/2]\) it is the concave quadratic
\[
\frac a2-\frac{a^2}{2}
+\frac12-\frac{(a+1/2)^2}{4},
\]
whose endpoint values are \(7/16\) and \(3/8\).  On
\([1/2,3/2]\) it is
\[
\frac18+\frac{(3/2-a)^2}{4}\geq\frac18,
\]
and it is identically \(1/8\) for \(a\geq3/2\).  Hence
\[
G_0=\frac18.
\tag{13}
\]

At \(\lambda=1\), the values for \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) reduce to
\[
F_{0,1}(t)=\frac{t^2}{2}\quad(0\leq t\leq1/2),
\qquad
F_{2,1}(t)=\frac12-\frac{(1-t)^2}{2}
\quad(0\leq t\leq1),
\]
with the constant zero and full-area pieces outside those intervals.  The
sum in (4) equals
\[
\frac12-\frac{(a+1/2)^2}{2}
\quad(-1/2\leq a\leq0),
\]
\[
\frac38-\frac a2
\quad(0\leq a\leq1/2),
\]
and \(1/8\) for \(a\geq1/2\).  It is \(1/2\) for \(a\leq-1/2\).
Thus
\[
G_1=\frac18.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)--(14) audit the entire closed parameter interval, including
the parallel limit and every breakpoint coalescence.

Using \(a=c-k\) and \(c+k=2c-a\), the definition of \(M_\lambda\) gives
\[
M_\lambda(1/4)
=F_{1,\lambda}(1/4)+G_\lambda.
\]
Combining (3) and (12)--(14),
\[
M_\lambda(1/4)
=\frac{9(3-2\lambda)}{32(2-\lambda)}
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1).
\tag{15}
\]
More precisely,
\[
M_\lambda(1/4)-\frac{11}{48}
=\frac{37-32\lambda}{96(2-\lambda)}>0
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1).
\tag{16}
\]
For each fixed slope \(k\), all three slice thresholds are nondecreasing in
\(c\), so the corresponding total mass is nondecreasing.  Taking the
infimum over \(k\) preserves monotonicity.  Equation (16) therefore implies
\[
R_1(\lambda)\leq\frac14
\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq1).
\tag{17}
\]

Accepted fact `3c143dfcc90fab8e` gives
\[
R_2=-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12},\qquad
R_3=\frac{\sqrt{26}-10}{12}<0.
\]
It follows from (17) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_1(\lambda)+R_2+\lambda R_3
&\leq
\frac14-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12}
+\lambda\frac{\sqrt{26}-10}{12}\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{78}-9}{12}<0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
where the last strict inequality is the exact comparison \(78<9^2\).
This proves the endpoint assertion, including \(\lambda=0\).

Finally, let \(0<\lambda\leq1\), and suppose three halfspaces with the
displayed normals and height-one thresholds \(c_1,c_2,c_3\) all have mass
strictly below \(11/48\).  Their optimized masses satisfy
\(M_r(c_r)<11/48\), so \(c_r\leq R_r\).  Equation (18) gives
\[
c_1+c_2+\lambda c_3<0.
\]
The closed complements of their height-one trace interiors are
\[
x+\lambda y\geq c_1,\qquad x\leq-c_2,\qquad y\leq-c_3.
\]
They contain the common point \((-c_2,-c_3)\), so the three trace
interiors do not cover \(\mathbb R^2\).  At \(\lambda=0\), equation (18)
gives \(c_1+c_2<0\), and the same point again lies in all three closed
complements.  This is the exact parallel-limit feasibility statement.
