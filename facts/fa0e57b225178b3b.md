## Proof

We first derive the uniform endpoint-cap form.  Accepted fact
`32a80f2a555ef83b` says that if a full \(T=0\) chord is
\[
R\in[r,r+L],
\]
the full \(R=0\) section contains \((\sigma,0)\), the physical area on
\(T\le0\) is \(D\), and a target cell of physical area \(C\) lies in
\(T,R\ge0\), then, whenever
\[
\frac r\sigma-\frac{L^2}{2\Delta D}>0,
\]
\[
2\Delta C
\left(
\frac r\sigma-\frac{L^2}{2\Delta D}
\right)
\le(r+L)^2.
\]
For \(\sigma>0\), multiplication by \(D\sigma\) gives (20) in the
active branch.  In the complementary branch (20) is automatic.  If
\(\sigma=0\), full-section consistency forces \(r=0\), and (20) is
literally \(0\le0\).  This proves (20)-(21), including every endpoint
stratum.

For the first row of (22), use
\[
(T,R)=(p_j,p_\ell).
\]
The \(T\le0\) cap is \(P_j\), the target quadrant is \(P_0\), and the
two lower offsets are \(\alpha,\beta\).  Reversing the two coordinates
gives the second row; now the \(T\le0\) cap has area \(c+d\).

For the third row, use
\[
(T,R)=(p_k,-p_\ell).
\]
The cap is \(P_{k\ell}\), the target is \(P_\ell\), and the offsets
are \(\gamma,\tau\).  Reversal gives the fourth row; its cap is
\(P_j\cup P_0\), of area \(a+b\).

For the last pair, use \((p_j,p_k)\) and its reversal.  On \(p_j=0\),
\[
p_k\in[\delta-\alpha-J,\delta-\alpha]
=[\chi,\chi+J],
\]
while on \(p_k=0\),
\[
p_j\in[\delta+\gamma,\delta+\gamma+K]
=[\omega,\omega+K].
\]
The target quadrant is
\(\overline{P_0}\cup\overline{P_\ell}\), of area \(b+c\).
The two caps are respectively \(P_j\) and \(P_{k\ell}\).  This proves
all rows of (22).  These are the six ordered pairs of the three
\(A_0\) cutting lines, so the list is exhaustive.

For \(A_2\), accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1` gives the first two
sections in (16) and their lengths.  Positive \(Q_N,Q_{k\ell}\) cells
place the \(q_j=0\) line through the interior.  Unsupported-cell
exclusion forces \(q_k,q_\ell\le0\) on that section, yielding (16k)
with \(0\le w_0\le w_0+K_2\le S\).

Joining \((0,y)\) on \(U=0\) to \((S-t,-t)\) on \(q_j=0\)
meets \(V=0\) at
\[
U=G(y,t)=\frac{y(S-t)}{y+t}.
\]
On the boxes in (17), \(G\) is increasing in \(y\) and decreasing in
\(t\).  The full \(V=0\) section therefore gives (19a).

The hull of the \(U=0\) and \(V=0\) sections has coordinate area
\[
\frac12(uJ_2+vH_2+J_2H_2),
\]
and lies in \(\overline{Q_{jk}}\), proving (19b).  In coordinates
\((-q_j,-V)\), the two axis intervals have lower offsets
\(w_0,\theta\) and lengths \(K_2,H_2\).  Their hull gives (19c).
In coordinates \((U,-q_j)\), the lower offsets are
\(S+v,\psi\), giving (19d).

Finally, apply the two-interval boundary-completion lemma used in
accepted fact `989c9b585d2b42cc`.  In coordinates \((U,V)\), the
\(U\le0\) cap has area \(e\), which gives (19e).  In coordinates
\((-q_j,-V)\), the \(-q_j\le0\) cap has area \(s\), which gives
(19f).  Cutting-line boundaries have zero area in both applications.

Apply the same ordered-pair argument to \((U,V)\),
\((-q_j,-V)\), and \((U,-q_j)\), together with their reversals.
The respective target quadrants and caps are exactly those displayed
in (23).  On \(V=0\),
\[
-q_j=S-U\in[\theta,\theta+H_2],
\]
on \(q_j=0\),
\[
U\in[\psi,\psi+K_2],
\]
and on \(U=0\),
\[
-q_j=S+V\in[S+v,S+v+J_2].
\]
This proves all rows of (23).  If two relevant cutting lines meet at
an endpoint, the full section on the other line must contain the same
intersection point.  This gives (13) and (19); the two far offsets
\(\chi,\psi\) are strict because the other full chord lies in the
opposite positive interval.
