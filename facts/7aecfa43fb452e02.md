## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies of areas \(a_0,a_2>0\), let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad b=|B|,\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
and fix \(u\in\mathbb S^1\).  Write
\[
m_i(u)=\min_{x\in A_i}\langle u,x\rangle,\qquad
w_i(u)=\max_{x\in A_i}\langle u,x\rangle-m_i(u),
\]
and define the lower cap profiles on all of \(\mathbb R\) by
\[
\gamma_i^u(r)=
\left|A_i\cap\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\le m_i(u)+r\}\right|.
\]
Thus \(\gamma_i^u(r)=0\) for \(r\le0\) and
\(\gamma_i^u(r)=a_i\) for \(r\ge w_i(u)\).  Define
\(\gamma_B^u\) analogously using the lower support of \(B\), and put
\[
E_u(d)=\inf_{r\in\mathbb R}
\left(\gamma_0^u(r)+\gamma_2^u(d-r)\right)
\]
and
\[
Q_u=
\frac{(w_0(u)+w_2(u))^2}
{w_0(u)^2/a_0+w_2(u)^2/a_2}.
\]
For every \(d\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
x=\frac{\gamma_B^u(d/2)}{b}.
\]
Then \(0\le x\le1\), \(Q_u\ge m\), and
\[
E_u(d)\ge
\min\left\{
m,\,
Q_u\left(1-\sqrt{1-x}\right)^2
\right\}
\ge
m\left(1-\sqrt{1-x}\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, suppose that for \(i=1,2,3\) there are directions
\(u_i\in\mathbb S^1\) and numbers \(d_i\in\mathbb R\) such that the three
caps
\[
C_i=
B\cap\left\{x:
\langle u_i,x\rangle\le m_B(u_i)+\frac{d_i}{2}
\right\}
\]
cover \(B\).  If \(c_i=|C_i|\), then
\[
\max_{1\le i\le3}\bigl(c_i+E_{u_i}(d_i)\bigr)
\ge
\frac b3+
\min_{1\le i\le3}
\min\left\{
m,\,
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac23}\right)^2Q_{u_i}
\right\}
\ge
\frac b3+
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac23}\right)^2m.
\tag{2}
\]

In the locked three-slice problem, accepted fact
`8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` gives \(B\subseteq A_1\).  Accepted fact
`508b5d8f1a3d6fca` identifies \(E_u(d)\) as the optimized endpoint
payment for the middle cut at the same threshold.  Accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` says that a global counterexample at \(t=2T/9\)
produces three strict-low-mass halfspaces whose middle cuts cover the
middle plane.  Applying (2) to their induced caps of \(B\) shows that no
such counterexample can occur whenever
\[
\frac{|B|}{3}+
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac23}\right)^2\min\{a_0,a_2\}
\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

We first record the two elementary cap estimates that have the needed
inequality directions.

Let \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body of area \(a\), and let \(w\)
be its width in direction \(u\).  For \(0\le r\le w\), choose a point
\(p\in A\) on the lower support line.  Convexity gives
\[
p+\frac rw(A-p)\subseteq
A\cap\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\le m_A(u)+r\}.
\]
The set on the left has area \(a(r/w)^2\), so
\[
\gamma_A^u(r)\ge a\left(\frac rw\right)^2
\qquad(0\le r\le w).
\tag{4}
\]

For the reverse estimate on a cap of \(A\), use coordinates in which
\(t=\langle u,x\rangle-m_A(u)\in[0,w]\), and let \(\ell(t)\) be the
one-dimensional length of the section at level \(t\).  Convexity of \(A\)
implies that \(\ell\) is nonnegative and concave.  Fix
\(0<\delta<w\).  Concavity and \(\ell(w)\ge0\) give
\[
\ell(s)\le\frac{w-s}{w-\delta}\ell(\delta)
\quad(0\le s\le\delta),
\qquad
\ell(t)\ge\frac{w-t}{w-\delta}\ell(\delta)
\quad(\delta\le t\le w).
\tag{5}
\]
If \(\ell(\delta)=0\), the first inequality already makes the lower cap
area zero.  Otherwise, integrating (5) gives
\[
\int_0^\delta\ell(s)\,ds
\le
\frac{\ell(\delta)\delta(2w-\delta)}{2(w-\delta)},
\qquad
\int_\delta^w\ell(t)\,dt
\ge
\frac{\ell(\delta)(w-\delta)}2.
\]
Taking the ratio of the first integral to their sum yields
\[
\frac{\gamma_A^u(\delta)}{a}
\le
\frac{\delta(2w-\delta)}{w^2}
=2\frac\delta w-\left(\frac\delta w\right)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The endpoint cases \(\delta=0,w\) follow directly.

We next prove the endpoint-payment bound.  Abbreviate
\[
w_i=w_i(u),\qquad
D=\frac{w_0^2}{a_0}+\frac{w_2^2}{a_2}.
\]
For \(d\ge0\), fix any \(r\in\mathbb R\) and put \(s=d-r\).  If
\(r\ge w_0\) or \(s\ge w_2\), the corresponding cap is full, and hence
\[
\gamma_0^u(r)+\gamma_2^u(s)\ge m.
\tag{7}
\]
Otherwise \(r<w_0\) and \(s<w_2\).  Estimate (4), with the zero
convention for a nonpositive argument, gives
\[
\gamma_0^u(r)+\gamma_2^u(s)
\ge
\frac{a_0}{w_0^2}(r_+)^2+
\frac{a_2}{w_2^2}(s_+)^2.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(r,s\ge0\), the weighted Cauchy inequality makes the right side of
(8) at least \(d^2/D\).  If \(r<0\), then \(s>d\), and the second term
alone is at least
\[
\frac{a_2d^2}{w_2^2}\ge\frac{d^2}{D};
\]
the case \(s<0\) is symmetric.  Equations (7)-(8), followed by the
infimum over \(r\), prove
\[
E_u(d)\ge\min\left\{m,\frac{d^2}{D}\right\}
\qquad(d\ge0).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(d\le0\), the right side of the desired transfer (1) will be zero,
so no additional estimate is needed.

The width of \(B\) in direction \(u\) is
\[
w_B(u)=\frac{w_0+w_2}{2}.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(d>0\), put
\[
\theta=\min\left\{1,\frac{d}{w_0+w_2}\right\}.
\]
If \(d\ge w_0+w_2\), then \(\gamma_B^u(d/2)=b\).  If
\(0<d<w_0+w_2\), apply (6) to \(B\), using (10), to obtain
\[
x\le2\theta-\theta^2.
\]
Both cases therefore give
\[
\theta\ge1-\sqrt{1-x}.
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
Q_u=\frac{(w_0+w_2)^2}{D}\ge m,
\tag{12}
\]
because
\[
D\le\frac{w_0^2+w_2^2}{m}
\le\frac{(w_0+w_2)^2}{m}.
\]
If \(d<w_0+w_2\), (9) reads
\[
E_u(d)\ge\min\{m,\theta^2Q_u\}.
\]
If \(d\ge w_0+w_2\), (9) gives \(E_u(d)\ge m\).  In either case,
(11), monotonicity in \(\theta\), and (12) prove both inequalities in
(1).  When \(d\le0\), the \(B\)-cap has area zero, so \(x=0\) and (1)
is immediate.

Now assume the three caps \(C_i\) cover \(B\).  Subadditivity of area
gives \(\sum_i c_i\ge b\), so some index \(j\) satisfies
\(c_j/b\ge1/3\).  The function
\[
x\longmapsto1-\sqrt{1-x}
\]
is nondecreasing on \([0,1]\).  Applying (1) to \(u_j,d_j\) therefore
gives
\[
E_{u_j}(d_j)\ge
\min\left\{
m,\,
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac23}\right)^2Q_{u_j}
\right\}.
\]
Adding \(c_j\ge b/3\), then taking the minimum over the three possible
directions and using \(Q_{u_i}\ge m\), proves (2).

For the locked problem, accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` puts every
\(C_i\) inside the corresponding middle-slice cap.  By accepted fact
`508b5d8f1a3d6fca`, the actual endpoint contribution of a halfspace with
that middle cut is at least \(E_{u_i}(d_i)\), since \(E\) is the slope
infimum.  Thus (2) is a lower bound on the actual mass of one member of
any three-halfspace middle cover.  Accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` supplies exactly such a cover with every mass strictly
below \(2T/9\) from any global counterexample.  Condition (3) contradicts
that strict inequality.

Finally, the advertised regime is nonempty and is realized by polytopes.
Let
\[
L=\sqrt{\frac{19}{5}}+\sqrt{\frac{14}{5}},
\quad
A_0=[0,L]\times[0,L^{-1}],
\quad
A_2=[0,L^{-1}]\times[0,L],
\quad
A_1=B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Since
\[
L^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{19}{5}}-\sqrt{\frac{14}{5}},
\]
one has \(a_0=a_2=1\), while \(B\) is a square of side
\((L+L^{-1})/2=\sqrt{19/5}\).  Hence
\[
a_1=b=\frac{19}{5},\qquad
T=\frac{29}{5},\qquad
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{58}{45}.
\]
The inclusion \(B=A_1\), together with accepted fact
`8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`, shows that these slices arise from a compact polytope
of the locked type.  The middle-core pigeonhole term alone is
\[
\frac b3=\frac{19}{15}=\frac{57}{45}<\frac{58}{45},
\]
so it does not close the budget.  But (2) gives
\[
\frac{19}{15}+
\left(1-\sqrt{\frac23}\right)^2
>
\frac{58}{45},
\]
because this strict inequality is equivalent to
\[
\frac{74}{45}>2\sqrt{\frac23},
\]
and squaring the positive sides gives
\[
\frac{5476}{2025}>\frac{5400}{2025}.
\]
Thus the endpoint lower-payment term supplies a genuine, quantitatively
necessary \(2T/9\) implication.
