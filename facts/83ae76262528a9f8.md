## Proof

We first normalize every compatible endpoint pair without choosing a minimum-attaining column. Put
\[
\ell_i=\min_jU_{ij},\qquad
n_i=\min_jV_{ij},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
s=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\ell.
\tag{26}
\]
Column stochasticity and \(\det U\ne0\) give \(s<1\): if \(s=1\), every nonnegative column of \(U-\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\) has sum zero, so all columns of \(U\) equal \(\ell\). Similarly \(\sum_i n_i<1\). Summing (4) gives \(s\ge-\sum_i n_i>-1\). Thus
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}{1-s},
\qquad
Y=\frac{V+\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}{1+s}
\tag{27}
\]
are entrywise nonnegative and column stochastic. Writing \(x=1-s\),
\[
U=\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+xX,
\qquad
V=-\ell\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+(2-x)Y.
\tag{28}
\]
The determinant lemma, using \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}X=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Y=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), gives
\[
|\det X|=\frac A{x^2}=:\alpha,
\qquad
|\det Y|=\frac B{(2-x)^2}=:\beta.
\tag{29}
\]
The images \(T_X=X\Delta\) and \(T_Y=Y\Delta\) are subtriangles of \(\Delta\), so \(\alpha,\beta\le1\). This proves (6) and also shows that the two arguments in (8) are less than one.

Put \(\delta=g\ell+\sigma_3\) and
\[
r=-\frac\delta x,\qquad z=\frac\delta{2-x}.
\tag{30}
\]
Equation (28), translation by a multiple of \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), and positive homogeneity give
\[
\rho_X:=\mathcal C(gU;-\sigma_3)=\mathcal C(gX;r),
\qquad
\rho_Y:=\mathcal C(gV;\sigma_3)=\mathcal C(gY;z),
\tag{31}
\]
and the allocation identity is
\[
x r+(2-x)z=0.
\tag{32}
\]

We need two elementary triangle facts. First, if \(T\) is a nondegenerate triangle of area \(\theta\), \(f\) is affine on \(T\), and the upper cap \(T\cap\{f\ge q\}\) has area fraction \(0\le\rho<1\), then the lower part \(T\cap\{f\le q\}\) contains a nondegenerate triangle of area at least
\[
\theta(1-\sqrt\rho).
\tag{33}
\]
For \(\rho=0\), if some point had \(f>q\), a relative neighborhood of that point would give positive upper-cap area; hence \(T\subseteq\{f\le q\}\), and \(T\) itself proves (33). Otherwise the complete cap cells of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` leave two possibilities. If the upper cap is a corner triangle with edge ratios \(r_1,r_2\), then \(\rho=r_1r_2\), while the complementary quadrilateral contains a triangle of area fraction \(1-\min\{r_1,r_2\}\ge1-\sqrt\rho\). If the lower cap is a corner triangle, its fraction is \(1-\rho\ge1-\sqrt\rho\). This includes cuts through vertices, repeated vertex values, and the constant case.

Second, for \(q\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
K_q=\{\lambda\in\Delta:g\lambda\le q\}
\]
and define its triangle capacity by the literal convention
\[
m(q)=\sup\left(
\left\{|\Theta|:\Theta\subseteq K_q
\text{ is a nondegenerate triangle}\right\}\cup\{0\}
\right).
\tag{34}
\]
All areas are normalized by \(|\Delta|=1\). If \(d>e\), then
\[
m(q)=
\begin{cases}
0,&q\le-d,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(q+d)^2}{(d-e)(p+d)},&-d\le q\le-e,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{q+d}{p+d},&-e\le q\le p,\\[3mm]
1,&q\ge p.
\end{cases}
\tag{35}
\]
If \(d=e\), then
\[
m(q)=
\begin{cases}
0,&q\le-d,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{q+d}{p+d},&-d\le q\le p,\\[3mm]
1,&q\ge p.
\end{cases}
\tag{36}
\]
For \(q<-d\), \(K_q\) is empty. At \(q=-d\), it is a single vertex when \(d>e\), and it is the edge joining the two equal-minimum vertices when \(d=e\). Neither set contains a nondegenerate triangle, so (34) makes \(m(q)=0\) literally in all these cases.

For \(-d<q<-e\) when \(d>e\), \(K_q\) is the similar corner triangle at the \(-d\) vertex, giving the quadratic branch of (35). In the next branch \(K_q\) is the quadrilateral obtained by cutting off the \(p\) vertex. A maximum-area inscribed triangle can be moved vertex by vertex to polygon vertices. The four resulting area fractions are
\[
u,\quad v,\quad u(1-v),\quad v(1-u),
\qquad
u=\frac{q+d}{p+d},\quad v=\frac{q+e}{p+e}.
\]
When \(d>e\), one has \(0<v<u<1\), so the maximum is \(u\). When \(d=e\), one has \(u=v\); the two cut points are distinct points at the same edge ratio, \(K_q\) is a trapezoid, and the same four-vertex calculation gives maximum \(u\). The endpoint values agree, proving (35)--(36).

In the only range used below, namely \(0\le w<1\), direct inversion of (35)--(36) gives
\[
m(q)>w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q>\psi(w).
\tag{37}
\]
This includes \(w=0\): the strict inequality \(m(q)>0\) excludes every empty or lower-dimensional \(K_q\), so necessarily \(q>-d=\psi(0)\).

Suppose now that the conclusion of (10) fails, so
\[
A\rho_X+B\rho_Y<\varepsilon.
\tag{38}
\]
Then \(\rho_X<\varepsilon/A\) and \(\rho_Y<\varepsilon/B\). Applying (33) inside \(T_X,T_Y\), and then (37), gives strictly
\[
r>\psi\left(\alpha\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\varepsilon A}\right)\right)
=\psi\left(\frac{\mu_\varepsilon}{x^2}\right),
\tag{39}
\]
\[
z>\psi\left(\beta\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\varepsilon B}\right)\right)
=\psi\left(\frac{\nu_\varepsilon}{(2-x)^2}\right).
\tag{40}
\]
Multiplying by \(x,2-x>0\) and using (32) yields
\[
0>G_\varepsilon(x)\ge\Gamma_\varepsilon.
\tag{41}
\]
This contradicts \(\Gamma_\varepsilon\ge0\), proving (10).

For the finite evaluation, direct substitution into (1)--(2) gives, precisely in the used range \(q>0,r>0\) and \(q/r^2\in[0,1)\),
\[
r\psi(q/r^2)
=-dr+\max\left\{
\sqrt{q(d-e)(p+d)},\frac{(p+d)q}{r}
\right\}=H_q(r).
\tag{42}
\]
For \(d=e\), the square-root term is zero and the reciprocal term is active throughout, exactly matching (2). This proves (12). Partition \(I\) at the two switch points in (14) when \(d>e\). Where both reciprocal terms in (11) are active, the unique stationary point is the third point in (13). Where exactly one reciprocal term is active, the sum is respectively decreasing or increasing. Where neither is active, it is constant. Thus a minimum occurs at an endpoint, a switch point, or that stationary point, proving (15). When \(d=e\), there are no switch points and the endpoint/stationary-point list in (13) gives the same conclusion.

For fixed \(x\), \(G_\varepsilon(x)\) is nonincreasing in \(\varepsilon\), because \(\mu_\varepsilon,\nu_\varepsilon\) decrease and \(\psi\) increases. If \(E(U,V,\sigma_3)<E_{\rm cap}\), the definition of the supremum supplies \(\varepsilon>E(U,V,\sigma_3)\) with \(\Gamma_\varepsilon\ge0\), contradicting (10). This proves (17). Accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98` proves \(0<\eta<4A/9<A<B\) in the contained chamber, so substituting \(\varepsilon=\eta\) proves (19).

It remains to verify the exact obstruction. Let
\[

\begin{pmatrix}

\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\tag{43}
\]
Then \(\det X=12/25\), and (22) is exactly
\[

\qquad

\tag{44}
\]
Hence their columns sum to one and
\[

\qquad

\tag{45}
\]
Every row minimum of \(U\) is \(1/6\), and every row minimum of \(V\) is \(-1/6\), so (4) holds with equality. Also
\[
gX=(-940,-56,-44/5),\qquad g\ell=\frac23.
\tag{46}
\]
With \(\sigma_3=-2/3\),
\[
gU+\sigma_3\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}=\frac12gX<0,
\qquad
gV-\sigma_3\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}=\frac32gX<0
\tag{47}
\]
entrywise. Both cap fractions in (23) are therefore zero. The one-positive formula of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives (21). The residual normalized data are \(R=A/B=1/9\), \(S=1/B=25/27\), so \(1-R<S<1+R\). Also \(R<(27-2\sqrt{26})/25\), and hence all strict residual area inequalities hold.

V\bar\lambda=(11/150,89/150,1/3)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For (24),
\[

\]
\[

=(4171/12,19573/60,-2512/15)^{\mathsf T},
\]
\[
