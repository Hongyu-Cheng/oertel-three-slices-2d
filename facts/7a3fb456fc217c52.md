## Statement

Normalize \(|\Delta|=1\).  For a nonconstant affine function \(f\) on
\(\Delta\), write
\[
C(f)=|\{f\geq0\}|.
\]
Put
\[
P=\frac{225}{529},\qquad Q=\frac{43}{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the following asymmetric two-cap theorem holds:
\[
\boxed{
C(f)=P,\quad C(g)=Q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\{f+g\leq0\}|\geq\frac{19}{40}.}
\tag{2}
\]
It includes every vertex-on-line, repeated-value, parallel-line,
constant-sum, and cap-order boundary case.

The monotone form of (2) is
\[
\boxed{
C(f)\leq P,\quad C(g)\leq Q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\{f+g\leq0\}|\geq\frac{19}{40}.}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently, let \(L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) be any nondegenerate
middle-triangle vertex matrix with column sums one, and put
\[
c_r=\mathcal C(L_{r*};0)
\qquad(r=1,2,3),
\]
using the exact cap notation of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.
For any distinct \(i,j,k\), the closed parameter region
\[
c_i\leq\frac{225}{529},\qquad
c_k\leq\frac{43}{100},\qquad
t\leq\frac{19}{40}
\tag{4}
\]
admits no strict row system
\[
c_r+
A\mathcal C((LU)_{r*};-\sigma_r)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{r*};\sigma_r)
<t
\qquad(r=1,2,3)
\tag{5}
\]
for any \(A,B>0\), any real compatible \(U,V\), either determinant
sign, and any \(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\).

In particular, consider the common scalar data in the proposed
one- and two-missing-cell singleton-singleton survivors:
\[
A=\frac1{100},\qquad B=1,\qquad
t=\frac{67}{150},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
c_1=\frac{225}{529},\qquad
c_2=\frac{80009}{180000},\qquad
c_3=\frac{43}{100}.
\tag{7}
\]
No nondegenerate rational or real middle triangle, and hence no
rational or real endpoint completion, realizes (5).  This excludes
all three proposed missing sets \(X_2\), \(X_3\), and \(X_2,X_3\)
at once.  The conclusion does not fix or use the previously proposed
endpoint pair in any of those chambers.

## Proof

We first prove (2).  The complete exact cap formulas of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` and the fixed-level reduction used in accepted fact
`b12dbfdde261e9de` give the following parametrization.  For
\(0<r<1/2\), define
\[
S_1^r(u)=
\left(1,\ 1-u,\ 1-\frac1{ru}\right),
\qquad
S_2^r(u)=
\left(1-\frac1{ru},\ 1,\ 1-u\right),
\tag{8}
\]
\[
D_1^r(u)=
\left(-1,\ u-1,\ \frac1{(1-r)u}-1\right),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
D_2^r(u)=
\left(\frac1{(1-r)u}-1,\ -1,\ u-1\right),
\qquad
D_3^r(u)=
\left(u-1,\ \frac1{(1-r)u}-1,\ -1\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The \(S\)-parameters satisfy
\[
1\leq u\leq\frac1r,
\]
and the \(D\)-parameters satisfy
\[
1\leq u\leq\frac1{1-r}.
\]
The vector \(S_\ell^r(u)\) has one nonnegative support vertex and cap
fraction \(r\); the vector \(D_\ell^r(u)\) has two nonnegative support
vertices and cap fraction \(r\).  The parameter endpoints include a
zero vertex value.  Replacing \(u\) by its reciprocal partner swaps
the two nondistinguished vertices, so no ordering is lost.

Here
\[
\frac12-P=\frac{79}{1058}>0,\qquad
\frac12-Q=\frac7{100}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus every affine function in (2), after a positive scaling and a
vertex permutation, has exactly one of the \(S\)- or \(D\)-forms.
Because \(P\neq Q\), interchange of \(f,g\) is not a symmetry.  Up to
simultaneous vertex permutations, the complete list has eight
support-incidence orbits:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{orbit}&f&g\\ \hline
SS_{\rm same}&S_1^P(u)&S_1^Q(v)\\
SS_{\rm diff}&S_1^P(u)&S_2^Q(v)\\
SD_{\rm in}&S_1^P(u)&D_3^Q(v)\\
SD_{\rm out}&S_1^P(u)&D_1^Q(v)\\
DS_{\rm in}&D_3^P(u)&S_1^Q(v)\\
DS_{\rm out}&D_1^P(u)&S_1^Q(v)\\
DD_{\rm same}&D_1^P(u)&D_1^Q(v)\\
DD_{\rm diff}&D_1^P(u)&D_2^Q(v).
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
After normalizing \(f\), the relative positive scale remains arbitrary:
\[
h=f+\lambda g,\qquad \lambda>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (8)-(13) therefore parameterize every pair in (2),
including every support boundary.

We reduce a strict violation of (2) to seven exact sign predicates.
Write the vertex values of \(h\) as \(h_1,h_2,h_3\).  If all three are
nonnegative and not all zero, the lower cap has area zero.  If
\(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), \(h_i<0\), and \(h_j,h_k\geq0\), the exact
one-negative formula says
\[
|\{h\leq0\}|<\frac{19}{40}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
40h_i^2<
19(h_j-h_i)(h_k-h_i).
\tag{14}
\]
If \(h_i,h_j\leq0<h_k\), the exact one-positive complement formula says
\[
|\{h\leq0\}|<\frac{19}{40}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
40h_k^2>
21(h_k-h_i)(h_k-h_j).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus the all-nonnegative predicate, the three choices in (14), and the
three choices in (15) exhaust every strict violation.  If all three
values are nonpositive, the lower cap is full.  If all three vanish,
it is also full by the closed constant-row convention.

Substitute each row of (12) into these seven predicates, together with
the literal parameter intervals after (8)-(10) and \(\lambda>0\).
All denominators are strictly positive on the displayed domains.
Exact real cylindrical algebraic decomposition gives

Here \(N\) is the all-nonnegative case, \(P_i\) is (14) with unique

We next prove (3).  Suppose \(C(f)\leq P\).  Since \(f\) is
nonconstant and \(P\in(0,1)\), there is a unique threshold
\(\alpha\) such that
\[
|\{f\geq\alpha\}|=P.
\]
Monotonicity and \(C(f)\leq P\) give \(\alpha\leq0\).  Likewise there
is \(\beta\leq0\) such that
\[
|\{g\geq\beta\}|=Q.
\]
Apply (2) to \(f-\alpha\) and \(g-\beta\).  It gives
\[
|\{f+g\leq\alpha+\beta\}|\geq\frac{19}{40}.
\]
Since \(\alpha+\beta\leq0\), this set is contained in
\(\{f+g\leq0\}\), proving (3).

Now let \(L\) satisfy (4), and let
\(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).  Apply (3) on \(\Delta\) to
\[
f(\lambda)=L_{i*}\lambda,\qquad
g(\lambda)=L_{k*}\lambda.
\]
The column-sum identity gives pointwise
\[
f(\lambda)+g(\lambda)=1-L_{j*}\lambda.
\]
Hence (3) is exactly
\[
\left|\{\lambda:L_{j*}\lambda\geq1\}\right|
\geq\frac{19}{40}.
\tag{18}
\]
The threshold-\(1\) cap in (18) is contained in the middle
threshold-\(0\) cap, so
\[
c_j\geq\frac{19}{40}.
\tag{19}
\]
All endpoint cap contributions in (5) are nonnegative.  Equations
(4) and (19) therefore make the left side of row \(j\) at least
\(19/40\geq t\), contradicting its strict inequality.  This proves
the chamber-wide exclusion (4).

Finally, the scalar data (6)-(7) satisfy
\[
c_1=P,\qquad c_3=Q,\qquad
\frac{19}{40}-t=\frac{17}{600}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
They lie outside the endpoint criterion of accepted fact
`37652924248e8019`: their three bottom allowances are
\[
\left(

\frac{t-c_2}{A},
\frac{t-c_3}{A}
\right)
=
\left(
\frac{3386}{1587},
\frac{391}{1800},
\frac53
\right),
\tag{21}
\]
so only the middle entry is below both \(p_0\) and \(1/4\).  The three
proposed endpoint sign patterns accompanying these scalar data obey
the missing-cell realizability restrictions of accepted fact
`53099f38de81eac5`; the present obstruction is instead the first joint
middle-triangle constraint.  Unlike the one-positive-row
endpoint floor of accepted fact `86e9bf31e8f5df7a`, (18) uses only
the middle column-sum identity and therefore covers every endpoint

By the exact reconstruction equivalence in accepted fact
