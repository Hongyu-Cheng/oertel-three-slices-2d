## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(D\), let \(V_0\) be one of its vertices, and let \(a\ge c>0\), \(a<1\), satisfy
\[
a+c\le2,\qquad
a^2<1+c^2,\qquad
1<a^2+c^2,
\]
\[
a^2+2>3c^2+2ac,\qquad
1+c^2<2a^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
A_0=V_0+a(\Delta-V_0),\qquad
A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_2=V_0+c(\Delta-V_0),
\]
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\qquad
T=(a^2+1+c^2)D.
\]
If \(g\) is the centroid of \(\Delta\), then
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]

Equivalently, writing
\[
x=\frac ca,\qquad
q=\frac{\sqrt{10}-1}{3},\qquad
\theta=\frac{\sqrt{26}-1}{5},
\]
conclusion (2) holds on the complete disjoint parameter union
\[
0<x\le\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}<a<1,
\tag{U4}
\]
\[
\frac1{\sqrt2}<x\le q,
\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{2-x^2}}<a<1,
\tag{U5}
\]
and
\[
q<x<\theta,
\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{2-x^2}}<a<
\sqrt{\frac2{3x^2+2x-1}}.
\tag{U6}
\]

## Proof

Accepted facts `8be4b2011a05cfd2` and `56bc39a3b6a791db` prove that (1) with \(a<1\) is exactly the union \((U4)\)-\((U6)\). In particular,
\[
0<c<a<1,\qquad R:=a^2+c^2>1.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
s=a+c,\qquad Q=\frac{2(R+1)}9.
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(TQ/(R+1)=DQ\), so it suffices to prove that the normalized depth is at least \(Q\). From (3),
\[
1<s<2,\qquad R<s^2,\qquad R<2,
\tag{5}
\]
and, writing \(t=s-1\),
\[
R<1+t^2,
\tag{6}
\]
because
\[
1+(s-1)^2-R=2(1-a)(1-c)>0.
\]

Apply the affine normal form of accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` at the middle centroid, and its centroid specialization from accepted fact `bacab27d6c43776b`. For a normalized projected direction, write the ordered projected vertex values as
\[
\gamma,\qquad \gamma+\rho,\qquad \gamma+1,
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1.
\]
After shifting by \(\gamma\), the cap fraction is
\[
\phi_\rho(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2/\rho,&0\le u\le\rho,\\
1-(1-u)^2/(1-\rho),&\rho\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
when \(0<\rho<1\). At the tied endpoints,
\[
\phi_0(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
1-(1-u)^2,&0\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\phi_1(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2,&0\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus tied directions are included.

The normalized middle-slice contribution is
\[
p(\rho)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{5-5\rho-\rho^2}{9(1-\rho)},
&0\le\rho\le\frac12,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{(1+\rho)^2}{9\rho},
&\frac12\le\rho\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
It is nonincreasing, since its derivatives on the two intervals are respectively
\[
\frac{\rho(\rho-2)}{9(1-\rho)^2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\rho^2-1}{9\rho^2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
p(\rho)\ge p(1)=\frac49.
\tag{10}
\]

For an arbitrary slope parameter, let \(u_0,u_2\) be the shifted cap arguments on the two outer slices. The normalized mass is
\[
p(\rho)+a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2).
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
au_0+cu_2=K,
\tag{12}
\]
where the position of \(V_0\) among the three projected vertices gives
\[
K_{\min}=\frac{2(1+\rho)}3,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
K_{\mathrm{mid}}=\frac23+\rho\left(s-\frac43\right),
\tag{14}
\]
or
\[
K_{\max}=s-\frac43+\frac{2\rho}{3}.
\tag{15}
\]
Each case represents the two permutations of the other vertices, so (13)-(15) cover all six direction chambers.

Define
\[
\psi(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2,&0\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
Equations (7)-(8) give
\[
\phi_\rho(u)\ge\psi(u)
\tag{16}
\]
for every \(0\le\rho\le1\). On the lower quadratic branch this follows from \(u^2/\rho\ge u^2\). On the upper branch,
\[
\phi_\rho(u)-u^2
=
\frac{(1-u)((2-\rho)u-\rho)}{1-\rho}\ge0,
\]
and the tied cases follow directly from (8).

Whenever \(K\ge0\), (12), (16), and \(a\ge c\) imply
\[
a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2)
\ge
\min\left\{c^2,\frac{K^2}{2}\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, put \(X=au_0\), \(Y=cu_2\). If \(X,Y\ge0\) and neither cap is saturated, the right side before taking the minimum is
\[
X^2+Y^2\ge\frac{(X+Y)^2}{2}=\frac{K^2}{2}.
\]
If \(X\ge a\), the first cap contributes at least \(a^2\ge c^2\); if \(Y\ge c\), the second contributes at least \(c^2\). If \(X<0\), then \(Y>K\), so either the second cap is saturated or contributes \(Y^2>K^2\). The case \(Y<0\) is symmetric.

One side of the minimum in (17) always suffices. By (1) and (10),
\[
p(\rho)+c^2-Q
\ge
\frac49+c^2-Q
=
\frac{2+7c^2-2a^2}{9}
>
\frac{5c^2}{9}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
It remains to prove \(p(\rho)+K^2/2>Q\) whenever the relevant \(K\) is nonnegative, except where \(p(\rho)>Q\) already.

If \(V_0\) is the projected minimum, (13) gives \(K\ge2/3\). Hence, by (5) and (10),
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\ge\frac49+\frac29
=\frac23
>Q.
\tag{19}
\]

Suppose \(V_0\) is the projected middle vertex. If \(s\ge4/3\), (14) again gives \(K\ge2/3\), so (19) applies. If \(1<s<4/3\), both \(p(\rho)\) and the positive quantity
\[
K=\frac23-\rho\left(\frac43-s\right)
\]
are nonincreasing in \(\rho\). Therefore
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\ge
\frac49+\frac12\left(s-\frac23\right)^2.
\]
Using \(R<s^2\),
\[
\frac49+\frac12\left(s-\frac23\right)^2
-\frac{2(s^2+1)}9
=
\frac{5s^2-12s+8}{18}
=
\frac{5(s-6/5)^2+4/5}{18}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus the middle-vertex chambers also satisfy the required inequality.

Finally suppose \(V_0\) is the projected maximum. With \(t=s-1\in(0,1)\), equation (15) becomes
\[
K=t+\frac{2\rho-1}{3}.
\tag{21}
\]
By (6),
\[
Q<\overline Q:=\frac49+\frac{2t^2}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]

For \(1/2\le\rho\le1\), put \(z=2\rho-1\in[0,1]\). Then \(K=t+z/3>0\), and (9) gives
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}-\overline Q
=
\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{9\rho}
+\frac{5t^2}{18}
+\frac{tz}{3}
+\frac{z^2}{18}
>0.
\tag{23}
\]

For \(0\le\rho<1/2\), put \(z=1-2\rho\in(0,1]\). Then
\[
K=t-\frac z3
\tag{24}
\]
and
\[
p(\rho)=\frac49+P(z),
\qquad
P(z)=\frac{1+4z-z^2}{18(1+z)}.
\tag{25}
\]
If \(K\le0\), then \(t\le z/3\le1/3\). Since \(P(z)\ge1/36\),
\[
p(\rho)-\overline Q
=
P(z)-\frac{2t^2}{9}
\ge
\frac1{36}-\frac2{81}
=
\frac1{324}>0.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the outer contributions are not needed in this case.

If \(K>0\), then
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}-\overline Q
=
\frac{
5(1+z)t^2-6z(1+z)t+z^3+4z+1
}{18(1+z)}.
\tag{27}
\]
As a quadratic in \(t\), its minimum occurs at \(t=3z/5\), where its value is
\[
\frac{5+20z-9z^2-4z^3}{90(1+z)}.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(0\le z\le1\),
\[
5+20z-9z^2-4z^3
\ge5+20z-13z
=5+7z>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (22), (27)-(29) prove the required inequality in the final slope and direction regime.

For every chamber with \(K\ge0\), equations (17)-(20), (23), and (27)-(29), together with (18), yield
\[
p(\rho)+a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2)>Q.
\]
When \(K\le0\), equation (26) and nonnegativity of the outer caps give the same conclusion. The argument allows arbitrary slope, including empty, quadratic, and saturated outer-cap branches. Equations (8), (13)-(15) include all tied directions.

The two horizontal halfspaces have normalized masses \(a^2+1\) and \(1+c^2\), both exceeding \(1\), whereas \(Q<2/3\) by (5). Hence the exact depth formula of accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` gives
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge DQ=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
This covers every point of \((U4)\)-\((U6)\), so there is no residual parameter remainder.
