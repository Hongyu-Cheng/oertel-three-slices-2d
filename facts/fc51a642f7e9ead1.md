## Statement

Let \(g_i,g_\ell,g_q\) be affine functions on one affine plane with
\[
g_i+g_\ell+g_q=1,
\]
and use the affine coordinates
\[
x=g_i,\qquad y=g_\ell,\qquad g_q=1-x-y.
\]
Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\), \(a+b>0\), and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,-a),(b,-a),(-a,b)\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2\) be nonempty two-dimensional centrally symmetric rational
parallelograms satisfying the vertexwise midpoint compatibility condition
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and assume that \((M,m,k)\) lies in the exact strict residual area domain of
accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Assume also
\[
D_0=3t-k-m>m.
\]
For \(j\in\{i,\ell,q\}\), put
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{g_j\ge0\}|.
\]
For \(r\in\{0,2\}\) and \(j\in\{i,\ell\}\), define
\[
\alpha_{rj}=\min_{A_r}g_j,\qquad
\beta_{rj}=\max_{A_r}g_j,\qquad
w_{rj}=\beta_{rj}-\alpha_{rj},
\]
\[
R_j=\beta_{0j}+\beta_{2j},\qquad
\sigma_j=\frac{w_{0j}^2}{M}+\frac{w_{2j}^2}{m},
\]
and
\[
\Psi_j(R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&R\le0,\\
\min\{R^2/\sigma_j,m\},&R>0.
\end{cases}
\]
If
\[
\varepsilon=t-m-c_q>0,\qquad
\omega=c_i+c_\ell-(k-c_q),
\]
then
\[
\mathcal P
=\varepsilon+\omega+\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)-D_0
>0.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)>2t-c_i-c_\ell.
\]
This remains true on every support-contact and clamp boundary allowed by the
other strict hypotheses.

## Proof

Let
\[
L=a+b>0,\qquad
\Delta=\left|\det(\nabla g_i,\nabla g_\ell)\right|>0.
\]
Coordinate area in \((x,y)\) is \(\Delta\) times planar area.  Scale
\((x,y)\) by \(L\), and scale every area and every cap payment by
\(\Delta/L^2\).  The sign of \(\mathcal P\) is unchanged.  In these normalized
coordinates put
\[
\alpha=\frac aL,\qquad z=\frac1L.
\]
The normalized triangle has vertices
\[
(-\alpha,-\alpha),\quad
(1-\alpha,-\alpha),\quad
(-\alpha,1-\alpha)
\]
and normalized area
\[
k=\frac12.
\]
For the rest of the proof, \(M,m,k,t,c_j,\Psi_j,\mathcal P\) denote the
normalized quantities.

We first exhaust the clipping cells of \(K\).  Accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e` gives, in particular,
\[
M-m<\frac12<M+m.
\tag{1}
\]
If the \(q\)-cap is all of \(K\), then \(c_q=k\).  From \(M-m<k\) one
has \(M<k+m\), and hence
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
<\frac{4(k+m)}9<k+m,
\]
contrary to \(c_q+m<t\).  Thus the full \(q\)-cap cell is infeasible.

If \(a\le0\), then \(K\subseteq\{x\ge0,y\ge0\}\), so
\[
c_i=c_\ell=k.
\]
Moreover, \(D_0>m\) says \(2M>k+4m\).  Combining this with
\(M<k+m\) gives \(k>2m\), and then
\[
M+m<k+2m<2k.
\]
Consequently \(t<2k/3<k\), and therefore
\[
\mathcal P
=2k+\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)-2t>0.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(a>0\) but \(b\le0\), then \(x+y\le b-a<1\) on \(K\), so the
\(q\)-cap is full, which was just ruled out.  The only surviving
middle-cap cell therefore has
\[
a>0,\qquad b>0.
\tag{3}
\]
In this cell,
\[
c_i=c_\ell=\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
The tail condition also forces \(b-a>1\), since otherwise \(g_q\ge0\)
on all of \(K\).  Hence
\[
0<2\alpha+z<1
\]
and the remaining, nonempty nonfull \(q\)-cap is
\[
c_q=\frac{(2\alpha+z)^2}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

The strict tail and (1) imply two scalar inequalities needed below.
First,
\[
\frac{(2\alpha+z)^2}{2}+m
<t
=\frac{2(M+m+1/2)}9,
\]
so
\[
(2\alpha+z)^2
<\frac{4M-14m+2}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(M+m>1/2\),
\[
5M+14m=5(M+m)+9m>\frac52>2.
\]
It follows that the right side of (6) is strictly smaller than \(M\).
Thus
\[
2\alpha<\sqrt M.
\tag{7}
\]
Second, \(M-m<1/2\) gives \(M<1/2+m\), and the strict tail gives
\[
m+2\alpha^2
<\frac{2(1+2m)}9.
\]
Therefore
\[
18\alpha^2<2-5m<2,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\alpha<\frac13.
\tag{8}
\]

We next record the entire vertexwise midpoint constraint.  Write the
center of \(A_r\) as \(z_r\), and let \(w_{rx},w_{ry},w_{r,x+y}\)
denote its widths in the three facet-normal directions \(x,y,x+y\).
The midpoint body is a centrally symmetric zonotope centered at
\[
\bar z=\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}.
\]
Checking its midpoint vertices against the three facets of \(K\) is
equivalent to
\[
\bar z_x-\frac{w_{0x}+w_{2x}}4\ge-\alpha,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\bar z_y-\frac{w_{0y}+w_{2y}}4\ge-\alpha,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\bar z_x+\bar z_y
+\frac{w_{0,x+y}+w_{2,x+y}}4
\le1-2\alpha.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, (9)-(10) give the support bounds
\[
R_i\ge w_{0x}+w_{2x}-2\alpha,
\qquad
R_\ell\ge w_{0y}+w_{2y}-2\alpha.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the compatibility used below is the actual common-coordinate,
vertexwise compatibility, not an area-only surrogate.

We prove a two-direction clamp lemma.  For \(r\ge1\) and \(u,v>0\),
define
\[
F_r(u,v)
=\min\left\{
\frac{[\,r(u-1)+v\,]_+^2}{u^2+v^2},\,1
\right\},
\qquad [s]_+=\max\{s,0\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
u_1u_2\ge1,\quad v_1v_2\ge1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_r(u_1,v_1)+F_r(u_2,v_2)\ge1.
\tag{14}
\]

To prove (14), first observe that \(F_r\) is nondecreasing in each
argument.  Put
\[
h=r(u-1)+v,\qquad D=u^2+v^2.
\]
On the only nonconstant branch \(0<h<\sqrt D\),
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\frac{h^2}{D}
=\frac{2h\,[\,r(v^2+u)-uv\,]}{D^2},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\frac{h^2}{D}
=\frac{2h\,[\,u^2+rv(1-u)\,]}{D^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
If the bracket in (16) were negative, then \(u>1\) and
\(rv(u-1)>u^2\), whence
\[
h^2-D
=r^2(u-1)^2+2rv(u-1)-u^2>0,
\]
contrary to the branch condition.  If the bracket in (15) were
negative, then
\[
uv>r(v^2+u),
\]
so \(v>r\) and \(u(v-r)>rv^2\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
h^2-D
&=(r^2-1)u^2+2ru(v-r)+r^2-2rv\\
&>(r^2-1)u^2+2r^2v^2+r^2-2rv>0,
\end{aligned}
\]
again a contradiction.  The last expression is positive because
\[
2r^2v^2-2rv+r^2
=2(rv-\tfrac12)^2+r^2-\tfrac12>0.
\]
Equations (15)-(16), together with continuity at \(h=0\) and
\(h=\sqrt D\), prove the claimed coordinatewise monotonicity.  In
particular, a saturated branch cannot re-enter the quadratic branch
while a coordinate increases, because the derivative on the
quadratic side is nonnegative.

By monotonicity, it is enough to prove
\[
F_r(u,v)+F_r(1/u,1/v)\ge1.
\tag{17}
\]
Put
\[
A=ru+v-r,\qquad
B=u+rv-ruv,\qquad
D=u^2+v^2.
\]
The two unclamped squared ratios in (17) are \(A_+^2/D\) and
\(B_+^2/D\).  If \(A\le0\), write \(s=1-u\).  Then
\[
0<v\le rs,\qquad
B=u+rv(1-u)>0,
\]
and
\[
B^2-D
=v\{2rs(1-s)-v(1-r^2s^2)\}\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, (18) is immediate when \(rs\ge1\); when \(rs<1\), use
\(v\le rs\) to bound the expression in braces below by
\[
rs\{1-2s+r^2s^2\}
=rs\{(1-s)^2+(r^2-1)s^2\}\ge0.
\]
Thus the reciprocal term is saturated.  If \(B\le0\), write
\(s=u-1>0\).  Then \(rsv\ge1+s\), \(A=rs+v>0\), and
\[
A^2-D
=r^2s^2+2rsv-(1+s)^2
\ge(r^2-1)s^2+1>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus the first term is saturated.  Finally, if \(A,B>0\), the exact
identity
\[
A^2+B^2-D
=r(u-1)^2\bigl(r-2v+rv^2\bigr)
\tag{20}
\]
has a nonnegative right side, because
\[
r-2v+rv^2
=(r-1)(1+v^2)+(v-1)^2\ge0.
\]
If either ratio in (20) is at least one, its clamp is saturated; if
both are below one, (20) says their sum is at least one.  This proves
(17), and monotonicity then proves (14).  The proof includes the
zero, quadratic, saturated, and equality branches of both clamps.

Apply (14) to the endpoint widths.  Put
\[
p=\sqrt M,\qquad q=\sqrt m,\qquad r=\frac pq\ge1,
\]
\[
u_1=\frac{w_{0x}}p,\quad
u_2=\frac{w_{0y}}p,\qquad
v_1=\frac{w_{2x}}q,\quad
v_2=\frac{w_{2y}}q.
\tag{21}
\]
Each parallelogram is contained in its axis-parallel bounding
rectangle, so
\[
w_{0x}w_{0y}\ge M,\qquad
w_{2x}w_{2y}\ge m.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
u_1u_2\ge1,\qquad v_1v_2\ge1.
\tag{22}
\]
For either direction \(j\), its denominator is
\[
\sigma_j=u_j^2+v_j^2.
\]
Equations (7), (12), and monotonicity of the clamped profile give
\[
\frac{\Psi_j(R_j)}m
\ge
F_r(u_j,v_j).
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\frac{R_j}{q}
\ge ru_j+v_j-\frac{2\alpha}{q}
>ru_j+v_j-r,
\]
and (23) is exactly the positive-part quadratic branch followed by
the full-\(m\) clamp.  Combining (14), (22), and (23) yields the
shape-uniform but genuinely two-directional payment
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)\ge m.
\tag{24}
\]

It remains to compare areas.  The definitions give the exact
identity
\[
\mathcal P
=c_i+c_\ell+\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)-2t.
\tag{25}
\]
Put
\[
\eta=\frac12+m-M>0,
\]
where positivity is the strict left inequality in (1).  Then
\[
t=\frac{2(1+2m-\eta)}9.
\]
Using (4), (8), and (24) in (25) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P
&\ge(1-\alpha)^2+m-\frac{4(1+2m-\eta)}9\\
&=\left((1-\alpha)^2-\frac49\right)
  +\frac m9+\frac{4\eta}9
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
This proves the claimed strict certificate on the only surviving
middle-cap cell.  Together with (2) and the infeasibility of the full
\(q\)-cap cells, every clipping cell of \(K\) has been covered.
The definition (13) and cases (18)-(20) cover all nine pairs of
endpoint zero/quadratic/plateau cells and all their boundaries.

For sharpness of the normalized minimization, the strict lower bound
has infimum zero.  Here is an explicit rational sequence.  Let
\(n\ge18\) be an integer and put
\[
x=\frac1{n^2},\qquad
L=n^4,\qquad
\alpha=\frac13-x,\qquad
a=\alpha L,\qquad b=(1-\alpha)L.
\]
In normalized coordinates set
\[
S_x=\frac23+\frac1n,\qquad
S_y=\frac34+\frac1n,
\]
\[
C=\left(-\alpha+\frac{S_x}{4},
         -\alpha+\frac{S_y}{4}\right),
\]
and define the actual rational rectangles
\[
A_0
=L\left(C+
\left[-\frac13,\frac13\right]
\times
\left[-\frac38,\frac38\right]\right),
\]
\[
A_2
=L\left(C+
\left[-\frac1{2n},\frac1{2n}\right]^2\right).
\tag{27}
\]
Their normalized areas are
\[
M=\frac12,\qquad m=x,\qquad k=\frac12.
\]
The midpoint rectangle has lower corner
\((-\alpha,-\alpha)\), and its upper-right vertex satisfies the top
facet because
\[
\frac{S_x+S_y}{2}
=\frac{17}{24}+\frac1n<1.
\]
Thus every midpoint vertex lies in \(K\).  The residual ratios are
\[
\frac mM=2x<\frac9{25},\qquad \frac kM=1,
\]
so the strict first branch of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`
holds.  Also
\[
D_0-m
=\frac{1/2-x}{3}-x>0.
\]
Since \(z=1/L=x^2\), the exact tail slack is
\[
t-m-c_q
=\frac{x(10-48x+36x^2-9x^3)}{18}>0.
\tag{28}
\]

The two normalized support sums and denominators are
\[
R_i=\frac1n+\frac2{n^2},\qquad
\sigma_i=\frac{17}{9},
\]
\[
R_\ell=\frac1{12}+\frac1n+\frac2{n^2},\qquad
\sigma_\ell=\frac{17}{8}.
\]
For \(n\ge18\), the first profile is on its quadratic branch and the
second is on its full-\(m\) plateau:
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)
=\frac{9x}{17}\left(1+\frac2n\right)^2,
\qquad
\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)=x.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathcal P
=\frac{17x}{9}+x^2
  +\frac{9x}{17}\left(1+\frac2n\right)^2
\longrightarrow0^+.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus zero is the exact infimum after the natural normalization
\(\Delta/L^2\), but no feasible point in the declared strict family
attains it.
