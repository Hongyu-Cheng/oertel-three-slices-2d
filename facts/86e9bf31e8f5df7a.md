## Statement

For a row vector \(h\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(z\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
\mathcal C(h;z)
:=
\frac{\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:h\lambda\geq z\}\right|}{|\Delta|}
\]
for its normalized closed cap in the standard triangle \(\Delta\).

Work in the \(t\leq1/2\) orbit and the five middle sign chambers of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98`.  Thus, up to its stated row and column symmetries, the pairs \((J_1,J_2)\) are
\[
(\{2\},\{3\}),\quad
(\{2\},\{1,3\}),\quad
(\{2\},\{2,3\}),\quad
(\{1,2\},\{2,3\}),\quad
(\{2,3\},\{2,3\}),
\tag{1}
\]
and the common one-positive third middle row can, after interchanging its two nonpositive entries, be written
\[
g=(p,-a,-b),
\qquad p>0,\qquad a\geq b\geq0,\qquad p-a-b>3.
\tag{2}
\]
The open chambers have \(a,b>0\); the inequalities below also include \(a=b\) and \(b=0\).  Put
\[
c=\mathcal C(g;0)=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)},
\qquad
\delta=t-c.
\tag{3}
\]
For a possible strict row in the surviving system, accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98` gives
\[
0<\delta<A\leq B.
\tag{4}
\]

Define the inverse triangle-capacity function \(\Theta:[0,1]\to[-a,p]\) as follows.  If \(a>b\), put
\[
j=\frac{a-b}{p+a}
\]
and
\[
\Theta(w)=
\begin{cases}
-a+\sqrt{(a-b)(p+a)w},&0\leq w\leq j,\\[1mm]
-a+(p+a)w,&j\leq w\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
If \(a=b\), put
\[
\Theta(w)=-a+(p+a)w
\qquad(0\leq w\leq1).
\tag{6}
\]
Let
\[
I=[\sqrt A,\,2-\sqrt B],
\tag{7}
\]
and, for \(\rho\in I\), \(\lambda\in[0,\delta]\), set
\[
\eta=2-\rho,\qquad
w_A(\lambda)=A-\sqrt{A\lambda},\qquad
w_B(\lambda)=B-\sqrt{B(\delta-\lambda)},
\tag{8}
\]
\[
G(\rho,\lambda)
=
\rho\,\Theta\!\left(\frac{w_A(\lambda)}{\rho^2}\right)
+\eta\,\Theta\!\left(\frac{w_B(\lambda)}{\eta^2}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
The arguments of \(\Theta\) in (9) lie in \([0,1]\), and \(G\) is continuous on the closed rectangle \(I\times[0,\delta]\), including all branch boundaries.

If
\[
\boxed{\
\min_{\rho\in I,\ 0\leq\lambda\leq\delta}
G(\rho,\lambda)\geq0,
\ }
\tag{10}
\]
then every pair \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) satisfying
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\geq0
\qquad(k=1,2,3)
\tag{12}
\]
and every allocation \(\tau\in\mathbb R\) satisfy the endpoint floor
\[
A\mathcal C(gU;-\tau)+B\mathcal C(gV;\tau)\geq\delta.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently the full row mass is at least \(c+\delta=t\), so its strict row inequality fails.

Condition (10) is an explicit chamber-wide parameter region depending only on
\[
(p,a,b,A,B,t-c).
\]
It closes its intersection with every one of the five chambers in (1); no additional feature of \(J_1,J_2\) enters.  Two simpler sufficient subregions are the following.  Define
\[
H(\rho)
=-2a+(p+a)\left[
\frac A\rho+\frac B{2-\rho}
-\sqrt{\delta\left(\frac A{\rho^2}
+\frac B{(2-\rho)^2}\right)}
\right].
\tag{14}
\]
Then (10) holds if
\[
\min_{\rho\in I}H(\rho)\geq0.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, if
\[
u=A-\sqrt{A\delta},\qquad
v=B-\sqrt{B\delta},
\tag{16}
\]
then the closed inequality
\[
\boxed{\
(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2\geq\frac{4a}{p+a}
\ }
\tag{17}
\]
implies (10).

The region (10) is not the whole five-chamber domain.  An exact new survivor of all middle/scalar restrictions of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98` is
\[
A=\frac{31}{250},\qquad
B=\frac{1123}{1000},\qquad
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-69&275/2&-69\\
-50&-81/2&90\\
120&-96&-20
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{18}
\]
It lies in the open chamber \((J_1,J_2)=(\{2\},\{3\})\), but for its third row \(g=(120,-96,-20)\),
\[
\min_{\rho\in I,\ 0\leq\lambda\leq\delta}G(\rho,\lambda)<0.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (18) is not an endpoint construction; it is an exact witness that the determinant-capacity envelope (10) leaves a genuine part of an open chamber unresolved.

## Proof

We first derive (10).  Accepted fact `00691900b5632946` proves the determinant-scale reduction for arbitrary compatible endpoints, without fixing either determinant sign.  For completeness, let
\[
\ell_k=\min_jU_{kj},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s=\mathbf1^\top\ell.
\]
Nondegeneracy of \(U,V\) and (12) give \(-1<s<1\).  Put
\[
\rho=1-s,\qquad \eta=1+s=2-\rho,
\]
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\rho},
\qquad
Y=\frac{V+\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\eta}.
\tag{20}
\]
Then \(X,Y\) are entrywise nonnegative and column-stochastic.  Moreover
\[
U=(\rho I+\ell\mathbf1^\top)X,\qquad
V=(\eta I-\ell\mathbf1^\top)Y,
\]
so the matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
|\det X|=\frac A{\rho^2},\qquad
|\det Y|=\frac B{\eta^2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(X\Delta,Y\Delta\subseteq\Delta\), both determinant magnitudes in (21) are at most one.  Hence \(\rho\in I\).  This is the only use of endpoint determinant data, so both signs remain allowed.

Fix \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), and put
\[
d=g\ell+\tau,\qquad
r=-\frac d\rho,\qquad
z=\frac d\eta.
\tag{22}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal C(gU;-\tau)=\mathcal C(gX;r),\qquad
\mathcal C(gV;\tau)=\mathcal C(gY;z),
\tag{23}
\]
and the allocation identity is
\[
\rho r+\eta z=0.
\tag{24}
\]

We use the two exact geometric statements proved in accepted fact `2aa015ef34a96b3e`.  First, if an upper cap of a triangle \(T\) has fraction \(q\), then its lower complement contains a triangle of area at least
\[
(1-\sqrt q)|T|.
\tag{25}
\]
This remains literal for empty or full caps, threshold equality, and repeated vertex values.  Second, for
\[
K_z=\{\lambda\in\Delta:g\lambda\leq z\},
\]
the maximum normalized area \(m(z)\) of a triangle contained in \(K_z\) is, when \(a>b\),
\[
m(z)=
\begin{cases}
0,&z\leq-a,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(z+a)^2}{(a-b)(p+a)},&-a\leq z\leq-b,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{z+a}{p+a},&-b\leq z\leq p,\\[3mm]
1,&z\geq p.
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]
Its inverse on \([0,1]\) is exactly (5).  When \(a=b\), one has
\[
g\lambda=-a+(p+a)\lambda_1.
\]
The lower section is a quadrilateral whose largest inscribed vertex triangle has normalized area \((z+a)/(p+a)\); this gives (6) directly.  Formula (26) is also continuous at \(b=0\), so all zero and repeated boundaries in (2) are retained.

Suppose, contrary to (13), that
\[
Aq_X+Bq_Y<\delta,
\qquad
q_X:=\mathcal C(gX;r),\quad
q_Y:=\mathcal C(gY;z).
\tag{27}
\]
Choose \(\lambda\) strictly between \(Aq_X\) and \(\delta-Bq_Y\).  By (21), \(X\Delta\) and \(Y\Delta\) have normalized areas \(A/\rho^2\) and \(B/\eta^2\).  Equations (25) and (27) therefore give a triangle in the \(X\)-lower section of area strictly greater than
\[
\frac A{\rho^2}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{A}}\right)
=\frac{w_A(\lambda)}{\rho^2},
\tag{28}
\]
and a triangle in the \(Y\)-lower section of area strictly greater than
\[
\frac B{\eta^2}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\delta-\lambda}{B}}\right)
=\frac{w_B(\lambda)}{\eta^2}.
\tag{29}
\]
The inverse capacity (5)-(6) now implies
\[
r>
\Theta\!\left(\frac{w_A(\lambda)}{\rho^2}\right),
\qquad
z>
\Theta\!\left(\frac{w_B(\lambda)}{\eta^2}\right).
\tag{30}
\]
Multiplying by \(\rho,\eta>0\), adding, and using (24) gives
\[
0>G(\rho,\lambda).
\tag{31}
\]
This contradicts (10), proving the endpoint floor (13).

We next prove the simpler tests.  For every \(w\in[0,1]\),
\[
\Theta(w)\geq-a+(p+a)w.
\tag{32}
\]
It is equality on the linear branch.  On the square-root branch, (32) follows after squaring from
\[
w\leq\frac{a-b}{p+a}.
\]
Thus, for every \(\rho,\lambda\),
\[
G(\rho,\lambda)
\geq
-2a+(p+a)\left[
\frac{w_A(\lambda)}{\rho}
+\frac{w_B(\lambda)}{\eta}
\right].
\tag{33}
\]
For fixed \(\rho\), the bracket in (33) equals
\[
\frac A\rho+\frac B\eta
-
\left(
\frac{\sqrt A}{\rho}\sqrt\lambda
+\frac{\sqrt B}{\eta}\sqrt{\delta-\lambda}
\right).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz shows that its exact minimum over
\(\lambda\in[0,\delta]\) is
\[
\frac A\rho+\frac B\eta
-\sqrt{\delta\left(\frac A{\rho^2}+\frac B{\eta^2}\right)}.
\]
This proves (14)-(15).

Also \(w_A(\lambda)\geq u\) and \(w_B(\lambda)\geq v\) for every
\(\lambda\in[0,\delta]\).  Hence (33) and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
G(\rho,\lambda)
\geq
-2a+(p+a)\left(\frac u\rho+\frac v\eta\right)
\geq
-2a+\frac{p+a}{2}(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2.
\tag{34}
\]
Condition (17) makes the last expression nonnegative, proving that
closed sufficient region.

Nothing in (20)-(34) uses \(J_1,J_2\), or any strict sign in the other
two middle rows.  Accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98` supplies the same
one-positive row (2) in all five chambers.  Thus (10), (15), and (17)
apply to every chamber in (1).  Closed caps, the separate definition
(6), and continuity of (5) at its join retain zero entries, repeated
values, and cap-order boundaries.

It remains to audit the limit example (18).  Its columns sum to one,
\[
\det L=28910,

and its row sums are
\[
-\frac12,\qquad-\frac12,\qquad4.

Thus its first two row means are negative, its third row mean is

c_1=\frac{75625}{170569},\qquad
c_2=\frac{90}{203},\qquad

\tag{35}
\]
Here
\[
t=\frac{749}{1500},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{1999}{4500},\qquad

\tag{36}
\]
The tight cap and total-budget margins are
\[
\kappa-c_1=\frac{654931}{767560500}>0,\qquad
\kappa-c_2=\frac{797}{913500}>0,
\tag{37}

\[
3t-\left(A+c_1+c_2+c_3\right)
=\frac{82291061}{7419751500}>0,
\qquad

The second inequality in (1) of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98`

Writing \(r=A/B\) and \(S=1/B\), the residual and ratio margins are

Then

Since \(R>0\), (42) proves after two reversible squarings

Thus (18) satisfies all the stated middle/scalar restrictions.

To prove (19), take
\[
\rho=\frac25,\qquad \eta=\frac85,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad

\tag{43}
\]
The scale is feasible because
\[

\]
Moreover
\[
\frac9{10000}
<
\frac{A\delta}{2}
=\frac{2511}{1750000}
<
\frac1{400},
\tag{44}
\]
\[
\frac1{100}
<
\frac{B\delta}{2}
=\frac{90963}{7000000}
<
\frac1{25}.
\tag{45}
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{37}{500}
<
w_A(\lambda)
<
\frac{47}{500},
\qquad
\frac{923}{1000}
<
w_B(\lambda)
<
\frac{1023}{1000}.
\tag{46}
\]
The lower bounds put both arguments of \(\Theta\) strictly on its
linear branch, because
\[
\frac{37}{500}>\frac{38}{675}=j\rho^2,
\qquad
\frac{923}{1000}>\frac{608}{675}=j\eta^2.
\tag{47}
\]
Using the upper bounds in (46) and the linear formula in (5) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
G\left(\frac25,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
&=-192+216\left(
\frac52w_A(\lambda)+\frac58w_B(\lambda)
\right)\\
&<
-192+216\left(
\frac52\frac{47}{500}
+\frac58\frac{1023}{1000}
\right)\\
&=-\frac{627}{200}<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
This proves (19) exactly and completes the proof.
