For completeness, the next missing joint invariant follows directly
from the same endpoint segment used in accepted fact
`6d887319cbb83136`.  Convexity of \(A_2\) gives
\[
 D=\operatorname{conv}\{(-\theta,0),(0,-w_0)\}\subseteq A_2.
\]
If some \((X,Y)\in P_0^\circ\) satisfied
\[
 \frac X\theta+\frac Y{w_0}>1,
\]
there would be a \(\lambda\in(0,1)\) with
\[
 X>\lambda\theta,\qquad Y>(1-\lambda)w_0.
\]
Adding
\[
 (-\lambda\theta,-(1-\lambda)w_0)\in D
\]
and dividing by two would put the midpoint strictly in
\(\{r>0,s>0\}\).  Since \(f_j+f_k+f_\ell=-1\), this also has
\(f_k=-1-r-s<0\).  The inequalities remain strict on a neighborhood
of \((X,Y)\), producing positive middle-cell area.  Hence zero
forbidden-cell area forces
\[
 P_0^\circ\subseteq
 \left\{(X,Y):w_0X+\theta Y\le\theta w_0\right\}.
\]
Closure gives the support inequality stated in ADVANCES.  The three
adjoined scalar rows are only area shadows of this pointwise
containment; they do not encode the support function of the common
\(A_0\) polygon.
