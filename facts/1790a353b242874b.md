## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty convex bodies, let \(a_i=|A_i|\), and let \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\). Let \(X_0\) and \(X_2\) be independent random variables, with \(X_i\) uniform on \(A_i\), and let \(\nu\) be the law of their midpoint \((X_0+X_2)/2\). Define
\[
\eta(E)=|A_1\cap E|+m\nu(E).
\]
For every \(c\in A_1\), \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
P=\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|A_0\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le\lambda\}\right|
+\left|A_1\cap P\right|\\
&\qquad+
\left|A_2\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le-\lambda\}\right|
\ge \eta(P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The two slice-parallel halfspaces through \((1,c)\) have masses \(a_0+a_1\) and \(a_1+a_2\), both at least \(\eta(\mathbb R^2)=a_1+m\).

Nevertheless, take
\[
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
A_0=A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1/2)\}.
\tag{2}
\]
These are compatible rational polygons. Let \(X_0,X_2\) be independent and uniform on \(A_0,A_2\), respectively, and define \(\nu\) and \(\eta\) as above. If
\[
d_\eta(c)=\inf\{\eta(Q):Q\subset\mathbb R^2
\text{ is a closed halfplane containing }c\},
\]
then
\[
\max_{c\in A_1}d_\eta(c)
\le \frac{44237}{160000}
<\frac5{18}
=\frac49(a_1+m).
\tag{3}
\]
Hence no \(c\in A_1\) has \(\eta\)-halfplane depth \(4(a_1+m)/9\).

## Proof

By the accepted normal-form lemma \(400272f6de6c67bb\), the left side of (1) is exactly the mass of the tilted normal-form halfspace through \((1,c)\) with parameters \(q,\lambda\).

Set
\[
p_0=\mathbb P(\langle q,X_0-c\rangle\le\lambda),
\qquad
p_2=\mathbb P(\langle q,X_2-c\rangle\le-\lambda).
\]
If
\[
\left\langle q,\frac{X_0+X_2}{2}-c\right\rangle\le0,
\]
then at least one of
\[
\langle q,X_0-c\rangle\le\lambda,
\qquad
\langle q,X_2-c\rangle\le-\lambda
\]
holds: otherwise their strict reverse inequalities would add to
\(\langle q,X_0+X_2-2c\rangle>0\). Therefore
\[
\nu(P)\le p_0+p_2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
a_0p_0+a_2p_2
\ge m(p_0+p_2)
\ge m\nu(P).
\]
Adding \(|A_1\cap P|\) proves (1). The asserted horizontal bounds follow immediately from \(m\le a_0,a_2\).

For (2), write \(\Delta=A_1\) and \(K=A_0=A_2=\frac12\Delta\), with the homothety centered at \((0,0)\). Since \(K\) is convex,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=K\subseteq\Delta.
\]
Thus the accepted compatibility lemma \(8ad0785e5b4ae9ef\) supplies a compact polytope having exactly these three integer-height sections. Moreover,
\[
a_1=\frac12,\qquad a_0=a_2=m=\frac18,
\qquad a_1+m=\frac58.
\tag{4}
\]

Use the barycentric coordinates
\[
\ell_1(x,y)=1-x-y,\qquad \ell_2(x,y)=x,\qquad \ell_3(x,y)=y
\]
on \(\Delta\). For \(c\in\Delta\), put \(t_i=\ell_i(c)\), so \(t_i\ge0\) and \(t_1+t_2+t_3=1\). The closed vertex halfplane
\[
Q_i(c)=\{z:\ell_i(z)\ge t_i\}
\]
contains \(c\), and its triangular intersection with \(\Delta\) has area
\[
|\Delta\cap Q_i(c)|=a_1(1-t_i)^2.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(U,V\) be independent random variables, each uniform on \(\Delta\). Then \(U/2\) and \(V/2\) are independent and uniform on \(A_0\) and \(A_2\), respectively. Hence the midpoint having law \(\nu\) is distributed as
\[
M=\frac{U+V}{4}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(i=2,3\), the random variable \(R=\ell_i(U)\) has density \(2(1-r)\) on \([0,1]\). Since \(U,V\) are independent, \(R=\ell_i(U)\) and \(S=\ell_i(V)\) are independent copies, and
\[
\ell_i(M)=\frac{R+S}{4}.
\]
For \(1/4\le t\le1/2\), set \(R'=1-R\), \(S'=1-S\). The variables \(R'\) and \(S'\) are independent, and each has density \(2r\) on \([0,1]\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\ell_i(M)\ge t)
&=\mathbb P(R'+S'\le2(1-2t))\\
&=\int_0^{2(1-2t)}2r
   \int_0^{2(1-2t)-r}2s\,ds\,dr\\
&=\frac83(1-2t)^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For \(t\ge1/2\), this probability is zero. Combining (4), (5), and (7), for \(i=2,3\) and \(1/4\le t_i\le1/2\),
\[
\frac{\eta(Q_i(c))}{a_1}
=(1-t_i)^2+\frac23(1-2t_i)^4=:F(t_i).
\tag{8}
\]
Here
\[
F'(t)=-2(1-t)-\frac{16}{3}(1-2t)^3<0
\]
on \([1/4,1/2]\). For \(t\ge1/2\), the normalized mass is simply \((1-t)^2\).

For \(Q_1(c)\), the bound \(\nu(Q_1(c))\le1\) gives
\[
\frac{\eta(Q_1(c))}{a_1}
\le(1-t_1)^2+\frac14.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(t_1\ge9/20\), (9) yields
\[
\frac{\eta(Q_1(c))}{a_1}
\le\left(\frac{11}{20}\right)^2+\frac14
=\frac{221}{400}
<\frac{44237}{80000}.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(t_1\le9/20\), then
\[
\max\{t_2,t_3\}\ge\frac{1-t_1}{2}\ge\frac{11}{40}.
\]
Choose \(j\in\{2,3\}\) with \(t_j\ge11/40\). Monotonicity in (8), together with the formula for \(t_j\ge1/2\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\eta(Q_j(c))}{a_1}
&\le F\left(\frac{11}{40}\right)\\
&=\left(\frac{29}{40}\right)^2
 +\frac23\left(\frac9{20}\right)^4
=\frac{44237}{80000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus every \(c\in\Delta\) lies in one of three explicit closed halfplanes whose \(\eta\)-mass is at most
\[
a_1\frac{44237}{80000}
=\frac{44237}{160000}.
\]
This proves the first inequality in (3). Finally,
\[
18\cdot44237=796266<800000=5\cdot160000,
\]
so \(44237/160000<5/18\), while (4) gives
\[
\frac49(a_1+m)=\frac49\cdot\frac58=\frac5{18}.
\]
This proves (3).
