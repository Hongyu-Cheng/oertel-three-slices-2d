## Statement

Define
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le\frac12,\\[2pt]
2-r,&\frac12<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
Use the area-preserving coordinates
\[
u=y,\qquad v=x+y,\qquad (x,y)=(v-u,u).
\]
Put
\[
\theta=\frac1{1000},\qquad
\eta=\frac1{2000},\qquad
\ell=\frac{5488420}{5488550199}.
\]
Let \(P\) be the triangle and \(Q\) the quadrilateral whose \((u,v)\)-coordinate vertex lists are
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(1,-\frac{97999}{1000}\right),
\left(99,-\frac{1951}{49000}\right),
\left(99,\frac1{1000}\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-105,-\frac1{2000}\right),
\left(-105+\ell,-\frac1{2000}\right),
\left(35,\frac{279999}{2000}\right),
\left(35,\frac{1960193}{14000}\right)
\right\}.
\]
The displayed points denote the corresponding rational vertices
\((x,y)=(v-u,u)\) in the original coordinates. These are compact
two-dimensional rational convex polygons. Their areas are
\[
M=|P|=2,\qquad
m=|Q|=1+70\ell
=\frac{5872739599}{5488550199}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac12<r:=\frac mM
=\frac{5872739599}{10977100398}
<\rho.
\]
In particular, this \(r\) lies in the second branch of the displayed
definition of \(U\), so \(U(r)=2-r\).
Set
\[
\delta=\frac{4852951}{49000},\qquad a=\frac1{100}.
\]
Then
\[
\min_Px=-\delta,\qquad P\subseteq\{y\ge0\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
d_2:=|Q\cap\{y\le0\}|
=\frac{75686278552276995409}
{120495872477237952816},
\]
and
\[
b:=|Q\cap\{x\ge\delta+2\}|=m.
\]
These values satisfy
\[
0<a<m,\qquad 0<d_2<m,\qquad a+d_2<m,
\]
\[
4\min\{a,d_2\}+5\max\{a,d_2\}<4m,
\qquad b>m-a.
\]

Both required complete shifted profiles hold. In the \(y=u\) row,
\[
|P\cap\{u\le s\}|+|Q\cap\{u\le-s\}|\ge d_2
\quad\text{for every }s<0,
\]
whereas the same sum is strictly below \(d_2\) for every \(0<s<1\).
In the \(x+y=v\) row, with
\[
p:=\frac1{2000000},
\]
one has
\[
|P\cap\{v\ge0\}|=p,\qquad
|Q\cap\{v\ge0\}|=m-p.
\]
In particular both caps are strictly between \(0\) and \(m\), their
sum is \(m\), and
\[
|P\cap\{v\ge-s\}|+|Q\cap\{v\ge s\}|\ge m
\quad\text{for every }s<0,
\]
while the same sum is strictly below \(m\) for every sufficiently
small \(s>0\).

Finally, exact Minkowski addition and clipping give
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\cap\{y>0\}\right|
=\frac{24815084561147}{13446851940200}.
\]
Since the computed \(r\) lies in the second branch, \(U(r)=2-r\), and
\[
\frac{7M\,U(r)-2M-2m}{9}+d_2
=\frac{146529527093}{65862131952}.
\]
The strict reverse inequality is certified by
\[
\frac{146529527093}{65862131952}
-\frac{24815084561147}{13446851940200}
=\frac{30608163322043}{80681111641200}>0.
\]

## Proof

The map \((u,v)\mapsto(v-u,u)\) has determinant of absolute value
\(1\), so all areas may be computed in \((u,v)\)-coordinates. For
\(P\), the two edge vectors based at its first vertex have the same
\(u\)-component \(98\), while the \(v\)-coordinates of the last two
vertices differ by \(2/49\). Hence
\[
|P|=\frac12\cdot98\cdot\frac2{49}=2.
\]
For \(Q\), first omit its second vertex. The resulting triangle has
edge vectors
\[
(140,140),\qquad
\left(140,140+\frac1{70}\right),
\]
so it has area \(1\). The added triangle formed by the first, second,
and third vertices has area \(70\ell\). All four successive cross
products are positive, so the displayed order is convex and
\[
|Q|=1+70\ell=m.
\]

The exact inequality
\[
0<\ell<\frac1{1000}
\]
follows from
\[
5488420000<5488550199.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac12<r<\frac{107}{200}.
\]
Also \(107/200<\rho\), because this is equivalent to
\[
16\sqrt{26}<109,
\]
and squaring gives \(6656<11881\). Therefore the computed
\(r\) satisfies
\[
\frac12<r<\rho
\]
and lies in the second branch of the complete piecewise definition
of \(U\). Hence \(U(r)=2-r\).

At the three vertices of \(P\), the values of \(x=v-u\) are
\[
-\frac{98999}{1000},\qquad
-\frac{4852951}{49000},\qquad
-\frac{98999}{1000}.
\]
Therefore \(\min_Px=-4852951/49000=-\delta\). All three
\(u\)-coordinates are positive, so \(P\subseteq\{y\ge0\}\).
For \(Q\), the minimum value of \(x=v-u\) occurs at its second
vertex and equals
\[
\frac{209999}{2000}-\ell.
\]
The difference from the required threshold is
\[
\left(\frac{209999}{2000}-\ell\right)-(\delta+2)
=\frac{388049}{98000}-\ell>0.
\]
Thus every point of \(Q\) lies in \(\{x\ge\delta+2\}\), proving
\(b=m\).

To compute \(d_2\), again decompose \(Q\) into the original triangle
and the added triangle. The \(u\le0\) cap of the original triangle is
the corner at \(u=-105\) with linear ratio \(105/140=3/4\), so its
area is \(9/16\). The added triangle has area \(70\ell\). Its
\(u\ge0\) cap at the vertex with \(u=35\) has area
\[
70\ell\,
\frac{35^2}{140(140-\ell)}
=\frac{1225\ell}{2(140-\ell)}.
\]
Hence
\[
d_2=\frac9{16}+70\ell
-\frac{1225\ell}{2(140-\ell)},
\]
which reduces to the rational number in the statement.
In particular,
\[
\frac9{16}<d_2
<\frac9{16}+\frac7{100}
=\frac{253}{400}.
\]
Since \(m>1\), these bounds give
\[
0<d_2<m,\qquad
a+d_2<\frac1{100}+\frac{253}{400}
=\frac{257}{400}<1<m.
\]
They also give \(a<d_2\) and
\[
4a+5d_2
<\frac1{25}+5\frac{253}{400}
=\frac{1281}{400}<4<4m.
\]
Finally \(b=m>m-a\), so all scalar and far-cap conditions hold.

The complete \(u\)-profile is immediate but not merely local. If
\(s<0\), then \(P\cap\{u\le s\}\) is empty and
\[
Q\cap\{u\le0\}\subseteq Q\cap\{u\le-s\},
\]
which proves the required inequality for every negative \(s\). If
\(0<s<1\), the \(P\)-cap is still empty. The line \(u=0\) crosses the
interior of \(Q\), so \(Q\cap\{-s<u\le0\}\) has positive area. Hence
\[
|Q\cap\{u\le-s\}|<|Q\cap\{u\le0\}|=d_2
\]
for every \(0<s<1\), proving the required strict right side.

It remains to verify the complete proper-proper \(v\)-profile.
The positive \(v\)-cap of \(P\) is the triangular corner at its last
vertex. Its two full vertex depths in the \(v\)-direction are
\(98\) and \(2/49\), while the depth to the cutting line \(v=0\) is
\(\theta=1/1000\). Therefore
\[
|P\cap\{v\ge0\}|
=2\frac{\theta^2}{98(2/49)}
=\frac{\theta^2}{2}
=p.
\]

For \(Q\), let \(w_Q(v)\) denote the \(u\)-length of its section at
height \(v\). On the interval \(-\eta\le v\le0\), direct interpolation
along the two incident edges gives
\[
w_Q(v)=\ell+c(v+\eta),\qquad
c:=\frac1{9801}-\frac{\ell}{140}
=\frac{520796}{5488550199}.
\]
The definition of \(\ell\) gives the exact identities
\[
\ell\eta+\frac{c\eta^2}{2}
=\frac1{2000000}=p,
\]
\[
w_Q(0)-\theta
=\ell+c\eta-\theta
=\frac{130199}{5488550199000}
=:A>0.
\]
Thus \(|Q\cap\{v\le0\}|=p\), and consequently
\[
|Q\cap\{v\ge0\}|=m-p.
\]

Let \(t>0\). For \(0<t<\eta\), the area gained by the \(Q\)-cap when
its threshold moves from \(0\) to \(-t\), minus the area lost by the
\(P\)-cap when its threshold moves from \(0\) to \(t\), is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^t\bigl(w_Q(-\tau)-(\theta-\tau)\bigr)\,d\tau\\
&\qquad=
\int_0^t\bigl(A+(1-c)\tau\bigr)\,d\tau>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the section length of the triangular \(P\)-cap at height
\(v=\tau\) is exactly \(\theta-\tau\). If \(t\ge\eta\), the \(Q\)-cap
has already gained its entire negative cap of area \(p\), while the
\(P\)-cap can lose at most its positive cap of the same area \(p\).
It follows for every \(t>0\), equivalently for every \(s=-t<0\), that
\[
|P\cap\{v\ge-s\}|+|Q\cap\{v\ge s\}|\ge m.
\]

For the strict right side, put
\[
s_0=\frac{A}{2(1-c)}>0.
\]
In fact \(0<c<1/2\) and \(0<A<\eta\), so
\[
0<s_0<A<\eta.
\]
Thus \(s_0\) lies inside the same affine section intervals, and for
\(0\le\tau<s_0\) one has
\[
w_Q(\tau)-w_P(-\tau)
=\bigl(\theta+A+c\tau\bigr)-(\theta+\tau)
=A-(1-c)\tau>\frac A2.
\]
Integration from \(0\) to any \(0<s<s_0\) shows that the area lost by
the positive \(Q\)-cap is strictly larger than the area gained by the
positive \(P\)-cap. Hence
\[
|P\cap\{v\ge-s\}|+|Q\cap\{v\ge s\}|<m
\qquad(0<s<s_0),
\]
which proves the genuine proper-proper crossing.

For completeness, compute the directional midpoint cap itself.
Adding the cyclic edge vectors of the two polygons gives the exact
cyclic vertex list of \(P+Q\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\left(-104,-\frac{195999}{2000}\right),\quad
\left(-104+\ell,-\frac{195999}{2000}\right),\\
\left(-6+\ell,-\frac{3951}{98000}\right),\quad
\left(134,\frac{13716049}{98000}\right),\\
\left(134,\frac{1960207}{14000}\right),\quad
\left(36,\frac{588207}{14000}\right).
\end{gathered}
\]
Equivalently, direct inspection of all twelve pairwise vertex sums
gives this same convex hull. Clipping this polygon by \(u\ge0\)
produces the cyclic vertex list
\[
\begin{gathered}
\left(0,\frac{801261136226701}{134468519402000}\right),\quad
\left(134,\frac{13716049}{98000}\right),\\
\left(134,\frac{1960207}{14000}\right),\quad
\left(36,\frac{588207}{14000}\right),\quad
\left(0,\frac{589089}{98000}\right).
\end{gathered}
\]
The shoelace formula gives
\[
|(P+Q)\cap\{u\ge0\}|
=\frac{24815084561147}{3361712985050}.
\]
The line \(u=0\) has planar area zero. Scaling by \(1/2\) multiplies
area by \(1/4\), so
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\cap\{y>0\}\right|
=\frac14|(P+Q)\cap\{u\ge0\}|
=\frac{24815084561147}{13446851940200}.
\]
Finally, the complete definition of \(U\) and
\(1/2<r<\rho\) give \(U(r)=2-r\). Since \(M=2\),
\[
M U(r)=4-m.
\]
Consequently the claimed lower-bound right side is
\[
\frac{7(4-m)-4-2m}{9}+d_2
=\frac83-m+d_2
=\frac{146529527093}{65862131952}.
\]
Subtracting the exact clipped midpoint-cap area gives the positive
rational difference displayed in the statement. This verifies the
strict reverse inequality and completes the counterexample.
