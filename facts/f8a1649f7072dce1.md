## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
and write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},
\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},
\qquad
b=|B|,
\qquad
T=M+b+m.
\]
Let \(g_B\) be the centroid of \(B\), and define
\[
\Phi(M,m,b)
=
\frac{4b}{9}
+
\max\left\{
0,\,
m-
\left(
\max\left\{0,\,2\sqrt{\frac{5b}{9}}-\sqrt M\right\}
\right)^2
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Phi(M,m,b)=
\begin{cases}
m+\dfrac{4b}{9},
&\dfrac{20b}{9}\le M,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{4b}{9}+m-
\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{20b}{9}}-\sqrt M\right)^2,
&M<\dfrac{20b}{9}<(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2,\\[8pt]
\dfrac{4b}{9},
&\dfrac{20b}{9}\ge(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
h_S((1,g_B))\ge \Phi(M,m,b).
\tag{3}
\]

More precisely, for a middle-cap area \(c\), outer lower-cap areas \(x,y\), and the scalar conditions
\[
\frac{4b}{9}\le c\le\frac{5b}{9},
\qquad
0\le x\le M,
\qquad
0\le y\le m,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)^2\le4c
\quad\text{whenever }x>0,\ y>0,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
(\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-y})^2\le4(b-c)
\quad\text{whenever }x<M,\ y<m,
\tag{6}
\]
one has
\[
\inf(c+x+y)=\Phi(M,m,b).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus (1) exactly optimizes all cap-area information in (4)-(6), including branches with an empty or saturated outer cap.

Finally, if
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM
\]
satisfy
\[
\frac14\le r\le\frac9{25},
\qquad
1-r<s\le\frac45,
\tag{8}
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
\frac{2T}{9}+\frac{7M}{180}
>
\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]
The parameter region (8) is nonempty and belongs to the residual area domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.

## Proof

After exchanging the two outer slices by the height reflection \(i\mapsto2-i\), assume
\[
|A_0|=M,\qquad |A_2|=m.
\]
Fix a projection direction \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and a slope \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). The tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\) has slice caps
\[
L_0=A_0\cap\{w:\langle q,w-g_B\rangle\le\lambda\},
\]
\[
L_B=B\cap\{w:\langle q,w-g_B\rangle\le0\},
\]
\[
L_2=A_2\cap\{w:\langle q,w-g_B\rangle\le-\lambda\}.
\]
Put
\[
x=|L_0|,\qquad c=|L_B|,\qquad y=|L_2|.
\tag{10}
\]

Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` supplies the planar centroid inequality. Applied to \(L_B\) and to the opposite halfplane through \(g_B\), it gives
\[
\frac{4b}{9}\le c\le\frac{5b}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]

The translated cap inclusions from accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` apply directly because
\[
B-g_B
=
\frac{(A_0-g_B)+(A_2-g_B)}2.
\]
For the lower caps,
\[
\frac{L_0+L_2}{2}\subseteq L_B.
\tag{12}
\]
Hence, when both lower caps have positive area, planar Brunn-Minkowski gives
\[
\sqrt c\ge\frac{\sqrt x+\sqrt y}{2}.
\tag{13}
\]
If one lower cap is empty, its Minkowski sum with the other is empty, so no term from the other cap can be retained. A zero-area support face may satisfy the stronger inequality, but its area alone does not distinguish it from an empty cap.

Define the closed upper caps using the reverse inequalities. Their areas are \(M-x\), \(b-c\), and \(m-y\), since planar boundary lines have area zero. They satisfy
\[
\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}\subseteq U_B.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore, whenever \(x<M\) and \(y<m\),
\[
\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-y}\le2\sqrt{b-c}.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(x=M\) or \(y=m\), the corresponding upper cap may be empty, so the contribution from the other upper cap cannot be inferred. Equations (11), (13), and (15) are exactly (4)-(6), with every empty, support-face, interior, and saturated case accounted for.

Fix \(c\) and put
\[
R=2\sqrt{b-c}.
\]
Suppose first that \(x<M\) and \(y<m\), and set
\[
p=\sqrt{M-x},\qquad q_2=\sqrt{m-y}.
\]
Then
\[
0<p\le\sqrt M,\qquad
0<q_2\le\sqrt m,\qquad
p+q_2\le R.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(R\le\sqrt M\), then
\[
p^2+q_2^2\le(p+q_2)^2\le R^2\le M,
\]
and consequently
\[
x+y=M+m-p^2-q_2^2\ge m.
\tag{17}
\]

If
\[
\sqrt M<R<\sqrt M+\sqrt m,
\]
maximizing \(p^2+q_2^2\) under (16) reduces to the endpoints of the line \(p+q_2=R\), because \(p^2+(R-p)^2\) is convex. The endpoint values are
\[
M+(R-\sqrt M)^2
\]
and
\[
m+(R-\sqrt m)^2.
\]
Their difference is
\[
2(\sqrt M-\sqrt m)(\sqrt M+\sqrt m-R)\ge0.
\]
Thus
\[
p^2+q_2^2\le M+(R-\sqrt M)^2
\]
and
\[
x+y\ge m-(R-\sqrt M)^2.
\tag{18}
\]

If \(R\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt m\), the trivial bound \(x+y\ge0\) is optimal at the scalar level. If instead \(x=M\) or \(y=m\), then directly
\[
x+y\ge m.
\tag{19}
\]
Combining (17)-(19) gives, in every empty, interior, or saturated case,
\[
x+y\ge
\max\left\{
0,\,
m-
\left(\max\{0,R-\sqrt M\}\right)^2
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]

This bound is the exact infimum under both conditional cap constraints. Indeed, at fixed \(c\):

If \(R\le\sqrt M\), take \(x=0,y=m\). The lower constraint is inapplicable because \(x=0\), and the upper constraint is inapplicable because \(y=m\); equality holds in (20).

If \(\sqrt M<R<\sqrt M+\sqrt m\), take
\[
x=0,\qquad y=m-(R-\sqrt M)^2.
\]
The lower constraint is again inapplicable, while the upper constraint holds with equality:
\[
\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-y}
=\sqrt M+(R-\sqrt M)=R.
\]

If \(R\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt m\), take \(x=y=0\). The lower constraint is inapplicable, and the upper constraint holds because
\[
\sqrt M+\sqrt m\le R.
\]
Thus adding the lower-cap Brunn-Minkowski inequality cannot improve (20) from these scalar data alone: every scalar extremizer has \(x=0\), so that inequality may be vacuous. Quantitative improvement outside the resulting region requires information that prevents or controls this empty large-outer-cap branch.

It remains to optimize \(c\). Define the right-hand side of \(c+x+y\) supplied by (20) as
\[
E(c)=
c+
\max\left\{
0,\,
m-
\left(\max\{0,2\sqrt{b-c}-\sqrt M\}\right)^2
\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
On the branch \(2\sqrt{b-c}\le\sqrt M\), one has \(E(c)=c+m\). On the branch \(2\sqrt{b-c}\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt m\), one has \(E(c)=c\). On the intermediate branch,
\[
E(c)
=
5c+m-M-4b+4\sqrt{M(b-c)},
\]
so
\[
E'(c)
=
5-\frac{2\sqrt M}{\sqrt{b-c}}
>1,
\]
because \(2\sqrt{b-c}>\sqrt M\). The three formulas agree at their endpoints. Hence \(E\) is increasing, and (11) gives
\[
c+x+y\ge E\left(\frac{4b}{9}\right)=\Phi(M,m,b).
\tag{22}
\]
The fixed-\(c\) extremizers above, with \(c=4b/9\), also prove the scalar equality (7). Substitution yields both (1) and the finite piecewise formula (2).

The direction \(q\) and slope \(\lambda\) were arbitrary. A halfspace containing \((1,g_B)\) can be translated inward parallel to itself until its boundary contains that point. Every non-slice-parallel boundary is covered by the preceding parameterization. The two slice-parallel halfspaces through the point have masses \(M+b\) and \(b+m\), while
\[
\Phi(M,m,b)\le\frac{4b}{9}+m<b+m\le M+b.
\]
Thus every containing halfspace has mass at least \(\Phi\), proving (3).

Now assume (8). Since
\[
s>1-r\ge\frac{16}{25},
\]
and \(s\le4/5\),
\[
1<\frac{20s}{9}
<
(1+\sqrt r)^2.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, the left inequality follows from \(20(16/25)/9=64/45>1\), while
\[
\frac{20s}{9}\le\frac{16}{9}<\frac94\le(1+\sqrt r)^2.
\]
Therefore the middle branch of (2) applies, and
\[
\frac{\Phi(M,m,b)}{M}
=
\frac{4s}{9}+r-
\left(2\sqrt{\frac{5s}{9}}-1\right)^2.
\tag{24}
\]
A direct simplification gives
\[
\frac{9}{M}
\left(
\Phi(M,m,b)-\frac{2T}{9}
\right)
=
12\sqrt{5s}-18s+7r-11.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(s>16/25>5/9\), the right-hand side is decreasing in \(s\), and it is increasing in \(r\). Hence throughout (8) it is at least its value at \(r=1/4,s=4/5\), namely
\[
24-\frac{72}{5}+\frac74-11
=
\frac7{20}.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (25)-(26) prove (9).

Finally, accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
L(r)=1-r,\qquad U(r)=1+r
\quad\text{for }0<r\le\frac9{25}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac9{25}<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
and (8) gives
\[
L(r)<s\le\frac45<1+r=U(r).
\]
Thus (8) lies strictly inside its residual area strip and is not certified by any of the three previously synthesized area criteria.

For a concrete exact-Minkowski member of this region, take
\[
A_0=\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2,
\qquad
A_2=
\left[-\frac1{14},\frac1{14}\right]
\times
\left[-\frac78,\frac78\right].
\]
Then
\[
M=1,\qquad m=\frac14,
\]
while \(B=(A_0+A_2)/2\) is a rectangle of side lengths \(4/7\) and \(11/8\), so
\[
b=\frac{11}{14},
\qquad
r=\frac14,
\qquad
s=\frac{11}{14},
\]
and
\[
\frac34<\frac{11}{14}<\frac45.
\]
The convex hull of \(\{0\}\times A_0\) and \(\{2\}\times A_2\) has middle section exactly \(\{1\}\times B\), so this is a compatible compact-polytopal three-slice family. Hence the new residual region is nonempty.
