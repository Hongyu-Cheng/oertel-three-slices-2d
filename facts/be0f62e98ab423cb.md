## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional compact
convex bodies, let \(a_k=|A_k|\), and let \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\). Assume
\[
a_0<a_1+a_2,\qquad a_1<a_0+a_2,\qquad a_2<a_0+a_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
S=\bigcup_{k=0}^2(\{k\}\times A_k),
\]
and let the normalized strict three-halfspace certificate of accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` be
\[
H_r=\{(h,w):f_r(w)+s_r(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad r\in\{1,2,3\},
\tag{2}
\]
with
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_r)<\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), distinguish the open section halfplane used for
routing from the closed section used to measure \(H_r\):
\[
Q^\circ_{r,k}
=\{w:f_r(w)+s_r(k-1)<0\},\qquad
Q_{r,k}
=\{w:f_r(w)+s_r(k-1)\le0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Choose \(j\in\{0,2\}\) such that
\(\bigcup_{r=1}^3Q^\circ_{r,j}=\mathbb R^2\), whose existence is supplied
by accepted fact `0b5d84877f51b7c3`. Let \(g_1\) and \(g_j\) be the
centroids of \(A_1\) and \(A_j\). If, for some \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
g_1\in Q^\circ_{r,1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
g_j\in Q^\circ_{r,j},
\tag{5}
\]
then
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_r)>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, no halfspace in the strict-low-mass certificate can satisfy
(5).

## Proof

The planar centroid-cap ingredient stated in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` is exactly the following: if \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\)
is a convex body with centroid \(g\), then every closed planar halfplane
\(Q\) containing \(g\) satisfies
\[
|A\cap Q|\ge\frac49|A|.
\tag{7}
\]

For every slice index \(k\), (2) gives the exact closed section identity
\[
S\cap H_r\cap(\{k\}\times\mathbb R^2)
=\{k\}\times(A_k\cap Q_{r,k}).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the mass of \(H_r\) is computed with the closed caps \(Q_{r,k}\),
not with the strict routing sections \(Q^\circ_{r,k}\). Since
\(Q^\circ_{r,k}\subseteq Q_{r,k}\), (5) implies
\[
g_1\in Q_{r,1},\qquad g_j\in Q_{r,j}.
\tag{9}
\]
The affine functions \(f_r\) in the normalized certificate are
nonconstant, so \(Q_{r,k}\setminus Q^\circ_{r,k}\) is a line and has
planar area zero; in particular, the strict-versus-closed convention
creates no boundary mass. Applying (7) to the two actual closed slice
sections in (9) yields
\[
|A_1\cap Q_{r,1}|\ge\frac49a_1,\qquad
|A_j\cap Q_{r,j}|\ge\frac49a_j.
\tag{10}
\]

The three slices are disjoint Borel subsets of parallel planes, so their
two-dimensional Hausdorff measures add. The remaining slice contribution
is nonnegative, and hence (8)-(10) give
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_r)
\ge\frac49(a_1+a_j).
\tag{11}
\]
Put \(k=2-j\), the endpoint distinct from \(j\). The corresponding strict
inequality in (1) is
\[
a_k<a_1+a_j.
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(T=a_1+a_j+a_k\), strict algebra now gives
\[
\frac49(a_1+a_j)-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{2}{9}(a_1+a_j-a_k)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (11) and (13) proves (6). This contradicts (3), so the aligned
same-index routing in (5) cannot occur in the certificate.
