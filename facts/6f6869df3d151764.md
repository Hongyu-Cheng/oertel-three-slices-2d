## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact convex polygons, let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\]
and let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be any measurable finite-area set with \(B\subseteq K\). In particular, \(K\) may be a compact convex polygon. Fix real numbers \(L_1,L_2,L_3\), and write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k_0=|B|,
\]
\[
u_i=\bigl|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}\bigr|,
\qquad
v_i=\bigl|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-L_i\}\bigr|,
\]
\[
b_i=\bigl|B\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr|
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Define
\[
h_0=\frac{2(M+m+k_0)}9,
\qquad
F_i=u_i+v_i+b_i-h_0.
\]
Assume that \(K\) satisfies the three crossing equalities
\[
u_i+v_i+\bigl|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr|
=\frac{2(M+m+|K|)}9
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Put
\[
A=|K\setminus B|.
\]
Then
\[
\max\left\{
0,\ \frac92\max_{1\leq i\leq3}F_i,\
\frac97\max_{1\leq i\leq3}(-F_i)
\right\}
\leq A
\leq
-3(F_1+F_2+F_3).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, a necessary condition for any such \(K\) to exist is
\[
F_1+F_2+F_3\leq0,
\]
and no measurable, hence no convex, completion exists whenever the left endpoint in (1) exceeds the right endpoint.

## Proof

Let
\[
E=K\setminus B,
\qquad
e_i=\bigl|E\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr|.
\]
Because \(B\subseteq K\), the unions
\[
K=B\mathbin{\dot\cup}E
\]
and
\[
K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}
=
\bigl(B\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr)
\mathbin{\dot\cup}
\bigl(E\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr)
\]
are disjoint. Hence
\[
|K|=k_0+A,
\qquad
\bigl|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\bigr|=b_i+e_i.
\tag{2}
\]
Substituting (2) into the \(i\)-th crossing equality gives
\[
u_i+v_i+b_i+e_i
=
h_0+\frac{2A}{9}.
\]
By the definition of \(F_i\), this is exactly
\[
e_i=\frac{2A}{9}-F_i.
\tag{3}
\]

Since \(E\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\subseteq E\),
\[
0\leq e_i\leq A.
\]
The lower bound \(e_i\geq0\), together with (3), gives
\[
A\geq\frac92F_i.
\tag{4}
\]
The upper bound \(e_i\leq A\), together with (3), gives
\[
A\geq-\frac97F_i.
\tag{5}
\]
Taking the maximum of \(0\) and all bounds in (4)-(5) proves the left inequality in (1).

It remains to prove the upper inequality. For every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), the identity
\[
f_1(x)+f_2(x)+f_3(x)=-1
\]
precludes \(f_1(x)>0,f_2(x)>0,f_3(x)>0\) simultaneously. Thus every \(x\in E\) belongs to at least one of the three sets
\[
E\cap\{f_i\leq0\}.
\]
Integrating the corresponding indicator inequality over \(E\) yields
\[
e_1+e_2+e_3\geq A.
\tag{6}
\]
On the other hand, summing (3) gives
\[
e_1+e_2+e_3
=
\frac{2A}{3}-(F_1+F_2+F_3).
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (6)-(7) gives
\[
A\leq-3(F_1+F_2+F_3),
\]
which is the right inequality in (1). Since \(A\geq0\), this also forces \(F_1+F_2+F_3\leq0\). Every step used only measurability, finite area, \(B\subseteq K\), the three crossings, and the affine-plane identity, so the conclusion applies a fortiori to compatible convex polygonal realizations.
