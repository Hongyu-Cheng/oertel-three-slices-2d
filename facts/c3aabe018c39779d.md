## Statement

Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex set, let
\(p,q>0\), and assume
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-p,0),(0,-q)\}\subseteq K.
\]
Define
\[
\beta=\left|K\cap\{x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y<0\}\right|,
\qquad
\gamma=\left|K\cap\{x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y<0\}\right|,
\]
and
\[
\alpha=\left|K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}\right|,
\qquad
\eta=\left|K\cap\{0<x+y\leq1\}\right|.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha\leq\eta
\]
or
\[
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q\right)>1.
\]
More precisely, every point \((X,Y)\in K\) with \(X,Y>0\) and
\(X+Y>1\) forces the strict second inequality.  Consequently, if the
weighted expression is at most \(1\), then
\[
K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}\subseteq K\cap\{0<x+y\leq1\}.
\]
If the weighted expression is strictly less than \(1\), the right-hand
inequality can be sharpened to \(x+y<1\) on the open positive quadrant.

## Proof

Accepted fact `0548e5e23c7fb2cc` proves the corresponding obstruction for
one fixed numerical calibration.  We use its forced-triangle mechanism, but
derive all formulas and inequalities below for arbitrary \(p,q,\beta,\gamma\).

Write
\[
O=(0,0),\qquad A=(-p,0),\qquad B=(0,-q).
\]
Fix an arbitrary \(Z=(X,Y)\in K\) with \(X>0\) and \(Y>0\), and put
\(h=X+Y>0\).  Convexity and the assumed triangle containment give
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{A,O,Z\}\subseteq K,
\qquad
\operatorname{conv}\{B,O,Z\}\subseteq K.
\]

The segment from \(A\) to \(Z\) meets \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_A=
\left(-\frac{pY}{h+p},\frac{pY}{h+p}\right).
\]
Indeed, the affine parameter of this intersection on the segment
\([A,Z]\) is \(p/(h+p)\).  The interior of
\(\operatorname{conv}\{A,O,W_A\}\) has \(x<0\), \(y>0\), and
\(x+y<0\): in an interior barycentric combination, the coefficients of
\(A\) and \(W_A\) are positive, both of their \(x\)-coordinates are
negative, only \(W_A\) has a positive \(y\)-coordinate, and only \(A\)
has a nonzero coordinate sum, namely \(-p\).  Hence this triangle, apart
from its null boundary, lies in the cone defining \(\beta\).  Its area is
\[
\frac12\left|\det(A,W_A)\right|
=\frac{p^2Y}{2(h+p)}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\beta\geq\frac{p^2Y}{2(h+p)}
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
\frac{Y}{h}\leq
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1{ph}\right).                 \tag{1}
\]

Similarly, the segment from \(B\) to \(Z\) meets \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_B=
\left(\frac{qX}{h+q},-\frac{qX}{h+q}\right).
\]
The interior of \(\operatorname{conv}\{B,O,W_B\}\) has \(x>0\),
\(y<0\), and \(x+y<0\), so it lies in the cone defining \(\gamma\).
Its area is
\[
\frac12\left|\det(B,W_B)\right|
=\frac{q^2X}{2(h+q)}.
\]
Thus
\[
\gamma\geq\frac{q^2X}{2(h+q)}
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
\frac{X}{h}\leq
2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1{qh}\right).                \tag{2}
\]

Adding (1) and (2), and using \(X/h+Y/h=1\), gives the pointwise bound
\[
1\leq
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1{ph}\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1{qh}\right).               \tag{3}
\]
If \(h>1\), both forced triangles have positive area because \(X,Y,p,q\)
are positive.  In particular, \(\beta,\gamma>0\).  Also
\[
\frac1{ph}<\frac1p,\qquad \frac1{qh}<\frac1q.
\]
Consequently (3) yields the strict chain
\[
1\leq
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1{ph}\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1{qh}\right)
<
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q\right).
\]
Thus the existence of even one positive-quadrant point with \(x+y>1\)
forces the strict second alternative.

If no such point exists, then every point of
\(K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}\) has \(0<x+y\leq1\).  Hence there is the exact set
inclusion
\[
K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}
\subseteq
K\cap\{0<x+y\leq1\},
\]
and monotonicity of planar Lebesgue measure gives \(\alpha\leq\eta\).
This proves the dichotomy.

For completeness, if \(h=1\), then (3) gives only
\[
1\leq
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q\right),
\]
with no strictness asserted.  This is the correct boundary behavior:
points with \(x+y=1\) are included in the strip defining \(\eta\).  Thus a
weighted cost equal to \(1\) permits this boundary in the conclusion,
whereas a cost strictly below \(1\) excludes it and forces \(x+y<1\).
If the open positive quadrant is empty, then \(\alpha=0\leq\eta\)
immediately.

Finally, each of the cutting lines \(x=0\), \(y=0\), and \(x+y=0\), as
well as the strip boundary \(x+y=1\), has planar Lebesgue measure zero.
The boundaries of the two forced triangles are finite unions of line
segments and are also null.  Therefore all uses of the strict open-cone
definitions above count the full triangle areas, and changing any cutting
line from open to closed would not alter \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta\).
