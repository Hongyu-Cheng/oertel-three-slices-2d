## Statement

Let
\[
v(u)=a+hu+d\min\{u,t\},
\qquad
a>0,\quad h\geq0,\quad d>0,\quad 0<t<1,
\]
and fix \(0<\lambda<1\).  Suppose the clipping points satisfy
\[
v(\alpha)=\lambda-\alpha
\]
and either
\[
\lambda<\beta<1,\qquad v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda,
\]
or
\[
\beta=1,\qquad v(1)\geq1-\lambda.
\]
Define
\[
I=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,\qquad
R=\int_0^\lambda v(u)\,du,\qquad
X=\int_0^\alpha v(u)\,du,
\]
\[
T=\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du,\qquad
C=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-(\lambda-\alpha)^2}{2},
\qquad
J=-X+C+T.
\]
Assume explicitly that one of the following four hypotheses holds:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(i)}&0<\alpha<t<\lambda<\beta<1,\\
\mathrm{(ii)}&0<\alpha<\lambda<t<\beta<1,\\
\mathrm{(iii)}&\beta=1\ \text{ and }\ 0<\alpha<t<\lambda<1,\\
\mathrm{(iv)}&\beta=1\ \text{ and }\ 0<\alpha<\lambda<t<1.
\end{array}
\tag{S}
\]
Then
\[
I+9J<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5J+8R-3I>0.
\]
In particular, the required weak conclusion
\(5J+8R-3I\geq0\) holds in all four cells.

## Proof

First dispose of the two cells with \(\beta<1\).  Put
\[
I_c=I-T,\qquad J_c=J-T=-X+C.
\]
The integral \(R\) is unchanged by truncation at \(\beta\), and direct
substitution gives
\[
A=I+9J=(I_c+9J_c)+10T,
\]
\[
K=5J+8R-3I=(5J_c+8R-3I_c)+2T.
\tag{1}
\]
Rescale the truncated interval \([0,\beta]\) isotropically by defining
\[
\widehat v(u)=\frac{v(\beta u)}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\alpha=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat t=\frac{t}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{\beta}.
\]
Then
\[
\widehat v(\widehat\alpha)
=\widehat\lambda-\widehat\alpha,\qquad
\widehat v(1)=1-\widehat\lambda.
\]
All area integrals and the quadratic clipping term are divided by
\(\beta^2\).  Hence the normalized endpoint cell has
\[
\widehat A=\frac{I_c+9J_c}{\beta^2},\qquad
\widehat K=\frac{5J_c+8R-3I_c}{\beta^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
The two kink orders are preserved.  Thus the endpoint result below,
together with \(A<0\), gives \(I_c+9J_c=A-10T<0\) and then
\(5J_c+8R-3I_c\geq0\).  Since \(T>0\) when \(\beta<1\), (1) gives
\(K>0\).  It remains to prove the two cells with \(\beta=1\).

Write
\[
x=\alpha,\qquad \ell=\lambda-\alpha,\qquad b=1-\lambda.
\]
Thus
\[
x>0,\qquad \ell>0,\qquad b>0,\qquad x+\ell+b=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
m=h+d.
\]
Then \(m>h\geq0\), and the left clipping equation, which is before the
kink in both remaining cells, becomes
\[
a+mx=\ell,\qquad a=\ell-mx.
\tag{4}
\]
The right endpoint condition is not needed below, so the calculation
proves the implication on a larger parameter set.

For reference, (4) gives
\[
X=ax+\frac{mx^2}{2},\qquad
J=-X+\frac{b^2-\ell^2}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also
\[
I=a+m\left(t-\frac{t^2}{2}\right)
  {}+\frac{h(1-t)^2}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(t<\lambda\), then
\[
R=a\lambda+m\left(t\lambda-\frac{t^2}{2}\right)
  {}+\frac{h(\lambda-t)^2}{2},
\tag{7}
\]
whereas, if \(t>\lambda\), then
\[
R=a\lambda+\frac{m\lambda^2}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]

In either endpoint cell, (4)--(8) give affine expressions
\[
A=A_0+A_m m+A_hh,\qquad
K=K_0+K_m m+K_hh,
\tag{9}
\]
with the common constant terms
\[
A_0=\frac{9(b+\ell)^2-16\ell}{2},\qquad
K_0=\frac{5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2}{2}.
\tag{10}
\]
The latter is strictly positive because
\[
5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2
=(b+\ell)^2+4(b-\ell)^2>0.
\tag{11}
\]

For the order \(0<x<t<\lambda<1\), write
\[
t=x+s,\qquad 0<s<\ell.
\]
The four remaining coefficients in (9) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_m&=\frac{2bs+2\ell s-s^2+8x^2}{2},
&
A_h&=\frac{(b+\ell-s)^2}{2},\\
K_m&=-\frac{s(6b-10\ell+5s)}{2},
&
K_h&=\frac{-3b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2+6bs-10\ell s+5s^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(A_m>0\) because
\(2bs+2\ell s-s^2=s(2b+2\ell-s)>0\), and \(A_h>0\).

For the order \(0<x<\lambda<t<1\), write
\[
t=\lambda+s,\qquad 0<s<b.
\]
Now the coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_m&=\frac{2b\ell+\ell^2+2bs-s^2+8x^2}{2},
&
A_h&=\frac{(b-s)^2}{2},\\
K_m&=\frac{-6b\ell+5\ell^2-6bs+3s^2}{2},
&
K_h&=-\frac{3(b-s)^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Again \(A_m>0\), since \(s(2b-s)>0\), and \(A_h>0\).

Consequently, \(A<0\) forces \(A_0<0\).  Set
\[
D=-A_0=\frac{16\ell-9(b+\ell)^2}{2}>0
\tag{14}
\]
and put
\[
\theta=\frac hm\in[0,1).
\]
The closed interval \([0,1]\) may be used in the sign certificate.
From \(A<0\),
\[
m(A_m+\theta A_h)<D.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(K_m+\theta K_h\geq0\), then \(K\geq K_0>0\).  If instead
\(K_m+\theta K_h<0\), (15) reverses when multiplied by this negative
quantity and gives
\[
K>K_0+
\frac{D(K_m+\theta K_h)}{A_m+\theta A_h}.
\tag{16}
\]
It is therefore enough to prove
\[
N(\theta):=
K_0(A_m+\theta A_h)+D(K_m+\theta K_h)\geq0.
\tag{17}
\]
This is affine in \(\theta\), so it suffices to check
\(\theta=0,1\).

The exact endpoint checks in (17) reduce to three polynomials:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_B={}&5b^2-10b^3+5b^4-6b\ell+2b^2\ell+8b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-4b\ell^2-b^2\ell^2-4b\ell^3,\\
P_0={}&5b^2-10b^3+5b^4-6b\ell+2b^2\ell+12b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-10b\ell^2+5b^2\ell^2,\\
P_1={}&5b^2-10b^3+9b^4-6b\ell-4b^2\ell+18b^3\ell\\
&+5\ell^2-10b\ell^2+9b^2\ell^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
For a quadratic on \(0\leq s\leq u\), call
\((B_0,B_1,B_2)\) its Bernstein coefficients when, for \(r=s/u\),
the quadratic equals
\[
B_0(1-r)^2+2B_1r(1-r)+B_2r^2.
\]
Substitution in (17) gives the following exact table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
\text{kink order}&B_0\text{ for }N(0)&B_1\text{ for }N(0)
&B_2\text{ for }N(0)&N(1)\\ \hline
t=x+s,\ 0\leq s\leq\ell
&2x^2(5b^2-6b\ell+5\ell^2)&2P_B&2P_0&2P_1\\
t=\lambda+s,\ 0\leq s\leq b
&2P_0&2P_1&2P_1&2P_1
\end{array}
\tag{19}
\]
Thus it remains only to prove
\[
P_B>0,\qquad P_0>0,\qquad P_1\geq0
\tag{20}
\]
under (3) and (14).

Put
\[
L=b+\ell,\qquad z=\frac{\ell}{L},\qquad
\varepsilon=16z-9L.
\tag{21}
\]
Then \(0<z<1\), and (14) is exactly \(\varepsilon>0\).  Since
\(0<L<1\),
\[
\max\{0,16z-9\}<\varepsilon<16z,\qquad
L=\frac{16z-\varepsilon}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]
For \(i\in\{B,0,1\}\), define
\[
Q_i=\frac{81P_i}{L^2}.
\]
All signs are preserved.

For \(P_1\), direct substitution in (18) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1={}&9\varepsilon^2(1-z)^2
{}+18\varepsilon(1-z)(5-24z+24z^2)\\
&+9(5-16z+16z^2)(9-32z+32z^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
With \(q(z)=5-24z+24z^2\), the exact completion is
\[
\frac{Q_1}{9}
=\big((1-z)\varepsilon+q(z)\big)^2
{}+4(2z-1)^2(5+4z-4z^2).
\tag{24}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative, and
\(5+4z-4z^2=5+4z(1-z)>0\).  Hence \(P_1\geq0\).

For \(P_0\), substitution gives the quadratic
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0={}&\varepsilon^2(1-z)^2(5+2z-2z^2)\\
&+2\varepsilon(1-z)
(45-179z+147z^2+64z^3-32z^4)\\
&+(5-16z+16z^2)
(81-288z+256z^2+64z^3-32z^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Its leading coefficient is positive, and its discriminant with
respect to \(\varepsilon\) factors as
\[
\operatorname{disc}_{\varepsilon}Q_0
=324(z-1)^3z(40-153z+153z^2)<0.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, \(0<z<1\), while the last quadratic is positive because its
leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is
\[
153^2-4\cdot153\cdot40=-1071<0.
\]
Therefore \(Q_0>0\), and hence \(P_0>0\).

It remains to certify \(P_B\).  Define
\[
F(z)=45-179z+184z^2+32z^3,
\qquad
G(z)=81-288z+256z^2+32z^3.
\tag{27}
\]
The exact substitution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_B={}&(1-z)(5-7z-2z^2)\varepsilon^2
{}+2(1-z)F(z)\varepsilon\\
&+(5-16z+16z^2)G(z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Both \(F\) and \(G\) are positive on \(0<z<1\).  To see this with
rational signs only, put \(y=2z-1\).  Then
\[
F=4y^3+58y^2+\frac{29}{2}y+\frac{11}{2},
\qquad
G=4y^3+76y^2-4y+5.
\tag{29}
\]
For \(0\leq y<1\), every term in \(F\) is nonnegative and
\[
76y^2-4y+5>0
\]
because its discriminant is \(16-4\cdot76\cdot5=-1504<0\);
therefore \(G>0\) as well.  For \(-1<y\leq0\), put \(u=-y\).
Since \(0\leq u<1\),
\[
F=-4u^3+58u^2-\frac{29}{2}u+\frac{11}{2}
\geq54u^2-\frac{29}{2}u+\frac{11}{2}>0,
\tag{30}
\]
where the final quadratic has discriminant
\(-3911/4<0\).  Likewise,
\[
G=-4u^3+76u^2+4u+5
\geq72u^2+4u+5>0.
\tag{31}
\]

Let
\[
z_0=\frac{-7+\sqrt{89}}4.
\]
The function \(5-7z-2z^2\) is decreasing for \(z>0\), vanishes at
\(z_0\), and has value \(55/128>0\) at \(z=9/16\).  Thus
\[
\frac9{16}<z_0<1.
\tag{32}
\]
If \(0<z\leq z_0\), then differentiating (28) with respect to
\(\varepsilon\) gives
\[
2(1-z)\big((5-7z-2z^2)\varepsilon+F(z)\big)>0.
\]
Consequently \(Q_B\) is increasing for \(\varepsilon\geq0\), and
\[
Q_B(\varepsilon)>Q_B(0)
=(5-16z+16z^2)G(z)>0,
\tag{33}
\]
because
\[
5-16z+16z^2=1+16\left(z-\frac12\right)^2>0.
\]

If \(z_0<z<1\), the quadratic (28) is concave in
\(\varepsilon\).  By (22) and (32), its admissible interval is
\[
16z-9<\varepsilon<16z.
\]
The two endpoint values factor exactly as
\[
Q_B(16z)=81(5-16z+16z^2)>0,
\]
\[
Q_B(16z-9)=81z(2z-1)(9z-4)>0.
\tag{34}
\]
A concave function lies above the chord joining its endpoint values,
so (34) gives \(Q_B>0\) throughout the admissible interval.  This
proves \(P_B>0\), completing (20).

All Bernstein coefficients in (19) are therefore nonnegative, so
\(N(0)\geq0\) and \(N(1)\geq0\).  Affineness gives
\(N(\theta)\geq0\), and (16) proves \(K>0\) in both endpoint cells.
Equations (1)--(2) then prove the two interior-right-clipping cells.

The sign certificate was deliberately proved on the closed kink
intervals in (19): \(s=0\), \(s=\ell\), and \(s=b\) are included.
The common boundary \(t=\lambda\) is therefore covered from both kink
orders.  The slope endpoints \(\theta=0,1\) are also included, and
\(\beta=1\) is the case \(T=0\) in (1), while every \(\beta<1\) has
\(T>0\).  Hence no boundary order between the four systems is omitted.
