## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system in the canonical orientation and normalization of accepted facts `6600e139055be506` and `77db94881d26f221`. Thus there are nondegenerate triangles
\[
P_0\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1+q\},\qquad
P_1\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1\},\qquad
P_2\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1-q\},
\]
with normalized areas
\[
|P_0|=A,\qquad |P_1|=1,\qquad |P_2|=B,
\]
such that
\[
0<A<B,\qquad B<1+A,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9,\qquad \frac12<t<1,
\qquad \frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
For each coordinate \(k\), define the closed coordinate caps
\[
u_k=\frac{|P_0\cap\{z_k\ge0\}|}{A},\qquad
c_k=|P_1\cap\{z_k\ge0\}|,\qquad
v_k=\frac{|P_2\cap\{z_k\ge0\}|}{B},
\]
and assume all three literal strict row inequalities
\[
Au_k+c_k+Bv_k<t.
\]
Let the columns of \(L=(\ell_{kj})\) be the three vertices of \(P_1\), with
\[
\sum_{k=1}^3\ell_{kj}=1\quad(j=1,2,3),
\qquad \det L\ne0,
\]
and let \(I_1\) be the set of coordinates whose value at the centroid of \(P_1\) is nonnegative. Then
\[
c_i<\frac12\qquad(i\in I_1).
\]
Moreover, every row \(L_{i*}\) with \(i\in I_1\) is all-nonzero and has exactly one positive vertex value. In the existing common column labeling, if \(r\) is that unique positive column and the row entries are denoted by \(p,-a,-b\), with \(p,a,b>0\), then
\[
p\ge a+b,\qquad
c_i=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)},\qquad
\frac49\le c_i<\frac12.
\]
No independent rowwise permutation of the vertex columns is used.

## Proof

The cap definitions, area normalization, midpoint containment, mean convention, and zero-entry formulas are exactly those of accepted fact `6600e139055be506`. The inequalities \(0<A<B<1+A\) and the exclusion of every two-positive \(I_1\)-row are supplied by accepted fact `77db94881d26f221`.

Fix \(i\in I_1\), and suppose for contradiction that \(c_i\ge1/2\). Put
\[
X:=Au_i,\qquad Y:=Bv_i,\qquad c:=c_i,
\qquad \delta:=t-c,\qquad
\delta_0:=t-\frac12=\frac{4(A+B)-5}{18}.
\]
The literal strict row inequality gives
\[
X+Y<\delta.
\]
Since \(X,Y\ge0\), this also gives \(\delta>0\). The assumption \(c\ge1/2\) gives \(\delta\le\delta_0\). Moreover,
\[
18(A-\delta_0)=14A-4B+5
>14A-4(1+A)+5
=10A+1>0,
\]
where the strict inequality uses \(B<1+A\). Hence
\[
0\le X,Y,\qquad X+Y<\delta\le\delta_0<A<B.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(j=0,1,2\), define the closed nonpositive cap
\[
K_j:=P_j\cap\{z_i\le0\}.
\]
We first justify its area exactly, including all zero-level cases. Since \(c<t<1\) and \(c\ge1/2\), the coordinate functional on \(P_1\) is neither nonnegative everywhere nor nonpositive everywhere. It is nonconstant, and its zero section is a line segment of planar area zero. Therefore
\[
|K_1|=1-c.
\tag{2}
\]
Equation (1) gives \(X<A\) and \(Y<B\). On either endpoint triangle, a coordinate functional that is identically zero or nonnegative everywhere would have full closed positive cap, contrary to these strict inequalities. If the functional is nonconstant, its zero section has planar area zero; if it is constant, it must be strictly negative. Thus in every endpoint zero-level case,
\[
|K_0|=A-X,\qquad |K_2|=B-Y.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, both endpoint complement caps have positive area.

If \(x\in K_0\) and \(y\in K_2\), then the \(i\)-th coordinate of \((x+y)/2\) is nonpositive. Midpoint containment therefore gives the literal inclusion
\[
\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\subseteq K_1.
\tag{4}
\]
Identify the three parallel slice planes by translation with their common two-dimensional direction space. The planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality applied to the two positive-area sets \(K_0,K_2\), together with (2)-(4), yields
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}\le2\sqrt{1-c}.
\tag{5}
\]

We now derive the opposite strict inequality. Let \(E:=X+Y\). By (1), \(0\le E<A\). For \(0\le r\le E\), put
\[
f(r):=\sqrt{A-r}+\sqrt{B-E+r}.
\]
The function \(f\) is concave, so its minimum is attained at an endpoint. Since \(A<B\),
\[
\sqrt A-\sqrt{A-E}
\ge \sqrt B-\sqrt{B-E},
\]
and hence \(f(E)\le f(0)\). Taking \(r=X\) and using \(E<\delta\le\delta_0\) gives
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
\ge\sqrt{A-E}+\sqrt B
>\sqrt{A-\delta_0}+\sqrt B.
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to compare the last quantity with \(\sqrt2\). Put
\[
s:=A+B,\qquad \eta:=B-A,\qquad a_*:=A-\delta_0.
\]
The inequalities \(t>1/2\) and \(0<A<B<1+A\) give
\[
s>\frac54,\qquad 0<\eta<1.
\]
Direct calculation gives
\[
a_*=\frac{5s+5-9\eta}{18},\qquad
B=\frac{s+\eta}{2},\qquad
a_*+B=\frac{14s+5}{18}>\frac54.
\tag{7}
\]
Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
36a_*B
&=(5s+5-9\eta)(s+\eta)\\
&=(5s-4)(s+1)+(1-\eta)(4+4s+9\eta)\\
&>(5s-4)(s+1)
>\left(\frac94\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(a_*B>9/64\), and (7)-(8) imply
\[
(\sqrt{a_*}+\sqrt B)^2
=a_*+B+2\sqrt{a_*B}
>\frac54+\frac34=2.
\]
Combining this with (6) gives
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}>\sqrt2.
\tag{9}
\]
But \(c\ge1/2\) gives
\[
2\sqrt{1-c}\le\sqrt2,
\tag{10}
\]
contradicting (5). Notice that when \(c=1/2\), inequality (10) is an equality, while (9) remains strict because the original row budget is strict. Therefore the equality boundary is included, and \(c_i<1/2\) for every \(i\in I_1\).

We now derive the sign conclusion, retaining every zero-entry case. Accepted fact `6600e139055be506` proves that if an actual \(I_1\)-row has a zero entry, then, in the existing common column labeling, it has exactly one positive entry \(p\), one negative entry \(-a\), and one zero entry, where \(p,a>0\). Its exact closed cap and mean conditions are
\[
c_i=\frac{p}{p+a},\qquad p\ge a.
\]
This gives \(c_i\ge1/2\), including equality when \(p=a\), contrary to the result just proved. Hence every \(I_1\)-row is all-nonzero.

Accepted fact `6600e139055be506` also gives at least one positive and at least one negative entry in every actual all-nonzero \(I_1\)-row. Such a three-entry row therefore has either one or two positive entries. Accepted fact `77db94881d26f221` excludes the two-positive case without any hypothesis on the other rows. Thus exactly one entry is positive.

Name its existing common column \(r\), without permuting any row independently, and write the row values as \(p,-a,-b\), where \(p,a,b>0\). Since \(i\in I_1\), its centroid coordinate is nonnegative, so
\[
p-a-b\ge0.
\]
The exact one-positive cap formula from accepted fact `6600e139055be506` gives
\[
c_i=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}.
\]
Finally, \(a+b\le p\) and \(ab\le(a+b)^2/4\) imply
\[
(p+a)(p+b)
=p^2+p(a+b)+ab
\le p^2+p^2+\frac{p^2}{4}
=\frac94p^2.
\]
Therefore \(c_i\ge4/9\). Together with \(c_i<1/2\), this proves the claimed interval and completes all three incidence orbits, including every zero-entry boundary, under the fixed common vertex labeling.
