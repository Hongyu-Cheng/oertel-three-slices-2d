## Statement

Set
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal D=\{(r,s):0<r<\rho,\ L(r)<s<U(r)\},
\]
where, as in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`,
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le 9/25,\\[2pt]
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\[2pt]
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(0<r<\rho\), let \(t_r\) be the unique root in \((1/3,1/2)\) of
\[
r t^3-3rt^2+3t-1=0,
\]
and define
\[
\beta(r)=\frac{1+r+rt_r+t_r^{-1}}4.
\]
Also define
\[
A(r,s)=12\sqrt{5s}-18s+7r-11
\]
and, for \(r>1/5\),
\[
\alpha(r)=\frac{\bigl(10+\sqrt{70r-10}\bigr)^2}{180}.
\]

Let
\[
p(t)=241t^6-1122t^5+4935t^4-11164t^3+3279t^2+30t+25.
\]
There is a unique \(t_*\in(37/100,187/500)\) satisfying \(p(t_*)=0\). Define
\[
r_*=\frac{3t_*-1}{t_*^2(3-t_*)}.
\]
Then
\[
r_*=0.331359920325\ldots,\qquad \frac15<r_*<\frac13.
\]

For every \((r,s)\in\mathcal D\), the lower bound in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8`, including its maximum with the area-only term from accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1`, reaches the target \(2(1+r+s)/9\) if and only if
\[
A(r,s)\ge0
\quad\text{or}\quad
s\ge\beta(r).
\]
Equivalently, the certified \(s\)-sections of the residual strip are exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
[\beta(r),U(r)),&0<r\le1/5,\\[2pt]
(L(r),\alpha(r)]\mathbin{\cup}[\beta(r),U(r)),
   &1/5<r<r_*,\\[2pt]
(L(r),U(r)),&r_*\le r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
At \(r=r_*\), one has \(\alpha(r_*)=\beta(r_*)\). Thus \(r_*\) is the sharp threshold above which this accepted envelope certifies the entire residual strip. For \(1/5<r<r_*\), its exact open gap is
\[
\alpha(r)<s<\beta(r),
\]
while for \(0<r\le1/5\) its exact open gap is
\[
L(r)<s<\beta(r).
\]

The transition curve of
\[
H(r,s)=
\max\left\{0,\,
r-\left(2\sqrt{\frac{5s}{9}}-1\right)^2
\right\}
\]
on \(\mathcal D\) is
\[
Q(r)=\frac9{20}(1+\sqrt r)^2.
\]
Writing
\[
\sigma=\frac{1061-252\sqrt5}{841},
\]
one has:
\[
H=0\ \text{throughout the strip if }0<r\le\frac{121}{841};
\]
\[
L(r)<Q(r)<U(r)\ \text{if }\frac{121}{841}<r<\sigma,
\]
with the displayed positive branch below \(Q(r)\) and \(H=0\) at and above \(Q(r)\); and
\[
Q(r)\ge U(r)\ \text{if }\sigma\le r<\rho,
\]
so the positive branch holds throughout the strip.

For the support term, if
\[
C=4s-1-r,\qquad
\theta_\pm=\frac{C\pm\sqrt{C^2-4r}}{2r},
\qquad
F_r(\theta)=\frac{r(1+\theta)^2}{9(1+r\theta^2)},
\]
then throughout the open residual strip
\[
\underline\Psi(r,s)=F_r(\theta_-).
\]
Thus neither the endpoint minimum nor the truncation by \(r\) changes branch anywhere inside \(\mathcal D\); its only certification transition is \(s=\beta(r)\).

## Proof

Normalize \(M=1\), \(m=r\), and \(b=s\). Accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8` gives
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
\min\left\{
1+s,\,
s+r,\,
\frac{4s}{9}+\max\{H(r,s),\underline\Psi(r,s)\}
\right\}.
\]
The target is \(2(1+r+s)/9\). Since accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives \(s>1-r\), one has
\[
9(1+s)-2(1+r+s)=7(1+s)-2r>0
\]
and
\[
9(s+r)-2(1+r+s)=7(s+r)-2>5.
\]
Consequently only the tilted branch can be limiting, and it reaches the target exactly when
\[
\max\{H(r,s),\underline\Psi(r,s)\}
\ge
K(r,s):=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9.
\tag{1}
\]
The residual upper bound \(s<U(r)\le1+r\) makes \(K(r,s)>0\).

First analyze \(H\), which is the normalized area-only term from accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1`. The residual lower endpoint satisfies \(L(r)\ge16/25\): this is immediate from \(L=1-r\) for \(r\le9/25\), and the second branch of \(L\) is increasing from \(16/25\). Hence \(s>16/25>9/20\), so the inner maximum in the definition of \(H\) is always its positive branch. Put
\[
R(r,s)=r-\left(2\sqrt{\frac{5s}{9}}-1\right)^2.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
9\bigl(R(r,s)-K(r,s)\bigr)
=
12\sqrt{5s}-18s+7r-11
=
A(r,s).
\tag{2}
\]
Because \(K>0\), equation (2) proves
\[
H(r,s)\ge K(r,s)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A(r,s)\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, either inequality forces \(R\ge K>0\), so truncation at zero is irrelevant at every certified point.

Moreover,
\[
\frac{\partial A}{\partial s}
=
\frac{6\sqrt5}{\sqrt s}-18<0
\tag{4}
\]
for \(s>16/25\). The zero of \(R\) is
\[
Q(r)=\frac9{20}(1+\sqrt r)^2.
\]
Writing \(x=\sqrt r\), direct subtraction gives
\[
Q-(1-r)=\frac{(x+1)(29x-11)}{20},
\]
\[
Q-\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}
=\frac{(x+1)(19-21x)}{20},
\]
\[
Q-(1+r)=-\frac{11x^2-18x+11}{20},
\]
and
\[
Q-(2-r)=\frac{29x^2+18x-31}{20}.
\]
Since \(\rho<4/5<(19/21)^2\), the second difference is positive on the relevant branch of \(L\). The quadratic \(11x^2-18x+11\) is positive because its discriminant is negative. The positive zero of the last difference is
\[
x=\frac{-9+14\sqrt5}{29},
\]
whose square is \(\sigma\). These identities prove every stated transition of \(H\), including its relation to both changes of \(L\) and \(U\).

Now analyze \(\underline\Psi\). On the open strip, compatibility is strict relative to the active lower endpoint, so
\[
C>2\sqrt r,\qquad
0<\theta_-<\frac1{\sqrt r}<\theta_+,
\qquad
\theta_-\theta_+=\frac1r.
\]
Put \(t=\theta_-\), so \(\theta_+=1/(rt)\). Direct substitution gives
\[
F_r\left(\frac1{rt}\right)
=
\frac{(1+rt)^2}{9(1+rt^2)}
\]
and therefore
\[
F_r(t)-F_r\left(\frac1{rt}\right)
=
\frac{(1-r)(rt^2-1)}{9(1+rt^2)}<0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the smaller endpoint value is always the one at \(\theta_-\).

It remains to check the truncation in
\[
\Psi_r(\theta)=\min\{r,F_r(\theta)\}.
\]
If \(r<1/3\), then \(L(r)=1-r\), and \(s>L(r)\) gives
\[
C>3-5r>1+r.
\]
The quadratic \(rz^2-Cz+1\) is positive at \(z=0\) and negative at \(z=1\), so \(t<1\). Hence
\[
\frac{F_r(t)}r
=
\frac{(1+t)^2}{9(1+rt^2)}
<\frac49<1.
\]
If \(r\ge1/3\), then \(t<1/\sqrt r<1/r\), and \(F_r\) is increasing up to \(1/r\). Therefore
\[
\frac{F_r(t)}r
<
\frac{(1+1/\sqrt r)^2}{18}
=
\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{18r}<1.
\]
The last inequality follows because \(17x^2-2x-1>0\) for \(x=\sqrt r\ge1/\sqrt3\). Consequently
\[
\underline\Psi(r,s)=F_r(t)
\tag{6}
\]
everywhere in \(\mathcal D\). This proves that there is no endpoint or truncation transition inside the strip.

Since \(C=rt+t^{-1}\) and
\[
s=\frac{C+1+r}{4},
\]
put \(d=1+r-s\). Then
\[
d=\frac{3+3r-C}{4}.
\]
Using \(C=rt+t^{-1}\), direct factorization yields
\[
r(1+t)^2-2d(1+rt^2)
=
\frac{(rt-1)(rt^3-3rt^2+3t-1)}{2t}.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(rt<\sqrt r<1\), equations (1), (6), and (7) show
\[
\underline\Psi(r,s)\ge K(r,s)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
rt^3-3rt^2+3t-1\le0.
\tag{8}
\]

For fixed \(r<1\), define
\[
q_r(t)=rt^3-3rt^2+3t-1.
\]
Then
\[
q_r'(t)=3\bigl(r(t-1)^2+1-r\bigr)>0,
\]
while
\[
q_r(1/3)=-\frac{8r}{27}<0,
\qquad
q_r(1/2)=\frac{4-5r}{8}>0,
\]
because \(r<\rho<4/5\). Thus \(q_r\) has exactly one root \(t_r\in(1/3,1/2)\). The smaller quadratic root \(\theta_-(r,s)\) is strictly decreasing in \(s\), and the value of \(s\) for which \(\theta_-=t_r\) is
\[
s=\frac{1+r+rt_r+t_r^{-1}}4=\beta(r).
\]
Equation (8) therefore proves the exact support criterion
\[
\underline\Psi(r,s)\ge K(r,s)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s\ge\beta(r).
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (3) and (9) proves the first exact characterization
\[
A(r,s)\ge0\quad\text{or}\quad s\ge\beta(r).
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to determine when the two certified bands meet. Solving \(q_r(t)=0\) for \(r\) gives
\[
r(t)=\frac{3t-1}{t^2(3-t)}.
\tag{11}
\]
Substitution into \(\beta\) gives
\[
s(t)=
\frac{(t+1)(-t^2+6t-1)}{4t^2(3-t)}.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(1/3<t<1/2\),
\[
r'(t)=\frac{6(t-1)^2}{t^3(t-3)^2}>0
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
\frac{ds}{dr}
=
\frac{3-6t-t^2}{12(1-t)}
\le\frac19.
\tag{14}
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to
\[
5-14t-3t^2\le0,
\]
which holds because equality occurs at \(t=1/3\) and the left side is strictly decreasing.

Also \(rt+t^{-1}>2\), so \(s(t)>3/4\). Hence
\[
-18<\frac{\partial A}{\partial s}(r(t),s(t))<0.
\]
If \(ds/dr\ge0\), equations (13)-(14) give
\[
\frac{d}{dr}A(r,\beta(r))
=
7+\frac{\partial A}{\partial s}\frac{ds}{dr}
>7-\frac{18}{9}=5.
\]
If \(ds/dr<0\), the second term is positive, so the derivative exceeds \(7\). Therefore
\[
r\longmapsto A(r,\beta(r))
\]
is strictly increasing throughout \(0<r<\rho\).
\tag{15}

The equation \(A(r(t),s(t))=0\) can be squared without introducing a sign ambiguity because
\[
18s(t)-7r(t)+11>0.
\]
Indeed, it is equivalent to
\[
\bigl(18s(t)-7r(t)+11\bigr)^2=720s(t).
\]
Direct substitution of (11)-(12) gives
\[
\bigl(18s(t)-7r(t)+11\bigr)^2-720s(t)
=
\frac{p(t)}{4t^4(3-t)^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
The left side of (16) has sign opposite to \(A(r(t),s(t))\). Direct exact evaluation gives
\[
p(37/100)
=
\frac{4832801439169}{10^{12}}>0
\]
and
\[
p(187/500)
=
-\frac{2\,371\,235\,105\,951\,631}{15625000000000000}<0.
\]
Together with the strict monotonicity in (15), this proves the existence and uniqueness of \(t_*\) and \(r_*\). Moreover,
\[
r(37/100)=\frac{110000}{360047}>\frac15,
\]
while
\[
r(187/500)=\frac{15250000}{45914297}<\frac13.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac15<r_*<\frac13.
\]
Numerical evaluation gives the stated approximation.

Finally, because \(r_*<1/3\), throughout \(r<r_*\) one has
\[
L(r)=1-r,\qquad U(r)=1+r.
\]
On this range,
\[
A(r,L(r))
=
12\sqrt{5(1-r)}+25r-29.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
25-\frac{6\sqrt5}{\sqrt{1-r}}>0
\]
for \(r<1/3\), and its value at \(r=1/5\) is zero. Since \(A\) is strictly decreasing in \(s\), it follows that no area-certified point exists for \(0<r\le1/5\), while for \(1/5<r<r_*\) the area-certified points are exactly
\[
L(r)<s\le\alpha(r).
\]
The formula for \(\alpha\) follows by solving \(A(r,s)=0\); the other quadratic root lies below \(16/25\).

At the support boundary, equation (7) gives
\[
1+r-\beta(r)
=
\frac{r(1+t_r)^2}{2(1+rt_r^2)}.
\]
Since \(t_r<1/2\), this quantity lies strictly between \(0\) and \(2r\). Hence
\[
L(r)<\beta(r)<U(r)
\]
for every \(r<r_*\). By (15),
\[
A(r,\beta(r))<0\quad(r<r_*),
\]
equals zero at \(r=r_*\), and is positive for \(r>r_*\). Therefore \(\alpha(r)<\beta(r)\) for \(1/5<r<r_*\), while at \(r=r_*\) the two boundaries coincide.

If \(r\ge r_*\) and \(s\ge\beta(r)\), the support term certifies the target by (9). If \(s<\beta(r)\), monotonicity of \(A\) in \(s\) gives
\[
A(r,s)>A(r,\beta(r))\ge0,
\]
so the area term certifies it. Thus every residual point is certified for \(r\ge r_*\). Conversely, for every \(r<r_*\), the open intervals stated in the theorem satisfy both \(A<0\) and \(s<\beta(r)\), so the accepted envelope is strictly below the target there. This proves the exact region and the sharpness of \(r_*\) for this envelope.
