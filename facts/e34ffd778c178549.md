## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies with nonempty interiors and set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
b=|B|,\qquad
T=M+m+b,
\]
and let
\[
z_0=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},
\]
where \(g_0,g_2\) are the outer centroids. Let \(g_B\) be the centroid of \(B\), put
\[
\kappa=\left(\frac45\right)^4=\frac{256}{625},
\]
and, for \(0\le\theta\le1\), define
\[
z_\theta=(1-\theta)g_B+\theta z_0.
\]
For the functions \(\Phi_q\) and regions \(R_q(t)\) of accepted fact `a6099e9164a694ce`,
\[
\Phi_q\!\left(\left\langle q,z_\theta-z_0\right\rangle\right)
\ge
\min\left\{
\kappa m+\frac{4b}{9}(1-\theta)^2,\,
\frac{4b}{9}+\kappa m\theta^4
\right\}
\tag{1}
\]
for every \(q\in\mathbb S^1\).

Consequently, if
\[
\min\left\{
\kappa m+\frac{4b}{9}(1-\theta)^2,\,
\frac{4b}{9}+\kappa m\theta^4
\right\}
\ge\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{2}
\]
then
\[
z_\theta\in\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}R_q(2T/9).
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, the intersection in (3) is nonempty throughout the following explicit subregion:
\[
0<M+m-b
\le
\frac{72m^5}{390625(M+m)^4}.
\tag{4}
\]
This subregion lies strictly inside
\[
M<m+b,\qquad \frac{M+m}{2}<b<M+m.
\]

## Proof

Relabel the outer bodies if necessary so that
\[
|A_0|=M,\qquad |A_2|=m.
\]
Use the centered bodies
\[
K_i=A_i-g_i,\qquad
K_B=B-z_0=\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}
\]
from accepted fact `a6099e9164a694ce`. The origin belongs to \(K_B\).

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\). On the four-dimensional product body
\[
P=K_0\times K_2
\]
define
\[
L_q(x_0,x_2)
=
\frac12\langle q,x_0+x_2\rangle
\]
and
\[
D_q(s)=\{(x_0,x_2)\in P:L_q(x_0,x_2)\le s\},
\qquad
d_q(s)=|D_q(s)|.
\]
For every \(u+v=2s\),
\[
D_q(s)
\subseteq
\bigl(\{\langle q,x_0\rangle\le u\}\times K_2\bigr)
\cup
\bigl(K_0\times\{\langle q,x_2\rangle\le v\}\bigr).
\]
Therefore, using the cumulative cap areas \(F_0,F_2\) of the accepted fact,
\[
d_q(s)
\le
mF_0(u)+MF_2(v)
\le
M\bigl(F_0(u)+F_2(v)\bigr).
\]
Taking the minimum over \(u+v=2s\) gives
\[
\frac{d_q(s)}{M}\le G_q(s).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus
\[
F_B(s)+\frac{d_q(s)}M
\le
F_B(s)+G_q(s)
=
\Phi_q(s).
\tag{6}
\]

The centroid of \(P\) is \((0,0)\), and \(|P|=Mm\). Grünbaum's inequality in dimension four gives
\[
|P\cap\{L_q\le0\}|
\ge
\kappa Mm.
\tag{7}
\]
Likewise, writing
\[
c_B=g_B-z_0,\qquad
a=\langle q,c_B\rangle,
\]
the planar Grünbaum inequality gives
\[
|K_B\cap\{\langle q,x\rangle\le a\}|
\ge
\frac{4b}{9}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(z_\theta\), the scalar threshold in (6) is
\[
s_\theta
=
\langle q,z_\theta-z_0\rangle
=
(1-\theta)a.
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose first that \(a\ge0\). Then \(s_\theta\ge0\), so (7) gives
\[
d_q(s_\theta)\ge\kappa Mm.
\tag{10}
\]
The homothety
\[
x\longmapsto(1-\theta)x
\]
maps \(K_B\) into itself because \(0\in K_B\). It maps the cap in (8) into
\[
K_B\cap\{\langle q,x\rangle\le(1-\theta)a\}.
\]
Its planar Jacobian is \((1-\theta)^2\), hence
\[
F_B(s_\theta)
\ge
\frac{4b}{9}(1-\theta)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (6), (10), and (11) yield
\[
\Phi_q(s_\theta)
\ge
\kappa m+\frac{4b}{9}(1-\theta)^2.
\tag{12}
\]

Now suppose that \(a\le0\). Since \(s_\theta\ge a\), the middle cap contains \(g_B\), and (8) gives
\[
F_B(s_\theta)\ge\frac{4b}{9}.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(g_B\in B\), there is a point \(p=(p_0,p_2)\in P\) such that
\[
\frac{p_0+p_2}{2}=c_B,
\qquad
L_q(p)=a.
\]
The homothety
\[
X\longmapsto\theta X+(1-\theta)p
\]
maps \(P\) into itself. It maps \(P\cap\{L_q\le0\}\) into \(D_q(s_\theta)\), because
\[
L_q\bigl(\theta X+(1-\theta)p\bigr)
\le
(1-\theta)a
=
s_\theta.
\]
Its four-dimensional Jacobian is \(\theta^4\). Thus (7) implies
\[
d_q(s_\theta)\ge\kappa\theta^4Mm.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (6), (13), and (14) give
\[
\Phi_q(s_\theta)
\ge
\frac{4b}{9}+\kappa m\theta^4.
\tag{15}
\]
The two cases prove (1).

Since \(z_\theta\in B\), condition (2) and the exact equivalence in `a6099e9164a694ce` imply
\[
z_\theta\in R_q(2T/9)
\]
for every \(q\), proving (3). This directly supplies a common point. No Helly or separation theorem is invoked, and every one-, two-, or three-direction subfamily contains \(z_\theta\).

It remains to verify the explicit subregion. Put
\[
\delta=M+m-b
\]
and assume (4). Then \(\delta>0\), while
\[
\delta
\le
\frac{72m}{390625}
<
\min\left\{2m,\frac{M+m}{2}\right\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
M<m+b,\qquad
\frac{M+m}{2}<b<M+m,
\]
so this is indeed part of the strict balanced upper-\(b\) branch.

Choose
\[
\theta=\frac{m}{10b}.
\]
Since \(b>(M+m)/2\ge m\), one has \(0<\theta\le1/10\). Also,
\[
\frac{2T}{9}
=
\frac{4b}{9}+\frac{2\delta}{9}.
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(b<M+m\), condition (4) gives
\[
\frac{2\delta}{9}
\le
\frac{256}{625}m
\left(\frac{m}{10b}\right)^4
=
\kappa m\theta^4.
\tag{17}
\]
Hence the second term in the minimum in (1) is at least \(2T/9\).

For the first term, (16), (17), and \(\theta\le1/10\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa m+\frac{4b}{9}(1-\theta)^2-\frac{2T}{9}
&=
\kappa m-\frac{4b}{9}(2\theta-\theta^2)-\frac{2\delta}{9}\\
&\ge
\kappa m-\frac{8b\theta}{9}-\kappa m\theta^4\\
&\ge
m\left[
\frac{256}{625}\left(1-\frac1{10000}\right)-\frac4{45}
\right]
>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (2) holds, completing the proof.

To verify that (4) is nonempty even outside the previously certified region \(b+m\ge2M\), take
\[
A_0=[-1/2,1/2]^2
\]
and
\[
A_2=
\left[-\frac{460263}{250000},\frac{460263}{250000}\right]
\times
\left[-\frac{15625}{460263},\frac{15625}{460263}\right].
\]
Then
\[
M=1,\qquad m=\frac14,
\]
and direct rectangle multiplication gives
\[
b=\frac{287664372919}{230131500000},
\qquad
M+m-b=\frac{2081}{230131500000}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{2081}{230131500000}
<
\frac{18}{244140625}
=
\frac{72(1/4)^5}{390625(5/4)^4},
\]
so (4) holds, while
\[
b+m<\frac32<2=2M.
\]
Hence the certified upper-\(b\) subregion is genuinely nonempty beyond the earlier area-only middle-cover range.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, Theorem 2, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. In dimension \(n\), every halfspace containing the centroid of a convex body has volume at least \((n/(n+1))^n\) times the body's volume. The proof uses the cases \(n=2\) and \(n=4\).
