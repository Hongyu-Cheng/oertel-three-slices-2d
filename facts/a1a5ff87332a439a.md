## Statement

There exists a constant \(r_*>0\) with the following property.

Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a compact polytope whose three nonempty
two-dimensional integer slices are
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
where every \(A_i\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a convex polytope. Put
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\]
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
r=\frac mM.
\]
If
\[
0<r<r_*,
\]
then there exists \(y^*\in S\) such that
\[
h_S(y^*)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Equivalently, no compatible convex-polytopal three-slice counterexample
has endpoint-area ratio smaller than \(r_*\).

## Proof

Suppose the statement is false. For every positive integer \(n\), choose
a compact three-slice polytopal counterexample with
\[
0<r_n<\frac1n.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(r_n\to0\). Apply a common planar affine homothety to all three
slices, dividing every planar area and every halfspace mass by the larger
endpoint area \(M_n\). If necessary, apply the height reflection
\(h\mapsto2-h\). These invertible affine operations preserve compatibility,
counterexample status, and every depth-to-total-area ratio. After them,
\[
|A_{0,n}|=1,\qquad
|A_{2,n}|=r_n,\qquad
|A_{1,n}|=s_n:=\frac{a_{1,n}}{M_n}.
\tag{2}
\]

Because every member of the sequence is a counterexample, none of the
sufficient area criteria entering accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` can
hold. Its exact residual-area conclusion therefore applies and gives
\[
1-r_n<s_n<1+r_n.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular \(s_n\to1\). The normalized target is
\[
t_n=\frac{2(1+r_n+s_n)}9.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus (2)--(4) are the arbitrary-body pinch regime, with no hypothesis on
the shapes of the three polytopes.

For each \(n\), now choose one normalized minimal certificate supplied by
accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`. This choice may vary arbitrarily with
\(n\), but it is made once and retained throughout the argument. Write
its three proper closed halfspaces as
\[
H_{i,n}
=\{(h,w):
f_{i,n}(w)+\sigma_{i,n}(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad i=1,2,3,
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
f_{1,n}+f_{2,n}+f_{3,n}=-1,
\]
the three middle-slice interiors cover the whole middle plane, and
\[
\mu_n(H_{i,n})<t_n
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{6}
\]
Define the actual cap areas of this same certificate by
\[
p_{i,n}
=\left|A_{1,n}\cap\{f_{i,n}\le0\}\right|,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
e_{i,n}
=\left|A_{0,n}\cap
\{f_{i,n}-\sigma_{i,n}\le0\}\right|
+\left|A_{2,n}\cap
\{f_{i,n}+\sigma_{i,n}\le0\}\right|.
\tag{8}
\]
Then (6) is exactly
\[
p_{i,n}+e_{i,n}<t_n
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{9}
\]
Put
\[
D_n=\frac{4s_n}{3}-\sum_{i=1}^3p_{i,n}.
\tag{10}
\]

Accepted fact `f882a944b9fbef55` applies to the arbitrary counterexample
sequence (2)--(3) and to the already selected certificate sequence
(5). It supplies, after common affine gauges and passage to subsequences,
the full arbitrary-body pinch compactification: the large endpoint and
middle body converge to nondegenerate triangles of equal area, the small
endpoint converges to either a point or a nondegenerate compact segment,
and the three oriented middle cutting lines converge, after row
permutation, to the three centroidal vertex-cap lines. No triangular
hypothesis is imposed at finite \(n\).

Accepted fact `33f64ca8c4f58f43` applies to these same halfspaces and their
exact paired endpoint thresholds. It proves, for all sufficiently large
\(n\),
\[
D_n>\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{11}
\]
This includes support contact at a vertex or edge and both possible
point-or-segment limits of the small endpoint.

Accepted fact `8261fac79ed77a4f` applies to the same selected certificate
sequence and proves the opposite arbitrary-rate estimate
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{D_n}{r_n}\le0.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
D_n\le\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (11) and (13) are incompatible.

We audit the subsequence and certificate quantifiers. The certificate in
(5) was chosen before passing to any subsequence, and both accepted
estimates explicitly allow an arbitrary normalized minimal certificate
at every index. Accepted fact `33f64ca8c4f58f43` first proves its
double-cover estimate on an arbitrary compactified subsequence and then
argues that failure on infinitely many original indices would produce a
bad subsequence having a further compactified subsequence, a
contradiction. Hence (11) holds eventually on the original sequence.
Accepted fact `8261fac79ed77a4f` similarly proves its estimate on an
arbitrary compactified subsequence and then observes that a positive
limsup on the original sequence would give a fixed-positive-deficit
subsequence; every further compactified subsequence would retain that
lower bound and contradict the compactified estimate. Hence (12) also
holds for the original sequence. The permitted row permutations do not
change the sum defining \(D_n\), and neither argument reselects the
certificates. Therefore (11) and (13) concern exactly the same \(D_n\).

The contradiction shows that no counterexample sequence can satisfy
\(r_n\to0\). If counterexamples existed with arbitrarily small positive
endpoint ratio, choosing one with \(r_n<1/n\) would produce precisely
such a sequence. Consequently the set of counterexample ratios has a
positive lower bound, and some \(r_*>0\) has the stated property.
