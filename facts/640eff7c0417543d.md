## Statement

Let \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{V_1,V_2,V_3\}\) be a nondegenerate triangle, let \(O\in\operatorname{int}T\), and let \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) be closed halfplanes whose three distinct boundary lines pass through \(O\). Suppose, for every \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), that
\[
 V_i\in\operatorname{int}H_i,\qquad
 V_i\notin H_j\cup H_k.
\]
Thus \(V_i\) lies in the \(i\)-th singleton sector. Let \(G=(V_1+V_2+V_3)/3\) be the centroid of \(T\), assume
\[
 G\in\operatorname{int}H_3,\qquad G\notin H_1\cup H_2,
\]
and set \(p_i=|T\cap H_i|/|T|\). If
\[
 \frac16<p_1<\frac49,\qquad
 p_1=\min\{p_1,p_2,p_3\},\qquad
 p_i+p_j<1\ \ (i\ne j),\qquad
 p_1+p_2+p_3<\frac43,
\]
then
\[
 5(p_2+p_3)-p_1\ge 4.
\]

## Proof

Let \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\) be the barycentric coordinates of \(O\) in \(T\). They are positive and sum to \(1\).

We first record the cap-area formula that will be used repeatedly. For fixed \(i\), write \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), with the order of \(j,k\) specified below. The line \(\partial H_i\) meets \(V_iV_j\) and \(V_iV_k\) at
\[
 (1-t_i)V_i+t_iV_j,\qquad (1-u_i)V_i+u_iV_k
\]
for some \(t_i,u_i\in(0,1)\). In barycentric coordinates this line has equation
\[
 \frac{x_j}{t_i}+\frac{x_k}{u_i}=1.
\]
Since \(O\) lies on the line,
\[
 \frac{\lambda_j}{t_i}+\frac{\lambda_k}{u_i}=1.
\]
Put \(q_i=\lambda_j/t_i\). Then \(1-q_i=\lambda_k/u_i\), and \(t_i,u_i<1\) give
\[
 \lambda_j<q_i<1-\lambda_k.
\]
The cap \(T\cap H_i\) is the triangle at \(V_i\) with the two edge ratios \(t_i,u_i\), so
\[
 p_i=t_iu_i=\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{q_i(1-q_i)}. \tag{1}
\]

Next, \(\lambda_3<\lambda_1,\lambda_2\). Indeed, choose an affine functional \(L_i\) with \(H_i=\{L_i\ge0\}\) and write
\[
 L_i(V_i)=a_i>0,\qquad L_i(V_j)=-b_{ij}<0,\qquad L_i(V_k)=-b_{ik}<0.
\]
Because \(L_i(O)=0\),
\[
 \lambda_i a_i=\lambda_jb_{ij}+\lambda_kb_{ik}. \tag{2}
\]
For \(i=1,2\), the centroid is strictly outside \(H_i\), hence \(a_i<b_{ij}+b_{ik}\). If \(\lambda_i\le\min\{\lambda_j,\lambda_k\}\), then
\[
 \lambda_i a_i
 <\lambda_i(b_{ij}+b_{ik})
 \le\lambda_jb_{ij}+\lambda_kb_{ik},
\]
contradicting (2). Thus neither \(\lambda_1\) nor \(\lambda_2\) can be a smallest barycentric coordinate, and consequently
\[
 \lambda_3<\lambda_1,\lambda_2. \tag{3}
\]

We claim that \(\lambda_1\ge1/3\). Suppose instead that \(\lambda_1<1/3\). By (3), \(\lambda_3<1/3\), so \(\lambda_2>1/3\).

First suppose \(\lambda_2\ge1/2\). Use (1) for \(p_1\) with \((j,k)=(2,3)\). Since \(q_1>\lambda_2\ge1/2\) and \(q(1-q)\) decreases on \([1/2,1]\),
\[
 p_1\ge
 \frac{\lambda_2\lambda_3}{\lambda_2(1-\lambda_2)}
 =\frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_1+\lambda_3}.
\]
Use (1) for \(p_3\) with \((j,k)=(1,2)\). Here \(q_3<1-\lambda_2=\lambda_1+\lambda_3\le1/2\), and \(q(1-q)\) increases on \([0,1/2]\), so
\[
 p_3\ge
 \frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{(\lambda_1+\lambda_3)\lambda_2}
 =\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_3}.
\]
Therefore \(p_1+p_3\ge1\), contrary to the hypotheses.

It remains to consider \(1/3<\lambda_2<1/2\). Formula (1) and \(q(1-q)\le1/4\) give
\[
 p_1\ge4\lambda_2\lambda_3,\qquad
 p_3\ge4\lambda_1\lambda_2. \tag{4}
\]
Write \(x=\lambda_1\) and \(z=\lambda_3\). Now \(x+z=1-\lambda_2>1/2\), while \(0<z<x<1/3\). Hence
\[
 \frac14<x<\frac13,\qquad \frac12-x<z<x.
\]
For fixed \(x\), the function
\[
 (1-x-z)(x+2z)
\]
is concave in \(z\), so its minimum on \([1/2-x,x]\) occurs at an endpoint. At those endpoints its values are respectively
\[
 \frac{1-x}{2}\ge\frac13,\qquad
 3x(1-2x)\ge\frac13.
\]
The second inequality follows because \(3x(1-2x)\) decreases from \(3/8\) to \(1/3\) on \([1/4,1/3]\). Consequently
\[
 \lambda_2(\lambda_1+2\lambda_3)\ge\frac13.
\]
Using (4) and \(p_2\ge p_1\),
\[
 p_1+p_2+p_3
 \ge 2p_1+p_3
 \ge4\lambda_2(\lambda_1+2\lambda_3)
 \ge\frac43,
\]
again a contradiction. This proves \(\lambda_1\ge1/3\).

We next claim that \(\lambda_1<1/2\). If \(\lambda_1\ge1/2\), use (1) for \(p_2\) with \((j,k)=(1,3)\) and for \(p_3\) with \((j,k)=(1,2)\). In both cases \(q_i>\lambda_1\ge1/2\), whence
\[
 p_2\ge
 \frac{\lambda_1\lambda_3}{\lambda_1(1-\lambda_1)}
 =\frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_2+\lambda_3},
 \qquad
 p_3\ge
 \frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{\lambda_1(1-\lambda_1)}
 =\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_2+\lambda_3}.
\]
Thus \(p_2+p_3\ge1\), contrary to the hypotheses. We have proved
\[
 \frac13\le\lambda_1<\frac12. \tag{5}
\]

Finally, (1) and \(q(1-q)\le1/4\) yield
\[
 p_2+p_3
 \ge4\lambda_1\lambda_3+4\lambda_1\lambda_2
 =4\lambda_1(1-\lambda_1)
 \ge\frac89
\]
by (5). Since \(p_1<4/9\),
\[
 5(p_2+p_3)-p_1
 >5\cdot\frac89-\frac49
 =4.
\]
This proves the stated inequality.
