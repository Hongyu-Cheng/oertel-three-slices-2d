## Statement

Assume the following planar assertion. For every compact convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) with nonempty interior, every \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\), and every three closed halfplanes \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) through \(z\) whose nonzero oriented normals are pairwise nonparallel and admit a strictly positive linear dependence, label the caps so that \(c_1\ge c_2\ge c_3\), where \(c_j=|K\cap Q_j|\). Set \(s_3=|K\setminus(Q_1\cup Q_2)|\) and \(h=5c_1/4+c_2-|K|\). The quantity \(h\) is nonnegative by accepted fact `c4077ad10a0441a5`; the assertion assumed here is

\[
4s_3\le\left(\frac32\sqrt{c_1}+\sqrt h\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Then, for every compact polytope \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) such that

\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\]

with each \(A_i\) a nonempty two-dimensional convex body, there exists \(y^*\in S\) satisfying \(h_S(y^*)\ge 2T/9\), where \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and

\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H): H\subset\mathbb R^3
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]

This is a conditional implication; (1) is not asserted as an unconditional theorem here.

## Proof

Assume (1), and suppose a polytope in the statement is a counterexample. Its slices are compact convex polygons. Put \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), and \(t=2T/9\). Reflecting integer height by \(k\mapsto2-k\), if necessary, preserves depth and gives \(a_0=M\), \(a_2=m\). Convexity of \(C\) gives \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\). Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` supplies

\[
M-m<a_1<M+m,\qquad a_i<T/2\quad(i=0,1,2).
\tag{2}
\]

Apply accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346`. There are \(z\in\operatorname{int}A_1\), \(0<d<t\), pairwise nonparallel unit normals \(q_j\), real slopes \(\lambda_j\), and \(\alpha_j>0\) with \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\), \(\sum_j\alpha_jq_j=0\), such that \(d=h_S((1,z))\) and each actual halfspace

\[
\widehat H_j=\{(k,w):q_j\cdot(w-z)+\lambda_j(k-1)\le0\}
\]

has mass \(d\). Here \(k\) is the height coordinate denoted \(h\) in that predecessor. We use only its attained active-family conclusion, retaining its \(z,d,q_j,\lambda_j,\alpha_j\). Its chord-midpoint symbols \(c_{1j}\) and \(c_j\) are not used: throughout this proof \(c_j\) is a cap area, as in (1).

Set \(g_j(w)=\alpha_jq_j\cdot(w-z)\), \(\sigma_j=\alpha_j\lambda_j\), and \(Q_j=\{w:g_j(w)\le0\}\). Thus \(\sum_jg_j=0\), and these \(Q_j\) satisfy every fan hypothesis in (1). Define the actual endpoint cap areas and payments by

\[
a_{ij}=\left|A_i\cap\{w:g_j(w)+\sigma_j(i-1)\le0\}\right|
\quad(i\in\{0,2\}),\qquad
c_j=|A_1\cap Q_j|,\qquad e_j=a_{0j}+a_{2j}=d-c_j.
\tag{3}
\]

For each \(j\), hold \(q_j\) fixed and replace its slope by a finite sufficiently negative number. Compactness of the endpoint bodies makes the height-zero cap empty and the height-two cap full, while the middle cap stays unchanged. This halfspace contains \((1,z)\) and has mass \(c_j+m\). Since \(d\) is the depth at that point, \(d\le c_j+m\). Consequently \(0\le e_j\le m\). Relabel all three cuts together so that \(e_1\le e_2\le e_3\), equivalently \(c_1\ge c_2\ge c_3\). In particular \(d\ge c_1>0\), the strict positivity following from \(z\in\operatorname{int}A_1\).

Apply accepted fact `851e063a1b9908a3` after translating the center to zero: its body is \(A_1-z\), its forms are \(\ell_j(v)=g_j(z+v)\), its total area \(A\) is \(a_1\), and its cap areas \(b_j\) are \(c_j\). They satisfy all its hypotheses, including \(\sum_j\ell_j=0\). With \(p=e_1+e_2\), its inequality gives

\[
4a_1\le5(c_1+c_2+c_3)-6c_3=9d-5p+e_3.
\]

Using \(9d<2T\) and (2), we obtain

\[
5p-e_3\le9d-4a_1
<2M+2m-2a_1<4m.
\]

As \(e_3\le m\), this proves \(p<m\). Set \(n=m-p\); then \(0<n\le m\le M\).

Trim the first two actual cuts from both endpoints using the closed opposite halfplanes:

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_0&=A_0\cap\{g_1\ge\sigma_1\}\cap\{g_2\ge\sigma_2\},\\
K_2&=A_2\cap\{g_1\ge-\sigma_1\}\cap\{g_2\ge-\sigma_2\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(x=a_{01}+a_{02}\). From (3), \(0\le x\le p\) and \(a_{21}+a_{22}=p-x\). The union bound, and the fact that cutting lines have area zero, give

\[
|K_0|\ge M-x>0,\qquad |K_2|\ge m-(p-x)=n+x>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus both trims are compact convex bodies with nonempty interior. For \(u_0\in K_0\), \(u_2\in K_2\), averaging each of the two inequalities in (4) cancels the slopes and yields \(g_j((u_0+u_2)/2)\ge0\) for \(j=1,2\). Compatibility therefore gives

\[
\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\subseteq
A_1\cap\{g_1\ge0\}\cap\{g_2\ge0\}.
\tag{6}
\]

The right side of (6) has area exactly \(s_3=|A_1\setminus(Q_1\cup Q_2)|\), since replacing its weak inequalities by strict inequalities changes it only on two null lines. Apply the planar Brunn-Minkowski theorem in the form recorded in accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8`, with its bodies \(B=K_0\), \(D=K_2\) and mixing parameter \(\lambda=1/2\). Equations (5)-(6) imply

\[
4s_3\ge\bigl(\sqrt{|K_0|}+\sqrt{|K_2|}\bigr)^2
\ge\bigl(\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{n+x}\bigr)^2
\ge\bigl(\sqrt M+\sqrt n\bigr)^2.
\tag{7}
\]

For the last inequality, the two sums under the squares have the same sum of squared terms \(M+n\), and

\[
(M-x)(n+x)-Mn=x(M-n-x)\ge0,
\]

because \(x\le p=m-n\) and \(m\le M\). All square roots in (7) have positive arguments.

Now use accepted fact `c4077ad10a0441a5` with its \(K=A_1\) and the same \(Q_j,c_j,s_3\). Its scalar \(u\) is denoted here by \(h\), so
\(h=5c_1/4+c_2-a_1\ge0\). Since \(p=2d-c_1-c_2\), we have \(n=m-2d+c_1+c_2\). The inequalities \(9d<2T\) and \(M<a_1+m\) from (2) imply \(9d<4(a_1+m)\). Hence

\[
n>\frac d4+c_1+c_2-a_1
\ge\frac54c_1+c_2-a_1=h.
\tag{8}
\]

Also \(9d<2T\) gives

\[
M+n=M+m-2d+c_1+c_2
>\frac52d+c_1+c_2-a_1
\ge\frac94c_1+h.
\tag{9}
\]

Because \(M\ge n>h\ge0\),

\[
Mn-h(M+n-h)=(M-h)(n-h)>0.
\]

Together with (9) and \(h\ge0\), this yields \(Mn>9c_1h/4\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\sqrt M+\sqrt n\bigr)^2
&=M+n+2\sqrt{Mn}\\
&>\frac94c_1+h+3\sqrt{c_1h}
=\left(\frac32\sqrt{c_1}+\sqrt h\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

No division by \(h\) was used; the strict comparison also holds when \(h=0\). Applying the assumed planar inequality (1) to \(K=A_1\) contradicts (7) and (10). Thus the supposed counterexample cannot exist, proving the stated conditional implication.
