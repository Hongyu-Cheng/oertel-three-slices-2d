## Statement

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) have column sums one and satisfy
\[
|\det U|=\frac15,\qquad |\det V|=\frac{11}{10},
\qquad
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\ge0
\quad(k=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) have first row
\[
L_{1*}=(68,-32,-32).
\tag{2}
\]
There is no real number \(\sigma_1\) for which
\[
\frac15\mathcal C((LU)_{1*};-\sigma_1)=\frac1{25},
\qquad
\frac{11}{10}\mathcal C((LV)_{1*};\sigma_1)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the fixed row (2), with exactly the endpoint contributions (3), has no real compatible lift with the determinant areas in (1).

## Proof

For \(W=U,V\), write
\[
m_k^W:=\min_jW_{kj},
\qquad
h_W:=1-\sum_{k=1}^3m_k^W.
\tag{4}
\]
Every column \(w^j\) of \(W\) has coordinate sum one. Hence
\[
w^j-m^W\ge0,
\qquad
\mathbf1^\top(w^j-m^W)=h_W,
\tag{5}
\]
so the column triangle of \(W\) is contained in the translate
\[
m^W+h_W\Delta.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(h_W\ge0\), and \(h_W>0\) when \(\det W\ne0\). The normalized area of (6) is \(h_W^2\). The determinant-area identity in accepted fact 64760c76cadff481 therefore gives
\[
\frac15=|\det U|\le h_U^2,
\qquad
h_U\ge\frac1{\sqrt5}.
\tag{7}
\]
Summing the three compatibility inequalities in (1) gives
\[
\sum_km_k^V\ge-\sum_km_k^U.
\]
Thus
\[
h_V=1-\sum_km_k^V
\le1+\sum_km_k^U
=2-h_U
\le2-\frac1{\sqrt5}.
\tag{8}
\]

The special coefficients in (2) identify the endpoint projection with the first barycentric coordinate. Indeed, the column identities give, for each \(j\),
\[
(LU)_{1j}=68U_{1j}-32U_{2j}-32U_{3j}
=100U_{1j}-32,
\tag{9}
\]
and similarly
\[
(LV)_{1j}=100V_{1j}-32.
\tag{10}
\]
Neither \(U_{1*}\) nor \(V_{1*}\) is constant. If, for example, \(U_{1*}\) were constant, the three column differences of \(U\) would have first coordinate zero and coordinate sum zero, a one-dimensional subspace, so \(\det U=0\). The same argument applies to \(V\).

Divide the two equations in (3) by their positive area factors. The exact nonconstant cap branches (2)-(4) of accepted fact 64760c76cadff481 show that
\[
\mathcal C((LU)_{1*};-\sigma_1)=\frac15
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\min_j(LU)_{1j}<-\sigma_1<\max_j(LU)_{1j},
\tag{11}
\]
whereas
\[
\mathcal C((LV)_{1*};\sigma_1)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma_1\ge\max_j(LV)_{1j}.
\tag{12}
\]
These implications include both repeated-value branches and threshold equality at the upper support. The constant branch is already excluded by the nonzero determinants.

Put
\[
\alpha:=\frac{32-\sigma_1}{100},
\qquad
\beta:=\frac{32+\sigma_1}{100}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (9)-(12) give
\[
m_1^U<\alpha,
\qquad
M_1^V:=\max_jV_{1j}\le\beta.
\tag{14}
\]
Compatibility in coordinate one gives \(m_1^V\ge-m_1^U\). Therefore the first-coordinate width \(\omega:=M_1^V-m_1^V\) of the \(V\)-triangle satisfies the strict exact bound
\[
\omega
\le\beta+m_1^U
<\beta+\alpha
=\frac{16}{25}.
\tag{15}
\]

Translate the three columns of \(V\) by \(-m^V\). They lie in
\[
Q(h_V,\omega)
:=
\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^3:
x_1+x_2+x_3=h_V,\ 0\le x_1\le\omega\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(m_1^V\) is attained by a column, the translated first-coordinate range is exactly \([0,\omega]\), and \(0<\omega\le h_V\). In the coordinates \((x_1,x_2)\), the four vertices of (16) are
\[
(0,0),\quad(0,h_V),\quad(\omega,0),
\quad(\omega,h_V-\omega).
\tag{17}
\]
The signed doubled area of a triangle is affine in each of its three vertices. Maximizing it and its negative over the product of three copies of this quadrilateral therefore reduces to vertex triples. The four distinct triples in (17) have normalized areas
\[
\omega h_V,\quad \omega h_V,
\quad \omega(h_V-\omega),
\quad \omega(h_V-\omega).
\tag{18}
\]
Thus every triangle contained in (16) has normalized area at most \(\omega h_V\). Translation preserves area, and accepted fact 64760c76cadff481 identifies the normalized area of the \(V\)-triangle with \(|\det V|\). Hence
\[
\frac{11}{10}=|\det V|
\le\omega h_V
<\frac{16}{25}\left(2-\frac1{\sqrt5}\right).
\tag{19}
\]
Finally,
\[
\frac1{\sqrt5}>\frac9{32}
\]
because both sides are positive and \(1/5>81/1024\), equivalently \(1024>405\). Consequently
\[
\frac{16}{25}\left(2-\frac1{\sqrt5}\right)
<\frac{16}{25}\left(2-\frac9{32}\right)
=\frac{11}{10},
\tag{20}
\]
contradicting (19). Therefore no real \(U,V,\sigma_1\) satisfying (1)-(3) exists.
