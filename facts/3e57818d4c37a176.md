## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and assume the complete canonical finite-interior
common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention: the three affine
cutting functions have pairwise nonparallel linear parts and sum to
\(-1\); their offsets have sum less than \(-1\); the two source polygons
and the middle polygon are nonempty and two-dimensional; the midpoint
containment holds; \(M\geq m\); the three crossing equalities and both
canonical tails hold; and the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{N,\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}.
\]
Write
\[
q=q_N>0,\qquad x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\]
and assume
\[
p_\varnothing>0,\qquad
r_N>0,\qquad r_{\{1\}},r_{\{2\}},r_{\{3\}}>0.
\]
Assume all exact partial-union, overlap, source-cap, and middle-cap
inequalities in the hypotheses of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31`, and
suppose
\[
W=0.
\]

Put
\[
t_i=\frac{x_i}{q},\qquad R=t_1+t_2+t_3,\qquad
D=\frac{q}{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
For \(\{i,j,k\}=N\), define
\[
A_k=\frac{1+R}{t_k},\qquad
c_k=2\sqrt{t_it_j},
\]
\[
d_{1k}=\frac{4A_kc_k^2}{(A_k+c_k^2)^2},
\qquad
d_{2k}
=1-\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}
\right)^2,
\]
where
\[
\operatorname{clip}(z)=\min\{1,\max\{0,z\}\},
\]
and put
\[
d_0=\min\left\{
1,\frac3{t_1^{-1}+t_2^{-1}+t_3^{-1}}
\right\},
\qquad
\delta=\min\{d_0,d_{11},d_{12},d_{13},d_{21},d_{22},d_{23}\}.
\]
Use the functions \(g,\epsilon,\tau,\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\) of accepted fact
`0009896ebff12f31`; explicitly,
\[
g(t_1,t_2,t_3)
=
\min_{S\subseteq N}
\left(
2\sum_{i\notin S}t_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\sqrt{t_it_j}
\right),
\]
\[
\epsilon
=
\max\left\{
0,\min\left\{
\frac32,\frac{1-R}{2}+g(t_1,t_2,t_3)
\right\}
\right\},
\]
\[
\tau(R)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{1+R}+\sqrt{204+120R}\right)^2},
\]
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{def}}(t_1,t_2,t_3)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+\sqrt{204+120R+28\epsilon}
\right)^2}.
\]

Apply the endpoint-preserving normal form of accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`, and write its three side gaps as
\[
L_i=1-a_{i,i^-}-a_{i,i^+}>0.
\]
All eight choices
\[
(a_{ij},a_{ji})=(0,0)
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
a_{ij}>0,\ a_{ji}>0
\]
for the three cyclic endpoint pairs are allowed.  Assume
\[
\frac1{50}\leq t_1\leq\frac3{100},
\qquad
3\leq t_2\leq\frac72,
\qquad
3\leq t_3\leq\frac72,
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\max\{L_2,L_3\}>L_1.

\]
Then no such full common-plane realization exists.

## Proof

We first verify that the entire closed ratio prism (1) lies in the exact
survivor \(\delta<\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\), rather than in a region already
closed by the global minimum.

For every \(S\subseteq N\), the expression defining \(g\) is at least
\(76/25\).  The only potentially smallest choices are \(S=\{2,3\}\) and
\(S=N\).  For the first,
\[
2t_1+\sqrt{t_2t_3}
\geq\frac1{25}+3=\frac{76}{25}.
\]
For the second,
\[
\sqrt{t_1t_2}+\sqrt{t_1t_3}+\sqrt{t_2t_3}
>
\frac15+\frac15+3
>
\frac{76}{25},
\]
where \(\sqrt{t_1t_j}\geq\sqrt{3/50}>1/5\).  Every other choice of
\(S\) has either a term \(2t_2\) or \(2t_3\), or is still larger
directly.  Also
\[
R\leq\frac3{100}+\frac72+\frac72=\frac{703}{100}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{1-R}{2}+g
\geq
-\frac{603}{200}+\frac{608}{200}
=\frac1{40}>0,
\tag{3}
\]
so
\[
\epsilon\geq\frac1{40}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
0<\tau_{\mathrm{def}}<\tau.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(k=1\),
\[
A_1
=1+\frac{1+t_2+t_3}{t_1}
\geq1+\frac7{3/100}
=\frac{703}{3},
\]
whereas
\[
c_1=2\sqrt{t_2t_3}\leq7.
\]
Hence
\[
\sqrt{A_1}>15\geq1+2c_1,
\]
because \(703/3>225\).  It follows that
\[
\frac{\sqrt{A_1}-c_1}{1+c_1}>1.
\]
Thus the clipped value in \(d_{21}\) is \(1\), and
\[
d_{21}=0,\qquad \delta=0<\tau_{\mathrm{def}}.
\tag{5}
\]
This proves that every ratio point in (1), including every boundary point,
is a genuine point of the defect-refined survivor.

We next retain the identity of the actual largest side gap instead of
taking the minimum over all three possible identities.  Suppose first that
all six chord endpoints are noncoincident, and let \(k\) be an index for
which
\[
L_k=\max\{L_1,L_2,L_3\}.
\]
The proof of accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246`, before its final minimum over
the three possible values of \(k\), proves the sharper local implication
\[
D\geq\delta_k:=\min\{d_0,d_{1k},d_{2k}\}.
\tag{6}
\]
For completeness, if \(D<\delta_k\), then \(D<d_0\) forces \(L_k\geq D\).
The cap-vertex determinant in that accepted proof gives the necessary
strict inequality
\[
\sqrt{L_k-D}
+c_k\left(1+\sqrt{1-D}\right)
-\sqrt{A_kL_k}<0.
\tag{7}
\]
As a function of \(L_k\in[D,1]\), the left side of (7) has no interior
minimum.  The assumptions \(D<d_{1k}\) and \(D<d_{2k}\) make its values at
\(L_k=D\) and \(L_k=1\), respectively, strictly positive.  This
contradicts (7), proving (6), including equality \(D=\delta_k\).

By (2), an actual largest gap can be chosen with
\[
k\in\{2,3\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Fix such a \(k\), and let \(j\) be the other element of \(\{2,3\}\).
On (1),
\[
\frac{376}{175}
\leq
A_k=1+\frac{1+t_1+t_j}{t_k}
\leq
\frac{251}{100},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\frac6{25}
\leq
c_k^2=4t_1t_j
\leq
\frac{21}{50}.
\tag{10}
\]
Put \(z=c_k^2/A_k\).  Equations (9)-(10) give
\[
\frac{24}{251}\leq z\leq\frac{147}{752}<\frac15.
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore
\[
d_{1k}
=\frac{4z}{(1+z)^2}
\geq
\frac{25}{9}z
\geq
\frac{200}{753}.
\tag{12}
\]

For the other endpoint component, (9) gives
\[
\sqrt{A_k}<\frac85,
\]
while (10) gives
\[
c_k\geq\frac{\sqrt6}{5}>\frac{12}{25}.
\]
The unclipped quotient therefore satisfies
\[
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}
<
\frac{8/5-12/25}{1+12/25}
=\frac{28}{37}<\frac45.
\]
Clipping cannot increase this upper bound, so
\[
d_{2k}>\frac9{25}.
\tag{13}
\]
Finally,
\[
t_1^{-1}+t_2^{-1}+t_3^{-1}
\leq
50+\frac13+\frac13
=\frac{152}{3},
\]
and hence
\[
d_0\geq\frac9{152}.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)-(14) prove
\[
\delta_k\geq\frac9{152}.
\tag{15}
\]

The same closed prism gives
\[
R\geq\frac1{50}+3+3=\frac{301}{50}.
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt{1+R}>\frac52,
\qquad
\sqrt{204+120R}>30,
\]
because \(1+R\geq351/50>25/4\) and
\(204+120R\geq4632/5>900\).  Therefore
\[
\tau
<
\frac{49}{(65/2)^2}
=\frac{196}{4225}
<
\frac9{152},
\tag{16}
\]
where the last comparison is the exact integer inequality
\[
196\cdot152=29792<38025=9\cdot4225.
\]
Combining (4), (6), (15), and (16) gives
\[
D\geq\frac9{152}>\tau>\tau_{\mathrm{def}}.
\tag{17}
\]

Accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` applies under the stated common-plane
hypotheses and \(\epsilon>0\).  Its strict defect bridge says that \(W=0\)
forces
\[
D<\tau_{\mathrm{def}},
\tag{18}
\]
contradicting (17).

It remains to justify every geometric boundary.  Accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8` includes coincident endpoint pairs, collinear labels,
redundant labels, and the full closed ranges of its central-arc variables.
For a polygon with coincident chord endpoints, use the area-convergent
truncations in accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246`.  Condition (2) has positive
gap, so for all sufficiently small truncations an actual largest gap still
has index \(2\) or \(3\).  The cap ratios and \(D\) converge to their
original values.  Passing to a subsequence fixes that largest-gap index,
and continuity of \(d_0,d_{1k},d_{2k}\) in the positive ratios lets (6)
pass to the limit.  Hence (17) holds on every endpoint-coincidence stratum
as well.

All inequalities defining (1) were non-strict, and the estimates
(3), (9)-(16) retain equality at every ratio face.  Cutting lines have zero
area, so weak/strict exact-cell assignments do not alter the argument.
Thus all ratio, endpoint, collinearity, redundant-vertex, and proper-cell
boundaries claimed in the statement are covered.  The sole part of this
rational prism not claimed here is the explicitly identified closed gap
chamber
\[
L_1\geq\max\{L_2,L_3\}.
\]
