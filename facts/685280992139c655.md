## Statement

For each \(n\), assume the affine sign-plane and exact-cell hypotheses
of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, the single-double support of
accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`,
\[
\mathcal S_Q=\{123,1,2,12\},
\tag{1}
\]
and actual nonempty two-dimensional compact convex polytopes
\[
A_{0,n},A_{2,n},K_n\subset\mathbb R^2,\qquad
\frac{A_{0,n}+A_{2,n}}2\subseteq K_n.
\tag{2}
\]
Write
\[
M_n=|A_{0,n}|,\qquad m_n=|A_{2,n}|,\qquad k_n=|K_n|,
\]
\[
p_n=p_{\varnothing,n},\qquad
u_{i,n}=\sum_{J\ni i}p_{J,n},\qquad
v_{i,n}=\sum_{H\ni i}q_{H,n},\qquad
c_{i,n}=\sum_{G\ni i}r_{G,n}.
\]
Assume
\[
m_n=1,\qquad M_n\geq1,\qquad M_n\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{3}
\]
the three crossings and both canonical tails,
\[
u_{i,n}+v_{i,n}+c_{i,n}
=\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
c_{i,n}+1\geq\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9,\qquad
c_{i,n}+M_n\geq\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9,
\tag{5}
\]
\(W=0\), and the no-half inequalities
\[
M_n<1+k_n,\qquad
1<M_n+k_n,\qquad
k_n<M_n+1.
\tag{6}
\]
Then no such sequence exists.

Indeed, every hypothetical sequence first satisfies
\[
\frac{p_n}{M_n}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{k_n}{M_n}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{c_{i,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\frac49
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{7}
\]
and, with
\[
E_{R,n}=r_{12,n}+r_{13,n}+r_{23,n}+2r_{123,n},
\]
\[
\frac{E_{R,n}}{M_n}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\tag{8}
\]
The actual convex middle geometry then forces
\[
\frac{r_{i,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\frac29
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\frac{r_{ij,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\frac19
\quad(1\leq i<j\leq3),\qquad
\frac{r_{123,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
The stronger reflected-support argument below contradicts (7), so
(9)-(10) are necessary escape limits that cannot actually be attained
by a full sequence.

For the concrete scalar family of accepted fact
`d6e2a038c400f196`, let \(N\geq4\), retain all its \(P\)- and
\(Q\)-cell masses, and write
\[
e_N=\frac{20N^2-47N-60}{120N},
\qquad
s_N=\frac{10N+77}{90}+\frac1N.
\tag{11}
\]
Replace only the following middle masses:
\[
r_{12}=\frac1N,\qquad
r_{13}=r_{23}=e_N-\frac1{N^2},
\tag{12}
\]
\[
r_1=r_2=
\frac{100N^2-13N-180}{360N},
\qquad
r_3=s_N+\frac1{N^2},
\qquad
r_{123}=\frac1{N^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
All remaining middle cells have the same zero values as in
`d6e2a038c400f196`. This is an exact positive-central scalar
calibration with \(m=1\), \(M=k=N\). It retains all crossings, both
tails, \(W=0\), all three no-half inequalities, and the Scott
inequalities in accepted fact `e51585de76810353`. It also satisfies
the support implication of accepted fact `93f1cefaa4795a25`. However,
there is no infinite set of integers \(N\) for which these exact
masses have actual common-plane realizations satisfying (1)-(6).
The same conclusion holds after arbitrary \(o(N)\) changes to the
middle masses if their normalized limits remain those of
`d6e2a038c400f196`.

## Proof

We first derive the scalar asymptotics of every hypothetical actual
sequence. Combining the left tail in (5) with the crossing in (4)
gives
\[
u_{i,n}+v_{i,n}\leq1.
\tag{14}
\]
In particular,
\[
0\leq u_{i,n}\leq1.
\]
Every nonempty \(P\)-index contributes at least its cell mass to
\(\sum_i u_{i,n}\). Since \(P_{123}\) is forced empty by accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`,
\[
0\leq M_n-p_n
=\sum_{J\ne\varnothing}p_{J,n}
\leq\sum_{i=1}^3u_{i,n}\leq3.
\tag{15}
\]
This proves the first limit in (7). The first and third inequalities
in (6) give
\[
M_n-1<k_n<M_n+1,
\tag{16}
\]
which proves the second limit.

Put
\[
h_n=\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9.
\]
Because \(u_{i,n},v_{i,n}\leq1\), equations (4) and (16) give
\[
\frac{c_{i,n}}{M_n}
=\frac{h_n-u_{i,n}-v_{i,n}}{M_n}
\longrightarrow\frac49.
\]
Together with \(k_n/M_n\to1\), this proves the last limits in (7).

Accepted fact `e51585de76810353` gives in the single-double branch
\[
2M_n=1+k_n+
3\left(
\sum_i u_{i,n}+q_{12,n}+2q_{123,n}+E_{R,n}
\right).
\tag{17}
\]
The terms preceding \(E_{R,n}\) in parentheses are bounded because
\(m_n=1\) and (14) holds. Dividing (17) by \(M_n\) and using (16)
proves (8).

There is also a uniform positive singleton proportion. For
\(\{i,j,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}\), exact cell counting gives
\[
r_{i,n}
=k_n-c_{j,n}-c_{\ell,n}
+r_{j\ell,n}+r_{123,n}.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence (7) implies
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_{i,n}}{k_n}\geq\frac19.
\tag{19}
\]
All three middle singleton cells therefore have positive area for all
large \(n\).

We next prove the triangle-fan rigidity needed here. This repeats the
compactness and equality argument underlying accepted fact
`4dedeef20dd1efbf`, but uses only (7), (19), convexity, and the affine
identity, so no all-double hypothesis is imported.

For each \(n\), send the John ellipse of \(K_n\) to the closed unit
disk \(B\) by an affine isomorphism \(G_n\). Replace the bodies and
affine functions by their images and compositions, and retain the same
notation. Then
\[
B\subseteq K_n\subseteq2B.
\tag{20}
\]
All cell-area ratios and all signs are unchanged. After passage to a
subsequence,
\[
K_n\longrightarrow K
\tag{21}
\]
in Hausdorff distance, where \(B\subseteq K\subseteq2B\).

Let
\[
H_{i,n}=\{f_{i,n}\leq0\},\qquad
\ell_{i,n}=\|\nabla f_{i,n}\|,\qquad
S_n=\ell_{1,n}+\ell_{2,n}+\ell_{3,n},
\]
\[
b_{i,n}=\frac{\ell_{i,n}}{S_n},\qquad
\phi_{i,n}=\frac{f_{i,n}}{\ell_{i,n}},\qquad
d_n=\frac1{S_n}.
\tag{22}
\]
Each \(\ell_{i,n}>0\), and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3b_{i,n}\phi_{i,n}=-d_n.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (7) and (19) show that both sides of every zero line meet
\(\operatorname{int}K_n\) for all large \(n\). The linear part of each
\(\phi_{i,n}\) has norm one, so (20) bounds its constant term. Pass to
a further subsequence such that
\[
\phi_{i,n}\longrightarrow\phi_i,\qquad
b_{i,n}\longrightarrow b_i,\qquad
d_n\longrightarrow d.
\tag{24}
\]
Local uniform convergence, Hausdorff convergence, and the nullity of
the limiting lines give
\[
|K\cap H_i|=\frac49|K|,
\qquad
H_i=\{\phi_i\leq0\}.
\tag{25}
\]

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(K\). We claim
\[
\phi_i(g)\geq0.
\tag{26}
\]
If \(\phi_i(g)<0\), the parallel halfplane
\[
\{\phi_i\leq\phi_i(g)\}
\]
has boundary through \(g\) and is strictly contained in \(H_i\) inside
\(K\). The planar Grünbaum inequality recorded in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` gives it area at least \(4|K|/9\); the intervening
strip has positive area. This contradicts (25), proving (26).

Passing (23) to the limit and evaluating at \(g\) yields
\[
\sum_i b_i\phi_i(g)=-d.
\]
All terms on the left are nonnegative, so
\[
d=0,\qquad
\phi_i(g)=0\quad\text{whenever }b_i>0.
\tag{27}
\]
There cannot be only one positive \(b_i\), because then a nonconstant
affine function would vanish identically. If exactly two coefficients
were positive, the corresponding unit-normal affine functions would
be negatives of one another and their halfplanes would be
complementary. Their areas would then sum to \(|K|\), contrary to
\(2(4|K|/9)=8|K|/9\). Therefore
\[
b_1b_2b_3>0,
\tag{28}
\]
and all three limiting lines pass through \(g\).

Equality in planar Grünbaum now applies to each halfplane in (25).
The equality characterization audited in accepted fact
`4dedeef20dd1efbf` implies that \(K\) is a triangle and each \(H_i\)
is one of its centroidal vertex caps. Let
\(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3\) be the barycentric coordinates of \(K\), and put
\[
\chi_j=\frac13-\mu_j.
\tag{29}
\]
Every \(\phi_i\) is a positive multiple of one \(\chi_j\). The only
linear dependence among the three functions in (29) is
\[
\chi_1+\chi_2+\chi_3=0.
\]
The limiting form of (23), together with (28), therefore forces every
one of the three centroidal caps to occur exactly once. After
relabeling,
\[
H_i=\{\mu_i\geq1/3\}.
\tag{30}
\]

The cell areas of this fan are exact. A vertex cap has area
\(4|K|/9\), the intersection of two distinct caps has area
\(|K|/9\), and the triple intersection is the centroid. Hence its
singleton part has area \(2|K|/9\). Dominated convergence away from
the three null limiting lines proves (9)-(10).

For completeness, this also audits the finite-stage central support.
For all large \(n\), choose \(x_{i,n}\) in the interior of \(R_{i,n}\)
and put
\[
\lambda_{i,n}=-f_{i,n},\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_{i,n}=1.
\]
The matrix whose \(i\)-th column is
\((\lambda_{1,n}(x_{i,n}),\lambda_{2,n}(x_{i,n}),
\lambda_{3,n}(x_{i,n}))\)
has diagonal entries greater than one, negative off-diagonal entries,
and column sums one. The minimum-coordinate argument used in accepted
fact `4dedeef20dd1efbf` shows that its inverse is nonnegative. Thus
each standard barycentric vertex belongs to
\(\operatorname{conv}\{x_{1,n},x_{2,n},x_{3,n}\}\subseteq K_n\).
Consequently the full central triangle
\[
\Delta_n=\{\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n},\lambda_{3,n}\geq0\}
\tag{31}
\]
is contained in \(K_n\), and
\[
|\Delta_n|=r_{123,n}>0.
\tag{32}
\]
Also, (15) and accepted fact `d3afd91c459bcce8` give \(r_{12,n}>0\).
Equations (10) give \(r_{13,n},r_{23,n}>0\) for all large \(n\), so
accepted fact `93f1cefaa4795a25` independently confirms (32).

We now adapt the high-occupancy and reflected-support parts of accepted
fact `043e84c0c8d6e05c`. This is where the single-double support (1)
is sufficient.

Define
\[
\psi_{i,n}=\frac{f_{i,n}}{S_n}=b_{i,n}\phi_{i,n}.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (27)-(30) imply, after relabeling,
\[
\psi_{i,n}\longrightarrow
\tau_i\left(\frac13-\mu_i\right),
\qquad \tau_i>0.
\]
Since \(\sum_i\psi_{i,n}=-1/S_n\to0\), the unique dependence in
(29) gives
\[
\tau_1=\tau_2=\tau_3=:\tau>0.
\tag{34}
\]
Put
\[
A_n=\max_{1\leq i\leq3}\max_{z\in K_n}\psi_{i,n}(z),
\qquad
a_n=S_nA_n.
\tag{35}
\]
Then
\[
A_n\longrightarrow\frac{\tau}{3},\qquad
S_n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
a_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{36}
\]
By definition,
\[
K_n\subseteq
\mathcal C_n:=\{f_{i,n}\leq a_n\ (i=1,2,3)\}.
\tag{37}
\]
In the John coordinates, the three defining inequalities of
\(\mathcal C_n\) converge to \(\mu_i\geq0\). Thus
\(\mathcal C_n\to K\) in Hausdorff distance and
\[
\theta_n:=\frac{k_n}{|\mathcal C_n|}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{38}
\]
Because (31) is the central triangle and (32) gives its area, the
homothety in the affine plane \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) gives
\[
|\mathcal C_n|
=r_{123,n}(1+3a_n)^2.
\tag{39}
\]

It remains to bound \(P_{\varnothing,n}\) inside its reflected ambient
triangle. Undoing the John maps does not change \(a_n\), any cell-area
ratio, or the following argument. Suppress \(n\), and define
\[
\beta_i=-L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-\sum_iL_i>0.
\tag{40}
\]
Then \(\sum_i\beta_i=1+\delta\), and
\[
P_\varnothing\subseteq
\mathcal T_P:=\{\lambda_i\leq\beta_i\ (i=1,2,3)\}.
\tag{41}
\]

For every coordinate there is a positive \(Q\)-cell omitting it:
\(Q_2\) omits \(1\), \(Q_1\) omits \(2\), and \(Q_1\) also omits
\(3\). Choose a point \(y_i\) in the indicated cell. The exact strict
sign convention gives
\[
\lambda_i(y_i)<L_i=-\beta_i.
\tag{42}
\]
For any \(x\in P_\varnothing\), compatibility and (37) give
\[
\lambda_i(x)+\lambda_i(y_i)\geq-2a.
\]
Together with (42),
\[
\lambda_i(x)>\beta_i-2a
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{43}
\]

Use reflected barycentric coordinates
\[
\nu_i=\frac{\beta_i-\lambda_i}{\delta}.
\tag{44}
\]
On \(\mathcal T_P\), they are nonnegative and sum to one. Equations
(41) and (43) put \(P_\varnothing\) inside
\[
\left\{\nu_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i\nu_i=1,\quad
\nu_i<\frac{2a}{\delta}\ (i=1,2,3)\right\}.
\tag{45}
\]
The triangle \(\mathcal T_P\) is a reflected dilation of \(\Delta\) by
factor \(\delta\), so its area is
\[
|\mathcal T_P|=\delta^2r_{123}.
\tag{46}
\]

For the unit barycentric simplex, the relative area on which
\(\max_i\nu_i\leq s\) is
\[
F(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq s\leq1/3,\\
(3s-1)^2,&1/3\leq s\leq1/2,\\
1-3(1-s)^2,&1/2\leq s\leq1,\\
1,&s\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{47}
\]
Therefore
\[
p_\varnothing
\leq\delta^2r_{123}F(2a/\delta).
\tag{48}
\]
Checking the four ranges in (47) gives the sharp uniform estimate
\[
\delta^2F(2a/\delta)\leq6a^2.
\tag{49}
\]
Indeed, for \(\delta\leq2a\) the left side is at most \(4a^2\); for
\(2a\leq\delta\leq4a\) it is
\[
-2\delta^2+12a\delta-12a^2\leq6a^2;
\]
for \(4a\leq\delta\leq6a\) it is
\((6a-\delta)^2\leq4a^2\); and for \(\delta\geq6a\) it is zero.
Thus
\[
\boxed{\quad p_n\leq6a_n^2r_{123,n}.\quad}
\tag{50}
\]

Combining (38)-(39) with (50) gives
\[
\frac{k_n}{p_n}
\geq
\theta_n\frac{(1+3a_n)^2}{6a_n^2}.
\tag{51}
\]
Since the right side tends to \(3/2\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{k_n}{p_n}\geq\frac32.
\]
This contradicts \(k_n/p_n\to1\) from (7). The hypothetical
mass-growing sequence cannot exist.

We finish by auditing the positive-central perturbation
(11)-(13). Relative to the scalar calibration in accepted fact
`d6e2a038c400f196`, its only changes are
\[
\Delta r_{13}=\Delta r_{23}=-\frac1{N^2},\qquad
\Delta r_3=\Delta r_{123}=\frac1{N^2}.
\tag{52}
\]
These changes preserve total middle area. They also preserve every
middle marginal:
\[
\Delta c_1=\Delta r_{13}+\Delta r_{123}=0,
\]
\[
\Delta c_2=\Delta r_{23}+\Delta r_{123}=0,
\]
\[
\Delta c_3
=\Delta r_{13}+\Delta r_{23}
+\Delta r_3+\Delta r_{123}=0.
\]
Finally,
\[
\Delta E_R
=\Delta r_{13}+\Delta r_{23}+2\Delta r_{123}=0.
\tag{53}
\]
Hence the crossings, tails, \(W=0\), totals, and no-half inequalities
verified in `d6e2a038c400f196` are unchanged.

For \(N\geq4\),
\[
0<\frac1{N^2}<e_N,\qquad \frac1{N^2}<\frac{13}{100},
\]
so every displayed cell is positive. The middle Scott inequality in
accepted fact `e51585de76810353` had exact slack \(13/100\); replacing
\(r_{123}=0\) by \(1/N^2\) decreases its left side by only \(1/N^2\).
Thus it remains strict, while the endpoint Scott inequality is
unchanged. All three doubletons and the central cell are now positive,
so the implication in accepted fact `93f1cefaa4795a25` is satisfied.

The normalized limits of (12)-(13) are
\[
\left(\frac{r_1}{N},\frac{r_2}{N},\frac{r_3}{N}\right)
\longrightarrow
\left(\frac5{18},\frac5{18},\frac19\right),
\tag{54}
\]
\[
\left(\frac{r_{12}}N,\frac{r_{13}}N,\frac{r_{23}}N,
\frac{r_{123}}N\right)
\longrightarrow
\left(0,\frac16,\frac16,0\right).
\tag{55}
\]
These already contradict the necessary triangle-fan limits (9)-(10),
and the reflected-support argument excludes the entire mass-growing
sequence independently of the particular limits. This proves every
assertion.
