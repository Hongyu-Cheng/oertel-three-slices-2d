## Statement

Assume the affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted
fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Let \(A_2\) be a convex quadrilateral whose only
positive \(Q\)-cells are
\[
q_{123}=\frac25|A_2|,
\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15|A_2|.
\]
Put
\[
\ell_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad
C_i=A_2\cap\{\ell_i\ge0\},\qquad
\mathcal T_Q=\bigcap_{i=1}^3\{\ell_i\le0\}.
\]
Then, up to relabeling, exactly one of the following holds:

1. Each \(C_i\) strictly contains one distinct vertex, and the fourth vertex
   is strictly contained in no cap.
2. One cap strictly contains two adjacent vertices, and the other two caps
   strictly contain one of the remaining vertices each.

In a full area-one realization of the strict rational table, accepted scale
lemma `b1eaab0ae37367d1` gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}.
\tag{1}
\]
Both incidences are incompatible with this bound. Hence \(A_2\) cannot be a
convex quadrilateral in such a realization.

## Proof

Up to the zero-area cutting lines, \(C_i\) is the union of the exact cells
whose index does not contain \(i\). The only positive such cell is the
doubleton missing \(i\), so
\[
|C_i|=\frac15|A_2|.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every \(C_i\) is positive and proper.

For \(i\ne j\), the relative interiors of \(C_i\) and \(C_j\) are disjoint.
Otherwise their intersection would contain a relatively open planar set on
which \(\ell_i,\ell_j>0\). That set lies in exact cells whose indices omit
both \(i\) and \(j\), namely the empty or remaining-singleton cells, all of
which have area zero. This is impossible.

We now classify the strict vertices without assuming that cutting lines avoid
polygon vertices. Let \(S_i\) be the set of vertices \(v\) of \(A_2\) for
which \(\ell_i(v)>0\). Each \(S_i\) is nonempty: if
\(\ell_i\le0\) at all four vertices, affinity and convexity give
\(\ell_i\le0\) throughout \(A_2\), so \(C_i\) has area zero, contrary to
(2). The sets \(S_i\) are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if a vertex belonged to
\(S_i\cap S_j\), continuity would give a positive-area relative
neighborhood of that vertex contained in the interiors of both caps.

The vertices in each \(S_i\) are consecutive in the cyclic vertex order.
The cutting line cannot contain a polygon edge, since one of its closed
halfplanes would then cut from \(A_2\) either a zero-area face or all of
\(A_2\), contrary to (2). It therefore meets the polygon boundary in at most
two points, and the portion of the boundary strictly on the cap side is one
cyclic interval. In particular, if \(S_i\) has two vertices, they are
adjacent.

The three nonempty, pairwise disjoint sets \(S_1,S_2,S_3\) occupy at most
four vertices. Hence their cardinalities, after relabeling, are either
\[
(1,1,1)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
(2,1,1).
\tag{3}
\]
The first allocation leaves one vertex strictly in no cap and is incidence
1. In the second, the two vertices in the larger set are adjacent, and all
four vertices are assigned, giving incidence 2. Equation (3) also excludes
every cap with three or four strict vertices and every sharing or omitted-cap
allocation.

Vertices lying on one or more cutting lines satisfy \(\ell_i=0\) and do not
belong to the corresponding \(S_i\). Thus such contacts do not create a
third incidence type. They are allowed in incidence 1 by accepted fact
`f64276364335ecd5`; incidence 2, including its possible boundary contacts,
is covered by accepted fact `3396e5df0b84518c`.

Finally, the exact sign-plane map identifies \(\mathcal T_Q\) with the
bounded global central triangle used in all three accepted bounds. In a full
area-one strict-table realization, accepted fact `b1eaab0ae37367d1` gives
(1). In incidence 1,
accepted fact `f64276364335ecd5` gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\le16|A_2|=16<\frac{169}{10},
\]
contradicting (1). In incidence 2, accepted fact `3396e5df0b84518c` gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|<\frac{77}{5}|A_2|
=\frac{77}{5}<\frac{169}{10},
\]
again contradicting (1). Since (3) is exhaustive, no convex
quadrilateral \(A_2\) occurs in the full strict rational table.
