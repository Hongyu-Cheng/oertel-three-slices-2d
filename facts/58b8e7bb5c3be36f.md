## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compatible nonempty two-dimensional convex-polytopal slices. Put
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Assume the full strict residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. Choose \(j\in\{0,2\}\) with \(a_j=M\), and put \(k=2-j\), so \(a_k=m\). Then
\[
0<t<a_1+m.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), let
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}|
\]
and let \(Q_{i,q}:[0,a_i]\to\mathbb R\) be the endpoint-normalized cap quantile
\[
Q_{i,q}(0)=\min_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,\qquad
Q_{i,q}(u)=\inf\{c:F_{i,q}(c)\ge u\}\quad(0<u\le a_i).
\]
Use the exact threshold \(\rho_q(t)\), the actual conventions
\[
D_{1,q}(0)=E_q(0)=-\infty,
\]
and the unique optimizer \(a_*(q)\) in
\[
\rho_q(t)=
\min_{0\le a\le t}
\max\left\{D_{1,q}(a),\frac12E_q(t-a)\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
from accepted facts `17ca54faa05ad878` and `65512ded3a06d971`.

Fix a noncollinear positive-spanning triple
\[
q_1,q_2,q_3\in\mathbb S^1,\qquad
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell=0.
\]
Write
\[
\rho_\ell=\rho_{q_\ell}(t),\qquad
p_\ell=a_*(q_\ell),\qquad
b_\ell=t-p_\ell,\qquad
W=\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_\ell.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(0\le b_\ell\le m\), and
\[
F_{1,q_\ell}(\rho_\ell)=p_\ell
\qquad(\ell=1,2,3).
\tag{4}
\]

For \(0\le b\le m\), define the exact endpoint contact set
\[
\mathcal U_q(b)=
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{0\le u\le b}
\left(
Q_{j,q}(u)+Q_{k,q}(b-u)
\right).
\tag{5}
\]
This set is nonempty and compact. When \(b=0\), (5) is the singleton \(\{0\}\) and records only the finite endpoint extension
\[
Q_{j,q}(0)+Q_{k,q}(0);
\]
it does not replace the actual value \(E_q(0)=-\infty\) in (2). Formula (5) includes \(b=m\), zero-mass splits, and full-cap splits.

If \(W>0\), then
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3p_\ell\ge a_1,
\tag{6}
\]
and, for every choice \(u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\),
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3u_\ell
\le3t-a_1<M,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)<0.
\tag{8}
\]
Equivalently, every violation has the strict larger-slice contact signature
\[
\max_{\substack{
u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\\
\ell=1,2,3}}
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)<0.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently, the following single implication would finish the residual weighted-threshold theorem:
\[
W>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_{\substack{
u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\\
\ell=1,2,3}}
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\ge0.
\tag{CB}
\]
Namely, if (CB) is proved from compatibility and the full strict residual strip for every positive-spanning triple, then \(W\le0\) for every such triple. Accepted facts `9328166a552ec875` and `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` then make this the required bridge ruling out an empty exact middle depth region under a global counterexample.

The two accepted counterexamples calibrate the reduction. The compatible family in accepted fact `2fdbcddaa185b50e` lies outside the residual strip, violates the unconditional weighted-threshold inequality, and has the strict negative contact signature (9); hence (CB) is not an unconditional compatibility law. Accepted fact `74e60bee4b401289` proves that its displayed residual two-routing witness has no common point although the true mixed depth region contains the origin. For that example \(W\le0\), so it does not contradict the conditional implication (CB).

## Proof

Write
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac{a_1}{M}.
\]
The strict residual strip in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` includes
\[
s>1-r.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(a_1+m>M\), so
\[
T=M+(a_1+m)<2(a_1+m).
\]
All areas are positive, and therefore
\[
0<t=\frac{2T}{9}
<\frac49(a_1+m)<a_1+m.
\]
This proves (1) before any use of accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971`, and verifies its range hypothesis.

That accepted fact now gives the feasible allocation interval
\[
L=\max\{0,t-m\},\qquad U=\min\{t,a_1\},
\tag{11}
\]
and proves that \(p_\ell=a_*(q_\ell)\) lies in \([L,U]\). Hence
\[
0\le b_\ell=t-p_\ell\le t-L\le m.
\tag{12}
\]

We next prove (4), including every endpoint convention. Fix \(\ell\), suppress \(q_\ell\), and use the notation of accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971`,
\[
A(a)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(a),\qquad
B(a)=\frac12\overline E_{q_\ell}(t-a),\qquad
\delta(a)=A(a)-B(a).
\]

If the optimizer is \(L\) and \(\delta(L)\ge0\), first suppose \(L=0\). The actual value formula gives
\[
\rho_\ell=B(0)\le A(0)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(0).
\]
The closed cap at or below this lower support endpoint has area zero, so
\[
F_{1,q_\ell}(\rho_\ell)=0=L=p_\ell.
\]
If \(L>0\), the actual value formula gives
\[
\rho_\ell=A(L)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(L),
\]
and strict invertibility of the cap function in accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` gives
\[
F_{1,q_\ell}(\rho_\ell)=L=p_\ell.
\]
This subcase includes the capacity-active endpoint demand \(b_\ell=m\).

If the optimizer is \(U\) and \(\delta(U)\le0\), first suppose \(U=t\). This includes the actual zero endpoint demand \(b_\ell=0\), and the value formula gives
\[
\rho_\ell=A(t)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(t),
\]
so \(F_{1,q_\ell}(\rho_\ell)=t=U=p_\ell\). If \(U<t\), then \(U=a_1\), and
\[
\rho_\ell=B(U)\ge A(U)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(a_1).
\]
Thus the closed middle cap is full and
\[
F_{1,q_\ell}(\rho_\ell)=a_1=U=p_\ell.
\]
This is the middle-capacity endpoint.

In the crossing regime,
\[
\rho_\ell=A(p_\ell)=B(p_\ell)=Q_{1,q_\ell}(p_\ell),
\]
so strict invertibility again gives (4). Coincidences such as \(t=m\) or \(t=a_1\) are already included in these cases. The actual value \(E_q(0)=-\infty\) is used in (2); the finite extension \(\overline E_q(0)\) is used only in the optimizer classification, exactly as in accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971`.

The continuity of the quantiles in accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` makes the maximand in (5) continuous on \([0,b]\), so \(\mathcal U_q(b)\) is nonempty and compact. For \(b>0\), the exact max-plus identity in that accepted fact is
\[
E_q(b)=
\max_{0\le u\le b}
\left(
Q_{j,q}(u)+Q_{k,q}(b-u)
\right).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(b=0\), accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` gives the separate finite extension
\[
\overline E_q(0)=Q_{j,q}(0)+Q_{k,q}(0),
\tag{14}
\]
while the actual optimization convention remains \(E_q(0)=-\infty\). Thus (5) includes all endpoint cases without changing (2).

Assume \(W>0\). For every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), at least one index \(\ell\) satisfies
\[
q_\ell\cdot x<\rho_\ell.
\tag{15}
\]
Otherwise
\[
0=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell\right)\cdot x
\ge\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_\ell=W>0.
\]
Hence the three closed caps
\[
A_1\cap\{x:q_\ell\cdot x\le\rho_\ell\}
\]
cover \(A_1\). Their areas are \(p_\ell\) by (4), and finite subadditivity proves (6).

For arbitrary \(u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\),
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3u_\ell
\le\sum_{\ell=1}^3b_\ell
=3t-\sum_{\ell=1}^3p_\ell
\le3t-a_1.
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{3t-a_1}{M}
=\frac{2+2r-s}{3}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(3t-a_1<M\) is equivalent to \(s>2r-1\). If \(0<r\le1/2\), this follows from \(s>0\). If \(r>1/2\), the residual lower bound in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}>2r-1,
\]
because
\[
\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}-(2r-1)
=\frac{1-r+2\sqrt r}{2}>0.
\]
Together with (16), this proves (7).

Fix any choices \(u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\). If (8) failed, then
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]
The three closed halfplanes
\[
\{x:q_\ell\cdot x\le Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\}
\tag{19}
\]
would cover \(\mathbb R^2\): a point satisfying all three strict reverse inequalities would give
\[
0=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell\right)\cdot x
>
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\ge0.
\]
By endpoint normalization and strict cap inversion in accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878`,
\[
\left|A_j\cap
\{x:q_\ell\cdot x\le Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\}\right|
=u_\ell
\tag{20}
\]
for every \(0\le u_\ell\le a_j\), including zero and full capacity. Intersecting (19) with \(A_j\) and taking areas gives
\[
M=a_j\le\sum_{\ell=1}^3u_\ell,
\]
contrary to (7). This proves (8) for every selection.

The product of the three compact contact sets is compact, and the weighted quantile sum is continuous on it. Its maximum is attained and is strictly negative by (8), proving (9). If (CB) were established, a triple with \(W>0\) would satisfy both (9) and the opposite weak inequality in (CB), which is impossible. Hence (CB) would imply \(W\le0\) for every positive-spanning triple.

We verify the range needed to connect this conclusion to the exact profile intersection. From (10), \(a_1+m>M\), so \(M<T/2\); also \(m\le M<T/2\). Therefore
\[
a_0+a_1=T-a_2>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9}=t,
\qquad
a_1+a_2=T-a_0>\frac T2>t.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the range hypothesis of accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` holds throughout the residual strip. That accepted fact shows that a global failure produces a noncollinear positive-spanning triple with \(W>0\). Accepted fact `9328166a552ec875` identifies the reverse inequalities \(W\le0\) as the required numerical cross-direction balance for the exact profile halfplanes. Hence (CB) would finish the registered residual bridge. This conclusion remains conditional on (CB).

For the first calibration, accepted fact `2fdbcddaa185b50e` verifies compatibility and
\[
0<t<a_1+m
\]
for its family, so accepted facts `17ca54faa05ad878` and `65512ded3a06d971` apply. With \(D=1/2\), its areas are
\[
M=D(1+2\varepsilon^2)^2,\qquad
a_1=D,\qquad
T=D(2+5\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon^4).
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
M-(3t-a_1)
&=D\left(
\frac23+\frac23\varepsilon^2+\frac43\varepsilon^4
\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
That accepted fact gives a positive-spanning triple with \(W>1\). The proof of (4), (6), and (8) uses the residual strip only to establish the range (1) and \(3t-a_1<M\); the accepted range check and (22) supply those two inputs directly. Therefore its exact contact sets have the strict negative signature (9). Since this family is outside the residual strip, it proves only that (CB) cannot be an unconditional compatibility law.

For the second calibration, accepted fact `74e60bee4b401289` places its areas in the strict residual strip. Therefore the argument proving (21) verifies explicitly that
\[
0<t<\min\{a_0+a_1,a_1+a_2\},
\]
so accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` applies. Accepted fact `74e60bee4b401289` proves
\[
h_S((1,0))>t
\]
and proves that its displayed two-routing witness has no common point. The exact profile identity in accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` gives
\[
0=q\cdot0\ge\rho_q(t)
\qquad(q\in\mathbb S^1).
\]
Thus every positive-spanning triple in that example has \(W\le0\), so the antecedent of (CB) is false and the example does not contradict (CB). Its separate proved conclusion is precisely the failure of the displayed two-routing witness despite the true depth point.
