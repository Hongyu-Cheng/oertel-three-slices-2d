## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system in the common orientation and normalization of accepted facts 6600e139055be506, 77db94881d26f221, and 48d8328cbba82dba. Thus there are nondegenerate triangles \(P_0,P_1,P_2\) in parallel planes with
\[
|P_0|=A,\qquad |P_1|=1,\qquad |P_2|=B,\qquad
0<A<B<1+A,\qquad
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,
\]
and
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\in\left(\frac12,1\right),\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49,\qquad
\beta=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9<\frac{4A}{9}.
\]
For each coordinate \(i\), let \(u_i,c_i,v_i\) be the normalized closed nonnegative-coordinate cap fractions of \(P_0,P_1,P_2\), respectively, and assume the literal inequalities
\[
Au_i+c_i+Bv_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
Let the columns of \(L=(\ell_{ij})\) be the vertices of \(P_1\), and put
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_{ij}=1\quad(j=1,2,3),\qquad
\det L\ne0,\qquad
s_i:=\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_{ij}.
\tag{2}
\]
After a simultaneous coordinate relabeling, the incidence pair and exact mean conditions are one of
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\mathcal O_{11}&(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2\})&s_2>3,\ s_1,s_3<0,\\
\mathcal O_{12}&(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2,3\})&s_1<0,\ s_2,s_3\ge0,\ s_1+s_2+s_3=3,\\
\mathcal O_{21}&(I_0,I_1)=(\{1,2\},\{3\})&s_3>3,\ s_1,s_2<0.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
Every \(i\in I_1\) has three nonzero entries, exactly one of them positive, and
\[
\frac49\le c_i<\frac12.
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose first that all nine entries of \(L\) are nonzero, and define
\[
J_i:=\{j\in\{1,2,3\}:\ell_{ij}>0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The allowed symmetry is one common permutation of the three columns together with a row permutation preserving the canonical pair \((I_0,I_1)\). The row stabilizer is trivial in \(\mathcal O_{11}\), is generated by \(2\leftrightarrow3\) in \(\mathcal O_{12}\), and is generated by \(1\leftrightarrow2\) in \(\mathcal O_{21}\). There are no independent rowwise column permutations. Under exactly this group, every remaining support orbit occurs exactly once in the following tables.

For \(\mathcal O_{11}\), normalize the unique positive column of row \(2\) to \(J_2=\{1\}\). The 12 representatives \((J_1,J_2,J_3)\) are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
(\varnothing,\{1\},\{2,3\})&(\{1\},\{1\},\{2,3\})&(\{2\},\{1\},\{3\})\\
(\{2\},\{1\},\{1,3\})&(\{2\},\{1\},\{2,3\})&(\{1,2\},\{1\},\{3\})\\
(\{1,2\},\{1\},\{2,3\})&(\{2,3\},\{1\},\varnothing)&(\{2,3\},\{1\},\{1\})\\
(\{2,3\},\{1\},\{2\})&(\{2,3\},\{1\},\{1,2\})&(\{2,3\},\{1\},\{2,3\}).
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]

For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), the three representatives are
\[
(\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}),\qquad
(\{1,2\},\{1\},\{3\}),\qquad
(\{1,2\},\{3\},\{3\}).
\tag{7}
\]

For \(\mathcal O_{21}\), normalize the unique positive column of row \(3\) to \(J_3=\{1\}\). The seven representatives are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
(\varnothing,\{2,3\},\{1\})&(\{1\},\{2,3\},\{1\})&(\{2\},\{3\},\{1\})\\
(\{2\},\{1,3\},\{1\})&(\{2\},\{2,3\},\{1\})&(\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{1\})\\
(\{2,3\},\{2,3\},\{1\})&&.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the exact residual all-nine-nonzero orbit counts in \(\mathcal O_{11},\mathcal O_{12},\mathcal O_{21}\) are respectively
\[
12,\qquad 3,\qquad 7,
\tag{9}
\]
for a total of 22.

These are support indices, not assertions of endpoint-compatible feasibility. On every listed index an actual system must still satisfy the common column equations and determinant condition (2), the mean conditions (3), midpoint compatibility, the literal inequalities (1), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
i\in I_0&:\quad c_i+Bv_i<\kappa,\quad c_i<\kappa,\\
i\in I_1&:\quad Au_i+Bv_i<t-c_i\le\beta,\quad \frac49\le c_i<\frac12,\\
i\notin I_0\cup I_1&:\quad c_i<t,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge1.
\tag{10}
\]
The continuous middle-cap equations are retained. An all-nonzero one-positive row, in its actual common column positions, has the form \((p,-a,-b)\), with \(p,a,b>0\), and
\[
c=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}.
\tag{11}
\]
An all-nonzero two-positive row has the form \((-d,x,y)\), with \(d,x,y>0\), and
\[
c=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)},
\tag{12}
\]
while an all-negative row has \(c=0\). Every \(I_1\)-row is of type (11) with \(p\ge a+b\); in \(\mathcal O_{11}\) and \(\mathcal O_{21}\), its strict mean condition is \(p-a-b>3\). In \(\mathcal O_{12}\), the two \(I_1\)-row sums are nonnegative and cannot both vanish; if one is zero, the other is \(>3\). All exact equalities among row values and all restrictions obtained by substituting (11)-(12) into (1) and (10) remain part of the indexed continuous system.

Now allow at least one zero entry and define the weak-sign data
\[
P_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}>0\},\qquad
N_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}<0\},\qquad
Z_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}=0\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The corresponding necessary zero-entry boundary catalog is the following finite collection, modulo the same common column group and the same orbitwise row stabilizer as above. Each \((P_i,N_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^3\) partitions the three columns rowwise, satisfies
\[
N_i\ne\varnothing\quad(i=1,2,3),\qquad
P_1\cup P_2\cup P_3=\{1,2,3\},
\tag{14}
\]
and has, for every \(h\in I_1\),
\[
Z_h=\varnothing,\qquad |P_h|=1,\qquad
N_h=\{1,2,3\}\setminus P_h.
\tag{15}
\]
The zero positions are therefore exactly confined as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\mathcal O_{11}&Z_2=\varnothing,\quad Z_1\cup Z_3\ne\varnothing,\\
\mathcal O_{12}&Z_2=Z_3=\varnothing,\quad Z_1\ne\varnothing,\\
\mathcal O_{21}&Z_3=\varnothing,\quad Z_1\cup Z_2\ne\varnothing.
\end{array}
\tag{16}
\]
For either singleton-\(I_1\) orbit, if \(h\) is the \(I_1\)-row and \(a,b\) are the other rows, the catalog retains only the necessary tests
\[
\{1,2,3\}\setminus P_h\subseteq P_a\cup P_b,\qquad
P_h\cap(N_a\cup N_b)\ne\varnothing.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), if \(s_e=0\) and \(\{e,h\}=\{2,3\}\), the catalog additionally requires
\[
s_h>3,\qquad P_h\cap(N_1\cup N_e)\ne\varnothing.
\tag{18}
\]
On every boundary index, (1)-(4), (10), midpoint compatibility, the column sums, and \(\det L\ne0\) remain explicit continuous requirements. Formula (11) applies to every \(I_1\)-row. If \(i\notin I_1\), \(Z_i\ne\varnothing\), and \(P_i=\varnothing\), then \(c_i=0\). If \(i\notin I_1\), \(Z_i\ne\varnothing\), and \(P_i\ne\varnothing\), then (14) forces \(|P_i|=|N_i|=|Z_i|=1\); writing its nonzero values as \(p,-a\), with \(p,a>0\), gives
\[
c_i=\frac{p}{p+a}.
\tag{19}
\]
Rows outside \(I_1\) with \(Z_i=\varnothing\) retain (11), (12), or \(c_i=0\), according to their sign support. This catalog is only a necessary finite over-index: an indexed weak-sign stratum may be empty, may force singularity before the explicit determinant condition, or may fail the cap, mean, literal row-mass, or endpoint-compatibility conditions. No boundary index is asserted to be feasible or sufficient.

## Proof

Accepted fact 77db94881d26f221 excludes a two-positive \(I_1\)-row without any hypothesis on the other two rows. Accepted fact 48d8328cbba82dba proves, including every equality and zero-level case, that each \(I_1\)-row is all-nonzero, has exactly one positive entry, and satisfies (4). Thus in the all-nine-nonzero catalog of accepted fact 6600e139055be506, the invariant condition to impose is precisely
\[
|J_i|=1\qquad(i\in I_1).
\tag{20}
\]

In \(\mathcal O_{11}\), the exact representatives of 6600e139055be506 were already separated into the cases \(J_2=\{1\}\) and \(J_2=\{1,2\}\), after normalization by a common column permutation. Condition (20) retains exactly the 12 representatives in the first case, which are (6), and discards all 19 in the second. Because support cardinality is invariant under the allowed group, filtering an exhaustive duplicate-free orbit list this way remains exhaustive and duplicate-free.

The identical argument applies to \(\mathcal O_{21}\). Its accepted catalog was separated into \(J_3=\{1\}\) and \(J_3=\{1,2\}\); (20) retains exactly the seven representatives in the first case, namely (8), and discards all 11 in the second. The additional row swap \(1\leftrightarrow2\) is exactly the stabilizer already used in that accepted quotient.

For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), both \(J_2\) and \(J_3\) are singletons. The column-cover condition from 6600e139055be506 rules out \(J_1=\varnothing\). If \(|J_1|=1\), the three singleton supports must be pairwise distinct, and common column permutations together with the swap \(2\leftrightarrow3\) give the first representative in (7). If \(|J_1|=2\), normalize \(J_1=\{1,2\}\). At least one of \(J_2,J_3\) must then equal \(\{3\}\). Up to the row swap and the residual common-column swap \(1\leftrightarrow2\), either the other singleton lies in \(J_1\), giving the second representative in (7), or it also equals \(\{3\}\), giving the third. These three cases are distinct because they have, respectively,
\[
(|J_1|,\ |J_2\cup J_3|)=(1,2),\quad(2,2),\quad(2,1).
\]
This proves (7) and the counts (9). The cap formulas, mean conditions, and all continuous restrictions (1)-(4) and (10)-(12) are exactly the conditions retained by 6600e139055be506, strengthened on \(I_1\) by 77db94881d26f221 and 48d8328cbba82dba; none follows from the support index alone. Hence the enumeration makes no endpoint-feasibility claim.

Finally, let an actual surviving system have a zero entry. Accepted fact 6600e139055be506 gives the weak-sign partition, column cover, the necessity of a negative entry in every row, the singleton-\(I_1\) tests (17), the \(\mathcal O_{12}\) mean-boundary test (18), and the exact zero-entry cap formula (19). Accepted fact 48d8328cbba82dba further gives (15). Since at least one zero exists but none can occur in an \(I_1\)-row, the three alternatives in (16) follow immediately from the three incidence pairs. Thus every actual zero-entry system determines an index in (13)-(18) and satisfies the stated continuous equations and inequalities. No converse was used or proved, so the boundary collection has exactly the claimed necessary-over-index status.
