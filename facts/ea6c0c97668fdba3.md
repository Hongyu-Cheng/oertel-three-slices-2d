## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional compact convex bodies satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(s_1+s_2+s_3>1\), and define
\[
x_j=\left|A_0\cap\{f_j-s_j\leq0\}\right|,\qquad
b_j=\left|A_1\cap\{f_j\leq0\}\right|,\qquad
y_j=\left|A_2\cap\{f_j+s_j\leq0\}\right|.
\]
If
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3x_j=|A_0|,\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^3b_j=|A_1|,\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^3y_j=0,
\]
then there is \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\) such that
\[
\left|A_0\setminus\{f_r-s_r\leq0\}\right|=0,\qquad
\left|A_1\setminus\{f_r\leq0\}\right|=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
x_r+b_r+y_r=|A_0|+|A_1|,
\]
and in particular the \(r\)-th row has mass at least \(|A_0|+|A_1|\).

## Proof

Write
\[
g_j=f_j-s_j,\qquad h_j=f_j+s_j,
\]
and let
\[
N_0(x)=\sum_{j=1}^3\mathbf 1_{\{g_j(x)\leq0\}},\qquad
N_1(w)=\sum_{j=1}^3\mathbf 1_{\{f_j(w)\leq0\}}.
\]
Since
\[
\sum_jg_j=-1-\sum_js_j<0,\qquad \sum_jf_j=-1<0,
\]
at every point of the plane at least one corresponding affine function is strictly negative. Thus \(N_0\geq1\) on \(A_0\) and \(N_1\geq1\) on \(A_1\). Tonelli and the two zero-surplus identities give
\[
\int_{A_0}N_0=\sum_jx_j=|A_0|,\qquad
\int_{A_1}N_1=\sum_jb_j=|A_1|.
\]
It follows that
\[
N_0=1\quad\hbox{almost everywhere on }A_0,\qquad
N_1=1\quad\hbox{almost everywhere on }A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Every set \(\{g_j=0\}\) and \(\{f_j=0\}\) is a line because the functions are nonconstant, hence is planar null. After deleting these six lines and the exceptional sets in (1), there are full-measure sets \(G_0\subseteq A_0\) and \(G_1\subseteq A_1\) with literal unique labels
\[
\ell_0(x)=j\iff g_j(x)<0,\qquad
\ell_1(w)=j\iff f_j(w)<0.
\tag{2}
\]
For the other two indices the relevant affine functions are strictly positive.

The top identity is also literal. Since every \(y_j\geq0\), it gives \(y_j=0\) for every \(j\). In fact
\[
h_j\geq0\quad\hbox{on all of }A_2.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if \(h_j(z)<0\) at some \(z\in A_2\), choose \(c\in\operatorname{int}A_2\). Points on the open segment from \(z\) to \(c\) lie in \(\operatorname{int}A_2\), and points sufficiently close to \(z\) still satisfy \(h_j<0\). One of them therefore has a small Euclidean ball contained in \(A_2\cap\{h_j<0\}\), contradicting \(y_j=0\). Equality in (3) is allowed: since \(\{h_j=0\}\) is a line, it is a null support contact. Hence
\[
h_j(z)>0\quad\hbox{for almost every }z\in A_2,\quad j=1,2,3.
\tag{4}
\]

We now make the same-index midpoint implication precise. This is the graph inclusion recorded in accepted fact `a6fbd3f54d7ec4c7` and used in the exact defect split of accepted fact `3081dc9f6b1f023f`; here it follows directly from affinity, including the boundary orientation:
\[
2f_j\!\left(\frac{x+z}{2}\right)=g_j(x)+h_j(z).
\tag{5}
\]
The set of pairs \((x,z)\in A_0\times A_2\) for which \((x+z)/2\notin G_1\) is null. Indeed, for every fixed \(z\), its \(x\)-section is contained in
\[
2(A_1\setminus G_1)-z,
\]
which is planar null, and Fubini applies. The pairs with \(x\notin G_0\), or with (4) failing for some \(j\), are null as well. For every remaining pair put \(j=\ell_1((x+z)/2)\). Then the left side of (5) is strictly negative while \(h_j(z)>0\), so \(g_j(x)<0\). By uniqueness in (2),
\[
\ell_1\!\left(\frac{x+z}{2}\right)=\ell_0(x)
\quad\hbox{for almost every }(x,z)\in A_0\times A_2.
\tag{6}
\]

Fubini supplies a full-measure set \(X\subseteq G_0\cap\operatorname{int}A_0\) such that, for each \(x\in X\), (6) holds for almost every \(z\in A_2\). Here \(\operatorname{int}A_0\) has full measure in \(A_0\): if \(c\in\operatorname{int}A_0\), then
\[
c+t(A_0-c)\subseteq\operatorname{int}A_0\qquad(0<t<1)
\]
and this set has area \(t^2|A_0|\); letting \(t\uparrow1\) proves the claim. The same observation will apply to \(A_1\).

For \(x\in X\), set
\[
D_x=\frac{x+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
The homothety \(z\mapsto(x+z)/2\) sends null sets to null sets, so (6) says that almost every point of \(D_x\) lies in \(G_1\) and has middle label \(\ell_0(x)\).

Because \(A_2\) is two-dimensional, choose a closed ball \(\overline B(c,\rho)\subseteq A_2\) with \(\rho>0\). Then
\[
\overline B\!\left(\frac{x+c}{2},\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\subseteq D_x.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(x,x'\in X\) satisfy \(|x-x'|<2\rho\), the two balls in (7) have centers at distance \(<\rho\), strictly less than the sum of their radii. Their intersection contains a nonempty open set, so \(|D_x\cap D_{x'}|>0\). If \(\ell_0(x)\ne\ell_0(x')\), deleting the two null exceptional subsets of \(D_x\) and \(D_{x'}\) would leave a point of this positive-area intersection with two different unique \(G_1\)-labels, impossible. Therefore
\[
x,x'\in X,\quad |x-x'|<2\rho
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell_0(x)=\ell_0(x').
\tag{8}
\]

No connectedness of the measurable label cells is being assumed. Instead, take arbitrary \(u,v\in X\). The segment \([u,v]\) is a compact subset of the convex open set \(\operatorname{int}A_0\). Choose an integer \(n\) such that \(|u-v|/n<2\rho\), and put \(p_k=(1-k/n)u+(k/n)v\). Since there are finitely many \(p_k\), one can choose \(\varepsilon>0\) such that every \(B(p_k,\varepsilon)\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}A_0\) and
\[
\frac{|u-v|}{n}+2\varepsilon<2\rho.
\]
The complement of \(X\) is null, so every one of these positive-area balls meets \(X\). Choose \(u_k\in X\cap B(p_k,\varepsilon)\), with \(u_0=u\) and \(u_n=v\). Consecutive choices have distance \(<2\rho\); applying (8) along the finite chain gives \(\ell_0(u)=\ell_0(v)\). Thus one index \(r\) labels all of \(X\), and since \(X\) has full measure in \(A_0\),
\[
\left|A_0\setminus\{g_r\leq0\}\right|=0,\qquad x_r=|A_0|.
\tag{9}
\]

It remains to show that the same label occupies all of \(A_1\), rather than only the midpoint translates. First, the zero middle surplus forces a single affine label on the whole interior of \(A_1\). Suppose that \(\{f_i=0\}\) met \(\operatorname{int}A_1\) at \(p\). Since \(\sum_jf_j(p)=-1\), some \(k\ne i\) has \(f_k(p)<0\). In a sufficiently small ball about \(p\) contained in \(\operatorname{int}A_1\), one still has \(f_k<0\). Because \(f_i\) is nonconstant and vanishes at the center, the part of that ball where \(f_i<0\) is a nonempty open half-ball. It has positive area and satisfies \(N_1\geq2\), contradicting (1). Hence none of the three cutting lines meets \(\operatorname{int}A_1\).

The set \(\operatorname{int}A_1\) is convex and therefore connected. Each \(f_j\) is nonzero there, so each has constant sign there. At least one sign is negative because their sum is \(-1\), and at most one is negative by (1). Consequently there is an index \(r_1\) such that almost every point of \(A_1\) has unique label \(r_1\), and
\[
b_{r_1}=|A_1|.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose any \(x\in X\). The set \(D_x\) has area \(|A_2|/4>0\). By (6), almost every point of \(D_x\) has label \(r\), while by (10) almost every point of \(D_x\subseteq A_1\) has label \(r_1\). The unique-label cells are disjoint, so \(r_1=r\). Combining (9) and (10) proves both asserted collapses. Finally \(y_r=0\), and hence
\[
x_r+b_r+y_r=|A_0|+|A_1|.
\]
