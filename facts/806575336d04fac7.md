## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system, residual-area regime, and centroid-incidence orbit common to accepted facts `27ae487131bcd122`, `31dfbaba9be6f34d`, `6986d9aa136ba907`, `89f1aef98e9b8915`, and `cc32eb5039621c6b`. Thus
\[
0<A<B,\qquad B<1+A,\qquad t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\leq\frac12,
\]
the endpoint and middle matrices and the shift satisfy the compatibility, column-sum, nonsingularity, and literal-cap hypotheses of those facts, all three literal row masses are strictly below \(t\), and the fixed incidence orbit has
\[
I_0=\{1,2\},\qquad I_1=\{3\}.
\]
When all nine entries of the middle matrix \(L\) are nonzero, use the one fixed allowed interchange of rows \(1,2\) and columns \(2,3\) supplied by accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915` to write its contained-support chamber as
\[
J_1=\{2\},\qquad J_2=\{2,3\},\qquad J_3=\{1\}.
\]
Then no strict system exists. This exclusion includes every admissible \(0<A<B\), every all-nine-nonzero inverse-capacity switch branch, \(B=1\), and every stratum on which one or more entries of \(L\) vanish.

No conclusion is asserted for any other centroid-incidence orbit or any other residual-area regime.

## Proof

First suppose that all nine entries of \(L\) are nonzero and put the system in the displayed contained-support representative using the fixed allowed relabeling from accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915`.

There are two exhaustive possibilities for \(B\). If \(B\leq1\), all hypotheses of the open-chamber part of accepted fact `27ae487131bcd122` hold, so that fact excludes the strict system. Its weak hypothesis includes \(B=1\) literally. It makes no endpoint projection-ordering or distinctness assumption, so repeated values, constant projections, and threshold equalities do not leave this case.

It remains to consider \(B>1\). Since \(A>0\), this gives \(1-A<B\); together with the assumed \(B<1+A\), these are exactly the residual inequalities used by accepted fact `31dfbaba9be6f34d`. In the canonical notation shared by accepted facts `31dfbaba9be6f34d` and `cc32eb5039621c6b`, write
\[
L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),\qquad d=\max\{a,b\},\qquad e=\min\{a,b\},
\]
\[
C=c=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)},\qquad \eta=t-C,
\]
and
\[
h=\frac{d-e}{p+d},\qquad \mu=\mu_\eta=A-\sqrt{A\eta},\qquad \nu=\nu_\eta=B-\sqrt{B\eta}.
\]
Let
\[
x=1-\sum_i\min_jU_{ij}\in[\sqrt A,2-\sqrt B]
\]
be the actual row-minimum normalization, and set
\[
r_A=\frac{\mu}{x^2},\qquad r_B=\frac{\nu}{(2-x)^2}.
\]
These are the two actual inverse-capacity arguments. Their denominators are nonzero because \(x\geq\sqrt A>0\) and, as \(B>1\), \(x\leq2-\sqrt B<1\).

The following weak-inequality cover is exhaustive:
\[
\bigl(r_A\geq h\ \text{and}\ r_B\geq h\bigr)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\bigl(r_A\leq h\ \text{or}\ r_B\leq h\bigr).
\]
Indeed, if the first alternative fails, then \(r_A<h\) or \(r_B<h\), which implies the second. The two alternatives may overlap at a switch equality, so no equality is omitted.

In the first alternative, accepted fact `cc32eb5039621c6b` applies to the actual \(x\). It gives
\[
A\mathcal C(L_{3*}U;-\sigma_3)+B\mathcal C(L_{3*}V;\sigma_3)\geq\eta.
\]
Adding the middle contribution \(C=t-\eta\) makes the third literal row mass at least \(t\), contradicting its strict inequality.

In the second alternative, accepted fact `31dfbaba9be6f34d` applies to the same actual \(x\) and gives \(G_\eta(x)>0\). Its accepted per-instance inverse-capacity implication says that a third endpoint contribution strictly below \(\eta\) would force \(G_\eta(x)<0\). Hence the third endpoint contribution is at least \(\eta\), and the third literal row mass is again at least \(C+\eta=t\), a contradiction.

This cover includes all switch boundaries. If both arguments equal \(h\), or one equals \(h\) while the other is above it, the first alternative applies. If one equals \(h\) while the other is below it, the second alternative applies. It also includes \(d=e\): then \(h=0\), and the same order-theoretic dichotomy applies without dividing by \(d-e\). Accepted fact `cc32eb5039621c6b` explicitly includes \(d=e\) and equality in either linear switch condition, while accepted fact `31dfbaba9be6f34d` uses weak quadratic switch inequalities. Thus no all-nine-nonzero capacity branch is omitted. The two accepted facts also quantify over both determinant signs, endpoint cap-order boundaries, row-minimum attainment patterns, and the shift, so no additional cell assumption is made.

Therefore every all-nine-nonzero strict system in the contained-support chamber is excluded, for both \(B\leq1\) and \(B>1\).

Now suppose that the middle matrix has an arbitrary nonempty zero set, with no restriction on the number or locations of its zero entries. Apply accepted fact `6986d9aa136ba907` directly to this boundary system. It keeps \(U,V,A,B,t\), compatibility, the strict literal row inequalities, and the entire centroid-incidence orbit fixed, and produces arbitrarily close all-nine-nonzero strict systems. A tail of those systems lies, after one fixed allowed relabeling, in the displayed contained-support open chamber. Every member of that tail is impossible by the open-chamber exclusion just proved. This contradiction excludes the original boundary system.

This perturbation step neither assigns an inverse-capacity branch to the boundary system nor assumes that a branch is preserved at the boundary. For each perturbed all-nine-nonzero system, the completed open-chamber argument uses its actual inverse-capacity arguments and the exhaustive weak-inequality cover above. Thus accepted fact `6986d9aa136ba907` transfers the branch-complete open-chamber exclusion to every zero-entry stratum without any boundary branch hypothesis.

The argument uses accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915` only inside the stated incidence orbit and uses only the stated residual hypotheses. It therefore proves no exclusion for a different centroid-incidence orbit and no exclusion for a different residual-area regime.
