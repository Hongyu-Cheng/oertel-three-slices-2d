## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a compact polytope whose integer-height sections are exactly
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
where each \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a nonempty two-dimensional convex polytope. Write
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\]
and assume
\[
a_i<\frac T2\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\]
Let \(c_i\) be the centroid of \(A_i\), and define
\[
z_0=\frac{c_0+c_2}{2},\qquad
\delta=c_1-z_0,\qquad
D=\|\delta\|,\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
Let
\[
r_1=\max\{r\ge0:c_1+r\overline B_2\subseteq A_1\}
\]
be the radius of the largest Euclidean disk centered at \(c_1\) and contained in \(A_1\). Then \(r_1>0\) and \(z_0\in A_1\).

If \(D\le r_1\), then
\[
h_S((1,z_0))
\ge
\min\left\{
T-a_0,\,
T-a_2,\,
\max\left\{
\frac{(\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2})^2+4m}{9},\,
\frac49\left(m+a_1\left(1-\frac D{r_1}\right)^2\right)
\right\}
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
0<
r_1\left(
1-\sqrt{\frac{T/2-m}{a_1}}
\right)
<r_1.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, the explicit hypothesis
\[
\|\delta\|
\le
r_1\left(
1-\sqrt{\frac{T/2-\min\{a_0,a_2\}}{a_1}}
\right)
\tag{3}
\]
implies
\[
h_S((1,z_0))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Because \(A_1\) is full-dimensional, its centroid \(c_1\) lies in its interior. Indeed, if \(c_1\in\partial A_1\), a supporting line would give a nonzero \(q\) such that
\[
\langle q,w-c_1\rangle\le0\qquad(w\in A_1),
\]
with strict inequality on a positive-area subset. Integrating would then give
\[
0=\int_{A_1}\langle q,w-c_1\rangle\,dw<0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence \(r_1>0\). Accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` gives \(z_0\in A_1\).

By accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, every non-slice-parallel halfspace through \((1,z_0)\) has, for some \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), total cap area
\[
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
A_i\cap
\left\{
w:\langle q,w-z_0\rangle\le\lambda(1-i)
\right\}
\right|.
\tag{4}
\]
Accepted fact `9fa81a67fd2eaafc` bounds (4) below by
\[
\frac{(\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2})^2+4m}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]

We derive a second bound involving \(D/r_1\). The middle cap in (4) is
\[
A_1\cap\{w:\langle q,w-z_0\rangle\le0\}.
\]
Set \(s=\langle q,\delta\rangle\). Since \(z_0=c_1-\delta\), this cap equals
\[
A_1\cap\{w:\langle q,w-c_1\rangle\le-s\}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(s\le0\), (6) contains \(c_1\), so the planar Grünbaum inequality gives it area at least \(4a_1/9\).

Suppose \(s>0\). Then \(s\le D\le r_1\). Let
\[
B=A_1\cap\{w:\langle q,w-c_1\rangle\le0\},
\qquad
p=c_1-r_1q,
\qquad
\theta=\frac{s}{r_1}.
\]
The definition of \(r_1\) gives \(p\in A_1\). Convexity gives
\[
(1-\theta)B+\theta p\subseteq A_1.
\]
For every \(w\in B\),
\[
\left\langle q,(1-\theta)w+\theta p-c_1\right\rangle
=
(1-\theta)\langle q,w-c_1\rangle-\theta r_1
\le-s.
\]
Thus
\[
(1-\theta)B+\theta p
\subseteq
A_1\cap\{w:\langle q,w-c_1\rangle\le-s\}.
\]
Area scales by \((1-\theta)^2\), while Grünbaum gives \(|B|\ge4a_1/9\). Therefore (6) has area at least
\[
\left(1-\frac{s}{r_1}\right)^2\frac{4a_1}{9}
\ge
\left(1-\frac D{r_1}\right)^2\frac{4a_1}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]
The same bound is valid when \(s\le0\).

For the outer slices, set
\[
d=\langle q,c_0-z_0\rangle
=\frac12\langle q,c_0-c_2\rangle.
\]
The slice-\(0\) cap contains \(c_0\) whenever \(\lambda\ge d\). Since
\[
\langle q,c_2-z_0\rangle=-d,
\]
the slice-\(2\) cap contains \(c_2\) whenever \(\lambda\le d\). At least one condition always holds. Applying Grünbaum to that outer slice shows that the two outer caps together contribute at least
\[
\frac49m.
\tag{8}
\]
Adding (7) and (8), every cap (4) has area at least
\[
\frac49\left(
m+a_1\left(1-\frac D{r_1}\right)^2
\right).
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (5) and (9), then using the exact minimum formula in accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, whose two slice-parallel terms at height \(1\) are \(T-a_2\) and \(T-a_0\), proves (1).

It remains to verify the tolerance. Let \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\). The balanced assumption \(M<T/2\) is equivalent to
\[
M<a_1+m.
\]
Consequently,
\[
0<
\frac{T/2-m}{a_1}
=
\frac{a_1+M-m}{2a_1}
<1,
\]
which proves (2). Under (3),
\[
1-\frac D{r_1}
\ge
\sqrt{\frac{T/2-m}{a_1}},
\]
and hence (9) is at least
\[
\frac49\left(m+T/2-m\right)=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Finally, \(T-a_0>T/2\) and \(T-a_2>T/2\), so both slice-parallel terms also exceed \(2T/9\). Formula (1) therefore yields
\[
h_S((1,z_0))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, Theorem 2 and the centroid statement proved there, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257.

The exact result used is: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is a convex body with centroid \(c_K\), then every closed halfspace containing \(c_K\) intersects \(K\) in volume at least
\[
\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n|K|.
\]
For \(n=2\), this is \(4|K|/9\). Each application above is to a two-dimensional convex polytope and a closed planar halfspace containing its centroid.
