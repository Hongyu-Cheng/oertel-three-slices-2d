## Statement

Let \(\rho,\sigma\ge1\), with \((\rho,\sigma)\ne(1,1)\), and let \(0<a,b<1\). Suppose
\[
a(2\sigma-1)\le\sigma,\qquad b(2\rho-1)\le\rho.
\]
For the triangle and halfplanes
\[
\begin{aligned}
K&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,1),(-\rho,1-\rho),(\sigma,1-\sigma)\},\\
Q_L&=\{(x,y):(1-a)x+ay\le0\},\\
Q_R&=\{(x,y):(1-b)x-by\ge0\},
\end{aligned}
\]
put \(C_L=|K\cap Q_L|\), \(C_R=|K\cap Q_R|\), \(s_3=|K\setminus(Q_L\cup Q_R)|\), and \(D_3=|K\cap Q_L\cap Q_R|\). The areas \(C_L,C_R,s_3,D_3\) are the quantities called \(C_1,C_2,S,D\), respectively, in the registered subgoal.

If \(C_L\ge C_R\ge1\) and \(C_L+C_R<\rho\sigma\), then \(D_3\ge s_3^2\), and therefore \(s_3^2\le C_LD_3\).

More explicitly, with
\[
\begin{aligned}
H&=2\rho\sigma-\rho-\sigma,\\
X&=\rho+\sigma-2\sigma a,\qquad
Y=\rho+\sigma-2\rho b,
\end{aligned}
\]
the geometric areas are
\[
C_L=\frac{\sigma(\rho-a)^2}{X},\qquad
C_R=\frac{\rho(\sigma-b)^2}{Y},\qquad
s_3=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad
D_3=\frac{H^2(a+b-2ab)}{2XY}.
\]
All three quantities \(H,X,Y\) are positive. Thus the conclusion also proves the corresponding rational implication with these explicit formulas.

## Proof

We first establish the formulas and all geometric restrictions directly. Write \(P_0=(0,1)\), \(P_-=(-\rho,1-\rho)\), and \(P_+=(\sigma,1-\sigma)\). The determinant of \(P_--P_0=(-\rho,-\rho)\) and \(P_+-P_0=(\sigma,-\sigma)\) gives \(|K|=\rho\sigma\). The part of \(K\) in \(y\ge0\) is the triangle with vertices \((-1,0),(1,0),(0,1)\), so its area is one and its chord in \(y=0\) is bisected by the origin.

The base line has equation \((\sigma-\rho)x+(\rho+\sigma)y=-H\). Also
\(H=\rho(\sigma-1)+\sigma(\rho-1)>0\), so the origin is interior to \(K\). The upper intersections of the two cutting lines with the sides through \(P_0\) are
\[
U_-=(-a,1-a),\qquad U_+=(b,1-b).
\]
The defining form of \(Q_L\) is negative at \(P_-\), positive at \(P_0\), and has value \(\sigma+a-2\sigma a\) at \(P_+\). Consequently its lower ray meets the base exactly when \(a(2\sigma-1)\le\sigma\). This includes a hit at \(P_+\). The reflected argument gives the exact condition \(b(2\rho-1)\le\rho\) for the other lower ray.

Under these conditions
\[
X=(\rho-a)+(\sigma+a-2\sigma a)>0,\qquad
Y=(\sigma-b)+(\rho+b-2\rho b)>0.
\]
Solving the two line equations with the base gives the lower intersections
\[
V_+=\left(\frac{aH}{X},-\frac{(1-a)H}{X}\right),
\qquad
V_-=\left(-\frac{bH}{Y},-\frac{(1-b)H}{Y}\right).
\]
The left cap is the vertex triangle at \(P_-\). Its two edge-intercept fractions are \((\rho-a)/\rho\) and \((\rho-a)/X\); multiplying them by \(|K|=\rho\sigma\) gives the formula for \(C_L\). The reflected computation gives \(C_R\). Up to null cutting lines, the upper singleton is the quadrilateral with cyclic vertices \(0,U_+,P_0,U_-\). Its area is \((a+b)/2\). The lower doubleton is the triangle with vertices \(0,V_-,V_+\), whose determinant gives the displayed formula for \(D_3\). These descriptions remain valid when a lower ray hits a base vertex.

Inclusion-exclusion gives the useful identity
\[
C_L+C_R=|K|+D_3-s_3.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the strict pair condition is exactly \(D_3<s_3\).

We now reduce to a cap of area one by moving the base toward the origin while keeping its slope and the two cutting lines fixed. Define
\[
k=\frac{\rho-\sigma}{\rho+\sigma},\qquad
\eta_0=\frac{H}{\rho+\sigma},\qquad
f=1-a+ka,\qquad g=1-b-kb.
\]
We have \(-1<k<1\), \(f=X/(\rho+\sigma)>0\), and \(g=Y/(\rho+\sigma)>0\).

For \(|k|\le\eta\le\eta_0\), let
\[
\rho(\eta)=\frac{1+\eta}{1-k},\qquad
\sigma(\eta)=\frac{1+\eta}{1+k},
\]
and let \(K_\eta\) be the triangle with these extension factors. Its base is \(y=kx-\eta\). Both extension factors are at least one exactly when \(\eta\ge|k|\), and the original triangle is \(K_{\eta_0}\). The base-intersection conditions become
\[
(2a-1)\eta\le f,\qquad (2b-1)\eta\le g.
\tag{2}
\]
They persist as \(\eta\) decreases: if the coefficient on the left is nonnegative, its left side decreases; otherwise its left side is nonpositive whereas the right side is positive.

The already proved area formulas give
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_L(\eta)&=\frac{(\eta+f)^2}{2(1-k)f},&
C_R(\eta)&=\frac{(\eta+g)^2}{2(1+k)g},\\
D_3(\eta)&=\frac{\eta^2(a+b-2ab)}{2fg},&
s_3(\eta)&=\frac{a+b}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The formulas also hold at a possible endpoint \(\eta=0\) by continuity, with \(D_3(0)=0\). The two caps are strictly increasing in \(\eta\ge0\), and \(D_3(\eta)\) is nondecreasing there. The singleton is fixed. In particular, the strict inequality \(D_3(\eta)<s_3\), and hence the strict pair inequality by (1), persists as \(\eta\) decreases.

Set
\[
\eta_*=\max\left\{
|k|,\,
\sqrt{2(1-k)f}-f,\,
\sqrt{2(1+k)g}-g
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The initial cap floors give \(\eta_*\le\eta_0\). At \(\eta_*\), both caps are at least one. We claim that \(\eta_*>|k|\). Otherwise one extension factor equals one. If \(\rho=1\), the exact cap formula gives
\[
C_R-(\sigma-1)=\frac{(1-b)^2}{1+\sigma-2b}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Together with \(C_L\ge1\), this contradicts \(C_L+C_R<|K|=\sigma\). If \(\sigma=1\), reflection gives the same contradiction. This also covers \(\rho=\sigma=1\). Therefore \(\eta_*>|k|\), so at least one of the two square-root terms realizes the maximum in (4), and at least one cap equals one.

It suffices to prove \(D_3\ge s_3^2\) for this last triangle, because \(s_3\) stayed fixed and \(D_3\) did not increase as \(\eta\) decreased. Reflection \(x\mapsto-x\), if necessary, interchanges \(\rho,\sigma\), \(a,b\), and \(C_L,C_R\), leaving \(s_3,D_3\) unchanged. We can therefore assume that the boundary triangle has \(C_R=1\) and \(C_L\ge1\). In the rest of the boundary argument, \(\rho,\sigma\) denote its extension factors. They are both strictly greater than one by \(\eta_*>|k|\), and all original base-intersection and strict-pair conditions still hold.

Put
\[
t=\sigma-b,\qquad
Z=a+b-2ab,\qquad
F=t^2+2b-1,\qquad
E=(1-2a)t^2+2Z,\qquad
N=t+Z-at^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The equation \(C_R=1\) gives \(\rho F=\sigma=t+b\). Since \(\sigma>1\) and \(b<1\), \(t>0\), and
\[
F=(\sigma-b)^2+2b-1\ge(1-b)^2+2b-1=b^2>0.
\]
Direct substitution yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{t+b}{F},&
H&=\frac{(t+b)t(2-t)}{F},\\
X&=\frac{(t+b)E}{F},&
Y&=\frac{(t+b)t^2}{F},\\
C_L&=\frac{N^2}{FE},&
D_3&=\frac{(2-t)^2Z}{2E}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
In deriving (7), one uses \(E=1+(1-2a)F\) and \(N=t+b-aF\), which are identical to (6). The positivity of \(H\) gives \(t<2\), and the positivity of \(X\) gives \(E>0\). Also \(Z=a(1-b)+b(1-a)>0\). The cap floor \(C_L\ge1\) is exactly
\[
N^2-FE\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
For clarity, \(N=(E+t(2-t))/2>0\); no sign choice is hidden in the squared cap formula.

Define \(L=(a+b)E-(2-t)^2Z\). The preserved inequality \(D_3<s_3\), together with (7), gives \(L>0\). We also have \(|K|=\rho\sigma=(t+b)^2/F>2\), since both caps are at least one and their sum is strictly less than \(|K|\). Therefore \((t+b)^2-2F>0\). The exact identity
\[
(t+b)^2-2F
=(1-b)(2-t^2)-b(t-1)^2-b(1-b)
\tag{9}
\]
then gives \(2-t^2>0\), because \(0<b<1\).

We apply accepted fact f4698a89f141eff4 to the variables \(a,b,t\) in (6). Its polynomial \(C\) is \(N^2-FE\), and its variables \(Z,F,E,L\) have exactly the meanings above. Its auxiliary variables \(x=2a-1\), \(y=2b-1\), and \(w=t-1\) are polynomial variables, not the planar coordinates used to define \(K\). Their generators \(1-x^2\), \(1-y^2\), and \(1-w^2\) are positive because \(0<a,b<1\) and \(0<t<2\). The remaining generators \(F,E,2-t^2,L\) are positive by (6)-(9), and its cap generator \(4C\) is nonnegative by (8). All hypotheses of that accepted polynomial certificate are therefore satisfied. It gives
\[
2(2-t)^2Z-(a+b)^2E\ge0.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(E>0\), equations (7) and (10) imply \(D_3\ge(a+b)^2/4=s_3^2\) at the boundary triangle.

Finally return along the height motion. Equation (3) shows that the original doubleton area is at least this boundary doubleton area and that the singleton area is unchanged. Thus the original triangle satisfies \(D_3\ge s_3^2\). Its original cap floor \(C_L\ge1\) gives \(C_LD_3\ge D_3\ge s_3^2\), as required.
