## Statement

Let
\[
v_1=(0,0),\qquad v_2=(1,0),\qquad v_3=(0,1),
\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},
\]
and let \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) be the affine barycentric coordinates relative to these vertices. Set
\[
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_2=\frac45v_2+\frac15\Delta
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
v_2,\frac45v_2+\frac15v_1,\frac45v_2+\frac15v_3
\right\}.
\]
Define affine functions
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=\beta_1-\frac{39}{20}\beta_2-\frac1{10}\beta_3,\\
g_2&=-\frac15\beta_1+\beta_2-\frac15\beta_3,\\
g_3&=-\frac1{10}\beta_1-\frac{39}{20}\beta_2+\beta_3,
\end{aligned}
\]
and proper closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^3\) by
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):g_i(w)+h-1\ge0\},\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]
There is a compact polytope \(C\) having exactly \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) as its integer-height sections, the strict area triangle inequalities hold, and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
The open middle sections of \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) cover \(\mathbb R^2\), but, for
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
=\frac{788989}{1168200}
<\frac{17}{25}
=\frac{2T}{3}.
\]
Nevertheless,
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_2)=\frac{661}{1800}
>\frac{17}{75}
=\frac{2T}{9},
\]
whereas the first and third masses are below \(2T/9\).

## Proof

The three slice areas are
\[
a_0=a_1=\frac12,\qquad a_2=\frac1{50},\qquad T=\frac{51}{50}.
\]
Thus
\[
a_0<a_1+a_2,\qquad a_1<a_0+a_2,\qquad a_2<a_0+a_1.
\]
Also \(A_2\subseteq\Delta\). Since \(\Delta\) is convex, the midpoint of every point of \(A_0=\Delta\) and every point of \(A_2\) lies in \(\Delta=A_1\). Hence
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]

Let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\bigr).
\]
This is a compact polytope. Its sections at heights \(0\) and \(2\) are plainly \(A_0\) and \(A_2\). For its section at height \(1\), write the total weights assigned to the three generating heights as \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\). The equations
\[
\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1,\qquad
\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=1
\]
give \(\lambda_0=\lambda_2=:t\) and \(\lambda_1=1-2t\), where \(0\le t\le1/2\). Its planar component is therefore a convex combination of a point of \(A_1\) and a point of \((A_0+A_2)/2\), so it lies in \(A_1\). The reverse inclusion is one of the generating sections. Thus \(C\) has exactly the three asserted integer sections.

Directly from the definitions,
\[
g_1+4g_2+g_3
=\frac1{10}(\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3)
=\frac1{10}
\]
on the whole plane. Therefore at every \(w\in\mathbb R^2\), at least one \(g_i(w)\) is strictly positive. Since the open middle section of \(H_i\) is \(\{w:g_i(w)>0\}\), these three open sections cover \(\mathbb R^2\).

At height \(0\), the section condition is \(g_i\ge1\). On \(\Delta\), each \(g_i\) takes the value \(1\) at \(v_i\) and a strictly smaller value at the other two vertices. Hence \(\Delta\cap\{g_i\ge1\}=\{v_i\}\), which has planar area zero.

If an affine function on a triangle takes the values \(1,-a,-b\) at its three vertices, with \(a,b>0\), then its nonnegative cap is the corner triangle whose two incident edge ratios are \(1/(1+a)\) and \(1/(1+b)\). Its area is therefore \(1/((1+a)(1+b))\) times the area of the original triangle. The vertex values here are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &v_1&v_2&v_3\\ \hline
g_1&1&-39/20&-1/10\\
g_2&-1/5&1&-1/5\\
g_3&-1/10&-39/20&1 .
\end{array}
\]
Consequently the three middle-slice areas are
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac{1}{(59/20)(11/10)}
=\frac{100}{649},
\qquad
\frac12\cdot\frac{1}{(6/5)^2}
=\frac{25}{72},
\qquad
\frac{100}{649}.
\]

At height \(2\), the section condition is \(g_i\ge-1\). The three vertices of \(A_2\) are
\[
v_2,\qquad
\frac45v_2+\frac15v_1,\qquad
\frac45v_2+\frac15v_3.
\]
On these vertices, \(g_1\) takes the values
\[
-\frac{39}{20},\qquad -\frac{34}{25},\qquad-\frac{79}{50},
\]
and \(g_3\) takes the same three values in the reverse order. All are strictly below \(-1\), so the height-\(2\) sections of \(H_1,H_3\) miss \(A_2\). On the same vertices, \(g_2\) takes the values
\[
1,\qquad\frac{19}{25},\qquad\frac{19}{25},
\]
so the height-\(2\) section of \(H_2\) contains all of \(A_2\).

Combining the three heights gives
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_1)
=\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_3)
=\frac{100}{649},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_2)
=\frac{25}{72}+\frac1{50}
=\frac{661}{1800}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
=\frac{200}{649}+\frac{661}{1800}
=\frac{788989}{1168200}.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{2T}{3}=\frac{17}{25}
=\frac{794376}{1168200},
\]
the aggregate deficit is the positive exact number
\[
\frac{2T}{3}
-\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
=\frac{5387}{1168200}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{17}{75}
=\frac{408}{1800}
<\frac{661}{1800}
=\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_2),
\]
while
\[
\frac{100}{649}<\frac{17}{75}.
\]
Thus one individual mass is still above \(2T/9\), exactly as claimed.
