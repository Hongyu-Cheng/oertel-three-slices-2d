## Statement

Let \(L>0\), let \(h:[0,L]\to[0,\infty)\) be a concave Lebesgue probability density, and suppose
\[
\int_0^L yh(y)\,dy=1.
\]
Extend \(h\) by zero for purposes of defining
\[
G(w)=\int_w^\infty h(y)\,dy.
\]
Let \(a\ge c>0\) satisfy
\[
1<a^2+c^2,\qquad a+c<2,\qquad a^2<1+c^2,
\]
and let \(w_0,w_2\in\mathbb R\) satisfy \(aw_0+cw_2=2\). Then
\[
G(1)+a^2G(w_0)+c^2G(w_2)
\ge \frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\]

## Proof

Reset the two endpoint values of \(h\) to their finite one-sided limits. This changes neither the density nor any tail or moment. Accepted fact `c415ce7703455561` then gives a Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,L]\) and normalized common-support tents
\[
T_\tau(y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2y}{L\tau},&0\le y\le\tau,\\[5pt]
\dfrac{2(L-y)}{L(L-\tau)},&\tau\le y\le L,
\end{cases}
\]
with the endpoint tents interpreted as
\[
T_0(y)=\frac{2(L-y)}{L^2},
\qquad
T_L(y)=\frac{2y}{L^2},
\]
such that
\[
h(y)=\int_{[0,L]}T_\tau(y)\,\nu(d\tau),
\qquad
\int_{[0,L]}\tau\,\nu(d\tau)=3-L,
\qquad
\frac32\le L\le3.
\]

Put
\[
r=\frac{\tau}{L},\qquad
z=\frac{3-L}{L},\qquad
x=\frac1L=\frac{1+z}{3}.
\]
Thus \(0\le z\le1\) and the induced probability law of \(r\) has mean \(z\). If \(H_q(r)\) denotes the tail at \(qL\) of \(T_{rL}\), then
\[
H_q(r)=
\begin{cases}
1,&q\le0,\\
0,&q\ge1,\\
\dfrac{(1-q)^2}{1-r},&0<q<1,\ r\le q,\\[6pt]
1-\dfrac{q^2}{r},&0<q<1,\ r\ge q.
\end{cases}
\]

Define
\[
m=\frac{(1+x)^2}{4}=\frac{(4+z)^2}{36},
\qquad
p=\frac{16+3z^2+z^3}{36}.
\]
The central tail has the affine minorant
\[
H_x(r)\ge p+m(r-z)
\qquad(0\le r\le1).
\]
To prove it, set
\[
\ell(r)=1-x^2-m(1-r).
\]
On \(r\le x\), the function \(H_x(r)=(1-x)^2/(1-r)\) is convex, and \(\ell\) is its tangent at
\[
r_*=\frac{3x-1}{1+x}\in[0,x].
\]
Thus \(H_x\ge\ell\) there. On \(r\ge x\),
\[
H_x(r)-\ell(r)
=(1-r)\left(m-\frac{x^2}{r}\right)\ge0,
\]
because \(x^2/r\le x\le(1+x)^2/4=m\). Finally, \(\ell(z)=p\), proving the claimed minorant.

For every real \(q\), define
\[
\psi(q)=
\begin{cases}
1,&q\le0,\\
(1-q)^2,&0\le q\le1,\\
0,&q\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
The formulas for \(H_q(r)\) show that it is nondecreasing in \(r\), and hence
\[
H_q(r)\ge H_q(0)=\psi(q).
\]

Set \(x_i=w_i/L\) for \(i=0,2\), \(s=a+c\), and
\[
u=a(1-x_0),\qquad v=c(1-x_2).
\]
Since \(aw_0+cw_2=2\),
\[
u+v=s-\frac2L=s-\frac{2(1+z)}3=:k.
\]
Moreover,
\[
a^2\psi(x_0)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2,&0\le u\le a,\\
a^2,&u\ge a,
\end{cases}
\]
and analogously for \(c^2\psi(x_2)\). It follows that
\[
a^2\psi(x_0)+c^2\psi(x_2)
\ge \min\left\{c^2,\frac{k_+^2}{2}\right\},
\qquad k_+=\max\{k,0\}.
\]
Indeed, if \(k\le0\), the assertion is trivial. If \(k>0\) and \(u,v\ge0\), either neither term is saturated, in which case \(u^2+v^2\ge k^2/2\), or a saturated term contributes at least \(c^2\). If \(u<0\), then \(v>k\), so the second term is either \(c^2\) or exceeds \(k^2\). The case \(v<0\) is symmetric.

Write
\[
Q=\frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\]
Since \(p\ge4/9\) and \(a^2<1+c^2\),
\[
Q<\frac49+\frac{4c^2}{9}<p+c^2.
\]
It remains to show
\[
Q<p+\frac{k_+^2}{2}.
\]

Let \(d=a-c\). The hypotheses give \(1<s<2\) and \(sd=a^2-c^2<1\), whence
\[
a^2+c^2=\frac{s^2+d^2}{2}
<\frac{s^2+s^{-2}}2.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q<\frac{(s+s^{-1})^2}{9}.
\]
Also
\[
p\ge\frac49+\frac{z^2}{12}.
\]
We prove
\[
\frac49+\frac{z^2}{12}+\frac{k_+^2}{2}
\ge\frac{(s+s^{-1})^2}{9}.
\]

If \(k\le0\), then \(z\ge3s/2-1\) and \(1\le s\le4/3\). Hence the difference between the two sides is at least
\[
\frac{11s^4-36s^3+44s^2-16}{144s^2}.
\]
The numerator has derivative
\[
4s(11s^2-27s+22)>0,
\]
because the quadratic has discriminant \(-239\). Its value at \(s=1\) is \(3\), so the difference is positive.

Suppose \(k\ge0\). Multiplying the desired difference by \(36\) gives
\[
R(s,z)
=14s^2-24s(1+z)+16+16z+11z^2-\frac4{s^2}.
\]
For fixed \(s\), this quadratic has vertex \(z=(12s-8)/11\). If \(1\le s\le19/12\), minimizing over \(0\le z\le1\) gives
\[
R(s,z)\ge
P(s):=\frac{10s^2-72s+112}{11}-\frac4{s^2}.
\]
Here
\[
P''(s)=\frac{4(5s^4-66)}{11s^4}<0
\]
on this interval. Thus \(P\) is concave and its minimum is at an endpoint, while
\[
P(1)=\frac6{11},
\qquad
P\left(\frac{19}{12}\right)=\frac{13039}{25992}.
\]
Both are positive. If \(19/12\le s\le2\), the vertex is at least \(1\), so
\[
R(s,z)\ge R(s,1)
=14\left(s-\frac{12}{7}\right)^2+\frac{13}{7}-\frac4{s^2}
\ge\frac{13}{7}-\frac{576}{361}
=\frac{661}{2527}>0.
\]
This proves \(p+k_+^2/2>Q\). Combining it with \(p+c^2>Q\) yields
\[
p+a^2\psi(x_0)+c^2\psi(x_2)\ge Q.
\]

Therefore every common-support tent satisfies the affine kernel bound
\[
H_x(r)+a^2H_{x_0}(r)+c^2H_{x_2}(r)
\ge Q+m(r-z).
\]
Integrating against the tent mixing law and using \(\int(r-z)\,d\nu=0\) proves
\[
G(1)+a^2G(w_0)+c^2G(w_2)\ge Q.
\]

The support anchor is necessary. Without it, take the decreasing triangular density on \([9/10,6/5]\), whose mean is \(1\), together with
\[
a=1,\qquad c=\frac12,\qquad w_0=w_2=\frac43.
\]
Then \(aw_0+cw_2=2\), both outer tails vanish, and
\[
G(1)=\frac49
<\frac12
=\frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\]
Thus the unanchored wording is false, while the anchored statement above is exactly the class produced by the geometric normalization.
