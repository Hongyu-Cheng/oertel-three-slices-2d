## Statement

The universal assertion being refuted is the following fully displayed
claim.

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex
polytopes, with planar area denoted by \(|\cdot|\), such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{H1}
\]
After interchanging the endpoint labels if necessary, define
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,\qquad
k=|K|>0,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\tag{H2}
\]
and
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac kM.
\]
Assume the following explicit residual-area conditions:
\[
0<r\le1,\qquad s>0,
\tag{R1}
\]
\[
s\ge\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4},
\tag{R2}
\]
\[
1-r<s<1+r,
\tag{R3}
\]
\[
s<2-r,
\tag{R4}
\]
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\tag{R5}
\]
These are the raw exact residual conditions supplied by the sole accepted
dependency `15bb2c1680a0254e`.

Let \(f_i,f_\ell,f_q:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be nonconstant affine
functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_i+f_\ell+f_q=-1.
\tag{H3}
\]
Put
\[
g_j=-f_j\qquad(j\in\{i,\ell,q\}),
\]
so that
\[
g_i+g_\ell+g_q=1.
\tag{H4}
\]
For \(j\in\{i,\ell,q\}\), define the middle cap area
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|
=|K\cap\{g_j\ge0\}|,
\tag{H5}
\]
the exact shifted mass profile
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j
+|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\qquad a\in\mathbb R,
\tag{H6}
\]
and
\[
I_j=\{a\in\mathbb R:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\tag{H7}
\]

Fix \(q\), write \(\{i,\ell,q\}\) for the three indices, and assume
\[
c_q+m<t,
\tag{H8}
\]
\[
D_0:=3t-k-m>m.
\tag{H9}
\]
For \(u\in\{0,2\}\) and \(j\in\{i,\ell\}\), define
\[
\alpha_{uj}=\min_{A_u}g_j,\qquad
\beta_{uj}=\max_{A_u}g_j,\qquad
w_{uj}=\beta_{uj}-\alpha_{uj},
\tag{H10}
\]
\[
R_j=\beta_{0j}+\beta_{2j},\qquad
\sigma_j=\frac{w_{0j}^2}{M}+\frac{w_{2j}^2}{m},
\tag{H11}
\]
and
\[
\Psi_j(R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&R\le0,\\
\min\{R^2/\sigma_j,m\},&R>0.
\end{cases}
\tag{H12}
\]
The universal target to be refuted is
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)
\ge 2t-c_i-c_\ell.
\tag{J}
\]

Here is the counterexample.  Work in the standard \((x,y)\)-plane and set
\[
g_i=x,\qquad g_\ell=y,\qquad g_q=1-x-y,
\tag{1}
\]
equivalently
\[
f_i=-x,\qquad f_\ell=-y,\qquad f_q=x+y-1.
\tag{2}
\]
Let
\[
A_0=
\left[-\frac{11}{800},-\frac3{800}\right]
\times
\left[-\frac{165}{4},\frac{235}{4}\right],
\tag{3}
\]
\[
A_2=
\left[-\frac{17}{800},\frac3{800}\right]
\times
\left[\frac{25}{4},\frac{45}{4}\right],
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
K=
\left[-\frac7{400},0\right]
\times
\left[-\frac{35}{2},35\right].
\tag{5}
\]
Then (H1)-(H12) and (R1)-(R5) all hold, while
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)
=\frac18
<
\frac{71}{240}
=2t-c_i-c_\ell.
\tag{6}
\]
The exact violation of (J) is therefore
\[
2t-c_i-c_\ell-\Psi_i(R_i)-\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)
=\frac{41}{240}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

For the same instance,
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_i\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a\}|
\right)=0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_\ell\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_\ell\le-a\}|
\right)=\frac18=m.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently
\[
I_\ell=\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]

In addition, every row-aligned triangle
\[
\widetilde K(a,b,c)
=\{(x,y):x\ge-a,\ y\ge-b,\ x+y\le c\}
\tag{11}
\]
containing \((A_0+A_2)/2\) satisfies
\[
|\widetilde K(a,b,c)|
\ge\frac{441294049}{320000}
>\frac98=M+m.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus no such triangle satisfies the residual upper inequality
\[
\frac{|\widetilde K(a,b,c)|}{M}<1+\frac mM
\tag{13}
\]
from (R3).

## Proof

The three sets in (3)-(5) are rational rectangles with positive side
lengths, so they are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes.  The
Minkowski average of the two \(x\)-intervals is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\left(
\left[-\frac{11}{800},-\frac3{800}\right]
+
\left[-\frac{17}{800},\frac3{800}\right]
\right)
&=
\left[
\frac{-11/800-17/800}{2},
\frac{-3/800+3/800}{2}
\right]\\
&=\left[-\frac7{400},0\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The Minkowski average of the two \(y\)-intervals is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\left(
\left[-\frac{165}{4},\frac{235}{4}\right]
+
\left[\frac{25}{4},\frac{45}{4}\right]
\right)
&=
\left[
\frac{-165/4+25/4}{2},
\frac{235/4+45/4}{2}
\right]\\
&=\left[-\frac{35}{2},35\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (14)-(15) prove the stronger identity
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=K,
\tag{16}
\]
and hence (H1).

The side lengths of \(A_0\) are \(1/100\) and \(100\), the side lengths
of \(A_2\) are \(1/40\) and \(5\), and the side lengths of \(K\) are
\(7/400\) and \(105/2\).  Therefore
\[
M=|A_0|=1,\qquad
m=|A_2|=\frac18,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
k=|K|
=\frac7{400}\cdot\frac{105}{2}
=\frac{147}{160},
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
t=\frac{2(1+1/8+147/160)}9
=\frac{109}{240}.
\tag{19}
\]
This verifies (H2), including \(M\ge m>0\) and \(k>0\).

We now audit every residual condition.  Here
\[
r=\frac18,\qquad s=\frac{147}{160},
\]
so (R1) is immediate.  Since \(\sqrt{1/8}<1/2\),
\[
\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4}
<
\frac{(1+1/2)^2}{4}
=\frac9{16}
<
\frac{147}{160}=s,
\]
which proves (R2).  Moreover,
\[
1-r=\frac78=\frac{140}{160}
<
\frac{147}{160}
<
\frac{180}{160}=\frac98=1+r,
\tag{20}
\]
which proves both strict inequalities in (R3).  Also,
\[
s=\frac{147}{160}<\frac{15}{8}=2-r,
\]
so (R4) holds.  Finally, again using \(\sqrt{1/8}<1/2\),
\[
\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}
<
\frac{3/8+1-1}{2}
=\frac3{16}
<
\frac{147}{160}=s,
\]
which proves (R5).  Thus the sole accepted dependency's complete raw
residual system is verified term by term, rather than invoked only by
name.

The functions in (2) have linear parts
\[
(-1,0),\qquad(0,-1),\qquad(1,1).
\]
They are nonzero and pairwise nonparallel, and
\[
f_i+f_\ell+f_q
=-x-y+x+y-1
=-1.
\]
This proves (H3).  Taking negatives gives (1) and (H4).

We next compute all three middle caps from (H5).  Since
\(K\subseteq\{x\le0\}\), its intersection with \(\{g_i=x\ge0\}\) is
the null edge \(x=0\).  Hence
\[
c_i=0.
\tag{21}
\]
The cap \(\{g_\ell=y\ge0\}\) cuts from \(K\) a rectangle of width
\(7/400\) and height \(35\), so
\[
c_\ell=\frac7{400}\cdot35=\frac{49}{80}.
\tag{22}
\]
The \(q\)-cap is \(K\cap\{x+y\le1\}\).  For each
\(x\in[-7/400,0]\), the line \(y=1-x\) lies strictly between
\(-35/2\) and \(35\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_q
&=\int_{-7/400}^{0}
\left((1-x)-\left(-\frac{35}{2}\right)\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{-7/400}^{0}
\left(\frac{37}{2}-x\right)\,dx\\
&=\frac{103649}{320000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (21)-(23) verify (H5) for the chosen functions.

The strict \(q\)-tail in (H8) has the exact positive slack
\[
\begin{aligned}
t-m-c_q
&=\frac{109}{240}
-\frac18
-\frac{103649}{320000}\\
&=\frac{5053}{960000}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_0
&=3t-k-m\\
&=\frac{109}{80}
-\frac{147}{160}
-\frac18\\
&=\frac{51}{160}
>\frac18=m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Thus (H8)-(H9) both hold strictly.

We now verify the support and width definitions (H10)-(H12).  In the
\(i\)-direction \(g_i=x\),
\[
\beta_{0i}=-\frac3{800},\qquad
\beta_{2i}=\frac3{800},
\]
\[
w_{0i}=\frac1{100},\qquad
w_{2i}=\frac1{40}.
\]
Consequently
\[
R_i=\beta_{0i}+\beta_{2i}=0,
\tag{26}
\]
\[
\sigma_i
=\frac{(1/100)^2}{1}
+\frac{(1/40)^2}{1/8}
=\frac{51}{10000},
\tag{27}
\]
and the first branch in (H12) gives
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)=0.
\tag{28}
\]

In the \(\ell\)-direction \(g_\ell=y\),
\[
\beta_{0\ell}=\frac{235}{4},\qquad
\beta_{2\ell}=\frac{45}{4},
\]
\[
w_{0\ell}=100,\qquad
w_{2\ell}=5.
\]
It follows that
\[
R_\ell=70,
\tag{29}
\]
\[
\sigma_\ell
=\frac{100^2}{1}
+\frac{5^2}{1/8}
=10200.
\tag{30}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{R_\ell^2}{\sigma_\ell}
=\frac{4900}{10200}
=\frac{49}{102}
>\frac18=m,
\tag{31}
\]
the second profile is on its \(m\)-plateau:
\[
\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)=\frac18.
\tag{32}
\]

Combining (19), (21), and (22) gives
\[
2t-c_i-c_\ell
=\frac{109}{120}-\frac{49}{80}
=\frac{71}{240}.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (28), (32), and (33) yield
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)
=\frac18
<\frac{71}{240}
=2t-c_i-c_\ell,
\]
and the difference is
\[
\frac{71}{240}-\frac18
=\frac{41}{240}>0.
\]
Thus all hypotheses (H1)-(H12) and (R1)-(R5) hold, but the explicitly
stated target (J) fails.  This proves (6)-(7).

It remains to verify the claims involving the exact profiles
\(\mu_j\) and the sets \(I_j\), using their definitions (H6)-(H7).
For convenience, put
\[
\Phi_j(a)
=|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\]
so that
\[
\mu_j(a)=c_j+\Phi_j(a).
\tag{34}
\]

For \(j=i\), take \(a=3/800\).  Since \(f_i=-x\),
\[
A_0\cap\{f_i\le3/800\}
=A_0\cap\left\{x\ge-\frac3{800}\right\}
\]
is the null right edge of \(A_0\), while
\[
A_2\cap\{f_i\le-3/800\}
=A_2\cap\left\{x\ge\frac3{800}\right\}
\]
is the null right edge of \(A_2\).  Hence
\[
\Phi_i(3/800)=0.
\]
Planar areas are nonnegative, so
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}\Phi_i(a)=0,
\]
which proves (8).  In fact, (21), (19), and (34) give
\[
\mu_i(3/800)=0<t,
\]
so \(3/800\in I_i\).

For \(j=\ell\), the two endpoint caps in (34) are
\[
A_0\cap\{f_\ell\le a\}=A_0\cap\{y\ge-a\},
\tag{35}
\]
\[
A_2\cap\{f_\ell\le-a\}=A_2\cap\{y\ge a\}.
\tag{36}
\]
If \(a\le25/4\), then (36) is all of \(A_2\), because \(25/4\) is its
minimum \(y\)-coordinate.  Therefore
\[
\Phi_\ell(a)\ge|A_2|=m.
\tag{37}
\]
If \(a>25/4\), then \(-a<-25/4\), and the cap in (35) contains
\[
A_0\cap\left\{y\ge-\frac{25}{4}\right\}.
\]
That contained rectangle has area
\[
\frac1{100}
\left(
\frac{235}{4}+\frac{25}{4}
\right)
=\frac{13}{20}
>\frac18=m.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (37)-(38) show \(\Phi_\ell(a)\ge m\) for every real \(a\).
At \(a=-235/4\), the cap in (35) is the null top edge
\(\{y=235/4\}\) of \(A_0\), whereas (36) is all of \(A_2\).
Thus equality is attained:
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}\Phi_\ell(a)=m=\frac18.
\]
This proves (9).

By (22), (34), and (9),
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}\mu_\ell(a)
=c_\ell+\inf_a\Phi_\ell(a)
=\frac{49}{80}+\frac18
=\frac{59}{80}.
\tag{39}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{59}{80}
>\frac{109}{240}=t,
\]
one has \(\mu_\ell(a)>t\) for every real \(a\).  Definition (H7) now
gives
\[
I_\ell=\varnothing,
\]
proving (10).  Therefore the same instance satisfies the explicit
tail-exclusion conclusion
\[
I_i=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad I_\ell=\varnothing,
\]
through its second alternative.  Only the stronger auxiliary inequality
(J) is refuted.

Finally, consider a triangle of the form (11) containing
\((A_0+A_2)/2=K\).  Since
\[
\min_K x=-\frac7{400},\qquad
\min_K y=-\frac{35}{2},\qquad
\max_K(x+y)=35,
\]
containment forces
\[
a\ge\frac7{400},\qquad
b\ge\frac{35}{2},\qquad
c\ge35.
\tag{40}
\]
The three vertices of \(\widetilde K(a,b,c)\) are
\[
(-a,-b),\qquad (b+c,-b),\qquad(-a,a+c).
\]
Both perpendicular leg lengths are \(a+b+c\), so
\[
|\widetilde K(a,b,c)|
=\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{2}.
\tag{41}
\]
Equations (40)-(41) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widetilde K(a,b,c)|
&\ge
\frac12
\left(
\frac7{400}+\frac{35}{2}+35
\right)^2\\
&=
\frac12\left(\frac{21007}{400}\right)^2\\
&=
\frac{441294049}{320000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
For the unchanged endpoint areas \(M=1,m=1/8\), condition (R3) for a
replacement middle body requires
\[
\frac{|\widetilde K(a,b,c)|}{M}
<1+\frac mM
=\frac98.
\]
But the lower bound in (42) is strictly larger than \(9/8\), proving
(12)-(13).  This last obstruction is an exact failure of the residual-area
transfer; it does not use any monotonicity claim for the three cap areas.
