## Statement

Under the affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `ce343e749df349fe`, write
\[
r_i=|R_{\{i\}}|,
\qquad
t=|R_{\{1,2,3\}}|,
\qquad
k=|K|.
\]
If
\[
r_1r_2r_3>0,
\]
then
\[
\left(
\sqrt{r_1+\frac{2t}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_2+\frac{2t}{9}}
+\sqrt{r_3+\frac{2t}{9}}
\right)^2
\le2k.
\tag{1}
\]
For every support pattern, including \(t=0\),
\[
\left(\sqrt{r_1}+\sqrt{r_2}+\sqrt{r_3}\right)^2
<2k.
\tag{2}
\]
The constant \(2\) in (2) is sharp. More precisely, if
\[
\lambda_i=-f_i,
\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1,
\]
\[
\Delta=\{\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\ge0\},
\qquad
d=|\Delta|,
\]
and
\[
K_a=\{\lambda_i\ge-a\ (i=1,2,3)\},
\qquad a>0,
\]
then
\[
|K_a|=d(1+3a)^2,
\qquad
r_1=r_2=r_3=2da^2,
\tag{3}
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{\left(\sqrt{r_1}+\sqrt{r_2}+\sqrt{r_3}\right)^2}{|K_a|}
=
\frac{18a^2}{(1+3a)^2}
\longrightarrow2.
\tag{4}
\]
No finite member of this family, and no two-dimensional admissible \(K\), attains \(2\).

## Proof

Put
\[
\lambda_i=-f_i.
\]
Then
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1,
\tag{5}
\]
and the affine map \(x\mapsto(\lambda_1(x),\lambda_2(x),\lambda_3(x))\) is an affine isomorphism onto the plane in (5). Cutting lines have planar area zero, so strict and weak choices on their boundaries do not affect any area below.

Assume first that
\[
r_1r_2r_3>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Accepted fact `ce343e749df349fe` proves in this case that
\[
\Delta\subseteq K,
\qquad
R_{\{1,2,3\}}=\Delta
\quad\text{up to cutting-line boundaries}.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
t=|\Delta|>0.
\tag{8}
\]

Shift the three affine functions by setting
\[
g_i=f_i+\frac13=\frac13-\lambda_i.
\]
They satisfy
\[
g_1+g_2+g_3=0,
\]
so their three distinct zero lines are concurrent at
\[
o=\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13\right).
\]
For each \(i\), let \(B_i\) be the open shifted sector
\[
B_i=
\{g_i<0,\ g_j>0\ (j\ne i)\}
=
\left\{
\lambda_i>\frac13,\
\lambda_j<\frac13\ (j\ne i)
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The three \(B_i\) are alternate sectors of this concurrent fan.

The original singleton chamber is
\[
S_i=
\{\lambda_j<0\ (j\ne i)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (5) makes \(\lambda_i>1\) on \(S_i\), so
\[
S_i\subseteq B_i.
\tag{11}
\]
Inside the reference triangle define
\[
C_i=\Delta\cap\overline{B_i}.
\]
For example, in the affine coordinates \((\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\),
\[
C_1=
\left\{
0\le\lambda_2\le\frac13,\
0\le\lambda_3\le\frac13
\right\}
\tag{12}
\]
up to its boundary. The remaining coordinate
\[
\lambda_1=1-\lambda_2-\lambda_3
\]
is at least \(1/3\) throughout this square. The full reference triangle is
\[
\{\lambda_2,\lambda_3\ge0,\ \lambda_2+\lambda_3\le1\}.
\]
The coordinate square in (12) has Euclidean coordinate area \(1/9\), while the coordinate triangle has area \(1/2\). The affine Jacobian is constant, so
\[
|C_i|=\frac{2t}{9}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{13}
\]

The interiors of \(C_i\) and the original singleton cell \(R_{\{i\}}\) are disjoint: in \(C_i\), the two other barycentric coordinates are nonnegative, while in \(R_{\{i\}}\) they are negative. By (7), (11), and (13),
\[
\left|K\cap\overline{B_i}\right|
\ge
r_i+\frac{2t}{9}.
\tag{14}
\]

Translate the concurrency point \(o\) to the origin and put
\[
L_i=K\cap\overline{B_i},
\qquad
a_i=|L_i|.
\]
Each \(L_i\) is compact and convex, contains \(o\), and lies in the closure of one of three alternate sectors. Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{conv}(L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3)\subseteq K.
\]
Accepted fact `22d750674c663f88` therefore gives
\[
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2
\le
\left|\operatorname{conv}(L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3)\right|
\le k.
\tag{15}
\]
Using (14) and monotonicity of the square root in (15) proves (1).

By (8), every added term \(2t/9\) in (1) is strictly positive. Hence (1) immediately yields
\[
\left(\sqrt{r_1}+\sqrt{r_2}+\sqrt{r_3}\right)^2
<
\left(
\sum_i\sqrt{r_i+\frac{2t}{9}}
\right)^2
\le2k.
\tag{16}
\]
This proves (2) when all singleton cells are positive. It also shows that the case \(t=0\) cannot occur under (6).

We now treat every zero-singleton support pattern without assuming anything about \(t\). If all three \(r_i\) vanish, the left side of (2) is zero, while \(k>0\) because \(K\) is two-dimensional.

If exactly one singleton area, say \(r_i\), is positive, then
\[
\left(\sum_j\sqrt{r_j}\right)^2=r_i\le k<2k.
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose exactly two singleton areas \(r_i,r_j\) are positive, and let \(\ell\) be the remaining index. Because cutting lines and \(\partial K\) have area zero, choose
\[
x\in\operatorname{int}K\cap S_i,
\qquad
y\in\operatorname{int}K\cap S_j.
\]
Along the segment from \(x\) to \(y\), one has
\[
\lambda_\ell<0
\]
everywhere. At \(x\),
\[
\lambda_i>1,\qquad \lambda_j<0,
\]
whereas at \(y\),
\[
\lambda_i<0,\qquad \lambda_j>1.
\]
The two affine coordinates \(\lambda_i,\lambda_j\) cross zero at distinct parameters because
\[
\lambda_i+\lambda_j=1-\lambda_\ell>1.
\]
Between those two crossing parameters,
\[
\lambda_i>0,\qquad
\lambda_j>0,\qquad
\lambda_\ell<0.
\tag{18}
\]
An interior point of this subsegment lies in the strict doubleton chamber \(R_{\{i,j\}}\) and in \(\operatorname{int}K\). A sufficiently small disk around it remains in both sets, so
\[
r_{\{i,j\}}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
The exact-cell partition and (19) imply
\[
r_i+r_j<k.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left(\sqrt{r_i}+\sqrt{r_j}\right)^2
\le2(r_i+r_j)
<2k.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (17) and (20), together with the all-zero case, prove (2) for every zero-singleton pattern and every value of \(t\). Line-boundary contacts do not alter the argument because the finitely many cutting lines have area zero, while the disk in (19) lies in a strict chamber.

It remains to verify sharpness exactly. Let \(d=|\Delta|\) and \(a>0\). Define
\[
\mu_i=\frac{\lambda_i+a}{1+3a}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_i\mu_i=1,
\qquad
\lambda_i\ge-a\iff\mu_i\ge0.
\]
Thus \(K_a\) is the image of \(\Delta\) under the homothety about \(o\) with ratio \(1+3a\):
\[
\lambda-o=(1+3a)(\mu-o).
\]
Therefore
\[
|K_a|=d(1+3a)^2.
\tag{21}
\]

For the first singleton cell inside \(K_a\), the exact strict inequalities are
\[
-a\le\lambda_2<0,
\qquad
-a\le\lambda_3<0.
\tag{22}
\]
They automatically give
\[
\lambda_1=1-\lambda_2-\lambda_3>1,
\]
so no further constraint is active. In the same affine coordinates \((\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\), (22) is a square of coordinate area \(a^2\), while \(\Delta\) is the coordinate triangle of area \(1/2\). Hence
\[
r_1=2da^2.
\]
The cyclic calculations are identical, proving (3). All open or closed boundary choices in (22) differ only on lines and therefore have the same area.

Equation (4) follows from (3). For every finite \(a>0\),
\[
\frac{18a^2}{(1+3a)^2}<2
\]
because
\[
2(1+3a)^2-18a^2=2+12a>0.
\]
As \(a\to\infty\), the ratio tends to \(2\). Thus no smaller universal constant is possible. Finally, (2) is strict for every two-dimensional \(K\), so the sharp constant \(2\) is unattained.
