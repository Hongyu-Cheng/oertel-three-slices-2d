## Statement

Use the affine coordinates and common area normalization of accepted
fact `6d2e762bf26bbce5`:
\[
r_i=2\lambda_i-s\tau_i,\qquad s>0,\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_i=\frac{1+s}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix a row \(j\).  Choose a transverse coordinate so that \((x,y)\),
with \(x=r_j\), is an area coordinate chart in the \(r\)-plane and
\((t,z)\), with \(t=\tau_j\), is the corresponding chart in the
\(\tau\)-plane.  Let \(P,C_\tau\) be nonempty planar compact convex
sets.  Write their nonempty sections as
\[
P_x=[A(x),B(x)]\quad(x\in X),\qquad
(C_\tau)_t=[L(t),R(t)]\quad(t\in T),
\tag{2}
\]
where \(X,T\) are their compact projection intervals.  Put
\[
H=\frac12(P+2\lambda-sC_\tau),
\tag{3}
\]
and, for \(u\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
I(u)=\{t\in T:u+st\in X\},\qquad
D=\{u\leq0:I(u)\neq\varnothing\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(u\in D\) and \(t_-,t_+\in I(u)\), define the two-rail
functional
\[
\begin{split}
\Phi(u;t_-,t_+)
={}&B(u+st_+)-A(u+st_-)\\
&+sR(t_-)-sL(t_+).
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\left|H\cap\{r_j\leq\lambda_j\}\right|
=\frac14\int_D
\max_{t_-,t_+\in I(u)}\Phi(u;t_-,t_+)\,du .
}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, for every measurable \(D_0\subseteq D\) and every
Borel pair \(\sigma_-,\sigma_+\) with
\(\sigma_\pm(u)\in I(u)\),
\[
\boxed{
\left|H\cap\{r_j\leq\lambda_j\}\right|
\geq\frac14\int_{D_0}
\bigl[\Phi(u;\sigma_-(u),\sigma_+(u))\bigr]_+\,du .
}
\tag{7}
\]
If
\[
w_P=B-A,\quad c_P=\frac{A+B}{2},\qquad
w_C=R-L,\quad c_C=\frac{L+R}{2},
\]
then the integrand before taking the positive part is exactly
\[
\begin{split}
\Phi(u;t_-,t_+)
={}&c_P(u+st_+)-c_P(u+st_-)
-s\bigl(c_C(t_+)-c_C(t_-)\bigr)\\
&+\frac12\bigl(
w_P(u+st_+)+w_P(u+st_-)
+s w_C(t_+)+s w_C(t_-)\bigr).
\end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (7) contains both rail widths and the variation of their
centerlines.

If \(H\subseteq K\), if the row-\(j\) crossing and no-half conditions
of accepted fact `6d2e762bf26bbce5` hold, and if the right side of
(7) is at least
\[
U_j=\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_j-v_j,
\tag{9}
\]
then such a \(K\) cannot exist.

The formula passes the following exact two-rail calibration.  Set
\[
s=\frac{1001}{1000},\qquad
\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac{2001}{6000},
\tag{10}
\]
and let
\[
\begin{split}
P=\operatorname{conv}\{&
(91/100,0),(1823/2000,0),\\
&(2077/4000,1923/4000),(519/1000,481/1000),\\
&(0,901/1000),(0,9/10)\}.
\end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
In \((\tau_1,\tau_2)\)-coordinates, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_1&=(626/625,-1/10000),&
Y_2&=(-1/10000,626/625),\\
e_1&=(1,0),&e_2&=(0,1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
C_\tau=\operatorname{conv}\{Y_1,Y_2,e_2,e_1\}.
\tag{13}
\]
For
\[
M=
\frac{16038523713803046358461709}
{368394169893750000000000000},
\qquad
m=\frac{60231282059897949}{40000000000000000000},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
u_1=\frac{314859717}{31486000000000000000},
\qquad
u_2=\frac{17306988739}{1153800000000000000000},
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
q_1=q_2=\frac{3006003}{40000000000000},
\qquad v_1=m-q_2,\quad v_2=m-q_1,
\tag{16}
\]
the exact identity (6) gives, for
\(H=(P+2\lambda-sC_\tau)/2\),
\[
\left|H\cap\{r_1\leq\lambda_1\}\right|
=\frac{189788263753}{10000000000000},
\tag{17}

\[

## Proof

Translate \(H\) by \(-\lambda\) and dilate it by \(2\).  In the
chosen coordinates this gives
\[
2(H-\lambda)=P-sC_\tau.
\tag{21}
\]
The row cap \(r_j\leq\lambda_j\) becomes the halfplane \(u\leq0\),
where \(u\) is the first coordinate in \(P-sC_\tau\).  Therefore
\[
\left|H\cap\{r_j\leq\lambda_j\}\right|
=\frac14\left|(P-sC_\tau)\cap\{u\leq0\}\right|.
\tag{22}
\]

Fix \(u\in D\).  A point in the \(u\)-section of \(P-sC_\tau\) has
the form
\[
(u,v)=(u+st,y)-s(t,z),
\]
where \(t\in I(u)\),
\[
A(u+st)\leq y\leq B(u+st),\qquad
L(t)\leq z\leq R(t).
\]
For a fixed \(t\), the possible transverse coordinates form the
interval
\[
\left[
A(u+st)-sR(t),\
B(u+st)-sL(t)
\right].
\tag{23}
\]
The whole \(u\)-section is an interval because \(P-sC_\tau\) is
convex.  Its two endpoints are consequently
\[
\underline E(u)=
\min_{t\in I(u)}\bigl(A(u+st)-sR(t)\bigr),
\tag{24}
\]
\[
\overline E(u)=
\max_{t\in I(u)}\bigl(B(u+st)-sL(t)\bigr).
\tag{25}
\]
These extrema are attained because the feasible fiber is compact.
Their difference is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline E(u)-\underline E(u)
&=\max_{t_+,t_-\in I(u)}
\bigl[
B(u+st_+)-sL(t_+)\\
&\hspace{34mm}
-A(u+st_-)+sR(t_-)
\bigr]\\
&=\max_{t_+,t_-\in I(u)}\Phi(u;t_-,t_+).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Taking \(t_-=t_+=t\) in (26) gives
\[
\Phi(u;t,t)
=w_P(u+st)+s w_C(t)\geq0,
\tag{27}
\]
so the maximum in (26) is nonnegative.  Cavalieri integration of the
sections, followed by (22), proves (6).  Pointwise, the maximum in
(26) is at least both \(0\) and
\(\Phi(u;\sigma_-(u),\sigma_+(u))\).  Integration over \(D_0\)
proves (7).  Expanding \(A,B,L,R\) in terms of their widths and
centerlines gives (8).

For the exclusion assertion, accepted fact `6d2e762bf26bbce5`
gives from the crossing and \(k<M+m\)
\[
c_j
=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9-u_j-v_j
<\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_j-v_j=U_j.
\tag{28}
\]
But \(H\subseteq K\) and (7) give \(c_j\geq U_j\) whenever its
right side reaches (9), a contradiction.

It remains to audit the calibration entirely by exact section
integration.  The halfspace description of (13) is
\[
t+z\geq1,\qquad 16t+z\geq1,\qquad
t+16z\geq1,\qquad t+z\leq\frac{2003}{2000}.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus in row \(1\), and also in row \(2\) after swapping coordinates,
its two rails are
\[
L(t)=
\begin{cases}
1-16t,&-1/10000\leq t\leq0,\\
1-t,&0\leq t\leq1,\\
(1-t)/16,&1\leq t\leq626/625,
\end{cases}
\qquad
R(t)=\frac{2003}{2000}-t.
\tag{30}
\]
This is the transverse information absent from a one-segment bound.
