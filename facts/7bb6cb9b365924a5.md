## Statement

Assume the common-plane affine hypotheses, midpoint containment, exact-cell
convention, and all-double positive \(Q\)-support used in accepted facts
`b530304a83fe1e51` and `2186f01c16b1f4e3`.  Let
\[
q=q_N>0,\qquad
C_i=\overline{Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}},\qquad
x_i=|C_i|,
\]
and suppose
\[
x_1=x_2=x_3=x=\frac5{21}q.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad p=|P|=p_\varnothing>0,
\]
and, for each \(i\), let
\[
P^{(i)}
=\overline{P[0;\{i\}]}
=\overline{A_0\cap\{p_i>0\}}.
\]
Define the three complete-family \(\mathrm{F1}\) midpoint bodies and their
common-core subbodies by
\[
E_i=\frac{P^{(i)}+C_i}{2},
\qquad
B_i=\frac{P+C_i}{2}\subseteq E_i.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
\mathcal S_R=\bigcup_{|G|=1}\overline{R_G},
\qquad
\mathcal D_R=\bigcup_{|G|=2}\overline{R_G},
\]
\[
s_R=\sum_{|G|=1}r_G,\qquad
d_R=\sum_{|G|=2}r_G,
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal U=\bigcup_{i=1}^3E_i,\qquad
\mathcal O=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq3}|E_i\cap E_j|.
\tag{3}
\]
Finally set
\[
\Delta_Q
=
\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}-3\sqrt{x}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then \(\Delta_Q\geq0\), and the following exact deficit identity holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
2s_R+d_R
&-\frac14\sum_{i=1}^3
\left(\sqrt{M-u_i}+\sqrt{x}\right)^2\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^3
\left[
|E_i|-\frac14
\left(\sqrt{M-u_i}+\sqrt{x}\right)^2
\right]\\
&\quad
+\left(s_R-|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal S_R|\right)
+\left(d_R-|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal D_R|\right)
+\left(s_R-\mathcal O\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Every term on the right of (5) is nonnegative.

The common \(P_\varnothing\) core gives the shape-sensitive lower bound
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3|E_i|
\geq
\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt x\right)^2
+\frac12\sqrt p\,\Delta_Q.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently
\[
2s_R+d_R
\geq
\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt x\right)^2
+\frac12\sqrt p\,\Delta_Q,
\tag{7}
\]
and, more sharply,
\[
d_R+\mathcal O
\geq
\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt x\right)^2
+\frac12\sqrt p\,\Delta_Q-s_R.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus, whenever the right side of (8) is positive, at least one of
\(d_R\) and \(\mathcal O\) is at least half that right side.  In
particular, in the exact no-overlap subcase \(\mathcal O=0\), the defect
of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` satisfies
\[
\eta
\geq3d_R
\geq
3\left[
\frac34\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt x\right)^2
+\frac12\sqrt p\,\Delta_Q-s_R
\right].
\tag{9}
\]

There is an explicit formula for \(\Delta_Q\) on the following
common-plane-compatible triangle-cap shape subfamily.  Let
\[
A_2=T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},
\qquad |T|=\frac{12q}{7}.
\]
Let
\[
w=\frac{\sqrt5}{6},\qquad
0<r<s<2r,\qquad
rs=w^2,
\tag{10}
\]
and assume
\[
2r<1,\qquad s+w<1.
\tag{11}
\]
Let the three caps be
\[
C_1=\operatorname{conv}
\{v_1,(1-r)v_1+rv_2,(1-s)v_1+sv_3\},
\]
\[
C_2=\operatorname{conv}
\{v_2,(1-r)v_2+rv_1,(1-s)v_2+sv_3\},
\]
\[
C_3=\operatorname{conv}
\{v_3,(1-w)v_3+wv_1,(1-w)v_3+wv_2\}.
\tag{12}
\]
These caps have pairwise disjoint interiors, all have area \(x=5q/21\),
and their complement in \(T\) is the central cell of area \(q\).
With
\[
y=\frac{s}{w},
\]
conditions (10) are equivalent to
\[
1<y<\sqrt2.
\tag{13}
\]
One has the exact shape formula
\[
|C_1+C_2+C_3|
=x\left(3+4y+2y^2\right),
\tag{14}
\]
and hence
\[
\Delta_Q
=\sqrt x\left(
\sqrt{3+4y+2y^2}-3
\right)>0.
\tag{15}
\]
The compatible quantitative interval
\[
\frac75\leq y<\sqrt2
\tag{16}
\]
is nonempty, and throughout it
\[
\Delta_Q
\geq
\sqrt x\left(\frac{\sqrt{313}}5-3\right).
\tag{17}
\]

For completeness, this subfamily has explicit compatible common-plane
cutting functions.  Affinely put
\[
v_1=(0,0),\qquad v_2=(1,0),\qquad v_3=(0,1),
\]
and write the coordinates as \((\xi,\zeta)\).  Define the three
central-side cap-base functions
\[
g_1=\frac{\xi}{r}+\frac{\zeta}{s}-1,
\]
\[
g_2=\frac{1-\xi-\zeta}{r}+\frac{\zeta}{s}-1,
\qquad
g_3=1-w-\zeta.
\tag{18}
\]
Then the central halfplanes are \(g_i\geq0\).  The positive rescalings
\[
G_1=rs\,g_1,\qquad
G_2=rs\,g_2,\qquad
G_3=(2r-s)g_3
\tag{19}
\]
have linear parts summing to zero and satisfy
\[
G_1+G_2+G_3
=
(2r-s)(1-w)+s(1-2r)
=:C>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus
\[
\psi_i=-\frac{G_i}{C},\qquad
\widetilde q_i=3\psi_i,\qquad
\Lambda_i=-\frac23,\qquad
f_i=\widetilde q_i-\Lambda_i
\tag{21}
\]
satisfy
\[
\sum_i f_i=-1,\qquad
\sum_i\Lambda_i=-2<-1,\qquad
\widetilde q_i=f_i+\Lambda_i,
\tag{22}
\]
and the three linear parts are pairwise nonparallel.  Hence (12) is an
actual \(A_2\)-shape specialization of the normal form in accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`, not merely three disjoint caps in an arbitrary
triangle.  Conclusions (5)--(9) still require this \(A_2\) geometry to be
part of a full common-plane realization.

## Proof

We first prove the exact incidence and deficit identity.  Row
\(\mathrm{F1}_i\) of accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3`, at the set level
before Brunn--Minkowski is applied, is the partial-union inclusion from
accepted fact `b530304a83fe1e51`:
\[
E_i
\subseteq
\bigcup_{\varnothing\ne G\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}\overline{R_G}
\quad\text{up to cutting-line null sets}.
\tag{23}
\]
The use of closures changes no area.  It follows that
\[
E_i\cap E_j\subseteq\overline{R_{\{k\}}}
\quad\text{when }\{i,j,k\}=N,
\tag{24}
\]
again up to null sets, and that the triple intersection has area zero.
The three pairwise intersections in (24) lie in three different singleton
cells, so their mutual intersections also have area zero.  Moreover,
\[
\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S_R\cup\mathcal D_R
\quad\text{up to null sets}.
\tag{25}
\]
Inclusion--exclusion using (24)--(25) therefore gives the exact
multiplicity identity
\[
\sum_i|E_i|
=
|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal S_R|
+|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal D_R|
+\mathcal O.
\tag{26}
\]

Each \(E_i\) is the midpoint Minkowski sum of two positive-area convex
bodies of areas \(M-u_i\) and \(x\).  Planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
|E_i|
\geq
\frac14\left(\sqrt{M-u_i}+\sqrt x\right)^2.
\tag{27}
\]
Subtract the sum of (27) from \(2s_R+d_R\), then use (26).  The result is
exactly (5).  Its first three displayed types of terms are nonnegative by
(27), (25), and the definitions of \(s_R,d_R\).  Equation (24) gives
\(\mathcal O\leq s_R\), proving nonnegativity of the final term.  This is
the shape-level refinement of the scalar multiplicity inequality in
accepted fact `2bb72698f0248a65`.

We next prove the common-core estimate.  We use only the following planar
mixed-area facts, and include their polygonal proof.  For positive-area
convex polygons \(K,L\), define \(V(K,L)\) by
\[
|aK+bL|
=a^2|K|+2ab\,V(K,L)+b^2|L|
\qquad(a,b\geq0).
\tag{28}
\]
To verify (28), merge the counterclockwise edge-vector lists of \(K\) and
\(L\), inserting zero edges when a direction is absent.  The edge vector
in each direction for \(aK+bL\) is the corresponding linear combination.
The shoelace determinant is therefore a homogeneous quadratic polynomial
in \(a,b\), with the pure coefficients \(|K|\) and \(|L|\).  The same
merged-list description shows that the cross coefficient is symmetric
and additive in either polygon under Minkowski addition.  Thus
\[
V(K,L_1+L_2)=V(K,L_1)+V(K,L_2).
\tag{29}
\]
Applying planar Brunn--Minkowski to \(K+tL\), squaring its two positive
sides, and comparing with (28) gives
\[
V(K,L)\geq\sqrt{|K||L|}.
\tag{30}
\]
Equality in (30) for positive-area convex bodies holds exactly when they
are homothetic.  Polygonal approximation extends (28)--(30) unchanged to
the closures used here.

Since \(P\subseteq P^{(i)}\), (2) gives \(B_i\subseteq E_i\).  Let
\[
S_Q=C_1+C_2+C_3.
\]
Using (28)--(30), additivity, and \(|C_i|=x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i|E_i|
&\geq\sum_i|B_i|\\
&=\frac14\sum_i|P+C_i|\\
&=\frac34(p+x)+\frac12V(P,S_Q)\\
&\geq
\frac34(p+x)+\frac12\sqrt{p|S_Q|}\\
&=
\frac34(\sqrt p+\sqrt x)^2
+\frac12\sqrt p
\left(\sqrt{|S_Q|}-3\sqrt x\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Repeated planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
\sqrt{|S_Q|}
\geq\sqrt{|C_1|}+\sqrt{|C_2|}+\sqrt{|C_3|}
=3\sqrt x,
\]
so \(\Delta_Q\geq0\), and (31) proves (6).  Equality
\(\Delta_Q=0\) holds exactly when the three equal-area caps are translates.
Equation (26), together with
\[
|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal S_R|\leq s_R,\qquad
|\mathcal U\cap\mathcal D_R|\leq d_R,
\]
now proves (7) and (8).  The definition of \(\eta\) in accepted fact
`0009896ebff12f31` gives \(\eta\geq3d_R\), which proves (9).

It remains to compute the triangle-cap modulus and prove compatibility.
Apply an affine map taking
\[
v_1=0,\qquad v_2=e_1,\qquad v_3=e_2.
\]
Area ratios and the ratio of both sides of (14) are unchanged.  After
independent translations, which do not change their Minkowski-sum area,
the three caps in (12) become
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{0,re_1,se_2\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{0,-re_1,s(e_2-e_1)\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{0,-we_2,w(e_1-e_2)\}.
\tag{32}
\]
Because \(s>r\), the counterclockwise merged edge list of their Minkowski
sum is
\[
(2r+w,0),\quad
(-r,s),\quad
(-s-w,s+w),\quad
(0,-s-w),\quad
(s-r,-s).
\tag{33}
\]
Starting at the origin, its vertices are
\[
0,\quad
(2r+w,0),\quad
(r+w,s),\quad
(r-s,2s+w),\quad
(r-s,s).
\tag{34}
\]
Shoelace expansion gives doubled area
\[
2rs+4sw+w^2+2s^2
=3w^2+4sw+2s^2,
\tag{35}
\]
where \(rs=w^2\) was used in the last equality.  The reference triangle
has doubled area \(1\), so (35) is also the area ratio of the Minkowski sum
to \(T\).  Since \(x=w^2|T|\), division by \(w^2\) proves (14).

Conditions (10)--(11) make the three corner-cap interiors disjoint.  Their
areas are \(rs|T|,rs|T|,w^2|T|\), all equal to
\[
\frac5{36}\frac{12q}{7}=\frac5{21}q.
\]
Their total area is \(5q/7\), so the remaining central cell has area \(q\).
Since \(rs=w^2\) and \(y=s/w>0\),
\[
r=\frac{w}{y},\qquad s=wy.
\]
Thus
\[
r<s<2r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1<y<\sqrt2,
\]
which proves (13).  Equation (15) follows from (14), and \(y>1\) gives
\[
3+4y+2y^2>9.
\]
The interval (16) is nonempty because
\[
\left(\frac75\right)^2=\frac{49}{25}<2.
\]
For \(y\geq7/5\),
\[
3+4y+2y^2
\geq
3+\frac{28}{5}+\frac{98}{25}
=\frac{313}{25},
\]
which proves (17).  On the same interval, (11) is consistent: indeed
\[
2r=\frac{2w}{y}
\leq\frac{10w}{7}
=\frac{5\sqrt5}{21}<1,
\]
and
\[
s+w=w(y+1)
<w(\sqrt2+1)<1.
\]
The last inequality follows by squaring positive quantities:
\[
5(1+\sqrt2)^2=15+10\sqrt2<36.
\]

We finally verify the affine common-plane condition rather than inferring
it from disjointness.  In the coordinates \((\xi,\zeta)\), the three cap
base lines in (12) have central halfplanes \(g_i\geq0\), with the functions
in (18).  The linear parts of the positive rescalings (19) are
\[
(s,r),\qquad (-s,r-s),\qquad (0,-(2r-s)),
\]
whose sum is zero.  Their pairwise determinants are nonzero because
\[
s>0,\qquad 2r-s>0.
\]
Their affine sum is the constant in (20).  Its second expression is
obtained by direct expansion, and it is strictly positive because
\[
2r-s>0,\qquad 1-w>0,\qquad s>0,\qquad 1-2r>0.
\]
Hence (21) is well defined.  It gives
\[
\sum_i\psi_i=-1,\qquad
\sum_i\widetilde q_i=-3,
\]
and (22) follows.  Multiplication by the negative common factor
\(-3/C\) changes \(g_i\geq0\) exactly into
\(\widetilde q_i\leq0\), so the three central halfplanes and the three
outer caps are precisely the required \(Q\)-sign cells.  Conditions
(10)--(11) exclude every cap overlap, so all other \(Q\)-cells have zero
area.  Thus the cutting functions have the exact common-plane affine
normalization and pairwise-nonparallel linear parts required by accepted
fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8`.

All calculations are affine invariant and use closed convex bodies; the
exact weak/strict cutting-line assignments differ only on null lines.
This covers every boundary in (1), (5), (11), and the closed lower endpoint
\(y=7/5\) in (16).  The necessary compatibility boundary
\(y=\sqrt2\), where the first two cap-base lines become parallel, is
explicitly excluded.
