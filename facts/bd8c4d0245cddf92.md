## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
polytopes, and let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3\}=\mathbb R^2.
\]
For cyclic \((i,j,k)\), let \(O_i\) be the intersection of \(f_j=0\) and
\(f_k=0\), and put
\[
\mathcal T=\{f_1\leq0,\ f_2\leq0,\ f_3\leq0\},\qquad
B_0=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Assume
\[
B_0\subseteq K,\qquad \mathcal T\subseteq K,
\]
and define the common augmented core
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(B_0\cup\mathcal T)\subseteq K.
\]
Also put
\[
C_i^0=\operatorname{conv}(B_0\cup\{O_i\}),
\]
which is the forced core obtained by applying accepted fact
b4a2962b4691ff67 directly to \(B_0\).
These are precisely the geometric inclusions furnished in the actual
common-plane setting by accepted fact 29dfdfc94477e532 and in the
finite-rank-two interior normalization by accepted fact
ed22242313aecf82.

For a compact set \(X\) and an affine function \(g\), write
\[
h_X(g)=\max_{x\in X}g(x).
\]
For each cyclic \((i,j,k)\), define the convex, piecewise-affine support
envelope
\[
\Psi_i(r)=h_{B_0}(f_k+r f_j)\quad(r\geq0),
\qquad
I_i=\{r>0:\Psi_i(r)\leq0\}.
\]
Then the following are equivalent: \(O_i\) is admissible for \(C_i^0\);
\(O_i\) is admissible for the common augmented core \(D\); and
\[
\boxed{
h_{B_0}(f_j)>0,\qquad h_{B_0}(f_k)>0,\qquad
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R} I_i\neq\varnothing .
}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus failure at corner \(i\) has the exact trichotomy
\[
h_{B_0}(f_j)\leq0,\qquad
h_{B_0}(f_k)\leq0,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R} I_i=\varnothing.
\tag{2}
\]
No rational-hexagon calibration is used in (1).

The criterion is computable directly from the actual endpoints, since for
every affine \(g\),
\[
\boxed{
h_{B_0}(g)=\frac{h_{A_0}(g)+h_{A_2}(g)}2.
}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, the following strict conditions give a nontrivial general
parameter region on which corner \(i\) is admissible:
\[
h_{B_0}(f_j)>0,\qquad h_{B_0}(f_k)>0,\qquad
h_{B_0}(f_k+r_0f_j)<0
\quad\text{for some }r_0>0.
\tag{4}
\]
All three inequalities in (4) are strict support inequalities and hence
persist under sufficiently small Hausdorff perturbations of both endpoint
polytopes.

Whenever (1) holds, set
\[
p_i=\inf I_i,\qquad q_i=\sup I_i.
\]
Then
\[
0<p_i<q_i<\infty,\qquad
\Psi_i(p_i)=\Psi_i(q_i)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
There are endpoint-support contacts \(F_i,E_i\in B_0\) such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
&f_j(F_i)=-a_i<0,\qquad f_k(F_i)=p_i a_i>0,\\
&f_j(E_i)=c_i>0,\qquad f_k(E_i)=-q_i c_i<0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
where \(F_i\) and \(E_i\) may be chosen farthest from \(O_i\) on their
respective support rays.  Write
\[
J=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_k)|,\qquad
\delta=|K\setminus D|,\qquad
e_\ell=|(K\setminus D)\cap\{f_\ell\leq0\}|.
\]
The corner parameters of accepted fact b4a2962b4691ff67 are then exactly
\[
\rho_{i,j}=\frac{p_i a_i^2}{2J},\qquad
\rho_{i,k}=\frac{q_i c_i^2}{2J},\qquad
\gamma_i=\frac{q_i a_i c_i}{J}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
e_j<\frac{p_i a_i^2}{2J},\qquad
e_k<\frac{q_i c_i^2}{2J}
\tag{8}
\]
imply the necessary inequality
\[
\boxed{
\frac{q_i a_i c_i}{J}\,(\delta-e_j-e_k)
\leq4e_je_k.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, this admissible corner closes the open region consisting of
(8) and
\[
\delta>e_j+e_k+\frac{4J\,e_je_k}{q_i a_i c_i}.
\tag{10}
\]

If (1) holds for all three cyclic choices, then \(D\) is the same core at
all three corners.  Hence, if all six threshold inequalities (8) hold and
\[
E=e_1+e_2+e_3,\qquad
\gamma=\min_i\frac{q_i a_i c_i}{J},
\]
then
\[
\delta\leq\frac{2E}{3}+\frac{4E^2}{9\gamma}.
\tag{11}
\]

The strict region (4) is nonempty even among genuine full-midpoint endpoint
models.  In the cyclic affine coordinates
\[
s=f_j,\qquad t=f_k,\qquad J=1,
\]
take
\[
B_\star=\operatorname{conv}\{(-2,1),(1,-2),(-2,-2)\}.
\]
Let
\[
A_0=O_i+\frac43(B_\star-O_i),\qquad
A_2=O_i+\frac23(B_\star-O_i).
\]
Then \((A_0+A_2)/2=B_\star\), and
\[
\Psi_i(r)=\max\{1-2r,-2+r\},\qquad
I_i=\left[\frac12,2\right].
\tag{12}
\]
Thus (4) holds at \(r_0=1\), with
\[
p_i=\frac12,\quad q_i=2,\quad
a_i=2,\quad c_i=1,\quad
\rho_{i,j}=\rho_{i,k}=1,\quad\gamma_i=4.
\tag{13}
\]
Taking any convex \(K\) containing
\(\operatorname{conv}(B_\star\cup\mathcal T)\) gives the required actual
midpoint-plus-central-triangle geometry.

## Proof

Accepted fact 29dfdfc94477e532 supplies the actual full midpoint inclusion
\((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\).  Accepted fact ed22242313aecf82 supplies, after
the finite-rank-two normalization, the rank-two affine arrangement and the
nondegenerate central triangle \(\mathcal T\subseteq K\).  Thus \(D\) is a
compact two-dimensional convex polytope contained in \(K\), and it contains
all three \(O_i\).

Fix a cyclic \((i,j,k)\), and use the affine coordinates
\[
s=f_j,\qquad t=f_k.
\]
Their Jacobian has absolute value \(J>0\), and \(O_i=(0,0)\).  In these
coordinates the central triangle has vertices
\[
(0,0),\qquad(-1,0),\qquad(0,-1).
\tag{14}
\]
For \(r>0\), the affine function
\[
\ell_r=t+rs
\]
is nonpositive on \(\mathcal T\), and its maximum there is \(0\), attained
at \(O_i\).

We first prove necessity in (1).  Suppose \(O_i\) is admissible for either
\(C_i^0\) or \(D\), and denote that core by \(C\).  Write its two adjacent
edge endpoints as in accepted fact
b4a2962b4691ff67:
\[
F_i=(-a,b),\qquad E_i=(c,-d),\qquad a,b,c,d>0.
\]
The supporting line through \(O_i,F_i\) is
\(\ell_{b/a}=0\), and the exterior-positive-axis condition puts \(C\) in
\(\ell_{b/a}\leq0\).  Similarly, the supporting line through
\(E_i,O_i\) is \(\ell_{d/c}=0\), and \(C\subseteq\{\ell_{d/c}\leq0\}\).
Since \(C\) is two-dimensional and the two displayed edges are distinct,
\[
\frac ba<\frac dc.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, \(E_i\in\{\ell_{b/a}\leq0\}\) gives \(b/a\leq d/c\), and equality
would make the two adjacent edges collinear.  Because \(B_0\subseteq C\),
\[
\Psi_i(b/a)\leq0,\qquad \Psi_i(d/c)\leq0.
\]
Convexity of \(\Psi_i\) then puts the nondegenerate interval
\([b/a,d/c]\) in \(I_i\).  Finally, \(F_i\in C\) has \(t>0\).  The only
generators added to \(B_0\) in either \(C_i^0\) or \(D\) have \(t\leq0\),
so the convex-hull representation of \(C\) forces
\(h_{B_0}(f_k)>0\).  The point \(E_i\) similarly forces
\(h_{B_0}(f_j)>0\).  This proves necessity.

Conversely, assume the three conditions in (1).  The function \(\Psi_i\)
is convex and continuous because it is the maximum over the compact
polytope \(B_0\) of affine functions of \(r\).  Its sublevel set \(I_i\)
is therefore an interval.  Since
\[
\Psi_i(0)=h_{B_0}(f_k)>0,
\]
its left endpoint \(p_i\) is positive.  Moreover,
\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi_i(r)}r=h_{B_0}(f_j)>0,
\]
so its right endpoint \(q_i\) is finite.  Nonempty interior gives
\(p_i<q_i\), and continuity gives (5).

Choose a sequence \(r_n\uparrow p_i\) with \(r_n<p_i\).  Then
\(\Psi_i(r_n)>0\).  A fixed vertex of \(B_0\) maximizes
\(\ell_{r_n}\) along a subsequence; call it \(F_i\).  Passing to the limit
gives \(\ell_{p_i}(F_i)=0\).  Also,
\[
0<\ell_{r_n}(F_i)-\ell_{p_i}(F_i)
=(r_n-p_i)f_j(F_i),
\]
so \(f_j(F_i)<0\).  Hence \(F_i\) has the first sign pattern in (6).
The identical argument with a sequence \(r_n\downarrow q_i\),
\(r_n>q_i\), gives a contact \(E_i\) with \(f_j(E_i)>0\) and the second
sign pattern in (6).  Choosing the farthest such contacts gives the
endpoints of the two exposed faces.

Because \(\Psi_i(p_i)=\Psi_i(q_i)=0\), the body \(B_0\), and hence \(D\),
lies in
\[
t+p_i s\leq0,\qquad t+q_i s\leq0.
\tag{16}
\]
The inclusion for \(\mathcal T\) follows directly from (14).  Since
\(0<p_i<q_i\), the intersection in (16) is a pointed cone whose boundary
rays have respectively the sign patterns
\[
s<0<t,\qquad t<0<s.
\]
The corresponding exposed faces of \(D\) are the nondegenerate segments
\([O_i,F_i]\) and \([E_i,O_i]\).  Thus \(O_i\) is a boundary vertex and
these are its adjacent edges.  The open positive quadrant is disjoint from
(16).  The positive \(t\)-axis is on the exterior side of
\(t+p_i s=0\), and the positive \(s\)-axis is on the exterior side of
\(t+q_i s=0\).  All admissibility requirements of accepted fact
b4a2962b4691ff67 are therefore satisfied for \(D\).  The two support
contacts lie in \(B_0\), so the same exposed edges and the same pointed
cone occur in the smaller core \(C_i^0\).  Thus \(C_i^0\) is admissible as
well.  This proves the equivalence and the exact failure trichotomy (2).

For an affine \(g\),
\[
g\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
=\frac{g(x_0)+g(x_2)}2.
\]
Maximizing independently over \(x_0\in A_0\) and \(x_2\in A_2\) proves
(3).  If (4) holds, continuity makes \(\Psi_i<0\) on an interval around
\(r_0\), so (1) applies.  The standard continuity of support functions
under Hausdorff convergence proves the asserted openness.

Equations (6) identify the notation of accepted fact
b4a2962b4691ff67 as
\[
b_i=p_i a_i,\qquad d_i=q_i c_i.
\]
Substitution into that fact's definitions of
\(\rho_{i,j},\rho_{i,k},\gamma_i\) proves (7), and its corner inequality
proves (8)--(10).  Since \(D\) contains every \(O_i\), applying the
common-core conclusion of the same accepted fact proves (11).

For the displayed endpoint family, positive homothetic copies of a convex
body obey
\[
\frac{\frac43B_\star+\frac23B_\star}{2}=B_\star,
\]
with homotheties taken about \(O_i\).  Evaluation at the three vertices
gives (12); (13) follows immediately.  This also proves directly that the
strict region (4) is nonempty.
