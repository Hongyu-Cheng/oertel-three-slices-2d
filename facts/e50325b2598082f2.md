## Statement

Use spatial coordinates \((x,u,v)\), where \(x\) is the integer-height coordinate. For every real \(0<\varepsilon\leq3/5\), let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\varepsilon\Delta,
\]
and let \(C\) be the convex hull of the three sets \(\{i\}\times A_i\), \(i=0,1,2\). These are exactly the integer-height slices of \(C\). They have areas \(a_0=a_1=1/2\), \(a_2=\varepsilon^2/2\), and \(T=(2+\varepsilon^2)/2\). They belong to the strict residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`: with that fact's normalization \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(r=m/M\), and \(s=a_1/M\), one has
\[
0<r=\varepsilon^2\leq\frac9{25}<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad 1-r<s=1<1+r.
\]

Let \(g_i\) be the centroid of \(A_i\). In the segment notation of accepted fact `009a0ffca7cbccd8`, the alias is \(c_i=g_i\), and the objects \(z_0=(g_0+g_2)/2\), \(\delta=g_1-z_0\), and \(z_t=z_0+t\delta\) have exactly their meanings in that fact. For every \(t\in[0,1]\), define
\[
\gamma_t=\frac{1+\varepsilon+t(1-\varepsilon)}3,
\qquad
H_t=\{(x,u,v):u+v\leq\gamma_t x\}.
\]
The closed halfspace \(H_t\) has \((1,z_t)\) on its boundary, and its exact slice mass is
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_t)
=\frac12\left(\gamma_t^2+\varepsilon^2\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, \(h_S((1,z_t))\geq2T/9\) requires
\[
1-t\leq
\frac{2\left(1-\sqrt{1-7\varepsilon^2/4}\right)}{1-\varepsilon}.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a necessary condition, without a claim that it is sufficient for the depth bound. In particular, any area-only rule \(t=t(a_0,a_1,a_2)\in[0,1]\) that succeeds universally on the strict residual strip must obey
\[
\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{1-t(1/2,1/2,\varepsilon^2/2)}{\varepsilon^2}
\leq\frac74.
\tag{3}
\]

For the selector in the registered subgoal,
\[
z=\frac{a_1g_1+\sqrt{a_0a_2}(g_0+g_2)}{a_1+2\sqrt{a_0a_2}},
\]
take \(\varepsilon=1/10\). Then \(z=(37/120,37/120)\in A_1\), and the exact bad halfspace is
\[
H=\{(x,u,v):u+v\leq37x/60\}.
\]
It gives
\[
h_S((1,z))\leq\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)
=\frac{281}{1440}
<\frac{67}{300}=\frac{2T}{9},
\qquad
\frac{2T}{9}-\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)=\frac{203}{7200}>0.
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof

The planar Minkowski identity \(\Delta+\varepsilon\Delta=(1+\varepsilon)\Delta\) follows from convexity: dividing any point in the left side by \(1+\varepsilon\) gives a convex combination of two points of \(\Delta\), and the reverse inclusion follows by using the same point twice. Thus
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{5}
\]
To check the actual slices directly, group a convex combination defining a point of \(C\) by heights, giving weights \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\geq0\) whose sum is one. A point at height one satisfies \(\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=1\), hence \(\lambda_0=\lambda_2\). Its planar component is a convex combination of a point of \(A_1\) and a point of \((A_0+A_2)/2\), so (5) places it in \(A_1\). The reverse inclusion holds by the definition of \(C\). At height zero or two only the corresponding height can receive positive weight, so those sections are exactly \(A_0\) and \(A_2\). The polytope has no points with height outside \([0,2]\), establishing the claimed integer sections. Each slice is two-dimensional because \(\varepsilon>0\).

Area scales quadratically, which gives the stated areas and \(r=\varepsilon^2\), \(s=1\). The inequality \(9/25<(27-2\sqrt{26})/25\) is equivalent to \(\sqrt{26}<9\). Because \(0<r\leq9/25<1/2\), the two boundary functions in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` are \(L(r)=1-r\) and \(U(r)=1+r\). Both inequalities \(L(r)<1<U(r)\) are strict. This verifies the strict residual condition for the actual polygons, including \(\varepsilon=3/5\).

Write \(g=(1/3,1/3)\). The triangle centroid formula gives \(g_0=g_1=g\) and \(g_2=\varepsilon g\). With the precise alias to accepted fact `009a0ffca7cbccd8` given in the statement, this yields
\[
z_0=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}g,\qquad
\delta=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}g,\qquad
z_t=\frac{1+\varepsilon+t(1-\varepsilon)}2g
=\left(\frac{\gamma_t}{2},\frac{\gamma_t}{2}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Here \((1+\varepsilon)/3\leq\gamma_t\leq2/3\), so \(z_t\in\Delta\), and (6) shows that \((1,z_t)\in\partial H_t\).

At height zero, \(H_t\) cuts \(A_0\) by \(u+v\leq0\), leaving only the origin, of planar area zero. At height one, the cap is exactly the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(\gamma_t,0),(0,\gamma_t)\},
\]
whose area is \(\gamma_t^2/2\), because \(0<\gamma_t\leq2/3<1\). At height two, every point of \(A_2\) satisfies \(u+v\leq\varepsilon\), while
\[
2\gamma_t\geq\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)}3\geq\varepsilon;
\]
the final inequality holds because \(\varepsilon\leq3/5<2\). Therefore \(H_t\) contains all of \(A_2\), of area \(\varepsilon^2/2\). Summing these three exact contributions proves (1). Since \(H_t\) is an admissible closed halfspace in the definition of depth, it bounds that depth from above.

If the depth were at least \(2T/9\), (1) would imply
\[
\gamma_t^2+\varepsilon^2\geq\frac{2(2+\varepsilon^2)}9,
\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
9\gamma_t^2\geq4-7\varepsilon^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Both \(\gamma_t\) and \(4-7\varepsilon^2\) are positive on the stated parameter range. Taking nonnegative square roots in (7) and using
\[
\frac{3\gamma_t}{2}=1-\frac{(1-t)(1-\varepsilon)}2
\]
therefore gives (2), with the inequality direction preserved because \(1-\varepsilon>0\). Rationalizing its right-hand side yields
\[
\frac{2\left(1-\sqrt{1-7\varepsilon^2/4}\right)}{1-\varepsilon}
=\frac{(7/2)\varepsilon^2}
{(1-\varepsilon)\left(1+\sqrt{1-7\varepsilon^2/4}\right)}.
\tag{8}
\]
The factor multiplying \(\varepsilon^2\) on the right tends to \(7/4\). Applying (2) to each member of the family proves (3). In particular the midpoint weight is necessarily \(O(\varepsilon^2)=O(r)\) along these area triples.

For the proposed selector, \(\sqrt{a_0a_2}=\varepsilon/2\), so its point is
\[
z=\frac{1+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2}{1+2\varepsilon}g
=z_t\quad\text{with}\quad t=\frac1{1+2\varepsilon}.
\tag{9}
\]
Its midpoint weight \(1-t=2\varepsilon/(1+2\varepsilon)\) thus has order \(\varepsilon\), incompatible with the necessary scale (8) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). To give an entirely rational witness without relying on that asymptotic comparison, set \(\varepsilon=1/10\). Equation (9) gives \(t=5/6\) and \(z=(37/40)g=(37/120,37/120)\), hence \(\gamma_t=37/60\) and \(H_t=H\). The three cap areas are exactly
\[
0,\qquad \frac12\left(\frac{37}{60}\right)^2=\frac{1369}{7200},
\qquad\frac1{200}.
\]
Their sum is \(1405/7200=281/1440\). Meanwhile \(T=201/200\), so \(2T/9=67/300=1608/7200\). Their positive difference is \(203/7200\), proving (4).

As a separate arithmetic and geometry check, SageMath 10.8 over \(\mathbb Q\) verified the convex-hull integer sections, the selected point, and the clipped planar vertices for \(\varepsilon=1/10\). The cap vertex sets were \(\{(0,0)\}\), \(\{(0,0),(0,37/60),(37/60,0)\}\), and \(\{(0,0),(0,1/10),(1/10,0)\}\); the exact areas and gap were those displayed above. The proof does not depend on this computational check.
