## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex bodies, let \(a_h=|A_h|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and
\[
S=\bigcup_{h=0}^2(\{h\}\times A_h).
\]
For the normalized certificate supplied by accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, write
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\leq0\},\qquad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
where the \(f_i\) are affine and \(s_i\in\mathbb R\). Put
\(\sigma=s_1+s_2+s_3\). Then, at height \(h\in\{0,1,2\}\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\bigr)
=-1+\sigma(h-1).
\]
Consequently, if \(|\sigma|<1\), the three open sections
\[
\{w:f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)<0\},\qquad i=1,2,3,
\]
cover \(\mathbb R^2\) separately for every \(h=0,1,2\). If
\(|\sigma|\leq1\), the corresponding three closed sections cover
\(\mathbb R^2\) at every height, and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)\geq T,\qquad
\max_i\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)\geq\frac T3>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
At \(\sigma=1\), the open-cover conclusion holds at heights \(0,1\), while
the height-\(2\) conclusion asserted here is only a closed cover. At
\(\sigma=-1\), the height-\(0\) conclusion asserted here is only a closed
cover, while the open-cover conclusion holds at heights \(1,2\). More
generally, \(\sigma\geq1\) guarantees an open endpoint cover at height \(0\),
and \(\sigma\leq-1\) guarantees one at height \(2\). Thus any certificate
with \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)<2T/9\) for all \(i\) has
\(|\sigma|>1\), which implies the requested \(|\sigma|\geq1\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` supplies the displayed normalized
halfspaces, the identity \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), and, in the global
counterexample application, their strict-low masses. For
\[
g_{i,h}(w)=f_i(w)+s_i(h-1),
\]
direct summation gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3g_{i,0}(w)=-1-\sigma,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3g_{i,1}(w)=-1,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3g_{i,2}(w)=-1+\sigma. \tag{1}
\]
Whenever one of these sums is strictly negative, not all three summands can
be nonnegative. Hence, for every \(w\), some \(g_{i,h}(w)<0\), so the three
open sections cover \(\mathbb R^2\). If the sum is merely nonpositive, not
all three summands can be strictly positive, so some \(g_{i,h}(w)\leq0\);
this gives a cover by the closed sections. Thus strict negativity is used
exactly for the open-cover claim, while equality gives only the closed-cover
claim.

If \(|\sigma|<1\), all three quantities in (1) are strictly negative, proving
the requested three open covers. If \(|\sigma|\leq1\), all three are
nonpositive, proving the three closed covers. The boundary values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&h=0&h=1&h=2\\ \hline
\sigma=1&-2&-1&0\\
\sigma=-1&0&-1&-2.
\end{array}
\]
This proves precisely the asserted open-versus-closed behavior at
\(\sigma=\pm1\), without asserting an open cover in either zero entry.
Also, if \(\sigma\geq1\), then \(-1-\sigma<0\), giving the open endpoint
cover at height \(0\); if \(\sigma\leq-1\), then \(-1+\sigma<0\), giving the
open endpoint cover at height \(2\).

For \(h=0,1,2\) and \(i=1,2,3\), set
\[
C_{h,i}=A_h\cap\{w:g_{i,h}(w)\leq0\}.
\]
When \(|\sigma|\leq1\), the closed-cover conclusion gives
\(A_h=\bigcup_iC_{h,i}\). Therefore finite subadditivity of planar area
gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3|C_{h,i}|\geq a_h
\qquad(h=0,1,2). \tag{2}
\]
The three pieces of \(S\cap H_i\) lie in three distinct parallel planes.
They are Borel sets, planar area on each plane equals its two-dimensional
Hausdorff measure, and finite additivity therefore gives
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)=\sum_{h=0}^2|C_{h,i}|. \tag{3}
\]
Summing (3), changing the order of the two finite sums, and using (2) yields
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
=\sum_{h=0}^2\sum_{i=1}^3|C_{h,i}|
\geq\sum_{h=0}^2a_h=T. \tag{4}
\]
Hence one of the three masses is at least \(T/3\). Since every \(A_h\) has
positive area, \(T>0\), and \(T/3>2T/9\).

If all three masses were strictly below \(2T/9\), their sum would be
strictly below \(2T/3\), contradicting (4). Therefore
\(|\sigma|\leq1\) is impossible for a strict-low certificate. This proves
the stronger \(|\sigma|>1\), and in particular the required
\(|\sigma|\geq1\).
