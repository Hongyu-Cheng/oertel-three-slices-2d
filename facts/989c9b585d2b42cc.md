## Proof

We first justify the full-section locations.  Every positive-area cell
in (3) contains an interior point of \(A\) with its displayed strict
sign pattern.

If a point of \(A\cap\{X=0\}\) had \(Y<0\), moving it slightly toward
an interior point of \(P_j^\circ\) would produce an interior point in
the unsupported chamber \(P_{j\ell}^\circ\).  If it had \(Y>\delta\),
moving it slightly toward an interior point of \(P_0^\circ\) would
produce an interior point in the unsupported chamber \(P_k^\circ\).
Thus this section lies in \(0\le Y\le\delta\).  Positive area in
\(P_j^\circ,P_0^\circ\) places interior points on both sides of
\(X=0\), so its section is a nondegenerate interval.

The same argument on \(Y=0\), using \(P_\ell^\circ\) when \(X<0\)
and \(P_0^\circ\) when \(X>\delta\), excludes the unsupported chambers
\(P_{j\ell}^\circ\) and \(P_k^\circ\).  Hence this section lies in
\(0\le X\le\delta\), and positive area in
\(P_0^\circ,P_\ell^\circ\) makes it nondegenerate.

Finally, take a point on \(p_k=0\) with \(Y>0\).  If \(X>0\), moving it
slightly toward an interior point of \(P_{k\ell}^\circ\) enters
\(P_k^\circ\).  If \(X<0\), the same motion enters
\(P_{jk}^\circ\); at \(X=0\) it enters \(P_k^\circ\).  All are
unsupported.  Therefore \(Y\le0\) on the section, and (1) gives
\(X=\delta-Y\ge\delta\).  Positive area in
\(P_\ell^\circ,P_{k\ell}^\circ\) makes this section nondegenerate.
This proves (6)-(8), including endpoints by closure.

For the perspective relation, join
\[
(0,y)\in A\cap\{X=0\}
\quad\hbox{to}\quad
(\delta+t,-t)\in A\cap\{p_k=0\}.
\]
The joining segment meets \(Y=0\) at first coordinate
\[
F(y,t)=\frac{y(\delta+t)}{y+t}.
\tag{38}
\]
On \(0\le y\le\delta,t\ge0\),
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}
=\frac{t(\delta+t)}{(y+t)^2}\ge0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}
=\frac{y(y-\delta)}{(y+t)^2}\le0.
\tag{39}
\]
Because \(J,K>0\), both endpoint values in (10) have positive
denominators.  Their entire interval lies in the full horizontal
section, proving (10).  If \(\alpha=\gamma=0\), the two values are
\(0\) and \(\delta\), so (10) itself forces \(\beta=0,H=\delta\).

The convex hull of the vertical and horizontal sections lies in the
closed \(P_0\)-chamber.  Its coordinate area is
\[
\frac12\bigl((\beta+H)(\alpha+J)-\beta\alpha\bigr)
=\frac12(\beta J+\alpha H+JH).
\tag{40}
\]
The chamber has coordinate area \(\Delta b\), proving (11).

For (12), use
\[
R=-Y,\qquad T=p_k=\delta-X-Y.
\]
The diagonal section is
\[
T=0,\qquad R\in[\gamma,\gamma+K],
\]
and the horizontal section is
\[
R=0,\qquad T\in[\tau,\tau+H].
\]
Their hull lies in the closed \(P_\ell\)-chamber and has coordinate
area
\[
\frac12\bigl(\gamma H+\tau K+KH\bigr)
=\frac12\bigl(\gamma H+K(\delta-\beta)\bigr).
\tag{41}
\]
Comparison with \(\Delta c\) proves (12).

For (13), use
\[
U=X,\qquad T=p_k.
\]
The vertical section is
\[
U=0,\qquad
T\in[\delta-\alpha-J,\delta-\alpha],
\]
and the diagonal section is
\[
T=0,\qquad
U\in[\delta+\gamma,\delta+\gamma+K].
\]
Their hull lies in
\[
A\cap\{p_j\ge0,p_k\ge0\}
=\overline{P_0}\cup\overline{P_\ell}

up to cutting lines.  Its coordinate area is
\[
\frac12\bigl((\delta+\gamma)J
+(\delta-\alpha-J)K+JK\bigr)
=\frac12\bigl((\delta+\gamma)J+K(\delta-\alpha)\bigr).
\tag{42}
\]
The containing union has coordinate area \(\Delta(b+c)\), proving
(13).

We now prove the boundary-completion lemma used twice in (15).  Let
\(C\) be a compact convex polygon in affine coordinates \((R,T)\)
with the same coordinate-area factor \(\Delta\).  Suppose its full
sections are
\[
C\cap\{T=0\}
=\{(r,0):\gamma_0\le r\le\gamma_0+K_0\},
\tag{43}
\]
\[
C\cap\{R=0\}
=\{(0,t):\tau_0\le t\le\tau_0+H_0\},
\tag{44}
\]
where \(\gamma_0,\tau_0\ge0\) and \(K_0,H_0>0\).  Let \(D_0\) be the
physical area of \(C\cap\{T\le0\}\).  Then
\[
2\Delta D_0(\gamma_0H_0-\tau_0K_0)_+
\le K_0^2\tau_0(\tau_0+H_0).
\tag{45}
\]

Assume first \(\tau_0>0\).  Let \(W(T)\) be the full \(R\)-chord
length of \(C\).  The convex hull of (43)-(44) gives, for
\(0\le T\le\tau_0\),
\[
W(T)\ge
(\gamma_0+K_0)
\left(1-\frac{T}{\tau_0+H_0}\right)
-
\gamma_0\left(1-\frac{T}{\tau_0}\right).
\tag{46}
\]
Since \(W(0)=K_0\), the right derivative satisfies
\[
W'_+(0)\ge
\frac{\gamma_0H_0-\tau_0K_0}
{\tau_0(\tau_0+H_0)}.
\tag{47}
\]
If the numerator is nonpositive, (45) is automatic.  If it is
positive, call the right side of (47) \(G>0\).  The chord profile
\(W\) is nonnegative, concave, and piecewise affine.  Its right
derivative at zero is a supergradient, so for \(T\le0\),
\[
W(T)\le K_0+W'_+(0)T\le K_0+GT.
\tag{48}
\]
Consequently the negative-side support cannot extend below
\(-K_0/W'_+(0)\), and
\[
\Delta D_0
\le
\int_{-K_0/W'_+(0)}^0
\bigl(K_0+W'_+(0)T\bigr)\,dT
=\frac{K_0^2}{2W'_+(0)}
\le\frac{K_0^2}{2G}.
\tag{49}
\]
Substitution of \(G\) proves (45).  If \(\tau_0=0\), (44) contains
\((0,0)\).  Since (43) is the full \(T=0\) section and
\(\gamma_0\ge0\), it follows that \(\gamma_0=0\), and (45) is
\(0\le0\).  This proves the lemma, including the boundary.
