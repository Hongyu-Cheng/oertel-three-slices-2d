## Statement

Put
\[
E=\frac{16072081}{640000000}.
\]
The full nine-vertex system under consideration has variables
\[
x,a,b,h,c,s,t,g,G,r,R,y>0,\qquad
0<h<12,\qquad 0<c<12-h,
\]
and definitions
\[
L=12-h,\qquad H=h+c+s+t,\qquad
z=\frac{(12+x)b}{a},\qquad
Y=\frac{L(s+t)}c.
\]
Its breakpoint conditions are
\[
h+c<h+c+s<H,\qquad y>\frac{Ls}{c}.
\]
Its six positive coordinate cell areas are required to satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_1&=\frac{xg}{2},\\
a_{13}&=12x+\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{(12+x)a}{2},\\
a_3&=\frac{x(R+r)+R(a+b)+(12+x)b}{2},\\
a_{23}&=\frac{hr}{2},\\
a_{123}&=12h-\frac{h^2}{2}+\frac{Lc}{2},\\
a_{12}
&=\frac{h(g+G)}2
+\frac{(c+s)(G+y)}2
+\frac{t(y+Y)}2
-\frac{L(s+t)^2}{2c}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Its endpoint-convexity conditions are
\[
-\frac{12+x}{a}
\le 1-\frac gx
\le 1-\frac{G-g}{h}
\le 1-\frac{y-G}{c+s}
\le 1-\frac{Y-y}{t}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\frac{R-z}{a+b}
\ge \frac{r-R}{x}
\ge-\frac rh
\ge1-\frac Lc.
\]
The full coordinatewise ten-percent box is
\[
\frac9{5000}E\le a_1,a_{23}\le\frac{11}{5000}E,\qquad
\frac{53991}{5000}E\le a_3\le\frac{65989}{5000}E,
\]
\[
\frac{17991}{5000}E\le a_{12}\le\frac{21989}{5000}E,\qquad
\frac{27}{10}E\le a_{13}\le\frac{33}{10}E,\qquad
\frac9{10}E\le a_{123}\le\frac{11}{10}E.
\]
In particular, every solution of the declared full system in this
box satisfies
\[
\frac9{5000}E\le a_1\le\frac{11}{5000}E,\qquad
a_{13}\ge\frac{27}{10}E,\qquad
a_{123}\le\frac{11}{10}E.
\tag{2}
\]
Any positive variables satisfying the four displayed area identities
for \(a_1,a_{13},a_{123},a_{12}\), the left chain (1), and (2) obey
\[
a_{12}
<
\frac{8453526281}{99008000000}
<
\frac9{100}.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, upon putting \(d=s+t\) and
\[
\Delta_{\rm kink}
=\frac{(c+d)y-tG-(c+s)Y}{2},
\]
they obey
\[
0\le\Delta_{\rm kink}<\frac{16}{595}
<\frac3{40}
<
\frac{
38147810777079388186060782356906840980757
}{
477534754162653027653180705158400000000000
}.
\tag{4}
\]
But every full-box solution also satisfies
\[
a_{12}\ge\frac{17991}{5000}E
=\frac9{100}
+\frac{1152809271}{3200000000000}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the declared full nine-vertex system has no point in the
ten-percent box.  Its exact target is infeasible as well.

## Proof

The implication from the declared full system to the subsystem used
below is immediate term by term, but we record it explicitly.  Full
system positivity gives
\(x,a,h,c,s,t,g,G,y>0\), \(0<h<12\), and \(0<c<12-h\).
Its definitions give the same \(L\), \(d=s+t\), and
\(Y=Ld/c\).  Its six area equations contain, without alteration, the
four displayed equations for \(a_1,a_{13},a_{123},a_{12}\).  Its
first endpoint-convexity condition is exactly (1).  Finally, its
box bounds for \(a_1,a_{13},a_{123}\) imply (2), and its box lower
bound for \(a_{12}\) is exactly (5).  Hence a contradiction from
(1)--(2) and the four selected area equations to (5) proves
infeasibility of the full system; the unused breakpoint condition,
right slope chain, and \(a_3,a_{23}\) equations can only further
restrict it.

Set \(x_0=7/25000\).  The \(a_1\)- and \(a_{13}\)-identities give
\[
g=\frac{2a_1}{x},\qquad
a=\frac{2a_{13}-24x-x^2}{12+x}.
\]
The first inequality in (1), after multiplication by positive
denominators, therefore gives
\[
(2a_{13}-24x-x^2)(2a_1-x^2)
\le x^2(12+x)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(0<x\le x_0\), the two factors on the left are bounded below by
\[
2\frac{27}{10}E-24x_0-x_0^2
=\frac{10311048403}{80000000000}>0,
\]
\[
2\frac9{5000}E-x_0^2
=\frac{144523289}{1600000000000}>0.
\]
Both lower-bound factors decrease with \(x\), whereas
\(x^2(12+x)^2\) increases with \(x\).  At \(x=x_0\), the lower
bound for the left side minus the right side is
\[
\frac{45050390908997787}
{128000000000000000000000}>0,
\]
contradicting (6).  Hence
\[
x>\frac7{25000}.
\tag{7}
\]

Since \(c>0\),
\[
a_{123}>12h-\frac{h^2}{2}.
\]
The right side is increasing on \(0<h<12\), and
\[
12\frac1{400}-\frac1{2\cdot400^2}-\frac{11}{10}E
=\frac{15187109}{6400000000}>0.
\]
Thus
\[
h<\frac1{400},\qquad L>\frac{4799}{400}.
\tag{8}
\]

Put
\[
q=\frac{L}{c},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{G-g}{h},\qquad
\lambda=q-\kappa.
\]
Because \(12h-h^2/2>0\), the \(a_{123}\)-identity and (2) imply
\[
\frac{Lc}{2}<a_{123}\le\frac{11}{10}E.
\]
Consequently
\[
q>\frac{(4799/400)^2}{2(11E/10)}
=\frac{460608020000}{176792891}
=2600+\frac{946503400}{176792891}
>2600.
\tag{9}
\]
The second inequality in (1) gives \(\kappa\le g/x\).  From (2) and
(7),
\[
\kappa\le\frac gx=\frac{2a_1}{x^2}
<
\frac{176792891}{125440}
=1410-\frac{77509}{125440}
<1410,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
g=\frac{2a_1}{x}
<
\frac{176792891}{448000000}
=\frac25-\frac{2407109}{448000000}
<\frac25,
\tag{11}
\]
and, by (7)--(8),
\[
\frac hx<\frac{125}{14}.
\tag{12}
\]
Therefore
\[
G=g+h\kappa
\le g\left(1+\frac hx\right)
<\frac25\left(1+\frac{125}{14}\right)
=\frac{139}{35}<4.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) now give
\[
\lambda>1190,\qquad L+G<16.
\tag{14}
\]

Put \(p=c+d=c+s+t\).  The third inequality in (1) gives
\[
y\le G+(c+s)\kappa,
\tag{15}
\]
while the last inequality gives
\[
t(y-G)\ge(c+s)(Y-y),
\]
which is exactly \(\Delta_{\rm kink}\ge0\).  Using (15) and
\(Y=Ld/c\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\Delta_{\rm kink}
&\le(c+s)\left(G+p\kappa-\frac{Ld}{c}\right)\\
&=(c+s)\bigl(L+G-\lambda p\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The bracket in (16) is nonnegative because its positive prefactor
upper-bounds the nonnegative left side.  Since \(c+s\le p\),
\[
2\Delta_{\rm kink}
\le p(L+G-\lambda p)
\le\frac{(L+G)^2}{4\lambda}.
\]
Together with (14), this proves
\[
\Delta_{\rm kink}<\frac{16^2}{8\cdot1190}
=\frac{16}{595}.
\tag{17}
\]
For the rational number \(K\) on the far right of (4), exact
subtraction gives
\[
K-\frac3{40}
=
\frac{
2332704214880411112072229470026840980757
}{
477534754162653027653180705158400000000000
}>0,
\]
and
\[
\frac3{40}-\frac{16}{595}=\frac{229}{4760}>0.
\]
This proves (4).

It remains to prove the full cell-area contradiction without using
any earlier profile bound.  Direct rearrangement of the
\(a_{12}\)-identity gives
\[
a_{12}
=\frac{h(g+G)}2+\frac{Gp+Ld}{2}+\Delta_{\rm kink}.
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (16) with \(c+s\le p\), and then using \(d=p-c\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{12}
&\le
\frac{h(g+G)}2
+(L+G)p-\frac{\lambda p^2}{2}-\frac{Lc}{2}\\
&\le
\frac{h(g+G)}2
+\frac{(L+G)^2}{2\lambda}-\frac{Lc}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The second line is the maximum of the displayed quadratic in \(p\).
By (8), (11), and (13),
\[
\frac{h(g+G)}2<\frac{11}{2000}.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(q=L/c>2600\), equations (9)--(14) give
\[
\frac{(L+G)^2}{2\lambda}-\frac{Lc}{2}
<
\frac{128}{q-1410}
-\frac{(4799/400)^2}{2q}.
\tag{21}
\]
The function
\[
F(q)=\frac{128}{q-1410}
-\frac{(4799/400)^2}{2q}
\]
is strictly decreasing for \(q>2600\).  Indeed,
\[
F'(q)
=-\frac{128}{(q-1410)^2}
+\frac{(4799/400)^2}{2q^2}<0,
\]
because
\[
\frac{4799}{400}(q-1410)<16q.
\]
Thus (19)--(21) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{12}
&<
\frac{11}{2000}
+\frac{128}{1190}
-\frac{(4799/400)^2}{5200}\\
&=
\frac{8453526281}{99008000000}
=\frac9{100}
-\frac{457193719}{99008000000}
<\frac9{100}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (3).  Equation (5) contradicts (3), so the full
ten-percent box is infeasible.  In the order
\((a_1,a_3,a_{12},a_{13},a_{23},a_{123})/E\), the exact target is
\[
\left(\frac1{500},\frac{5999}{500},\frac{1999}{500},
3,\frac1{500},1\right)
\]
and it lies in the displayed box because
\[
9<10<11,\quad
53991<59990<65989,\quad
17991<19990<21989,
\]
\[
\frac{27}{10}<3<\frac{33}{10},\qquad
\frac9{10}<1<\frac{11}{10}.
\]
Thus the exact target is infeasible as well.
