## Statement

Let \(s,a,b,\delta>0\).  Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact
two-dimensional convex body such that
\[
P\subseteq\{(x,y):x+y\leq\delta\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume that, for some \(0<u<\delta\), the point
\[
r=(u,\delta-u)
\tag{2}
\]
belongs to \(P\).  Assume also that there are explicitly specified points
\(p_x,p_y\in P\) satisfying
\[
x(p_x)=-a<0,\qquad y(p_y)=-b<0.
\tag{3}
\]
If
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},
\tag{4}
\]
then, on putting
\[
d_x=-\min_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
d_y=-\min_{p\in P}y(p),
\tag{5}
\]
one has
\[
\delta\geq d_x+d_y\geq a+b.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, there do not exist \(s,a,b,\delta>0\), rational points
\[
r=(u,\delta-u),\quad p_x,\quad p_y,\quad q_1,\quad q_2
\tag{7}
\]
which are the five distinct vertices of a strictly convex rational
pentagon \(P\), and which satisfy all of the following conditions:
\[
0<u<\delta,\qquad
x(p_x)=-a<0,\qquad
y(p_y)=-b<0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
x(q_i)>0,\qquad y(q_i)>0\qquad(i=1,2),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
x(v)+y(v)<\delta
\quad\text{for every }v\in\{p_x,p_y,q_1,q_2\},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\delta<a+b.
\tag{12}
\]
Here (10), together with \(x(r)+y(r)=\delta\), says explicitly that
\(r\) is the unique support vertex of \(P\) for the functional \(x+y\).

## Proof

We prove the convex-body implication.  The explicitly quantified points in
(3) give
\[
d_x\geq a>0,\qquad d_y\geq b>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Write
\[
M_x=\max_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
M_y=\max_{p\in P}y(p).
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(p\in P\) has \(y(p)\geq-d_y\) and \(x(p)\geq-d_x\).  Combining
these inequalities with (1) gives
\[
M_x\leq\delta+d_y,\qquad
M_y\leq\delta+d_x.
\tag{15}
\]

Choose \(v_x\in P\) with \(x(v_x)=-d_x\), and set
\[
\lambda_x=\frac{d_x}{d_x+M_x}.
\tag{16}
\]
The point \(r\) in (2) has positive \(x\)-coordinate, so
\(0<\lambda_x<1\).  Convexity gives
\[
v_x+\lambda_x(P-v_x)\subseteq P.
\tag{17}
\]
For every \(p\in P\), the \(x\)-coordinate of its image in (17) satisfies
\[
-d_x+\lambda_x\bigl(x(p)+d_x\bigr)
\leq-d_x+\lambda_x(M_x+d_x)=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus the homothetic copy in (17) is contained in
\(P\cap\{x\leq0\}\).  Since its area is \(\lambda_x^2|P|\), (4) implies
\[
\frac{s}{8}
\geq16s\left(\frac{d_x}{d_x+M_x}\right)^2,
\qquad
(d_x+M_x)^2\geq128d_x^2.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(M_x\leq10d_x\), the last left side is at most
\(121d_x^2<128d_x^2\).  Hence
\[
M_x>10d_x.
\tag{20}
\]

Likewise, choose \(v_y\in P\) with \(y(v_y)=-d_y\) and use the homothetic
copy with ratio \(d_y/(d_y+M_y)\).  The point \(r\) has positive
\(y\)-coordinate, and the copy lies in \(P\cap\{y\leq0\}\).  The last two
equalities in (4) therefore give
\[
\frac{s}{5}
\geq16s\left(\frac{d_y}{d_y+M_y}\right)^2,
\qquad
(d_y+M_y)^2\geq80d_y^2.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(M_y\leq7d_y\), the last left side is at most
\(64d_y^2<80d_y^2\).  Hence
\[
M_y>7d_y.
\tag{22}
\]

Suppose that \(\delta<d_x+d_y\).  Equations (15), (20), and (22) yield
\[
10d_x<M_x\leq\delta+d_y<d_x+2d_y,
\tag{23}
\]
\[
7d_y<M_y\leq\delta+d_x<2d_x+d_y.
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently
\[
9d_x<2d_y,\qquad 3d_y<d_x.
\tag{25}
\]
The second inequality gives \(27d_y<9d_x\), while the first gives
\(9d_x<2d_y\), contradicting \(d_y>0\).  Hence
\(\delta\geq d_x+d_y\), and (13) gives (6).

Finally, suppose that a pentagon satisfying (7)-(12) existed.  Conditions
(8) supply the exact points \(p_x,p_y\) required in (3).  Equations
(7)-(8) give the point \(r\) and \(0<u<\delta\) required in (2).
Condition (10), together with \(P\) being the convex hull of its five
listed vertices, gives (1).  Condition (11) is exactly (4).  The
convex-body implication then gives
\(\delta\geq d_x+d_y\geq a+b\), contradicting (12).  Therefore the full
pentagon cell stated in (7)-(12) is impossible.
