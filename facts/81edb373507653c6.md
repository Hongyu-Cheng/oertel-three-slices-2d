## Statement

Define
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le\frac12,\\[2pt]
2-r,&\frac12<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
\delta=100,\qquad a=\frac1{100},
\]
and let
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(-100,1),\ (-100,99),\
\left(-100+\frac2{49},99\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(105,-105),\ (105,35),\
\left(105+\frac1{70},35\right)
\right\}.
\]
Then \(P,Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) are compact two-dimensional convex polygons with
\[
M=|P|=2,\qquad m=|Q|=1,\qquad
r=\frac mM=\frac12<\rho.
\]
They satisfy
\[
P\subseteq\{x\ge-\delta,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le0\},
\qquad \min_Px=-\delta,
\]
\[
Q\subseteq\{x+y\ge0\},
\qquad Q\cap\{x+y=0\}\ne\varnothing.
\]
For
\[
v_1=|Q\cap\{x\le\delta+2\}|,\qquad
d_2=|Q\cap\{y\le0\}|,
\]
one has
\[
v_1=0,\qquad d_2=\frac9{16},
\]
and the displayed value of \(a\) satisfies every cap and gate hypothesis:
\[
0\le v_1<a<m,\qquad 0<d_2<m,\qquad a+d_2<m,
\]
\[
4\min\{a,d_2\}+5\max\{a,d_2\}<4m.
\]
Moreover,
\[
|P\cap\{y\le s\}|+|Q\cap\{y\le-s\}|\ge d_2
\quad\text{for every }s<0,
\]
while this sum is strictly below \(d_2\) for every \(0<s<1\).  Nevertheless,
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|=\frac{61}{28}
<3=M\,U\!\left(\frac12\right).
\]

## Proof

Write the vertices of \(P\), in the order displayed, as \(A,B,C\), and those of \(Q\) as \(S,D,E\).  The edge vectors based at \(A\) are
\[
B-A=(0,98),\qquad C-A=\left(\frac2{49},98\right).
\]
Their determinant has absolute value \(4\), so
\[
M=|P|=\frac12\cdot4=2.
\]
Likewise,
\[
D-S=(0,140),\qquad E-S=\left(\frac1{70},140\right),
\]
whose determinant has absolute value \(2\).  Hence
\[
m=|Q|=\frac12\cdot2=1.
\]
In particular, both polygons are two-dimensional and \(M\ge m>0\).

At the three vertices of \(P\), the values of \(x+y\) are
\[
-99,\qquad -1,\qquad -1+\frac2{49}=-\frac{47}{49}.
\]
All three \(y\)-coordinates are positive, and all three \(x\)-coordinates are at least \(-100\).  Convexity therefore gives
\[
P\subseteq\{x\ge-100,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le0\}.
\]
The whole edge \([A,B]\) lies on \(x=-100\), so \(\min_Px=-100=-\delta\), including the positive-length support-face case.

At the three vertices of \(Q\), the values of \(x+y\) are
\[
0,\qquad 140,\qquad 140+\frac1{70}.
\]
Thus \(Q\subseteq\{x+y\ge0\}\), and \(S=(105,-105)\) is a contact point on \(x+y=0\).  Every point of \(Q\) has \(x\ge105\), whereas
\[
\delta+2=102.
\]
Consequently \(Q\cap\{x\le\delta+2\}\) is literally empty and
\[
v_1=0.
\]

Translate \(Q\) by \(-S\) and use coordinates
\[
u=x-105,\qquad v=y+105.
\]
The translated triangle has vertices
\[
(0,0),\qquad (0,140),\qquad
\left(\frac1{70},140\right).
\]
At height \(v\in[0,140]\), its horizontal section has length \(v/9800\).  Since \(y\le0\) is \(v\le105\),
\[
d_2
=\int_0^{105}\frac{v}{9800}\,dv
=\frac{105^2}{19600}
=\frac9{16}.
\]
Now
\[
a+d_2=\frac1{100}+\frac9{16}
=\frac{229}{400}<1=m,
\]
and \(0=v_1<a<m\), while \(0<9/16<m\).  Since \(a<d_2\), the gate is
\[
4\min\{a,d_2\}+5\max\{a,d_2\}
=\frac1{25}+\frac{45}{16}
=\frac{1141}{400}
<4=4m.
\]

The genuine-crossing profile is also exact.  If \(s<0\), then
\[
P\cap\{y\le s\}=\varnothing
\]
because \(\min_Py=1\), and
\[
Q\cap\{y\le0\}\subseteq Q\cap\{y\le-s\}.
\]
Thus the required sum is at least \(d_2\) for every \(s<0\), including shifts for which the \(Q\)-cap is already full.  On the other hand, for every \(0<s<1\), the \(P\)-cap is still empty and the same section computation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P\cap\{y\le s\}|+|Q\cap\{y\le-s\}|
&=0+\int_0^{105-s}\frac{v}{9800}\,dv\\
&=\frac{(105-s)^2}{19600}
<\frac{105^2}{19600}
=d_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence \(0\) is a genuine lower crossing, with strict failure on the positive side for arbitrarily small \(s>0\).

It remains to compute the midpoint area without approximation.  Translation does not change area, so put
\[
\widehat P=P-A
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),(0,98),\left(\frac2{49},98\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
\widehat Q=Q-S
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),(0,140),\left(\frac1{70},140\right)
\right\}.
\]
The two nonvertical lower-edge slopes, measured as horizontal displacement divided by vertical displacement, satisfy
\[
\frac{(2/49)}{98}=\frac1{2401}
>\frac1{9800}
=\frac{(1/70)}{140}.
\]
Equivalently, inspection of the nine pairwise vertex sums shows
\[
\widehat P+\widehat Q
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),\
\left(\frac2{49},98\right),\
\left(\frac{27}{490},238\right),\
(0,238)
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, all pairwise sums other than
\[
\left(\frac1{70},140\right)
\]
are visibly vertices of the quadrilateral in (1), points of its vertical edge, or points of its horizontal top edge.  At height \(140\), the right edge of (1) has \(x\)-coordinate
\[
\frac2{49}
+\frac{140-98}{140}\frac1{70}
=\frac2{49}+\frac3{700}.
\]
This exceeds \(1/70\) by
\[
\frac2{49}-\frac1{100}
=\frac{151}{4900}>0,
\]
so the remaining pairwise sum also lies in the quadrilateral.  Conversely, every vertex in (1) is a pairwise vertex sum, proving (1).

The shoelace formula, or direct expansion along the four displayed vertices, now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P+Q|
&=|\widehat P+\widehat Q|\\
&=|P|+|Q|+\frac2{49}\cdot140\\
&=2+1+\frac{40}{7}
=\frac{61}{7}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Scaling by \(1/2\) multiplies planar area by \(1/4\), and therefore
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|
=\frac14|P+Q|
=\frac{61}{28}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\rho-\frac12
=\frac{29-4\sqrt{26}}{50}>0,
\]
because \(29^2=841>416=16\cdot26\).  Hence \(r=1/2\) lies in the first branch of the stated definition of \(U\), so
\[
U(r)=1+r=\frac32,
\qquad
M U(r)=2\cdot\frac32=3.
\]
Since \(61/28<84/28=3\), these exact rational polygons violate the proposed mixed-area lower bound while satisfying every stated hypothesis.
