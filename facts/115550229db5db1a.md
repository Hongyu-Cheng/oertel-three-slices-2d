## Statement

Assume the common-plane hypotheses, exact-cell convention, compatibility
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=t,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9\qquad(i\in\{1,2,3\}),
\]
canonical tails
\[
c_i+m\ge t,\qquad c_i+M>t,
\]
and finite interior
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad 0<c_i<\kappa
\]
of accepted facts `0e51a47a9add571f` and `a2a9387275a2c97c`. Fix \(j\), write \(\{k,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{j\}\), and use the normalization of accepted fact `8375b9cde7d74d1a`.

Suppose that almost everywhere on the respective projections the zero graphs have the common outer orders
\[
x_{\alpha,j}<x_{\alpha,\ell}<x_{\alpha,k},\qquad
x_{\beta,k}<x_{\beta,j}<x_{\beta,\ell},\qquad
x_{\gamma,k}<x_{\gamma,j}<x_{\gamma,\ell},
\]
and that the positive \(Q\)-support contains the minimal outer path
\[
Q_{\{j\}},\quad Q_{\{j,k\}},\quad Q_{\{k\}},\quad Q_{\{k,\ell\}}.
\]
If \(p_\varnothing>0\), these assumptions are inconsistent. In particular, no compatible realization satisfying the crossings and tails can violate
\[
\delta h_0-2p_\varnothing-p_{\{\ell\}}
-Dh_2+q_{\{j,k\}}-q_{\{k\}}
-h+r_{\{j,k\}}-r_{\{k\}}\ge0
\]
in this outer common-order branch.

## Proof

The three orders imply that, up to cutting lines of area zero, the only possible cells are
\[
P_{\{j\}},P_\varnothing,P_{\{\ell\}},P_{\{k,\ell\}},
\]
\[
Q_{\{j\}},Q_{\{j,k\}},Q_{\{k\}},Q_{\{k,\ell\}},
\]
\[
R_{\{j\}},R_{\{j,k\}},R_{\{k\}},R_{\{k,\ell\}}.
\]
Abbreviate their areas, in these orders, by
\[
a,b,c,d;\qquad e,f,g,s;\qquad x,y,z,w.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(b=p_\varnothing>0\). Finite interior gives
\[
a=u_j>0,
\tag{2}
\]
and the assumed minimal \(Q\)-path gives \(e>0\) and \(g>0\).

Subtract the \(\ell\)-crossing from the \(k\)-crossing. Using (1),
\[
0=(u_k+v_k+c_k)-(u_\ell+v_\ell+c_\ell)
=-c+f+g+y+z,
\]
so
\[
c=f+g+y+z.
\tag{3}
\]
The left \(\ell\)-tail and the \(\ell\)-crossing give
\[
u_\ell+v_\ell\le m.
\]
Since \(u_\ell=c+d\), \(v_\ell=s\), and \(m=e+f+g+s\), (3) yields
\[
e\ge d+y+z.
\tag{4}
\]
Equating the \(j\)- and \(\ell\)-crossings and again using (3) gives
\[
a+e+x=g+z+d+s+w.
\tag{5}
\]

We next record the geometric input. If planar convex bodies \(E,F,G\) satisfy
\[
\frac{E+F}{2}\subseteq G,
\]
then
\[
|G|\ge\frac14\bigl(\sqrt{|E|}+\sqrt{|F|}\bigr)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Here is a section proof sufficient for the present cells. Let the \(w\)-projection widths of \(E,F\) be \(h_E,h_F>0\), and pair the sections at
\[
w_E=s_E+h_E\tau,\qquad
w_F=s_F+h_F\tau,\qquad 0\le\tau\le1.
\]
The corresponding section of \((E+F)/2\), at \((w_E+w_F)/2\), contains the midpoint sum of the two section intervals and therefore has length at least half the sum of their lengths. The paired midpoint coordinate sweeps an interval of width \((h_E+h_F)/2\). Fubini therefore gives
\[
|G|\ge
\frac{h_E+h_F}{4}
\left(\frac{|E|}{h_E}+\frac{|F|}{h_F}\right)
\ge
\frac14\bigl(\sqrt{|E|}+\sqrt{|F|}\bigr)^2,
\]
where the last inequality is AM-GM applied to
\[
|E|\frac{h_F}{h_E}+|F|\frac{h_E}{h_F}.
\]

The singleton midpoint inclusions in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, followed by closure on cutting lines, give
\[
\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_{\{j\}}}}2
\subseteq\overline{R_{\{j\}}},
\qquad
\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_{\{k\}}}}2
\subseteq\overline{R_{\{k\}}}.
\]
Applying (6), with cutting boundaries ignored because they have area zero, yields
\[
x\ge\frac14(\sqrt b+\sqrt e)^2,
\qquad
z\ge\frac14(\sqrt b+\sqrt g)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
This is the retained-section restriction absent from a scalar cell table.

All three crossings imply the weighted identity from accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c`:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&-2b+a+c+4d+e+4f+g+4s\\
&+x+4y+z+4w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, the left side is
\[
3\sum_i(u_i+v_i+c_i)-2(M+m+\kappa)
=9t-2(M+m+\kappa)=0.
\]
Substituting (3) and then (5) into (8) gives the exact identity
\[
2b=5f+3g+5y+3z+5d+5s+5w.
\tag{9}
\]

By (2), (4), and (5),
\[
g+z+d+s+w=a+e+x>e+x\ge d+y+z+x,
\]
hence
\[
g+s+w>x+y\ge x.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(r\ge0\), put
\[
\Phi_b(r)=\frac14(\sqrt b+\sqrt r)^2.
\]
It is increasing. Equations (4), (7), and (10) give
\[
g+s+w>x\ge\Phi_b(e)\ge\Phi_b(z).
\tag{11}
\]
The second inequality in (7) also gives
\[
z\ge\Phi_b(g)\ge\Phi_b(0)=\frac b4.
\tag{12}
\]
Finally, (9)--(12) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
2b
&\ge3(g+s+w+z)\\
&>3\bigl(z+\Phi_b(z)\bigr)\\
&\ge3\left(\frac b4+\Phi_b\!\left(\frac b4\right)\right)
=\frac{39}{16}b
>2b,
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting \(b>0\). Thus the outer common-order branch has no realization. The displayed retained-gap inequality consequently holds there vacuously.
