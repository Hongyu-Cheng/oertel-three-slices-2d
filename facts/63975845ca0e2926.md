## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^3:\mathbf1^\top\lambda=1\},
\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal C(z;\tau)=|\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\geq\tau\}|.
\]
Suppose \(U^\circ,V^\circ\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), \(L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), and \(\sigma^\circ\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U^\circ=\mathbf1^\top,
\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V^\circ=\mathbf1^\top,
\qquad
\det U^\circ\det V^\circ\ne0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3L_{ij}=1\quad(j=1,2,3),
\]
\[
\min_jU^\circ_{kj}+\min_jV^\circ_{kj}\geq0
\quad(k=1,2,3).
\]
Put
\[
A^\circ=|\det U^\circ|,
\qquad B^\circ=|\det V^\circ|,
\qquad
R=\frac{\min\{A^\circ,B^\circ\}}{\max\{A^\circ,B^\circ\}},
\qquad
S=\frac1{\max\{A^\circ,B^\circ\}},
\]
and assume the exact strict residual strip of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`:
\[
0<R<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad L_0(R)<S<U_0(R),
\]
where
\[
L_0(R)=
\begin{cases}
1-R,&0<R\leq9/25,\\
(3R+2\sqrt R-1)/2,&9/25<R,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U_0(R)=
\begin{cases}
1+R,&0<R\leq1/2,\\
2-R,&1/2<R.
\end{cases}
\]
Let
\[
q^\circ=\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i^\circ,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A^\circ+B^\circ)}9,
\]
and assume all three literal row masses
\[
M_i^\circ=
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A^\circ\mathcal C((LU^\circ)_{i*};-\sigma_i^\circ)
+B^\circ\mathcal C((LV^\circ)_{i*};\sigma_i^\circ)
<t
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]
These are precisely the triangular \(N=3\) hypotheses of accepted facts `64760c76cadff481` and `09393c6edf1fc4e2`.

The strict system has \(q^\circ\notin[-1,1]\). Orient its endpoints by
\[
(U,A;V,B;\sigma,q)=
\begin{cases}
(U^\circ,A^\circ;V^\circ,B^\circ;\sigma^\circ,q^\circ),&q^\circ>1,\\
(V^\circ,B^\circ;U^\circ,A^\circ;-\sigma^\circ,-q^\circ),&q^\circ<-1.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), define the oriented row mass
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i).
\]
Then \(M_i=M_i^\circ<t\), \(q>1\), \(0<A<B\), \(1+A<2B\), \(\det L\ne0\), and
\[
P_0=LU\Delta+\sigma,
\qquad P_1=L\Delta,
\qquad P_2=LV\Delta-\sigma
\]
are nondegenerate triangles of normalized areas \(A,1,B\), respectively, satisfying
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,
\]
with coordinate sums \(1+q,1,1-q\). Let \(g_j\) be the area centroid of \(P_j\), and define the closed centroid-incidence sets
\[
I_j=\{i\in\{1,2,3\}:(g_j)_i\geq0\}
\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\]
There is a permutation \(\pi\) of the three coordinates such that, after applying it simultaneously to all three \(P_j\), precisely one of the following alternatives holds:

1. \(t>1/2\).
2. \(t\leq1/2\), \(\pi(I_2)=\varnothing\), and
   \[
   (\pi(I_0),\pi(I_1))=(\{1\},\{2\})
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   (\pi(I_0),\pi(I_1))=(\{1\},\{2,3\}).
   \]

In particular, when \(t\leq1/2\), the alternative
\[
(\pi(I_0),\pi(I_1))=(\{1,2\},\{3\})
\]
is impossible by accepted fact `806575336d04fac7`, including every all-nine-nonzero middle chamber and every stratum with one or more zero entries of \(L\). No exclusion or existence assertion is made for either displayed survivor orbit or for the regime \(t>1/2\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` first excludes \(-1\leq q^\circ\leq1\) under the three strict row inequalities. It also gives the exact endpoint reversal
\[
(U^\circ,A^\circ;V^\circ,B^\circ;\sigma^\circ)
\longmapsto
(V^\circ,B^\circ;U^\circ,A^\circ;-\sigma^\circ).
\tag{1}
\]
The map (1) leaves \(L\) fixed, sends \(q^\circ\) to \(-q^\circ\), and preserves compatibility because the row-minimum inequalities are symmetric in \(U^\circ,V^\circ\). It also preserves each literal row mass, since in the reversed system its two endpoint terms are
\[
B^\circ\mathcal C((LV^\circ)_{i*};\sigma_i^\circ)
\quad\text{and}\quad
A^\circ\mathcal C((LU^\circ)_{i*};-\sigma_i^\circ),
\]
which are the original two terms in the opposite order. Thus \(M_i=M_i^\circ\), and the threshold \(t\) is unchanged.

The endpoint geometry is reversed literally. If
\[
P_0^\circ=LU^\circ\Delta+\sigma^\circ,
\qquad P_1^\circ=L\Delta,
\qquad P_2^\circ=LV^\circ\Delta-\sigma^\circ,
\]
then (1) gives
\[
P_0=P_2^\circ,
\qquad P_1=P_1^\circ,
\qquad P_2=P_0^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the endpoint centroids and their incidence sets are exchanged when (1) is used. After the orientation, accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` gives
\[
q>1,
\qquad B=\max\{A^\circ,B^\circ\},
\qquad A=\min\{A^\circ,B^\circ\},
\qquad 1+A<2B,
\tag{3}
\]
as well as \(\det L\ne0\), the displayed transformed triangles, compatibility, their coordinate-sum planes, their area ratio, and the literal coordinate-cap interpretation of every \(M_i\).

Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives \(A^\circ,B^\circ>0\) and
\[
R<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}<1.
\]
The last strict inequality follows from \(\sqrt{26}>1\). Hence the two endpoint areas are unequal, and (3) sharpens to
\[
0<A<B.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently \(P_2\), not merely an endpoint bearing an arbitrary height label, is exactly the large endpoint after the orientation, so \(I_2\) is its centroid-incidence set.

Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` applies to this same oriented coordinate-cap model. It supplies
\[
A+B>1,
\qquad B<1+A,
\tag{5}
\]
and proves that \(I_0,I_1\) are nonempty and disjoint. Accepted fact `a5bbbf5d61041b23` applies using \(q>1\), \(0<A\leq B\), (5), compatibility, and the strict literal row inequalities. It gives
\[
I_2=\varnothing.
\tag{6}
\]
This uses the closed convention \((g_j)_i\geq0\), so no centroid-on-coordinate-line boundary is omitted.

We now make the coordinate relabeling exact. For a permutation \(\pi\in S_3\), let \(R_\pi e_i=e_{\pi(i)}\), so
\[
(R_\pi x)_k=x_{\pi^{-1}(k)}.
\]
The simultaneous coordinate permutation is
\[
L'=R_\pi L,
\qquad \sigma'=R_\pi\sigma,
\qquad P_j'=R_\pi P_j,
\qquad g_j'=R_\pi g_j.
\tag{7}
\]
The endpoint matrices \(U,V\) and their vertex columns are unchanged. Since \(\mathbf1^\top R_\pi=\mathbf1^\top\), (7) preserves the column sums of \(L\), \(q\), all coordinate-sum planes, compatibility, areas, and \(t\). Moreover,
\[
(L'U)_{k*}=(LU)_{\pi^{-1}(k),*},
\qquad
(L'V)_{k*}=(LV)_{\pi^{-1}(k),*},
\]
so the new literal row mass is \(M'_{k}=M_{\pi^{-1}(k)}<t\). Finally,
\[
I_j'=\pi(I_j)
\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (7) is one genuine simultaneous coordinate permutation, not an independent relabeling of the three incidence sets.

Because \(I_0,I_1\) are nonempty disjoint subsets of a three-element set, their cardinalities are exactly one of
\[
(|I_0|,|I_1|)=(1,1),\quad(1,2),\quad(2,1).
\tag{9}
\]
For the first case choose the single \(\pi\) in (7) to send the element of \(I_0\) to \(1\), the element of \(I_1\) to \(2\), and the remaining index to \(3\). For the second case send the element of \(I_0\) to \(1\) and the two elements of \(I_1\) to \(2,3\). For the third case send the two elements of \(I_0\) to \(1,2\) and the element of \(I_1\) to \(3\). These choices give, respectively,
\[
(I_0',I_1')=(\{1\},\{2\}),
\quad(\{1\},\{2,3\}),
\quad(\{1,2\},\{3\}).
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (6) and (8) give \(I_2'=\varnothing\) for every one of these choices.

The numerical split is exhaustive and disjoint. If \(t>1/2\), the first alternative holds, and no structural exclusion is asserted. Suppose therefore that \(t\leq1/2\), equivalently \(A+B\leq5/4\). If the third pair in (10) occurred, then (4), (5), \(t\leq1/2\), \(\det L'\ne0\), the compatibility and column-sum identities, the literal-cap identities, and all three strict row inequalities would match every hypothesis of accepted fact `806575336d04fac7`. That accepted fact excludes the whole orbit
\[
I_0'=\{1,2\},
\qquad I_1'=\{3\}.
\]
Its conclusion includes all all-nine-nonzero middle chambers, every inverse-capacity switch branch, and every arbitrary nonempty zero set among the entries of \(L'\). Thus neither a nonzero chamber nor a zero-entry boundary stratum survives in the third orbit.

Only the first two pairs in (10) remain when \(t\leq1/2\), and (6) says that their large-endpoint incidence set is empty. The cases \(t>1/2\) and \(t\leq1/2\) are mutually exclusive, as are the two remaining incidence pairs. Accepted fact `806575336d04fac7` expressly makes no conclusion outside the excluded third orbit and the \(t\leq1/2\) residual regime. The argument therefore proves only the stated frontier classification and does not exclude either remaining orbit or the regime \(t>1/2\).
