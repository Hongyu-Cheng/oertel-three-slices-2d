## Statement

Let three distinct lines through \(0\) have cyclic rays
\[
r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_6,\qquad r_{j+3}=-r_j,
\]
labeled, after choosing one of the two orientations and cyclically changing the
starting index, so that
\[
Q_1=\operatorname{cone}(r_1,r_2),\quad
Q_2=\operatorname{cone}(r_3,r_4),\quad
Q_3=\operatorname{cone}(r_5,r_6).
\]
Let \(T=\operatorname{conv}(P_1,P_2,P_3)\) be a nondegenerate triangle of
area \(1\), with \(0\in\operatorname{int}T\) and
\(P_i\in\overline{Q_i}\). Then there are finite parameters
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\rho,\sigma,\tau>0,\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1,
\]
such that
\[
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}\le \rho\le1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta},
\qquad
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\le \sigma\le1+\frac{\beta}{\gamma},
\qquad
\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\le \tau\le1+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma},
\]
\[
\sigma+\tau\ge1,\qquad
\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho\ge1,\qquad
\rho+\frac1\tau\ge1.
\]
Moreover, if
\[
A_i=|T\cap Q_i|,\qquad b=1-A_1-A_2-A_3,
\]
then
\[
A_1=2\beta\gamma+\beta^2(1-\rho)+\gamma^2(1-\sigma),
\]
\[
A_2=2\gamma\alpha+\gamma^2(1-\tau)
     +\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\rho\right),
\]
\[
A_3=2\alpha\beta+\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)
     +\beta^2\left(1-\frac1\tau\right),
\]
\[
b=\gamma^2(\sigma+\tau-1)
  +\alpha^2\left(\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho-1\right)
  +\beta^2\left(\rho+\frac1\tau-1\right).
\]
These assertions remain exact when a vertex \(P_i\) lies on a fan ray.

## Proof

Because \(0\in\operatorname{int}T\), every ray from \(0\) meets
\(\partial T\) exactly once, and radial projection
\(\partial T\to S^1\) is a homeomorphism. Thus cyclic order on
\(\partial T\) is the cyclic order of directions. Denote the intersection of
\(r_j\) with \(\partial T\) by \(X_j\). The vertex and ray assignments give
\[
X_2,X_3\in[P_1,P_2],\qquad
X_4,X_5\in[P_2,P_3],\qquad
X_6,X_1\in[P_3,P_1],
\]
with the respective side orders
\[
P_1,X_2,X_3,P_2;\qquad
P_2,X_4,X_5,P_3;\qquad
P_3,X_6,X_1,P_1.
\]
Coincidence of an \(X_j\) with an endpoint is allowed here.

Use barycentric coordinates relative to \(P_1,P_2,P_3\). Since \(0\) is
interior, uniquely
\[
0=\alpha P_1+\beta P_2+\gamma P_3,
\qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma>0,\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1.
\]
Introduce side coordinates
\[
X_2=(1-t_2)P_1+t_2P_2,\qquad
X_3=(1-t_3)P_1+t_3P_2,
\]
\[
X_4=(1-u_4)P_2+u_4P_3,\qquad
X_5=(1-u_5)P_2+u_5P_3,
\]
\[
X_6=(1-v_6)P_3+v_6P_1,\qquad
X_1=(1-v_1)P_3+v_1P_1.
\]
The side orders are precisely
\[
0\le t_2\le t_3\le1,\qquad
0\le u_4\le u_5\le1,\qquad
0\le v_6\le v_1\le1. \tag{1}
\]

Opposite rays lie on the same fan line, so \(0\) lies in the open segments
\((X_1,X_4)\), \((X_2,X_5)\), and \((X_3,X_6)\). Define
\[
\rho=\frac{v_1-\alpha}{\beta},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{1-\alpha-t_2}{\gamma},\qquad
\tau=\frac{t_3-\beta}{\gamma}. \tag{2}
\]
These quantities are positive even at vertex incidences. Indeed, writing
\(0=\lambda X_1+(1-\lambda)X_4\), with \(0<\lambda<1\), gives
\(\alpha=\lambda v_1\), hence \(v_1>\alpha\). Similarly,
\(0=\lambda X_2+(1-\lambda)X_5\) gives
\(\alpha=\lambda(1-t_2)<1-t_2\), and
\(0=\lambda X_3+(1-\lambda)X_6\) gives
\(\beta=\lambda t_3<t_3\).

Equation (2), followed by the collinearity of each opposite pair with \(0\),
gives all six endpoints:
\[
t_2=1-\alpha-\gamma\sigma,\qquad
t_3=\beta+\gamma\tau,
\]
\[
u_4=1-\beta-\frac{\alpha}{\rho},\qquad
u_5=\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{\sigma},
\]
\[
v_6=1-\gamma-\frac{\beta}{\tau},\qquad
v_1=\alpha+\beta\rho. \tag{3}
\]
For completeness, the first opposite pair satisfies
\(0=\lambda X_1+(1-\lambda)X_4\). From
\(\alpha=\lambda v_1\) and
\(\beta=(1-\lambda)(1-u_4)\), one obtains
\[
1-u_4=\frac{\beta v_1}{v_1-\alpha}
=\frac{v_1}{\rho},
\]
which is the formula for \(u_4\). The other two formulas follow identically:
\(\alpha=\lambda(1-t_2)\), \(\gamma=(1-\lambda)u_5\) give the formula for
\(u_5\), while \(\beta=\lambda t_3\),
\(\gamma=(1-\lambda)(1-v_6)\) give the formula for \(v_6\).

The endpoint conditions in (1) now give exactly the six displayed parameter
bounds. Namely, \(v_1\le1\) and \(u_4\ge0\) give
\[
\rho\le\frac{1-\alpha}{\beta}=1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta},
\qquad
\rho\ge\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}
=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}.
\]
Likewise, \(t_2\ge0\) and \(u_5\le1\) give
\[
\sigma\le\frac{1-\alpha}{\gamma}=1+\frac{\beta}{\gamma},
\qquad
\sigma\ge\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},
\]
and \(t_3\le1\) and \(v_6\ge0\) give
\[
\tau\le\frac{1-\beta}{\gamma}=1+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma},
\qquad
\tau\ge\frac{\beta}{1-\gamma}
=\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
All remaining endpoint inequalities in (1) are automatic from positivity.
The three order relations in (1), using (3), are respectively equivalent to
\[
\sigma+\tau\ge1,\qquad
\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho\ge1,\qquad
\rho+\frac1\tau\ge1. \tag{4}
\]

Since \(|T|=1\), the three barycentric subtriangle areas are
\[
|\operatorname{conv}(0,P_1,P_2)|=\gamma,\quad
|\operatorname{conv}(0,P_2,P_3)|=\alpha,\quad
|\operatorname{conv}(0,P_3,P_1)|=\beta.
\]
The selected-sector pieces, allowing degenerate summands at a vertex
incidence, therefore have areas
\[
A_1=\gamma t_2+\beta(1-v_1),\qquad
A_2=\gamma(1-t_3)+\alpha u_4,\qquad
A_3=\alpha(1-u_5)+\beta v_6. \tag{5}
\]
Substitution of (3) into (5) yields the three claimed formulas. The three
intervening sectors are the triangles with bases
\([X_2,X_3]\), \([X_4,X_5]\), and \([X_6,X_1]\), so their areas are
\[
\gamma(t_3-t_2)=\gamma^2(\sigma+\tau-1),
\]
\[
\alpha(u_5-u_4)
=\alpha^2\left(\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho-1\right),
\]
\[
\beta(v_1-v_6)
=\beta^2\left(\rho+\frac1\tau-1\right).
\]
Their sum is the claimed formula for \(b\).

It remains only to check that fan-line passage through a triangle vertex does
not create an infinite or zero parameter. There are exactly six possible
incidences, and (3) identifies them with finite endpoint equalities:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P_1=X_1 & \rho=1+\gamma/\beta\\
P_1=X_2 & \sigma=1+\beta/\gamma\\
P_2=X_3 & \tau=1+\alpha/\gamma\\
P_2=X_4 & \rho=\alpha/(\alpha+\gamma)\\
P_3=X_5 & \sigma=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)\\
P_3=X_6 & \tau=\beta/(\alpha+\beta).
\end{array}
\]
All entries are finite and strictly positive because
\(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\). A vertex cannot lie on both of its bounding fan
rays, since distinct fan rays meet only at \(0\), which is not a triangle
vertex. Thus the list covers every boundary case exactly, without a limiting
argument.
