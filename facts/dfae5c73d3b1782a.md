## Statement

Use the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  Let \(N=\{j,k,\ell\}\), let
\[
f_j+f_k+f_\ell=-1,\qquad L_j+L_k+L_\ell<-1,
\]
put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-(L_j+L_k+L_\ell)>0,
\]
and let \(A_0\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon whose positive-area \(P\)-cells are precisely
\[
P_j,\qquad P_\varnothing,\qquad P_\ell,\qquad P_{k\ell},
\]
all four having positive area.  In the affine coordinates
\[
X=p_j,\qquad Y=p_\ell,\qquad p_k=\delta-X-Y,
\]
put
\[
B=\{(p_j(w),p_\ell(w)):w\in\overline{P_\varnothing}\}.
\]
Let \(\Delta=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_\ell)|\), and define the one-sided trace lengths \(\lambda_{0j},\lambda_{0\ell}\) as in accepted fact `b431dbefa8a4aec7`.

There are numbers \(y_-,x_-\ge0\) such that
\[
B\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb R)
=\{0\}\times[y_-,y_-+\lambda_{0j}],
\tag{1}
\]
\[
B\cap(\mathbb R\times\{0\})
=[x_-,x_-+\lambda_{0\ell}]\times\{0\},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
y_-+\lambda_{0j}\le\delta,\qquad
x_-+\lambda_{0\ell}\le\delta.
\tag{3}
\]
These are the images of the full chords
\(A_0\cap\{p_j=0\}\) and \(A_0\cap\{p_\ell=0\}\), not truncated pieces of those chords.

For any \(a,d>0\) and
\[
\chi=\max_{(X,Y)\in B}(aX+dY),
\]
the two far endpoints satisfy only
\[
d(y_-+\lambda_{0j})\le\chi,\qquad
a(x_-+\lambda_{0\ell})\le\chi.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (1)-(4) do not imply either condition (16) or
\(|B|\le\Phi_{j\ell}\) from accepted fact `b431dbefa8a4aec7`.
The single missing coupled chord inequality is
\[
\left(1+\frac{d\bigl((y_-+\lambda_{0j})-y_-\bigr)}{\chi}\right)
\left(1+\frac{a\bigl((x_-+\lambda_{0\ell})-x_-\bigr)}{\chi}\right)
\ge2.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, (5) is exactly
\[
\frac{d\lambda_{0j}}{\chi}
+\frac{a\lambda_{0\ell}}{\chi}
+\frac{ad\lambda_{0j}\lambda_{0\ell}}{\chi^2}\ge1,
\]
which is condition (16) for the \(j\ell\)-row.

More explicitly, there is an exact rational common-plane pair \(A_0,A_2\) with the prescribed \(P\)- and \(Q\)-supports for which the two \(Q\)-traces are retained, \(a=d=1\) is an actual nondegenerate row separator, but
\[
|B|=\frac{27}{50}>
\Phi_{j\ell}=\frac{11}{40},
\qquad
\text{the left side of condition (16)}=\frac{11}{45}<1.
\tag{6}
\]
This is a counterexample only to the proposed row-local implication from exact endpoint support, chord placement, retained traces, and a separator to (16).  No \(K\), crossing completion, or \(W=0\) completion is asserted, so it is not a counterexample to the full hypotheses or conclusions of accepted fact `b431dbefa8a4aec7`.  Accepted fact `decc0d9db44fe9e5` still supplies automatic retained traces and nondegeneracy in every full central common-order configuration.

## Proof

In the \((X,Y)\)-plane, the four supported strict chambers are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P_j&X<0,\ Y>0,\ X+Y<\delta,\\
P_\varnothing&X>0,\ Y>0,\ X+Y<\delta,\\
P_\ell&X>0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y<\delta,\\
P_{k\ell}&X>0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y>\delta.
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
We first locate the full \(p_j=0\) chord.  If
\((0,Y)\in A_0\) with \(Y<0\), move slightly from that point toward a strict interior point of \(P_j\).  The resulting points are interior to \(A_0\) and satisfy
\[
X<0,\qquad Y<0,\qquad X+Y<\delta,
\]
so the unsupported cell \(P_{j\ell}\) would have positive area.  If instead \(Y>\delta\), move slightly toward a strict interior point of \(P_\ell\).  The resulting interior points satisfy
\[
X>0,\qquad Y>0,\qquad X+Y>\delta,
\]
so the unsupported cell \(P_k\) would have positive area.  Both are contradictions.  Therefore
\[
A_0\cap\{p_j=0\}\subseteq
\{X=0,\ 0\le Y\le\delta\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The same argument locates the full \(p_\ell=0\) chord.  A point
\((X,0)\in A_0\) with \(X<0\), moved slightly toward a strict interior point of \(P_\ell\), produces positive area in \(P_{j\ell}\).  A point with \(X>\delta\), moved slightly toward a strict interior point of \(P_\varnothing\), produces positive area in \(P_k\).  Hence
\[
A_0\cap\{p_\ell=0\}\subseteq
\{Y=0,\ 0\le X\le\delta\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The elementary convexity fact used here is that if \(z\) is interior to a convex body and \(w\) belongs to it, then \((1-t)w+tz\) is interior for every \(0<t\le1\).

Fix a strict interior point of \(P_\varnothing\).  Moving any point in either chord (8)-(9) slightly toward that point gives strict \(P_\varnothing\) points.  Thus both full chords belong to
\(\overline{P_\varnothing}\), and conversely every point of either axis face of \(B\) comes from the corresponding full chord.  Positive area in \(P_j,P_\varnothing\) puts interior points of \(A_0\) on both sides of \(p_j=0\); positive area in \(P_\varnothing,P_\ell\) does the same for \(p_\ell=0\).  Both cutting lines therefore meet \(\operatorname{int}A_0\).

For a polygon, the chord endpoints, and hence the chord length, are continuous at every level whose cutting line meets the interior; this is the piecewise-affine polygonal chord regularity used in accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc`.  Along \(p_j=0\), the absolute derivative of \(p_\ell\) with respect to Euclidean arclength is
\[
\frac{|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_\ell)|}{\|\nabla f_j\|}
=\frac{\Delta}{\|\nabla f_j\|}.
\]
Consequently the coordinate length of the full vertical chord is
\(\lambda_{0j}\).  Interchanging \(j,\ell\) gives coordinate length
\(\lambda_{0\ell}\) for the full horizontal chord.  This proves
(1)-(3).  Applying \(aX+dY\le\chi\) at their far endpoints proves (4).
Nothing in (3)-(4) is a lower bound on the two normalized chord lengths, so it remains to test (5).

Take
\[
L_j=L_k=L_\ell=-\frac56,
\qquad
f_j=X-\frac56,\qquad
f_\ell=Y-\frac56,\qquad
f_k=\frac23-X-Y.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(f_j+f_k+f_\ell=-1\),
\(\delta=3/2\), the three gradients are pairwise nonparallel, and
\(\Delta=1\).  Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{21}{71},\frac{43}{142}\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac7{10}\right),
\left(\frac45,\frac35\right),
\left(\frac{29}{19},-\frac3{133}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The four consecutive turning determinants in the displayed order are
\[
-\frac{1833}{7100},\quad
-\frac{1587}{6650},\quad
-\frac{84801}{94430},\quad
-\frac{173289}{188860},
\]
so (11) is a two-dimensional convex quadrilateral.

Its three cutting chords are read directly from its four edges:
\[
A_0\cap\{X=0\}
=\{0\}\times\left[\frac14,\frac12\right],
\tag{12}
\]
\[
A_0\cap\{Y=0\}
=\left[\frac75,\frac32\right]\times\{0\},
\tag{13}
\]
and the line \(X+Y=3/2\) meets the boundary at
\[
\left(\frac32,0\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac{35}{23},-\frac1{46}\right).
\tag{14}
\]
These cuts partition \(A_0\), up to their null boundary lines, into exactly the four chambers in (7).  Their exact areas are
\[
|P_j|=\frac{21}{568},\qquad
|P_\varnothing|=\frac{27}{50},\qquad
|P_\ell|=\frac1{920},\qquad
|P_{k\ell}|=\frac1{24472}.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus all four required cells have positive area and every other \(P\)-cell has area zero.  Moreover
\[
B=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(0,\frac14\right),
\left(0,\frac12\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac7{10}\right),
\left(\frac45,\frac35\right),
\left(\frac32,0\right),
\left(\frac75,0\right)
\right\},
\tag{16}
\]
whose shoelace area is \(27/50\).  Equations (12)-(13) give
\[
y_-=\frac14,\quad \lambda_{0j}=\frac14,\qquad
x_-=\frac75,\quad \lambda_{0\ell}=\frac1{10}.
\tag{17}
\]

Choose \(a=d=1\).  The maximum of \(X+Y\) over the six vertices in
(16) is
\[
\chi=\frac32.
\tag{18}
\]
To verify that this coefficient pair can be one actual row separator, rather than merely a supporting halfspace for \(B\), use the same affine plane and put
\[
u=-q_j=\frac53-X,\qquad
v=-q_\ell=\frac53-Y,\qquad
w=-q_k=\frac72-u-v.
\]
Define \(A_2\) by declaring its image in the \((u,v)\)-coordinates to be the single convex quadrilateral
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{36}{115},\frac{101}{46}\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac{12}{5}\right),
\left(\frac32,\frac32\right),
\left(\frac{104}{25},-\frac35\right)
\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Its four consecutive turning determinants are
\[
-\frac{1833}{2300},\quad
-\frac{63}{500},\quad
-\frac{4501}{2300},\quad
-\frac{30221}{11500},
\]
so it is two-dimensional and convex.  Direct clipping by \(u=0\), \(v=0\), and \(u+v=7/2\) gives positive area only in
\[
Q_j,\quad Q_{jk},\quad Q_N,\quad Q_{k\ell},
\]
with respective areas
\[
\frac3{800},\qquad \frac9{160},\qquad
\frac{127}{100},\qquad \frac{27}{575}.
\tag{20}
\]
The actual \(Q_N\)-coordinate polygon is
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,2),\left(0,\frac{23}{10}\right),
\left(\frac3{10},\frac{12}{5}\right),
\left(\frac32,\frac32\right),
\left(\frac{17}{5},0\right),
\left(\frac{16}{5},0\right)
\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Its two axis intervals are the full \(u=0\) and \(v=0\) chords of
(19), and both cutting lines meet its interior.  Chord continuity therefore makes their lengths \(3/10\) and \(1/5\) exactly
\(\lambda_{2j}\) and \(\lambda_{2\ell}\); they strictly exceed the
\(A_0\)-lengths in (17).  Every point of \(D\) satisfies
\[
U+V\ge2>\frac32\ge X+Y
\quad((X,Y)\in B).
\tag{22}
\]
Thus the \(j\ell\)-traces are retained and \(a=d=1\) is a genuine nondegenerate separator for this one common-plane row.  No independent row models have been combined.

Finally, (17)-(18) give
\[
\frac{\lambda_{0\ell}}{\chi}
+\frac{\lambda_{0j}}{\chi}
+\frac{\lambda_{0j}\lambda_{0\ell}}{\chi^2}
=\frac1{15}+\frac16+\frac1{90}
=\frac{11}{45}<1,
\tag{23}
\]
so (5) and condition (16) fail.  Formula (14) of accepted fact
`b431dbefa8a4aec7` gives
\[
\Phi_{j\ell}
=\frac12\left(
\frac32\cdot\frac14+
\frac32\cdot\frac1{10}+
\frac1{40}\right)
=\frac{11}{40},
\]
whereas the shoelace computation for (16) gives
\[
|B|=\frac{27}{50}>\frac{11}{40}.
\]
This proves strict bulging and isolates (5) as the missing chord-density inequality.
