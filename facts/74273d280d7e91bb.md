## Statement

For each affine plane \(\{x_1+x_2+x_3=\text{constant}\}\), use the common planar area normalization given by Lebesgue area in coordinates \((x_1,x_2)\). Let
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{(20,20,60),(105,-1,-4),(-1,105,-4)\},
\]
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(-27,-27,-48),(2,-108,4),(-108,2,4)\}.
\]
Write their coordinates as \(p_i\) and \(q_i\), respectively, and define
\[
G_i(s)=|P\cap\{p_i\le s\}|+|Q\cap\{q_i\le-s\}|.
\]
Then
\[
|P|=M=3392,\qquad |Q|=m=2860,
\]
\[
G_1(0)=G_2(0)=m-\frac{1256}{609},
\qquad
G_3(0)=m-\frac{641}{52}.
\]
There is no nonempty two-dimensional convex polygon
\[
K\subseteq\{r_1+r_2+r_3=-1\}
\]
such that
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq K
\]
and, for all \(i=1,2,3\),
\[
|K\cap\{r_i\le0\}|+G_i(0)
=\frac{2(M+m+|K|)}9.
\]

## Proof

Put
\[
A=(20,20,60),\quad B=(105,-1,-4),\quad C=(-1,105,-4),
\]
\[
D=(-27,-27,-48),\quad E=(2,-108,4),\quad F=(-108,2,4).
\]
The coordinate sums of \(A,B,C\) are \(100\), while those of \(D,E,F\) are \(-102\). In first-two-coordinate area,
\[
2|P|
=
\left|\det
\begin{pmatrix}
85&-21\\
-21&85
\end{pmatrix}\right|
=7225-441=6784,
\]
and
\[
2|Q|
=
\left|\det
\begin{pmatrix}
29&-81\\
-81&29
\end{pmatrix}\right|
=6561-841=5720.
\]
Thus \(M=3392\), \(m=2860\), and \(M-m=532>0\).

For the first coordinate, \(C\) is the unique vertex of \(P\) with negative coordinate. The line \(p_1=0\) cuts the two incident edges at fractions \(1/21\) and \(1/106\) from \(C\). Hence
\[
|P\cap\{p_1\le0\}|
=M\frac1{21}\frac1{106}
=\frac{32}{21}.
\]
The vertex \(E\) is the unique vertex of \(Q\) with positive first coordinate. The positive cap has edge fractions \(2/29\) and \(2/110=1/55\) from \(E\), so
\[
|Q\cap\{q_1>0\}|
=m\frac2{29}\frac1{55}
=\frac{104}{29}.
\]
Therefore
\[
G_1(0)
=m+\frac{32}{21}-\frac{104}{29}
=m-\frac{1256}{609}.
\]
Symmetry under interchange of the first two coordinates gives the same value for \(G_2(0)\).

For the third coordinate, the complement of \(P\cap\{p_3\le0\}\) is the triangle at \(A\), whose two edge fractions are \(60/64=15/16\). Thus
\[
|P\cap\{p_3\le0\}|
=M\left(1-\frac{225}{256}\right)
=\frac{1643}{4}.
\]
The lower cap of \(Q\) is the triangle at \(D\), whose two edge fractions are \(48/52=12/13\). Hence
\[
|Q\cap\{q_3\le0\}|
=m\frac{144}{169}
=\frac{31680}{13}.
\]
Consequently
\[
G_3(0)
=\frac{1643}{4}+\frac{31680}{13}
=\frac{148079}{52}
=m-\frac{641}{52}.
\]

Suppose such a polygon \(K\) exists. Put
\[
k=|K|>0,\qquad c_i=|K\cap\{r_i\le0\}|.
\]
Since \(r_1+r_2+r_3=-1\), every point of \(K\) has at least one strictly negative coordinate. Therefore, pointwise on \(K\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf 1_{\{r_i\le0\}}\ge1,
\]
and integration gives
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge k.
\]
Summing the three assumed equalities yields
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3+\sum_{i=1}^3G_i(0)
=\frac{2(M+m+k)}3.
\]
It follows that
\[
k\le2M+2m-3\sum_{i=1}^3G_i(0).
\]
Using the exact values above,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3G_i(0)
=
3m-\frac{2512}{609}-\frac{641}{52},
\]
so
\[
\begin{aligned}
k
&\le
2M-7m
+3\left(\frac{2512}{609}+\frac{641}{52}\right)\\
&=
-13236+\frac{520993}{10556}\\
&=
-\frac{139198223}{10556}<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This contradicts \(k>0\). Therefore no such \(K\) exists.
