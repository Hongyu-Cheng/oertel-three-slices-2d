## Statement

There is no sequence of full common-plane realizations satisfying the
complete canonical finite-interior hypotheses, the all-double positive
\(Q\)-support
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
\(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\), the three crossings, the canonical tails,
\(M\geq m\), and \(W=0\), for which
\[
\frac{r_{123}}q\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\eta}q\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\eta=s_P+4d_P+3d_R+6r_{123}.
\]
This excludes the degeneration in every unequal-cap normal-form stratum
of accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8`, including coincident endpoints,
vanishing normalized cap areas, zero side gaps, collinear labels, and
redundant labels.

## Proof

Scale every table so that \(q=1\), and abbreviate \(r=r_{123}\).
Use the notation of accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7`:
\[
m=1+x_1+x_2+x_3,\qquad
M=p_0+s_P+d_P,\qquad
k=r_1+r_2+r_3+d_R+r.
\]
The last equality includes the canonical absence of \(R_\varnothing\);
the all-double support and this canonical cell convention are those of
accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`.

We first record the compact unequal-cap closure.  Let
\[
D=\frac1{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
The scale inequality in accepted facts `c49613ac26c0c4a8` and
`e40abbed940a40ab` gives
\[
0<D\leq
\frac1{(\sqrt{p_0}+2\sqrt r)^2}
<\frac1{p_0}.
\tag{1}
\]
The exact \(W=0\) identity in `084bf2f472bf29c7` is
\[
2p_0=4m+3+k+\eta.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \(p_0>7/2\).  Multiplying (2) by \(D\) and using (1) gives
\[
0<D<\frac27,\qquad
0<Dm\leq\frac12,\qquad
0<Dk\leq2,\qquad
0<Dx_i\leq\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]

In the normal form of `c49613ac26c0c4a8`, put
\[
X_i=Dx_i.
\]
Then \(0\leq a_{ij}<1\), \(0<L_i\leq1\),
\(\rho_{ij},\theta_{ij}\in[0,1]\), and
\(\tau_i\in(0,1)\).  Moreover
\[
h_i=\frac{X_i}{L_i},
\]
so after denominators are cleared, every convex-order polynomial
\(\Phi_{r,s}\) is a polynomial in the bounded variables
\[
D,\ X_i,\ a_{ij},\ \rho_{ij},\ \theta_{ij},\ \tau_i.
\]
For each of the three cyclic endpoint pairs, pass to a subsequence on
which the exact choice is fixed: either both entries are zero or both are
positive.  There are exactly eight such choices.  Pass once more to a
convergent subsequence in the closed boxes supplied by (3).  This includes
all finite zero/nonzero strata of the six limiting \(a_{ij}\), the three
limits \(L_i=0\) or \(L_i>0\), the limits \(D=0\) or \(D>0\), the limits
\(X_i=0\) or \(X_i>0\), all endpoints of the
\(\rho,\theta,\tau\) intervals, and all equalities among the finitely many
\(\Phi_{r,s}\).  Thus no endpoint or proper-cell boundary is lost.  The
contradiction below does not divide by any of these limiting quantities
and hence applies to every one of these strata.

We next derive the nontrivial compact scalar limit.  Set
\[
z_n=\frac1{p_{0,n}},\qquad
a_n=\frac{m_n}{p_{0,n}},\qquad
t_n=\frac{k_n}{p_{0,n}},
\]
\[
\xi_{i,n}=\frac{x_{i,n}}{p_{0,n}},\qquad
y_{i,n}=\frac{r_{i,n}}{p_{0,n}}.
\]
Since \(M\geq m\), \(s_P+d_P\leq\eta\), and \(p_0>7/2\),
\[
\frac{M_n}{p_{0,n}}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
0\leq a_n\leq1+o(1).
\]
Equation (2) shows that all the displayed variables are bounded.  After
passing to a subsequence, write their limits as
\[
z,\quad a,\quad t,\quad \xi_i,\quad y_i.
\]
They satisfy
\[
0\leq a\leq1,\qquad z,t,\xi_i,y_i\geq0,
\qquad
a=z+\xi_1+\xi_2+\xi_3,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
2=4a+3z+t.
\tag{5}
\]
Row \(\mathrm{F0}\), as used in `084bf2f472bf29c7`, gives
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2\leq4k.
\]
After division by \(p_0\) and passage to the limit,
\[
(1+\sqrt a)^2\leq4t.
\tag{6}
\]

The ratio constraint of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` also has a
compact homogeneous limit.  For nonnegative triples define
\[
g(w_1,w_2,w_3)
=
\min_{S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}
\left(
2\sum_{i\notin S}w_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\sqrt{w_iw_j}
\right).
\]
That accepted fact gives
\(\eta_n>\epsilon(x_{1,n},x_{2,n},x_{3,n})\), hence
\(\epsilon(x_{1,n},x_{2,n},x_{3,n})\to0\).  If
\[
F_n=\frac{1-\sum_i x_{i,n}}2+
g(x_{1,n},x_{2,n},x_{3,n}),
\]
then the definition of \(\epsilon\) implies
\(\limsup F_n\leq0\).  Since \(g\) is homogeneous of degree one,
\[
z_nF_n=z_n-\frac{a_n}{2}
+g(\xi_{1,n},\xi_{2,n},\xi_{3,n}).
\]
Consequently the compact limit also satisfies
\[
z-\frac a2+g(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\leq0.
\tag{7}
\]
This is the exact projective form of
\(\epsilon(\lambda)\to0\), including \(z=0\), where the unscaled cap
ratios may diverge.

It remains to retain the three crossings.  In the all-double support,
\[
v_i=m-x_i.
\]
Also \(0\leq u_i\leq s_P+d_P\leq\eta\), and
\[
0\leq c_i-r_i\leq d_R+r\leq\frac{\eta}{3}.
\]
Divide
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
\]
by \(p_0\) and pass to the limit.  With
\[
H=\frac{2(1+a+t)}9,
\]
one obtains
\[
a-\xi_i+y_i=H.
\tag{8}
\]
Using (5),
\[
H=\frac{2a+z+t}{3},
\]
and therefore
\[
y_i=\xi_i+B,\qquad
B:=\frac{z+t-a}{3}.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover \(t=y_1+y_2+y_3\), since
\((d_R+r)/p_0\to0\).

The common additive term in (9) is strictly positive.  Indeed, if
\(z+t\leq a\), then (6) gives
\[
(1+\sqrt a)^2\leq4t\leq4(a-z)\leq4a.
\]
For \(0\leq a\leq1\),
\[
(1+\sqrt a)^2-4a
=(1-\sqrt a)(1+3\sqrt a)\geq0,
\]
with equality only at \(a=1\).  Thus every inequality above would be an
equality, forcing \(a=t=1\) and \(z=0\), contrary to (5), whose right side
would then be \(5\).  Hence
\[
B>0,\qquad y_i\geq B>0\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{10}
\]
In particular \(t>0\).

We now use only the common middle plane of the realizations.  For each
\(n\), choose a diameter segment of \(K_n\).  Apply an affine map sending
its endpoints to \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\), and scale the perpendicular
coordinate so that the maximum absolute distance of \(K_n\) from the
diameter line is \(1\).  A diameter has every point of \(K_n\) projected
between its endpoints.  Therefore the transformed body
\(\widetilde K_n\) satisfies
\[
\widetilde K_n\subseteq[0,1]\times[-1,1].
\]
It contains the triangle formed by the diameter and a point at
perpendicular distance \(1\), so
\[
\frac12\leq|\widetilde K_n|\leq2.
\tag{11}
\]
Affine maps preserve all exact-cell area ratios.

By (10),
\[
\frac{r_{i,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\frac{y_i}{t}>0.
\tag{12}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\frac{d_{R,n}+r_n}{k_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]
The empty middle cell has area zero in the canonical table.  Thus in
\(\widetilde K_n\), each of the three exact singleton cells has area
bounded away from zero, whereas the total area of the cells with
membership count \(0\), \(2\), or \(3\) tends to zero.

The compact convex sets \(\widetilde K_n\) have a Hausdorff-convergent
subsequence.  One direct justification is to take their support
functions on the unit circle: they are uniformly bounded and uniformly
Lipschitz, so a uniformly convergent subsequence defines a compact convex
limit \(\widetilde K\).  By (11), its area is at least \(1/2\).

For \(i=1,2,3\), let \(\widetilde H_{i,n}\) be the transformed middle
halfspace \(f_i\leq0\), and let \(\widetilde\ell_{i,n}\) be its boundary.
The \(i\)-th singleton cell gives a positive area on the
\(\widetilde H_{i,n}\) side, while the other two singleton cells give
positive area on the complementary side.  Hence every
\(\widetilde\ell_{i,n}\) meets the fixed rectangle.  Normalize its
oriented normal to unit length.  The normals and offsets lie in compact
sets, so after another subsequence the three oriented halfspaces converge
to halfspaces \(\widetilde H_i\) bounded by lines
\(\widetilde\ell_i\).

Away from the boundary of \(\widetilde K\) and the three limiting lines,
membership in all four sets
\(\widetilde K_n,\widetilde H_{1,n},
\widetilde H_{2,n},\widetilde H_{3,n}\)
eventually stabilizes.  These exceptional boundaries have planar area
zero.  Dominated convergence, (11)--(13), and area continuity for convex
bodies therefore give
\[
\left|
\widetilde K\cap\widetilde H_i
\cap\bigcap_{j\ne i}\operatorname{int}
(\mathbb R^2\setminus\widetilde H_j)
\right|>0
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{14}
\]
while almost every point of \(\widetilde K\) belongs to exactly one of
\(\widetilde H_1,\widetilde H_2,\widetilde H_3\).
\tag{15}

We finish with an elementary three-halfspace lemma.  Let \(K\) be a
two-dimensional convex body and \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) closed halfspaces such
that each exact singleton region has positive area.  Then it is
impossible that almost every point of \(K\) belongs to exactly one
\(H_i\).

To prove the lemma, put \(U=\operatorname{int}K\).  On each component of
\[
U\setminus(\partial H_1\cup\partial H_2\cup\partial H_3)
\]
the membership count is constant.  If it were not one, that component
would be a nonempty open set of forbidden membership and hence would have
positive area.  Thus the count is one on every such component.

Each line \(\partial H_i\) crosses \(U\): the \(i\)-th singleton supplies
positive area on its \(H_i\) side, and either of the other singleton
regions supplies positive area on its complementary side.  If
\(\partial H_i\) were distinct from both other boundary lines, choose a
point of \(\partial H_i\cap U\) away from their at most two intersection
points.  In a small disk about that point, crossing only
\(\partial H_i\) changes the membership count by one.  It therefore
cannot equal one on both sides, a contradiction.  Hence every one of the
three boundary lines must coincide with another.  With three lines this
forces all three to coincide.  If \(s\) of the three halfspaces select
one side of their common line, the membership counts on its two sides
are \(s\) and \(3-s\).  They cannot both equal one.  This proves the
lemma.

Applying the lemma to (14)--(15) is a contradiction.  Since the argument
was independent of the compact unequal-cap endpoint variables, it rules
out the asserted sequence on every one of their endpoint and proper-cell
boundary strata.
