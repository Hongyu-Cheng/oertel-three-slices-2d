## Statement

Put
\[
\begin{split}
B_* = \operatorname{conv}\{&(-100,-100),
\left(-90,-\frac{660}{7}\right),
\left(-30,-\frac{380}{7}\right),
\left(0,-\frac{67}{14}\right),\\
&\left(0,-\frac12\right),
\left(-\frac12,0\right),
\left(-\frac{67}{14},0\right),
\left(-\frac{380}{7},-30\right),
\left(-\frac{660}{7},-90\right)\}.
\end{split}
\]
There is no compact two-dimensional convex polygon \(K'\subset\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
B_*\subseteq K',\qquad |K'|=\frac{14553}{2},
\]
and simultaneously
\[
\left|K'\cap\{x\geq0\}\right|<\frac{205904}{63},
\qquad
\left|K'\cap\{y\geq0\}\right|<\frac{205904}{63},
\]
\[
\left|K'\cap\{x+y\leq1\}\right|<\frac{195104}{63}.
\]

## Proof

Suppose such a polygon \(K'\) exists.  Abbreviate
\[
k=\frac{14553}{2},\qquad
\tau=\frac{205904}{63},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{195104}{63}=\frac{27872}{9}.
\]
All cutting lines below have planar area zero.  Let
\[
U=K'\cap\{x+y>1\},
\]
and let the two positive-sum singleton cells be
\[
S_x=K'\cap\{x+y>1,\ y<0\},
\qquad
S_y=K'\cap\{x+y>1,\ x<0\}.
\]
On \(S_x\), one automatically has \(x>0\), and on \(S_y\), one automatically has \(y>0\).  The tail bound gives
\[
|U|=k-\left|K'\cap\{x+y\leq1\}\right|>k-\sigma.
\]
The complement of \(S_x\) in \(U\) is contained, up to a null line, in \(K'\cap\{y\geq0\}\).  Similarly, the complement of \(S_y\) in \(U\) is contained in \(K'\cap\{x\geq0\}\).  Therefore
\[
|S_x|>k-\sigma-\tau,
\qquad
|S_y|>k-\sigma-\tau.
\]
The common exact lower bound is
\[
R:=k-\sigma-\tau=\frac{114823}{126}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, both singleton cells are nonempty.

Choose \(X\in S_x\) and \(Y\in S_y\).  The segment \([X,Y]\subset K'\) lies strictly in \(x+y>1\).  It crosses \(y=0\) at a point \((\xi,0)\) with \(\xi>1\), and it crosses \(x=0\) at a point \((0,\eta)\) with \(\eta>1\).  Since \(B_*\) contains \((-1/2,0)\) and \((0,-1/2)\), convexity along the two axes gives
\[
(0,0)\in K'.
\tag{2}
\]

The intersections of \(K'\) with the coordinate axes are compact intervals.  Define their negative support depths by
\[
\alpha=-\min\{x:(x,0)\in K'\},
\qquad
\beta=-\min\{y:(0,y)\in K'\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(B_*\subseteq K'\), one has
\[
\alpha,\beta\geq\frac{67}{14}>\frac{100}{101}.
\tag{4}
\]
Also \(\alpha,\beta<100\).  Indeed, \(B_*\) contains \(Z=(-100,-100)\).  If, for example, \(\beta\geq100\), then convexity and (2) put
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{Z,(0,0),(0,-\beta)\}\subseteq
K'\cap\{x+y\leq1\}.
\]
This triangle has area \(50\beta\geq5000>\sigma\), contrary to the tail bound.  The proof for \(\alpha\) is symmetric.

We next convert each large singleton area in (1) into an exact axis-support bound.  Fix \((x,y)\in S_x\).  The segment from \(Z=(-100,-100)\) to \((x,y)\) meets \(x=0\) at
\[
\left(0,-\frac{100(x-y)}{x+100}\right).
\]
This point belongs to \(K'\), so (3) gives
\[
\beta\geq\frac{100(x-y)}{x+100},
\]
or equivalently
\[
(100-\beta)x-100y\leq100\beta.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(S_x\) is contained in the triangle cut out by
\[
x\geq0,\qquad y\leq0,\qquad x+y\geq1,
\qquad (100-\beta)x-100y\leq100\beta.
\]
The three vertices of this triangle are
\[
(1,0),\qquad
\left(\frac{100\beta}{100-\beta},0\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{100(\beta+1)}{200-\beta},
-\frac{101\beta-100}{200-\beta}\right).
\]
Its area is
\[
F(\beta):=
\frac{(101\beta-100)^2}
{2(100-\beta)(200-\beta)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(|S_x|\leq F(\beta)\).  On the interval
\[
\frac{100}{101}<s<100,
\]
the expression
\[
F(s)=\frac12
\left(\frac{101s-100}{100-s}\right)
\left(\frac{101s-100}{200-s}\right)
\]
is strictly increasing.  Moreover
\[
F(40)=\frac{38809}{48},
\qquad
R-F(40)=\frac{103595}{1008}>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (1), (4), (6), and (7) imply
\[
\beta>40.
\tag{8}
\]
Interchanging \(x\) and \(y\), the same argument applied to \(S_y\) gives
\[
\alpha>40.
\tag{9}
\]

By (2), (3), (8), and (9), convexity along the coordinate axes puts
\[
(-40,0),(0,-40)\in K'.
\]
Together with the following three vertices of \(B_*\), this forces the convex hexagon
\[
\begin{split}
H=\operatorname{conv}\{&(-100,-100),
\left(-90,-\frac{660}{7}\right),(0,-40),(0,0),\\
&(-40,0),\left(-\frac{660}{7},-90\right)\}
\subseteq K'.
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
The six displayed vertices are in cyclic order.  Their successive turn determinants are
\[
\frac{200}{7},\quad3600,\quad1600,\quad3600,
\quad\frac{200}{7},\quad\frac{3300}{49},
\]
so \(H\) is two-dimensional and convex.  Shoelace gives
\[
|H|=\frac{28200}{7}.
\tag{11}
\]
Every vertex in (10) lies in the closed negative quadrant.  Hence
\[
H\subseteq K'\cap\{x+y\leq1\}.
\]
But (11) exceeds the tail budget by the exact positive margin
\[
\frac{28200}{7}-\frac{195104}{63}
=\frac{58696}{63}>0.
\]
This contradicts the assumed strict tail bound and proves that no such \(K'\) exists.
