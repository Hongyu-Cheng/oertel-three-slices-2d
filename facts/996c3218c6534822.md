## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact
two-dimensional convex bodies, and write \(a_i=|A_i|\).  For
\(u\in\mathbb S^1\), put
\[
G_i^u(c)=
\left|A_i\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq c\}\right|,
\qquad
R_i(u)=\max_{w\in A_i}\langle u,w\rangle ,
\]
and
\[
(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(r)
=
\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\left(G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)\right).
\]
Then, for every \(u\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(r\in\mathbb R\),
\[
(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(r)
\geq
\min\left\{a_2,G_0^u\bigl(r-R_2(u)\bigr)\right\},
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(r)
\geq
\min\left\{a_0,G_2^u\bigl(r-R_0(u)\bigr)\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For any nonempty compact two-dimensional convex body \(A\), define its
planar depth at every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), not only at points of \(A\), by
\[
d_A(x)=
\inf\left\{
|A\cap Q|:
Q\text{ is a closed planar halfplane and }x\in Q
\right\},
\tag{3}
\]
and define the closed depth region
\[
D_A(\alpha)=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:d_A(x)\geq\alpha\}
\qquad(0\leq\alpha\leq |A|).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(z\in A_1\), set
\[
\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)
=
\min_{x\in 2z-A_2}d_{A_0}(x),
\qquad
\delta_{2\leftarrow0}(z)
=
\min_{x\in 2z-A_0}d_{A_2}(x).
\tag{5}
\]
These minima exist, and they are exactly the largest closed depth levels
containing the corresponding reflected bodies:
\[
\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)
=
\max\{\alpha\in[0,a_0]:2z-A_2\subseteq D_{A_0}(\alpha)\},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\delta_{2\leftarrow0}(z)
=
\max\{\alpha\in[0,a_2]:2z-A_0\subseteq D_{A_2}(\alpha)\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)
\]
carry the sum of planar areas, as in the locked problem.  For
\(z\in A_1\), the direction-dependent consequence of (1) and (2) is
\[
\begin{split}
h_S((1,z))\geq
\min\Bigg\{&
a_0+a_1,\ a_1+a_2,\\
&\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\Big[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+\max\Big\{
\min\{a_2,G_0^u(2\langle u,z\rangle-R_2(u))\},\\
&\hspace{42mm}
\min\{a_0,G_2^u(2\langle u,z\rangle-R_0(u))\}
\Big\}
\Big]
\Bigg\}.
\end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, the reflected-depth lower bound is
\[
\begin{split}
h_S((1,z))\geq
\min\Big\{&
a_0+a_1,\ a_1+a_2,\\
&d_{A_1}(z)
+\max\big\{
\min\{a_2,\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)\},
\min\{a_0,\delta_{2\leftarrow0}(z)\}
\big\}
\Big\}.
\end{split}
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently, for a prescribed \(q\geq0\), the reflected-depth bound (9)
certifies \(h_S((1,z))\geq q\) if and only if
\[
q\leq a_0+a_1,\qquad q\leq a_1+a_2,
\tag{10}
\]
and at least one of the following two routing conditions holds:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{\rm(i)}&
\text{there is }\alpha\in[0,a_0]\text{ such that }
2z-A_2\subseteq D_{A_0}(\alpha)
\text{ and }
d_{A_1}(z)+\min\{a_2,\alpha\}\geq q,\\[1mm]
\text{\rm(ii)}&
\text{there is }\alpha\in[0,a_2]\text{ such that }
2z-A_0\subseteq D_{A_2}(\alpha)
\text{ and }
d_{A_1}(z)+\min\{a_0,\alpha\}\geq q.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]
The phrase "if and only if" here concerns certification by the explicit
lower bound (9); it is not a necessary condition for the actual depth to be
at least \(q\).

For the target \(q=2(a_0+a_1+a_2)/9\), conditions (10) are automatic in
the strict balanced regime \(a_i<(a_0+a_1+a_2)/2\).  Outside that regime
they must not be omitted.  No centroid, and in particular no identity
relating a centroid of \((A_0+A_2)/2\) to endpoint centroids, is used.

## Proof

The cap profile \(G_i^u\) is nondecreasing because its defining sublevel
sets are nested.  Compactness gives the finite support maximum \(R_i(u)\),
and the use of a closed cap gives
\[
G_i^u(c)=a_i\qquad\text{whenever }c\geq R_i(u),
\tag{12}
\]
including equality at \(c=R_i(u)\).

Fix \(u,r,\tau\).  If \(r-\tau\geq R_2(u)\), then (12) gives
\[
G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)\geq a_2.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(r-\tau<R_2(u)\), then
\(\tau>r-R_2(u)\), so monotonicity gives
\[
G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)
\geq G_0^u(r-R_2(u)).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus every value indexed by \(\tau\) is at least the minimum on the
right-hand side of (1).  Taking the infimum over \(\tau\) proves (1).
Notice that the equality case \(r-\tau=R_2(u)\) belongs to (13), so there
is no open-boundary gap.

For (2), if \(\tau\geq R_0(u)\), then the first cap is exactly \(a_0\).
If \(\tau<R_0(u)\), then
\(r-\tau>r-R_0(u)\), and monotonicity of \(G_2^u\) gives
\[
G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)
\geq G_2^u(r-R_0(u)).
\tag{15}
\]
Taking the infimum proves (2), again with \(\tau=R_0(u)\) assigned to
the saturated branch.

For completeness, all extrema used above and below are genuine extrema.
If \((u_n,c_n)\to(u,c)\), the indicators of
\(\{\langle u_n,w\rangle\leq c_n\}\) converge pointwise away from the
line \(\{\langle u,w\rangle=c\}\).  The intersection of that line with a
two-dimensional convex body has planar area zero.  Dominated convergence
therefore makes \((u,c)\mapsto G_A^u(c)\) jointly continuous.  For fixed
\(u,r\), the continuous function
\[
\tau\longmapsto G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)
\]
is exactly \(a_2\) on a sufficiently negative tail and exactly \(a_0\)
on a sufficiently positive tail.  Its infimum is therefore attained on
a bounded closed interval.

Moving the boundary of any closed planar halfplane containing \(x\)
parallel toward \(x\) can only decrease its intersection with \(A\).
Both orientations occur as \(v\) ranges over \(\mathbb S^1\), so
\[
d_A(x)=
\min_{v\in\mathbb S^1}G_A^v(\langle v,x\rangle).
\tag{16}
\]
The minimum is attained by joint continuity and compactness of the circle.
The same joint continuity shows that \(d_A\) is continuous in \(x\).
Consequently \(D_A(\alpha)\) is closed, each reflected body in (5) is
compact, and both minima in (5) are attained.  Directly from (4) and (5),
\[
2z-A_2\subseteq D_{A_0}(\alpha)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha\leq\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z),
\tag{17}
\]
and the analogous equivalence holds after exchanging \(0\) and \(2\).
Because the inequality in (4) is nonstrict, (17) remains true at
\(\alpha=\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)\), proving (6) and (7) without an
epsilon loss.  Also, \(D_A(0)=\mathbb R^2\).  If \(\alpha>0\), then
\(D_A(\alpha)\) lies in the interior of \(A\), since strict separation
gives depth zero outside \(A\), while a supporting line gives depth zero
on its boundary.

We next derive the halfspace bound and keep the horizontal cases explicit.
For a closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\), move its boundary parallel
toward \((1,z)\) until that point lies on the boundary.  This only
decreases its \(S\)-mass.  If the planar component of the boundary normal
is nonzero, normalize it to a unit vector \(u\).  There is then a unique
\(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) for which the halfspace is
\[
\langle u,w-z\rangle\leq\lambda(1-x).
\tag{18}
\]
Writing \(t=\langle u,z\rangle\), its three slice thresholds are
\[
t+\lambda,\qquad t,\qquad t-\lambda.
\tag{19}
\]
The substitution \(\tau=t+\lambda\) turns the sum of the endpoint caps
into
\[
G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(2t-\tau).
\tag{20}
\]
If the planar component of the normal is zero, the halfspace is one of
\(\{x\leq1\}\) and \(\{x\geq1\}\).  Since these halfspaces are closed,
their boundary contains the whole middle slice, so their exact masses are
\[
a_0+a_1,\qquad a_1+a_2.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus
\[
h_S((1,z))
=
\min\left\{
a_0+a_1,\ a_1+a_2,\
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_1^u(t)+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2t)
\right]
\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
The horizontal terms in (21) are not limits of (18): a tilted boundary
meets each slice in a planar line of area zero, whereas a horizontal
boundary contains all of \(A_1\), of area \(a_1\).

Apply (1) and (2) to \(r=2t\).  Both inequalities concern the same
infimal convolution, hence
\[
\begin{split}
(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2t)\geq
\max\Big\{&
\min\{a_2,G_0^u(2t-R_2(u))\},\\
&\min\{a_0,G_2^u(2t-R_0(u))\}
\Big\}.
\end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
Substitution in (22) proves (8).  There is no valid step replacing the
maximum in (23) by a sum.

The reflected interpretation is exact.  The minimum of
\(\langle u,x\rangle\) over \(x\in2z-A_2\) is attained and equals
\[
\min_{x\in2z-A_2}\langle u,x\rangle
=2t-R_2(u).
\tag{24}
\]
Choose a minimizer \(x_u\).  Equations (5), (6), and (16) give
\[
G_0^u(2t-R_2(u))
=G_0^u(\langle u,x_u\rangle)
\geq d_{A_0}(x_u)
\geq\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z).
\tag{25}
\]
The exchanged argument gives
\[
G_2^u(2t-R_0(u))
\geq\delta_{2\leftarrow0}(z).
\tag{26}
\]
Finally, (16) gives
\[
G_1^u(t)\geq d_{A_1}(z)
\tag{27}
\]
for every \(u\).  Insert (25), (26), and (27) into (8) to obtain (9).

It remains only to verify the routing formulation.  By (6), condition
(11)(i) holds for some \(\alpha\) if and only if it holds with the largest
possible choice
\(\alpha=\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)\).  The same observation using (7)
applies to (11)(ii).  Hence at least one line of (11) is equivalent to
\[
d_{A_1}(z)
+\max\big\{
\min\{a_2,\delta_{0\leftarrow2}(z)\},
\min\{a_0,\delta_{2\leftarrow0}(z)\}
\big\}
\geq q.
\tag{28}
\]
Together with the two horizontal requirements (10), this is exactly the
condition that the right-hand side of (9) be at least \(q\).  If
\(a_i<T/2\) for all \(i\), where \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), then both quantities
in (21) exceed \(T/2>2T/9\), proving the final assertion.  Every argument
uses the chosen point \(z\), support extrema, and cap depths only; no
centroid relation appears.
