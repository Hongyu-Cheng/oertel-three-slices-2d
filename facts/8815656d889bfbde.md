## Statement

Let all parameters in
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_\alpha&=(a,b),&
Z_v&=(-c,c+d),&
Z_\beta&=(-e-f,e),&
A&=(-p,0),\\
Z_a&=(-g,-h),&
B&=(0,-q),&
Z_\gamma&=(i,-i-j),&
Z_u&=(\ell+n,-\ell)
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
be positive and rational.  Suppose the eight displayed points are the
vertices, in this cyclic order, of a strictly convex octagon \(K\).  Put
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,B\},\qquad
P=\lambda D,\qquad Q=\mu D,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\lambda,\mu\) are rational and
\[
\lambda\geq\mu>0,\qquad \lambda+\mu=2.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
\Delta=|D|=\frac{pq}{2},\qquad
M=|P|,\qquad m=|Q|,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{x\geq0\}|,\qquad
c_\ell=|K\cap\{y\geq0\}|,\qquad
c_q=|K\cap\{x+y\leq1\}|,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
D_0=3t-k-m.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
r=\frac{m}{M}=\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^2,
\qquad s=\frac{k}{M},
\]
\[
p_x=|P\cap\{x\geq0\}|+|Q\cap\{x\geq0\}|,
\qquad
p_y=|P\cap\{y\geq0\}|+|Q\cap\{y\geq0\}|.
\]
For every compact convex polygon \(E\), define
\[
d(E)=\max_E(x+y)-\min_E x-\min_E y.
\]
Assume the exact residual area domain of accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e`, the high-defect condition \(D_0>m\), and the three
strict rows
\[
c_i<t,\qquad c_\ell<t,\qquad c_q+m<t.
\tag{7}
\]

Then all of the following conclusions hold.

First, the origin lies in \(\operatorname{int}K\), and
\[
D\subseteq K,\qquad
\frac{P+Q}{2}=D,\qquad
p_x=p_y=0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the accepted side-cone gate `c3aabe018c39779d` applies before the
remaining scalar filters.

Second, the support condition is strict:
\[
\frac{(d(P)+d(Q))^2}{8}>M+m.
\tag{9}
\]

Third, every such configuration satisfies the exact homothety cutoff
\[
0<\mu<\sqrt{71}-8.
\tag{10}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{\mu}{\lambda}<\frac{2\sqrt{71}-9}{29}.
\tag{11}
\]

Fourth, define the actual open-cone areas
\[
\alpha=|K\cap\{x>0,y>0\}|,
\]
\[
\beta=|K\cap\{x<0,y>0,x+y<0\}|,
\qquad
\gamma=|K\cap\{x>0,y<0,x+y<0\}|,
\tag{12}
\]
and put
\[
C=c+d,\qquad L=\ell+n,\qquad
H=CL-c\ell=cn+d\ell+dn.
\tag{13}
\]
Convexity forces
\[
\beta\geq\frac{p^2C}{2(p+d)},\qquad
\gamma\geq\frac{q^2L}{2(q+n)},\qquad
\alpha\geq\frac{H^2}{2(C+\ell)(c+L)}.
\tag{14}
\]

Finally, specialize only the scalar data to
\[
\mu=\frac25,\qquad
\lambda=\frac85,\qquad
k=\frac{13}{5}\Delta.
\tag{15}
\]
Then the residual and high-defect inequalities are strict, with
\[
r=\frac1{16},\qquad s=\frac{65}{64},\qquad
t=\frac{266}{225}\Delta,\qquad
D_0=\frac{59}{75}\Delta>m=\frac4{25}\Delta.
\tag{16}
\]
Any octagon satisfying (7) at this calibration necessarily obeys
\[
\frac{p^2C}{2(p+d)}
+\frac{q^2L}{2(q+n)}
<\frac{\Delta}{45}
=\frac{pq}{90}.
\tag{17}
\]

## Proof

Write the vertices in (1), in order, as \(V_0,\ldots,V_7\).  Since \(K\)
is strictly convex in this cyclic order, all eight turn determinants are
positive.  The signed facet evaluations of the origin are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{edge}&\det(V_r,V_{r+1})\\ \hline
Z_\alpha Z_v&a(c+d)+bc\\
Z_v Z_\beta&de+(c+d)f\\
Z_\beta A&pe\\
AZ_a&ph\\
Z_aB&gq\\
BZ_\gamma&qi\\
Z_\gamma Z_u&j\ell+(i+j)n\\
Z_uZ_\alpha&b(\ell+n)+a\ell .
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]
Every quantity in the right column is strictly positive.  For an oriented
edge \(V_rV_{r+1}\), its signed test at the origin is
\[
\det(V_{r+1}-V_r,-V_r)=\det(V_r,V_{r+1}).
\]
Thus the origin lies strictly to the left of every facet, proving
\((0,0)\in\operatorname{int}K\).  Since \(A,B\) are vertices of the
convex octagon, all three vertices of \(D\) lie in \(K\), and hence
\[
D\subseteq K.
\tag{19}
\]
This is the exact facet-containment check requested in the statement.

Accepted fact `c3aabe018c39779d` now applies to (19).  In particular, if
\[
\eta=|K\cap\{0<x+y\leq1\}|,
\]
then the actual cells satisfy
\[
\alpha\leq\eta
\]
or
\[
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q\right)>1.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus this family passes through the accepted side-cone gate; no cone-area
table has been assigned independently.

Homothety and (3) give
\[
M=\lambda^2\Delta,\qquad
m=\mu^2\Delta,\qquad
\frac{P+Q}{2}
=\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2}D
=D.
\tag{21}
\]
Both endpoint triangles lie in the closed negative quadrant.  Their
intersections with either \(x\geq0\) or \(y\geq0\) are contained in line
segments and therefore have area zero.  This proves \(p_x=p_y=0\), and
completes (8).

For the support statistic in the registered subgoal,
\[
d(D)=p+q.
\]
Consequently,
\[
d(P)+d(Q)=(\lambda+\mu)(p+q)=2(p+q),
\]
and
\[
\frac{(d(P)+d(Q))^2}{8}
=\frac{(p+q)^2}{2}
\geq2pq.
\tag{22}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
M+m=(\lambda^2+\mu^2)\frac{pq}{2}<2pq,
\tag{23}
\]
because
\[
\lambda^2+\mu^2
=(\lambda+\mu)^2-2\lambda\mu
<4.
\]
Equations (22)-(23) prove (9).

Put
\[
x=\frac{\mu}{\lambda},\qquad
r=x^2,\qquad
s=\frac{k}{M}.
\tag{24}
\]
The lower endpoint \(L(r)\) in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`
satisfies \(L(r)\geq1-r\).  Hence the residual strip gives
\[
s>1-r.
\tag{25}
\]
Also
\[
\frac{D_0}{M}
=\frac{3t-k-m}{M}
=\frac{2-r-s}{3}.
\]
The inequality \(D_0>m=rM\) is therefore exactly
\[
s<2-4r.
\tag{26}
\]
Combining (25) and (26) yields \(r<1/3\).  Thus the applicable residual
upper endpoint is \(U(r)=1+r\), and
\[
s<1+r,\qquad
k<M+m=(\lambda^2+\mu^2)\Delta.
\tag{27}
\]

Every point of \(D\) has \(x+y\leq0\).  By (19),
\[
c_q=|K\cap\{x+y\leq1\}|\geq\Delta.
\tag{28}
\]
The strict \(q\)-row in (7) and (28) imply
\[
\Delta+\mu^2\Delta
<
\frac{2\bigl((\lambda^2+\mu^2)\Delta+k\bigr)}9.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
k>\frac{9+7\mu^2-2\lambda^2}{2}\,\Delta.
\tag{29}
\]
For (29) to be compatible with the strict upper bound (27), it is
necessary that
\[
\frac{9+7\mu^2-2\lambda^2}{2}
<\lambda^2+\mu^2,
\]
or
\[
9+5\mu^2<4\lambda^2.
\tag{30}
\]
Substituting \(\lambda=2-\mu\) into (30) gives
\[
\mu^2+16\mu-7<0.
\tag{31}
\]
The positive root of the quadratic in (31) is \(\sqrt{71}-8\), while
the other root is negative.  Since \(\mu>0\), (31) is equivalent to
(10).  Finally,
\[
\frac{\sqrt{71}-8}{2-(\sqrt{71}-8)}
=\frac{\sqrt{71}-8}{10-\sqrt{71}}
=\frac{2\sqrt{71}-9}{29},
\]
which proves (11).

It remains to prove the forced cell bounds (14).  The segment
\([A,Z_v]\subseteq K\) meets \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_\beta=(-R_\beta,R_\beta),
\qquad
R_\beta=\frac{pC}{p+d}.
\tag{32}
\]
Apart from its null boundary, the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,W_\beta\}\) lies in the open
\(\beta\)-cone.  Therefore
\[
\beta\geq\frac{pR_\beta}{2}
=\frac{p^2C}{2(p+d)}.
\tag{33}
\]
Similarly, \([B,Z_u]\) meets \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_\gamma=(R_\gamma,-R_\gamma),
\qquad
R_\gamma=\frac{qL}{q+n},
\tag{34}
\]
so
\[
\gamma\geq\frac{qR_\gamma}{2}
=\frac{q^2L}{2(q+n)}.
\tag{35}
\]

The segment \([Z_v,Z_u]\subseteq K\) has \(x+y>0\) throughout, because
its endpoint coordinate sums are \(d,n>0\).  Its intersections with the
positive \(x\)- and \(y\)-axes are, respectively,
\[
\left(\frac{H}{C+\ell},0\right),
\qquad
\left(0,\frac{H}{c+L}\right).
\tag{36}
\]
The triangle formed by these two points and the origin is contained in
\(\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),Z_v,Z_u\}\subseteq K\), and its interior
lies in the positive quadrant.  Its area is
\[
\frac{H^2}{2(C+\ell)(c+L)}.
\]
This proves the third inequality in (14).

For the calibration (15), direct exact arithmetic gives
\[
M=\frac{64}{25}\Delta,\qquad
m=\frac4{25}\Delta,\qquad
r=\frac1{16},\qquad
s=\frac{65}{64}.
\]
Since \(r<9/25\), the residual interval is
\[
1-r=\frac{15}{16}<\frac{65}{64}
<\frac{17}{16}=1+r.
\]
Also
\[
t=\frac{266}{225}\Delta,\qquad
D_0=3t-k-m=\frac{59}{75}\Delta
>\frac4{25}\Delta=m.
\]
This proves (16).

The negative-quadrant cell of the octagon is exactly
\[
\left|K\cap\{x<0,y<0\}\right|
=\left|\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,Z_a,B\}\right|
=\frac{ph+gq}{2}.
\tag{37}
\]
The turn determinant at \(Z_a\) is
\[
\det(Z_a-A,B-Z_a)=ph+gq-pq>0,
\]
so (37) is strictly larger than \(\Delta=pq/2\).  The \(q\)-clip contains
the disjoint open negative-quadrant, \(\beta\), and \(\gamma\) cells.
Using (7), (16), and (37),
\[
\beta+\gamma
<
\frac{46}{45}\Delta-\frac{ph+gq}{2}
<\frac{\Delta}{45}.
\tag{38}
\]
Substitution of (33) and (35) into (38) proves (17).

All cutting lines and polygon boundaries used above have planar measure
zero, so the open-cone areas, closed clips, and strict inequalities are
compatible exactly as stated.
