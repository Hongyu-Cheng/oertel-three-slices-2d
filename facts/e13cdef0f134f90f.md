## Statement

Put
\[
\ell _1=z+\tau,\qquad \ell _2=\tau-z,\qquad \ell _3=-2\tau
\]
and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,2),(-1,-2),(2,-1/5),(2,1/5)\}.
\]
Let the six open fan sectors be
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A:\tau<0,\ \tau<z<-\tau,&B:\tau<0,\ z>-\tau,&F:\tau<0,\ z<\tau,\\
D:\tau>0,\ -\tau<z<\tau,&C:\tau>0,\ z>\tau,&E:\tau>0,\ z<-\tau.
\end{array}
\]
For every varied body \(K_s\), define
\[
T(s):=|K_s|,
\qquad
c_i(K_s):=\left|K_s\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}\right|
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
and define its sector areas by
\[
A(s):=|K_s\cap A|,\quad B(s):=|K_s\cap B|,\quad
C(s):=|K_s\cap C|,
\]
\[
D(s):=|K_s\cap D|,\quad E(s):=|K_s\cap E|,\quad
F(s):=|K_s\cap F|.
\]
For a differentiable convex quadrilateral variation \(K_s\) with \(K_0=K\), write
\[
(a,b,c,d,e,f)
:=(A'(0),B'(0),C'(0),D'(0),E'(0),F'(0)),
\qquad
t:=T'(0)=a+b+c+d+e+f.
\]
These are signed area derivatives: a positive component means that the area in that sector increases, and a negative component means that it decreases.

Then every vector in \(\mathbb R^6\) is induced by such a variation. Consequently, at the level of first-order data seen by the cap ratios, this is also the complete geometric realizability classification for arbitrary differentiable convex-body variations.

At \(K\),
\[
p:=\frac{c_1(K)}{|K|}=\frac{c_2(K)}{|K|}=\frac{75}{176},
\qquad
q:=\frac{c_3(K)}{|K|}=\frac{16}{33}.
\]
The variations tangent to both normalized first-cap constraints are precisely
\[
a+e+f=pt,\qquad a+b+c=pt.
\]
Equivalently, \(a,c,d,e\) are arbitrary and
\[
t=\frac{88}{13}(d-a),\qquad
b=\frac{75d-101a-26c}{26},\qquad
f=\frac{75d-101a-26e}{26}.
\]
On this four-dimensional tangent space,
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_3(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=\frac{1}{|K|}
\left(c+e+\frac{1408a-979d}{429}\right).
\]
Thus the tangent direction is descending exactly when
\[
c+e+\frac{1408a-979d}{429}<0.
\]

In particular, the signed sector derivative vector
\[
(a,b,c,d,e,f)=(-1,1,0,-1,0,1)
\]
is geometrically realizable, is tangent to both normalized first-cap constraints, and strictly decreases the normalized third cap. One realizing family of actual rational convex quadrilaterals is
\[
\begin{split}
K_s=\operatorname{conv}\{&
(-1+s/2,\,2+2s),\
(-1+s/2,\,-2-2s),\\
&(2+13s/2,\,-1/5+32s/5),\
(2+13s/2,\,1/5-32s/5)\},
\end{split}
\]
defined, for example, for \(|s|<1/100\). It satisfies
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_1(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_2(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=0,
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_3(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=-\frac5{33}.
\]

## Proof

The fan lines have area zero. Inspecting the signs of the three linear forms on the six open sectors gives, for every \(K_s\),
\[
\{\ell_1<0\}=A\cup E\cup F,\qquad
\{\ell_2<0\}=A\cup B\cup C,\qquad
\{\ell_3<0\}=C\cup D\cup E
\]
up to those fan lines. Hence the cap areas defined above satisfy the three sector-union identities
\[
c_1(K_s)=A(s)+E(s)+F(s),
\]
\[
c_2(K_s)=A(s)+B(s)+C(s),
\]
\[
c_3(K_s)=C(s)+D(s)+E(s).
\]

The vertical section of \(K\) is
\[
[-h(\tau),h(\tau)],\qquad
h(\tau)=\frac75-\frac35\tau,\qquad -1\leq\tau\leq2.
\]
The top and bottom edges meet \(z=\tau\) and \(z=-\tau\), respectively, at \(\tau=7/8\). Direct integration gives
\[
|K|=\int_{-1}^{2}2h(\tau)\,d\tau=\frac{33}{5},
\qquad
c_3(K)=\int_0^2 2h(\tau)\,d\tau=\frac{16}{5}.
\]
Reflection in \(z=0\) interchanges the first two caps, and
\[
c_1(K)=c_2(K)
=\int_{-1}^{7/8}\bigl(h(\tau)-\tau\bigr)\,d\tau
=\frac{45}{16}.
\]
This proves the displayed values of \(p\) and \(q\).

We next classify the geometrically realizable sector derivatives. The sign convention for all four functions below is outward positive: a positive integral is signed area added to the body. On the original left, right, upper, and lower edges, respectively, let the signed outward area-flux densities be
\[
g_L(z)=-\dot\tau,\qquad
g_R(z)=\dot\tau,\qquad
g_U(\tau)=\dot z+\frac35\dot\tau,\qquad
g_V(\tau)=-\dot z+\frac35\dot\tau.
\]
For a vertex variation each of these four functions is affine on its edge. Conversely, any four affine functions \(g_L,g_R,g_U,g_V\) arise from a vertex variation. Indeed, if
\[
P_{UL}=(-1,2),\quad P_{LL}=(-1,-2),\quad
P_{UR}=(2,1/5),\quad P_{LR}=(2,-1/5),
\]
their endpoint values give the four vertex velocities uniquely:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\dot P_{UL}=\left(-g_L(2),\ g_U(-1)+\frac35g_L(2)\right),&
\dot P_{LL}=\left(-g_L(-2),\ -g_V(-1)-\frac35g_L(-2)\right),\\[2mm]
\dot P_{UR}=\left(g_R(1/5),\ g_U(2)-\frac35g_R(1/5)\right),&
\dot P_{LR}=\left(g_R(-1/5),\ \frac35g_R(-1/5)-g_V(2)\right).
\end{array}
\]
The original quadrilateral has four strict convex corners. Hence, for every fixed choice of these velocities, the vertices \(P+s\dot P\) remain in the same cyclic order and form a convex quadrilateral for all sufficiently small \(|s|\).

A moving straight boundary element sweeps, to first order, its signed outward normal displacement times its length. At a transversal crossing with a fixed fan ray, the region missed by using the old crossing point has both dimensions \(O(s)\), hence area \(O(s^2)\). Therefore the six signed sector derivatives are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=\int_{-1}^{1}g_L(z)\,dz,\\
b&=\int_{1}^{2}g_L(z)\,dz+\int_{-1}^{0}g_U(\tau)\,d\tau,\\
c&=\int_{0}^{7/8}g_U(\tau)\,d\tau,\\
d&=\int_{7/8}^{2}g_U(\tau)\,d\tau
+\int_{7/8}^{2}g_V(\tau)\,d\tau
+\int_{-1/5}^{1/5}g_R(z)\,dz,\\
e&=\int_{0}^{7/8}g_V(\tau)\,d\tau,\\
f&=\int_{-2}^{-1}g_L(z)\,dz+\int_{-1}^{0}g_V(\tau)\,d\tau.
\end{aligned}
\]

This map onto the six sector derivatives is surjective. Given arbitrary signed \(a,b,c,d,e,f\), first take \(g_L\equiv a/2\). Next choose the affine function \(g_U(\tau)=u+v\tau\) so that
\[
\int_{-1}^{0}g_U=b-\frac a2,\qquad
\int_{0}^{7/8}g_U=c.
\]
These two conditions have a unique solution because their coefficient determinant is
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\[1mm]7/8&49/128\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{105}{128}\ne0.
\]
Choose \(g_V\) in the same way so that
\[
\int_{-1}^{0}g_V=f-\frac a2,\qquad
\int_{0}^{7/8}g_V=e.
\]
Finally take \(g_R\) constant and choose its integral over \([-1/5,1/5]\) to make the fourth displayed sector formula equal to the prescribed \(d\). Thus every vector in \(\mathbb R^6\) is realized by an actual small convex quadrilateral variation. Since every differentiable boundary variation produces some vector in \(\mathbb R^6\), and the constraints and objective depend only on this vector, this also proves the asserted complete classification modulo variations invisible to all six sector areas.

Differentiating the three already established sector-union identities gives
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_1(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=\frac{a+e+f-pt}{|K|},
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_2(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=\frac{a+b+c-pt}{|K|},
\]
and
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_3(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=\frac{c+d+e-qt}{|K|}.
\]
This proves the two tangent equations. Using
\[
t=a+(b+c)+d+(e+f)
\]
in those equations gives
\[
(1-2p)t=d-a.
\]
Since \(1-2p=13/88\), solving for \(t,b,f\) gives the stated four-parameter description. Substituting \(q=16/33\) and \(t=88(d-a)/13\) in the third quotient derivative gives
\[
c+d+e-qt
=c+e+\frac{1408a-979d}{429},
\]
which proves the exact descent criterion.

It remains to verify that the displayed descending vector is realized by the displayed polygon family, rather than only by abstract sector bookkeeping. Its four edge-flux functions at \(s=0\) are
\[
g_L=-\frac12,\qquad
g_R=\frac{13}{2},\qquad
g_U(\tau)=g_V(\tau)=\frac7{10}-\frac85\tau.
\]
Substitution into the six sector formulas gives
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A'(0)=-1,&
B'(0)=-\frac12+\frac32=1,&
C'(0)=0,\\[1mm]
E'(0)=0,&
F'(0)=-\frac12+\frac32=1,&
D'(0)=-\frac95-\frac95+\frac{13}{5}=-1.
\end{array}
\]
Thus \(T'(0)=0\),
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}c_1(K_s)\right|_{s=0}
=
\left.\frac{d}{ds}c_2(K_s)\right|_{s=0}
=0,
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{ds}c_3(K_s)\right|_{s=0}
=-1,
\]
which already proves the three ratio derivatives in the statement.

For completeness, the family is convex with the same fan-intersection pattern throughout \(|s|<1/100\): its vertical sides lie at
\[
L_s=-1+\frac s2,\qquad R_s=2+\frac{13s}{2},
\]
and have positive half-heights
\[
H_L(s)=2+2s,\qquad H_R(s)=\frac15-\frac{32s}{5}.
\]
The region between the two vertical lines and the two affine graphs joining these positive endpoint heights is an intersection of four half-planes. Moreover \(H_L(s)>-L_s\), \(H_R(s)<R_s\), and \(0\) lies strictly between \(L_s\) and \(R_s\), so the same transversal sector decomposition used above persists.

An independent exact calculation gives
\[
T(s)=\frac{33}{5}(1-4s^2),
\]
\[
c_1(K_s)=c_2(K_s)
=\frac{45(2+s)^2(1+2s)}{64(1+3s)},
\]
and
\[
c_3(K_s)
=\frac{(4+13s)(16-25s-74s^2)}{20(1+2s)}.
\]
Differentiating at zero again yields
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_1(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_2(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=0,
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{c_3(K_s)}{T(s)}\right|_{s=0}
=-\frac5{33}<0.
\]
Hence \(c_3(K_s)/T(s)<16/33\) for every sufficiently small positive \(s\), completing the convex geometric realization of the descent direction.
