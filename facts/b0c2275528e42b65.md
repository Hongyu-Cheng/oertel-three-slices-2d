## Statement

Let \(\ell _1,\ell _2,\ell _3\) be nonzero pairwise nonparallel linear
forms on \(\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
\ell _1+\ell _2+\ell _3=0,
\]
and let \(O\) be their common zero. For an area-one convex polygon \(P\),
put
\[
p_i(P)=|P\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|,\qquad
\Delta(P)=p_3(P)-(1-p_1(P))(1-p_2(P)).
\]
If
\[
p_1(P),p_2(P)\leq\frac12,
\]
then
\[
\Delta(P)\geq0.
\]
More precisely, if a polygon with \(\Delta<0\) existed, compactness of a
fixed negative-defect sublevel would produce a negative local minimizer in
a fan-transverse polygon stratum. Hence the accepted rank localization and
terminal-triangle facts apply.

## Proof

Write \(H_i=\{\ell_i\leq0\}\), and use the sector labels
\(A,B,C,D,E,F\) from accepted fact `1b4ea45680c75628`. Thus, away from
the fan lines,
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_1&=A\cup E\cup F,\\
H_2&=A\cup B\cup C,\\
H_3&=C\cup D\cup E.
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{1}
\]

Suppose that an area-one polygon \(P_0\) satisfies the two cap
inequalities and
\[
\Delta(P_0)<0.                                                    \tag{2}
\]
Accepted fact `44d54af9bf8d0bd5` implies
\[
O\in\operatorname{int}P_0.                                      \tag{3}
\]
We may also arrange that both cap inequalities are strict. Indeed,
translation by \(t v\) changes
\[
|P_0\cap H_i|
=\int_{-\infty}^{-t\ell_i(v)} L_i(s)\,ds,
\]
where \(L_i(s)\) is the appropriately normalized length of the section
\(P_0\cap\{\ell_i=s\}\). Since \(O\) is interior,
\(L_i(0)>0\). Thus an active cap strictly decreases for small \(t>0\)
whenever \(\ell_i(v)>0\). For one active cap choose such a \(v\); for two
active caps choose \(v\) with
\(\ell_1(v),\ell_2(v)>0\). The inactive cap remains below one half,
\(O\) remains interior, and (2) persists by continuity. Denote the
resulting polygon by \(Q\). Then
\[
p_1(Q),p_2(Q)<\frac12,\qquad \Delta(Q)<0.                         \tag{4}
\]

Let \(N\) be the number of vertices of \(Q\), and put
\[
\phi(t)=\frac{2t}{1-2t}\qquad(0\leq t<1/2).
\]
This function is nonnegative, strictly increasing, and tends to infinity
at \(1/2\). Choose \(\eta>0\) so small that
\[
\mathcal F(Q):=\Delta(Q)+
\eta\bigl(\phi(p_1(Q))+\phi(p_2(Q))\bigr)<0.                      \tag{5}
\]
Minimize
\[
\mathcal F(P)=\Delta(P)+
\eta\bigl(\phi(p_1(P))+\phi(p_2(P))\bigr)                         \tag{6}
\]
over area-one convex polygons with at most \(N\) vertices and
\(p_1,p_2<1/2\).

This minimum is attained. To see this, restrict to the nonempty sublevel
\(\mathcal F\leq\mathcal F(Q)\). Since the barrier is nonnegative,
\[
\Delta(P)\leq\mathcal F(P)\leq\mathcal F(Q)<0.                   \tag{7}
\]
Every polygon in this sublevel is centered by accepted fact
`44d54af9bf8d0bd5`, and accepted fact `c4b10eea7cd8ea81` gives a
uniform diameter bound. Since the polygons contain \(O\), they lie in
one fixed ball. Also \(\Delta\geq-1\), so (6) and the barrier blow-up
keep \(p_1,p_2\) a fixed positive distance below \(1/2\). The class of
area-one convex polygons with at most \(N\) vertices in a fixed ball is
Hausdorff closed: represent each polygon as the convex hull of \(N\)
points, allowing repetitions, and use continuity of area. Halfplane-cut
areas are Hausdorff continuous for two-dimensional convex bodies.
Consequently the sublevel is compact.

Let \(P\) be a minimizer, and retain only its genuine vertices. By (7),
\[
\Delta(P)<0,\qquad O\in\operatorname{int}P.                      \tag{8}
\]
We next prove that no vertex of \(P\) lies on a fan ray.

For this purpose we record the first variation also at a fan-ray vertex.
No edge of a centered polygon can lie on a fan line. If a vertex lies on
a fan ray, moving the vertex by \(O(s)\) changes the old and new
line-crossing descriptions only inside a triangle of area \(O(s^2)\).
Thus all six sector areas are differentiable in the vertex coordinates,
with the same swept-boundary formula as in accepted fact
`1b4ea45680c75628`. If an oriented edge is
\[
x_e(t)=V_e+t(V_{e+1}-V_e),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
\]
and \(\lambda(x_e(t))\) denotes the weight of the open sector containing
the point, stationarity is equivalent on every edge to
\[
\int_0^1(1-t)\lambda(x_e(t))\,dt=0,\qquad
\int_0^1t\lambda(x_e(t))\,dt=0.                                 \tag{9}
\]

Small arbitrary motions of the genuine vertices preserve convexity and
the condition \(O\in\operatorname{int}P\). If such a motion changes the
area, rescaling about \(O\) restores area one without changing any
\(p_i\) or \(\mathcal F\). Hence the derivative of \(\mathcal F\)
vanishes for every vertex velocity.

Let \(h_i\) be the six-component indicator vectors in (1), let
\(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), and put \(u_i=h_i-p_i\mathbf1\). Since
\[
d\Delta=dp_3+(1-p_2)\,dp_1+(1-p_1)\,dp_2,
\]
the sector weight in (9) is
\[
\lambda=u_3+\alpha u_1+\beta u_2,                               \tag{10}
\]
where
\[
\alpha=1-p_2+\eta\phi'(p_1)>0,\qquad
\beta =1-p_1+\eta\phi'(p_2)>0.                                  \tag{11}
\]
In particular \(\lambda\) belongs to
\[
\mathcal W=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,h_1,h_2,h_3\}.
\]
If
\[
m=p_3+\alpha p_1+\beta p_2,
\]
then its values in cyclic sector order are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
 &A&B&C&D&E&F\\ \hline
\lambda&
\alpha+\beta-m&\beta-m&1+\beta-m&
1-m&1+\alpha-m&\alpha-m.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{12}
\]
Every adjacent pair in (12) has difference, up to sign, equal to one
of \(\alpha,\beta,1\). Therefore
\[
\text{no two cyclically adjacent sector weights are both zero.} \tag{13}
\]

We now give the required fan-ray edge classification.

**Fan-ray lemma.** Let a centered convex polygon have at least one
vertex on a fan ray. Suppose a nonzero sector weight \(\lambda\) satisfies
(9), belongs to \(\mathcal W\), and has property (13). Then no such
polygon exists.

For an edge \(e\), let \(k_e\) be the number of fan rays meeting its
relative interior. An edge cannot meet both members of an opposite
pair of rays, since its supporting line would then pass through \(O\).
Thus \(0\leq k_e\leq3\). If \(r\geq1\) fan rays meet vertices, then
\[
\sum_e k_e=6-r\leq5.                                             \tag{14}
\]
For an interval \(I\subset(0,1)\), set
\[
M(I)=\left(\int_I(1-t)\,dt,\int_I t\,dt\right).
\]
For two disjoint positive-length intervals \(I<J\),
\[
\det(M(I),M(J))
=|I|\,|J|\,(\overline t_J-\overline t_I)>0.                      \tag{15}
\]
Consequently a \(k_e=0\) edge forces the weight of its sector to be
zero; a \(k_e=1\) edge forces two adjacent weights to be zero; and on a
\(k_e=2\) edge, one zero among its three sector weights forces the
other two to be zero.

By (13), no edge has \(k_e=1\). Equation (14), together with the fact
that a polygon has at least three edges, then forces at least one
\(k_e=0\) edge. The edges with \(k_e>0\) have crossing counts only two
or three, and there are at most two of them.

There is a short exhaustive boundary walk. If there are no positive-
crossing edges, every fan ray is a vertex between two zero-crossing
edges, giving adjacent zero sector weights. If there is one positive-
crossing edge, at least \(6-k_e\geq3\) fan rays are vertices; at most
two are endpoints of that edge, so another one again lies between two
zero-crossing edges and gives adjacent zeros.

It remains that there are exactly two positive-crossing edges. Their
crossing counts are either \(2,2\) or \(3,2\). In the first case
\(r=2\), and in the second \(r=1\). The zero-crossing edges form chains
between the two positive-crossing edges. A fan ray internal to such a
chain would have zero-weight edges in its two adjacent sectors,
contrary to (13). At an open-sector junction between a zero-crossing
chain and a two-crossing edge, the zero weight occurs among the three
weights of that edge, so (15) again gives adjacent zeros. It follows
that the two positive-crossing edges share one endpoint and all
zero-crossing edges form one chain in a single sector \(S\). In the
\((2,2)\) case both ends of the chain are the two fan-ray vertices. In
the \((3,2)\) case the end adjacent to the two-crossing edge is the
unique fan-ray vertex. The common endpoint of the two crossing edges
is the unique vertex in the opposite open sector \(S+3\). Thus all but
one vertices lie in \(\overline S\), the remaining vertex lies in
\(S+3\), and
\[
\lambda_S=0.                                                    \tag{16}
\]
This is precisely the closed fan-ray degeneration of rank-five case 1
in accepted fact `1b4ea45680c75628`.

It remains to check that the algebra excluding that case does not acquire
a boundary solution. The space \(\mathcal W\) is preserved by the cyclic
relabeling used in the accepted fact, so take \(S=D\) and the unique
opposite vertex in \(A\). Write that vertex as
\[
(-x,-y),\qquad x,y>0,\qquad
\theta=\frac{x}{x+y}\in(0,1).
\]
The two endpoints of the \(D\)-chain lie on the two sides of the ray
from \((-x,-y)\) through \(O\). In the notation of the accepted
edge-moment calculation, their normalized coordinates satisfy
\[
X>Y\geq0,\qquad Y'>X'\geq0,
\]
and
\[
R=\frac{1+Y}{X-Y}>0,\qquad
S=\frac{1+X'}{Y'-X'}>0.                                         \tag{17}
\]
Equality \(X=Y\) or \(X'=Y'\) would put \(O\) on a boundary edge.
The possible equalities \(Y=0\) and \(X'=0\) are exactly the fan-ray
endpoint cases.

Normalize the nonzero annihilator by
\(\lambda_A=1\); if \(\lambda_A=0\), (15) propagates
\(\lambda_A=\lambda_D=0\) through both crossing edges and gives
\(\lambda=0\). The moment calculation from accepted fact
`1b4ea45680c75628` gives
\[
\lambda_B=-\frac{R^2+2R+\theta}{1-\theta},
\qquad
\lambda_C=\frac{R^2}{\theta},                                  \tag{18}
\]
\[
\lambda_F=-\frac{S^2+2S+1-\theta}{\theta},
\qquad
\lambda_E=\frac{S^2}{1-\theta}.                                \tag{19}
\]
These formulas remain valid when \(Y=0\) or \(X'=0\), either by the
same two moment equations or by continuity. Every vector in
\(\mathcal W\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_A-\lambda_B=\lambda_E-\lambda_D,\qquad
\lambda_C-\lambda_D=\lambda_A-\lambda_F.                        \tag{20}
\]
Using \(\lambda_A=1\) and \(\lambda_D=0\), the first identity in (20)
and (18)-(19) give
\[
S^2=(R+1)^2,
\]
so \(S=R+1\). The second identity gives
\[
R^2=(S+1)^2,
\]
so \(R=S+1\). This is impossible. The fan-ray lemma follows.

Apply the lemma to the weight (10). Conditions (9), (10), and (13)
show that the minimizing polygon \(P\) has no fan-ray vertex. Since
\(O\) is interior, every fan ray therefore meets the relative interior
of an edge transversally. Thus \(P\) lies in a smooth fan-transverse
stratum.

Within that stratum, any nearby polygon with the same \(p_1,p_2\) has
the same defect product term and the same barrier term. Hence local
minimality of \(\mathcal F\) implies local minimality of \(p_3\) subject
to fixed \(p_1,p_2\). Accepted fact `1b4ea45680c75628` now says that
\(P\) is a triangle with its vertices in the open singleton sectors
\(B,D,F\). Accepted fact `d6a43e362072a4df`, which also covers the
closed-sector boundary cases, yields
\[
p_3(P)\geq(1-p_1(P))(1-p_2(P)).
\]
This contradicts (8). Therefore no polygon satisfying (2) exists, and
the stated polygonal inequality follows.
