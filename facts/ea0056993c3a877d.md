## Statement

First, let \(L_h>0\), let \(h:[0,L_h]\to[0,\infty)\) be a concave Lebesgue probability density satisfying
\[
\int_0^{L_h}yh(y)\,dy=1,
\]
extend \(h\) by zero outside \([0,L_h]\), and put
\[
G(w)=\int_w^\infty h(y)\,dy.
\]
If
\[
a\ge c>0,\qquad 1<a^2+c^2,\qquad a+c\le2,\qquad a^2<1+c^2,
\]
then, for every \(w_0,w_2\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(aw_0+cw_2=2\),
\[
G(1)+a^2G(w_0)+c^2G(w_2)
\ge \frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\tag{1}
\]

Second, let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty interior, area \(D\), and centroid \(g\), and let \(p\in K\). Under the same parameter hypotheses, set
\[
A_0=p+a(K-p),\qquad A_1=K,\qquad A_2=p+c(K-p),
\]
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),\qquad
T=D(a^2+1+c^2).
\]
If there is a compact polytope \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)=S,
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]

Finally, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) be any compact polytope whose mixed-integer part consists of three nonempty two-dimensional convex bodies \(A_0,A_1,A_2\), and write
\[
r_i=\sqrt{|A_i|},\qquad T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|.
\]
If
\[
r_0+r_2=2r_1,
\tag{3}
\]
then there exists \(y^*\in C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)\) such that
\[
h_S(y^*)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof

We first prove (1). If \(a+c<2\), it is exactly accepted fact `52e60b02804280c7`. Suppose \(a+c=2\). For \(0<t<1\) sufficiently close to \(1\), put
\[
a_t=ta,\qquad c_t=tc,\qquad
w_{0,t}=w_0,\qquad
w_{2,t}=\frac{2-ta w_0}{tc}.
\]
Then
\[
a_tw_{0,t}+c_tw_{2,t}=2,\qquad a_t+c_t=2t<2.
\]
Moreover, \(a_t\ge c_t>0\), and, for \(t\) sufficiently close to \(1\),
\[
1<a_t^2+c_t^2.
\]
Since \(a\ge c\) and \(a^2-c^2<1\),
\[
a_t^2-c_t^2=t^2(a^2-c^2)<1,
\]
so \(a_t^2<1+c_t^2\). Accepted fact `52e60b02804280c7` therefore gives
\[
G(1)+t^2a^2G(w_0)+t^2c^2G(w_{2,t})
\ge\frac{2(1+t^2a^2+t^2c^2)}9.
\]
The tail \(G\) is continuous because it is the indefinite integral of an \(L^1\) function, and the original constraint gives
\[
w_{2,t}\longrightarrow\frac{2-aw_0}{c}=w_2.
\]
Letting \(t\uparrow1\) proves (1).

This limiting proof also confirms directly that no hidden singularity occurs in the proof of `52e60b02804280c7`. There the hypothesis \(a+c<2\) is used only to place \(s=a+c\) in \(1<s<2\). The strict estimate
\[
d=a-c<\frac1s
\]
comes instead from \(sd=a^2-c^2<1\), which remains strict at \(s=2\). The subsequent polynomial argument already treats \(19/12\le s\le2\), and no expression is divided by \(2-s\). At \(s=2\), its parameter
\[
k=s-\frac{2(1+z)}3
\]
is positive for \(0\le z\le1\), so the endpoint belongs to that final case.

We next prove (2). The case \(a+c<2\) is accepted theorem `ec49e1b230bc2b95`. It remains to treat \(a+c=2\).

Fix a unit vector \(q\). Put
\[
L=\min_{k\in K}\langle q,k\rangle,\quad
U=\max_{k\in K}\langle q,k\rangle,\quad
\mu=\langle q,g\rangle,\quad
r=\langle q,p\rangle.
\]
As in the exact projection reduction of accepted fact `95f17bf1e5ee59f0`, let \(f(t)\) be the length of the section of \(K\) at projected coordinate \(t\), and define
\[
F(u)=\frac1D\int_{-\infty}^u f(t)\,dt.
\]
The section function is nonnegative and concave on \([L,U]\). With
\[
R=U-\mu>0,\qquad B=\frac{U-L}{R},\qquad
h_q(y)=\frac RD f(U-Ry)\quad(0\le y\le B),
\]
one has
\[
\int_0^B h_q(y)\,dy=1,\qquad
\int_0^B yh_q(y)\,dy=1.
\]
If \(G_q\) is its zero-extended tail, then
\[
F(u)=G_q\!\left(\frac{U-u}{R}\right)
\tag{5}
\]
for every real \(u\).

It suffices to consider halfspaces whose boundary contains \((1,g)\), since any halfspace containing that point can be translated inward parallel to itself until its boundary reaches the point. Every nonhorizontal such halfspace can, after choosing its orientation, be written on slice \(i\) as
\[
\langle q,w-g\rangle\le\lambda(1-i)
\]
for some \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Writing a point of \(A_0\) or \(A_2\) as \(p+a(k-p)\) or \(p+c(k-p)\), respectively, gives the exact cap thresholds
\[
u_0=r+\frac{\mu-r+\lambda}{a},\qquad
u_2=r+\frac{\mu-r-\lambda}{c}.
\tag{6}
\]
The halfspace mass is therefore
\[
D\bigl(F(\mu)+a^2F(u_0)+c^2F(u_2)\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(a+c=2\), equations (6) give
\[
a(u_0-\mu)+c(u_2-\mu)=0.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
w_i=\frac{U-u_i}{R}\qquad(i=0,2).
\]
Then (8) implies
\[
aw_0+cw_2
=\frac{(a+c)(U-\mu)}R
=2.
\tag{9}
\]
Using (5), \(F(\mu)=G_q(1)\), and the endpoint case of (1), expression (7) is at least
\[
D\left(G_q(1)+a^2G_q(w_0)+c^2G_q(w_2)\right)
\ge\frac{2D(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\tag{10}
\]

The only strict use of \(a+c<2\) in the geometric proof of `95f17bf1e5ee59f0` is the assertion
\[
\beta=2-a-c>0.
\]
For the closed range one only needs \(\beta\ge0\), because its comparison shift is
\[
\Delta=\frac{\beta(r_2-r_1)}{a+c}\ge0.
\]
There is no division by \(\beta\). At the endpoint \(\beta=0\), the constraint line is independent of \(r\), the shift is zero, and (8)-(9) give the reduction directly.

The two horizontal halfspaces through the middle slice have masses \(D(a^2+1)\) and \(D(1+c^2)\). They satisfy
\[
9(a^2+1)-2(1+a^2+c^2)
=7+7a^2-2c^2
\ge7+5a^2>0
\]
and
\[
9(1+c^2)-2(1+a^2+c^2)
=7+7c^2-2a^2
>5+5c^2>0,
\]
where the second inequality uses \(a^2<1+c^2\). Together with (10), this proves (2).

For the final assertion, we first record a planar consequence of accepted fact `c415ce7703455561`. If \(B_0\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a compact convex body of area \(D_0\) and centroid \(b\), then every closed halfplane containing \(b\) has area intersection at least \(4D_0/9\).

Indeed, it is enough to consider a halfplane whose boundary passes through \(b\). Normalize its one-dimensional section density exactly as above to an anchored concave probability density \(h\) of mean \(1\) on \([0,L_h]\). Accepted fact `c415ce7703455561` represents \(h\) as a mixture of common-support tents with
\[
\frac32\le L_h\le3,\qquad
\int\tau\,\nu(d\tau)=3-L_h.
\]
Put
\[
z=\frac{3-L_h}{L_h},\qquad x=\frac1{L_h}=\frac{1+z}{3},
\qquad r=\frac{\tau}{L_h}.
\]
Then \(0\le z\le1\) and \(\int r\,d\nu=z\). If \(H_x(r)\) denotes the tail at \(1=xL_h\) of the tent with peak ratio \(r\), put
\[
m=\frac{(1+x)^2}{4},\qquad
p=\frac{16+3z^2+z^3}{36}.
\]
For every \(0\le r\le1\),
\[
H_x(r)\ge p+m(r-z).
\tag{11}
\]
To verify (11), let
\[
\ell(r)=1-x^2-m(1-r).
\]
One checks that \(\ell(r)=p+m(r-z)\). On \(r\le x\),
\[
H_x(r)=\frac{(1-x)^2}{1-r}
\]
is convex and \(\ell\) is its tangent at
\[
r_*=\frac{3x-1}{1+x}\in[0,x].
\]
On \(r\ge x\),
\[
H_x(r)-\ell(r)
=(1-r)\left(m-\frac{x^2}{r}\right)\ge0,
\]
because \(x^2/r\le x\le m\). Integrating (11) against \(\nu\) yields
\[
G_h(1)\ge p\ge\frac49.
\]
This is exactly the normalized area of the central halfplane. A halfplane containing the centroid contains the corresponding central halfplane, proving the claim.

Now let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)\) satisfy (3). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Accepted fact `eee2bdfe64ccf5b1` therefore supplies a compact convex body \(K_0\) of area \(1\) and centroid \(0\), and a height shear followed by a common planar translation, taking the slices to
\[
r_0K_0,\qquad r_1K_0,\qquad r_2K_0.
\tag{12}
\]
The shear preserves slice areas and every halfspace depth by accepted fact `ecee03aff61db31e`; the common translation does likewise.

If necessary, apply the height reflection \((x,z)\mapsto(2-x,z)\), so that \(r_0\ge r_2\). This reflection is an affine bijection preserving \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), slice measures, and closed halfspaces, hence it also preserves total mass and depth. Put
\[
B=r_1K_0,\qquad
a=\frac{r_0}{r_1},\qquad
c=\frac{r_2}{r_1}.
\]
Then \(B\) has centroid \(0\),
\[
a\ge c>0,\qquad a+c=2,\qquad a^2+c^2\ge2>1.
\tag{13}
\]

If \(a^2<1+c^2\), the normalized triple (12) is
\[
aB,\qquad B,\qquad cB,
\]
so (2), with common center \(p=0\), proves the target at \((1,0)\).

If \(a^2\ge1+c^2\), use \(y^*=(0,0)\), the centroid of the large outer slice \(aB\). The intersection of any three-dimensional closed halfspace containing \(y^*\) with the plane \(x=0\) is either the whole plane or a closed planar halfplane containing \(0\). The planar bound just proved gives mass at least
\[
\frac49|aB|=\frac{4a^2|B|}{9}.
\]
Since \(a^2\ge1+c^2\),
\[
\frac{4a^2|B|}{9}
\ge\frac{2|B|(a^2+1+c^2)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Thus the target holds in this complementary case as well. Transporting the witness back through the reflection, translation, and shear proves (4) for the original equality-family triple.
