## Statement

Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) and its three nonempty two-dimensional
convex-polytopal integer slices \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) satisfy the hypotheses of
the locked problem.  Put
\[
 M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
 m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad r=\frac mM,
\]
and define the explicit rational number
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 r_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{629856^4\,10^{121}}.}
\]
If \(0<r<r_{\mathrm{eff}}\), then there is a point \(y^*\in S\) such that
\[
 h_S(y^*)\ge\frac29\bigl(|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|\bigr).
\]

## Proof

Assume for contradiction that the conclusion fails.  Reflect the height
coordinate if necessary and apply one common planar homothety so that
\[
 |A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=r,\qquad |A_1|=s.
\tag{1}
\]
These operations preserve compatibility and every depth-to-total-area
ratio.  Put
\[
 t=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\tag{2}
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` applies to a counterexample and gives
\[
 1-r<s<1+r.
\tag{3}
\]
Notice once and for all that
\[
 r<r_{\mathrm{eff}}<10^{-121}
 <\min\left\{\frac1{1000},\frac1{2000000}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

1. Effective near-triangularity of the middle body.

Let \(g_1\) be the centroid of \(A_1\).  The planar depth at \(g_1\) is at
most the three-slice depth of \((1,g_1)\), which is strictly below \(t\).
The minimizing planar halfplane through \(g_1\), whose existence follows
from continuity in the direction, therefore has relative Grünbaum defect
strictly smaller than
\[
 \varepsilon
 :=\frac ts-\frac49
 =\frac{2(1+r-s)}{9s}.
\tag{5}
\]
By (3),
\[
 0<\varepsilon<\frac{4r}{9(1-r)}<r.
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality uses \(r<5/9\).

Set
\[
 \delta=10^{-30},\qquad
 Q:=\left(\frac{\delta}{629856}\right)^4
 =\frac1{629856^4\,10^{120}}.
\tag{7}
\]
By the definition of \(r_{\mathrm{eff}}\), (4), and (6),
\[
 \varepsilon<r<\frac Q{10}<Q.
\tag{8}
\]
The dimension-two specialization of Tanganelli Castrillón's quantitative
Grünbaum stability theorem, stated precisely under EXTERNAL SOURCES, now
gives
\[
 A(A_1)
 \le314928\,\varepsilon^{1/4}
 <314928\,Q^{1/4}
 =\frac{\delta}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(A\) is relative symmetric-difference distance to a
two-dimensional cone, which in dimension two is a nondegenerate triangle.
The infimum defining \(A\) need not be attained, but (9) lets us choose a
triangle \(\Delta_1\) satisfying
\[
 |A_1\mathbin{\triangle}\Delta_1|<\delta |A_1|.
\tag{10}
\]
No equal-area normalization of \(\Delta_1\) is asserted or needed.

Apply one common invertible planar affine map to all three slices that
sends \(\Delta_1\) to
\[
 \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}.
\]
Let \(\lambda>0\) be the absolute determinant of this map and write
\[
 K_0,K,K_2
\]
for the three transformed bodies.  Thus
\[
 |K_0|=\lambda,\qquad |K|=\lambda s,\qquad
 |K_2|=\lambda r,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 \frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\tag{12}
\]
and, with
\[
 E:=|K\mathbin{\triangle}\Delta|,
\]
\[
 E<\delta |K|.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(|\Delta|=1/2\), (13) gives
\[
 \frac1{2(1+\delta)}<|K|<\frac1{2(1-\delta)}.
\tag{14}
\]
Using \(\delta<1/100\), \(1-r<s<1+r\), and (4), we obtain the convenient
rational bounds
\[
 \frac{50}{101}<|K|<\frac{50}{99}<\frac{51}{100},
\qquad
 \frac{12}{25}<\lambda=\frac{|K|}{s}<1.
\tag{15}
\]
For the lower bound on \(\lambda\), it is enough to note that
\[
 \lambda>
 \frac{50/101}{101/100}
 =\frac{5000}{10201}>\frac{12}{25}.
\]
Also, (13) and the upper estimate
\(|K|\le1/2+E\) imply
\[
 E<\frac{\delta}{2(1-\delta)}<\delta.
\tag{16}
\]

2. The quantitative common frame and the \(1/1000\) audit.

Choose the normalized minimal three-halfspace certificate from accepted
fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`.  Orient and positively rescale its middle
sections as
\[
 H_i=\{\varphi_i\ge0\},\qquad i=1,2,3,
\tag{17}
\]
so that their open interiors cover the plane and the Euclidean norm of
the three vertex values of each \(\varphi_i\) on \(\Delta\) is \(1\).
Let \(\pi_i\) be the transformed middle-cap area and let \(e_i\) be the
sum of the two transformed endpoint-cap areas in the same full
halfspace.  The strict-low certificate inequalities are
\[
 \pi_i+e_i<\lambda t.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \frac{\pi_i}{|K|}
 <\frac ts
 =\frac49+\varepsilon
 <\frac49+\delta.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (13) and (19) meet all hypotheses of accepted fact
`245c4d4609d6b9c6`.

Write the barycentric vertex-value vector of \(\varphi_i\) as \(z_i\).
After a row permutation, put
\[
 w_1=\frac{(2,-1,-1)}{\sqrt6},\quad
 w_2=\frac{(-1,2,-1)}{\sqrt6},\quad
 w_3=\frac{(-1,-1,2)}{\sqrt6}.
\tag{20}
\]
There is an apparent \(1/100\) versus \(1/1000\) mismatch in the
registered quantitative facts.  The statement of accepted fact
`245c4d4609d6b9c6` records the weaker requested conclusion \(1/100\),
but its accepted proof establishes, in its displayed equation (28), the
strict stronger estimate
\[
 \boxed{\ \|z_i-w_i\|_2<\frac1{1000}\ }\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{21}
\]
This is exactly the row hypothesis required by accepted fact
`83f224d79276b7c6`; no interpolation from \(1/100\) to \(1/1000\) is
being assumed.

The same accepted frame fact gives the unique positive dependence
weights \(\alpha_i\), normalized by
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i=3,\qquad
 \sum_i\alpha_i\nabla\varphi_i=0,
\tag{22}
\]
with
\[
 \boxed{\ \frac{99}{100}<\alpha_i<\frac{101}{100}\ }.
\tag{23}
\]
The affine function \(\sum_i\alpha_i\varphi_i\) is a positive constant.
Indeed, it is constant by (22).  If it were nonpositive, the intersection
point of the first two nonparallel boundary lines would have
\(\varphi_1=\varphi_2=0\) and \(\varphi_3\le0\), contradicting the open
cover.  Consequently, for every \(x\),
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i\varphi_i(x)>0.
\tag{24}
\]

3. An explicit fixed-window chord bound.

We record a fixed-window consequence of the coarea-concavity calculation
in accepted fact `83f224d79276b7c6`, with all constants displayed because
the registered statement only needs a smaller moving window.  Let
\[
 g=(1/3,1/3),\qquad
 m_i=\varphi_i(g),
\]
and define
\[
 h_i(u)=
 \frac{\mathcal H^1\!\left(
 K\cap\{\varphi_i=m_i+u\}\right)}
 {\|\nabla\varphi_i\|}.
\tag{25}
\]
Put
\[
 L_*=\frac{2\sqrt6}{9},\qquad
 L_-=\frac{49}{50}L_*,\qquad
 L_+=\frac{51}{50}L_*.
\tag{26}
\]
We claim
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 L_-<h_i(u)<L_+
 \quad\text{whenever }|u|\le\frac1{200}.
 \quad}
\tag{27}
\]

Fix \(i\), relabel the vertices within this paragraph, and write
\[
 z_i=(2c,-c,-c)+e,\qquad c=\frac1{\sqrt6},
\qquad \|e\|_2<\frac1{1000}.
\]
As in the accepted chord proof,
\[
 |m_i|<\frac1{1000\sqrt3}<\frac1{1000}.
\tag{28}
\]
The centered positive vertex value is greater than \(79/100\), and each
centered negative value is less than \(-39/100\).  Therefore the corner
triangles \(\{\beta_+\ge3/4\}\) and
\(\{\beta_-\ge3/4\}\) lie respectively above the level \(1/100\) and
below the level \(-1/100\).  Each has area \(1/32>E\), by (16).
Consequently the projection interval of \(K\) contains
\([-1/100,1/100]\) in its interior.

On this interval the section density \(h_i\) is concave.  The corresponding
triangle density \(\ell_i\) is affine, and coarea gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|h_i(u)-\ell_i(u)|\,du\le E.
\tag{29}
\]
The elementary concavity estimate used and proved in accepted fact
`83f224d79276b7c6` says that a concave function \(f\) on
\([-a,a]\) with \(\int|f|\le E\) satisfies
\[
 |f(u)|\le\frac{3E}{a}\qquad(|u|\le a/2).
\]
Applying it to \(h_i-\ell_i\), with \(a=1/100\), gives
\[
 |h_i(u)-\ell_i(u)|<300\delta
\qquad(|u|\le1/200).
\tag{30}
\]

For completeness, the triangle density has the exact form
\[
 \ell_i(u)=\frac{r_+-u}{pq},
\tag{31}
\]
where \(r_+\) is the centered positive vertex value and \(p,q\) are the
two positive-minus-negative vertex-value differences.  From (21) and
(28),
\[
 |r_+-2c|<\frac1{500}.
\]
Thus, for \(|u|\le1/200\), the numerator in (31), relative to \(2c\),
differs from \(1\) by less than \(7/800\), since \(2c>4/5\).
Likewise \(p\) and \(q\), relative to \(3c\), differ from \(1\) by less
than \(1/800\), since \(\sqrt2<3/2\) and \(3c>6/5\).  Hence
\[
 \frac{793}{800}\left(\frac{800}{801}\right)^2
 <\frac{\ell_i(u)}{L_*}<
 \frac{807}{800}\left(\frac{800}{799}\right)^2.
\tag{32}
\]
Exact cross multiplication gives
\[
 \frac{793\cdot800}{801^2}>\frac{247}{250},
\qquad
 \frac{807\cdot800}{799^2}<\frac{253}{250};
\tag{33}
\]
the respective positive margins after cross multiplication are
\(124553\) and \(115453\).  Since \(L_*>1/2\), (30) gives
\[
 \frac{|h_i(u)-\ell_i(u)|}{L_*}
 <600\delta<\frac1{1000}.
\tag{34}
\]
Equations (32)--(34) imply (27).  This proves the claimed fixed-window
bound without any Hausdorff or support convergence.

4. Quantitative centroid offsets and the upper deficit.

Let \(g_K\) be the centroid of \(K\).  Define the boundary and centroid
levels, measured from the triangle-centroid level, by
\[
 b_i=-m_i,\qquad
 c_i=\varphi_i(g_K)-m_i,\qquad
 d_i=b_i-c_i=-\varphi_i(g_K).
\tag{35}
\]
Thus \(|b_i|<1/1000\) by (28).  The selected middle cap is cut at level
\(b_i\), while the parallel halfplane through the actual centroid is cut
at level \(c_i\).

First suppose \(d_i<0\), so \(c_i>b_i\).  If \(c_i\ge1/200\), then (27)
and \(|b_i|<1/1000\) imply that the selected cap exceeds the parallel
centroidal cap by more than
\[
 \frac{L_-}{250}.
\]
On the other hand, the planar Grünbaum inequality in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7`, together with (18), gives the strict upper bound
\[
 \lambda t-\frac{4|K|}{9}
 =\frac{2\lambda(1+r-s)}9
 <\frac{4\lambda r}{9}
 <\frac{4r}{9}.
\tag{36}
\]
This is smaller than \(L_-/250\), because
\(L_->49/100\) and (4).  Therefore \(c_i<1/200\), and the whole interval
from \(b_i\) to \(c_i\) lies in the window (27).  It follows that
\[
 d_i^-:=\max\{-d_i,0\}
 <\frac{4\lambda r}{9L_-}.
\tag{37}
\]

Let
\[
 N=\sum_i d_i^-,\qquad P=\sum_i d_i^+.
\]
Evaluating (24) at \(g_K\) gives
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i d_i<0.
\]
Using (23),
\[
 P<\frac{101}{99}N,\qquad
 N<\frac{4\lambda r}{3L_-},
\tag{38}
\]
and hence
\[
 \sum_i|d_i|
 =P+N
 <\frac{800\lambda r}{297L_-}.
\tag{39}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 d_i^+\le P
 <\frac{404\lambda r}{297L_-}<3r.
\tag{40}
\]
The last inequality follows from
\(L_->49/100\), \(\lambda<1\), and
\(40400<3\cdot14553\).  Equations (28), (35), (40), and (4) show that
when \(d_i>0\), the whole interval from \(c_i=b_i-d_i\) to \(b_i\) also
lies in \([-1/200,1/200]\).  Thus (27) applies to every layer occurring
below, for either sign of \(d_i\).

Let \(G_i\) be the area of the \(\varphi_i\)-halfplane through \(g_K\)
with the selected orientation.  The signed layer formula is
\[
 \pi_i
 =G_i-\int_{c_i}^{b_i}h_i(u)\,du,
\qquad
 G_i\ge\frac{4|K|}{9}.
\tag{41}
\]
By (27), write
\[
 \int_{c_i}^{b_i}h_i(u)\,du
 =L_*d_i+R_i,\qquad
 |R_i|\le\frac{L_*}{50}|d_i|.
\tag{42}
\]
Also, (23) and \(\sum_i\alpha_i d_i<0\) imply
\[
 \sum_i d_i
 =\sum_i\alpha_i d_i+\sum_i(1-\alpha_i)d_i
 <\frac1{100}\sum_i|d_i|.
\tag{43}
\]
Summing (41), then using (39), (42), and (43), gives the explicit upper
deficit
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{4|K|}{3}-\sum_i\pi_i
 &<
 \left(\frac1{100}+\frac1{50}\right)
 L_*\sum_i|d_i|\\
 &<
 \frac3{100}L_*
 \frac{800\lambda r}{297L_-}\\
 &=
 \frac{400}{4851}\lambda r
 <\frac23\lambda r.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
Here \(L_-/L_*=49/50\), and
\[
 \frac3{100}\frac{800}{297}\frac{50}{49}
 =\frac{400}{4851}.
\]
Thus (44) is a completely numerical replacement for the qualitative
first-order chord/Farkas estimate.

We also need an individual endpoint-cap bound.  From (27), (40), and
(41),
\[
 \pi_i\ge\frac{4|K|}{9}-L_+d_i^+.
\]
Together with (18), (36), and (40), this gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e_i
 &<\lambda t-\frac{4|K|}{9}+L_+d_i^+\\
 &<
 \lambda r\left[
 \frac49+\frac{404}{297}\frac{L_+}{L_-}\right]\\
 &=\lambda r\left[
 \frac49+\frac{404}{297}\frac{51}{49}\right]
 <2\lambda r.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]
The last strict inequality is the exact integer comparison
\[
 20604\cdot9=185436
 <203742=14553\cdot14.
\tag{46}
\]

5. Explicit support clearance and the small-endpoint double cover.

For each row put
\[
 M_i=\max_{x\in K_0}\varphi_i(x)
\]
and let \(W_i\) be the width of \(K_0\) in the scalar coordinate
\(\varphi_i\).  We first bound this width without a Hausdorff estimate.
The chord density \(h_i\) is concave on the whole interior of its
projection interval and \(h_i(0)>L_-\).  Joining the value at \(0\) to
the two nonnegative limiting support-section values and integrating the
two resulting triangles gives
\[
 \operatorname{width}_{\varphi_i}(K)
 \le\frac{2|K|}{h_i(0)}
 <\frac{2|K|}{L_-}.
\tag{47}
\]
Fix any \(v_0\in K_2\).  Compatibility (12) puts
\((K_0+v_0)/2\) inside \(K\), so
\[
 W_i\le2\operatorname{width}_{\varphi_i}(K)
 <\frac{4|K|}{L_-}<\frac{204}{49}<5,
\tag{48}
\]
where (15) and \(L_->49/100\) were used.

Write the transformed height-zero and height-two sections of the \(i\)-th
full certificate halfspace as
\[
 K_0\cap\{\varphi_i\ge x_i\},
\qquad
 K_2\cap\{\varphi_i\ge y_i\}.
\tag{49}
\]
Because the height displacements from the middle slice are equal and
opposite after every positive row rescaling,
\[
 x_i+y_i=0.
\tag{50}
\]
The height-zero cap has area at most \(e_i<2\lambda r\), by (45).
For any convex body \(B\), affine function \(\varphi\), support maximum
\(M\), and width \(W\), the cap
\(\{\varphi\ge M-\theta W\}\) contains the homothetic copy
\[
 x_M+\theta(B-x_M)
\]
about any support point \(x_M\), and therefore has area at least
\(\theta^2|B|\).  Applying this to \(K_0\), whose area is \(\lambda\),
gives
\[
 x_i>M_i-W_i\sqrt{2r}
 >M_i-5\sqrt{2r}
 >M_i-\frac1{200}.
\tag{51}
\]
The same conclusion is automatic if \(x_i\ge M_i\).  The last inequality
uses \(r<1/2000000\) from (4).  This is the required explicit
support-clearance estimate.

It remains to show that every \(v\in K_2\) has a fixed frame margin in at
least two rows.  We claim that, for every such \(v\), at least two indices
satisfy
\[
 M_i+\varphi_i(v)>\frac1{200}.
\tag{52}
\]
Suppose instead that distinct rows \(j,k\) satisfy the reverse weak
inequality.  For every \(u\in K_0\), affinity and (12) give
\[
 \varphi_j\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\le\frac1{400},
\qquad
 \varphi_k\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\le\frac1{400}.
\tag{53}
\]
Thus the set
\[
 P_v:=\frac{K_0+v}{2}\subseteq K
\]
of area \(\lambda/4\) lies in the two sublevel sets in (53).

On \(\Delta\), (21) and \(\|\beta\|_2\le1\) show that
\(\varphi_j\le1/400\) implies
\[
 \frac{3\beta_j-1}{\sqrt6}
 \le\frac1{400}+\frac1{1000},
\]
and hence
\[
 \beta_j\le
 \frac13+\frac{\sqrt6}{3}
 \left(\frac1{400}+\frac1{1000}\right)
 <
 a:=\frac13+\frac7{2000}<\frac{17}{50}.
\tag{54}
\]
The same holds for \(k\).  Since \(2a<1\), the part of the standard
simplex satisfying both \(\beta_j\le a\) and \(\beta_k\le a\) is the
full \(a\)-by-\(a\) square in these two barycentric coordinates and has
area \(a^2\).  Equations (13), (15), (16), and (53) would therefore give
\[
 \frac3{25}
 <\frac{\lambda}{4}
 =|P_v|
 \le a^2+E
 <\frac{289}{2500}+\frac1{10000}
 =\frac{1157}{10000}
 <\frac3{25},
\tag{55}
\]
a contradiction.  This proves (52).

For each of the at least two rows in (52), (51) gives
\[
 \varphi_i(v)+x_i>0.
\]
Using (50), this says
\[
 \varphi_i(v)>y_i.
\]
Hence every point of \(K_2\) lies in the interiors of at least two of
the three actual height-two endpoint caps in (49).  Integrating this
pointwise multiplicity gives
\[
 \sum_i e_i
\ge\sum_i
\left|K_2\cap\{\varphi_i\ge y_i\}\right|
\ge2|K_2|
=2\lambda r.
\tag{56}
\]
This is the finite-body quantitative replacement for the compact
double-cover argument.

6. Contradiction.

Summing the three strict inequalities (18) and using (56) yields
\[
 \sum_i\pi_i<3\lambda t-2\lambda r.
\]
Consequently, by (2) and the strict residual lower bound in (3),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{4|K|}{3}-\sum_i\pi_i
 &>
 \lambda\left(\frac{4s}{3}-3t+2r\right)\\
 &=\frac{2\lambda(s-1+2r)}3
 >\frac{2\lambda r}{3}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{57}
\]
This contradicts the explicit upper estimate (44).  Therefore no
counterexample can have \(0<r<r_{\mathrm{eff}}\), proving the statement.

## External sources

Luca Tanganelli Castrillón, “An improved stability result for Grünbaum's
inequality,” Theorem 1.2, arXiv:2410.12072v3,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12072v3.  For a compact convex body
\(K\subset\mathbb R^n\) of positive volume and a closed halfspace \(H^+\)
whose boundary contains the centroid of \(K\), the theorem defines
\[
 A(K)=\inf_C\frac{|K\mathbin{\triangle}C|}{|K|}
\]
over \(n\)-dimensional cones \(C\) and proves
\[
 A(K)\le
 3^{n+7}n^{n+2}
 \left(
 \frac{|K\cap H^+|}{|K|}
 -\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^n
 \right)^{1/(2n)}.
\]
For \(n=2\), the baseline is \(4/9\), the exponent is \(1/4\), an
\(n\)-dimensional cone is a nondegenerate triangle, and the exact constant
is
\[
 3^9\,2^4=19683\cdot16=314928.
\]
In (5), \(K=A_1\), \(H^+\) is a minimizing halfplane through the actual
centroid, and its defect is strictly below \(\varepsilon\), so every
hypothesis and normalization of the theorem is met.  Affine invariance of
relative symmetric difference justifies the subsequent map to the standard
simplex.
