## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty convex polytopes. There
exists a compact polytope \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) whose
integer-height sections are exactly \(\{i\}\times A_i\), \(i=0,1,2\), if and
only if
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]

## Proof

Suppose such a \(C\) exists. For any \(y_0\in A_0\) and \(y_2\in A_2\),
convexity gives
\[
\frac12(0,y_0)+\frac12(2,y_2)
=\left(1,\frac{y_0+y_2}{2}\right)\in C.
\]
The height-\(1\) section is \(\{1\}\times A_1\), so
\((y_0+y_2)/2\in A_1\). Hence \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\).

Conversely, assume this inclusion and set
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr).
\]
This is a compact polytope, and its first-coordinate projection is \([0,2]\).
It therefore has no integer-height sections outside \(0,1,2\). Its sections
at heights \(0\) and \(2\) are \(A_0\) and \(A_2\), since these heights are
the minimum and maximum first coordinates.

Take \((1,y)\in C\). After aggregating terms from the same height, using
convexity of each \(A_i\), write
\[
(1,y)=\lambda_0(0,y_0)+\lambda_1(1,y_1)+\lambda_2(2,y_2),
\]
where \(y_i\in A_i\), \(\lambda_i\ge0\), and
\(\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1\). The first-coordinate equation implies
\(\lambda_0=\lambda_2=t\) and \(\lambda_1=1-2t\) for some
\(t\in[0,1/2]\). Thus
\[
y=(1-2t)y_1+2t\frac{y_0+y_2}{2}.
\]
Both points on the right belong to \(A_1\), so \(y\in A_1\). The reverse
inclusion follows from \(\{1\}\times A_1\subseteq C\). Hence the height-\(1\)
section is exactly \(A_1\).
