## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(D\), fix one of its vertices \(v\), and interpret every homothety about \(v\). Set
\[
a=\frac{2503}{2500},\qquad c=\frac1{20},
\]
and
\[
S=(\{0\}\times a\Delta)\cup(\{1\}\times\Delta)
  \cup(\{2\}\times c\Delta).
\]
If \(g\) is the centroid of \(\Delta\), then
\[
h_S((1,g))
\ge D\left(\frac49+c^2\right)
=\frac{1609}{3600}D
>\frac{2D(a^2+1+c^2)}9.
\]
The final strict margin is
\[
D\left(\frac49+c^2-\frac{2(a^2+1+c^2)}9\right)
=\frac{79357}{56250000}D.
\]

## Proof

By accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, it is enough to consider halfspaces whose boundary contains \((1,g)\), including separately the two horizontal halfspaces. Apply the affine normalization and projected-vertex formula of accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd`.

For a nonhorizontal halfspace, order the three normalized projected vertex values as
\[
\gamma,\quad \gamma+\rho,\quad \gamma+1,
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1.
\]
Define
\[
\Psi_\rho(s):=\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(\gamma+s).
\]
Thus \(\Psi_\rho(s)=0\) for \(s\le0\), \(\Psi_\rho(s)=1\) for \(s\ge1\), and, when \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
\Psi_\rho(s)=
\begin{cases}
s^2/\rho,&0\le s\le\rho,\\[2mm]
1-(1-s)^2/(1-\rho),&\rho\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
The tied cases are
\[
\Psi_0(s)=1-(1-s)^2,\qquad
\Psi_1(s)=s^2,\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
These formulas include every tied projected direction.

For every \(0\le\rho\le1\) and \(0\le s\le1\),
\[
\Psi_\rho(s)\ge s^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, on the lower branch this follows from \(s^2/\rho\ge s^2\). On the upper branch,
\[
\Psi_\rho(s)-s^2
=
\frac{(1-s)\bigl(2s-\rho(1+s)\bigr)}{1-\rho}\ge0,
\]
because \(s\ge\rho\) implies
\[
2s-\rho(1+s)\ge \rho(1-\rho)\ge0.
\]
The endpoint formulas give the same conclusion directly.

Since \(g\) has barycentric coordinates \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\), its shifted projected value is
\[
\mu:=m-\gamma=\frac{1+\rho}{3}.
\]
Consequently its middle-slice cap fraction is
\[
F(\rho):=\Psi_\rho(\mu)
=
\begin{cases}
1-\dfrac{(2-\rho)^2}{9(1-\rho)},
&0\le\rho\le\frac12,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{(1+\rho)^2}{9\rho},
&\frac12\le\rho\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
For \(0\le\rho\le1/2\),
\[
F(\rho)-\frac12
=
\frac{(1-2\rho)(1+\rho)}{18(1-\rho)}\ge0,
\]
while for \(1/2\le\rho\le1\),
\[
F(\rho)-\frac49=\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{9\rho}\ge0.
\]
Hence
\[
F(\rho)\ge\frac49
\qquad(0\le\rho\le1).
\tag{3}
\]
We shall also use the stronger bound
\[
F(\rho)\ge\frac{64}{135}
\qquad\left(0\le\rho\le\frac35\right).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(\rho\le1/2\), this follows from \(F(\rho)\ge1/2>64/135\). For \(1/2\le\rho\le3/5\),
\[
F(\rho)-\frac{64}{135}
=
\frac{15(\rho-\frac35)(\rho-\frac53)}{135\rho}\ge0.
\]

For the bottom and top slices, put
\[
P=\frac{m+\sigma}{a}-\gamma,
\qquad
Q=\frac{m-\sigma}{c}-\gamma.
\]
The normalized mass of the nonhorizontal halfspace is
\[
a^2\Psi_\rho(P)+F(\rho)+c^2\Psi_\rho(Q).
\tag{5}
\]
The actual common slope \(\sigma\) cancels from the identity
\[
aP+cQ
=2m-(a+c)\gamma
=2\mu+(2-a-c)\gamma.
\tag{6}
\]

We first record a consequence of (1). If
\[
aP+cQ\ge2c,
\tag{7}
\]
then
\[
a^2\Psi_\rho(P)+c^2\Psi_\rho(Q)\ge c^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Otherwise the left side of (8) would be strictly below \(c^2\). This would imply \(\Psi_\rho(Q)<1\), hence \(Q<1\), and
\[
\Psi_\rho(P)<\left(\frac ca\right)^2.
\]
Since \(c/a=125/2503\in(0,1)\), inequality (1), together with \(\Psi_\rho(P)=1\) for \(P\ge1\), would force \(P<c/a\). Therefore
\[
aP+cQ<a\frac ca+c=2c,
\]
contradicting (7).

It remains to check (7), or else use the stronger middle-slice bound. Here
\[
2-a-c=\frac{593}{625},
\qquad 2c=\frac1{10}.
\]
The three possible positions of the homothety vertex in the ordered projected list give exactly the following cases.

If that vertex is a projected minimum, then \(\gamma=0\), and
\[
aP+cQ=\frac{2(1+\rho)}3
\ge\frac23
=\frac1{10}+\frac{17}{30}.
\]

If it is a projected middle vertex, then \(\gamma=-\rho\), and
\[
aP+cQ
=\frac23-\frac{529}{1875}\rho
\ge\frac{721}{1875}
=\frac1{10}+\frac{1067}{3750}.
\]

If it is a projected maximum, then \(\gamma=-1\), and
\[
aP+cQ=\frac{1250\rho-529}{1875}.
\]
This is at least \(2c=1/10\) exactly when
\[
\rho\ge\frac{1433}{2500}.
\]
In all these cases, (8) and (3) applied to (5) give
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}D
\ge c^2+\frac49.
\]
The only remaining case has the homothety vertex projected maximally and
\[
\rho<\frac{1433}{2500}
=\frac35-\frac{67}{2500}.
\]
Then (4) and nonnegativity of both outer caps give
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)}D
\ge\frac{64}{135}
=\frac49+c^2+\frac{293}{10800}
>\frac49+c^2.
\]
Thus every nonhorizontal halfspace through \((1,g)\), including all tied directions and every slope, has the required mass.

Finally, the two horizontal halfspaces have normalized masses \(a^2+1\) and \(1+c^2\). Their exact margins above \(4/9+c^2\) are respectively
\[
a^2+1-\left(\frac49+c^2\right)
=\frac{10936807}{7031250},
\qquad
1+c^2-\left(\frac49+c^2\right)=\frac59.
\]
Therefore
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge D\left(\frac49+c^2\right).
\]
Since
\[
\frac49+c^2-\frac{2(a^2+1+c^2)}9
=\frac{79357}{56250000}>0,
\]
the asserted strict comparison follows.
