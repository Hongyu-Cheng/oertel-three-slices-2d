## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex bodies, let \(a_i=|A_i|\), and put \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\). Let
\[
\mu_i(B)=\frac{|A_i\cap B|}{a_i}
\]
be the uniform probability measure on \(A_i\). Let \(\pi\) be any Borel probability measure on \(A_0\times A_2\) whose two marginals are \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu_2\). Let \(\nu_\pi\) be the pushforward of \(\pi\) under \((z_0,z_2)\mapsto(z_0+z_2)/2\), and define
\[
\eta_\pi(E)=|A_1\cap E|+m\nu_\pi(E)
\]
for Borel \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^2\).

Fix \(c\in A_1\). For every \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
P=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le0\}
\]
and
\[
H_{q,\lambda}
=
\{(x,z)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2:
\langle q,z-c\rangle\le\lambda(1-x)\}.
\]
Then, for
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_{q,\lambda})
&=
\left|A_0\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le\lambda\}\right|
+|A_1\cap P|\\
&\quad+
\left|A_2\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le-\lambda\}\right|\\
&\ge \eta_\pi(P).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the two slice-parallel halfspaces through \((1,c)\),
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap\{x\le1\})=a_0+a_1\ge\eta_\pi(\mathbb R^2)
\]
and
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap\{x\ge1\})=a_1+a_2\ge\eta_\pi(\mathbb R^2).
\]
By the normal-form classification in accepted fact \(400272f6de6c67bb\), these tilted and slice-parallel cases exhaust all closed normal-form halfspaces whose boundary contains \((1,c)\).

## Proof

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). On the probability space \((A_0\times A_2,\pi)\), let \(X_0\) and \(X_2\) be the coordinate projections and define the events
\[
E_0=\{\langle q,X_0-c\rangle\le\lambda\},
\qquad
E_2=\{\langle q,X_2-c\rangle\le-\lambda\},
\]
and
\[
M=\left\{\left\langle q,\frac{X_0+X_2}{2}-c\right\rangle\le0\right\}.
\]
There is a deterministic event inclusion
\[
M\subseteq E_0\cup E_2.
\]
Indeed, outside \(E_0\cup E_2\) one has simultaneously
\[
\langle q,X_0-c\rangle>\lambda
\quad\text{and}\quad
\langle q,X_2-c\rangle>-\lambda.
\]
Adding gives
\[
\langle q,X_0+X_2-2c\rangle>0,
\]
so the midpoint is not in \(P\). Consequently,
\[
\nu_\pi(P)=\pi(M)
\le\pi(E_0\cup E_2)
\le\pi(E_0)+\pi(E_2).
\]
This step uses no independence.

Write \(p_i=\pi(E_i)\) for \(i=0,2\). The exact marginal assumptions give
\[
p_0
=
\frac{\left|A_0\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le\lambda\}\right|}{a_0}
\]
and
\[
p_2
=
\frac{\left|A_2\cap\{z:\langle q,z-c\rangle\le-\lambda\}\right|}{a_2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
a_0p_0+a_2p_2
\ge m(p_0+p_2)
\ge m\nu_\pi(P).
\]
Adding \(|A_1\cap P|\) proves the asserted tilted-halfspace domination. The exact mass formula and the fact that every non-slice-parallel normal-form halfspace through \((1,c)\) has these parameters follow from accepted fact \(400272f6de6c67bb\).

For a slice-parallel normal-form halfspace through \((1,c)\), the same accepted fact gives precisely \(\{x\le1\}\) or \(\{x\ge1\}\). Their masses are respectively \(a_0+a_1\) and \(a_1+a_2\). Their entire middle slice is included, so its corresponding middle set is \(\mathbb R^2\). Since \(\nu_\pi\) is a probability measure,
\[
\eta_\pi(\mathbb R^2)=a_1+m.
\]
The inequalities \(m\le a_0\) and \(m\le a_2\) prove both slice-parallel bounds.
