## Statement

Use the reduced normal form of accepted facts `b7cb6541c1d790bc` and
`eef70ce3abe7606e`, with cyclic boundary list
\[
(p_{13},u_1,p_{12},z_{12},p_{21},u_2,
  p_{23},z_{23},p_{32},u_3,p_{31},z_{31}).
\tag{1}
\]
Suppose its underlying convex polygon has exactly five geometric vertices.
For the three central blocks
\[
B_{12}=(p_{12},z_{12},p_{21}),\qquad
B_{23}=(p_{23},z_{23},p_{32}),\qquad
B_{31}=(p_{31},z_{31},p_{13}),
\tag{2}
\]
let \(m_{ij}\) be the number of geometric vertices contributed by that block
after coincident labels and nonvertex collinear labels are removed. Then,
up to cyclic relabeling,
\[
(m_{12},m_{23},m_{31})=(2,0,0)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(1,1,0).
\tag{3}
\]
For a block with \(m=1\), its unique vertex is of type \(Z\), \(E\), or
\(C\): respectively it carries no chord-endpoint label, one endpoint label,
or both coincident endpoint labels. For a block with \(m=2\), its two
vertices have type \(PP\) or \(PZ\): respectively both distinct chord
endpoints, or one endpoint and the central vertex. Reversal identifies the
two orientations of \(PZ\), while an \(E\)-occurrence retains its left/right
orientation when two occupied blocks are compared. Thus (3), together with
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(2,0,0)&PP,\ PZ\\
(1,1,0)&ZZ,\ ZE,\ ZC,\ EE,\ EC,\ CC
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
and the stated \(E\)-orientations, is the complete finite cap/chord-endpoint
classification.

If at least two of
\[
(x_{12},x_{21}),\qquad
(x_{23},x_{32}),\qquad
(x_{31},x_{13})
\tag{5}
\]
vanish, then
\[
D\ge\frac{2(3-\sqrt5)}7>\frac4{169}.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence no reduced convex pentagon in those endpoint strata can satisfy the
necessary strict-table scale condition \(D\le4/169\).

## Proof

In the normal form,
\[
u_i=-h_i e_i+(1+h_i)
\bigl((1-\tau_i)e_{i^-}+\tau_i e_{i^+}\bigr),
\qquad
h_i>0,\quad 0<\tau_i<1.
\]
Thus \(u_i\) has negative \(i\)-th barycentric coordinate and strictly
positive other coordinates. Every point in the other two cap blocks and in
the three central blocks has nonnegative \(i\)-th coordinate. Consequently
\(u_i\) cannot lie in the convex hull of the other listed points. The three
\(u_i\) are therefore three distinct geometric vertices of every reduced
polygon.

The reduction in `b7cb6541c1d790bc` leaves no other labeled arcs: every
remaining geometric vertex must occur in exactly one of the three blocks
in (2). Blocks belonging to different pairs are separated in (1) by one of
the distinct vertices \(u_i\), so they cannot contribute the same geometric
vertex. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{vert}A_2|
=3+m_{12}+m_{23}+m_{31}.
\tag{7}
\]
For a pentagon, the three nonnegative integers on the right sum to two.
Their only unordered partitions are \(2+0+0\) and \(1+1+0\), proving (3).

It remains to check that (4) records every endpoint incidence, including
coincidences. A central block has the ordered labeled list
\[
(P,Z,P')=(p_{ij},z_{ij},p_{ji}).
\]
If it contributes one vertex, that vertex carries neither endpoint label,
exactly one endpoint label, or both endpoint labels. These are \(Z,E,C\).
The last case occurs exactly when
\[
p_{ij}=p_{ji},
\]
which condition (2a) of `eef70ce3abe7606e` identifies with
\(x_{ij}=x_{ji}=0\). If a block contributes two vertices, the surviving
ordered subsequence is \(P,P'\), \(P,Z\), or \(Z,P'\). These are \(PP\)
and the two reversed versions of \(PZ\). There is only one \(Z\)-label, so
there is no further two-vertex type.

If \(p_{ij}=p_{ji}\), the block cannot contribute two distinct vertices.
Indeed, a nontrivial boundary walk
\[
p_{ij}\longrightarrow z_{ij}\longrightarrow p_{ij}
\]
would contain two oppositely directed nonzero edges. The convex-order
inequality (10) of `eef70ce3abe7606e`, applied to both edges, would force
every listed point onto their common line, contradicting two-dimensionality.
Thus a zero endpoint pair contributes either no vertex or one \(C\)-vertex.
This proves that the block types above are exhaustive. Combining the two
possible distributions gives exactly (4); cyclic relabeling locates the
occupied blocks, reversal identifies \(PZ\) with \(ZP\), and the side on
which a single \(E\)-contact occurs is the only residual orientation datum.

Condition (2a) of the accepted normal form independently places each pair in
(5) in either its zero or positive state, so the vertex types above intersect
only eight endpoint-pair strata. Accepted fact `be4033c5411ca728` applies to
the four strata with at least two zero pairs and gives the first inequality
in (6). The second is exact: \(\sqrt5<5/2\) gives
\[
\frac{2(3-\sqrt5)}7>\frac17>\frac4{169}.
\]
By formula (13) of `eef70ce3abe7606e`, these pentagons cannot satisfy the
necessary scale condition.

For clarity, the first unclosed boundary class is now finite without any
hidden vertex-count alternatives. After relabeling it has
\[
x_{12}=x_{21}=0,\qquad
x_{23},x_{32},x_{31},x_{13}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The zero block has \(m_{12}\le1\). If \(m_{12}=1\), it is a \(C\)-block and
exactly one other block has \(m=1\). If \(m_{12}=0\), the other two block
counts are either \((2,0)\) or \((1,1)\). These are the smallest remaining
endpoint-degeneration systems. The fully positive stratum has the two
distributions (3) with no \(C\)-block. No bound for (8) or for the fully
positive stratum is claimed here.
