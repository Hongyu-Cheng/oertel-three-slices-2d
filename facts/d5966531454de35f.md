## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}
\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^3:\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1\},
\qquad
g=\frac13(1,1,1),
\]
with planar area normalized by \(|\Delta|=1\).  Let \(u,v>0\), let
\(P=(P_1,P_2,P_3)\) satisfy \(\sum_iP_i=1\), and put
\[
A_0=P+u(\Delta-g),\qquad A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_2=P+v(\Delta-g).
\tag{1}
\]
Assume
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
a=\max\{u,v\},\qquad b=\min\{u,v\},\qquad
R=\frac{b^2}{a^2},
\]
and assume the lower inequality from the exact residual strip in accepted
fact `64760c76cadff481`,
\[
\frac1{a^2}>L_0(R),
\qquad
L_0(R)=
\begin{cases}
1-R,&0<R\le 9/25,\\[1mm]
(3R+2\sqrt R-1)/2,&R>9/25.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

For arbitrary real thresholds \(s_1,s_2,s_3\) satisfying
\[
s_1+s_2+s_3<1,
\tag{4}
\]
define the exact optimized mass of the \(i\)-th barycentric corner row by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_i={}&
\left|\Delta\cap\{\lambda_i\ge s_i\}\right|\\
&+\inf_{q\in\mathbb R}\left(
\left|A_0\cap\{\lambda_i\ge s_i-q\}\right|
+\left|A_2\cap\{\lambda_i\ge s_i+q\}\right|
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Then at least one \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal M_i>
\frac{2(1+u^2+v^2)}9.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently the displaced-homothetic family (1) admits no strict
three-row certificate of the form (4)-(5), even with independently
optimized endpoint slope in every row.  This conclusion holds over the
reals and hence, in particular, for every rational choice in the
registered search family.

## Proof

Reflecting the height coordinate exchanges \(u\) and \(v\) and replaces
\(q\) by \(-q\) in (5), without changing the infimum.  We may therefore
assume
\[
u=a\ge b=v,\qquad w=a+b,\qquad E=a^2+b^2.
\tag{7}
\]

For the \(i\)-th barycentric coordinate, the minimum over the midpoint
triangle in (2) is
\[
P_i-\frac{a+b}{6}.
\]
Thus compatibility is exactly
\[
P_i\ge\frac w6\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{8}
\]
Summing (8) gives \(w\le2\), and hence \(b\le1\).

We record one further consequence of (3).  On \(0<R\le9/25\),
\(L_0(R)=1-R\).  On \(R>9/25\),
\[
L_0(R)-(1-R)=\frac{5R+2\sqrt R-3}{2}\ge0,
\tag{9}
\]
because the right side is zero at \(R=9/25\) and is strictly increasing.
Therefore (3) implies
\[
a^2-b^2=a^2(1-R)<1.
\tag{10}
\]
Together with \(b\le1\), this gives \(E<3\), so
\[
\frac{2(1+E)}9<1.
\tag{11}
\]

We next solve the one-dimensional endpoint allocation exactly.  For
\(c>0\), write
\[
[x]_c=\min\{c,\max\{0,x\}\}.
\]
For \(D\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
\phi(D)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb R}
\left([x]_a^2+[D-x]_b^2\right).
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
\phi(D)=
\begin{cases}
0,&D\le0,\\[1mm]
\min\{D^2/2,b^2\},&D>0.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, if \(D\le0\), every \(x\in[D,0]\) makes both terms zero.  Suppose
\(D>0\).  If \(x\le0\) or \(x\ge D\), the nonzero raw cap has length at
least \(D\), so the sum in (12) is at least
\(\min\{b^2,D^2/2\}\).  If \(0<x<D\) and either cap is saturated, the
sum is at least \(b^2\); if neither is saturated, then
\[
x^2+(D-x)^2\ge\frac{D^2}{2}.
\]
This proves the lower bound in (13).  If \(0<D\le\sqrt2\,b\), equality
is attained at the unique interior stationary allocation \(x=D/2\).
If \(D\ge\sqrt2\,b\), equality is attained at the breakpoint \(x=0\):
the \(a\)-endpoint cap is empty and the \(b\)-endpoint cap is full.
Thus (13) includes the stationary point, both zero-cap breakpoints, both
full-cap breakpoints, and both allocation tails.  It also shows directly
that \(\phi\) is nondecreasing.

We now identify (12) with the actual endpoint optimization in (5).  The
cap formula of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`, applied to the projection
with two equal low vertex values and one high vertex value, says that a
cap of \(P+a(\Delta-g)\) with raw side length \(x\) has area
\([x]_a^2\); the factor \(a^2\) from the triangle area cancels the
\(a^{-2}\) in the cap fraction.  For row \(i\), put
\[
x=P_i+\frac{2a}{3}-s_i+q.
\]
The raw side length on the other endpoint is
\[
P_i+\frac{2b}{3}-s_i-q=D_i-x,
\qquad
D_i=2\left(P_i+\frac w3-s_i\right).
\tag{14}
\]
As \(q\) ranges over \(\mathbb R\), so does \(x\).  Hence the endpoint
infimum in (5) is exactly
\[
\phi(D_i).
\tag{15}
\]
Equivalently, if \(d=1-s_1-s_2-s_3>0\), the normalized rows
\[
L_{ij}=\frac{\delta_{ij}-s_i}{d}
\]
satisfy the column-sum identity in accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`, and its allocation parameter is \(q=d\sigma_i\).
Thus (15) is the actual row mass optimization, not a surrogate
convolution.

Let
\[
D_0=w-\frac23.
\tag{16}
\]
We claim
\[
9\phi(D_0)>2(E-1).
\tag{17}
\]
If \(D_0\le0\), then \(w\le2/3\) and
\(E\le w^2\le4/9<1\), so (17) follows from \(\phi\ge0\).
Suppose \(D_0>0\).  By (13),
\[
9\phi(D_0)=
\min\left\{\frac92D_0^2,\,9b^2\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The tail value is strictly larger than the right side of (17), because
(10) gives
\[
E-1=a^2+b^2-1<2b^2,
\qquad
2(E-1)<4b^2<9b^2.
\tag{19}
\]
The stationary value is also strictly larger, by the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac92\left(w-\frac23\right)^2-2(E-1)
&=\frac52w^2+4ab-6w+4\\
&=\frac52\left(w-\frac65\right)^2+4ab+\frac25
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (18)-(20) prove (17).

Finally, (4) supplies an index \(j\) with \(s_j<1/3\).  If \(s_j\le0\),
then its middle cap is all of \(\Delta\), so
\(\mathcal M_j\ge1>2(1+E)/9\) by (11).  Otherwise
\(0<s_j<1/3\), and the same repeated-value cap formula gives
\[
\left|\Delta\cap\{\lambda_j\ge s_j\}\right|
=(1-s_j)^2>\frac49.
\tag{21}
\]
Compatibility (8) and \(s_j<1/3\) give
\[
D_j=2\left(P_j+\frac w3-s_j\right)
>w-\frac23=D_0.
\tag{22}
\]
Using monotonicity of \(\phi\), (15), (17), and (21), we obtain
\[
\mathcal M_j
>\frac49+\frac{2(E-1)}9
=\frac{2(1+E)}9
=\frac{2(1+u^2+v^2)}9.
\tag{23}
\]
This is (6), and it contradicts the strict low-mass requirement for the
three corner rows.  All translations \(P\), all independent thresholds,
and every exact endpoint allocation have been covered.
