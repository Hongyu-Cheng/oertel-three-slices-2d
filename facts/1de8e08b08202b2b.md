## Statement

Assume the affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, and suppose that the source and middle polytopes realize the strict rational table of accepted fact `c5aad5c9408fcf74`. Thus
\[
Q_J=A_2\cap\{f_j\le-L_j\ (j\in J),\ f_j>-L_j\ (j\notin J)\},
\]
all unlisted \(Q\)-cell masses vanish, and
\[
q_{123}=\frac25,\qquad q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15.
\]
For \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), define the closed cap
\[
C_i=A_2\cap\{f_i+L_i\ge0\}
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal T_Q=\{x:f_i(x)+L_i\le0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\]
Then
\[
|A_2|=1,\qquad |C_i|=\frac15,
\]
the three caps have pairwise disjoint planar interiors, and \(\mathcal T_Q\) is the intersection of the three halfplanes complementary to their cap halfplanes.

Assume in addition that \(A_2\) is a parallelogram and the caps have type-2 vertex incidence: one cap strictly contains two adjacent vertices, and the other two caps strictly contain the remaining two vertices, one each. Then
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|=\frac25.
\]
This contradicts the conclusion
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}
\]
of accepted fact `b1eaab0ae37367d1`. Hence no full strict-table realization has a parallelogram \(A_2\) with type-2 cap incidence.

## Proof

By accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, the exact cells \(Q_J\) form a disjoint partition of \(A_2\), and every cutting line \(f_i+L_i=0\) has planar area zero. Accepted fact `c5aad5c9408fcf74` gives the four displayed positive \(Q\)-cell masses and assigns every other \(Q\)-cell mass zero. Therefore
\[
|A_2|
=q_{123}+q_{12}+q_{13}+q_{23}
=\frac25+3\cdot\frac15
=1. \tag{1}
\]

Fix \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\). Away from the zero-area line \(f_i+L_i=0\), the cap \(C_i\) is the union of the exact cells \(Q_J\) with \(i\notin J\). Among the supported cells, the only such cell is \(Q_{\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}\). Hence
\[
|C_i|=q_{\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}=\frac15. \tag{2}
\]
If \(i\ne j\), the intersection of the planar interiors of \(C_i\) and \(C_j\) is contained in
\[
A_2\cap\{f_i+L_i>0,\ f_j+L_j>0\},
\]
which is a union of exact cells \(Q_J\) with \(i,j\notin J\). Every such cell has zero mass in the strict table, so this union has area zero. The intersection of the planar interiors of \(C_i\) and \(C_j\) is an open subset of the plane contained in that zero-area union; it must therefore be empty. Thus the caps have pairwise disjoint interiors. Finally, the halfplane complementary to the cap halfplane \(\{f_i+L_i\ge0\}\) is \(\{f_i+L_i\le0\}\), so the intersection of the three complementary halfplanes is exactly \(\mathcal T_Q\).

It remains to prove the parallelogram geometry. Area ratios, cap incidences, and the ratio between the complementary-halfplane triangle and the parallelogram are invariant under invertible affine maps. Normalize \(A_2\) to the unit square with cyclic vertices
\[
v_1=(1,0),\quad v_2=(1,1),\quad
v_3=(0,1),\quad v_4=(0,0),
\]
where \(C_1\) contains \(v_1,v_2\), \(C_2\) contains \(v_3\), and \(C_3\) contains \(v_4\).

The line bounding \(C_1\) meets the bottom edge \(v_1v_4\) at fraction \(a\) from \(v_1\), and the top edge \(v_2v_3\) at fraction \(\kappa\) from \(v_2\). Thus the intersection points are
\[
(1-a,0),\qquad(1-\kappa,1).
\]
The cap is a trapezoid of area \((a+\kappa)/2\). Equation (2) gives
\[
a+\kappa=\frac25. \tag{3}
\]

Let the two edge fractions of the corner cap \(C_2\), measured from \(v_3\) toward \(v_2\) and \(v_4\), be \(b,d\). Its area is \(bd/2\), so
\[
bd=\frac25. \tag{4}
\]
Likewise, let the fractions of \(C_3\), measured from \(v_4\) toward \(v_3\) and \(v_1\), be \(c,e\). Then
\[
ce=\frac25. \tag{5}
\]
All six fractions are in \((0,1]\).

Pairwise interior-disjointness along the top, bottom, and left edges gives
\[
\kappa+b\le1,\qquad
a+e\le1,\qquad
d+c\le1. \tag{6}
\]
Eliminate
\[
a=\frac25-\kappa,\qquad
d=\frac{2}{5b},\qquad
e=\frac{2}{5c}. \tag{7}
\]
The first and third inequalities in (6), together with (7), imply
\[
b\le1-\kappa,
\qquad
b(1-c)\ge\frac25,
\]
and therefore
\[
(1-\kappa)(1-c)\ge\frac25. \tag{8}
\]
The middle inequality in (6) gives
\[
\frac25-\kappa+\frac{2}{5c}\le1,
\]
or equivalently
\[
c\ge\frac{2}{3+5\kappa}. \tag{9}
\]
Hence
\[
1-c\le\frac{1+5\kappa}{3+5\kappa}. \tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10) yields
\[
(1-\kappa)\frac{1+5\kappa}{3+5\kappa}
\ge\frac25.
\]
The denominator is positive, so this is equivalent to
\[
5(1-\kappa)(1+5\kappa)\ge2(3+5\kappa),
\]
and hence to
\[
-(5\kappa-1)^2\ge0. \tag{11}
\]
Therefore
\[
\kappa=\frac15,\qquad a=\frac15. \tag{12}
\]

At \(\kappa=1/5\), inequalities (8)--(10) force equality throughout:
\[
c=\frac12,\qquad b=\frac45.
\]
Equations (4)--(5) then give
\[
d=\frac12,\qquad e=\frac45. \tag{13}
\]
Thus all six fractions are uniquely determined.

The \(C_1\)-line passes through
\[
\left(\frac45,0\right),\qquad
\left(\frac45,1\right),
\]
and its complementary halfplane is \(x\le4/5\). The \(C_2\)-line passes through
\[
\left(\frac45,1\right),\qquad
\left(0,\frac12\right),
\]
and its complementary halfplane is
\[
y\le\frac12+\frac58x.
\]
The \(C_3\)-line passes through
\[
\left(0,\frac12\right),\qquad
\left(\frac45,0\right),
\]
and its complementary halfplane is
\[
y\ge\frac12-\frac58x.
\]
Their intersection is exactly the triangle with vertices
\[
\left(0,\frac12\right),\qquad
\left(\frac45,0\right),\qquad
\left(\frac45,1\right).
\]
It has vertical base \(1\) and horizontal altitude \(4/5\), so
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
=\frac12\cdot1\cdot\frac45
=\frac25. \tag{14}
\]

Accepted fact `b1eaab0ae37367d1` applies to the same strict-table realization and gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}.
\]
This contradicts (14), completing the exclusion.
