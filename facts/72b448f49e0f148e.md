## Statement

Let
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad f_2(x,y)=y,\qquad
f_3(x,y)=-1-x-y,
\]
and put
\[
t_i=f_i+\frac13\quad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Thus \(t_1+t_2+t_3=0\), and \((x,y)\mapsto(t_1,t_2)\) preserves planar area. Fix \(B,C>0\), and take the oppositely oriented endpoint triangles
\[
A_2=\{t_i\geq-B\ (i=1,2,3)\},\qquad
A_0=\{t_i\leq C\ (i=1,2,3)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(K\) be a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex polytope satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K
\tag{2}
\]
and invariant under the cyclic permutation
\[
(t_1,t_2,t_3)\longmapsto(t_2,t_3,t_1).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\), and use the exact cells
\[
P_J=A_0\cap
\{f_i\leq L_i\ (i\in J),\ f_i>L_i\ (i\notin J)\},
\]
\[
Q_H=A_2\cap
\{f_i\leq-L_i\ (i\in H),\ f_i>-L_i\ (i\notin H)\},
\]
\[
R_G=K\cap
\{f_i\leq0\ (i\in G),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin G)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Assume
\[
|P_\varnothing|>0,
\tag{5}
\]
the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\tag{6}
\]
and, writing
\[
q=|Q_{123}|,\qquad
x_i=|Q_{\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}|,\qquad
m=|A_2|,
\]
the dominant cell is labeled so that
\[
x_1>\frac{5m}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]
Put
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad k=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}|,\quad
v_i=|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-L_i\}|,\quad
c_i=|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,
\]
\[
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,\qquad
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,
\]
\[
r_G=|R_G|,\qquad
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123}.
\tag{8}
\]
Assume the three crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{9}
\]
the all-double \(W=0\) mass identity
\[
2M=4m+k+3(U+q+E_R),
\tag{10}
\]
and the no-half inequalities
\[
M<m+k,\qquad m<M+k,\qquad k<M+m.
\tag{11}
\]
Then no such \(A_0,A_2,K,L_1,L_2,L_3\) exist. Consequently this endpoint family cannot satisfy the full residual system, including the canonical tails and \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\).

The minimal midpoint choice in (2) is the explicit six-vertex polygon
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=
\left\{
-\frac{B+2C}{2}\leq t_i\leq\frac{2B+C}{2}
\ (i=1,2,3)
\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the conclusion covers that exact low-vertex choice and every cyclically symmetric convex enlargement of it.

## Proof

The canonical all-double \(W=0\) identity used in (10) is the scale-invariant form of equation (9) in accepted fact `366887b2b328c518`. Since \(U\geq0\), \(q>0\), and \(E_R\geq0\), (10) gives
\[
2M>4m+k.
\tag{13}
\]
The first no-half inequality in (11) is \(k>M-m\). Substituting it into (13) yields
\[
2M>4m+M-m,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
M>3m.
\tag{14}
\]

We now compute the endpoint caps exactly. The vertices of \(A_2\), in the \((t_1,t_2)\)-coordinates, are
\[
(2B,-B),\qquad(-B,2B),\qquad(-B,-B),
\]
and the vertices of \(A_0\) are
\[
(-2C,C),\qquad(C,-2C),\qquad(C,C).
\]
Therefore
\[
m=\frac92B^2,\qquad M=\frac92C^2.
\tag{15}
\]

For each \(i\), define
\[
d_i=2B-\left(\frac13-L_i\right)
=2B-\frac13+L_i.
\tag{16}
\]
The region of \(A_2\) on which \(f_i>-L_i\) is the corner cap
\[
\left\{t_i>2B-d_i\right\}.
\]
The all-double support (6) identifies this cap, up to null cutting lines, with \(Q_{\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}\). Its positivity and the positivity of \(Q_{123}\) give \(0<d_i<3B\). Similar triangles give
\[
x_i=\frac12d_i^2,\qquad v_i=m-x_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Since the four positive cells partition \(A_2\),
\[
q+x_1+x_2+x_3=m.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (7) and (18) imply, for \(j=2,3\),
\[
x_j<m-x_1<\frac{4m}{9}<x_1.
\]
By (17),
\[
d_1>d_2,\qquad d_1>d_3.
\tag{19}
\]

On \(A_0\), the \(i\)-th \(P\)-threshold is
\[
f_i\leq L_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_i\leq \frac13+L_i
=d_i-2B+\frac23.
\tag{20}
\]
Put
\[
D=2C-2B+\frac23.
\tag{21}
\]
If the right side of (20) were at least \(C\), then \(f_i>L_i\) would be impossible everywhere on \(A_0\), contrary to (5). Hence
\[
d_i+D<3C.
\tag{22}
\]
The lower corner of \(A_0\) in the \(t_i\)-direction is \(-2C\). Another similar-triangle computation, including the case in which the threshold lies below that corner, gives
\[
u_i=\frac12[d_i+D]_+^2,
\tag{23}
\]
where \([s]_+=\max\{s,0\}\). Condition (22) is exactly what rules out the full-cap branch of this formula.

The cyclic map in (3) preserves both \(K\) and planar area and sends
\[
\{f_i\leq0\}=\left\{t_i\leq\frac13\right\}
\]
onto the corresponding set for the next index. Therefore
\[
c_1=c_2=c_3.
\tag{24}
\]
The common right side \(h\) in (9), together with (17) and (24), now forces
\[
u_i-x_i
\quad\text{to be independent of }i.
\tag{25}
\]
By (17) and (23), this says that
\[
G_D(d_i)
:=
\frac12[d_i+D]_+^2-\frac12d_i^2
\tag{26}
\]
has the same value for \(i=1,2,3\).

We show that (19) and (26) force \(D=0\). If \(D>0\), every \(d_i+D\) is positive and
\[
G_D(d)=Dd+\frac12D^2.
\]
This is strictly increasing, contradicting \(d_1>d_2\) and (26).

Suppose \(D<0\), and write \(s=-D>0\). If \(d_1\leq s\), then \(d_2,d_3<d_1\leq s\), so
\[
G_D(d_i)=-\frac12d_i^2,
\]
again contradicting (19) and (26). Hence \(d_1>s\). If some \(d_j>s\) for \(j=2,3\), then on both \(d_1\) and \(d_j\),
\[
G_D(d)=-sd+\frac12s^2,
\]
whose strict monotonicity again contradicts (19) and (26). Thus \(d_j\leq s\). Equality of \(G_D(d_1)\) and \(G_D(d_j)\) becomes
\[
-sd_1+\frac12s^2=-\frac12d_j^2,
\]
or
\[
2sd_1=s^2+d_j^2\leq2s^2.
\]
This gives \(d_1\leq s\), a contradiction. Therefore \(D<0\) is also impossible, and
\[
D=0.
\tag{27}
\]

Equations (21) and (27) give
\[
C=B-\frac13.
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(C>0\), (28) implies \(0<C<B\). Equation (15) therefore gives
\[
M<m,
\tag{29}
\]
contradicting (14). This proves the incompatibility.

For completeness, (12) follows by vertex enumeration. In the plane \(t_1+t_2+t_3=0\), write
\[
w_1=(2,-1,-1),\quad
w_2=(-1,2,-1),\quad
w_3=(-1,-1,2).
\]
The endpoint triangles are
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{Bw_1,Bw_2,Bw_3\},
\qquad
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{-Cw_1,-Cw_2,-Cw_3\}.
\]
The six sums \(Bw_i-Cw_j\) with \(i\ne j\), divided by \(2\), are precisely the six vertices of the strip intersection on the right side of (12); the three sums with \(i=j\) lie inside it. Hence the two polygons in (12) agree.
