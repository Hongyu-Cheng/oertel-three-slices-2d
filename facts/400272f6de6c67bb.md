## Statement

Write points of \(\mathbb R^3\) as \((x,w)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\),
and fix \(y=(j,z)\in S\), where \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(z\in A_j\). Then
the infimum defining \(h_S(y)\) is unchanged if it is restricted to closed
halfspaces whose boundary contains \(y\). More precisely,
\[
h_S(j,z)
=
\inf_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathbb R,\ u\in\mathbb R^2\\
\alpha^2+\lVert u\rVert^2=1}}
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
A_i\cap
\left\{
w\in\mathbb R^2:
\langle u,w-z\rangle\le \alpha(j-i)
\right\}
\right|.
\tag{1}
\]
Here every summand is the exact planar area of the cap cut from \(A_i\).

Equivalently, separating the slice-parallel halfspaces, which correspond to
\(u=0\), gives
\[
h_S(j,z)
=
\min\left\{
\inf_{\substack{q\in\mathbb S^1\\ \lambda\in\mathbb R}}
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
A_i\cap
\left\{
w\in\mathbb R^2:
\langle q,w-z\rangle\le \lambda(j-i)
\right\}
\right|,
\ \sum_{i=0}^{j}a_i,
\ \sum_{i=j}^{2}a_i
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The last two quantities are respectively the measures of
\(S\cap\{x\le j\}\) and \(S\cap\{x\ge j\}\).

## Proof

Let \(\mathscr H_y\) be the family of closed halfspaces containing \(y\), and
let \(\mathscr B_y\subseteq\mathscr H_y\) be those whose boundary contains
\(y\). Every proper closed halfspace in \(\mathscr H_y\) can be written as
\[
H=\{(x,w):\alpha x+\langle u,w\rangle\le\beta\},
\]
where \((\alpha,u)\ne(0,0)\) and
\(\alpha j+\langle u,z\rangle\le\beta\). Define
\[
H_y=\{(x,w):\alpha x+\langle u,w\rangle
\le\alpha j+\langle u,z\rangle\}.
\]
Then \(y\in\partial H_y\) and \(H_y\subseteq H\). Consequently,
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_y)\le \mathcal H_2(S\cap H).
\]
Since \(\mathscr B_y\subseteq\mathscr H_y\), the infimum over
\(\mathscr H_y\) is at most the infimum over \(\mathscr B_y\). Conversely,
the displayed construction gives, for every \(H\in\mathscr H_y\), an
\(H_y\in\mathscr B_y\) of no larger measure. Thus the two infima are equal.
If the whole space is admitted as a degenerate halfspace, it contributes
\(T\) and does not change the infimum.

Every halfspace in \(\mathscr B_y\) has the form
\[
H_{\alpha,u}(j,z)
=
\{(x,w):
\alpha(x-j)+\langle u,w-z\rangle\le0\}
\]
for some nonzero \((\alpha,u)\). Both choices of side are included because
replacing \((\alpha,u)\) by \((-\alpha,-u)\) reverses the inequality.
Positive rescaling does not change the halfspace, so one may impose
\(\alpha^2+\lVert u\rVert^2=1\).

On the slice \(x=i\),
\[
S\cap H_{\alpha,u}(j,z)\cap(\{i\}\times\mathbb R^2)
=
\{i\}\times
\left(
A_i\cap
\{w:\langle u,w-z\rangle\le\alpha(j-i)\}
\right).
\]
The embedding \(w\mapsto(i,w)\) is an isometry, so the two-dimensional
Hausdorff measure of this set is exactly the planar area of the indicated cap.
The three slices are disjoint Borel sets. Finite additivity of
\(\mathcal H_2\) therefore yields
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_{\alpha,u}(j,z))
=
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
A_i\cap
\{w:\langle u,w-z\rangle\le\alpha(j-i)\}
\right|,
\]
which proves (1).

It remains to separate the case \(u=0\). If \(u\ne0\), set
\[
q=\frac{u}{\lVert u\rVert}\in\mathbb S^1,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\alpha}{\lVert u\rVert}\in\mathbb R.
\]
Dividing the slice inequality by the positive number \(\lVert u\rVert\) gives
\[
\langle q,w-z\rangle\le\lambda(j-i).
\]
Conversely, every \((q,\lambda)\in\mathbb S^1\times\mathbb R\) arises from a
nonzero normal, for example \((\alpha,u)=(\lambda,q)\), followed if desired
by normalization. Hence the first term in (2) is exactly the infimum over all
boundary-through-\(y\) halfspaces with \(u\ne0\).

If \(u=0\), then \(\alpha\ne0\). For \(\alpha>0\), the halfspace inequality
is \(x\le j\), and on slice \(i\) the condition \(0\le\alpha(j-i)\) holds
exactly when \(i\le j\). Its measure is therefore
\(\sum_{i=0}^{j}a_i\). For \(\alpha<0\), the inequality is \(x\ge j\), and
its measure is \(\sum_{i=j}^{2}a_i\). This proves (2) and also checks the
sign convention.

All cap inequalities above are closed. When \(u\ne0\), equality on any fixed
slice describes an affine line in \(\mathbb R^2\), so it has planar area zero
and does not alter the numerical cap area. When \(u=0\), however, the entire
set \(\{j\}\times A_j\) lies in the boundary and has
\(\mathcal H_2\)-measure \(a_j>0\). Thus the closed inequality is essential:
both horizontal terms in (2) correctly include the full boundary mass \(a_j\).
