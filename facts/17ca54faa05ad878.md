## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and areas \(a_0,a_1,a_2\). Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), and write
\[
m_i=\min_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,\qquad
M_i=\max_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,\qquad
F_i(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\leq c\}\right|.
\]
For \(0\leq u\leq a_i\), define the endpoint-normalized cap quantile
\[
Q_i(0)=m_i,\qquad
Q_i(u)=\inf\{c:F_i(c)\geq u\}\quad(0<u\leq a_i).
\]
Thus \(Q_i(a_i)=M_i\). Extend \(Q_i(u)=+\infty\) for \(u>a_i\).

Put
\[
I(s)=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\bigl(F_0(c)+F_2(s-c)\bigr),
\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
Then \(F_i\) is continuous and nondecreasing on \(\mathbb R\), is strictly increasing from \(0\) to \(a_i\) on \([m_i,M_i]\), and \(Q_i\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,a_i]\). The function \(I\) is continuous and nondecreasing,
\[
I(s)=0\quad(s\leq m_0+m_2),\qquad
I(s)=m\quad(s\geq M_0+M_2).
\]
For \(0<b\leq m\), define
\[
R(b)=\inf\{s:I(s)\geq b\}.
\]
Then the infimum is finite, is attained as the left endpoint of the superlevel interval, and
\[
\boxed{
R(b)=\max_{0\leq u\leq b}
\bigl(Q_0(u)+Q_2(b-u)\bigr).}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(R\) is continuous and nondecreasing on \((0,m]\), with
\[
\lim_{b\downarrow0}R(b)=m_0+m_2.
\tag{2}
\]

For zero-mass demands, use the distinct conventions
\[
D_i(u)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&u=0,\\
Q_i(u),&0<u\leq a_i,\\
+\infty,&u>a_i,
\end{cases}
\qquad
E(b)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&b=0,\\
R(b),&0<b\leq m,\\
+\infty,&b>m.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
The values \(Q_i(0)=m_i\) in (1) are support-endpoint limits; the values
\(D_i(0)=E(0)=-\infty\) in (3) instead mean that a zero-mass summand imposes no threshold constraint.

Define
\[
\Phi(r)=F_1(r)+I(2r).
\]
For every
\[
0<t<a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
the number
\[
\rho(t)=\inf\{r:\Phi(r)\geq t\}
\]
is finite, satisfies \(\Phi(\rho(t))=t\), and obeys the exact attained formula
\[
\boxed{
\rho(t)=
\min_{0\leq a\leq t}
\max\left\{D_1(a),\frac12E(t-a)\right\}.}
\tag{4}
\]
The factor \(1/2\) in (4) is forced by the argument \(I(2r)\).

Finally, let
\[
\Delta=\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1\},
\]
and let \(Q_q^\Delta(s)\) denote its endpoint-normalized directional cap quantile. For
\[
q_1=(-1,0),\qquad q_2=(0,-1),\qquad
q_3=(1,1)/\sqrt2,
\]
and
\[
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1,\qquad\alpha_3=\sqrt2,
\]
one has \(\sum_i\alpha_iq_i=0\), but for every \(2/9<s<1/2\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_iQ_{q_i}^\Delta(s)
=-2+3\sqrt{2s}>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus an individual directional quantile appearing in (1) or (4) is not, in general, a globally admissible support threshold. Equation (5) invalidates only the naive step that exchanges the direction variable with the extrema in (1) or (4) and then treats each quantile as a support function. It is not a counterexample to the balanced inequality for the final \(\rho_q(t)\), and it is not a counterexample to the locked theorem.

## Proof

For fixed \(i\), compactness gives finite \(m_i,M_i\). The cap has area zero for \(c\leq m_i\), because at \(c=m_i\) it is contained in a support line, and it has area \(a_i\) for \(c\geq M_i\). If \(c_n\to c\), the indicators of the caps converge pointwise away from the affine line \(q\cdot x=c\), which has planar area zero. Dominated convergence gives continuity of \(F_i\).

If \(m_i\leq c<d\leq M_i\), with \(c<d\), choose a point of \(\operatorname{int}A_i\) whose \(q\)-coordinate lies strictly between \(c\) and \(d\). Such a point exists because the image of \(\operatorname{int}A_i\) under \(x\mapsto q\cdot x\) is \((m_i,M_i)\). A sufficiently small ball around that point lies in
\[
A_i\cap\{x:c<q\cdot x<d\},
\]
so \(F_i(d)>F_i(c)\). Hence \(F_i\) is strictly increasing on its support interval. Its inverse there, with the stated endpoint values, is exactly \(Q_i\); therefore \(Q_i\) is finite, continuous, and strictly increasing on \([0,a_i]\).

For \(s\) in a bounded interval, the minimization defining \(I(s)\) can be restricted to one bounded interval of \(c\)'s. Indeed, sufficiently far to the left its value is exactly \(a_2\), and sufficiently far to the right its value is exactly \(a_0\), while \(I(s)\leq\min\{a_0,a_2\}\). Joint continuity of
\[
(s,c)\longmapsto F_0(c)+F_2(s-c)
\]
on that bounded rectangle shows, by taking convergent minimizers, that \(I\) is continuous. If \(s<s'\), every fixed-\(c\) summand at \(s\) is at most its value at \(s'\), so \(I(s)\leq I(s')\).

If \(s\leq m_0+m_2\), choosing \(c=m_0\) makes both caps null, hence \(I(s)=0\). If \(s\geq M_0+M_2\), then for every \(c\), either \(c\geq M_0\), in which case the first cap has area \(a_0\), or \(c<M_0\), in which case \(s-c>M_2\) and the second cap has area \(a_2\). Thus every sum is at least \(m\). The reverse inequality follows from the two tail choices, so \(I(s)=m\). This proves all asserted continuity, monotonicity, and endpoint facts for \(I\), and it makes \(R(b)\) finite for \(0<b\leq m\).

Fix such a \(b\), and set
\[
R_*(b)=\max_{0\leq u\leq b}
\bigl(Q_0(u)+Q_2(b-u)\bigr).
\]
The maximum exists because both quantiles are continuous and \(b\leq a_0,a_2\).

We prove
\[
I(s)<b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s<R_*(b).
\tag{6}
\]
First suppose \(I(s)<b\). Choose \(c\) with
\[
F_0(c)+F_2(s-c)<b.
\]
There is then a number \(u\) strictly between
\[
F_0(c)\quad\text{and}\quad b-F_2(s-c).
\]
It satisfies \(0<u<b\). By the definition and strict monotonicity of the cap quantiles,
\[
c<Q_0(u),\qquad s-c<Q_2(b-u).
\]
Adding gives \(s<Q_0(u)+Q_2(b-u)\leq R_*(b)\).

Conversely, suppose \(s<R_*(b)\), and choose \(u\in[0,b]\) for which
\[
s<Q_0(u)+Q_2(b-u).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(0<u<b\), choose \(c\) strictly between
\[
s-Q_2(b-u)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_0(u).
\]
Then \(F_0(c)<u\) and \(F_2(s-c)<b-u\), so their sum is less than \(b\). If \(u=0\), (7) reads \(s<m_0+Q_2(b)\); choose
\[
s-Q_2(b)<c<m_0.
\]
Then \(F_0(c)=0\) and \(F_2(s-c)<b\). The case \(u=b\) is symmetric: choose
\[
s-m_2<c<Q_0(b),
\]
so \(F_0(c)<b\) and \(F_2(s-c)=0\). Thus \(I(s)<b\) in every case, proving (6).

Since \(I\) is continuous and nondecreasing, (6) implies
\[
\{s:I(s)\geq b\}=[R_*(b),\infty).
\]
Therefore \(R(b)=R_*(b)\), proving (1) together with threshold attainment. Formula (1), after writing \(u=bv\) with \(v\in[0,1]\), is the maximum of a jointly continuous function of \((b,v)\) for \(b>0\). It follows directly from convergent maximizing subsequences that \(R\) is continuous on \((0,m]\). It is nondecreasing because its superlevel threshold is the generalized inverse of the nondecreasing function \(I\). Uniform continuity of \(Q_0,Q_2\) at zero in (1) gives
\[
\lim_{b\downarrow0}R(b)
=Q_0(0)+Q_2(0)=m_0+m_2,
\]
which proves (2). Notice that this finite right limit is different from the zero-demand convention \(E(0)=-\infty\).

The function \(\Phi(r)=F_1(r)+I(2r)\) is continuous and nondecreasing. It is zero for all sufficiently negative \(r\), and it equals \(a_1+m\) for all sufficiently positive \(r\). Hence, for
\(0<t<a_1+m\), its superlevel set is a nonempty closed ray
\[
\{r:\Phi(r)\geq t\}=[\rho(t),\infty),
\]
and continuity gives \(\Phi(\rho(t))=t\).

For any \(r\), one has
\[
\Phi(r)\geq t
\]
if and only if there is \(a\in[0,t]\) such that
\[
F_1(r)\geq a,\qquad I(2r)\geq t-a.
\tag{8}
\]
The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication, the interval
\[
\left[\max\{0,t-I(2r)\},\ \min\{t,F_1(r)\}\right]
\]
is nonempty, and any \(a\) in it satisfies (8). By the conventions (3), condition (8) is equivalent to
\[
r\geq D_1(a),\qquad
r\geq\frac12E(t-a).
\tag{9}
\]
The factor \(1/2\) follows because the endpoint condition is
\(2r\geq E(t-a)\).

Every finite value of the maximum in (4) therefore belongs to the superlevel ray of \(\Phi\), so it is at least \(\rho(t)\). Conversely, at \(r=\rho(t)\), choose \(a\) from (8). Then (9) makes the corresponding maximum in (4) at most \(\rho(t)\). Thus the minimum in (4) is attained and equals \(\rho(t)\).

It remains to prove the calibration (5). The triangle \(\Delta\) has area \(1/2\). For \(q_1=(-1,0)\) and \(-1\leq r\leq0\),
\[
\left|\Delta\cap\{-x\leq r\}\right|
=\left|\Delta\cap\{x\geq-r\}\right|
=\frac{(1+r)^2}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q_{q_1}^\Delta(s)=-1+\sqrt{2s}
\qquad(0<s<1/2).
\]
The same calculation gives the identical formula for \(q_2\). For
\(q_3=(1,1)/\sqrt2\) and \(0\leq r\leq1/\sqrt2\),
\[
\left|\Delta\cap\{(x+y)/\sqrt2\leq r\}\right|
=\frac{(\sqrt2r)^2}{2}=r^2,
\]
so \(Q_{q_3}^\Delta(s)=\sqrt s\). The stated weights are positive and satisfy
\[
q_1+q_2+\sqrt2q_3=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
Q_{q_1}^\Delta(s)+Q_{q_2}^\Delta(s)
+\sqrt2Q_{q_3}^\Delta(s)
=-2+3\sqrt{2s},
\]
which is positive exactly when \(s>2/9\). This proves (5). Since (4) forms the final threshold only after a direction-dependent minimum of maxima involving the max-plus convolution (1), failure of a balanced inequality for one operand does not imply failure for \(\rho_q(t)\). The final limitation statement follows.
