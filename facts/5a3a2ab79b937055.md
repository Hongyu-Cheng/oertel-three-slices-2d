## Statement

Let \(s,a,b,\delta>0\), and let \(P\) be a strictly convex pentagon with
clockwise vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0&=(-a,\delta+a),&
p_1&=(\delta+b,-b),\\
p_2&=(c_2,d_2),&
p_3&=(c_3,d_3),&
p_4&=(-e,f).
\end{aligned}
\]
Assume \(c_2,d_2,c_3,d_3,e,f>0\), every \(p_2,p_3,p_4\) lies strictly
below \(x+y=\delta\), \(p_0p_1\) is a support edge, \(p_0,p_4\) are the
only vertices with \(x<0\), and \(p_1\) is the only vertex with \(y<0\).
If
\[
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},
\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},
\tag{1}
\]
then, with \(L=\delta+a+b\),
\[
|P|\leq \frac{s}{8}+\frac{sL^2}{8a^2+5b^2+2s}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, no strictly convex rational pentagon in this sign cell
satisfies
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},\qquad
\delta<a+b.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

Apply the affine map
\[
\Phi(x,y)=(\xi,h)=(x+a,\delta-x-y).
\tag{4}
\]
Its linear part has determinant \(-1\), so it preserves area and
reverses orientation.  Put \(\widetilde P=\Phi(P)\) and
\(L=\delta+a+b\).  The support-edge vertices become
\[
q_0=(0,0),\qquad q_1=(L,0).
\tag{5}
\]
Write \(q_i=(\xi_i,h_i)\).  For \(i=2,3\), positivity of both
coordinates of \(p_i\), together with the strict support inequality,
gives
\[
\xi_i>a,\qquad h_i>0,\qquad
\xi_i+h_i=L-b-y(p_i)<L-b.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(q_4=(E,v)=\Phi(p_4)\), the corresponding conditions are
\[
E=a-e<a,\qquad v>0,\qquad E+v=L-b-f<L-b.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, \(q_1\) is the unique rightmost vertex.  All vertices
other than \(q_0,q_1\) lie strictly above the edge \(q_0q_1\).
Therefore, on the full strip \(0\leq\tau\leq L\), the vertical sections
have the form
\[
\widetilde P\cap\{\xi=\tau\}
=\{(\tau,h):0\leq h\leq F(\tau)\},
\tag{8}
\]
where \(F:[0,L]\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) is concave and \(F(L)=0\).
No assumption that \(E\geq0\) is needed.  If \(E<0\), then \(F(0)>0\)
is allowed.

The line \(x=0\) becomes \(\xi=a\).  The strict signs in (6)-(7) show
that the actual clipped polygon is
\[
\widetilde P\cap\{\xi\leq a\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{q_0,(a,0),z_x,q_4\},
\qquad
z_x=q_3q_4\cap\{\xi=a\},
\tag{9}
\]
where the two intersections in (9) lie in the relative interiors of
\(q_0q_1\) and \(q_3q_4\), respectively.  This remains the cyclic
clipping order whether \(E<0\), \(E=0\), or \(E>0\).  In particular,
the clipped polygon contains every vertical section in (8) with
\(0\leq\tau\leq a\).  Hence (1) implies
\[
A:=\int_0^a F(t)\,dt\leq\frac{s}{8}.
\tag{10}
\]

Fix \(\tau\in[a,L]\).  For \(0\leq t\leq a\), concavity and
nonnegativity give
\[
F(t)\geq
\left(1-\frac{t}{\tau}\right)F(0)
+\frac{t}{\tau}F(\tau)
\geq\frac{t}{\tau}F(\tau).
\tag{11}
\]
Integrating (11) over \(0\leq t\leq a\) and using (10) yields
\[
F(\tau)\leq\frac{2A\tau}{a^2}
\leq\frac{s\tau}{4a^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the nontriangular left cap supplies the global linear bound
\[
F(\tau)\leq\alpha\tau\quad(a\leq\tau\leq L),
\qquad
\alpha:=\frac{s}{4a^2}.
\tag{13}
\]

It remains to obtain the right bound from the actual \(y\)-cap.  Write
\[
q_2=(C,u),\qquad
\beta:=\frac{u}{L-C}.
\tag{14}
\]
By (6), \(C+u<L-b\).  Hence
\[
(1-\beta)(L-C)=L-C-u>b,
\tag{15}
\]
so \(0<\beta<1\).  The inverse of (4) is
\[
y=L-b-\xi-h,
\tag{16}
\]
and \(y=0\) is therefore the line \(h=L-b-\xi\).  On the edge
\(q_1q_2\), put \(r=L-\xi\).  The edge is \(h=\beta r\), while
\(y=0\) is \(h=r-b\).  Equation (15) shows that they meet in the
relative interior of \(q_1q_2\), at
\[
r=\frac{b}{1-\beta},
\qquad
h=\frac{b\beta}{1-\beta}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the image of the actual second clipped polygon is the triangle
\[
\Phi(P\cap\{y\leq0\})
=\operatorname{conv}\{q_1,(L-b,0),z_y\},
\qquad
z_y=q_1q_2\cap\{\xi+h=L-b\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Its horizontal base has length \(b\), and its height is the second
quantity in (17).  The second equation in (1) is exactly
\[
\frac{b^2\beta}{2(1-\beta)}=\frac{s}{5},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{2s}{5b^2+2s}.
\tag{19}
\]

The edge \(q_1q_2\) gives
\[
F(\tau)=\beta(L-\tau)\qquad(C\leq\tau\leq L).
\tag{20}
\]
For \(0\leq\tau<C\), apply concavity at \(C\), between \(\tau\) and
\(L\):
\[
F(C)\geq\frac{L-C}{L-\tau}F(\tau).
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(F(C)=\beta(L-C)\), equation (21), together with (20), proves
\[
F(\tau)\leq\beta(L-\tau)\qquad(0\leq\tau\leq L).
\tag{22}
\]

The exact \(x\)-cap accounts for all of \(\widetilde P\) to the left of
\(\xi=a\), while (8) accounts for the remainder.  Combining
(1), (13), and (22) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P|
&=|\widetilde P|
=\frac{s}{8}+\int_a^L F(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\leq
\frac{s}{8}
+\int_a^L
\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}\,d\tau\\
&\leq
\frac{s}{8}
+\int_0^L
\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}\,d\tau\\
&=
\frac{s}{8}
+\frac{\alpha\beta L^2}{2(\alpha+\beta)}\\
&=
\frac{s}{8}
+\frac{sL^2}{8a^2+5b^2+2s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
This proves (2).

If \(|P|=16s\), equation (23) implies
\[
L^2\geq\frac{127}{8}(8a^2+5b^2+2s).
\tag{24}
\]
The right side of (24) is strictly larger than \(4(a+b)^2\), because
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{127}{8}(8a^2+5b^2+2s)-4(a+b)^2\\
&\qquad=
4(a-b)^2+119a^2+\frac{571}{8}b^2+\frac{127}{4}s
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(L,a,b>0\), equations (24)-(25) give
\[
L>2(a+b).
\tag{26}
\]
Finally, \(L=\delta+a+b\), so (26) is
\[
\delta>a+b,
\tag{27}
\]
contradicting the last condition in (3).  The proof did not use
rationality and did not impose an order between the two \(x\)-negative
vertices.  It therefore covers every strictly convex rational pentagon
in the declared sign cell.
