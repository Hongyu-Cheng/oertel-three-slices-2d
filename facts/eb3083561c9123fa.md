## Statement

Let \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex set.  Suppose that,
for a linear functional \(\ell\) and \(h>0\),
\[
B\subseteq\{\ell\geq h\},
\]
and the exposed face \(B\cap\{\ell=h\}\) contains
\[
u=(u_x,u_y),\qquad p=(0,p_y),\qquad b=(b_x,0)
\]
with
\[
u_x<0,\qquad p_y>0,\qquad b_x<0,\qquad [u,p]\subseteq
B\cap\{\ell=h\}.
\]
Assume also that \(B\) contains a point \(v\) with \(v_x,v_y>0\).
For every Lebesgue-measurable convex set \(K\supseteq B\) for which
the following two areas are finite, put \(D=K\setminus B\) and
\[
e_x=|D\cap\{x\leq0\}|,\qquad
e_y=|D\cap\{y\leq0\}|.
\]
Then
\[
e_y>e_x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e_x\geq \frac{(-u_x)p_y}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the explicit \(A_0,A_2,B=(A_0+A_2)/2\) in accepted fact
`50ba22f0dcebcebd`, no Lebesgue-measurable convex \(K\supseteq B\)
can simultaneously satisfy the first two requirements in its
equations (41)-(42):
\[
\left|(K\setminus B)\cap\{x\leq0\}\right|
=
\frac{14056008580415861849411}
     {16636763915211600000000},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\left|(K\setminus B)\cap\{y\leq0\}\right|
=
\frac{53223406632483650320207}
     {3198422422674000000000}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently no convex completion realizes all three equations
(41)-(42), even without imposing \(|K\setminus B|=20\).

## Proof

We first prove (1).  Let
\[
Q=D\cap\{x\leq0,\ y\leq0\}.
\]
The disjoint decomposition by \(x\leq0\) and \(x>0\) gives
\[
e_y
=|Q|+|D\cap\{x>0,\ y\leq0\}|,
\qquad
e_x\geq |Q|.
\]
Thus \(e_y>e_x\) implies
\[
|D\cap\{x>0,\ y\leq0\}|>0.
\]
The line \(y=0\) has planar area zero, so there is
\(z\in D\) with \(z_x>0\) and \(z_y<0\).  The segment
\([z,v]\subseteq K\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at a point
\(w=(w_x,0)\) with \(w_x>0\).  Since \(b=(b_x,0)\in B\subseteq K\)
and \(b_x<0\), convexity gives
\[
O=(0,0)\in[b,w]\subseteq K.
\]
The triangle
\[
\mathcal T=\operatorname{conv}\{u,p,O\}
\]
is contained in \(K\) and in \(\{x\leq0\}\).  Its base \([u,p]\)
lies on \(\{\ell=h\}\), while \(\ell(O)=0<h\).  Hence every point of
\(\mathcal T\setminus[u,p]\), apart from its area-zero boundary, has
\(\ell<h\) and therefore lies outside \(B\).  It follows that
\[
e_x\geq|\mathcal T|
=\frac{|\det(u,p)|}{2}
=\frac{(-u_x)p_y}{2},
\]
which proves the lemma.

We now audit the explicit application from the defining rational data,
without using the arithmetic asserted in the Pro reply.  Write
\[
P=\left(-\frac{100001}{1000},-\frac{10000001}{100000}\right),
\quad
V=\left(\frac{19713}{100},-\frac{61331}{625}\right),
\quad
R=\left(3,-\frac{197}{2}\right),
\]
\[
c=\left(-\frac{99}{2},\frac{991}{10}\right),\qquad
a=\left(302,\frac{19}{10}\right),\qquad
s=\left(0,\frac1{100000}\right),
\]
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{P,V,R\},\qquad
A_2=c+\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]a
       +\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]s.
\]
Define
\[
\ell(x,y)=3020y-19x,
\]
and let
\[
q_0=c-\frac a2-\frac s2
=\left(-\frac{401}{2},\frac{19629999}{200000}\right),
\]
\[
q_1=c+\frac a2-\frac s2
=\left(\frac{203}{2},\frac{20009999}{200000}\right).
\]
Direct calculation gives
\[
\ell(V)-\ell(P)=\frac{7873}{2500}>0,\qquad
\ell(R)-\ell(P)=\frac{1608132}{625}>0,
\]
\[
\ell(a)=0,\qquad \ell(s)=\frac{151}{5000}>0.
\]
Therefore \(P\) is the unique minimizer of \(\ell\) on \(A_0\), the
minimizing face of \(A_2\) is exactly \([q_0,q_1]\).  For
\(x_0\in A_0,x_2\in A_2\),
\[
\ell\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
=\frac{\ell(x_0)+\ell(x_2)}2,
\]
and equality with the minimum occurs exactly when
\(x_0=P\) and \(x_2\in[q_0,q_1]\).  Hence the minimizing face of
\(B=(A_0+A_2)/2\) is exactly \([v_0,v_1]\), where
\[
v_0=\frac{P+q_0}{2}
=\left(-\frac{300501}{2000},-\frac{370003}{400000}\right),
\]
\[
v_1=\frac{P+q_1}{2}
=\left(\frac{1499}{2000},\frac{9997}{400000}\right).
\]
Both points have
\[
\ell(v_0)=\ell(v_1)=h:=\frac{1224737}{20000}>0,
\]
so
\[
B\subseteq\{\ell\geq h\}.
\tag{4}
\]

The line \(\ell=h\) meets the positive \(y\)-axis and the negative
\(x\)-axis at
\[
p=\left(0,\frac{1224737}{60400000}\right),\qquad
b=\left(-\frac{1224737}{380000},0\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(v_{0x}<0<v_{1x}\) and \(v_{0y}<0<v_{1y}\), respectively,
both points in (5) lie on \([v_0,v_1]\).  Finally
\[
v_2=\frac{V+q_1}{2}
=\left(\frac{29863}{200},\frac{384079}{400000}\right)\in B
\]
has two strictly positive coordinates.  Thus (1) applies with
\(u=v_0\), \(v=v_2\), and its forced area is
\[
\frac{(-v_{0x})p_y}{2}
=
\frac12\left(\frac{300501}{2000}\right)
       \left(\frac{1224737}{60400000}\right)
=\frac{368034693237}{241600000000}.
\tag{6}
\]

Let the right sides of (2)-(3) be \(\tau_1,\tau_2\).  The exact
comparisons are
\[
1-\tau_1
=\frac{2580755334795738150589}
       {16636763915211600000000}>0,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\tau_2-16
=\frac{2048647869699650320207}
       {3198422422674000000000}>0,
\tag{8}

\[
\frac{368034693237}{241600000000}-1
=\frac{126434693237}{241600000000}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, \(\tau_2>\tau_1\).  If a convex \(K\supseteq B\)
satisfied (2)-(3), implication (1) and (6) would give
\[
\tau_1\geq\frac{368034693237}{241600000000}>1,
\]
contradicting (7).  Hence no such \(K\) exists.  The extension-area
condition and the third target are not used.
