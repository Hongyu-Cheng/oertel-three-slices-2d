## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and assume the affine data, compatible two-dimensional polytopes \(A_0,A_2,K\), and exact cells \(P_J,Q_H,R_G\) of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,\qquad q_H=|Q_H|,\qquad r_G=|R_G|,
\]
\[
M=\sum_Jp_J,\qquad m=\sum_Hq_H,\qquad k=\sum_Gr_G,
\]
and assume \(M\ge m>0\).

Assume the all-double support from accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`:
\[
q_N>0,\qquad q_{N\setminus\{i\}}=x_i>0\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
with every other \(q_H=0\). Put
\[
p_0=p_\varnothing,\qquad
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H=m-x_i,
\]
\[
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
Assume the three crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h
\]
and the canonical left-tail inequalities supplied by accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`,
\[
c_i+m\ge h.
\]
Let
\[
C_i=Q_{N\setminus\{i\}},\qquad
B_i=\frac{P_\varnothing+C_i}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac14\sum_{i=1}^3(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{x_i})^2
\le k-r_N+\sum_{|G|=1}r_G. \tag{1}
\]

Nevertheless, (1), the product inequality
\[
q_N^2m\ge4x_1x_2x_3
\]
from accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031`, the three crossings, and the stated tail constraints do not imply
\[
\sum_i u_i+m+q_N+\sum_{|G|=2}r_G+2r_N
>
\frac{2M-m-k}{3}. \tag{2}
\]

## Proof

First, the source of every \(B_i\) has positive area. The crossings and tails give
\[
u_i+v_i=h-c_i\le m.
\]
Since \(v_i=m-x_i\), this is
\[
0\le u_i\le x_i.
\]
Writing \(U=\sum_i u_i\), the all-double support gives
\[
U\le x_1+x_2+x_3=m-q_N<m.
\]
Because \(P_N=\varnothing\) by accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`,
\[
M-p_0=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subsetneq N}p_J
\le\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subsetneq N}|J|p_J
=U.
\]
Hence
\[
p_0\ge M-U>M-m\ge0,
\]
so \(p_0>0\). The all-double support already gives \(x_i>0\). Thus no empty or zero-area source case remains.

Brunn-Minkowski applied to the measurable convex cells \(P_\varnothing\) and \(C_i\) gives
\[
|B_i|^{1/2}
\ge\frac{\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{x_i}}2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac14\sum_i(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{x_i})^2
\le\sum_i|B_i|. \tag{3}
\]

For \(z\in B_i\), apply the exact incidence statement of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` with \(J=\varnothing\) and \(H=N\setminus\{i\}\). If \(z\in R_G\), then
\[
\varnothing\ne G\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}.
\]
Consequently a point of a singleton cell \(R_G\) can belong to at most two of the \(B_i\), a point of a doubleton cell can belong to at most one, and no point of \(R_N\) belongs to any \(B_i\). Integrating this pointwise multiplicity bound over the exact disjoint cells gives
\[
\sum_i|B_i|
\le
2\sum_{|G|=1}r_G+\sum_{|G|=2}r_G
=
k-r_N+\sum_{|G|=1}r_G. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (3) and (4) prove (1). Vanishing target cells and vanishing pairwise intersections require no separate argument. The exact weak/strict convention assigns every cutting-line point to one cell, and accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` shows that all cutting lines have planar area zero. This covers all null-boundary cases.

Now perform the crossing substitution. Put
\[
S=\sum_{|G|=1}r_G,\qquad
D=\sum_{|G|=2}r_G,\qquad
r=r_N,\qquad
E_R=D+2r.
\]
Since
\[
x_1+x_2+x_3=m-q_N,
\]
one has
\[
\sum_i v_i=3m-(m-q_N)=2m+q_N.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_i c_i=S+2D+3r=k+E_R.
\]
Summing the crossings therefore gives
\[
U+2m+q_N+k+E_R
=
3h
=
\frac{2(M+m+k)}3.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
U+m+q_N+E_R
=
\frac{2M-m-k}{3}. \tag{5}
\]
Thus the desired strict inequality (2) can follow only if the remaining scalar constraints are inconsistent.

They are not. Consider the following exact nonnegative cell masses, with every unlisted cell assigned mass zero:
\[
M=\frac{32}{5},\qquad m=1,\qquad k=4,
\]
\[
p_\varnothing=\frac{29}{5},\qquad
p_{\{1\}}=p_{\{2\}}=p_{\{3\}}=\frac15,
\]
\[
q_N=\frac25,\qquad
q_{\{1,2\}}=q_{\{1,3\}}=q_{\{2,3\}}=\frac15,
\]
\[
r_{\{1\}}=r_{\{2\}}=r_{\{3\}}=\frac{71}{60},
\]
\[
r_{\{1,2\}}=r_{\{1,3\}}=r_{\{2,3\}}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
r_N=\frac3{20}.
\]
The totals are exactly
\[
\frac{29}{5}+3\cdot\frac15=\frac{32}{5},
\qquad
\frac25+3\cdot\frac15=1,
\]
and
\[
3\cdot\frac{71}{60}+3\cdot\frac1{10}+\frac3{20}=4.
\]
For every \(i\),
\[
u_i=\frac15,\qquad
v_i=\frac45,\qquad
c_i=\frac{71}{60}+2\cdot\frac1{10}+\frac3{20}
=\frac{23}{15}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
h=\frac{2(32/5+1+4)}9=\frac{38}{15},
\]
so all crossings hold:
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i
=
\frac15+\frac45+\frac{23}{15}
=
\frac{38}{15}.
\]
The tails hold at equality:
\[
c_i+m=\frac{23}{15}+1=\frac{38}{15}=h,
\]
with
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad 0<h<M.
\]
This is exactly the proper-proper equality-tail state allowed by accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`.

The three-cap product inequality is strict:
\[
q_N^2m=\frac4{25}
>
\frac4{125}
=
4x_1x_2x_3.
\]
The square-root inequality from accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031` is also strict:
\[
\sum_i\sqrt{\frac{x_i}{m}}=\frac3{\sqrt5}<\frac32.
\]

The corrected multiplicity inequality has strict slack. Its left side is
\[
\frac34\left(\sqrt{\frac{29}{5}}+\sqrt{\frac15}\right)^2
=
\frac92+\frac{3\sqrt{29}}{10},
\]
while its right side is
\[
4-\frac3{20}+3\cdot\frac{71}{60}
=
\frac{37}{5}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
\frac{37}{5}
-\left(\frac92+\frac{3\sqrt{29}}{10}\right)
=
\frac{29-3\sqrt{29}}{10}>0.
\]

Finally,
\[
\sum_i u_i+m+q_N+D+2r_N
=
\frac35+1+\frac25+\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}
=
\frac{13}{5},
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{2M-m-k}{3}
=
\frac{2(32/5)-1-4}{3}
=
\frac{13}{5}.
\]
Thus (2) is equality, not strict inequality. The multiplicity bound, strict product inequality, crossings, and canonical tail constraints are jointly consistent at the exact scalar level. Closing the all-double branch requires an additional inequality coupling the three arrangements through their common affine plane.
