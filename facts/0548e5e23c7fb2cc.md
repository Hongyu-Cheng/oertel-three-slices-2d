## Statement

Put
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(0,0),
\left(-\frac{174}{121},0\right),
\left(0,-\frac{121}{200}\right)\right\}.
\]
The fixed feasibility target asks whether there exists a compact
two-dimensional rational convex polygon \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
D\subseteq K,\qquad |K|=1,
\]
whose areas in the following six open cones, in the displayed order, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{cone}&\text{required area}\\ \hline
x<0,\ y<0&\dfrac{870}{2000}\\[2pt]
x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y>0&\dfrac{240}{2000}\\[2pt]
x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y>0&\dfrac{260}{2000}\\[2pt]
x>0,\ y>0&\dfrac{628}{2000}\\[2pt]
x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y<0&\dfrac1{2000}\\[2pt]
x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y<0&\dfrac1{2000},
\end{array}
\]
and such that
\[
\left|K\cap\{0<x+y\le1\}\right|=\frac1{3000}.
\]
No such rational convex polygon exists.  Indeed, no compact
two-dimensional convex set satisfies this full list of conditions.

## Proof

Set
\[
p=\frac{174}{121},\qquad q=\frac{121}{200},
\]
and write
\[
O=(0,0),\qquad A=(-p,0),\qquad B=(0,-q).
\]
Any object satisfying the full list in the statement contains
\(D=\operatorname{conv}\{O,A,B\}\) and, in particular, satisfies the four
area conditions
\[
\left|K\cap\{x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y<0\}\right|=\frac1{2000},             \tag{1}
\]
\[
\left|K\cap\{x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y<0\}\right|=\frac1{2000},             \tag{2}
\]
\[
\left|K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}\right|=\frac{628}{2000},                 \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\left|K\cap\{0<x+y\le1\}\right|=\frac1{3000}.                     \tag{4}
\]
We prove that convexity and \(D\subseteq K\) make these four conditions
inconsistent.  This suffices to rule out the complete list, independently of
the unused total-area and remaining cone-area equalities.

Let \(Z=(X,Y)\in K\) have \(X>0\), \(Y>0\), and put \(h=X+Y\).
Convexity and \(D\subseteq K\) imply
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{A,O,Z\}\subseteq K.
\]
The segment from \(A\) to \(Z\) meets the line \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_A=
\left(-\frac{pY}{h+p},\frac{pY}{h+p}\right).
\]
Apart from its three boundary segments, the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{A,O,W_A\}\) lies in
\(\{x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y<0\}\).  Its area is
\[
\frac12\left|\det(A,W_A)\right|
=\frac{p^2Y}{2(h+p)}.
\]
Condition (1) therefore gives
\[
\frac{p^2Y}{2(h+p)}\le\frac1{2000},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
Y\le\frac{h+p}{1000p^2}.                                       \tag{5}
\]

Likewise,
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{B,O,Z\}\subseteq K,
\]
and the segment from \(B\) to \(Z\) meets \(x+y=0\) at
\[
W_B=
\left(\frac{qX}{h+q},-\frac{qX}{h+q}\right).
\]
Apart from its boundary, the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{B,O,W_B\}\) lies in
\(\{x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y<0\}\), and its area is
\[
\frac12\left|\det(B,W_B)\right|
=\frac{q^2X}{2(h+q)}.
\]
Condition (2) therefore gives
\[
X\le\frac{h+q}{1000q^2}.                                       \tag{6}
\]

If \(h\ge1\), then (5) and (6), together with \(X+Y=h\), give
\[
1
\le
\frac1{1000}
\left(
\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p+\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q
\right).
\]
But \(p>1\) and \(q>\tfrac12\), so
\[
\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p+\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q
<1+1+4+2=8.
\]
This would imply \(1<8/1000\), a contradiction.  Hence every point of
\(K\) in the open positive quadrant satisfies \(0<x+y<1\).

Thus, up to boundary sets of area zero,
\[
K\cap\{x>0,\ y>0\}
\subseteq K\cap\{0<x+y\le1\}.
\]
Conditions (3) and (4) would consequently imply
\[
\frac{628}{2000}\le\frac1{3000},
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{628}{2000}=\frac{157}{500}=\frac{942}{3000}
>\frac1{3000}.
\]
This contradiction proves that no compact convex set can satisfy the four
conditions (1)--(4), and therefore no compact two-dimensional rational
convex polygon can satisfy the complete registered six-cone and strip-area
list.
