## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(D\), with a fixed vertex \(V_0\), and let
\[
A_0=V_0+a(\Delta-V_0),\qquad A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_2=V_0+c(\Delta-V_0),
\]
where
\[
a\ge c>0,\qquad 1<a^2+c^2,\qquad a+c<2,\qquad a^2<1+c^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\qquad T=(a^2+1+c^2)D.
\]
If \(g\) is the centroid of \(\Delta\), then
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge \frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]
Accepted fact `8be4b2011a05cfd2` shows that, after possibly interchanging the outer slices, every residual common-vertex homothetic-triangle triple satisfies (1). Consequently (2) holds on the whole exact residual domain, including all regions (U1)–(U6) identified in accepted fact `56bc39a3b6a791db`.

## Proof

Work with mass divided by \(D\), and put
\[
Q=\frac{2(a^2+1+c^2)}9,\qquad s=a+c,\qquad d=a-c.
\]
By (1),
\[
1<s<2,\qquad sd=a^2-c^2<1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
a^2+c^2=\frac{s^2+d^2}{2}
<\frac{s^2+s^{-2}}2.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q<\overline Q(s):=\frac{(s+s^{-1})^2}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

Apply the exact normal form in accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` at the centroid. For a normalized direction, write its ordered projected vertex values as
\[
\gamma,\qquad \gamma+\rho,\qquad \gamma+1,
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1,
\]
and define
\[
\phi_\rho(u)=\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(\gamma+u).
\]
The middle-slice cap fraction at the centroid is
\[
p(\rho)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{5-5\rho-\rho^2}{9(1-\rho)},
&0\le\rho\le\frac12,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{(1+\rho)^2}{9\rho},
&\frac12\le\rho\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
These formulas include the tied directions \(\rho=0,1\). They imply
\[
p(\rho)\ge\frac49,
\tag{5}
\]
and, when \(0\le\rho\le1/2\),
\[
p(\rho)\ge\frac12,
\qquad
p(\rho)\ge\frac{5-\rho}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed,
\[
p(\rho)-\frac12
=\frac{(1-2\rho)(1+\rho)}{18(1-\rho)},
\]
and
\[
p(\rho)-\frac{5-\rho}{9}
=\frac{\rho(1-2\rho)}{9(1-\rho)}.
\]

For a slope parameter \(\sigma\), put
\[
u_0=\frac{m+\sigma}{a}-\gamma,\qquad
u_2=\frac{m-\sigma}{c}-\gamma,
\qquad
m=\gamma+\frac{1+\rho}{3}.
\]
The normalized nonhorizontal mass is
\[
p(\rho)+a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2),
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
au_0+cu_2=2m-s\gamma=:K.
\tag{8}
\]

We first record a uniform outer-cap estimate. Define
\[
\psi(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2,&0\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
The splines in accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` give
\[
\phi_\rho(u)\ge\psi(u)
\tag{9}
\]
for every \(0\le\rho\le1\). On the upper spline branch this follows from
\[
\phi_\rho(u)-u^2
=\frac{(1-u)((2-\rho)u-\rho)}{1-\rho}\ge0,
\]
while the lower branch and tied endpoints are immediate.

Suppose \(K\ge0\), and write \(X=au_0\), \(Y=cu_2\), so \(X+Y=K\). If \(X,Y\ge0\) and neither cap is saturated, (9) gives the outer mass \(X^2+Y^2\ge K^2/2\). If one cap is saturated, its contribution is at least \(c^2\), since \(a\ge c\). If \(X<0\), then \(Y>K\); either the \(Y\)-cap is saturated or it contributes \(Y^2>K^2\). The case \(Y<0\) is symmetric. Hence
\[
a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2)
\ge \min\left\{c^2,\frac{K^2}{2}\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Also, by (1), (4), and (5),
\[
Q<\frac49+\frac{4c^2}{9},
\qquad
Q-p(\rho)<c^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently, whenever \(K\ge0\), it is enough to prove
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}\ge\overline Q(s).
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, if \(p(\rho)\ge Q\), (7) is already at least \(Q\). Otherwise, (3), (11), and (12) show that both \(c^2\) and \(K^2/2\) are strictly larger than \(Q-p(\rho)\), so (10) again makes (7) at least \(Q\).

It remains to verify (12) in the three possible positions of the common vertex.

If the common vertex is the minimum projected vertex, then \(\gamma=0\) and
\[
K=\frac{2(1+\rho)}3.
\]
Since \(\overline Q(s)\le\overline Q(2)=25/36\), for \(\rho\le1/2\), (6) gives
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\ge\frac12+\frac29
=\frac{26}{36}>\frac{25}{36}.
\]
For \(\rho\ge1/2\), (5) and \(K\ge1\) give an even larger lower bound. Thus (12) holds.

If the common vertex is the middle projected vertex, then \(\gamma=-\rho\) and
\[
K=\frac23+\rho\left(s-\frac43\right)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
For fixed \(\rho\), define
\[
G(s)=p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}-\overline Q(s).
\]
If \(0\le\rho\le1/2\), then on \(1\le s\le2\),
\[
G''(s)
=\rho^2-\frac29\left(1+\frac3{s^4}\right)
\le\frac14-\frac{19}{72}<0.
\]
Thus \(G\) is concave and its minimum is attained at an endpoint. At \(s=1\), (6) gives \(G(1)>0\). At \(s=2\),
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\ge\frac12+\frac29
=\frac{26}{36}>\overline Q(2).
\]
Hence \(G\ge0\).

If \(1/2\le\rho\le1\), the function
\[
\rho\left[\frac23+\rho\left(s-\frac43\right)\right]
\]
is nondecreasing in \(\rho\) for \(1\le s\le2\), because its derivative is
\[
\frac23+2\rho\left(s-\frac43\right)\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
G'(s)
=\rho K-\frac29(s-s^{-3})
\ge\frac{s}{4}-\frac29(s-s^{-3})
=\frac{s}{36}+\frac{2}{9s^3}>0.
\]
Since \(G(1)\ge p(\rho)-4/9\ge0\), (12) follows throughout the middle-vertex chamber.

Finally suppose the common vertex is the maximum projected vertex. Then \(\gamma=-1\) and
\[
K=s-\frac43+\frac{2\rho}{3}.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(\rho\ge1/2\), then \(K\ge s-1>0\). With \(G\) defined as above,
\[
G'(s)
=K-\frac29(s-s^{-3})
=\frac{7s+6\rho+2s^{-3}-12}{9}>0.
\]
Indeed, after replacing \(6\rho\) by \(3\), the numerator is
\(7s+2s^{-3}-9\), which vanishes at \(s=1\) and has derivative
\(7-6s^{-4}>0\) for \(s>1\). Since
\[
G(1)\ge p(\rho)-\frac49\ge0,
\]
condition (12) holds.

It remains to consider \(0\le\rho<1/2\). If \(s\le\sqrt2\), then (6) and monotonicity of \(\overline Q\) give
\[
p(\rho)\ge\frac12\ge\overline Q(s)>Q,
\]
so no outer estimate is needed. Suppose \(s>\sqrt2\). Then \(K>0\), and (6) gives
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\ge \frac{5-\rho}{9}
+\frac12\left(s-\frac43+\frac{2\rho}{3}\right)^2.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(\sqrt2<s\le3/2\), completing the square in \(K\) yields
\[
\frac{5-\rho}{9}+\frac{K^2}{2}
=
\frac13+\frac{s}{6}
+\frac12\left(K-\frac16\right)^2-\frac1{72}
\ge\frac{23+12s}{72}.
\]
The difference between this last quantity and \(\overline Q(s)\) is
\[
\frac{-8s^4+12s^3+7s^2-8}{72s^2}.
\]
The numerator is concave on \([\sqrt2,3/2]\), since its second derivative is
\[
-96s^2+72s+14<0.
\]
Its endpoint values are
\[
24\sqrt2-26>0,\qquad \frac{31}{4}>0.
\]
Thus (12) holds on this interval.

For \(3/2\le s<2\), put \(K_0=s-4/3\ge1/6\). The right-hand side of (15) minus
\[
\frac59+\frac{K_0^2}{2}
\]
equals
\[
\rho\left(\frac{2K_0}{3}-\frac19\right)+\frac{2\rho^2}{9}\ge0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac59+\frac12\left(s-\frac43\right)^2-\overline Q(s)
=
\frac{7s^4-24s^3+22s^2-2}{18s^2}.
\]
The numerator has derivative
\[
4s(s-1)(7s-11),
\]
so its minimum on \([3/2,2]\) is attained at \(s=11/7\), where its value is
\[
\frac{645}{343}>0.
\]
This proves (12) in the last chamber.

All nonhorizontal halfspaces through \((1,g)\) therefore have normalized mass at least \(Q\). The two horizontal masses from accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` are \(a^2+1\) and \(1+c^2\). They also exceed \(Q\), since
\[
a^2+1-Q=\frac{7(a^2+1)-2c^2}{9}>0
\]
and
\[
1+c^2-Q=\frac{7(1+c^2)-2a^2}{9}>0
\]
by \(a\ge c\) and \(a^2<1+c^2\). Multiplying by \(D\) proves (2).

Accepted fact `8be4b2011a05cfd2` supplies all hypotheses (1) for every residual pair. The argument is uniform on the complete closed direction range \(0\le\rho\le1\) and does not remove any transition value in the parameter decomposition. Hence every allowed boundary in the exact regions (U1)–(U6) of accepted fact `56bc39a3b6a791db` is included, while their strict residual endpoints remain strict exactly as stated there.
