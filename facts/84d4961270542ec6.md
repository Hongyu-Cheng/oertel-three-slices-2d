## Statement

Use the complete opposite notation of accepted fact `18e8f69f5a782c16`, the full endpoint coordinates of accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b`, and the physical Jacobian convention of accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1`.  Put
\[
A=\alpha+J,\qquad B=\beta+H=\delta-\tau,\qquad
G=\gamma+K,\qquad S=\delta+2,
\]
\[
\chi=\delta-A,\qquad
\psi=S-w_0-K_2.
\]
For a point \(p\in A_0\) and a point \(q\in A_2\), define their two contributions
\[
\zeta_0(p)=(-p_k,-p_\ell),\qquad
\zeta_2(q)=(-q_k,-q_\ell).
\]
The six fixed \(A_0\) chord endpoints have contribution set
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_0=\{&
(\alpha-\delta,-\alpha),\ (-\chi,-A),\\
&(-(H+\tau),0),\ (-\tau,0),\\
&(0,\gamma),\ (0,G)\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
and the six fixed \(A_2\) chord endpoints have contribution set
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_2=\{&
(0,-v),\ (0,-v-J_2),\\
&(u,0),\ (u+H_2,0),\\
&(S-w_0,w_0),\ (\psi,w_0+K_2)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Repeated points on a literal endpoint-incidence branch are retained only once.  Define
\[
D_{\mathrm{ep}}
=\operatorname{conv}(E_0)+\operatorname{conv}(E_2)
=\operatorname{conv}\{a+b:a\in E_0,\ b\in E_2\},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
F_N=D_{\mathrm{ep}}\cap T_N.
\tag{4}
\]
Then every actual compact convex endpoint pair realizing the full chords satisfies
\[
\Xi\left(
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap\overline{R_N}
\right)\supseteq F_N,
\qquad
\Xi(r)=(-2r_k,-2r_\ell).
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \(F_N\) is the largest convex set forced into \(\overline{R_N}\) by these fixed chord endpoints alone.

Here is the exact formula, including every zero, equality, and strict selector branch.  Form the 36 affine points
\[
W=\{a+b:a\in E_0,\ b\in E_2\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Remove duplicates, sort lexicographically, and run the lower and upper monotone-chain scans.  In either scan, for three successive points \(P,Q,R\), delete \(Q\) precisely on the branch
\[
\det(Q-P,R-P)\le0;
\tag{7}
\]
the equality branch is assigned to deletion.  This gives the counterclockwise vertices of \(\operatorname{conv}W\).  Clip that polygon successively by
\[
-x\le0,\qquad -y\le0,\qquad x+y-2\le0.
\tag{8}
\]
For an oriented edge \(PQ\) and one clipping affine form \(h\), an endpoint with \(h=0\) is assigned to the inside branch.  If \(h(P)\le0<h(Q)\), insert
\[
P+\frac{-h(P)}{h(Q)-h(P)}(Q-P);
\tag{9a}
\]
if \(h(P)>0\ge h(Q)\), insert the same intersection followed by \(Q\);
retain both endpoints on the inside-inside branch and none on the
outside-outside branch.  Let the resulting counterclockwise list,
after deleting consecutive duplicates, be
\[
Z_1,\ldots,Z_m,\qquad Z_{m+1}=Z_1.
\]
Then the exact coordinate and physical areas forced into \(R_N\) are
\[
\mathscr A_N
=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m\det(Z_i,Z_{i+1}),
\qquad
|F_N|_{\rm physical}=\frac{\mathscr A_N}{4\Delta}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (6)--(10) are a finite exhaustive branch formula: every
selector is the sign of an explicitly displayed affine or determinant
expression, and every branch value is rational in the accepted
parameters.  In particular, the endpoint datum is impossible for a
prescribed middle budget \(z\) whenever
\[
\mathscr A_N>4\Delta z.
\tag{11}
\]

The selected \(A_0\) diagonal chord and \(A_2\) \(V=0\) chord give
\[
[u,u+H_2]\times[\gamma,G]\subseteq D_{\mathrm{ep}}.
\tag{12}
\]
On the literal branch
\[
u+H_2+G\le2,
\tag{13}
\]
this whole rectangle lies in \(T_N\), so (10) contains the bare
physical area
\[
\frac{KH_2}{4\Delta}.
\tag{14}
\]
If (13) fails, the whole bare parallelogram is not in \(R_N\); in that
branch (6)--(10), rather than (14), is the exact fixed-endpoint
formula.

For an exact nonvacuous calibration, take the actual rational endpoint

## Proof

Accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1` gives
\[
p_j=X,\qquad p_\ell=Y,\qquad
p_k=\delta-X-Y
\]
and
\[
U=-q_k,\qquad V=q_\ell,\qquad
q_j=U-V-S.
\]
For the midpoint \(r=(p+q)/2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
-2r_k&=-p_k-q_k=U-p_k,\\
-2r_\ell&=-p_\ell-q_\ell=-Y-V.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(r_j+r_k+r_\ell=-1\),
\[
2r_j=-2+(-2r_k)+(-2r_\ell).
\tag{21}
\]
Thus \(r_j,r_k,r_\ell\le0\) is exactly
\[
x\ge0,\qquad y\ge0,\qquad x+y\le2,
\]
which proves the claimed image \(T_N\).  The linear map from the
physical middle plane to \((r_k,r_\ell)\) has Jacobian magnitude
\(\Delta\), while \((r_k,r_\ell)\mapsto(x,y)\) has determinant \(4\).
Therefore coordinate area in \((x,y)\) is \(4\Delta\) times physical
area, proving the factor in (10).

Substitution of the six full-chord endpoints from
`fa0e57b225178b3b` into \(\zeta_0,\zeta_2\) gives (1)--(2).
For example, on the selected chords,
\[
\zeta_0(\delta+t,-t)=(0,t),\qquad
\zeta_2(U,0)=(U,0),
\tag{22}
\]
which proves (12).  The maximum of \(x+y\) on (12) is
\(u+H_2+G\), so (13)--(14) follow.

Convexity gives
\[
\operatorname{conv}(E_0)\subseteq\zeta_0(A_0),
\qquad
\operatorname{conv}(E_2)\subseteq\zeta_2(A_2).
\tag{23}
\]
Minkowski addition and (20) therefore give
\[
D_{\mathrm{ep}}
\subseteq
\Xi\left(\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right).
\tag{24}
\]
Intersecting (24) with \(T_N\) proves (5).  Conversely, every point
that can be obtained by convexity from the fixed chord endpoints is a
convex combination of their 36 pairwise sums, hence lies in
\(D_{\mathrm{ep}}\).  Intersecting with the exact sign triangle
\(T_N\) proves the stated maximality among fixed-endpoint-only hulls.
No boundary arc or polygon completion has been used.

For clarity, the chord-pair sums that lie identically on the three
boundary lines of \(T_N\) are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{full-chord pair}&\text{forced interval}\\ \hline
Y=0,\ V=0&
[u-\tau-H,\ u+H_2-\tau]\times\{0\}\\[1mm]
p_k=0,\ U=0&
\{0\}\times[\gamma-v-J_2,\ G-v]\\[1mm]
X=0,\ q_j=0&
\{(x,2-x):
\alpha+2-w_0-K_2\le x\le A+2-w_0\}.
\end{array}
\tag{25}
\]
The selected pair is the rectangle (12).  The five remaining chord
pair types have respectively \(x<0\), \(y<0\), or \(x+y>2\), except
at an endpoint incidence already represented in (25).  Nevertheless,
one must not delete their fixed sums before taking (3): a hull edge
from an outside sum to another fixed sum can meet a side of \(T_N\)
at a new forced vertex.  The rational calculation below exhibits two
such clipping vertices.

It remains to justify that (6)--(10) is an exact formula rather than
a numerical prescription.  The monotone-chain rule (7) is simply the
orientation test for the lower and upper boundaries of a finite
convex hull.  Its weak branch deletes every non-extreme collinear
middle point, so zero and equality strata are literal.  On a strict
crossing branch in (9), the displayed denominator is nonzero and the
coefficient is the unique value in \((0,1)\) at which \(h=0\).
The equality branch is already inside and introduces no division.
After the three clips, the resulting polygon is exactly
\(\operatorname{conv}W\cap T_N=F_N\).  The shoelace identity then
proves (10).  Since there are only 36 initial points, finitely many
orientation signs and finitely many clipping signs occur.  This
proves branch exhaustiveness and the overflow implication (11).

Finally audit the accepted actual rational calibration.  The fact
`06afbd5c9e94d1a2` verifies actual compact convex \(A_0,A_2\) having

This completes both the universal fixed-endpoint formula and its exact
