## Statement

For a two-dimensional compact convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\), let
\[
F_{K,q}(s)=\left|K\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le s\}\right|
\qquad(q\in\mathbb S^1,\ s\in\mathbb R).
\]

1. If two such bodies \(K,L\) satisfy
\[
F_{K,q}(s)=F_{L,q}(s)
\qquad\text{for every }q\in\mathbb S^1,\ s\in\mathbb R,
\tag{1}
\]
then \(K=L\). Consequently, a deformation of a triple
\((A_0,A_1,A_2)\) that preserves every same-index cap profile
\(F_{A_i,q}(s)\) is the identity on all three bodies and cannot turn a
nontriangular slice into a triangle or any other prescribed extremal shape.

2. The feasible class defined by fixed slice areas and
\((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\) is not closed under independent
area-preserving parallel shadow motions. More precisely, with
\[
Q=[0,1]^2,\qquad S_\tau(x,y)=(x+\tau y,y),
\]
the compatible triple \((Q,Q,Q)\) has all slice areas equal to \(1\).
For every \(\tau>0\), the independent motion
\[
(A_0(\tau),A_1(\tau),A_2(\tau))=(S_\tau Q,Q,Q)
\tag{2}
\]
still preserves all three slice areas but violates compatibility. The
common motion
\[
(A_0(\tau),A_1(\tau),A_2(\tau))
=(S_\tau Q,S_\tau Q,S_\tau Q)
\tag{3}
\]
preserves the three areas and compatibility, but it does not preserve fixed
same-index caps: for \(q=(1,0)\) and \(s=1/2\),
\[
F_{Q,q}(1/2)=\frac12,\qquad
F_{S_1Q,q}(1/2)=\frac18.
\tag{4}
\]
If directions and thresholds are transported with the common invertible
linear map, then all cap data are merely relabeled and each slice remains a
quadrilateral.

Therefore no triangle or cone reduction follows from either full-profile
preservation or an ordinary independent/common affine shadow motion. The
first missing hypothesis in a proposed localization proof is the existence
of a nontrivial coordinated deformation that simultaneously

\[
|A_i(\tau)|=|A_i(0)|\quad(i=0,1,2),\qquad
\frac{A_0(\tau)+A_2(\tau)}2\subseteq A_1(\tau),
\tag{5}
\]

preserves the finitely active same-index cap inequalities of the balanced
certificate, and has the required one-sided convexity or concavity of the
certificate objective so that an endpoint is no better than the original
triple. None of these conclusions is implied merely by the existence of
sharp triangle examples.

## Proof

We first prove the rigidity assertion without an external theorem. Write
\(a_K=|K|\) and \(h_K(q)=\max_{x\in K}q\cdot x\). For every
\(q\in\mathbb S^1\),
\[
h_K(q)=\inf\{s\in\mathbb R:F_{K,q}(s)=a_K\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(s\ge h_K(q)\) gives the whole body. If \(s<h_K(q)\), choose
\(x\in K\) with \(q\cdot x>s\) and an interior point \(z\in K\).
Points sufficiently close to \(x\) on the segment from \(x\) to \(z\) are
interior points and still satisfy \(q\cdot x'>s\); a sufficiently small
open ball about one of them is contained in \(K\cap\{q\cdot x>s\}\).
Thus the excluded part has positive area and \(F_{K,q}(s)<a_K\), proving
(6).

Under (1), the limits as \(s\to+\infty\) first give \(a_K=a_L\), and (6)
then gives \(h_K(q)=h_L(q)\) for every \(q\). The elementary support
representation
\[
K=\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}\{x:q\cdot x\le h_K(q)\}
\tag{7}
\]
now gives \(K=L\). For completeness, the inclusion from left to right is
the definition of \(h_K\); if \(x\notin K\), strict separation of the point
from the compact convex set gives a direction \(q\) with
\(q\cdot x>h_K(q)\), proving the reverse inclusion. This proves part 1.

Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` expresses the endpoint depths and the exact
middle depth as infima of the same-index cap profiles, over every direction.
Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` reduces a genuine middle-depth failure to a
three-direction profile certificate, but its active directions and profile
levels are outputs of the original bodies. Thus preserving the full
directional problem invokes the rigidity just proved, while preserving only
the eventual three active constraints requires an additional deformation
theorem and cannot be obtained by silently discarding the other directions.
Accepted fact `23dcab2387927bc4` further rules out repairing this gap by a
single direction-independent fixed allocation: its fixed-allocation regions
are disjoint throughout the full allowed allocation interval although the
true middle depth region is nonempty.

We next verify the shadow-motion obstruction exactly. The determinant of
\(S_\tau\) is \(1\), so \(|S_\tau Q|=|Q|=1\). At \(\tau=0\), all three
bodies are \(Q\), and
\[
\frac{Q+Q}{2}=Q.
\]
Hence accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` verifies compatibility. For
\(\tau>0\), the points
\[
p_0=S_\tau(1,1)=(1+\tau,1)\in S_\tau Q,\qquad
p_2=(1,1)\in Q
\]
have midpoint
\[
\frac{p_0+p_2}{2}=(1+\tau/2,1)\notin Q.
\]
Therefore (2) violates \((A_0(\tau)+A_2(\tau))/2\subseteq A_1(\tau)\)
despite preserving all three areas. In support-function language, the same
failure is already the strict violation in direction \((1,0)\):
\[
h_{S_\tau Q}(1,0)+h_Q(1,0)=2+\tau>2=2h_Q(1,0).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus a one-body localization or shadow theorem cannot be applied
slice-by-slice unless one first proves that its motions satisfy the
infinitely many coupled support inequalities (5).

For the common motion (3), linearity gives
\[
\frac{S_\tau Q+S_\tau Q}{2}
=S_\tau\!\left(\frac{Q+Q}{2}\right)=S_\tau Q,
\]
so compatibility and area are preserved. At \(\tau=0\), the cap in (4) is
the rectangle \([0,1/2]\times[0,1]\), of area \(1/2\). At \(\tau=1\),
using the determinant-one coordinates \((X,Y)=S_1(x,y)=(x+y,y)\), the
same fixed cap pulls back to
\[
\{(x,y)\in[0,1]^2:x+y\le1/2\},
\]
a right triangle of area \((1/2)(1/2)(1/2)=1/8\). This proves (4).

More generally,
\[
F_{S_\tau K,q}(s)
=\left|K\cap\{x:(S_\tau^{\mathsf T}q)\cdot x\le s\}\right|.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus transporting \(q\) contragrediently preserves the cap only by
relabeling it. Since \(S_\tau\) is invertible, it maps extreme points
bijectively to extreme points: any nontrivial convex decomposition of an
image point pulls back to one of the original point, and conversely.
Accordingly, the common motion sends the four vertices of \(Q\) to four
vertices and cannot produce a triangle endpoint.

Accepted fact `43e70f89f224c5d7` proves that a compatible homothetic triangle
family is sharp in the limiting sense. It supplies no deformation satisfying
(5), no preservation of the active cap terms, and no endpoint convexity
statement. The exact calculations above show why sharpness alone cannot be
promoted to a localization theorem. They do not rule out a new,
problem-specific coordinated extremal theorem; they identify its first
currently unproved hypothesis precisely.
