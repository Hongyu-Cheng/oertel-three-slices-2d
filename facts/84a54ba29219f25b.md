## Statement

Use the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  Let \(N=\{j,k,\ell\}\), let \(f_j,f_k,f_\ell\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_j+f_k+f_\ell=-1,
\qquad
L_j+L_k+L_\ell<-1.
\]
Put
\[
\delta=-1-(L_j+L_k+L_\ell)>0,
\qquad
p_i=f_i-L_i,
\qquad
q_i=f_i+L_i.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\) be two-dimensional convex polygons satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Define
\[
P_{\{\ell\}}
=A_0\cap\{p_\ell\le0,\ p_j>0,\ p_k>0\}
\]
and
\[
S=P_{\{\ell\}}\cap\{p_j<\delta+2\}.
\]
If there is a point \(y_*\in A_2\) such that
\[
q_k(y_*)=q_\ell(y_*)=0,
\]
then
\[
\frac{S+y_*}{2}\subseteq R_{\{j,\ell\}}
\quad\hbox{and hence}\quad
r_{\{j,\ell\}}:=|R_{\{j,\ell\}}|
\ge\frac{|S|}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(p_k=p_\ell=0\) has a point in the closure of the two-dimensional interior of \(P_{\{\ell\}}\), then \(|S|>0\).  Consequently no compatible \(K\) can have the exact central middle support
\[
\{G:r_G>0\}
\subseteq
\bigl\{\{j\},\{j,k\},N,\{k,\ell\}\bigr\}.
\tag{2}
\]

The obstruction in (1) is active even after all three local one-sided density tests have the genuine-lower-crossing orientation.  Explicitly, in coordinates \((x,y)\), set
\[
f_k=x,\qquad f_\ell=y,\qquad f_j=-1-x-y,
\]
\[
L_k=L_\ell=0,\qquad L_j=-2.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\delta=1\).  For \(h>0\) and \(D>h\), put
\[
T(h,D)=
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),
\left(\frac{h+D}{2},\frac{h-D}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{h-D}{2},\frac{h+D}{2}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(A_0\) be the image of \(T(1/2,24)\) under
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto(x,y)=(s,-t),
\]
and let \(A_2\) be the image of \(T(3/5,20)\) under
\[
(S,T)\longmapsto(x,y)=(-S,T).
\tag{5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),
\left(\frac{49}{4},\frac{47}{4}\right),
\left(-\frac{47}{4},-\frac{49}{4}\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),
\left(-\frac{103}{10},-\frac{97}{10}\right),
\left(\frac{97}{10},\frac{103}{10}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Both polygons have area \(6\).  Their complete positive-area endpoint paths are
\[
P_{\{j\}},\ P_\varnothing,\ P_{\{\ell\}},\ P_{\{k,\ell\}}
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
Q_{\{j\}},\ Q_{\{j,k\}},\ Q_N,\ Q_{\{k,\ell\}},
\tag{8}
\]
respectively.  All eight cells in (7)-(8) have positive area.

At the three nominal levels \(p_i=0\) and \(q_i=0\), the one-sided chord-density differences from accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc` are
\[
\rho_{A_0,k}^{+}(0)-\rho_{A_2,k}^{-}(0)
=-\frac1{10},
\qquad
\rho_{A_0,\ell}^{+}(0)-\rho_{A_2,\ell}^{-}(0)
=-\frac1{10},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\rho_{A_0,j}^{+}(0)-\rho_{A_2,j}^{-}(0)
=-\frac{37}{2400}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus every pair is lexicographically negative already in its first entry, exactly as required at a genuine lower crossing in accepted facts `624b129b68e0fdee` and `eddda25c734d34bc`.  Nevertheless, every convex polygon \(K\) satisfying midpoint containment for (6) has
\[
|R_{\{j,\ell\}}|\ge\frac1{32},
\tag{11}
\]
so it cannot retain the exact central middle path (2), and the three central-path crossing equations cannot be solved on that support.

## Proof

The affine-coordinate sums are
\[
p_j+p_k+p_\ell=\delta,
\qquad
q_j+q_k+q_\ell=-\delta-2.
\tag{12}
\]
At the assumed point \(y_*\), (12) gives
\[
q_j(y_*)=-\delta-2.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(x\in S\), put \(z=(x+y_*)/2\).  Affinity gives the midpoint identities from accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`,
\[
2f_i(z)=p_i(x)+q_i(y_*)\qquad(i=j,k,\ell).
\tag{14}
\]
The definition of \(P_{\{\ell\}}\) and (13)-(14) yield
\[
2f_k(z)=p_k(x)>0,
\]
\[
2f_\ell(z)=p_\ell(x)\le0,
\]
\[
2f_j(z)=p_j(x)-\delta-2<0.
\]
Therefore \(z\in R_{\{j,\ell\}}\).  Midpoint containment puts every such \(z\) in \(K\), and the map
\[
x\longmapsto\frac{x+y_*}{2}
\]
scales planar area by \(1/4\).  This proves (1).

Suppose now that \(x_*\) lies in the closure of the two-dimensional interior of \(P_{\{\ell\}}\) and satisfies \(p_k(x_*)=p_\ell(x_*)=0\).  Equation (12) gives
\[
p_j(x_*)=\delta<\delta+2.
\]
By continuity, \(p_j<\delta+2\) on a neighborhood of \(x_*\).  That neighborhood meets the interior of \(P_{\{\ell\}}\) in positive area, so \(|S|>0\).  Then (1) gives \(r_{\{j,\ell\}}>0\), contradicting (2).  This proves the general common-breakpoint obstruction.

It remains to verify the exact rational calibration (3)-(11).  In the \((s,t)\)-coordinates of \(A_0\),
\[
p_k=s,\qquad p_\ell=-t,\qquad p_j=1-s+t.
\tag{15}
\]
In the \((S,T)\)-coordinates of \(A_2\),
\[
q_k=-S,\qquad q_\ell=T,\qquad q_j=-3+S-T.
\tag{16}
\]
The coordinate changes in (5) have absolute determinant \(1\).

Write
\[
w=u+v,\qquad d=u-v.
\]
The triangle \(T(h,D)\) has the exact description
\[
0\le w\le h,\qquad |d|\le\frac{D}{h}w.
\tag{17}
\]
Its area is
\[
|T(h,D)|=\frac{hD}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (18) and (5) give
\[
|A_0|=\frac{(1/2)24}{2}=6,
\qquad
|A_2|=\frac{(3/5)20}{2}=6.
\tag{19}
\]

Because \(w\ge0\), the triangle meets only the first, second, and fourth coordinate quadrants.  For \(A_0\), the first quadrant \(s,t>0\) is \(P_{\{\ell\}}\).  In the fourth quadrant \(s>0,t<0\), the line
\[
p_j=0\iff s-t=1
\]
separates positive-area \(P_\varnothing\) and \(P_{\{j\}}\) pieces because \(D=24>1\).  The second quadrant is \(P_{\{k,\ell\}}\).  This proves (7).  For \(A_2\), the first quadrant is \(Q_{\{j,k\}}\), the second is \(Q_{\{j\}}\), and in the fourth quadrant the line
\[
q_j=0\iff S-T=3
\]
separates positive-area \(Q_N\) and \(Q_{\{k,\ell\}}\) pieces because \(D=20>3\).  This proves (8).

The section of \(T(h,D)\) on either coordinate axis has coordinate length \(h\).  Taking the one-sided traces specified in accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc` therefore gives
\[
\rho_{A_0,k}^{+}(0)=\rho_{A_0,\ell}^{+}(0)=\frac12,
\]
\[
\rho_{A_2,k}^{-}(0)=\rho_{A_2,\ell}^{-}(0)=\frac35,
\]
which proves (9).  These profiles are nonconstant: their chord endpoints lie on fixed triangle edges, so the chord lengths vary affinely on each side of the displayed level.

For completeness, the \(j\)-direction densities can be computed without a metric convention.  Under the change \((u,v)\mapsto(w,d)\),
\[
du\,dv=\frac12\,dw\,dd.
\]
At a level \(d=z\) with \(0<z<D\), (17) gives
\[
\frac{hz}{D}\le w\le h.
\]
Hence the derivative of coordinate area with respect to the level \(d\) is
\[
\frac{h(D-z)}{2D}.
\tag{20}
\]
For \(A_0\), the level \(p_j=0\) is \(d=1\), while for \(A_2\), the level \(q_j=0\) is \(d=3\).  Formula (20) gives
\[
\rho_{A_0,j}^{+}(0)
=\frac{(1/2)(24-1)}{2\cdot24}
=\frac{23}{96},
\]
\[
\rho_{A_2,j}^{-}(0)
=\frac{(3/5)(20-3)}{2\cdot20}
=\frac{51}{200}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
\frac{23}{96}-\frac{51}{200}
=-\frac{37}{2400},
\]
proving (10).  Thus all three first density entries are strictly negative, so no slope tie-break is needed.

Finally, \(y_*=(0,0)\) is the common \(q_k=q_\ell=0\) vertex of \(A_2\).  The cell \(P_{\{\ell\}}\) is exactly
\[
\{(s,t):s>0,\ t>0,\ s+t<1/2\}
\]
up to its cutting-line boundaries, and therefore
\[
|P_{\{\ell\}}|=\frac12\left(\frac12\right)^2=\frac18.
\tag{21}
\]
For every point in its interior, (15) gives \(p_j<3=\delta+2\), so the whole cell contributes to \(S\) up to a null boundary.  Applying (1) to (21) gives
\[
r_{\{j,\ell\}}\ge\frac14\cdot\frac18=\frac1{32}.
\]
This is (11).  It is a midpoint-containment obstruction, not a relaxed scalar-cell contradiction: the forbidden positive-area cell is the explicit homothetic copy
\[
\frac{P_{\{\ell\}}+y_*}{2}
\subseteq
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Thus the common-breakpoint triangular escape passes all three local density orientations but fails before the exact central crossing system can be imposed.
