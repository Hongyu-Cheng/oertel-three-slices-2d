## Statement

Use all hypotheses and notation of accepted fact `05153059dfd604c8`.
In particular,
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad \kappa=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
the positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\{123,1,2,12\},
\]
and \(p=|P_\varnothing|>0\).  Assume also the canonical no-half
inequality
\[
\kappa<M+m.
\tag{1}
\]

For a line direction \(L\), let
\[
P_L=\max_x\mathcal H^1(A_0\cap(x+L)),\qquad
Q_L=\max_x\mathcal H^1(A_2\cap(x+L)).
\tag{2}
\]
Both numbers are positive.  Put
\[
t=\frac mM,\qquad r_L=\frac{P_L}{Q_L},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
r_\pm(t)=
\frac{3(1+t)\pm\sqrt{9(1+t)^2-4t}}{2t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then every compatible canonical configuration satisfying (1) obeys
\[
\boxed{\quad r_-(t)<r_L<r_+(t)\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
for every line direction \(L\).  In particular, (5) holds in each of
the three crossing-line directions.  Accepted fact
`05153059dfd604c8` further restricts the area parameter to
\[
0<t<
\frac{50}{93+12\sqrt{21}}.
\tag{6}
\]

There is also a retained-body closing region.  Put
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad
C=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2}),\qquad
\sigma=|C|,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
H_{12}=\frac{P+C}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) agrees up to a null boundary with the hull used in
accepted facts `d3afd91c459bcce8` and `f1fcc678305bbd1c`.
Let
\[
\Gamma=|H_{12}\cap R_{12}|.
\tag{9}
\]
Use the notation of accepted fact `f1fcc678305bbd1c`:
\[
\Delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3),\qquad
\alpha=\frac p\Theta,
\]
\[
\Lambda=\max\{A,B\},\qquad \lambda=\min\{A,B\},
\]
and set
\[
G=
\frac{p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda\left(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha}\right)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the accepted bridge facts give
\[
\Gamma>G>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Define the two row allowances
\[
U_i=\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_i-v_i
\quad(i=1,2).
\tag{12}
\]

For a line direction \(L\), let
\[
\pi_L=\max_x\mathcal H^1(P\cap(x+L)),\qquad
\chi_L=\max_x\mathcal H^1(C\cap(x+L)).
\tag{13}
\]
If for some \(L\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac14(\pi_L+\chi_L)
\left(\frac p{\pi_L}+\frac{\sigma}{\chi_L}\right)
+G
\geq U_1+U_2,
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
then no compatible canonical configuration satisfying (1) exists.
More precisely, (14) forces
\[
|H_{12}\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|\geq U_i
\tag{15}
\]
for at least one \(i\in\{1,2\}\).  By accepted fact
`cb3cba490b10885c`, the left side of (15) is exactly that row's
two-rail integral.

The complement of (14) has an exact chord-ratio description.  Set
\[
x=\frac{\sigma}{p},\qquad
S=U_1+U_2-G.
\tag{16}
\]
Every surviving configuration must have
\[
S>\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt\sigma)^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Put
\[
D=\frac{4S}{p}-(1+x).
\tag{18}
\]
Then \(D>2\sqrt x\), and for every line direction \(L\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\rho_-<\frac{\pi_L}{\chi_L}<\rho_+,\qquad
\rho_\pm=
\frac{D\pm\sqrt{D^2-4x}}{2x}.
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
Thus failure of (17), or a retained maximum-chord ratio outside the
interval in (19), is a nonempty canonical closing region.

## Proof

We first prove the exact planar maximal-section inequality used in
both parts.  Let \(E,F\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies, fix a
line direction \(L\), and choose a transverse coordinate.  Write
\(I_E,I_F\) for the compact transverse projection intervals and write
\(e(a)\), \(a\in I_E\), and \(f(b)\), \(b\in I_F\), for the lengths
of the \(L\)-parallel sections of \(E\) and \(F\).  Put
\[
P_*=\max_a e(a),\qquad Q_*=\max_b f(b).
\]
For \(0\leq z\leq P_*\), define
\[
e_-(z)=\min\{a:e(a)\geq z\},\qquad
e_+(z)=\max\{a:e(a)\geq z\},
\tag{20}
\]
and define \(f_\pm\) analogously.  Convexity makes every nonempty
section an interval and every section-length superlevel set an
interval.  The layer-cake identity therefore gives
\[
|E|=\int_0^{P_*}\bigl(e_+(z)-e_-(z)\bigr)\,dz,
\tag{21}
\]
\[
|F|=\int_0^{Q_*}\bigl(f_+(z)-f_-(z)\bigr)\,dz.
\tag{22}
\]

Fix \(a,b>0\) and put \(Z=aE+bF\).  At transverse coordinate
\[
a e_+(P_*\theta)+b f_+(Q_*\theta),
\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq1,
\]
the corresponding \(L\)-section of \(Z\) contains the Minkowski sum
of two intervals of lengths at least \(P_*\theta\) and
\(Q_*\theta\).  Its length is therefore at least
\[
(aP_*+bQ_*)\theta.
\]
If \(z_\pm\) are the transverse endpoints of the corresponding
section-length superlevels of \(Z\), the analogous lower-endpoint
assertion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&z_+\bigl((aP_*+bQ_*)\theta\bigr)
-z_-\bigl((aP_*+bQ_*)\theta\bigr)\\
&\qquad\geq
a\bigl(e_+(P_*\theta)-e_-(P_*\theta)\bigr)
+b\bigl(f_+(Q_*\theta)-f_-(Q_*\theta)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying (21) to \(Z\), integrating only up to
\(aP_*+bQ_*\), and then using (21)-(22), yields
\[
\boxed{\quad
|aE+bF|
\geq
(aP_*+bQ_*)
\left(a\frac{|E|}{P_*}+b\frac{|F|}{Q_*}\right).
\quad}
\tag{23}
\]
Flat maximal sections and zero-length support sections cause no
problem in (20)-(23): the endpoint levels have zero measure in the
layer-cake integrals.  This is Bonnesen's planar
maximal-section inequality.

Apply (23) to \(E=A_0,F=A_2\) and \(a=b=1/2\).  For every \(L\),
\[
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right|
\geq
\frac14(P_L+Q_L)
\left(\frac M{P_L}+\frac m{Q_L}\right).
\tag{24}
\]
Compatibility and (1) imply that the left side of (24) is strictly
less than \(M+m\).  Consequently,
\[
\frac14(P_L+Q_L)
\left(\frac M{P_L}+\frac m{Q_L}\right)<M+m.
\tag{25}
\]
Substitute \(m=tM\) and \(P_L=r_LQ_L\).  After division by \(M/4\),
(25) is
\[
1+t+\frac1{r_L}+t r_L<4(1+t),
\]
or equivalently
\[
t r_L^2-3(1+t)r_L+1<0.
\tag{26}
\]
The discriminant is \(9(1+t)^2-4t>0\), and the leading coefficient
is positive.  The two roots of (26) are exactly (4), proving (5).
This also fixes the ratio orientation: (4) is the interval for
\(P_L/Q_L\).  The interval for its reciprocal has reciprocal roots
and must not be written with the same parameter without conversion.

Accepted fact `05153059dfd604c8` gives
\[
\frac p m>
\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}.
\]
Since \(p\leq M\), this implies (6).

We next prove the retained-body gate.  Convexity of \(P\) gives the
exact compact-set identity
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left(
\frac{P+\overline{Q_1}}2
\cup
\frac{P+\overline{Q_2}}2
\right)
=
\frac{P+\operatorname{conv}(
\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2})}{2}
=H_{12}.
\tag{27}
\]
Replacing \(P_\varnothing\) by \(P\) changes only the boundary of the
set in (27), hence changes none of its planar cell areas.

Up to the three null cutting lines, \(H_{12}\) has only the middle
cells \(R_1,R_2,R_{12}\).  Indeed, the first singleton midpoint body
lies in \(R_1\), the second lies in \(R_2\), and \(f_3\geq0\)
throughout their convex hull.  Since
\(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), the two coordinates \(f_1,f_2\) cannot both be
positive there.  Therefore, with
\[
A_i=|H_{12}\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,
\]
the cell multiplicities on \(R_1,R_2,R_{12}\) give
\[
A_1+A_2=|H_{12}|+\Gamma.
\tag{28}
\]
Accepted facts `d3afd91c459bcce8` and
`f1fcc678305bbd1c` give (11).

Apply (23) to \(E=P,F=C\) and \(a=b=1/2\).  For every \(L\),
\[
|H_{12}|
\geq
\frac14(\pi_L+\chi_L)
\left(\frac p{\pi_L}+\frac{\sigma}{\chi_L}\right).
\tag{29}
\]
If (14) holds, (11), (28), and (29) give
\[
A_1+A_2>U_1+U_2.
\tag{30}
\]
Hence \(A_i\geq U_i\) for at least one \(i\in\{1,2\}\), proving
(15).  On the other hand, compatibility gives \(H_{12}\subseteq K\),
so \(A_i\leq c_i\).  The crossing and (1) give
\[
c_i=
\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9-u_i-v_i
<
\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_i-v_i
=U_i.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (15) and (31) contradict each other.  Accepted fact
`cb3cba490b10885c` identifies each \(A_i\) with its exact two-rail
integral, so this is precisely a two-rail row closure.

It remains to derive the exact hard interval (19).  In a surviving
configuration, (14) fails for every \(L\).  Write
\[
r=\frac{\pi_L}{\chi_L}.
\]
Equation (29) becomes
\[
|H_{12}|
\geq
\frac p4\left(1+x+\frac1r+xr\right).
\tag{32}
\]
Since (28), (11), and \(A_i<U_i\) imply
\[
|H_{12}|+G<U_1+U_2,
\]
(32) gives
\[
\frac p4\left(1+x+\frac1r+xr\right)<S.
\tag{33}
\]
The minimum over \(r>0\) of \(r^{-1}+xr\) is \(2\sqrt x\).
Thus (33) first forces (17).  With \(D\) as in (18), multiplying
(33) by \(4r/p>0\) gives
\[
xr^2-Dr+1<0.
\tag{34}
\]
A negative value in (34) is possible only if \(D>2\sqrt x\), and
then it occurs exactly between the two roots displayed in (19).
This proves the retained-chord reduction and completes the candidate.
