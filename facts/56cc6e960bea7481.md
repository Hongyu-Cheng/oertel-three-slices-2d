## Statement

Assume the exact \(N=3\) barycentric system of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` in the surviving common-plane normal form of accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `88133f67065def5c`. Thus
\[
P_0\subset\{\mathbf1^\top x=1+q\},\qquad
P_1\subset\{\mathbf1^\top x=1\},\qquad
P_2\subset\{\mathbf1^\top x=1-q\},
\]
are nondegenerate triangles,
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,\qquad
|P_0|=m,\quad |P_1|=1,\quad |P_2|=M,
\]
where \(q>1\), \(0<m\le M\), and \(M<1+m\). Let \(c_j\) be the centroid of \(P_j\), put
\[
\bar c=\frac{c_0+c_2}{2},
\]
and define the exact coordinate-cap areas
\[
u_i=|P_0\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|,\qquad
b_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|,\qquad
v_i=|P_2\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|.
\]
If, for some \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
(c_1)_i\ge0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bar c_i\ge0,
\]
then
\[
b_i+u_i+v_i
\ge \frac{4(1+m)}9
>
\frac{2(1+m+M)}9.
\]
Consequently all three exact rows cannot be strict. Therefore every strict survivor must satisfy
\[
\boxed{\ (c_1)_i<0\ \text{or}\ \bar c_i<0
\quad(i=1,2,3).\ }
\]
Since \(\mathbf1^\top c_1=\mathbf1^\top\bar c=1\), the two nonnegative-coordinate supports are nonempty and disjoint; their possible cardinality pairs are only \((1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\).

## Proof

We first record the triangle centroid-cap estimate, including repeated vertex values. Let \(h\) be a nonconstant affine function on a triangle, let \(\nu\) be normalized area measure, and suppose that \(h\) is nonnegative at the centroid. Sort the three vertex values and affinely normalize them to
\[
0,\qquad s,\qquad 1,\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
The normalized value at the centroid is \((1+s)/3\). The exact cap formulas of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` give, at that centroid level,
\[
\nu\!\left\{h\ge h(\operatorname{centroid})\right\}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(2-s)^2}{9(1-s)},&0\le s\le1/2,\\[2mm]
1-\dfrac{(1+s)^2}{9s},&1/2\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
The first expression is at least \(4/9\) because
\((2-s)^2-4(1-s)=s^2\). The second is at least \(4/9\) because
\[
3s-1-s^2\ge0\qquad(1/2\le s\le1).
\]
These formulas include \(s=0\) and \(s=1\), so both repeated-value strata are covered. Since \(h(\operatorname{centroid})\ge0\),
\[
\nu\{h\ge0\}\ge\frac49.
\tag{1}
\]
For nonconstant \(h\), its zero level has area zero, so the same bound holds for \(\nu\{h>0\}\). A constant positive function has cap fraction one.

Apply (1) to the coordinate function \(x_i\) on \(P_1\). This coordinate function is nonconstant: otherwise the nondegenerate triangle \(P_1\) would lie in the intersection of the two distinct planes \(\mathbf1^\top x=1\) and \(x_i=\text{constant}\), which is a line. The hypothesis \((c_1)_i\ge0\) therefore gives
\[
b_i\ge\frac49.
\tag{2}
\]

It remains to lower-bound the two endpoint caps with one common coupling, rather than separate midpoint images. Put
\[
e=\frac13(1,1,1),\qquad
A_{\mathrm L}=P_2+qe,\qquad
A_{\mathrm s}=P_0-qe.
\]
Both translated endpoint triangles lie in \(\{\mathbf1^\top x=1\}\), have areas \(M\) and \(m\), and satisfy
\[
\frac{A_{\mathrm L}+A_{\mathrm s}}2
=\frac{P_2+P_0}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\tag{3}
\]
Identify this affine plane with \(\mathbb R^2\), and use the affine function
\[
f_i(x)=-x_i.
\]
In the notation of accepted fact `f347fefedf08f4e2`, let
\[
E_i=
\left\{(y,x)\in A_{\mathrm L}\times A_{\mathrm s}:
\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)_i>0\right\}
\]
and let \(\tau_i\) be its minimum measurable vertex-cover cost. Accepted fact `f347fefedf08f4e2` gives the exact common-coupling duality
\[
\tau_i=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_m}\pi(E_i),
\tag{4}
\]
where the large-endpoint marginal is bounded by area measure on \(A_{\mathrm L}\) and the small-endpoint marginal equals area measure on \(A_{\mathrm s}\).

The actual two endpoint coordinate caps form one admissible vertex cover of \(E_i\). Indeed, set
\[
U_i=A_{\mathrm L}\cap\{y_i\ge q/3\},\qquad
V_i=A_{\mathrm s}\cap\{x_i\ge-q/3\}.
\]
If \(y\notin U_i\) and \(x\notin V_i\), then
\[
y_i<q/3,\qquad x_i<-q/3,
\]
so \(((x+y)/2)_i<0\). Hence
\[
E_i\subseteq
(U_i\times A_{\mathrm s})\cup(A_{\mathrm L}\times V_i).
\]
Translation shows \(|U_i|=v_i\) and \(|V_i|=u_i\). Therefore
\[
\tau_i\le u_i+v_i.
\tag{5}
\]

We now construct a coupling in (4). Let \(\Delta\) have normalized area measure \(\nu\). Choose affine bijections
\[
\phi_{\mathrm L}:\Delta\to A_{\mathrm L},\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm s}:\Delta\to A_{\mathrm s},
\]
and define
\[
\pi=m\,(\phi_{\mathrm L},\phi_{\mathrm s})_\#\nu.
\tag{6}
\]
Its first marginal is \((m/M)\) times area measure on \(A_{\mathrm L}\), and its second marginal is area measure on \(A_{\mathrm s}\). Thus \(\pi\in\Pi_m\).

The vertex orderings in the two affine bijections can be chosen so that
\[
h(\lambda)=
\left(\frac{\phi_{\mathrm L}(\lambda)+
\phi_{\mathrm s}(\lambda)}2\right)_i
\tag{7}
\]
is nonconstant. To see this, the \(i\)-th coordinate values
\(a_1,a_2,a_3\) at the vertices of \(A_{\mathrm L}\) are not all equal: otherwise this nondegenerate triangle would lie in the line cut out by \(\mathbf1^\top x=1\) and one constant coordinate. Relabel until \(a_1\ne a_2\). For any first ordering \(b_1,b_2,b_3\) of the small-endpoint values, either
\[
a_1+b_1\ne a_2+b_2,
\]
or equality holds. In the equality case, swapping \(b_1,b_2\) changes the difference to
\[
(a_1+b_2)-(a_2+b_1)=2(a_1-a_2)\ne0.
\]
Thus (7) can always be made nonconstant, including the possible rank-one or point degeneration of the paired midpoint image.

The average of \(h\) over \(\Delta\) is its value at the centroid, and by affine invariance of centroids it equals
\[
\int_\Delta h\,d\nu
=
\frac12\left(c_2+qe+c_0-qe\right)_i
=\bar c_i\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (1) on the reference triangle to the nonconstant function \(h\), and using zero area of its zero level, gives
\[
\pi(E_i)
=m\,\nu\{h>0\}
\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (4), (5), and (9) yield
\[
u_i+v_i\ge\tau_i\ge\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (2) and (10),
\[
b_i+u_i+v_i\ge\frac{4(1+m)}9.
\]
The strict residual area inequality \(M<1+m\), recorded in accepted fact `88133f67065def5c`, gives
\[
\frac{4(1+m)}9
>
\frac{2(1+m+M)}9.
\]
This is the exact row threshold from accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `64760c76cadff481`, so row \(i\) cannot be strict.

Finally,
\[
\mathbf1^\top\bar c
=\frac{(1+q)+(1-q)}2=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathbf1^\top c_1=1.
\]
Each centroid therefore has a strictly positive coordinate. The row exclusion just proved says their nonnegative-coordinate supports are disjoint. Two nonempty disjoint subsets of a three-element set have only the stated cardinality pairs. If \(\bar c=c_1\), any positive coordinate is shared, so that entire common-centroid branch is excluded.
