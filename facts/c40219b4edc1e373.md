## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and assume the exact residual area domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
For \(j=1,2,3\), put
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|,
\]
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j+|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|
       +|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|,
\qquad
I_j=\{a\in\mathbb R:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\]

Fix \(q\) such that
\[
c_q+m<t,
\tag{1}
\]
and write \(\{i,\ell,q\}=\{1,2,3\}\). Define
\[
\Delta=\left|\det(\nabla f_i,\nabla f_\ell)\right|>0,
\qquad
D_0=3t-k-m=\frac{2M-m-k}{3},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\alpha_0=\min_{A_0}f_q,
\qquad
\alpha_2=\min_{A_2}f_q.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(0<D_0<M/3\). For every \(a_i\in I_i\) and \(a_\ell\in I_\ell\),
\[
a_i+a_\ell
<
-1-\alpha_0-\sqrt{3\Delta t}.
\tag{4}
\]
If in addition \(D_0\le m\), then every such pair also satisfies
\[
a_i+a_\ell
>
1+\alpha_2+\sqrt{2\Delta(m-D_0)}.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
D_0\le m
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
1+\alpha_2+\sqrt{2\Delta(m-D_0)}
\ge
-1-\alpha_0-\sqrt{3\Delta t},
\tag{7}
\]
then
\[
I_i=\varnothing
\quad\text{or}\quad
I_\ell=\varnothing.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the tail-exclusion implication holds throughout the support-gap chamber (6)-(7). Equivalently, since affinity gives
\[
\min_{(A_0+A_2)/2}f_q=\frac{\alpha_0+\alpha_2}{2},
\]
condition (7) says
\[
\min_{(A_0+A_2)/2}f_q
\ge
-1-\frac12\left(
\sqrt{3\Delta t}
+\sqrt{2\Delta(m-D_0)}
\right).
\tag{9}
\]

This chamber is nonempty even after requiring every middle cap area to be strictly below \(t\). Namely, let
\[
A_0=K=\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2,
\qquad
A_2=\left[-\frac3{10},\frac3{10}\right]^2,
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
f_1(x,y)=x+\frac25,
\]
\[
f_2(x,y)=35y-\frac x2-\frac7{10},
\qquad
f_3(x,y)=-35y-\frac x2-\frac7{10}.
\tag{11}
\]
With \(q=1\), all hypotheses, (1), and (6)-(7) hold, while
\[
c_1=\frac1{10}<t,
\qquad
c_2=c_3=\frac{13}{25}<t.
\tag{12}
\]
Hence this is not a chamber in which (8) already follows from some \(c_j\ge t\).

## Proof

The residual strip in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` contains the strict inequalities
\[
M-m<k<2M-m.
\tag{13}
\]
The second one and (2) give \(D_0>0\), while the first gives
\[
D_0=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}<\frac M3.
\tag{14}
\]

Assume \(a_i\in I_i\) and \(a_\ell\in I_\ell\). Put
\[
u_j=|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a_j\}|,
\qquad
v_j=|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a_j\}|
\quad(j=i,\ell).
\tag{15}
\]
The strict-low conditions give
\[
u_i+v_i<t-c_i,
\qquad
u_\ell+v_\ell<t-c_\ell.
\tag{16}
\]
The three closed middle caps cover \(K\), because no point can satisfy all three strict inequalities \(f_1>0,f_2>0,f_3>0\) when their sum is \(-1\). Therefore
\[
c_q+c_i+c_\ell\ge k.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (1), (16), and (17) gives the strict common budget
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_i+u_\ell+v_i+v_\ell
&<2t-c_i-c_\ell\\
&\le2t-k+c_q\\
&<3t-k-m
=D_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
This argument permits every individual cap in (15) to be empty or full. The strictness comes only from membership in \(I_i,I_\ell\) and from (1).

We first use \(A_0\). Up to the two null cutting lines, the complement in \(A_0\) of the union of the two lower caps in (15) is
\[
P=A_0\cap\{f_i>a_i,\ f_\ell>a_\ell\}.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(x\in P\), set
\[
r_i=f_i(x)-a_i>0,
\qquad
r_\ell=f_\ell(x)-a_\ell>0.
\]
The affine identity and \(f_q(x)\ge\alpha_0\) give
\[
r_i+r_\ell
=-1-f_q(x)-a_i-a_\ell
\le -1-\alpha_0-a_i-a_\ell.
\tag{20}
\]
The affine coordinate map
\[
x\longmapsto(r_i,r_\ell)
\]
has absolute Jacobian \(\Delta\). Thus its image of \(P\) is contained in the positive quadrant below the line whose intercept is
\[
E_0=-1-\alpha_0-a_i-a_\ell.
\]
That comparison triangle is empty when \(E_0\le0\), and otherwise has coordinate area \(E_0^2/2\). Hence, with \(E_{0,+}=\max\{E_0,0\}\),
\[
|P|\le\frac{E_{0,+}^2}{2\Delta}.
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently
\[
u_i+u_\ell
\ge
\left|
\bigl(A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i\}\bigr)
\cup
\bigl(A_0\cap\{f_\ell\le a_\ell\}\bigr)
\right|
\ge
M-\frac{E_{0,+}^2}{2\Delta}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (18) and (22) imply
\[
\frac{E_{0,+}^2}{2\Delta}>M-D_0.
\tag{23}
\]
But
\[
M-D_0
=\frac{M+m+k}{3}
=\frac{3t}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus
\[
E_{0,+}^2>3\Delta t.
\]
In particular \(E_0>0\), and taking the positive square root gives exactly (4). The case \(P=\varnothing\) is also covered: then the two \(A_0\)-caps cover \(A_0\), so \(u_i+u_\ell\ge M>D_0\), directly contradicting (18).

Now suppose \(D_0\le m\), and apply the same quadrant argument to \(A_2\). Up to null cutting lines, the complement of its two caps in (15) is
\[
Q=A_2\cap\{f_i>-a_i,\ f_\ell>-a_\ell\}.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(x\in Q\), the positive affine coordinates
\[
s_i=f_i(x)+a_i,
\qquad
s_\ell=f_\ell(x)+a_\ell
\]
satisfy
\[
s_i+s_\ell
=-1-f_q(x)+a_i+a_\ell
\le -1-\alpha_2+a_i+a_\ell.
\tag{26}
\]
Writing
\[
E_2=-1-\alpha_2+a_i+a_\ell,
\qquad
E_{2,+}=\max\{E_2,0\},
\]
the same Jacobian calculation gives
\[
|Q|\le\frac{E_{2,+}^2}{2\Delta}
\tag{27}
\]
and therefore
\[
v_i+v_\ell
\ge m-\frac{E_{2,+}^2}{2\Delta}.
\tag{28}
\]
Together with (18),
\[
\frac{E_{2,+}^2}{2\Delta}>m-D_0.
\tag{29}
\]
If \(D_0<m\), (29) gives
\[
E_2>\sqrt{2\Delta(m-D_0)}.
\]
If \(D_0=m\), (29) still gives \(E_{2,+}>0\), hence \(E_2>0\), which is the same strict inequality with zero square root. Rearranging proves (5), including the equality boundary \(D_0=m\).

If both \(I_i\) and \(I_\ell\) were nonempty under (6), choose one point from each. Equations (4) and (5) would put their sum strictly between the two endpoints in (7). A non-strict reverse ordering, including equality, leaves no such real number. This proves (8). The support identity preceding (9) follows because for affine \(f_q\),
\[
f_q\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
=\frac{f_q(x_0)+f_q(x_2)}2,
\]
and the two minima can be attained independently on the compact endpoint polytopes.

It remains to verify the nonempty rational chamber (10)-(12). The areas are
\[
M=k=1,\qquad m=\frac9{25},
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+9/25+1)}9=\frac{118}{225}.
\tag{30}
\]
Also
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\left[-\frac25,\frac25\right]^2
\subseteq K.
\]
Here \(r=9/25\), \(s=1\), and the residual bounds in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` are
\[
\frac{16}{25}<1<\frac{34}{25},
\]
so every residual inequality is strict. The three gradients in (11) are pairwise nonparallel and the functions sum to \(-1\). The first middle cap is a strip of width \(1/10\), hence
\[
c_1=\frac1{10},
\qquad
c_1+m=\frac{23}{50}
<\frac{118}{225}=t,
\tag{31}
\]
where the strict difference is \(29/450\).

Reflection in the \(x\)-axis interchanges the second and third caps. Their union is all of \(K\), because \(f_2+f_3=-7/5-x<0\) on \(K\). Their intersection has, at each fixed \(x\in[-1/2,1/2]\), vertical length
\[
\frac{x+7/5}{35}.
\]
Thus
\[
|\,\{f_2\le0\}\cap\{f_3\le0\}\cap K\,|
=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\frac{x+7/5}{35}\,dx
=\frac1{25}.
\]
It follows that
\[
c_2=c_3=\frac12\left(1+\frac1{25}\right)
=\frac{13}{25}
=\frac{117}{225}<t,
\]
proving (12).

Finally,
\[
\Delta
=\left|\det\left(
\left(-\frac12,35\right),
\left(-\frac12,-35\right)
\right)\right|
=35,
\]
\[
D_0=\frac{2-9/25-1}{3}=\frac{16}{75}
<\frac9{25}=m,
\]
and
\[
\alpha_0=-\frac1{10},
\qquad
\alpha_2=\frac1{10}.
\]
The left endpoint in (7) is strictly positive, while its right endpoint is strictly negative:
\[
1+\alpha_2+\sqrt{2\Delta(m-D_0)}
=\frac{11}{10}+\sqrt{\frac{154}{15}}>0,
\]
\[
-1-\alpha_0-\sqrt{3\Delta t}
=-\frac9{10}-\sqrt{\frac{826}{15}}<0.
\]
Hence (7) holds strictly. This proves that the closed support-gap chamber is nonempty even after the trivial alternatives \(c_j\ge t\) have all been removed.

All area comparisons use closed caps. Every cutting line has planar area zero because its affine function is nonconstant, so open complements, support faces, empty caps, full caps, and equality in (6) or (7) introduce no omitted boundary case.
