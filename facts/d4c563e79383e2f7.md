## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\). Assume the common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`: \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
\(f_1,f_2,f_3\) are nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts,
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,
\]
and, for \(J,H,G\subseteq N\),
\[
P_J=A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\ (i\in J),\ f_i>L_i\ (i\notin J)\},
\]
\[
Q_H=A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-L_i\ (i\in H),\ f_i>-L_i\ (i\notin H)\},
\]
\[
R_G=K\cap\{f_i\leq0\ (i\in G),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin G)\}.
\]
Put
\[
p_J=|P_J|,\qquad q_H=|Q_H|,\qquad r_G=|R_G|,
\]
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G.
\]
Assume \(M\geq m\), the canonical finite-interior inequalities
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad 0<c_i<k\qquad(i\in N),
\]
the crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h\qquad(i\in N),
\]
and the two canonical tails
\[
c_i+m\geq h,\qquad c_i+M\geq h\qquad(i\in N).
\]
Assume that the positive-area \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{123,12,13,23\}.
\]
Finally, assume that the three-slice set with slice bodies \(A_0,K,A_2\) is an actual counterexample, meaning that it has no point of halfspace depth at least \(h\). Then these hypotheses are inconsistent.

## Proof

Every cutting set \(f_i=L_i\), \(f_i=-L_i\), or \(f_i=0\) is a line and therefore has planar area zero. The weak/strict convention in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` nevertheless assigns every point to exactly one cell. In particular, all area sums below respect the canonical boundary assignment. That fact also gives \(R_\varnothing=\varnothing\).

Write
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0.
\]
The assumed positive support gives the exact area identities
\[
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\qquad
v_i=q+\sum_{\substack{j\neq i}}x_j=m-x_i,
\qquad
\sum_i v_i=2m+q.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,\qquad
\Lambda=U+q.
\]
Since all cell areas are nonnegative and \(q>0\), one has
\[
\Lambda>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
t_i=x_i-u_i.
\]
Using (1) in the \(i\)-th crossing gives
\[
c_i=h-u_i-(m-x_i)=h-m+t_i.
\tag{3}
\]
The first canonical tail \(c_i+m\geq h\) and (3) give, with the inequality direction unchanged,
\[
t_i\geq0.
\tag{4}
\]
Also,
\[
\sum_i t_i=(x_1+x_2+x_3)-U=m-q-U=m-\Lambda.
\tag{5}
\]

We next audit the middle-cell conversion. For \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), set
\[
s_i=r_{\{i\}},\qquad
d_i=r_{\{j,\ell\}},\qquad
g=r_N,
\]
and put
\[
S=s_1+s_2+s_3,\qquad
E=d_1+d_2+d_3+2g.
\tag{6}
\]
These are exactly the singleton, opposite-doubleton, and triple cells used in accepted fact `5b75ecd9ec86dbcf`. Since \(R_\varnothing=\varnothing\), the seven displayed cells partition \(K\) exactly under the canonical convention. Hence
\[
k=S+d_1+d_2+d_3+g,
\qquad
\sum_i c_i=S+2(d_1+d_2+d_3)+3g=k+E.
\tag{7}
\]
For fixed \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), expand \(c_j+c_\ell\) cell by cell. The cell \(R_{\{i\}}\) is counted zero times, \(R_{\{j,\ell\}}\) twice, each of the other four nontriple cells once, and \(R_N\) twice. Subtracting from the exact partition of \(k\) therefore gives
\[
k-c_j-c_\ell=s_i-d_i-g.
\tag{8}
\]
By (3) and (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_i-d_i
&=k-c_j-c_\ell+g\\
&=k-2(h-m)-(t_j+t_\ell)+g\\
&=k-2h+m+\Lambda+t_i+g.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus, with
\[
\Delta=k-2h+m+\Lambda,
\tag{10}
\]
equations (4) and (9) yield
\[
\min_i(s_i-d_i)\geq\Delta+g.
\tag{11}
\]
No \(s_i\), \(d_i\), or \(g\) has been assumed positive.

Summing the three crossings and using (1) and (7) gives
\[
U+(2m+q)+(k+E)=3h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}3.
\]
Since \(\Lambda=U+q\), this is exactly
\[
2M=4m+k+3(\Lambda+E).
\tag{12}
\]
Substitution of (12) into (10), with \(h=2(M+m+k)/9\), gives
\[
\Delta=\frac{k-m+\Lambda-2E}{3}.
\tag{13}
\]

Because the realization is an actual counterexample, accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` applies contrapositively to every slice. In particular, \(M\) cannot be at least half of \(M+m+k\). Therefore
\[
M<m+k.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining the strict inequality \(2M<2m+2k\) with (12) gives
\[
k>2m+3(\Lambda+E).
\tag{15}
\]

Apply the exact-cell conclusion of accepted fact `5b75ecd9ec86dbcf` to \(K\) and \(f_1,f_2,f_3\). Its hypotheses hold because \(K\) is a nonempty two-dimensional convex polytope and the \(f_i\) are nonconstant, pairwise nonparallel, and sum to \(-1\). With the cells in (6), it states
\[
S+5g\geq6\min_i(s_i-d_i).
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (11) and (16) imply
\[
S+5g\geq6(\Delta+g).
\tag{17}
\]
On the other hand, (6) and (7) give
\[
S=k-E+g.
\tag{18}
\]
Using (13) and (18), the difference between the right and left sides of (17) is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
6(\Delta+g)-(S+5g)
&=6\Delta+g-S\\
&=k-2m+2\Lambda-3E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The strict bound (15) and (2) now give
\[
k-2m+2\Lambda-3E>5\Lambda>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus (19)-(20) say \(6(\Delta+g)>S+5g\), contradicting (17). Hence the assumed canonical all-double actual counterexample does not exist.
