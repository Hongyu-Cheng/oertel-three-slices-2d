## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be nonconstant pairwise
nonparallel affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,B\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies, write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k_0=|B|,
\]
and fix real levels \(L_1,L_2,L_3\).  Define
\[
u_i=\left|A_0\cap\{f_i\le L_i\}\right|,
\qquad
v_i=\left|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-L_i\}\right|,
\qquad
d_i=u_i+v_i.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose that \(K\supseteq B\) is a compact two-dimensional convex body,
put
\[
A=|K\setminus B|,
\]
and assume the three additive crossing equations
\[
u_i
{}\mathbin{+}v_i
{}\mathbin{+}\left|K\cap\{f_i\le0\}\right|
=
\frac{2(M+m+|K|)}9
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
Then the following exact necessary condition holds:
\[
\boxed{
A\le
M+m-k_0-\frac52(d_1+d_2+d_3)+3\max_i d_i.
}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, take
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad f_2(x,y)=y,\qquad
f_3(x,y)=-1-x-y,
\qquad
L_1=L_2=L_3=-100,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{999}{10},-\frac{981}{10}\right),
\left(197,-\frac{981}{10}\right),
\left(\frac{49}{2},-50\right)
\right\},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
A_2=
\left\{
\left(-\frac{99}{2},\frac{991}{10}\right)
{}\mathbin{+}s\left(302,\frac{19}{10}\right)
{}\mathbin{+}t\left(-\frac1{10},\frac1{20}\right):
-\frac12\le s,t\le\frac12
\right\},
\tag{7}
\]
with
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
No compact convex \(K\supseteq B\) satisfies (3) for any extension area in
the scalar interval
\[
\left[
\frac{23930259}{6650},
\frac{17881582275919}{2325037600}
\right].
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the general filter.  Put
\[
c_i=\left|K\cap\{f_i\le0\}\right|,
\qquad
h_0=\frac{2(M+m+k_0)}9,
\qquad
D=d_1+d_2+d_3,
\qquad
d_{\max}=\max_i d_i.
\]
Since \(B\subseteq K\),
\[
|K|=k_0+A.
\]
Consequently the additive equations (3) are exactly
\[
c_i=h_0+\frac{2A}{9}-d_i.
\tag{9}
\]
It follows that
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3
=3h_0+\frac{2A}{3}-D
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\min_i c_i
=h_0+\frac{2A}{9}-d_{\max}.
\tag{11}
\]

Accepted fact `5b75ecd9ec86dbcf` applies to the compact convex body \(K\)
and the three halfplanes in (1).  It gives
\[
5(c_1+c_2+c_3)-6\min_i c_i\ge4|K|.
\tag{12}
\]
Substituting (10)-(11) and \(|K|=k_0+A\) into (12), the left side minus
the right side is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&5\left(3h_0+\frac{2A}{3}-D\right)
-6\left(h_0+\frac{2A}{9}-d_{\max}\right)
-4(k_0+A)\\
&\qquad
=9h_0-4k_0-5D+6d_{\max}-2A\\
&\qquad
=2(M+m-k_0)-5D+6d_{\max}-2A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Inequality (12) says that (13) is nonnegative.  Dividing by \(2\) and
rearranging proves (4).

We now apply the filter to (5)-(7).  Exact determinant and shoelace
calculations give
\[
M=\frac{1428089}{200},
\qquad
m=\frac{1529}{100}.
\tag{14}
\]
Exact Minkowski addition gives
\[
k_0=|B|=\frac{2202453}{400}.
\tag{15}
\]
All three functions \(f_i-L_i=f_i+100\) are strictly positive on \(A_0\);
their respective minimum margins are
\[
\frac1{10},\qquad\frac{19}{10},\qquad\frac1{10}.
\]
Therefore
\[
u_1=u_2=u_3=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Exact rational clipping of the parallelogram \(A_2\) gives
\[
(v_1,v_2,v_3)=
\left(
\frac{918929}{60400},
\frac{56573}{3800},
\frac{3083993}{202600}
\right).
\tag{17}
\]
The maximum is \(v_3\), as witnessed by
\[
v_3-v_1=\frac{490809}{61185200}>0,
\qquad
v_3-v_2=\frac{643709}{1924700}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (14)-(18) substituted into (4) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
A
&\le
M+m-k_0-\frac52(v_1+v_2+v_3)+3v_3\\
&=
\frac{3678114899083}{2325037600}
=:A_{\mathrm{cap}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

For completeness, these endpoints retain the intended low-ratio
positive-opposite-pair geometry.  Writing \(q_i=f_i+L_i\), exact clipping
gives
\[
\left|
A_2\cap\{q_3\le0,\ q_1>0,\ q_2>0\}
\right|
=\frac{4587}{60400}>0,
\tag{20}
\]
\[
\left|
A_2\cap\{q_1\le0,\ q_2\le0,\ q_3>0\}
\right|
=\frac{13761}{202600}>0,
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
\frac mM=\frac{3058}{1428089}<\frac38.
\tag{22}
\]

Exact clipping of the three zero halfplanes on \(B\) gives
\[
(b_1,b_2,b_3)=
\left(
\frac{698967161}{241600},
0,
\frac{14291658397}{4862400}
\right).
\tag{23}
\]
Consequently
\[
h_0=\frac{2(M+m+k_0)}9=\frac{1688249}{600},
\]
\[
(F_1,F_2,F_3)
=
\left(
\frac{68523839}{724800},
-\frac{7976753}{2850},
\frac{684104333}{4862400}
\right),
\tag{24}
\]
where \(F_i=u_i+v_i+b_i-h_0\).  Accepted fact
`6f6869df3d151764` now gives the scalar-completion interval (8).  Its lower
endpoint already exceeds the coupled cutoff (19) by
\[
\frac{23930259}{6650}-A_{\mathrm{cap}}
=
\frac{32820303025091}{16275263200}>0.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus every scalar-feasible extension area violates the necessary convex
three-cap filter (4).  No compact convex middle completion exists anywhere
in (8), and in particular none exists in its part above \(3600\).

All determinant, Minkowski-area, cap-area, cap-order, cutoff, and interval
comparisons in (14)-(25) were independently checked over \(\mathbb Q\) with
SageMath \(10.8\).  The general implication (4) is the exact algebraic
specialization of the accepted three-cap theorem, not a numerical search.
