## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty interior and area \(T=|K|>0\). Let \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) be nonzero pairwise nonparallel linear forms such that
\[
\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0,
\]
and let \(O\) be their common zero. For
\[
c_i=\left|K\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}\right|,
\]
assume \(O\notin\operatorname{int}K\) and
\[
c_1\leq T/2,\qquad c_2\leq T/2.
\]
Then
\[
c_3T\geq(T-c_1)(T-c_2).
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(c_1=c_2=0\) and \(c_3=T\), equivalently the interior of \(K\) lies in the sector \(\{\ell_1>0,\ell_2>0\}\).

## Proof

Put \(u=\ell_1\) and \(v=\ell_2\). Pairwise nonparallelity makes \(x\mapsto(u(x),v(x))\) an invertible linear coordinate map, and \(\ell_3=-(u+v)\). The three fan lines have zero area. Denote by \(a,b,c,d,e,f\) the areas of the intersections of \(K\) with the following six open sectors in cyclic order:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A:\ u<0,\ v<0,&
B:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v<0,&
C:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v>0,\\
D:\ u>0,\ v>0,&
E:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v>0,&
F:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v<0.
\end{array}
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
T&=a+b+c+d+e+f,\\
c_1&=a+e+f,\qquad
c_2=a+b+c,\qquad
c_3=c+d+e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Adding the two cap assumptions and comparing with (1) gives
\[
2a+b+c+e+f=c_1+c_2\leq T,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad a\leq d.
\tag{2}
\]

We record the separation consequence of \(O\notin\operatorname{int}K\). There is a nonzero linear functional \(s\) such that
\[
s(x)\geq0\quad(x\in K),
\qquad
s(x)>0\quad(x\in\operatorname{int}K).
\tag{3}
\]
For completeness, if \(O\notin K\), let \(x_0\) minimize the Euclidean distance from \(O\) to \(K\). The nearest-point inequality gives
\[
x_0\mathbin{\cdot}x\geq |x_0|^2>0\quad(x\in K).
\]
If \(O\in\partial K\), take points \(y_j\notin K\) tending to \(O\), let \(x_j\) be a nearest point of \(K\) to \(y_j\), and pass to a convergent subsequence of
\[
n_j=\frac{x_j-y_j}{|x_j-y_j|}.
\]
The nearest-point inequalities \(n_j\mathbin{\cdot}(x-x_j)\geq0\) for \(x\in K\), together with \(x_j\to O\), give a unit limit \(n\) with \(n\mathbin{\cdot}x\geq0\) on \(K\). In either case a nonzero separator cannot vanish at an interior point, since a small ball about that point is contained in \(K\). This proves (3). It covers strict separation when \(O\notin K\), as well as every boundary situation, including \(O\) on an edge or at any other supporting point.

We next prove
\[
bf=0.
\tag{4}
\]
We use the elementary observation that positive area in any one of the open convex sectors above supplies a point of \(\operatorname{int}K\) in that sector: choose three noncollinear points of the positive-area intersection, and take an interior point of their triangle.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(b>0\) and \(f>0\). If also \(d>0\), choose interior points \(X_B,X_D,X_F\) in the three indicated sectors. In \((u,v)\)-coordinates write
\[
X_B=(p,-q),\qquad X_D=(r,s_0),\qquad X_F=(-m,n),
\]
where
\[
q>p>0,\qquad r,s_0>0,\qquad m>n>0.
\]
Their cyclic determinants are
\[
\det(X_B,X_D)=ps_0+qr>0,
\]
\[
\det(X_D,X_F)=rn+s_0m>0,
\]
\[
\det(X_F,X_B)=mq-np>0.
\]
The planar determinant identity therefore expresses the origin as a positive linear combination:
\[
\det(X_D,X_F)X_B+
\det(X_F,X_B)X_D+
\det(X_B,X_D)X_F=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Transporting the separator \(s\) to the \((u,v)\)-coordinate plane and applying it to (5) contradicts its strict positivity at all three interior points. Hence \(d=0\).

On the other hand, choose interior points \(X_B\in B\) and \(X_F\in F\). Along the segment from \(X_B\) to \(X_F\), the quantity \(u+v\) remains strictly negative. The coordinate \(u\) changes from positive to negative and \(v\) from negative to positive. At the zero of \(u\), one has \(v=u+v<0\), so the zero of \(u\) occurs strictly before the zero of \(v\). The intervening nonempty segment has \(u<0\) and \(v<0\), hence lies in \(A\). Since the segment joins two interior points of the convex body, this gives an interior point of \(K\) in \(A\), and therefore \(a>0\). This contradicts (2), because \(d=0\) implies \(a=0\). Thus (4) holds.

If \(b=0\), then (1) gives
\[
c_3=c+d+e\geq c+d=T-c_1.
\]
Consequently
\[
c_3T\geq(T-c_1)T\geq(T-c_1)(T-c_2).
\tag{6}
\]
If \(f=0\), the symmetric calculation gives
\[
c_3=c+d+e\geq d+e=T-c_2
\]
and hence
\[
c_3T\geq(T-c_2)T\geq(T-c_1)(T-c_2).
\tag{7}
\]
By (4), one of (6) and (7) always applies.

No strict cap inequality was used. In particular, \(c_i=T/2\) is covered directly by (2). If \(c_1=0\), then \(a=e=f=0\) and (7) applies; if \(c_2=0\), then \(a=b=c=0\) and (6) applies. Thus the empty-cap cases are also included.

Finally, suppose equality holds. Since \(T-c_1,T-c_2>0\), equality in (6), when \(b=0\), forces \(c_2=0\) and \(e=0\). Hence only \(d\) and \(f\) can be positive. If both were positive, a segment joining interior points in \(D\) and \(F\) would first cross \(u=0\), where \(u+v=v>0\), and only later cross \(u+v=0\); the intervening segment would lie in \(E\), contradicting \(e=0\). The alternative \(d=0\) would give \(c_1=T\), contrary to \(c_1\leq T/2\). Thus \(d=T\). Equality in (7), when \(f=0\), is symmetric: it forces \(c_1=0\) and \(c=0\); positive area in both \(B\) and \(D\) would force positive area in the intervening sector \(C\), while \(d=0\) would give \(c_2=T\). Again \(d=T\). Conversely, \(d=T\) gives \(c_1=c_2=0\), \(c_3=T\), and equality. This proves the stated equality characterization and completes the proof.
