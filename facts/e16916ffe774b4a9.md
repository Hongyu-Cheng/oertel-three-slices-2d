## Statement

Let three distinct lines through \(0\) divide \(\mathbb R^2\) into six sectors, and let \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) be alternate sectors. For \(a_1,a_2,a_3>0\), let \(\mathcal F(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) be the set of triples of compact convex sets \(K_i\subseteq\overline{Q_i}\) containing \(0\), with \(|K_i|=a_i\). Put
\[
M(a_1,a_2,a_3)
=
\inf_{(K_1,K_2,K_3)\in\mathcal F(a_1,a_2,a_3)}
\left|\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3)\right|.
\]
Then the infimum is attained. Moreover, there is a minimizing triangle \(T\) containing \(0\) such that
\[
|T\cap Q_i|=a_i\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
and \(T\) has one vertex in each \(\overline{Q_i}\).

Consequently, proving
\[
|T|\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{|T\cap Q_1|}
+\sqrt{|T\cap Q_2|}
+\sqrt{|T\cap Q_3|}\right)^2
\]
for these triangles proves the alternating-cone hull inequality for all positive-area compact convex pieces.

If at least one \(a_i\) is zero, then
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3)\right|
\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2.
\]
This inequality is strict unless all \(a_i=0\) and the hull has zero area.

## Proof

We first record why positive areas force the origin into the interior of the hull. An invertible linear change of coordinates sends the three lines to
\[
x=0,\qquad y=0,\qquad x+y=0
\]
and, after relabeling, sends the selected sectors to
\[
Q_1=\{x<0,\ y<0\},\qquad
Q_2=\{y<0,\ x+y>0\},\qquad
Q_3=\{x<0,\ x+y>0\}.
\]
Positive area lets us choose interior points
\[
z_1=(-a,-b),\qquad
z_2=(c+d,-d),\qquad
z_3=(-f,e+f)
\]
with \(a,b,c,d,e,f>0\). Their consecutive determinants are
\[
\det(z_1,z_2)=ad+b(c+d)>0,
\]
\[
\det(z_2,z_3)=ce+cf+de>0,
\]
\[
\det(z_3,z_1)=fb+a(e+f)>0.
\]
The identity
\[
\det(z_2,z_3)z_1+\det(z_3,z_1)z_2+\det(z_1,z_2)z_3=0
\]
expresses \(0\) as a convex combination of \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) with all coefficients positive. Thus
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{z_1,z_2,z_3\}
\subseteq
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3). \tag{1}
\]

The class \(\mathcal F(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) is nonempty, since a sufficiently scaled triangle with one vertex at \(0\) can be placed in each selected sector. We next prove compactness of minimizing sequences. Let
\[
H_n=\operatorname{conv}(K_{1,n}\cup K_{2,n}\cup K_{3,n})
\]
be a minimizing sequence with \(|H_n|\le M_0\). Suppose that \(D_n=\operatorname{diam}(H_n)\to\infty\). Let \(L_n\) be a line through two points of \(H_n\) at distance \(D_n\), and let \(e_n\) be a unit direction vector of \(L_n\). For every \(z\in H_n\), the triangle with base the diameter segment and third vertex \(z\) lies in \(H_n\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,L_n)\le \frac{2M_0}{D_n}.
\]
Since \(0\in H_n\), the whole body lies in a strip about the line through \(0\) parallel to \(e_n\), of total width at most \(4M_0/D_n\).

Let \(L_{i,n}\) be the length of the orthogonal projection of \(K_{i,n}\) onto \(\mathbb Re_n\). The strip bound gives
\[
a_i\le \frac{4M_0}{D_n}L_{i,n},
\qquad
L_{i,n}\ge \frac{a_iD_n}{4M_0}.
\]
Because \(0\in K_{i,n}\), there is \(w_{i,n}\in K_{i,n}\) with
\[
|w_{i,n}\cdot e_n|\ge \frac{a_iD_n}{8M_0}.
\]
Its component perpendicular to \(e_n\) is \(O(D_n^{-1})\). After taking a subsequence, \(e_n\) converges projectively to a line direction \(e\), and for every \(i\), one of the two directions \(\pm e\) belongs to \(\overline{Q_i}\). This is impossible: the three projective arcs
\[
\overline{Q_i\cup(-Q_i)}
\]
are the three distinct closed intervals cut out on the projective line by the original three line directions, and their triple intersection is empty. Thus the diameters are uniformly bounded.

Compact convex subsets of a fixed ball have a Hausdorff-convergent subsequence, as follows from compactness of their support functions. Passing to a common subsequence gives \(K_{i,n}\to K_i\). Planar area is continuous under Hausdorff convergence of bounded convex bodies, by sandwiching between inner and outer parallel bodies. Convex hull also commutes with this convergence. Hence \(|K_i|=a_i\), and the limiting triple attains \(M(a_1,a_2,a_3)\).

Fix a minimizing triple and write
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3).
\]
We next prove saturation:
\[
K_i=H\cap\overline{Q_i}. \tag{2}
\]
Certainly \(K_i\subseteq H\cap\overline{Q_i}\). Put \(d_i=|H\cap Q_i|\). Suppose \(d_i>a_i\), and set
\[
\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{a_i}{d_i}}<1,
\qquad
\widetilde K_i=\lambda(H\cap\overline{Q_i}),
\qquad
\widetilde K_j=K_j\quad(j\ne i).
\]
Then \(|\widetilde K_i|=a_i\), so the new triple is feasible, and its hull \(\widetilde H\) is contained in \(H\).

There is a linear functional \(\varphi_i\), supplied by the third fan line, which is negative on \(Q_i\) and nonnegative on \(Q_j\cup Q_k\). Since \(a_i>0\),
\[
m:=\min_H\varphi_i<0.
\]
For the new hull,
\[
\min_{\widetilde H}\varphi_i=\lambda m>m.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde H\) is a proper closed convex subset of \(H\). Since \(H\) has nonempty interior by (1), the strict support gap removes a positive-area cap, so \(|\widetilde H|<|H|\), contradicting minimality. Therefore \(d_i=a_i\). Inclusion and equality of the positive areas of the two compact convex sets imply (2).

Every extreme point of \(H\) belongs to \(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3\): otherwise a Carathéodory representation of that point by points of this compact union would express it as a nontrivial convex combination. Hence no open intervening sector contains an extreme point of \(H\). The boundary of \(H\) across each intervening sector is therefore one straight bridge segment.

Number the fan rays cyclically so that \(Q_i\) lies between the rays containing the nonzero boundary contacts \(A_i\) and \(B_i\), and the bridge after \(Q_i\) joins \(B_i\) to \(A_{i+1}\). The contacts are nonzero because \(0\in\operatorname{int}H\). We now replace the selected boundary arc from \(A_i\) to \(B_i\) by one vertex without changing its sector area, including the case of parallel adjacent bridge supports.

Fix \(i\), abbreviate
\[
P=B_{i-1},\qquad A=A_i,\qquad B=B_i,\qquad C=A_{i+1},
\]
and use the two fan-ray directions as a basis. Thus
\[
Q_i=\{xu+yv:x,y\ge0\},\qquad
A=\alpha u,\qquad B=\beta v,
\]
where \(\alpha,\beta>0\) and \(\Delta=\det(u,v)>0\). The supporting halfplane bounded by the bridge line \(PA\) and containing \(H\) has, in these coordinates, the form
\[
x\le\alpha+\kappa y,
\]
and the supporting halfplane bounded by \(BC\) and containing \(H\) has the form
\[
y\le\beta+\ell x
\]
for some real \(\kappa,\ell\). The part of the selected sector lying beyond the chord \(AB\) is described by
\[
\frac{x}{\alpha}+\frac{y}{\beta}\ge1.
\]
Consequently the outer cap of \(H\cap Q_i\) is contained in
\[
\mathcal E=
\left\{
(x,y):
x,y\ge0,\
\frac{x}{\alpha}+\frac{y}{\beta}\ge1,\
x\le\alpha+\kappa y,\
y\le\beta+\ell x
\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
The set \(\mathcal E\) is exactly the set of possible single vertices which preserve the three local convex turns at \(A\), at the new vertex, and at \(B\).

Put
\[
F(xu+yv)
=
\frac12\bigl(\det(A,xu+yv)+\det(xu+yv,B)\bigr)
=
\frac{\Delta}{2}(\alpha y+\beta x).
\]
On the chord \(AB\), \(F=\det(A,B)/2\). If \(\mathcal E\) is bounded and has positive area, direct intersection of the two last supporting halfplanes in (3) shows that
\[
\mathcal E=\operatorname{conv}\{A,U,B\}
\]
for their intersection point \(U\in Q_i\). The actual outer cap is contained in this triangle, so
\[
|H\cap Q_i|
\le F(U).
\]
If the two bridge support lines are parallel, or more generally if \(\mathcal E\) is unbounded, let \(d=(d_1,d_2)\ne0\) be a recession direction of \(\mathcal E\). Then \(d_1,d_2\ge0\), and
\[
F(d_1u+d_2v)
=
\frac{\Delta}{2}(\alpha d_2+\beta d_1)>0.
\]
Thus \(F\) is unbounded above on \(\mathcal E\). This is the required separate treatment of parallel supports.

In either case, choose \(W_i\in\mathcal E\) with
\[
F(W_i)\ge a_i.
\]
If \(\mathcal E\) is unbounded, choose the inequality strict. Choose \(D_i\) in the relative interior of \([A_i,B_i]\). Since \(\mathcal E\) is convex and \(F(D_i)=\det(A_i,B_i)/2\le a_i\), there is \(V_i\in[D_i,W_i]\) such that
\[
\frac12\bigl(\det(A_i,V_i)+\det(V_i,B_i)\bigr)=a_i. \tag{4}
\]
If equality already holds at \(D_i\), omit \(V_i\) and use the chord. Otherwise \(V_i\in Q_i\): in the unbounded case this follows because \(D_i\in Q_i\) and the endpoint was chosen with strict excess, while in the bounded equality case \(U\) cannot lie on a fan ray, since a bridge line through a nonzero point of the adjacent fan ray cannot coincide with that fan ray.

Condition (3) says exactly that the path
\[
P,A_i,V_i,B_i,C
\]
has nonnegative turns. Replacing the three selected arcs independently therefore gives a convex polygon \(P_0\). Its six fan-ray contacts are unchanged. They occur in three antipodal pairs, so the convex hull of the four contacts on any two fan lines contains \(0\) in its interior. Hence
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}P_0. \tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
L_i=P_0\cap\overline{Q_i}.
\]
Equation (4) gives \(|L_i|=a_i\), each \(L_i\) is compact and convex and contains \(0\), and every vertex of \(P_0\) belongs to one of the \(L_i\): fan contacts lie on selected-sector boundary rays, and every \(V_i\) lies in \(Q_i\). Therefore
\[
P_0=\operatorname{conv}(L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3). \tag{6}
\]
The three bridge regions were unchanged, and (4) preserved the three selected areas, so \(|P_0|=|H|\). Thus \(P_0\) is a feasible polygonal minimizer.

We now eliminate all extra fan-ray vertices by exact area-preserving variations. Write \([X,Y]=\det(X,Y)\). First suppose
\[
E_i:=2a_i-[A_i,B_i]>0.
\]
Then \(V_i\in Q_i\) is present and is a strict vertex. Consider the right ray point \(B=B_i\), with \(A=A_i\), \(V=V_i\), and let \(C=A_{i+1}\) be the other endpoint of the following bridge. If \(B\) is a strict polygon vertex, put
\[
B_\lambda=\lambda B,
\qquad
V_\lambda=A+\sigma_\lambda(V-A),
\qquad 0<\lambda<1,
\]
where
\[
\sigma_\lambda=
\frac{2a_i-\lambda[A,B]}
{[A,V]+\lambda\bigl([V,B]-[A,B]\bigr)}. \tag{7}
\]
At \(\lambda=1\), the numerator and denominator in (7) both equal \(E_i\), so \(\sigma_1=1\). Moreover,
\[
\sigma_\lambda'\big|_{\lambda=1}
=
-\frac{[V,B]}{E_i}<0.
\]
Thus \(\sigma_\lambda>1\) for \(\lambda<1\) sufficiently close to \(1\). Since \(V\in Q_i\), choosing \(\lambda\) close enough also ensures \(V_\lambda\in Q_i\). Direct substitution gives
\[
[A,V_\lambda]+[V_\lambda,B_\lambda]=2a_i. \tag{8}
\]
The preceding turn at \(A\) is multiplied by the positive factor \(\sigma_\lambda\), and the strict turns at \(V\) and \(B\) remain positive by continuity.

Only the turn at \(C\) requires attention. If it is strict, continuity suffices. If it is flat and \(W\) is the boundary point after \(C\), then
\[
W-C=\tau(C-B)
\]
for some \(\tau>0\). Hence
\[
[C-B_\lambda,W-C]
=
(1-\lambda)\tau[B,C]>0.
\]
Thus the perturbed cyclic vertex list defines a convex polygon \(P_\lambda\). Its six fan-ray contacts remain nonzero points on the same rays, so the antipodal-contact argument used in (5) gives
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}P_\lambda.
\]
Set
\[
L_j^{(\lambda)}=P_\lambda\cap\overline{Q_j}.
\]
Equation (8) gives \(|L_i^{(\lambda)}|=a_i\). All other selected boundaries are unchanged, while the changed segment \(B_\lambda C\) stays inside the intervening cone, so \(|L_j^{(\lambda)}|=a_j\) for \(j\ne i\). All altered vertices remain in the same selected sector closure or on the same fan ray. Every vertex of \(P_\lambda\) therefore belongs to \(\bigcup_jL_j^{(\lambda)}\), and
\[
P_\lambda
=
\operatorname{conv}\left(
L_1^{(\lambda)}\cup L_2^{(\lambda)}\cup L_3^{(\lambda)}
\right). \tag{9}
\]
Thus the new triple is feasible. Its following bridge area changes from \([B,C]/2\) to \(\lambda[B,C]/2\), while every selected area and every other bridge area is unchanged. This contradicts minimality. The symmetric left-endpoint variation gives the same conclusion. Therefore, whenever \(E_i>0\), neither \(A_i\) nor \(B_i\) is a strict polygon vertex. The only strict vertex contributed by that selected sector is \(V_i\).

It remains to treat \(E_i=0\). Then the selected boundary is the chord \([A_i,B_i]\). Suppose both endpoints \(A=A_i\) and \(B=B_i\) are strict vertices. Choose
\[
D=(1-r)A+rB,\qquad 0<r<1,
\]
and set
\[
A_\lambda=\lambda A,
\qquad
B_\lambda=\lambda B,
\qquad
V_\lambda=\lambda^{-1}D.
\]
The point \(D\), and hence \(V_\lambda\), lies in \(Q_i\). Moreover,
\[
[A_\lambda,V_\lambda]+[V_\lambda,B_\lambda]
=
[A,D]+[D,B]
=
[A,B]
=
2a_i. \tag{10}
\]
Thus the selected area is preserved exactly. The new vertex \(V_\lambda\) lies strictly outside the chord \([A_\lambda,B_\lambda]\), since the chord area has fallen to \(\lambda^2a_i\). The turns at \(A\) and \(B\) are positive for \(\lambda<1\) close to \(1\), because at \(\lambda=1\) they are respectively \(r\) and \(1-r\) times the original strict turns.

The preceding and following bridge areas are both multiplied by \(\lambda\). At their remote endpoints, strict turns persist. A flat remote turn becomes strict: for example, if \(P\) is the preceding bridge endpoint, \(W\) precedes \(P\), and
\[
A-P=\tau(P-W),
\]
then
\[
[P-W,A_\lambda-P]
=
-(1-\lambda)[P-W,A]
=
\frac{1-\lambda}{\tau}[P,A]>0.
\]
The calculation at the following remote endpoint is identical. Hence the new cyclic list defines a convex polygon \(P_\lambda\).

Again its nonzero fan contacts remain on the same six rays, so \(0\in\operatorname{int}P_\lambda\). Define
\[
L_j^{(\lambda)}=P_\lambda\cap\overline{Q_j}.
\]
Equation (10) preserves the \(i\)-th area. The only other changed regions are the two intervening bridge cones, so the other two selected areas remain \(a_j\). The new vertex lies in \(Q_i\), the scaled contacts stay on their original rays, and all other vertices are unchanged. Therefore every vertex belongs to one of the three \(L_j^{(\lambda)}\), and the hull identity (9) holds again. This is a feasible triple whose total area is strictly smaller, contradicting minimality. Therefore, when \(E_i=0\), at most one of \(A_i,B_i\) is a strict polygon vertex.

We can now count vertices. Each selected sector contributes at most one strict vertex:
\[
E_i>0:\quad V_i\text{ is the only possible strict vertex},
\]
\[
E_i=0:\quad \text{at most one of }A_i,B_i\text{ is strict}.
\]
There are no vertices in the interiors of the bridge sectors. Thus \(P_0\) has at most three strict vertices. Since \(P_0\) has positive area, it has at least three strict vertices. It therefore has exactly three and is a triangle. Equality in the count assigns one of its vertices to each \(\overline{Q_i}\). By construction,
\[
|P_0\cap Q_i|=a_i,
\qquad
|P_0|=M(a_1,a_2,a_3).
\]
This proves the positive-area reduction, including all fan-ray and parallel-support degeneracies.

Finally, suppose at least one \(a_i\) is zero. There are at most two positive areas. Distinct selected sectors have disjoint interiors, so
\[
|H|\ge \sum_i a_i.
\]
For at most two positive numbers,
\[
\frac12\left(\sum_i\sqrt{a_i}\right)^2
\le \sum_i a_i
\]
by \(2\sqrt{a_ia_j}\le a_i+a_j\). If exactly two areas are positive, convexity between interior disks in the two separated sectors adds a positive-area bridge outside their union, so \(|H|>\sum_i a_i\). With exactly one positive area, the displayed square-root bound is already strict. If all three areas vanish, equality holds exactly when \(|H|=0\).
