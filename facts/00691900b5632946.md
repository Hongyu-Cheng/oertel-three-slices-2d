## Statement

Take the first exact calibration of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98`:
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-29/2&53/2&-25/2\\
-29/2&-25/2&53/2\\
30&-13&-13
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=\frac{841}{6708},
\qquad
B=\frac{47089}{41925},
\]
and put
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9=\frac{377081}{754650}.
\]
Let \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,
\]
\[
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\]
\[
\min_jU_{ij}+\min_jV_{ij}\ge0
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Then, for every \(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\), the third row mass in the exact system of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3
:={}&
\mathcal C(L_{3*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{3*};-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{3*};\sigma_3)\\
\ge{}&
t+\frac{7297709}{64899900}>t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently no real, and hence no rational, \(U,V,\sigma\) completes this fixed interior calibration to a strict \(N=3\) matrix witness.  This conclusion permits both endpoint determinant signs, all endpoint cap-order chambers, and arbitrary endpoint allocations; it does not require the additional inequality \(\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3>1\).

## Proof

We first audit the fixed data.  The columns of \(L\) sum to one,
\[
\det L=1677,
\]
and the row-\(3\) vertex values are \((30,-13,-13)\).  The exact repeated-lower-value cap formula in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` therefore gives
\[
c_3:=\mathcal C(L_{3*};0)
=\frac{30^2}{43^2}
=\frac{900}{1849}.
\tag{1}
\]
The area pair is strictly interior.  Indeed, with
\[
R=\frac AB=\frac{21025}{188356},
\qquad
S=\frac1B=\frac{41925}{47089},
\]
one has \(R<9/25\) and
\[
S-(1-R)=\frac{369}{188356}>0,
\qquad
(1+R)-S=\frac{41681}{188356}>0,
\tag{2}
\]
as well as
\[
1+A-B=\frac{123}{55900}>0.
\tag{3}
\]

We use the lossless endpoint parametrization in accepted fact
`f74f24523b5f53b2`, including its short derivation to keep all signs and
normalizations explicit.  Put
\[
\ell_i=\min_jU_{ij},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s=\mathbf1^\top\ell.
\]
Nondegeneracy of \(U\) gives \(s<1\).  If \(n_i=\min_jV_{ij}\), then
compatibility gives \(\ell_i+n_i\ge0\), while nondegeneracy of \(V\)
gives \(\sum_i n_i<1\).  Hence \(s>-1\).  Define
\[
\rho=1-s,\qquad
\eta=1+s=2-\rho,
\]
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\rho},
\qquad
Y=\frac{V+\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\eta}.
\tag{4}
\]
Both \(X,Y\) are entrywise nonnegative and column-stochastic.  Since
\[
U=(\rho I+\ell\mathbf1^\top)X,
\qquad
V=(\eta I-\ell\mathbf1^\top)Y,
\]
the matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det U=\rho^2\det X,\qquad
\det V=\eta^2\det Y.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
d_X:=|\det X|=\frac{A}{\rho^2},
\qquad
d_Y:=|\det Y|=\frac{B}{\eta^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
For
\[
p_3=(L\ell)_3+\sigma_3,
\tag{7}
\]
the two row-\(3\) endpoint fractions are exactly
\[
\mathcal C\!\left(\rho(LX)_{3*}+p_3\mathbf1^\top;0\right),
\qquad
\mathcal C\!\left(\eta(LY)_{3*}-p_3\mathbf1^\top;0\right).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (4)-(8) retain both determinant signs, arbitrary compatible
endpoint triangles, and every allocation.

We next prove the determinant-cap envelope.  Let \(Z\) be any
nonnegative column-stochastic \(3\times3\) matrix, put
\[
d=|\det Z|>0,
\]
and sort the entries of its first row as
\[
0\le a\le b\le c\le1.
\]
Writing the second-row entries after the same column permutation as
\(z_1,z_2,z_3\), one has
\[
0\le z_1\le1-a,\qquad
0\le z_2\le1-b,\qquad
0\le z_3\le1-c,
\]
and
\[
\det Z
=(b-a)(z_3-z_1)-(c-a)(z_2-z_1).
\]
The absolute maximum of this affine expression over the displayed box
is attained at a box vertex.  Its three possible nonzero magnitudes are
\[
(b-a)(1-c),\qquad
(c-a)(1-b),\qquad
(c-b)(1-a).
\]
The middle quantity dominates the first, and its difference from the
third is
\[
(b-a)(1-c)\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
d\le D:=(c-a)(1-b).
\tag{9}
\]

For every real threshold \(\tau\), (9) and the exact cap formulas imply
\[
\mathcal C(Z_{1*};\tau)\ge
\psi_d(\tau):=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\le0,\\[1mm]
\left(\dfrac{d-\tau}{d}\right)^2,&0<\tau<d,\\[2mm]
0,&\tau\ge d.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
It remains only to prove the middle range \(0<\tau<d\).  Since
\[
d\le D\le c,
\]
the cap is nonempty.  If \(a<\tau\le b\), its omitted fraction is
\[
Q=\frac{(\tau-a)^2}{(b-a)(c-a)}.
\]
The inequalities \(\tau-a\le b-a\), \(\tau-a\le\tau\), and
\(1-b\le1\) give
\[
QD=\frac{(\tau-a)^2(1-b)}{b-a}\le\tau.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathcal C(Z_{1*};\tau)=1-Q
\ge1-\frac{\tau}{D}
\ge\left(1-\frac{\tau}{d}\right)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(b<\tau<c\), then
\[
\sqrt{\mathcal C(Z_{1*};\tau)}
=\frac{c-\tau}{\sqrt{(c-a)(c-b)}}
\ge\frac{c-\tau}{c-a}.
\]
Also
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(c-\tau)-(c-a)(D-\tau)
&=Da+\tau(c-a-D)\\
&=Da+\tau b(c-a)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt{\mathcal C(Z_{1*};\tau)}
\ge\frac{D-\tau}{D}
\ge\frac{d-\tau}{d}.
\tag{12}
\]
The cases \(\tau\le a\), repeated vertex values, and branch boundaries
follow directly or from the literal repeated-value formulas in
`64760c76cadff481`.  This proves (10).

Apply (10) to \(X\) and \(Y\).  Since
\[
L_{3*}=43e_1^\top-13\mathbf1^\top,
\]
define
\[
\alpha=\frac{13}{43}-\frac{p_3}{43\rho},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{13}{43}+\frac{p_3}{43\eta}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then the two caps in (8) are
\[
\mathcal C(X_{1*};\alpha),
\qquad
\mathcal C(Y_{1*};\beta),
\]
and
\[
\rho\alpha+\eta\beta=\frac{26}{43}=:H.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (6) and (10) give
\[
A\mathcal C(X_{1*};\alpha)\ge
\phi_{A,\rho}(\alpha),
\qquad
B\mathcal C(Y_{1*};\beta)\ge
\phi_{B,\eta}(\beta),
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
\phi_{K,r}(z)=
\begin{cases}
K,&z\le0,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(K-r^2z)^2}{K},&0<z<K/r^2,\\[2mm]
0,&z\ge K/r^2.
\end{cases}
\tag{16}
\]

The determinant bound (9) also gives \(d_X,d_Y\le1\), and hence
\[
\rho\ge\sqrt A,\qquad
\eta\ge\sqrt B.
\tag{17}
\]
The exact comparison
\[
A-\frac19=\frac{287}{20124}>0
\]
implies \(\rho>1/3\) and therefore \(\eta<5/3\).  Consequently
\[
\frac B\eta>\frac{3B}{5}
=H+\frac{4839}{69875}>H.
\tag{18}
\]

We now prove that the endpoint contribution in row \(3\) is always at
least \(A\).  If \(\alpha\le0\), the first term in (15) is already
\(A\).  Suppose \(\alpha>0\).  If \(\beta\le0\), the second term is
\(B>A\).  Also \(\beta\ge B/\eta^2\) is impossible, because it would
give
\[
H=\rho\alpha+\eta\beta>\frac B\eta>H.
\]
Thus, whenever \(\alpha>0\) and \(\beta>0\), the \(Y\)-term is on its
middle branch.

First take
\[
0<\alpha\le\frac{A}{\rho^2}.
\]
Put \(x=\rho\alpha\).  From (14), \(\eta\beta=H-x\).  The sum of the
two middle-branch lower bounds is
\[
F_\rho(x)
=\frac{(A-\rho x)^2}{A}
+\frac{(B-\eta H+\eta x)^2}{B}.
\tag{19}
\]
Its unconstrained minimum over \(x\in\mathbb R\) is
\[
m(\rho)
=\frac{(A\eta+B\rho-H\rho\eta)^2}
{B\rho^2+A\eta^2}.
\tag{20}
\]
This minimum is strictly larger than \(A\) throughout the feasible
\(\rho\)-interval.  Put
\[
P(\rho)
=(A\eta+B\rho-H\rho\eta)^2
-A(B\rho^2+A\eta^2),
\qquad
\eta=2-\rho.
\]
Exact differentiation and factorization give
\[
P'(\rho)
=\frac{2704}{1849}
\left(\rho-\frac{3611}{25350}\right)
\left(
\rho^2-\frac{5963}{15600}\rho+\frac{841}{4056}
\right).
\tag{21}
\]
The discriminant of the quadratic factor is
\[
-\frac{166282631}{243360000}<0,
\]
so that factor is positive.  Since
\[
\rho>\frac13>\frac{3611}{25350},
\]
equation (21) shows that \(P\) is strictly increasing on the feasible
interval.  Finally,
\[
P\!\left(\frac13\right)
=\frac{121017209}{63277402500}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(P(\rho)>0\), so (20) gives \(m(\rho)>A\), and (19) is strictly
larger than \(A\).

It remains to allow \(\alpha>A/\rho^2\), where the first lower function
in (16) is zero.  At the boundary \(\alpha=A/\rho^2\), formula (19)
remains literal and is already strictly larger than \(A\).  Increasing
\(\alpha\) decreases \(\beta\) by (14), so the \(Y\)-lower function is
nondecreasing.  If \(\beta\) reaches zero, it becomes the full value
\(B>A\).  Hence the endpoint sum remains strictly larger than \(A\) in
this last range as well.  Combining all cases proves
\[
A\mathcal C((LU)_{3*};-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{3*};\sigma_3)
\ge A.
\tag{23}
\]

Finally, (1) and (23) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3-t
&\ge
\frac{900}{1849}
+\frac{841}{6708}
-\frac{377081}{754650}\\
&=\frac{7297709}{64899900}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the third strict row fails for every compatible real endpoint
pair and every allocation, proving the statement.
