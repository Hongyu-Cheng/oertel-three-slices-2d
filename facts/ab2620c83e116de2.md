## Statement

Assume the canonical root setting of accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`, and impose all the area-only necessary conditions for failure of the large-slice, outer-centroid, and middle-cover criteria assembled in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Normalize \(M=1\), and write
\[
r=m/M,\qquad s=k/M,\qquad x=c_j/M,\qquad
\tau=t/M=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9,\qquad
\delta=\Delta_j/M=2\tau-s+x.
\]
Thus
\[
0<r<\rho:=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le9/25,\\
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\quad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose also that \(0\le x\le s\) and \(x+r<\tau\), as in the root-left-tail failure of accepted fact `c0b5fee36666b707`.  Define
\[
\alpha=\frac{5r+5s-4}{9},\qquad
\beta=\frac{2+2s-7r}{9},\qquad
\gamma=\alpha-\beta=\frac{s+4r-2}{3}.
\tag{3}
\]

1. The scalar Regime A conditions
\[
\delta>0,\qquad \delta\ge r
\tag{4}
\]
are feasible in \(x\) if and only if
\[
s+4r<2.
\tag{5}
\]
When (5) holds, their exact feasible interval is
\[
\alpha\le x<\beta.
\tag{6}
\]
Within (1)-(2), the projection of Regime A is exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
0<r\le1/5,\quad 1-r<s<1+r,\\
1/5<r<1/3,\quad 1-r<s<2-4r.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, its projection to \(r=m/M\) is exactly \(0<r<1/3\).

2. In scalar Regime B, put
\[
q=r-\delta.
\tag{8}
\]
The conditions
\[
\delta>0,\qquad \delta<r,\qquad
x>\frac{(1+\sqrt{r-\delta})^2}{4},\qquad
4rx>r-\delta
\tag{9}
\]
are feasible in \(x\) if and only if
\[
\alpha>\frac14
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\max\{0,\gamma\}
<
\min\left\{
r,\
\left(\frac{2\sqrt{5\alpha-1}-1}{5}\right)^2
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
More precisely, every feasible choice is parametrized by
\[
\max\{0,\gamma\}<q<
\min\left\{
r,\
\left(\frac{2\sqrt{5\alpha-1}-1}{5}\right)^2
\right\},
\qquad
x=\alpha-q.
\tag{11}
\]
The product inequality in (9) is redundant once the other inequalities in (9) hold.  The projection of Regime B to \(r=m/M\) is exactly
\[
\frac18<r<\frac14.
\tag{12}
\]
For every \(r\) in (12), a nonempty explicit open set of admissible middle-area ratios is
\[
\frac54-r<s<\min\{1+r,\,2-4r\}.
\tag{13}
\]

3. Both regimes contain exact rational points with strict slack in every global area-only necessary condition.  For Regime A one may take
\[
(M,m,k,c_j)=\left(1,\frac14,\frac45,\frac16\right),
\tag{14}
\]
for which \(T=41/20\), \(t=41/90\), and \(\Delta_j=5/18>m\).  For Regime B one may take
\[
(M,m,k,c_j)=
\left(1,\frac15,\frac{47}{40},\frac{557}{1800}\right),
\tag{15}
\]
for which \(T=19/8\), \(t=19/36\), \(\Delta_j=19/100<m\), and \(m-\Delta_j=1/100\).

No assertion is made that either scalar point is realized by compatible planar bodies together with an affine cap having area \(c_j\), and no assertion of an actual counterexample is made.

## Proof

Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives exactly (1)-(2) after the homogeneous normalization \(M=1\).  In particular,
\[
s>1-r,\qquad s<1+r.
\tag{16}
\]
The first inequality implies \(r+s>1\), and hence
\[
\alpha>\frac19>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Direct calculation from the definitions gives
\[
\delta-r=x-\alpha,
\qquad
x+r<\tau\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x<\beta,
\qquad
\beta-\alpha=\frac{2-s-4r}{3}.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover, (16) gives
\[
\beta-s=\frac{2-7r-7s}{9}<0.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every \(x<\beta\) automatically satisfies \(x<s\).

For Regime A, equations (17)-(18) show that all its conditions are exactly
\[
\alpha\le x<\beta.
\]
This interval is nonempty exactly when \(\alpha<\beta\), which by (18) is (5).  It also has \(x>0\), while \(\delta\ge r>0\), so none of the omitted sign conditions adds a restriction.  This proves (5)-(6).

To project (5) onto the residual area strip, combine \(s<2-4r\) with \(s>1-r\).  Their compatibility forces \(r<1/3<9/25\), so in this entire projection (2) reduces to
\[
1-r<s<1+r.
\]
If \(0<r\le1/5\), then \(1+r\le2-4r\); if \(1/5<r<1/3\), then \(2-4r<1+r\).  This proves the exact piecewise description (7).

Now consider Regime B.  With \(q\) as in (8), the two strict inequalities \(0<\delta<r\) are exactly
\[
0<q<r.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (18) give
\[
x=\alpha-q,\qquad
x<\beta\ \Longleftrightarrow\ q>\gamma.
\tag{21}
\]
The square-root inequality in (9) becomes
\[
\alpha-q>\frac{(1+\sqrt q)^2}{4}.
\tag{22}
\]
Writing \(v=\sqrt q>0\), inequality (22) is
\[
5v^2+2v+1-4\alpha<0.
\tag{23}
\]
It has a positive solution exactly when \(\alpha>1/4\).  In that case the positive root of the left side is
\[
v_*=\frac{2\sqrt{5\alpha-1}-1}{5},
\tag{24}
\]
and (23) is exactly \(0<v<v_*\).  Combining (20)-(24) proves (10)-(11).  These inequalities also imply \(x>1/4>0\), while (19) and \(x<\beta\) imply \(x<s\), so the area bounds on \(x\) add nothing further.

The product condition in (9) is automatic: (20) gives
\[
\frac{q}{4r}<\frac14,
\]
whereas (22) gives
\[
x>\frac{(1+\sqrt q)^2}{4}>\frac14.
\]
Therefore \(4rx>q=r-\delta\).

It remains to prove the exact ratio projection (12).  Necessity follows directly from any feasible \(q\).  Put \(v=\sqrt q\).  Expanding (22) in terms of \(\alpha\) yields
\[
s>
\frac{25+18v+45v^2}{20}-r.
\tag{25}
\]
On the other hand, (21) gives
\[
s<2-4r+3v^2,
\tag{26}
\]
and (16) gives \(s<1+r\).  Comparing (25) first with \(1+r\) and then with (26) yields
\[
r>\frac{5+18v+45v^2}{40}>\frac18
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
r<\frac{5-6v+5v^2}{20}<\frac14.
\tag{28}
\]
For the last strict inequality, (1) and (20) give \(0<v<1\), and hence \(5v^2-6v<0\).  Thus every Regime B point satisfies (12).

Conversely, suppose \(1/8<r<1/4\).  The interval in (13) is nonempty because
\[
\frac54-r<1+r\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r>\frac18
\]
and
\[
\frac54-r<2-4r\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r<\frac14.
\]
Choose any \(s\) in (13).  Since \(r<1/4<9/25\), one has
\[
1-r<\frac54-r<s<1+r,
\]
so (1)-(2) hold.  Also \(r+s>5/4\), hence \(\alpha>1/4\), and \(s<2-4r\), hence \(\gamma<0\).  The right side of (24) is therefore positive, and any
\[
0<q<\min\{r,v_*^2\}
\]
satisfies (11).  This proves sufficiency and hence the exact projection (12).

We finally audit the two rational witnesses.  For (14),
\[
T=\frac{41}{20},\qquad \frac T2=\frac{41}{40},
\]
so \(M,m,k<T/2\).  Compatibility is strict because
\[
4k=\frac{16}{5}>
\left(1+\frac12\right)^2=\frac94.
\]
The middle-cover criterion fails strictly because
\[
k+m=\frac{21}{20}<2M,
\]
and the outer-centroid criterion fails strictly because
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m
=\frac{13}{4}
<
\frac{41}{10}=2T.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
c_j+m=\frac5{12}<\frac{41}{90}=t,
\qquad
\Delta_j=2t-k+c_j=\frac5{18}>\frac14=m.
\]
Equivalently, here
\[
\alpha=\frac5{36}<\frac16=x<\frac{37}{180}=\beta.
\]
Thus (14) is a strict interior Regime A scalar point.

For (15),
\[
T=\frac{19}{8},\qquad \frac T2=\frac{19}{16}.
\]
The three strict large-slice inequalities hold; the narrowest one is
\[
k=\frac{47}{40}<\frac{19}{16},
\]
with gap \(1/80\).  Compatibility is strict because
\[
4k=\frac{47}{10}>
\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt5}\right)^2
=\frac65+\frac2{\sqrt5}.
\]
The middle-cover criterion fails strictly because
\[
k+m=\frac{11}{8}<2M.
\]
The outer-centroid criterion fails strictly because
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m
=2+\frac2{\sqrt5}
<
\frac{19}{4}=2T.
\]
For the tail quantities,
\[
c_j+m=\frac{917}{1800}
<
\frac{950}{1800}=t,
\qquad
\Delta_j=2t-k+c_j=\frac{19}{100},
\]
so \(0<\Delta_j<m=1/5\) and \(m-\Delta_j=1/100\).  The square-root inequality has exact gap
\[
c_j-\frac{(1+\sqrt{m-\Delta_j})^2}{4}
=\frac{557}{1800}-\frac{121}{400}
=\frac1{144}>0.
\]
Finally,
\[
4mc_j-M(m-\Delta_j)
=\frac{557}{2250}-\frac1{100}
=\frac{1069}{4500}>0.
\]
Thus (15) is a strict interior Regime B scalar point.  Since \(c_j<k\) in both (14) and (15), all elementary cap-area bounds are also strict.

These audits establish scalar feasibility only.  They do not construct \(A_0,K,A_2\), the affine functions \(f_i\), or a cap \(K\cap\{f_j\le0\}\) of the specified area.
