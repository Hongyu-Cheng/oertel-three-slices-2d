## Statement

Assume the actual dominant all-double common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted facts 174b54e9843ef8fc, 1ba5106642a3b8bd, and f5bb496aa51a2cf9. In particular, \(A_0,A_2,K\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
the positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
and, with the dominant index labeled \(1\),
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,\qquad
x_1>\frac{5m}{9}.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,
\]
\[
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123},
\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
The three crossings hold, and accepted fact 174b54e9843ef8fc gives
\[
2M=4m+k+3(U+q+E_R).
\tag{1}
\]

Use the dominant bodies of accepted facts f5bb496aa51a2cf9 and
1ba5106642a3b8bd:
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad p=|P|=p_\varnothing,
\]
\[
C=C_1^\sharp,\qquad d=|C|,
\qquad
S_1=\frac{P+C}{2},
\]
\[
\eta_1=(f_1+L_1)(g_{C_1})>0,\qquad
\lambda_1=\frac{r_{123}\eta_1}{2}>0,
\]
\[
A_1=\left|K\cap\{f_1\geq0\}\right|
=k-c_1.
\]
Let \(\mathcal V(P,C)\) denote planar mixed area, normalized by
\[
|P+tC|=p+2t\mathcal V(P,C)+t^2d
\qquad(t\geq0).
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
|S_1|=\frac14\bigl(p+d+2\mathcal V(P,C)\bigr).
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{4x_1}{9}\leq d\leq\frac{5x_1}{9}.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

Define the exact mixed-area budget
\[
\mathscr B
=
\frac{13p}{6}
-\frac{14}{3}(m+q+E_R)
-2x_1-2(u_2+u_3)
-\frac d2-2\lambda_1.
\tag{5}
\]
Every such actual realization necessarily satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
\sqrt{pd}
\leq \mathcal V(P,C)
\leq \mathscr B.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, \(\mathscr B<\sqrt{pd}\) closes the realization
immediately. The first proposed alternative has the exact, lossless form
\[
\boxed{\quad
|S_1|+\lambda_1>A_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal V(P,C)>
2(A_1-\lambda_1)-\frac{p+d}{2}.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]

For the transverse statement, retain the intervals from accepted fact
1ba5106642a3b8bd:
\[
a_i^-=\min_P(f_i-L_i),\qquad
a_i^+=\max_P(f_i-L_i),
\]
\[
b_i^-=\min_C(-f_i-L_i),\qquad
b_i^+=\max_C(-f_i-L_i),
\]
\[
\Omega_i=(a_i^+-a_i^-)+(b_i^+-b_i^-)>0,
\]
\[
\theta_i=
\min\left\{1,\max\left\{0,
\frac{b_i^+-a_i^-}{\Omega_i}\right\}\right\},
\qquad i=2,3.
\tag{8}
\]
If
\[
\theta_2^2+\theta_3^2\leq1,
\tag{9}
\]
then exactly one of the following two alternatives holds.

First, there is an exterior-support degeneration:
\[
(\theta_2,\theta_3)=(0,1),
\qquad
S_1\subseteq\{f_2\geq0,\ f_3\leq-1-\eta_1/2\},
\tag{10}
\]
or the same assertion with \(2\) and \(3\) interchanged.

Second,
\[
0<\theta_2,\theta_3<1,\qquad
\theta_2+\theta_3>1,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
\theta_3\Omega_3
\geq
2+\eta_1+(1-\theta_2)\Omega_2,
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\boxed{\quad
\theta_2\Omega_2
\geq
2+\eta_1+(1-\theta_3)\Omega_3.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Omega_2,\Omega_3
\geq
\frac{2+\eta_1}{\theta_2+\theta_3-1}
\geq
(1+\sqrt2)(2+\eta_1).
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
If the inequality in (9) is strict, only the second alternative is
possible.

Finally, if the lower and upper endpoints of (6) coincide,
\[
\mathscr B=\sqrt{pd},
\tag{15}
\]
then all inequalities producing (6) are equalities. In particular,
\[
\mathcal V(P,C)=\sqrt{pd},
\tag{16}
\]
so \(P\) and \(C\) are positively homothetic; all positive-area
nonempty \(P\)-cells are singleton cells; and the deep midpoint body
\(S_1\) together with the central-edge bridge \(G_1\) of accepted fact
f5bb496aa51a2cf9 fills \(K\cap\{f_1\geq0\}\) up to area zero, with
\[
|G_1|=\lambda_1.
\tag{17}
\]

## Proof

Equation (2) at \(t=1\), followed by scaling by \(1/2\), gives (3)
exactly. No Brunn--Minkowski replacement is made here.

Accepted fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 proves the lower bound in (4). The same
centroid cut gives the upper bound as well. Indeed, \(C\) is the
\(\{f_1+L_1\geq\eta_1\}\)-half of the two-dimensional convex body
\(\overline C_1\), whose centroid lies on the cutting line and whose
area is \(x_1\). The complementary closed halfplane also contains that
centroid. The planar centroid-halfplane inequality therefore gives
\[
|\overline C_1\setminus C|\geq\frac{4x_1}{9}.
\]
The cutting line has area zero, so
\[
d\leq x_1-\frac{4x_1}{9}=\frac{5x_1}{9}.
\]
This proves (4).

We next derive the upper endpoint in (6). Put
\[
D_0=M-p.
\]
The nonempty exact \(P\)-cells have total area \(D_0\), whereas their
index multiplicity sum is \(U\). Since \(P_{123}\) is empty,
\[
D_0\leq U.
\tag{18}
\]
Equation (1) gives
\[
k=2M-4m-3(U+q+E_R),
\]
\[
h=\frac{2(M-m-U-q-E_R)}3.
\tag{19}
\]
The first crossing and the all-double identity
\(v_1=m-x_1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1
&=k-c_1\\
&=k-h+u_1+m-x_1\\
&=\frac{4M-7m-7(U+q+E_R)}3+u_1-x_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Substitute \(M=p+D_0\), expand \(U\), and use (18):
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1
&=
\frac{4p-7m-7q-7E_R}{3}-x_1\\
&\quad+
\frac{4D_0-4u_1-7u_2-7u_3}{3}\\
&\leq
\frac{4p-7m-7q-7E_R}{3}
-x_1-u_2-u_3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

Accepted fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 constructs two area-disjoint subsets
\(S_1,G_1\subseteq K\cap\{f_1\geq0\}\), with
\[
|G_1|\geq\lambda_1.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac14\bigl(p+d+2\mathcal V(P,C)\bigr)+\lambda_1
\leq A_1.
\tag{22}
\]
Combining (21)--(22) and solving for the mixed area gives
\[
\mathcal V(P,C)\leq\mathscr B.
\tag{23}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski applied to \(P+tC\), followed by comparison of
the coefficient linear in \(t\) at \(t=0\), gives Minkowski's first
inequality
\[
\mathcal V(P,C)\geq\sqrt{pd}.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (23)--(24) prove (6). Substitution of (3) and elementary
rearrangement prove the equivalence (7).

It remains to prove the transverse width barrier. By the endpoint
inequalities on \(P\) and \(C\), accepted fact 1ba5106642a3b8bd gives
the exact support extrema
\[
\min_{S_1}f_i=\frac{a_i^- -b_i^+}{2},
\qquad
\max_{S_1}f_i=\frac{a_i^+ -b_i^-}{2},
\tag{25}
\]
and the \(f_i\)-width of \(S_1\) is \(\Omega_i/2\). Also
\[
f_1\geq\frac{\eta_1}{2}\quad\hbox{on }S_1.
\]
Since \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\),
\[
f_2+f_3\leq-1-\frac{\eta_1}{2}
\quad\hbox{on }S_1.
\tag{26}
\]

If \(\theta_2=0\), formula (8) and (25) give
\(\min_{S_1}f_2\geq0\). Equation (26) then gives
\(\max_{S_1}f_3\leq-1-\eta_1/2<0\), so the clipped support fraction is
\(\theta_3=1\). Under (9), this is exactly (10). The same reasoning
handles \(\theta_3=0\). If one \(\theta_i=1\), condition (9) forces the
other one to be zero, so these are all exterior cases.

We may therefore assume \(0<\theta_2,\theta_3<1\). The clipping in (8)
is then inactive, and (25) becomes
\[
\min_{S_1}f_i=-\frac{\theta_i\Omega_i}{2},
\qquad
\max_{S_1}f_i=\frac{(1-\theta_i)\Omega_i}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]
Choose a point of \(S_1\) maximizing \(f_2\). At that point, (26) and
the lower support bound for \(f_3\) give
\[
-\min_{S_1}f_3
\geq
1+\frac{\eta_1}{2}+\max_{S_1}f_2.
\]
Substitution of (27) proves (12). Interchanging \(2\) and \(3\) proves
(13).

Substitute (12) into (13). Since \(\theta_3>0\), direct simplification
gives
\[
(\theta_2+\theta_3-1)\Omega_2\geq2+\eta_1.
\tag{28}
\]
The same argument with the indices interchanged gives
\[
(\theta_2+\theta_3-1)\Omega_3\geq2+\eta_1.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus \(\theta_2+\theta_3>1\), and the first inequality in (14)
follows. Under (9),
\[
\theta_2+\theta_3\leq\sqrt2.
\]
Since \((\sqrt2-1)^{-1}=1+\sqrt2\), the second inequality in (14)
follows. If (9) is strict, neither clipped value can equal \(1\), and
the exterior alternative is impossible.

Finally assume (15). The chain (22)--(24) forces equality everywhere:
\[
\mathcal V(P,C)=\sqrt{pd},\qquad
|S_1|+\lambda_1=A_1,
\qquad
D_0=U.
\tag{30}
\]
The equality case of planar Brunn--Minkowski for two-dimensional convex
bodies says that (16) holds exactly when \(P\) and \(C\) are positively
homothetic. Also,
\[
U-D_0
=\sum_{J\neq\varnothing}(|J|-1)p_J.
\]
Because \(P_{123}\) is empty, \(D_0=U\) says precisely that every
double \(P\)-cell has area zero. The bridge construction in accepted
fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 has
\[
|S_1\cap G_1|=0,\qquad |G_1|\geq\lambda_1.
\]
The middle equality in (30) therefore forces
\[
|G_1|=\lambda_1
\]
and forces the complement of \(S_1\cup G_1\) in
\(K\cap\{f_1\geq0\}\) to have area zero. This proves (17) and completes
the proof.
