## Statement

Assume the Alternative 1 cell data of accepted fact
`bc026780453747b8`, and use the southwest-anchor conclusion of accepted
fact `92a1b537d7e7876c`.  Put
\[
p=\frac{97}{100},\qquad c=\frac{3}{200},\qquad
r_-=\frac{87-3\sqrt{65}}{388}.
\]
Normalize the two full positive axis traces to length one and put
\[
A=a+1,\qquad B=b+1,
\]
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(a,0),(A,0),(W,Y),(X,H),(0,B),(0,b)\}.
\]
The rightmost and topmost listed points are distinct.  The order
constraints are
\[
a,b\geq0,\qquad A\leq X\leq W,\qquad B\leq Y\leq H,
\qquad (W-X)+(H-Y)>0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
For an actual normalized positive cell, the full-trace condition
further forces exactly one of the two alternatives
\[
a,b>0,\qquad\text{or}\qquad a=b=0.                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular, exactly one of \(a,b\) cannot vanish.

With the vertices in the displayed cyclic order, the six consecutive
cross products are
\[
Y,\quad
(W-A)(H-Y)+Y(W-X),\quad
(W-X)(H-B)+X(H-Y),\quad
X,\quad a,\quad b.                                         \tag{3}
\]
Convexity means that all quantities in (3) are nonnegative.  Under
(1) this is automatic, but the non-strict form records all collinear
support faces.  The only allowed coincident southwest vertices occur
when \(a=b=0\); coincidence of \((W,Y)\) and \((X,H)\) is excluded by
(1).

The normalized area and physical axis-scale product are
\[
T=|K|
=\frac{AY+WH-XY+XB-ab}{2}>0,\qquad
D=\frac{p}{T}.                                              \tag{4}
\]
Define the two outer endpoint slopes
\[
\alpha=\frac{W-A}{Y},\qquad
\beta=\frac{H-B}{X}.                                       \tag{5}
\]
Every actual support hexagon satisfies the following necessary
two-cap inequalities.

If \(a,b>0\), its southwest edge is supported by the line
\[
y+\tau x=b=\tau a,
\qquad\text{so necessarily}\qquad \tau=\frac ba>0,          \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
D\geq2c\left(\frac1\tau+\alpha\right),\qquad
D\geq2c(\tau+\beta).                                       \tag{7}
\]
If \(a=b=0\), the actual convex completion has a genuine supporting
line
\[
y+\tau x=0
\]
at the common origin for some finite \(\tau>0\), and (7) again holds
for that supporting-line slope.

For the elimination, enlarge the actual geometric system to the
relaxation in which \(a,b\geq0\) are arbitrary and \(\tau>0\) is a
free variable subject only to (7).  Every actual support hexagon
belongs to this relaxation, but tuples in the relaxation need not
have any geometric cap completion.  Within this relaxation alone,
the existential quantifier can be eliminated algebraically: with
\[
R=\frac{D}{2c}=\frac{100D}{3},
\]
there exists \(\tau>0\) satisfying (7) if and only if
\[
R>\alpha,\qquad R>\beta,\qquad
(R-\alpha)(R-\beta)\geq1.                                  \tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) is an equivalent description only of the relaxed
inequalities, not a converse support-hexagon realization theorem.

The inherited southwest-anchor constraint for an actual support
hexagon is
\[
D\left(T+\frac{ab}{2}\right)
=p+\frac{Dab}{2}
\leq\frac{9}{200r_-^2}.                                    \tag{9}
\]

The maximum horizontal chord is attained at a level in
\(\{0,b,B,Y,H\}\).  Put
\[
G=A+\frac{(W-A)B}{Y}=A+\alpha B.                           \tag{10}
\]
If \(H>B\), put
\[
J_x=W-\frac{X(Y-B)}{H-B}.                                 \tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
M_x=\max\{G,J_x\}\quad(H>B),\qquad
M_x=W=G\quad(H=B).                                        \tag{12}
\]
The chord lengths at \(y=b,B,Y\) are respectively
\[
A+\frac{(W-A)b}{Y},\qquad G,\qquad J_x.
\]
The first is at most \(G\), and the remaining vertex levels give no
larger value.  When \(b=0\), the levels \(0\) and \(b\) coincide and
the chord has length \(A\).  When \(Y=H>B\), (11) is the top-face
length \(W-X\).  When \(Y=B<H\), both (10) and (11) equal \(W\).

The maximum vertical chord is attained at a level in
\(\{0,a,A,X,W\}\).  Put
\[
F=B+\frac{(H-B)A}{X}=B+\beta A.                            \tag{13}
\]
If \(W>A\), put
\[
J_y=H-\frac{Y(X-A)}{W-A}.                                 \tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
M_y=\max\{F,J_y\}\quad(W>A),\qquad
M_y=H=F\quad(W=A).                                        \tag{15}
\]
When \(a=0\), the levels \(0\) and \(a\) coincide and the vertical
chord has length \(B\).  When \(X=W>A\), (14) is the right-face
length \(H-Y\).  When \(X=A<W\), both (13) and (14) equal \(H\).

Thus the nondegenerate region \(H>B,\ W>A\) has the four closed
maximum branches obtained by independently choosing \(G\) or \(J_x\)
in (12), and \(F\) or \(J_y\) in (15), with the corresponding weak
comparison.  Ties lie in both adjacent branches.  Equations (12) and
(15) also give every denominator-degenerate support-face stratum.

Every tuple in the enlarged relaxation (1), (4)-(8), (12), (15)
satisfies
\[
\frac{W}{M_x}+\frac{H}{M_y}\leq\frac{5000}{291}.            \tag{16}
\]
Consequently every actual support hexagon, which additionally obeys
(2), (6) or the origin-support condition, and (9), satisfies (16).

## Proof

First consider the incidence at the southwest trace endpoints.  If
\(b=0<a\), then the edge from \((0,b)=(0,0)\) to \((a,0)\) lies on
the positive \(x\)-axis.  Together with the nominal unit trace
\([(a,0),(A,0)]\), it makes the full positive \(x\)-axis trace
\([(0,0),(A,0)]\), of length \(A=a+1>1\), contradicting the
normalization.  The coordinate-swapped argument excludes
\(a=0<b\).  This proves (2).

If \(a,b>0\), the southwest edge joins two distinct axis points.  Its
line has the unique equation
\[
y+\frac ba x=b,
\]
so the common inner support parameter is geometrically fixed as
\(\tau=b/a\), proving (6).  If \(a=b=0\), the two adjacent mixed
cells have positive area, so the full convex body has points in both
open mixed quadrants.  Its southwest cell has zero area and hence no
point in the open southwest quadrant; otherwise convexity together
with an interior point would create positive southwest area.  A
supporting-line separation at the origin therefore gives a normal
with nonnegative coordinates.  Neither coordinate can vanish,
because a horizontal supporting line would exclude the positive-area
lower mixed cell and a vertical supporting line would exclude the
positive-area left mixed cell.  After normalizing the second normal
coordinate, the line is \(y+\tau x=0\) with \(0<\tau<\infty\).

For either actual incidence alternative, inspect the lower mixed cap.
The inner support line has slope \(-\tau\).  At the outer endpoint
\((A,0)\), the positive-cell edge toward \((W,Y)\) supplies the
outer support slope \(1/\alpha\), interpreted as vertical when
\(\alpha=0\).  Monotonicity of supporting slopes for a convex planar
body confines the normalized lower cap to the triangle between these
two support lines and its unit axis trace.  Direct intersection of
the two lines gives triangle area
\[
\frac{1}{2(\tau^{-1}+\alpha)}.
\]
The coordinate-swapped left-cap triangle has normalized area
\[
\frac{1}{2(\tau+\beta)}.
\]
Both physical cap areas equal \(c\), and normalized areas are
multiplied by \(D\).  Hence every actual support hexagon satisfies
(7).  This proves only necessity.  It does not construct caps from a
tuple satisfying (7).

For the purely algebraic relaxation, (7) implies
\[
R-\alpha\geq\frac1\tau>0,\qquad
R-\beta\geq\tau>0,
\]
and therefore (8).  Conversely, as a statement only about the two
relaxed inequalities, (8) permits the algebraic choice
\(\tau=1/(R-\alpha)\), which satisfies (7).  No geometric incidence
or cap realization follows from this algebraic converse.

The cross products in (3) follow from the six successive edge pairs,
and shoelace gives (4).  The area added to \(K\) by adjoining the
origin is exactly the possibly degenerate triangle with vertices
\[
(0,0),\quad(a,0),\quad(0,b),
\]
of area \(ab/2\).  This proves the anchor formula (9) from accepted
fact `92a1b537d7e7876c`.

Horizontal section length is piecewise affine between the vertex
levels \(0,b,B,Y,H\), so its maximum occurs at one of those levels.
Interpolation on the edges from \((A,0)\) to \((W,Y)\), and from
\((0,B)\) to \((X,H)\), gives (10)-(12).  The vertical argument gives
(13)-(15).  This proves the finite branch list, including every tie,
coincident-origin case, and horizontal or vertical support face.

It remains to prove (16) on the larger relaxation.  Put
\[
L=T(2+\alpha+\beta),\qquad q_x=\frac{G}{W}.
\]
The chord in (10) exists in every branch, so \(M_x\geq G\).  Also
\[
0<q_x\leq1,\qquad
1-q_x=\frac{\alpha(Y-B)}{W}.                               \tag{17}
\]
Since \(H\geq Y\) and \(X\leq W\),
\[
\beta=\frac{H-B}{X}\geq\frac{Y-B}{W},
\]
and hence
\[
\alpha\beta\geq1-q_x.                                     \tag{18}
\]

The area identity is
\[
2T=A+B-1+GY+FX-(1-\alpha\beta)XY.                          \tag{19}
\]
Moreover,
\[
F-(1-\alpha\beta)Y
=(H-Y)+\beta(W-X)\geq0.                                   \tag{20}
\]
Using \(X\geq A\) in (19), followed by
\[
G-A(1-\alpha\beta)=\alpha F,
\]
gives
\[
2T\geq A+B-1+FW.                                          \tag{21}
\]
Since \(A,B\geq1\), \(G\geq1+\alpha\), \(F\geq1+\beta\), and
\(W=G/q_x\), equation (21) yields
\[
2T\geq1+\frac{(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}{q_x}.                   \tag{22}
\]

Write \(s_x=\sqrt{1-q_x}\), so \(0\leq s_x<1\).  From (18) and
arithmetic-geometric mean,
\[
\alpha+\beta\geq2s_x,\qquad
(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)\geq(1+s_x)^2.
\]
Multiplying (22) by \((2+\alpha+\beta)/2\) gives
\[
L\geq\frac{2(1+s_x)}{1-s_x}.                              \tag{23}
\]
In particular \(L\geq2\), and (23) implies
\[
s_x\leq\frac{L-2}{L+2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
q_x=1-s_x^2\geq\frac{8L}{(L+2)^2}.                        \tag{24}
\]

The same proof after exchanging the two coordinates, using
\[
q_y=\frac{F}{H},\qquad
G-(1-\alpha\beta)X=(W-X)+\alpha(H-Y)\geq0,
\]
gives
\[
q_y\geq\frac{8L}{(L+2)^2}.                                \tag{25}
\]
Since \(M_x\geq G\) and \(M_y\geq F\), equations (24)-(25) imply
\[
\frac{W}{M_x}+\frac{H}{M_y}
\leq\frac{(L+2)^2}{4L}
=\frac{L}{4}+1+\frac1L.                                   \tag{26}
\]
This estimate uses neither \(J_x\) nor \(J_y\), so it covers all four
maximum-chord branches and their boundary ties simultaneously.

Adding the two relaxed cap inequalities (7) and using
\(\tau+\tau^{-1}\geq2\) gives
\[
D\geq c(2+\alpha+\beta).
\]
Together with \(DT=p\), this yields
\[
L\leq\frac{p}{c}=\frac{194}{3}.                            \tag{27}
\]
The function \(L/4+1+1/L\) is nondecreasing for \(L\geq2\).
Equations (26)-(27) therefore give
\[
\frac{W}{M_x}+\frac{H}{M_y}
\leq
\frac{194/3}{4}+1+\frac{1}{194/3}
=\frac{5000}{291}.
\]
This proves (16) on the relaxation.  The anchor (9), the fixed
incidence \(\tau=b/a\) when \(a,b>0\), and the origin-support
condition only shrink that relaxation, so no converse realization is
needed.
