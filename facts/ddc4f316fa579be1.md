## Statement

For a planar convex body \(K\), define
\[
d_K(z)=\inf\{|K\cap H|:H\text{ is a closed halfplane containing }z\},
\qquad
d^*(K)=\max_{z\in K}d_K(z).
\]
Define the affine-invariant depth excess and normalized symmetric-difference distance to triangles by
\[
e(K)=\frac{d^*(K)}{|K|}-\frac49,
\qquad
\sigma(K)=
\inf_{\substack{T\text{ a nondegenerate triangle}\\ |T|=|K|}}
\frac{|K\mathbin{\triangle}T|}{|K|}.
\]
Then \(e(K)\ge0\), and the function
\[
\omega(t)=
\sup\{\sigma(K):K\text{ is a planar convex body and }e(K)\le t\},
\qquad t\ge0,
\]
is finite, nondecreasing, and satisfies
\[
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\omega(t)=0.
\]
Consequently every planar convex body satisfies the precise affine-invariant stability estimate
\[
\sigma(K)\le\omega(e(K)).
\]

More concretely, fix
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}.
\]
For every \(K_n\), choose a maximum-area triangle \(Q_n\subseteq K_n\), and let \(A_n\) be any invertible affine map with \(A_n(Q_n)=\Delta\). If
\[
\frac{d^*(K_n)}{4|K_n|/9}\longrightarrow1,
\]
then
\[
A_n(K_n)\longrightarrow\Delta
\]
in Hausdorff distance. In particular, the requested conclusion holds without passing to a subsequence, and
\[
\sigma(K_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
No explicit numerical rate for \(\omega\) is asserted.

## Proof

Every closed halfplane containing \(z\) contains a parallel halfplane whose boundary passes through \(z\). Therefore, writing
\[
c(K,z,u)=
\left|K\cap\{x:u\cdot(x-z)\ge0\}\right|
\qquad(u\in\mathbb S^1),
\]
we have
\[
d_K(z)=\min_{u\in\mathbb S^1}c(K,z,u).
\tag{1}
\]
The minimum exists because the line \(u\cdot(x-z)=0\) has area zero and dominated convergence makes \(c(K,z,u)\) continuous in \((z,u)\). The same continuity and compactness of \(K\) show that the maximum defining \(d^*(K)\) exists.

We first prove the compact normalization. A maximum-area inscribed triangle exists because the area of a triangle with three vertices in \(K\) is continuous on \(K^3\), and it is nondegenerate because \(K\) has interior. Let \(Q\) be such a triangle and let \(A(Q)=\Delta\). Write \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) for the barycentric coordinates of \(\Delta\). For \(x\in A(K)\), replacing the \(i\)-th vertex of \(\Delta\) by \(x\) gives an inscribed triangle of area
\[
|\beta_i(x)|\,|\Delta|.
\]
Maximality of \(\Delta\) therefore gives
\[
\Delta\subseteq A(K)\subseteq
P:=\{x:|\beta_i(x)|\le1\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The set \(P\) is fixed and compact. Thus all bodies in this normalization lie in one compact set and contain the fixed body \(\Delta\).

For completeness, this normalized family is sequentially compact in Hausdorff distance. Indeed, its support functions on \(\mathbb S^1\) are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous because of (2). Arzelà-Ascoli gives a uniformly convergent subsequence, and the uniform limit is the support function of a compact convex body \(C\) with
\[
\Delta\subseteq C\subseteq P.
\tag{3}
\]
Uniform convergence of support functions is exactly Hausdorff convergence here.

We next prove continuity of \(d^*\) on this family. Suppose \(C_n\to C\) in Hausdorff distance, \(z_n\to z\), and \(u_n\to u\). Outside the union of \(\partial C\) and the line \(u\cdot(x-z)=0\), both membership in \(C_n\) and the sign of \(u_n\cdot(x-z_n)\) eventually stabilize. Those exceptional sets have planar area zero, and all integrands are supported in \(P\). Dominated convergence gives
\[
c(C_n,z_n,u_n)\longrightarrow c(C,z,u).
\tag{4}
\]
It also gives \(|C_n|\to|C|\).

Choose \(z_n\) maximizing \(d_{C_n}\), pass to a subsequence with \(z_n\to z\in C\), and let \(u\) minimize \(c(C,z,\cdot)\). Equations (1) and (4) give
\[
\limsup_n d^*(C_n)
\le c(C,z,u)
=d_C(z)
\le d^*(C).
\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, let \(z\) maximize \(d_C\), choose \(z_n\in C_n\) with \(z_n\to z\), and let \(u_n\) minimize \(c(C_n,z_n,\cdot)\). After taking a subsequence \(u_n\to u\), equations (1) and (4) give
\[
\liminf_n d^*(C_n)
\ge c(C,z,u)
\ge d_C(z)
=d^*(C).
\tag{6}
\]
Applying (5)-(6) along subsequences realizing the limsup and liminf proves
\[
d^*(C_n)\longrightarrow d^*(C).
\tag{7}
\]

We record the equality classification needed at the compact limit. The planar Grünbaum inequality used in accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` gives, for the centroid \(g\) of every planar convex body,
\[
d_K(g)\ge\frac49|K|.
\tag{8}
\]
Hence \(e(K)\ge0\). If \(d^*(K)=4|K|/9\), then (8) forces
\[
d_K(g)=\frac49|K|.
\]
By (1), some centroidal halfplane attains this value. Translate \(g\) to the origin and apply the exact equality clause of the primary-source theorem stated under EXTERNAL SOURCES. It says that \(K\) is the convex hull of a nondegenerate segment and one point, hence a nondegenerate triangle.

Conversely, let \(K=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\) be a triangle. For any \(z\in K\), one barycentric coordinate, say \(\beta_i(z)\), is at least \(1/3\). The halfplane
\[
\{x:\beta_i(x)\ge\beta_i(z)\}
\]
contains \(z\), and its intersection with \(K\) is a vertex triangle of area
\[
(1-\beta_i(z))^2|K|\le\frac49|K|.
\]
Together with (8) at the centroid, this proves the exact classification
\[
d^*(K)=\frac49|K|
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K\text{ is a nondegenerate triangle}.
\tag{9}
\]

Now assume
\[
\frac{d^*(K_n)}{4|K_n|/9}\to1
\]
and put \(C_n=A_n(K_n)\). Halfplanes are carried bijectively to halfplanes by an invertible affine map, while every area is multiplied by the same absolute determinant. Thus the displayed ratio, \(e\), and \(\sigma\) are affine invariant.

By (2), every subsequence of \(C_n\) has a further Hausdorff-convergent subsequence, say \(C_{n_j}\to C\), satisfying (3). Equations (7), area continuity, and affine invariance give
\[
\frac{d^*(C)}{|C|}=\frac49.
\]
Equation (9) makes \(C\) a triangle.

The maximum-area property also passes to the limit. Given any three points of \(C\), choose convergent approximating points in \(C_{n_j}\). The corresponding triangle areas converge and are at most \(|\Delta|\), because \(\Delta\) is a maximum-area triangle in every \(C_{n_j}\). Hence every triangle contained in \(C\) has area at most \(|\Delta|\). Since \(C\) itself is a triangle and \(\Delta\subseteq C\), we obtain
\[
|C|\le|\Delta|\le|C|,
\]
so \(C=\Delta\). Every convergent subsequence has the same limit \(\Delta\). The precompactness already proved therefore yields the full-sequence conclusion
\[
C_n\to\Delta
\quad\text{in Hausdorff distance}.
\tag{10}
\]

To deduce normalized symmetric-difference convergence, let
\[
\lambda_n=\sqrt{\frac{|C_n|}{|\Delta|}}
\]
and let \(T_n\) be the image of \(\Delta\) under the homothety of ratio \(\lambda_n\) about its centroid. By (10) and area continuity, \(\lambda_n\to1\), \(T_n\to\Delta\), and \(|T_n|=|C_n|\). Indicator functions of convergent convex bodies converge almost everywhere away from their null boundaries, so dominated convergence gives
\[
|C_n\mathbin{\triangle}T_n|\to0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sigma(K_n)=\sigma(C_n)
\le\frac{|C_n\mathbin{\triangle}T_n|}{|C_n|}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

Finally, \(0\le\sigma(K)\le2\), so \(\omega\) is finite, and it is nondecreasing by definition. If \(\omega(t)\) did not tend to zero as \(t\downarrow0\), there would be an \(\varepsilon>0\), numbers \(t_n\downarrow0\), and convex bodies \(K_n\) such that
\[
e(K_n)\le t_n,
\qquad
\sigma(K_n)\ge\varepsilon.
\]
But \(e(K_n)\to0\) is equivalent to
\[
\frac{d^*(K_n)}{4|K_n|/9}\to1,
\]
and (11) then gives \(\sigma(K_n)\to0\), a contradiction. This proves \(\omega(t)\to0\) and completes the stability theorem.

The exact specialization used above is \(n=k=2\), \(E=\mathbb R^2\): if a planar convex body \(K\) has centroid \(0\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb S^1\), then
\[
\frac{|K\cap\{x:\langle x,\theta\rangle\ge0\}|}{|K|}
\ge\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2=\frac49.
\]
Equality holds if and only if
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}(-z/2+D,z),
\]
where \(z\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfies \(\langle z,\theta\rangle>0\) and \(D\) is a one-dimensional convex body in \(\theta^\perp\), hence a nondegenerate segment. Because the centroid here is \(0\), the midpoint of \(D\) is \(0\). Thus the equality bodies in this specialization are exactly nondegenerate triangles with the selected centroidal cap pointing toward \(z\). In the proof above, \(K\) is a planar convex body, its centroid is translated to \(0\), and the minimizing direction from (1) is the theorem's \(\theta\), so every hypothesis is met. The source supplies the sharp constant and equality classification; the Hausdorff and symmetric-difference stability conclusions and the compactness modulus \(\omega\) are proved above, not quoted from the source.
