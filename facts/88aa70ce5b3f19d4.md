## Statement

Assume the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses, exact-cell convention, \(M\ge m\), the three crossing equalities, the canonical left and right tails, the all-double positive \(Q\)-support
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
and \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\).  Put
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
\frac{x_1}{q}=\frac{x_2}{q}=\frac{x_3}{q}=t,
\qquad 0<t<\frac25,
\qquad D=\frac q{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
Let
\[
\tau(3t)=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{1+3t}+\sqrt{204+360t}\right)^2},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm def}(t)=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{1+3t}+\sqrt{218+402t}\right)^2},
\]
and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm sym}(z)={}&
552172816z^8+1111566016z^7+678285856z^6
-38662016z^5\\
&-192489704z^4-56844944z^3
+7084600z^2+5664976z+707281.
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm def}(z)={}&
842163088z^8+1636975232z^7+952280192z^6
-78168928z^5\\
&-272579928z^4-75696352z^3
+10579696z^2+7634184z+923521.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are unique roots
\[
\alpha,\beta\in\left(\frac{11}{30},\frac38\right)
\]
of \(P_{\rm sym}\) and \(P_{\rm def}\), respectively.  They satisfy the
exact strict ordering
\[
\beta<\frac{169}{454}<\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]
The number \(\alpha\) is the unique solution in
\((1/(2\sqrt2),2/5)\) of
\[
1-
\left(
\frac{\sqrt{3+1/t}-2t}{1+2t}
\right)^2
=\tau(3t).
\tag{2}
\]
The number \(\beta\) is the unique solution on the same interval of
\[
1-
\left(
\frac{\sqrt{3+1/t}-2t}{1+2t}
\right)^2
=\tau_{\rm def}(t).
\tag{3}
\]

The determinant lower bound \(\delta\) of accepted fact
`6aa12d1b76699246` has the exact symmetric specialization
\[
\delta(t,t,t)=
\begin{cases}
0,
&0<t\le 1/(2\sqrt2),\\[1mm]
1-\left(
\dfrac{\sqrt{3+1/t}-2t}{1+2t}
\right)^2,
&1/(2\sqrt2)<t<2/5.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
The positive-middle defect of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31`
specializes to
\[
\epsilon(t,t,t)=\frac{1+3t}{2}>0,
\tag{5}
\]
and its strengthened threshold is exactly \(\tau_{\rm def}(t)\) above.
Consequently,
\[
\delta(t,t,t)\ge\tau(3t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha\le t<\frac25.
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\delta(t,t,t)\ge\tau_{\rm def}(t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta\le t<\frac25.
\tag{7}
\]
No full common-plane realization with \(W=0\) exists on the interval in
(7), including \(t=\beta\).  The strip
\[
\beta\le t<\alpha
\]
is closed by the positive-middle defect but survives the older
\(\delta\)-versus-\(\tau\) test.  On the complementary lower interval
\[
0<t<\beta,
\]
one has \(\delta(t,t,t)<\tau_{\rm def}(t)<\tau(3t)\), so neither accepted
closure criterion decides existence.  This is a criterion-survival
statement, not a realization claim.  All coincident-endpoint,
collinear-label, redundant vertex, and exact-cell boundary strata are
included.

## Proof

On the symmetric line, the quantities of accepted fact
`6aa12d1b76699246` become
\[
A_k=3+\frac1t,\qquad c_k=2t,\qquad d_0=t,
\]
\[
d_{1k}
=\frac{16t^3(1+3t)}{(1+3t+4t^3)^2},
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
d_{2k}
=1-\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{\sqrt{3+1/t}-2t}{1+2t}
\right)^2.
\tag{9}
\]
All three values in each indexed family coincide.

Put
\[
a=\frac1{2\sqrt2},\qquad
z(t)=\frac{\sqrt{3+1/t}-2t}{1+2t}.
\]
For \(0<t<2/5\), the numerator of \(z(t)\) is positive.  Moreover,
\[
z(t)\ge1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{3+1/t}\ge1+4t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(1+2t)(1-8t^2)\ge0.
\]
Thus \(d_{2k}=0\) exactly for \(0<t\le a\), proving the first line of
(4).  On \(a<t<2/5\), one has \(0<z(t)<1\).  Its numerator is strictly
decreasing and positive while its denominator is strictly increasing, so
\(z(t)\) is strictly decreasing.  Hence
\[
d_2(t):=1-z(t)^2
\]
is strictly increasing on this interval.

The expression in (8) can be written as
\[
d_1(t)=\frac{4y(t)}{(1+y(t))^2},
\qquad
y(t)=\frac{4t^3}{1+3t}.
\]
Here \(y(t)\) is strictly increasing and \(0<y(t)<1\) for \(0<t<2/5\);
therefore \(d_1(t)\) is also strictly increasing.  Direct endpoint
calculation gives
\[
d_2(2/5)=\frac{40\sqrt{22}-145}{162}<\frac9{32},
\]
\[
\frac9{32}<a,
\qquad
d_1(a)=\frac{152-48\sqrt2}{289}>\frac9{32}.
\tag{10}
\]
The first strict inequality follows from
\[
3049^2=9296401>9011200=640^2\cdot22,
\]
the middle one from \(81<128\), and the last one from
\[
2263^2=5121169>4718592=2\cdot1536^2.
\]
Monotonicity and (10) imply, for every \(a<t<2/5\),
\[
d_2(t)<\frac9{32}<\min\{t,d_1(t)\}.
\]
Thus \(d_2\) is the minimum of all seven terms defining \(\delta\), and
the second line of (4) follows.

We next derive the original algebraic crossing.  Introduce a variable
\(y\).  The
two roots of
\[
Q_{\rm sym}(t,y):=
(203+357t)^2y^2
-98(205+363t)y+2401
\tag{11}
\]
are
\[
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{204+360t}+\sqrt{1+3t}\right)^2}
=\tau(3t)
\]
and
\[
\tau_+(t):=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{204+360t}-\sqrt{1+3t}\right)^2}.
\]
On \(a<t<2/5\), the actual value \(y=d_2(t)\) is the unique positive
root of
\[
E(t,y):=
\left[t(2t+1)^2y-(4t^2-2t-1)\right]^2
-16t^3(1+3t).
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, the other root of (12), viewed as a quadratic in \(y\), is
\[
\frac{4t^2-2t-1-4t\sqrt{t(1+3t)}}{t(2t+1)^2}<0.
\]

Exact elimination of \(y\) from (11) and (12) gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}_y(E,Q_{\rm sym})
=2401(2t+1)^4P_{\rm sym}(t).
\tag{13}
\]
We now isolate the relevant root without accepting a spurious radical
branch.  On \(a<t<3/8\), monotonicity gives
\[
d_2(t)<d_2(3/8)
=\frac{24\sqrt{51}-152}{147}
<\frac2{15}.
\tag{14}
\]
The last inequality is equivalent, after squaring positive quantities, to
\[
183600=60^2\cdot51<429^2=184041.
\]
On the same interval,
\[
\tau_+(t)>
\frac{49}{204+360t}>
\frac{49}{339}>
\frac2{15}.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore any root of \(P_{\rm sym}\) in \((a,3/8)\) makes the common
positive root in (11) and (12) equal to \(d_2(t)=\tau(3t)\), never to
\(\tau_+(t)\).

Direct exact substitution gives
\[
P_{\rm sym}(11/30)
=\frac{750853195863961}{41006250000}>0,
\]
\[
P_{\rm sym}(3/8)
=-\frac{1732139759}{1048576}<0.
\tag{16}
\]
Also \(a<11/30\), since \(15\sqrt2<22\).  Equations (13)-(16) and the
intermediate value theorem give a solution of (2) in
\((11/30,3/8)\).

Finally, \(d_2(t)\) is strictly increasing on \((a,2/5)\), whereas
\(\tau(3t)\) is strictly decreasing because both square roots in its
denominator are strictly increasing.  Hence (2) has at most one solution
on that whole interval.  This proves that the root \(\alpha\) isolated in
(16) is unique, and it also proves
\[
d_2(t)<\tau(3t)\quad(a<t<\alpha),
\qquad
d_2(t)\ge\tau(3t)\quad(\alpha\le t<2/5).
\tag{17}
\]
Together with \(\delta=0<\tau(3t)\) on \(0<t\le a\), this proves (6).

We now retain the positive-middle defect.  For equal ratios, the eight
values in the finite minimum defining \(g\) in accepted fact
`0009896ebff12f31`, grouped by \(|S|=0,1,2,3\), are
\[
6t,\qquad 4t,\qquad 3t,\qquad 3t.
\]
Hence
\[
g(t,t,t)=3t,\qquad
\frac{1-3t}{2}+g(t,t,t)=\frac{1+3t}{2}.
\]
For \(0<t<2/5\), the last quantity lies strictly between \(0\) and
\(3/2\).  Formula (2) of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` therefore gives
\[
\epsilon(t,t,t)=\frac{1+3t}{2},
\]
which proves (5), and its threshold (7) becomes exactly
\(\tau_{\rm def}(t)\) in the statement.

The two roots of
\[
Q_{\rm def}(t,y):=
(217+399t)^2y^2
-98(219+405t)y+2401
\tag{18}
\]
are \(\tau_{\rm def}(t)\) and
\[
\tau_{{\rm def},+}(t)=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{218+402t}-\sqrt{1+3t}\right)^2}.
\]
Eliminating \(y\) from (12) and (18) gives the exact identity
\[
\operatorname{Res}_y(E,Q_{\rm def})
=2401(2t+1)^4P_{\rm def}(t).
\tag{19}
\]
Both quadratics in each resultant calculation have nonzero leading
coefficient for \(t>0\), so vanishing of the resultant is equivalent to
the existence of a common root.

The second positive root in (18) is again separated from \(d_2\) on the
isolating interval.  Indeed, for \(a<t<3/8\),
\[
d_2(t)<d_2(3/8)
=\frac{24\sqrt{51}-152}{147}
<\frac{196}{1475},
\tag{20}
\]
where the last comparison follows, after squaring positive quantities,
from
\[
8850^2\cdot51=3994447500<4000942009=63253^2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\tau_{{\rm def},+}(t)>
\frac{49}{218+402t}>
\frac{196}{1475}.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus any root of \(P_{\rm def}\) in \((a,3/8)\) corresponds to
\(d_2(t)=\tau_{\rm def}(t)\), not to the other positive root in (18).

Direct exact substitution gives
\[
P_{\rm def}(11/30)
=\frac{674225547440593}{41006250000}>0,
\qquad
P_{\rm def}(3/8)
=-\frac{3238588775}{1048576}<0.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (19)-(22) give a solution of (3) in
\((11/30,3/8)\).  The function \(\tau_{\rm def}(t)\) is strictly
decreasing, so strict increase of \(d_2\) makes this solution unique.
Therefore
\[
d_2(t)<\tau_{\rm def}(t)\quad(a<t<\beta),
\qquad
d_2(t)\ge\tau_{\rm def}(t)\quad(\beta\le t<2/5).
\tag{23}
\]
Together with \(\delta=0<\tau_{\rm def}(t)\) for \(0<t\le a\), this
proves (7).

The two thresholds are strictly distinct.  At
\[
t_0=\frac{169}{454},
\]
accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` gives the exact chain
\[
\tau_{\rm def}(t_0)
<\frac8{69}
<\delta(t_0)
<\frac5{43}
<\tau(3t_0).
\tag{24}
\]
The point \(t_0\) lies in \((a,2/5)\): the two comparisons follow from
\[
676^2>2\cdot454^2,\qquad 5\cdot169<2\cdot454.
\]
Since both differences in
(17) and (23) are strictly increasing, (24) implies
\[
\beta<t_0<\alpha,
\]
which proves (1).

For completeness, the source-cap constraints of accepted fact
`8234d1112ec2f031` do not shorten this symmetric interval.  They reduce to
\[
1+3t\ge4t^3,
\qquad
3\sqrt{\frac{t}{1+3t}}\le\frac32.
\]
Both are strict for \(0<t<2/5\): the first follows from
\[
1+3t-4t^3=(1-t)(4t^2+4t+1)>0,
\]
and the second is equivalent to \(t<1\).

Accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7`, with
\(U=|\mathcal T_Q|=q/D\), gives under \(W=0\) the strict inequality
\[
D<\tau(3t).
\]
Equivalently, accepted fact `c44e67089f10e853` states that
\(\delta\ge\tau\) excludes \(W=0\), including equality and all geometric
boundary strata.  More strongly, accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31`
gives under \(W=0\)
\[
D<\tau_{\rm def}(t)
\]
and excludes equality \(\delta=\tau_{\rm def}\).  Applying it to (7)
proves the claimed exclusion for \(\beta\le t<2/5\).  Accepted fact
`8722d6b6d252a99a` excludes the remaining symmetric range \(t\ge2/5\).
Thus the entire symmetric ray \(t\ge\beta\) is closed, while the exact
interval left open by the strengthened accepted criterion is
\(0<t<\beta\).  The boundary statements in accepted facts
`0009896ebff12f31`, `c44e67089f10e853`, and `8722d6b6d252a99a` include
all coincident-endpoint, collinear, redundant-vertex, and exact-cell
boundary strata used here.
