## Statement

Let \(B\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body of
area \(S\), let \(O\in\operatorname{int}B\), and let \(g_1,g_2\) be
nonparallel linear forms vanishing at \(O\).  Put
\[
b_1=|B\cap\{g_1\le0\}|,\qquad
b_2=|B\cap\{g_2\le0\}|,\qquad
b_3=|B\cap\{g_1+g_2\ge0\}|.
\]
Then
\[
b_1,b_2<\frac{447S}{1000}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b_3>\frac{79S}{180}.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac S4<b_3<\frac{79S}{180},\qquad b_1,b_2<\frac S2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max\{b_1,b_2\}\ge\frac{447S}{1000}.
\]

## Proof

Translate \(O\) to the origin and set
\[
\ell_1=g_1,\qquad \ell_2=g_2,\qquad
\ell_3=-(g_1+g_2),\qquad H_i=\{\ell_i\le0\}.
\]
The three forms are nonzero and pairwise nonparallel, and
\(\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0\).  Areas will first be normalized to one.
Write
\[
M=\frac{447}{1000},\qquad Q=\frac{79}{180}.
\tag{2}
\]
For an area-one body \(K\), put
\[
p_i(K)=|K\cap H_i|.
\]

We first record the exact noncentered consequence needed for closedness
of the extremal problem.

**Noncentered side-cap lemma.**  If \(K\) is an area-one convex body,
\(O\notin\operatorname{int}K\), and \(p_1,p_2\le1/2\), then
\[
p_3\ge1-p_1\quad\hbox{or}\quad p_3\ge1-p_2.
\tag{3}
\]

Use the six sectors \(A,\ldots,F\) in cyclic order from accepted fact
`44d54af9bf8d0bd5`; their areas will be denoted by the corresponding
lower-case letters.  Thus
\[
p_1=a+e+f,\qquad p_2=a+b+c,\qquad p_3=c+d+e.
\tag{4}
\]
The proof of that accepted fact gives \(bf=0\), but we recall the short
argument because the stronger disjunction (3), rather than only the
stated product consequence of that fact, is used here.  The two cap
bounds give \(a\le d\).  A supporting separator through \(O\) is
nonnegative on \(K\) and strictly positive on its interior.  If both
\(b\) and \(f\) were positive, choose interior points in \(B\) and
\(F\).  If \(d>0\), an interior point in \(D\), together with those two
points, has the origin as a positive convex combination, contradicting
strict positivity of the separator.  Hence \(d=0\).  The segment between
the chosen points in \(B\) and \(F\) has a nonempty subsegment in \(A\),
so \(a>0\), contradicting \(a\le d\).  Thus \(bf=0\).  If \(b=0\), then
\[
p_3=c+d+e\ge c+d=1-p_1,
\]
and if \(f=0\), then
\[
p_3=c+d+e\ge d+e=1-p_2.
\]
This proves (3), including \(O\notin K\), \(O\in\partial K\), and every
supporting-ray degeneration.

We also record the strictification of the equality \(p_3=Q\).  Let \(K\)
be any area-one convex body with \(O\in\operatorname{int}K\),
\(p_1,p_2<M\), and \(p_3=Q\).  Choose a vector \(v\) with
\(\ell_3(v)>0\), and for \(t>0\) put
\[
K_t=K+t v.
\]
Translation preserves area, and the change of variables \(x=y+t v\)
gives the explicitly signed threshold identity
\[
p_3(K_t)
=\left|K\cap\{\ell_3\le-t\ell_3(v)\}\right|
<\left|K\cap\{\ell_3\le0\}\right|
=p_3(K).
\]
The inequality is strict for every sufficiently small \(t>0\): a ball
about \(O\) lies in \(K\), so the section of \(K\) on \(\ell_3=0\) has
positive length and the slab
\[
K\cap\{-t\ell_3(v)<\ell_3\le0\}
\]
has positive area.  Also
\[
p_i(K_t)
=\left|K\cap\{\ell_i\le-t\ell_i(v)\}\right|
\longrightarrow p_i(K)\qquad(i=1,2),
\]
so the two strict inequalities \(p_1,p_2<M\) persist for all sufficiently
small \(t\).  Finally,
\[
O\in\operatorname{int}K_t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
-t v\in\operatorname{int}K,
\]
which also holds for all sufficiently small \(t\).  Thus every
\(p_3=Q\) configuration under the strict side-cap bounds translates to
a configuration with the same area, the fan center still in the
interior, and \(p_3<Q\).  For a polygon, the translation also preserves
its number of vertices.

We next prove (1) for polygons by a genuine attained extremal argument.
Suppose an area-one convex polygon \(\widetilde K_0\), with \(O\) in its
interior, satisfies
\[
p_1(\widetilde K_0),p_2(\widetilde K_0)<M,\qquad
p_3(\widetilde K_0)\le Q.
\]
If equality holds in the last inequality, apply the preceding
strictification and replace \(\widetilde K_0\) by one sufficiently small
translate.  Renaming the resulting polygon \(K_0\), we may therefore
assume
\[
p_1(K_0),p_2(K_0)<M,\qquad p_3(K_0)<Q.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(N\) be its number of genuine vertices and define, for
\(0\le t<M\),
\[
\phi(t)=\frac{t}{M-t}.
\]
Choose \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small that
\[
\mathcal F(K_0):=p_3(K_0)-Q+
\eta\bigl(\phi(p_1(K_0))+\phi(p_2(K_0))\bigr)<0.
\tag{6}
\]
Minimize
\[
\mathcal F(K)=p_3(K)-Q+
\eta\bigl(\phi(p_1(K))+\phi(p_2(K))\bigr)
\tag{7}
\]
over all area-one convex polygons with at most \(N\) vertices and
\(p_1,p_2<M\).

We now justify that this minimum is attained.  Put
\[
c=\mathcal F(K_0)<0
\]
and restrict to the nonempty sublevel \(\mathcal F\le c\).  Since the
barrier is nonnegative,
\[
p_3\le Q+c=:\overline Q<Q.
\tag{8}
\]
Also \(p_3\ge0\), so
\[
\eta\bigl(\phi(p_1)+\phi(p_2)\bigr)\le Q+c.
\]
Consequently there is a number \(\overline M<M\), depending only on this
sublevel, such that
\[
p_1,p_2\le\overline M.
\tag{9}
\]
Notice that
\[
\overline Q<Q<1-M<1-\overline M.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (3), (8), and (9) show that every polygon in the sublevel has
\[
O\in\operatorname{int}K.
\tag{11}
\]

The same sublevel has uniformly bounded diameter.  Here is the full
coercivity argument.  Otherwise choose a sequence \(K_n\) in the
sublevel with diameters \(D_n\to\infty\), and orient a diameter by a
unit vector \(v_n\).  After passing to a subsequence, \(v_n\to v\).
The bodies \(D_n^{-1}K_n\) have diameter one, contain \(O\), and have
area \(D_n^{-2}\), so a Hausdorff subsequence converges to a nondegenerate
segment parallel to \(v\).

Use the longitudinal coordinate
\[
s=\frac{z\mathbin{\cdot}v_n}{D_n}.
\]
Its projection interval has length one and contains zero.  If \(w_n(s)\)
is the length of the perpendicular section of \(K_n\) at longitudinal
coordinate \(D_ns\), then
\[
f_n(s)=D_nw_n(s)
\]
is a nonnegative concave probability density on that interval.
If \(A_n=\max f_n\), the two chords from a maximizer to the endpoints
enclose a triangle below the graph of area \(A_n/2\).  Hence
\[
0\le f_n\le2.
\tag{12}
\]
After another subsequence, the \(f_n\)-mass of \(s\le0\) converges to
some \(\alpha\in[0,1]\).  The uniform bound (12) makes the mass of
\(|s|\le\varepsilon\) at most \(4\varepsilon\).

For every form \(\ell_i\) with \(\ell_i(v)\ne0\), the transverse width
of \(D_n^{-1}K_n\) tends to zero.  Away from \(|s|\le\varepsilon\), the
sign of \(\ell_i\) therefore agrees, for all large \(n\), with the sign
of \(s\ell_i(v)\).  Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) shows that the limiting \(i\)-th cap
fraction is \(\alpha\) or \(1-\alpha\), according to the sign of
\(\ell_i(v)\).  At most one \(\ell_i(v)\) can vanish, because the forms
are pairwise nonparallel.

If none vanishes and \(\ell_1(v),\ell_2(v)\) have the same sign, then
\(\ell_3(v)\) has the opposite sign, so in the limit
\[
p_3=1-p_1=1-p_2\ge1-\overline M>\overline Q,
\]
contrary to (8).  If \(\ell_1(v),\ell_2(v)\) have opposite signs, then
in the limit \(p_1+p_2=1\), contrary to
\(p_1+p_2\le2\overline M<1\).  If \(\ell_1(v)=0\), then
\(\ell_2(v)=-\ell_3(v)\ne0\), so the limiting relation is
\(p_2+p_3=1\), again giving
\[
p_3\ge1-\overline M>\overline Q.
\]
The case \(\ell_2(v)=0\) is symmetric.  Finally, if
\(\ell_3(v)=0\), then \(\ell_1(v)=-\ell_2(v)\ne0\), and the limiting
relation \(p_1+p_2=1\) is impossible.  Every segment direction,
including collapse along a fan line and a segment having \(O\) as an
endpoint, has now been covered.  This proves the uniform diameter
bound.

By (11), the bounded-diameter sublevel lies in a fixed ball about \(O\).
The class of its area-one polygons with at most \(N\) vertices is
Hausdorff closed: write each polygon as the convex hull of \(N\) points,
allowing repetitions and collinearities.  Area and the three fixed
halfplane-cut areas are continuous under bounded Hausdorff convergence
of two-dimensional convex bodies.  The barrier bound keeps the limit
strictly below \(M\), and (3), (8), and (10) keep \(O\) in the interior
of the limit.  Hence the sublevel is compact and (7) has a minimizer
\(P\).  Delete repeated and collinear vertices; the remaining genuine
polygon still has area one and satisfies
\[
\mathcal F(P)\le c<0,\qquad
p_1(P),p_2(P)<M,\qquad p_3(P)<Q,\qquad
O\in\operatorname{int}P.
\tag{13}
\]

We next verify every variation needed to reduce \(P\) to a finite
extremal class.  Small arbitrary motions of its genuine vertices
preserve strict convexity and \(O\in\operatorname{int}P\).  Rescaling
about \(O\) restores area one and leaves all three cap fractions
unchanged.  Thus the first derivative of \(\mathcal F\) vanishes for
every vertex velocity.  This remains true when a vertex lies on a fan
ray: no edge can lie on a fan line because \(O\) is interior, and moving
a ray vertex by \(O(t)\) changes the two possible sector descriptions
only in a triangle of area \(O(t^2)\).  Hence all six sector areas have
the same first derivative from both adjacent strata.

Let \(h_i\in\mathbb R^6\) be the sector-indicator vector of \(H_i\), let
\(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), and put
\[
u_i=h_i-p_i\mathbf1.
\]
The stationary sector weight is
\[
\lambda=u_3+\alpha u_1+\beta u_2,
\qquad
\alpha=\eta\phi'(p_1)>0,\qquad
\beta=\eta\phi'(p_2)>0.
\tag{14}
\]
If
\[
m=p_3+\alpha p_1+\beta p_2,
\]
then in cyclic sector order \(A,B,C,D,E,F\),
\[
\lambda=
(\alpha+\beta-m,\ \beta-m,\ 1+\beta-m,\
1-m,\ 1+\alpha-m,\ \alpha-m).
\tag{15}
\]
Every cyclic adjacent difference in (15) is, up to sign, one of
\(\alpha,\beta,1\).  Therefore no two adjacent sector weights vanish.

We now apply the exact fan-ray lemma proved inside accepted fact
`b0c2275528e42b65`.  Its statement is: a centered convex polygon with
a vertex on a fan ray cannot have a nonzero weight in
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,h_1,h_2,h_3\}\) which satisfies the two
swept-boundary moment equations on every edge and has no two adjacent
zero sector weights.  The proof of that accepted lemma enumerates all
crossing counts \(0,1,2,3\), all fan-ray vertices, and the closed
rank-five \(3+3\) degeneration.  Stationarity above gives its two edge
moment equations, (14) places \(\lambda\) in the required span, and
(15) gives both nonzeroness and the adjacent-zero hypothesis.
Consequently \(P\) has no vertex on a fan ray.

Since \(O\) is interior, each of the six fan rays now meets the relative
interior of an edge transversally.  In this smooth stratum, every nearby
polygon with \(p_1,p_2\) fixed has the same two barrier terms.  Local
minimality of \(\mathcal F\) therefore makes \(P\) a local minimizer of
\(p_3\) subject to fixed \(p_1,p_2\).  All hypotheses of accepted fact
`1b4ea45680c75628` are now verified.  That fact excludes the full-rank
strata and the nontriangular rank-five \(3+3\) class, and yields
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{V_1,V_2,V_3\},
\qquad
V_1\in F,\quad V_2\in B,\quad V_3\in D.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the extremizer is a fully asymmetric singleton-sector triangle.
Vertex collisions were already included in compactness, ray vertices
were excluded by the closed fan-ray lemma, and an edge on a fan line is
impossible for a centered body.  Hence no six-sector boundary class has
been omitted.

It remains to prove the required numerical bound for every triangle
in (16).  Let
\[
(u,v,w)
\]
be the positive barycentric coordinates of \(O\) with respect to
\((V_1,V_2,V_3)\).  They are arbitrary positive numbers with
\(u+v+w=1\); no equality between them is assumed.  We use the exact
corner-cap calculation from accepted fact `d6a43e362072a4df`.
If the \(i\)-th fan line cuts off \(V_i\), and the other two
barycentric coordinates are \(r,s\), its cap fraction is at least
\[
\mu(r,s)=
\begin{cases}
4rs,&r,s\le1/2,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\frac{s}{1-r},&r>1/2,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle\frac{r}{1-s},&s>1/2.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]
For clarity, (17) follows by writing the two edge-intersection fractions
as \(r/t\) and \(s/(1-t)\); the corner area is
\(rs/[t(1-t)]\), and \(t(1-t)\) is maximized on
\([r,1-s]\).  The endpoint values in (17) also cover a vertex on a fan
ray or an edge-intersection fraction equal to one.

Suppose first that \(u>1/2\).  Formula (17) gives
\[
p_2\ge\frac{w}{v+w},\qquad
p_3\ge\frac{v}{v+w}.
\]
Hence
\[
p_3\ge1-\frac{w}{v+w}>1-M>Q.
\tag{18}
\]
The case \(v>1/2\) is symmetric.  If \(w>1/2\), then (17) gives
\[
p_1\ge\frac{v}{u+v},\qquad
p_2\ge\frac{u}{u+v},
\]
so \(p_1+p_2\ge1\), contrary to \(p_1,p_2<M<1/2\).

We may therefore assume
\[
u,v,w\le\frac12.
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
x=\max\{u,v\},\qquad y=\min\{u,v\}.
\]
Then \(x+y=1-w\), \(x\le1/2\), and (17), applied to the side cap whose
other barycentric coordinate is \(x\), gives
\[
4wx<M.
\tag{20}
\]
The third cap satisfies
\[
p_3\ge4xy.
\tag{21}
\]

If \(w\le M/2\), the function
\[
t\longmapsto t(1-w-t)
\]
is decreasing for \(t\ge(1-w)/2\).  Since
\((1-w)/2\le x\le1/2\),
\[
4xy\ge4\cdot\frac12\left(\frac12-w\right)
=1-2w\ge1-M>Q.
\tag{22}
\]

Assume now that \(w>M/2\).  Equation (20) gives
\[
x<\frac{M}{4w}<\frac12.
\tag{23}
\]
Feasibility, using \(x\ge(1-w)/2\), also gives
\[
2w(1-w)<M.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(w_0\in(0,1/2)\) be the unique solution of
\[
2w_0(1-w_0)=M.
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(2w(1-w)\) is increasing on \([0,1/2]\), (24) gives
\(w<w_0\).  Using again the monotonicity in (22), now with (23),
\[
\begin{aligned}
4xy
&>
4\frac{M}{4w}\left(1-w-\frac{M}{4w}\right)\\
&=
\frac{M(1-w)}w-\frac{M^2}{4w^2}
=:F(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
On \(w>M/2\),
\[
F'(w)=\frac{M(M/2-w)}{w^3}<0.
\]
Thus (25)-(26) give
\[
4xy>F(w)>F(w_0)=(1-w_0)^2.
\tag{27}
\]

Put \(z=1-w_0>1/2\).  Then \(2z(1-z)=M\).  The exact rational
comparison
\[
Q-\left(Q+\frac M2\right)^2
=\frac{42071}{324000000}>0
\tag{28}
\]
implies
\[
M<2\sqrt Q(1-\sqrt Q).
\tag{29}
\]
If \(z^2\le Q\), then \(z\le\sqrt Q\); since
\(t\mapsto2t(1-t)\) is decreasing on \([1/2,1]\), this would give
\[
M=2z(1-z)\ge2\sqrt Q(1-\sqrt Q)>M,
\]
a contradiction.  Therefore
\[
(1-w_0)^2=z^2>Q.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (21), (27), and (30) yield \(p_3>Q\), contradicting (13).
This proves (1) for every convex polygon.  The calculation used all
three independent barycentric parameters and (17) includes every
closed singleton-sector boundary limit; the actual attained extremizer
was shown to lie in the open sectors.

Finally let \(B\) be an arbitrary compact two-dimensional convex body
with \(O\in\operatorname{int}B\), and suppose it violated (1).  After
area normalization,
\[
p_1(B),p_2(B)<M,\qquad p_3(B)\le Q.
\]
If \(p_3(B)=Q\), apply the strictification above and replace \(B\) by
one sufficiently small translate.  Translation leaves its area
unchanged, keeps \(O\) in its interior, preserves the two strict
side-cap bounds by continuity, and, because
\[
p_3(B+t v)
=\left|B\cap\{\ell_3\le-t\ell_3(v)\}\right|
\quad\text{with}\quad \ell_3(v)>0,
\]
makes \(p_3\) strictly smaller than \(Q\).  Thus in all cases we may
assume \(p_3(B)<Q\).

Choose convex polygons converging to \(B\) in Hausdorff distance, for
example convex hulls of successively finer boundary nets together with
a fixed small triangle around \(O\).  Their areas and their areas cut by
each fixed fan halfplane converge to the corresponding areas of \(B\),
because a convex boundary and a line have planar area zero.  Normalize
each polygon by a common homothety about \(O\); all three cap fractions
are unchanged.  The three strict inequalities persist for one
sufficiently close polygon, contradicting the polygonal result.
Rescaling from area one back to area \(S\) proves (1), and the registered
implication follows immediately.
