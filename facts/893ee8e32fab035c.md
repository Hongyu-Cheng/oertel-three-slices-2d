## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
polygons satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
m=|A_0|,\qquad k=|A_1|,\qquad M=|A_2|,\qquad
T=m+k+M,\qquad q=\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{2}
\]
and assume
\[
m<M,\qquad \frac T3<M<\frac{4T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) be nonconstant affine functions
with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(c\) is the centroid of \(A_1\), assume
\[
\lambda_1<0\quad\hbox{on }\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad
\lambda_1(c)<1.
\tag{5}
\]
For arbitrary \(s_2,s_3\in\mathbb R\), define, for \(j=2,3\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_j&=\left|A_0\cap\{\lambda_j\geq-s_j\}\right|,\\
b_j&=\left|A_1\cap\{\lambda_j\geq0\}\right|,\\
y_j&=\left|A_2\cap\{\lambda_j\geq s_j\}\right|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
These are, under \(f_j=-\lambda_j\), the literal signed section masses
in accepted fact `a6fbd3f54d7ec4c7`.  Put
\[
D=q-\frac{4k}{9}=\frac{2(m+M-k)}9
\tag{7}
\]
and assume
\[
0<D<m.
\tag{8}
\]
If
\[
x_j+b_j+y_j<q\qquad(j=2,3),
\tag{9}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\frac{\left(\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M\right)^2}{4}<q.
\qquad}
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently, the reverse weak inequality in (10) excludes the
simultaneous residual rows, including its equality boundary.

More explicitly, within (2), (3), \(m<M\), and (8), the scalar area
triples excluded by that reverse weak inequality are exactly
\[
\begin{gathered}
0<m<M,\qquad
\frac{5M}{4}-m<k<\min\{2M-m,m+M\},\\
36M(7m-2M+2k)\geq(m+M+6k)^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
\]
The last inequality is equivalently
\[
250mM-m^2-73M^2+(60M-12m)k-36k^2\geq0.
\tag{12}
\]
In normalized coordinates \(r=m/M\), \(s=k/M\), the same region is
exactly
\[
\frac{53-36\sqrt2}{10}<r<1,
\qquad
\frac54-r<s<\min\{2-r,1+r\},
\qquad
s\leq
\frac{5-r+4\sqrt{3(5r-1)}}6.
\tag{13}
\]
The final inequality in (11)--(13) is weak, so the scalar boundary is
part of the excluded region.

## Proof

First choose the residual row whose middle cap strictly contains the
centroid.  From (4) and (5),
\[
\lambda_2(c)+\lambda_3(c)=1-\lambda_1(c)>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence there is an \(r\in\{2,3\}\) with
\[
\lambda_r(c)>0.
\tag{15}
\]
Let \(t\) be the other index.

The strict centroid-cap argument proved in accepted fact
`3081dc9f6b1f023f` applies to the nonconstant affine function
\(\lambda_r\): a closed planar halfspace containing the centroid of a
two-dimensional convex body has at least \(4/9\) of its area, and the
bound is strict when the centroid lies strictly inside the halfspace.
Here is the boundary check in the present sign convention.  The
halfspace
\[
\{\lambda_r\geq\lambda_r(c)\}
\]
has boundary through \(c\), so its intersection with \(A_1\) has area
at least \(4k/9\).  It is contained in \(\{\lambda_r\geq0\}\).
Moreover, \(c\) is an interior point of \(A_1\).  Indeed, if the
centroid lay on a supporting line, the nonnegative affine distance to
that line would have integral zero and would vanish on all of \(A_1\),
contrary to two-dimensionality.  A sufficiently small ball about \(c\)
inside \(A_1\) therefore has a positive-area part on which
\[
0<\lambda_r<\lambda_r(c).
\]
This part belongs to \(\{\lambda_r\geq0\}\) but not to
\(\{\lambda_r\geq\lambda_r(c)\}\).  Thus
\[
b_r>\frac{4k}{9}.
\tag{16}
\]

Apply the strict row inequality (9) to \(r\).  With
\[
e=x_r+y_r,
\tag{17}
\]
equations (7), (9), and (16) give
\[
0\leq e<q-b_r<q-\frac{4k}{9}=D<m<M.
\tag{18}
\]
This is the shared deletion budget for the two endpoint bodies.

Define the closed complementary endpoint clips
\[
U_0=A_0\cap\{\lambda_r\leq-s_r\},
\qquad
U_2=A_2\cap\{\lambda_r\leq s_r\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(\lambda_r\) is nonconstant, each cutting set in (19) is a
line and has planar area zero.  The closed cap in (6) and the
corresponding set in (19) cover the endpoint polygon and overlap only
on that null line.  Consequently
\[
|U_0|=m-x_r,\qquad |U_2|=M-y_r.
\tag{20}
\]
Both areas are positive by (18).  Thus \(U_0,U_2\) are nonempty compact
convex sets of positive planar area.

Take arbitrary \(z_0\in U_0\) and \(z_2\in U_2\), and put
\[
z=\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}.
\]
Affinity and (19) give
\[
\lambda_r(z)
=\frac{\lambda_r(z_0)+\lambda_r(z_2)}2\leq0.
\tag{21}
\]
Equation (1) puts \(z\) in \(A_1\), while (5) gives
\(\lambda_1(z)<0\).  Therefore (4) yields the strict inequality
\[
\lambda_t(z)=1-\lambda_1(z)-\lambda_r(z)>1>0.
\tag{22}
\]
In particular, equality in (21), which is the only cutting-line
boundary case introduced by the closed complements, still gives a
strict residual middle-cap inequality.  Hence
\[
\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}
\subseteq A_1\cap\{\lambda_t>0\}
\subseteq A_1\cap\{\lambda_t\geq0\}.
\tag{23}
\]

Planar Brunn--Minkowski, (20), and (23) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_t
&\geq
\left|\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}\right|\\
&\geq
\frac{\left(\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}\right)^2}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
This is an inclusion between actual clipped convex sets, not an
allocation of scalar cap masses.

It remains to minimize the right side of (24) under the one shared
deletion budget (17).  For \(0\leq x\leq e\), put
\[
h_e(x)=\sqrt{m-x}+\sqrt{M-e+x}.
\tag{25}
\]
The two radicands are positive by (18), and
\[
h_e''(x)
=-\frac1{4(m-x)^{3/2}}
-\frac1{4(M-e+x)^{3/2}}<0.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the minimum of \(h_e\) on \([0,e]\) occurs at an endpoint.  The
endpoint that deletes all of \(e\) from the smaller body is the
smaller of the two endpoint values:
\[
\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
\leq \sqrt m+\sqrt{M-e}.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\sqrt m-\sqrt{m-e}
=\frac{e}{\sqrt m+\sqrt{m-e}}
\geq
\frac{e}{\sqrt M+\sqrt{M-e}}
=\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-e},
\tag{28}
\]
where \(m<M\), and \(e=0\) simply gives equality.  Since
\(y_r=e-x_r\), equations (25)--(28) give
\[
\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}
\geq\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
>\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M.
\tag{29}
\]
The last inequality is strict because \(e<D<m\).  All quantities are
positive, so squaring preserves it.

The \(t\)-th strict row inequality in (9), together with the
nonnegativity of \(x_t,y_t\), gives \(b_t<q\).  Combining this with
(24) and (29) yields
\[
\frac{\left(\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M\right)^2}{4}
<
\frac{\left(\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}\right)^2}{4}
\leq b_t<q.
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (10).  The first and last comparisons in (30) are strict,
so a reverse equality in (10) is impossible as claimed.

We finally derive the exact scalar region.  From (7),
\[
m-D=\frac{7m-2M+2k}{9}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Using \(q=2T/9\), the reverse weak inequality is equivalent to
\[
\frac{\left(\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M\right)^2}{4}\geq q
\]
\[
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2\sqrt{M(m-D)}
\geq\frac{m+M+6k}{9}.
\tag{32}
\]
The right side is positive.  Squaring (32), substituting (31), and
expanding therefore give the equivalences
\[
36M(7m-2M+2k)\geq(m+M+6k)^2
\tag{33}
\]
\[
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
250mM-m^2-73M^2+(60M-12m)k-36k^2\geq0.
\tag{34}
\]
There is no extraneous branch from squaring because both sides of
(32) are nonnegative.

The remaining scalar bounds are also exact.  The two strict
inequalities in (3) are respectively
\[
k<2M-m,\qquad k>\frac{5M}{4}-m,
\tag{35}
\]
while \(D>0\) is \(k<m+M\).  The condition \(D<m\) is
\[
k>M-\frac{7m}{2},
\]
which is already implied strictly by the second inequality in (35),
because
\[
\left(\frac{5M}{4}-m\right)
-\left(M-\frac{7m}{2}\right)
=\frac M4+\frac{5m}{2}>0.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus (11) is exactly the original scalar window intersected with the
reverse gate (33).

For the normalized form, set \(r=m/M\), \(s=k/M\).  The strict scalar
window becomes
\[
0<r<1,\qquad
\frac54-r<s<\min\{2-r,1+r\}.
\tag{37}
\]
After division by \(M^2\), (33) is
\[
-36s^2+(60-12r)s+250r-r^2-73\geq0.
\tag{38}
\]
Its discriminant as a quadratic in \(s\) is
\[
6912(5r-1),
\]
and, when \(r\geq1/5\), its two roots are
\[
s_\pm(r)=
\frac{5-r\pm4\sqrt{3(5r-1)}}6.
\tag{39}
\]
Since the coefficient of \(s^2\) in (38) is negative, (38) holds
exactly for \(s_-(r)\leq s\leq s_+(r)\).  On
\(1/5\leq r<1\), one has
\[
s_-(r)<\frac54-r.
\tag{40}
\]
For \(r\leq1/2\), (40) is immediate from (39); for
\(r\geq1/2\), its left-minus-right comparison reduces to
\[
4\sqrt{3(5r-1)}>5r-\frac52,
\]
whose left side is greater than \(8\) while the right side is less
than \(5/2\).  Thus the lower quadratic root never restricts the
window (37).

The upper root meets the strict lower boundary of (37) when
\[
4\sqrt{3(5r-1)}=\frac52-5r.
\tag{41}
\]
In the only relevant range \(1/5\leq r<1/2\), both sides are
nonnegative, and squaring (41) gives
\[
100r^2-1060r+217=0.
\tag{42}
\]
Its only root in \((0,1)\) is
\[
r=\frac{53-36\sqrt2}{10}.
\tag{43}
\]
For smaller \(r\), \(s_+(r)\leq5/4-r\), so (37) contains no point
satisfying (38).  For larger \(r\), (37), (38), and (40) are
equivalent to the three conditions in (13).  This proves that
(11)--(13) describe exactly the scalar area region excluded by the
reverse weak gate, with the equality boundary retained.
