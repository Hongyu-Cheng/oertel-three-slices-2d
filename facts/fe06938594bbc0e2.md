## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\subset\mathbb R^2
\]
be a fixed triangle of area \(1\), let
\(\beta=(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3)\) be its affine barycentric coordinate
map, and put
\[
\bar\beta=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad
V=\{a\in\mathbb R^3:a_1+a_2+a_3=0\}.
\]
Every oriented proper closed halfplane has a unique representation
\[
\mathscr H(a,c)
=
\{x:a\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)\ge c\},
\qquad
a\in V,\quad \|a\|_2=1,\quad c\in\mathbb R.
\tag{1}
\]
We define affine-coordinate convergence of oriented halfplanes, and of
their boundary lines, by
\[
\mathscr H(a_n,c_n)\longrightarrow\mathscr H(a,c)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a_n\longrightarrow a,\quad c_n\longrightarrow c.
\tag{2}
\]
This implies local Hausdorff convergence of the halfplanes and boundary
lines in the affine plane, and, for the limits occurring below,
Hausdorff convergence after intersection with \(\Delta\).  A boundary
passes through \(v_j\) exactly when \(c=a_j\), so (2) includes cuts
through vertices without a separate convention.

For each \(n\), let \(L_{1,n},L_{2,n},L_{3,n}\) be proper closed
halfplanes whose interiors cover \(\mathbb R^2\), and put
\[
p_{i,n}=|\Delta\cap L_{i,n}|.
\]
If
\[
\max_i p_{i,n}\longrightarrow\frac49,
\tag{3}
\]
then there are a subsequence and one permutation of the three indices
such that, in the sense of (2),
\[
L_{i,n}\longrightarrow\{\beta_i\ge1/3\},
\qquad
\partial L_{i,n}\longrightarrow\{\beta_i=1/3\}
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently none of the three offsets escapes, and the three limiting
lines are pairwise nonparallel.  The limiting closed halfplanes cover
the plane, but their interiors do not cover the centroid.

## Proof

We first justify the normalized parameters.  If an oriented proper
halfplane is \(\{\phi\ge0\}\), let \(r_j=\phi(v_j)\) and
\[
m=\frac{r_1+r_2+r_3}{3},\qquad b=(r_1-m,r_2-m,r_3-m).
\]
Properness gives \(b\ne0\).  Since
\(\phi(x)=\sum_jr_j\beta_j(x)\), division by \(\|b\|_2\) gives (1) with
\[
a=\frac b{\|b\|_2},\qquad c=-\frac m{\|b\|_2}.
\]
Positive rescaling of \(\phi\) changes neither \(a\) nor \(c\), and the
orientation excludes negative rescaling.  Thus the representation is
unique.

For later use, define
\[
F(a,c)
=
\left|\Delta\cap
\{x:a\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)\ge c\}\right|.
\tag{5}
\]
If \((a_n,c_n)\to(a,c)\), then the corresponding indicator functions
on \(\Delta\) converge except on the line
\[
a\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)=c.
\]
That line has planar area zero, even when it contains one or two
vertices of \(\Delta\).  Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
F(a_n,c_n)\longrightarrow F(a,c).
\tag{6}
\]
This proves the required cap-mass continuity through all vertex-cut
degeneracies.

We next prove the exact equality classification used at the limit.  Let
\[
g=\beta^{-1}(\bar\beta)
\]
be the centroid, and suppose that a proper closed halfplane
\(\mathscr H(a,c)\) contains \(g\) and has cap area at most \(4/9\).
Containment of \(g\) says \(c\le0\).  Consider first the parallel
halfplane through \(g\), namely \(\mathscr H(a,0)\).  The values of its
defining affine functional at the vertices are \(a_1,a_2,a_3\), whose
sum is zero.

Suppose first that exactly one of these values is positive, say
\(a_1>0\), while the other two are nonpositive.  Write
\[
r=-\frac{a_2}{a_1},\qquad s=-\frac{a_3}{a_1}.
\]
Then \(r,s\ge0\) and \(r+s=1\).  The positive side cuts the two edges
from \(v_1\) at fractions \(1/(1+r)\) and \(1/(1+s)\), measured from
\(v_1\).  Its area is therefore
\[
\frac1{(1+r)(1+s)}
=\frac1{2+rs}
\ge\frac49,
\tag{7}
\]
because \(rs\le1/4\).  Equality in (7) holds exactly when
\(r=s=1/2\), in which case the positive side is
\[
\{\beta_1\ge1/3\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The cases where the unique positive value is \(a_2\) or \(a_3\) are
identical.  A zero among the two nonpositive values is allowed in (7)
and gives area \(1/2\), so lines through vertices have not been
discarded.

If two vertex values are positive, apply the preceding calculation to
the negative side.  Its isolated-vertex triangle has area between
\(4/9\) and \(1/2\), so the positive side has area at least \(1/2\).
The remaining sign pattern has one positive, one zero, and one
negative, and was already included in (7).  Since \(a\ne0\), these are
all possibilities.  Hence
\[
F(a,0)\ge\frac49,
\tag{9}
\]
with equality only for one of the three sides in (8).

If \(c<0\), then \(\mathscr H(a,0)\) is contained in
\(\mathscr H(a,c)\).  Because \(g\) is an interior point of \(\Delta\)
and the affine functional has nonzero linear part, the strip
\[
\{x\in\Delta:c\le
a\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)<0\}
\]
has positive area.  Thus \(F(a,c)>F(a,0)\ge4/9\).  We have proved:
\[
\begin{split}
g\in\mathscr H(a,c),\quad F(a,c)\le\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c=0,\\
\mathscr H(a,c)=\{\beta_j\ge1/3\}
\text{ for a unique }j.
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]

We also need an exact three-halfplane consequence of (10).  Suppose
three proper closed halfplanes \(\mathscr H(a_i,c_i)\) cover the plane
and each has cap area at most \(4/9\).  Let
\[
I=\{i:g\in\mathscr H(a_i,c_i)\}
=\{i:c_i\le0\}.
\]
The cover gives \(I\ne\varnothing\), and (10) says that every member
indexed by \(I\) is one of the standard centroid caps.

If \(|I|\le2\), choose \(d=(d_1,d_2,d_3)\in V\) whose coordinates
corresponding to all standard caps indexed by \(I\) are strictly
negative.  Explicitly, for one distinct label take that coordinate
equal to \(-2\) and the other two equal to \(1\); for two distinct
labels take those two coordinates equal to \(-1\) and the remaining
coordinate equal to \(2\).  Repeated labels use the first choice.
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), let \(x_\varepsilon\) be the
unique point with
\[
\beta(x_\varepsilon)=\bar\beta+\varepsilon d.
\]
It lies strictly outside every halfplane indexed by \(I\).  For
\(i\notin I\) one has \(c_i>0\), while
\[
a_i\mathbin{\cdot}
(\beta(x_\varepsilon)-\bar\beta)
=\varepsilon a_i\mathbin{\cdot}d\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the same point is outside every halfplane not indexed by \(I\)
when \(\varepsilon\) is small, contradicting the cover.  Hence
\(|I|=3\), so all three halfplanes are standard centroid caps.  If a
label were repeated, there would be at most two distinct labels and
the same choice of \(d\) would again give an uncovered point.
Therefore the three labels are distinct.  We have established the
classification
\[
\bigcup_{i=1}^3\mathscr H(a_i,c_i)=\mathbb R^2,\quad
F(a_i,c_i)\le\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\{\mathscr H(a_i,c_i):1\le i\le3\}
=\{\beta_j\ge1/3:1\le j\le3\}.
\tag{11}
\]

We now apply compactness to the sequence.  Write
\[
L_{i,n}=\mathscr H(a_{i,n},c_{i,n})
\]
in the normalization (1).  By (3), for all sufficiently large \(n\)
every \(p_{i,n}\) is strictly less than \(1\).  On \(\Delta\), the
affine function in (1) has vertex values
\(a_{i,n,1},a_{i,n,2},a_{i,n,3}\).  If
\[
c_{i,n}\le\min_j a_{i,n,j},
\]
then \(L_{i,n}\) contains all of \(\Delta\), contrary to
\(p_{i,n}<1\).  Since \(\|a_{i,n}\|_2=1\), it follows that
\[
c_{i,n}>-1.
\tag{12}
\]
After passing to a common subsequence, compactness of the unit circle
in \(V\) and of the extended interval \([-1,+\infty]\) gives
\[
a_{i,n}\to a_i,\qquad c_{i,n}\to c_i\in[-1,+\infty]
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{13}
\]

Let \(J=\{i:c_i<+\infty\}\).  We claim that the limiting closed
halfplanes indexed by \(J\) cover the plane.  Fix \(x\).  For every
\(n\), strict coverage supplies an index \(i_n\) such that
\[
a_{i_n,n}\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)>c_{i_n,n}.
\tag{14}
\]
One index occurs along an infinite subsequence.  It cannot have
\(c_i=+\infty\), since the left side of (14) stays bounded for fixed
\(x\).  Passing to the limit along that subsequence yields
\[
a_i\mathbin{\cdot}(\beta(x)-\bar\beta)\ge c_i.
\]
Thus \(x\) belongs to a finite-offset limiting halfplane.  Notice that
only a weak inequality survives.  This is precisely where a strict
cover can lose the centroid in the limit.

For every \(i\in J\), equations (3), (6), and (13) give
\[
F(a_i,c_i)\le\frac49.
\tag{15}
\]
Since those limiting halfplanes cover \(\Delta\),
\[
1\le\sum_{i\in J}F(a_i,c_i)\le |J|\frac49.
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore \(|J|=3\).  This rules out every offset escaping to
\(+\infty\), while (12) already rules out escape to \(-\infty\).
All three limiting halfplanes are proper because \(\|a_i\|_2=1\).

The classification (11) now applies.  After one constant relabeling,
\[
\mathscr H(a_i,c_i)=\{\beta_i\ge1/3\}.
\]
In the normalized parameters this says
\[
a_i=
\frac{e_i-\bar\beta}{\|e_i-\bar\beta\|_2},
\qquad c_i=0.
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (13) and (17) are exactly the convergence asserted in (4).
The three vectors in (17) are pairwise nonproportional, so no two
limiting boundary lines are parallel.

For completeness, parameter convergence in (2) gives local Hausdorff
convergence of the lines and their chosen sides after applying the
affine coordinate map \(\beta:\mathbb R^2\to\{\sum_jz_j=1\}\).
Every standard limiting cap \(\Delta\cap\{\beta_i\ge1/3\}\) is a
nondegenerate compact triangle.  Any limit of points in the approximating
caps lies in it by the limiting weak inequality, and each of its
boundary points is a limit of points from its relative interior.
This proves Hausdorff convergence of the cap intersections as well.
Finally,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\beta_i(g)=1,\qquad \beta_i(g)=1/3,
\]
so the three limiting closed caps cover \(g\), but none of their
interiors contains it.  This explicitly accounts for the
strict-cover-to-limit boundary loss.
