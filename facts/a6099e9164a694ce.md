## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies with nonempty interiors, let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad
S_B=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
and write \(a_0=|A_0|\), \(a_B=|B|\), \(a_2=|A_2|\), and \(T=a_0+a_B+a_2\). Let \(g_i\) be the centroid of \(A_i\), set
\[
z_0=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},\qquad
K_i=A_i-g_i\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
K_B=B-z_0=\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}.
\]
This is the centered setup of accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f`.

For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), define
\[
F_i(u)=\left|K_i\cap\{x:\langle q,x\rangle\le u\}\right|
\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
F_B(s)=\left|K_B\cap\{x:\langle q,x\rangle\le s\}\right|.
\]
Let the corresponding projection supports be
\[
[\ell_i,r_i]\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
[\ell_B,r_B]
=
\left[\frac{\ell_0+\ell_2}{2},\frac{r_0+r_2}{2}\right].
\]
For \(s\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
G_q(s)=\min_{u+v=2s}\bigl(F_0(u)+F_2(v)\bigr),
\qquad
\Phi_q(s)=F_B(s)+G_q(s).
\]
The minimum is attained. Its exact clipping is
\[
G_q(s)=0\quad(s\le\ell_B),
\]
and
\[
G_q(s)=
\min_{u\in[\ell_0,\,2s-\ell_2]}
\bigl(F_0(u)+F_2(2s-u)\bigr)
\quad(s\ge\ell_B).
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
G_q(s)=\min(a_0,a_2)\quad(s\ge r_B).
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(z\in B\), with \(s=\langle q,z-z_0\rangle\), define
\[
H_{q,\lambda}(z)=
\{(x,w):\langle q,w-z\rangle\le\lambda(1-x)\}.
\]
Then
\[
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\mathcal H_2(S_B\cap H_{q,\lambda}(z))
=
\Phi_q(s).
\tag{3}
\]

The function \(\Phi_q\) is continuous and nondecreasing on \(\mathbb R\), with
\[
\Phi_q(s)=0\quad(s\le\ell_B),\qquad
\Phi_q(s)=a_B+\min(a_0,a_2)\quad(s\ge r_B).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, for
\[
I_q(t)=\{s\in\mathbb R:\Phi_q(s)\ge t\},
\]
one has
\[
I_q(t)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb R,&t\le0,\\
[\alpha_q(t),\infty),&
0<t\le a_B+\min(a_0,a_2),\\
\varnothing,&t>a_B+\min(a_0,a_2),
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
\alpha_q(t)=
\min\{s\in[\ell_B,r_B]:\Phi_q(s)\ge t\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the feasible scalar superlevel set for centers in \(B\) is
\[
I_q(t)\cap[\ell_B,r_B]=[\alpha_q(t),r_B]
\tag{7}
\]
whenever \(0<t\le a_B+\min(a_0,a_2)\).

For such \(t\), define
\[
R_q(t)=
B\cap
\{z:\langle q,z-z_0\rangle\ge\alpha_q(t)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
There exists \(z\in B\) with \(h_{S_B}((1,z))\ge t\) if and only if
\[
a_0+a_B\ge t,\qquad a_B+a_2\ge t,
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}R_q(t)\ne\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]
Equivalently, after pairing \(q\) and \(-q\), the selection region is
\[
B\cap
\bigcap_{\{q,-q\}}
\left\{
z:
\alpha_q(t)\le\langle q,z-z_0\rangle
\le-\alpha_{-q}(t)
\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Under (9), condition (10) holds if and only if every subfamily of at most three sets \(R_q(t)\) has nonempty intersection. In particular, at \(t=2T/9\), these horizontal inequalities and the three-direction condition are an exact planar selection criterion for a middle-slice witness.

## Proof

Accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` gives \(K_B=(K_0+K_2)/2\) and the displayed projection-support identity. Fix \(q\), \(z\in B\), and write
\[
s=\langle q,z-z_0\rangle,\qquad
\delta=\langle q,z_0-g_0\rangle.
\]
Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` shows that every non-slice-parallel halfspace through \((1,z)\) is represented by some \(H_{q,\lambda}(z)\). On slice \(0\), its inequality is
\[
\langle q,w-g_0\rangle\le s+\delta+\lambda,
\]
while on slice \(2\) it is
\[
\langle q,w-g_2\rangle\le s-\delta-\lambda.
\]
On the middle slice it is
\[
\langle q,w-z_0\rangle\le s.
\]
Putting
\[
u=s+\delta+\lambda,\qquad
v=s-\delta-\lambda
\]
gives \(u+v=2s\), and \(\lambda\mapsto(u,v)\) bijects \(\mathbb R\) with that affine line. Hence the exact mass is
\[
F_B(s)+F_0(u)+F_2(v),
\]
and minimizing proves (3).

Each \(F_i\) is continuous and nondecreasing, equals \(0\) on \((-\infty,\ell_i]\), and equals \(a_i\) on \([r_i,\infty)\); supporting lines have planar area zero. If \(s<\ell_B\), choosing \(u=\ell_0\) gives \(v=2s-\ell_0<\ell_2\), so \(G_q(s)=0\).

Now suppose \(s\ge\ell_B\). If \(u<\ell_0\), replacing \(u\) by \(\ell_0\) preserves \(F_0(u)=0\) and decreases \(v=2s-u\), so it cannot increase the sum. If \(u>2s-\ell_2\), then \(v<\ell_2\); replacing \((u,v)\) by \((2s-\ell_2,\ell_2)\) preserves the zero \(F_2\)-term and cannot increase the \(F_0\)-term. This proves the exact compact clipping (1), including its degenerate interval at \(s=\ell_B\).

If \(s\ge r_B\), then \(2s\ge r_0+r_2\). For every \(u+v=2s\), either \(u\le r_0\), in which case \(v\ge r_2\) and the cost is at least \(a_2\), or \(u\ge r_0\), in which case the cost is at least \(a_0\). The choices
\[
(u,v)=(\ell_0,2s-\ell_0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(u,v)=(2s-\ell_2,\ell_2)
\]
attain \(a_2\) and \(a_0\), respectively. This proves (2).

For continuity on \(s\ge\ell_B\), set
\[
L(s)=2s-\ell_0-\ell_2.
\]
The clipped pairs are parameterized by
\[
u=\ell_0+\theta L(s),\qquad
v=\ell_2+(1-\theta)L(s),\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\]
The resulting cost is continuous in \((s,\theta)\), so its minimum over the fixed compact interval \([0,1]\) is continuous in \(s\). It tends to \(0\) as \(s\downarrow\ell_B\), and (2) makes it constant after \(r_B\). Thus \(G_q\) is continuous on \(\mathbb R\).

If \(s_1<s_2\) and \((u_2,v_2)\) minimizes the cost at \(s_2\), put
\[
d=s_2-s_1,\qquad
u_1=u_2-d,\qquad v_1=v_2-d.
\]
Then \(u_1+v_1=2s_1\), and monotonicity of both cumulative functions gives
\[
G_q(s_1)
\le F_0(u_1)+F_2(v_1)
\le F_0(u_2)+F_2(v_2)
=G_q(s_2).
\]
Hence \(G_q\), and therefore \(\Phi_q=F_B+G_q\), is nondecreasing. The support identities and (2) give (4). Continuity and monotonicity now prove (5), (6), and (7).

For \(z\in B\), equations (3), (5), and (7) show
\[
\inf_{\lambda}
\mathcal H_2(S_B\cap H_{q,\lambda}(z))\ge t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z\in R_q(t).
\tag{12}
\]
Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` also shows that the two slice-parallel halfspaces through \((1,z)\) have masses \(a_0+a_B\) and \(a_B+a_2\). Taking all \(q\) in (12) proves (9) and (10). Applying the inequalities for both \(q\) and \(-q\) proves the slab formulation (11).

It remains to prove the three-direction selection criterion. Every \(R_q(t)\) is a compact convex subset of \(B\). For a finite family of planar convex sets with empty intersection, choose an inclusion-minimal empty subfamily \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\). If every subfamily of at most three intersects, then \(m\ge4\), and minimality supplies
\[
x_i\in\bigcap_{j\ne i}C_j\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\]
An affine dependence among \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) yields a Radon partition whose two convex hulls meet at a point \(x\). For each \(k\le4\), the part not containing \(x_k\) lies in \(C_k\), hence \(x\in C_k\). For \(k>4\), all four points lie in \(C_k\), so again \(x\in C_k\). This contradicts emptiness. Thus every finite empty family has an empty subfamily of at most three members.

If the full family \(\{R_q(t)\}_{q\in\mathbb S^1}\) had empty intersection, its complements would form an open cover of compact \(B\), yielding a finite subfamily with empty intersection and hence an empty subfamily of at most three. The converse is immediate. This proves the asserted exact selection criterion, including \(t=2T/9\).
