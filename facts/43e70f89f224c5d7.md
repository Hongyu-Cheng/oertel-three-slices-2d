## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate compact triangle of
area \(1\) with \(0\in\Delta\).  For every \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\), set
\[
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\varepsilon\Delta,
\qquad
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i).
\]
Then these three sections are compatible with a compact polytope, and
\[
\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)\leq \frac49+\varepsilon^2.             \tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)}
{|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|}
\leq\frac29.                                               \tag{2}
\]
In particular, no universal centerpoint constant larger than \(2/9\)
is possible.

## Proof

We first prove directly the planar triangle fact used below.  If \(D\) is
any nondegenerate triangle of area \(A\), define
\[
d_D(p)=\inf\{|D\cap P|:P\text{ is a closed planar halfspace and }p\in P\}.
\]
Let \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) be the barycentric coordinates of
\(p\in D\).  Choose \(i\) with \(\beta_i\geq1/3\).  The closed halfspace
\[
P_i=\{w:\beta_i(w)\geq\beta_i(p)\}
\]
has boundary through \(p\), and \(D\cap P_i\) is the triangle at vertex
\(i\) obtained by scaling both incident sides by
\(1-\beta_i\).  Hence
\[
|D\cap P_i|=(1-\beta_i)^2A\leq\frac49A.                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(d_D(p)\leq4A/9\) for every \(p\in D\).

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(D\).  We now show that every closed planar
halfspace containing \(g\) has intersection area at least \(4A/9\).
It is enough to consider a boundary line through \(g\): if the boundary
does not contain \(g\), translate it parallel toward \(g\), retaining the
side contained in the original halfspace.

Fix a line through \(g\).  One of the two parts of \(D\) is a triangle
with an isolated vertex \(v_i\); this remains true when the line passes
through a vertex, in which case either adjacent part may be used.  Write
the two points where the line meets the sides incident to \(v_i\) as
\[
(1-s)v_i+s v_j,\qquad (1-t)v_i+t v_k,
\qquad 0<s,t\leq1.
\]
Since \(g\) lies on the segment joining these two points, comparison of
the \(v_j\)- and \(v_k\)-barycentric coordinates gives
\[
\frac1s+\frac1t=3.                                        \tag{4}
\]
Put \(a=1/s\) and \(b=1/t\).  Then \(a,b\geq1\) and \(a+b=3\), so
\[
2\leq ab\leq\frac94.
\]
The isolated triangular part has area \(stA=A/(ab)\), and therefore
\[
\frac49A\leq stA\leq\frac12A.                             \tag{5}
\]
The other part has area \((1-st)A\geq A/2\).  Both closed sides of every
line through \(g\) thus have area at least \(4A/9\).  Together with (3),
applied at \(g\), this proves
\[
\max_{p\in D}d_D(p)=d_D(g)=\frac49A.                       \tag{6}
\]
This is a triangle-specific proof and uses no general centroid-depth
theorem.  Lines on the boundaries have planar area zero; all halfspaces
in the argument are nevertheless closed, so their boundary points are
included.

We next check compatibility.  Convexity gives
\[
\Delta+\varepsilon\Delta=(1+\varepsilon)\Delta.
\]
Indeed, one inclusion follows by writing
\((x+\varepsilon y)/(1+\varepsilon)\) as a convex combination of
\(x,y\in\Delta\), and the reverse inclusion follows by taking \(x=y\).
Since \(0\in\Delta\) and
\[
0<\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\leq1,
\]
convexity also gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.         \tag{7}
\]
Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` therefore supplies a compact polytope
whose integer-height sections are exactly the three displayed \(A_i\).
Also
\[
|A_0|=|A_1|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\varepsilon^2,\qquad
T=2+\varepsilon^2.                                        \tag{8}
\]
The restriction \(\varepsilon>0\) ensures that \(A_2\) remains
two-dimensional; no degenerate section at \(\varepsilon=0\) is used.

It remains to prove (1) uniformly on all three slices.  For
\(q\in\mathbb S^1\), \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), and a compact set \(K\), write
\[
m_K(q,z)=\min_{w\in K}\langle q,w-z\rangle,\qquad
M_K(q,z)=\max_{w\in K}\langle q,w-z\rangle.
\]
These extrema are finite.  For \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) and
\(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), let
\[
H(j,z;q,\lambda)
=\{(x,w):\langle q,w-z\rangle\leq\lambda(j-x)\}.           \tag{9}
\]
This is a proper closed tilted halfspace, its boundary contains
\((j,z)\), and accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` says that its contribution
on slice \(i\) is exactly
\[
\left|A_i\cap
\{w:\langle q,w-z\rangle\leq\lambda(j-i)\}\right|.         \tag{10}
\]

First take \(z\in A_0=\Delta\).  By (3), choose \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) so
that
\[
\left|\Delta\cap
\{w:\langle q,w-z\rangle\leq0\}\right|\leq\frac49.
\]
Choose
\[
R>\max\left\{0,-m_\Delta(q,z),
-\frac12m_{\varepsilon\Delta}(q,z)\right\},
\qquad \lambda=R.                                         \tag{11}
\]
The thresholds in (10) on slices \(1\) and \(2\) are respectively
\(-R<m_\Delta(q,z)\) and
\(-2R<m_{\varepsilon\Delta}(q,z)\), so both caps are empty.  Hence
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H(0,z;q,R))\leq\frac49.                \tag{12}
\]

Next take \(z\in A_1=\Delta\), and again choose \(q\) so that the middle
cap through \(z\) has area at most \(4/9\).  Choose
\[
R>\max\{0,-m_\Delta(q,z),M_{\varepsilon\Delta}(q,z)\},
\qquad \lambda=-R.                                        \tag{13}
\]
The slice-\(0\) threshold is \(-R<m_\Delta(q,z)\), so that cap is empty.
The slice-\(2\) threshold is
\(R>M_{\varepsilon\Delta}(q,z)\), so that cap is all of
\(\varepsilon\Delta\), including its boundary.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H(1,z;q,-R))
\leq\frac49+\varepsilon^2.                                \tag{14}
\]

Finally take \(z\in A_2=\varepsilon\Delta\).  Applying (3) to the triangle
\(\varepsilon\Delta\), choose \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) with
\[
\left|\varepsilon\Delta\cap
\{w:\langle q,w-z\rangle\leq0\}\right|
\leq\frac49\varepsilon^2.
\]
Choose
\[
R>\max\{0,-m_\Delta(q,z)\},\qquad \lambda=-R.              \tag{15}
\]
The slice-\(1\) and slice-\(0\) thresholds are \(-R\) and \(-2R\);
both are strictly below \(m_\Delta(q,z)\).  Thus both large-triangle
caps are empty, and
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H(2,z;q,-R))
\leq\frac49\varepsilon^2.                                 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (12), (14), and (16) give a finite explicit tilt through every
point of every slice.  Since depth is the infimum over closed halfspaces
containing the point, they prove (1).

By (1) and (8),
\[
\frac{\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)}{T}
\leq
\frac{4/9+\varepsilon^2}{2+\varepsilon^2}
=\frac29+\frac{7\varepsilon^2}{9(2+\varepsilon^2)}
\longrightarrow\frac29
\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0),                            \tag{17}
\]
which proves (2).  More explicitly, if \(c>2/9\), choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<
\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{18}{7}\left(c-\frac29\right)}\right\}.
\]
Then the last excess in (17) is at most
\(7\varepsilon^2/18<c-2/9\), so
\(\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y)<cT\).  Every such \(\varepsilon\) is positive, and
all halfspaces used above are proper, finite-slope, closed halfspaces.
Thus a universal constant \(c>2/9\) fails on an admissible
two-dimensional compatible family.  The limiting numerator \(4/9\) is
the planar triangle maximum (6), while the limiting total mass is \(2\);
this is exactly the factorization \(2/9=(4/9)(1/2)\).
