## Statement

Assume the normalized original \(A_2\)-geometry of accepted facts
`0a44ad1c2be95e40` and `c49613ac26c0c4a8` at
\[
q=1,\qquad
C_i=A_2\cap\{\alpha_i\leq0\},\qquad
|C_i|=\frac5{21}\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1,
\]
the three cutting lines are pairwise nonparallel, the central cell has
physical area \(1\), and the cap interiors are pairwise disjoint.  If
\[
\Delta_Q
=\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}
=0,
\]
then
\[
D\geq D_{\mathrm{tr}}
:=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
=0.749916\ldots>\frac19.
\]
This bound is sharp among original \(A_2\)-geometries satisfying these
hypotheses.  In fact, after passing only to the original barycentric
coordinates
\[
x=\alpha_1,\qquad y=\alpha_2,\qquad \alpha_3=1-x-y,
\]
there is a number \(L>0\) such that the three caps are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1&=\operatorname{conv}
\left\{\left(-L,\frac{1+L}{2}\right),
\left(0,\frac{1+L}{2}\right),
\left(0,\frac{1-L}{2}\right)\right\},\\
C_2&=\operatorname{conv}
\left\{\left(\frac{1+L}{2},-L\right),
\left(\frac{1+L}{2},0\right),
\left(\frac{1-L}{2},0\right)\right\},\\
C_3&=\operatorname{conv}
\left\{\left(\frac{1+L}{2},\frac{1+L}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{1+L}{2},\frac{1-L}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{1-L}{2},\frac{1+L}{2}\right)\right\},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
D=\frac{21}{5}L^2,\qquad
L\geq\frac{5+4\sqrt5}{33}.
\]

## Proof

By accepted fact `0a44ad1c2be95e40`, equality in the iterated planar
Brunn--Minkowski inequality, together with equality of the three cap
areas, implies that the original caps are translates.  Use only the
original affine barycentric normalization of accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`; no endpoint-preserving finite boundary reduction is
made.  In the coordinates
\[
x=\alpha_1,\qquad y=\alpha_2,\qquad \alpha_3=1-x-y,
\]
the central cutting triangle is
\[
\Delta=\{x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1\}.
\]
Write the coordinate image of \(A_2\) as \(K\), and write
\[
C_i=B+t_i
\]
for a fixed two-dimensional convex polygon \(B\).

We first record a support observation.  Suppose \(E\) is an edge of \(B\)
with outward normal \(u\), and \(E\) is not the edge which becomes the
cutting base of \(B+t_i\).  The relative interior of \(E+t_i\) lies in the
strict outer halfplane of the \(i\)-th cut.  In a neighborhood of that
relative interior,
\[
B+t_i=K\cap\{\alpha_i\leq0\}
\]
therefore makes \(E+t_i\) an edge of \(K\) with the same outward normal.
Consequently
\[
h_K(u)=h_B(u)+u\cdot t_i. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if \(K\) had a point strictly beyond that line, joining it to an
interior point of \(B+t_i\) tending to the relative interior of \(E+t_i\)
would produce points of \(K\) beyond the line in the neighborhood where
\(K\) and \(B+t_i\) coincide.

The three cutting-base edges of \(B\) have respective outward normals
\[
n_1=(1,0),\qquad n_2=(0,1),\qquad n_3=(-1,-1).
\]
For fixed \(i\), its base edge is a non-base edge in each of the other two
copies.  Applying (1) to those two copies gives
\[
n_1\cdot t_2=n_1\cdot t_3,\qquad
n_2\cdot t_1=n_2\cdot t_3,\qquad
n_3\cdot t_1=n_3\cdot t_2. \tag{2}
\]
Hence, for some \(u,v,\lambda\),
\[
t_3=(u,v),\qquad
t_1=(u+\lambda,v),\qquad
t_2=(u,v+\lambda). \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\lambda\ne0\), since otherwise all three positive-area caps would
coincide, contrary to their disjoint interiors.

There can be no further edge of \(B\).  If an additional edge had outward
normal \(w\), it would be a non-base edge in all three copies, so (1)
would give
\[
w\cdot(t_1-t_3)=w\cdot(t_2-t_3)=0.
\]
By (3) and \(\lambda\ne0\), the two translation differences are linearly
independent, forcing \(w=0\), which is impossible for an edge normal.
Thus \(B\) is a triangle whose three edges are precisely the three
cutting-base edge types.  For some \(a,b,c\),
\[
B=\{x\leq a,\ y\leq b,\ x+y\geq c\},\qquad
L:=a+b-c>0, \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
|B|=\frac{L^2}{2}. \tag{5}
\]

The three base-line equations are
\[
a+u+\lambda=0,\qquad
b+v+\lambda=0,\qquad
c+u+v=1.
\]
Together with (4), they give
\[
\lambda=-\frac{1+L}{2}. \tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
s=\frac{1+L}{2},\qquad r=\frac{1-L}{2}.
\]
Equations (3)--(6) determine the original caps:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-L,s),(0,s),(0,r)\},\\
C_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(s,-L),(s,0),(r,0)\},\\
C_3&=\operatorname{conv}\{(s,s),(s,r),(r,s)\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
All six nonextreme vertices in (7) lie on the three sides of
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(-L,s),(s,-L),(s,s)\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{conv}(C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3)
=\operatorname{conv}\{(-L,s),(s,-L),(s,s)\}, \tag{8}
\]
whose area is
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3)\right|
=\frac{(1+3L)^2}{8}. \tag{9}
\]

We now restore the determinant scale.  The physical cutting triangle has
area \(1/D\), while \(\Delta\) has coordinate area \(1/2\).  Thus
\[
\text{coordinate area}=\frac D2\,\text{physical area}, \tag{10}
\]
and, equivalently,
\[
\text{physical area}=\frac2D\,\text{coordinate area}. \tag{11}
\]
Using the physical cap area \(5/21\), equations (5) and (10) give
\[
\frac{L^2}{2}
=\frac D2\frac5{21},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
D=\frac{21}{5}L^2. \tag{12}
\]
The central cell and the three caps partition \(K\) up to cutting lines.
Their total physical area is
\[
1+3\frac5{21}=\frac{12}{7}.
\]
Equation (10) therefore gives
\[
|K|=\frac D2\frac{12}{7}
=\frac{6D}{7}
=\frac{18}{5}L^2. \tag{13}
\]
Since \(K\) is convex and contains all three caps, (8), (9), and (13)
imply
\[
\frac{(1+3L)^2}{8}\leq\frac{18}{5}L^2.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
99L^2-30L-5\geq0.
\]
Since \(L>0\),
\[
L\geq L_0:=\frac{5+4\sqrt5}{33}. \tag{14}
\]
Substitution of (14) into (12) gives
\[
D\geq\frac{21}{5}L_0^2
=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
>\frac19. \tag{15}
\]

For sharpness, take \(L=L_0\), define \(r,s\) as above, retain the three
original cutting lines
\[
x=0,\qquad y=0,\qquad x+y=1,
\]
and set
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-L,s),(s,-L),(s,s)\}. \tag{16}
\]
Its three outer caps are exactly the three triangles in (7), so they are
translates and each has coordinate area \(L^2/2\).  The defining equation
for \(L_0\) gives
\[
|K|=\frac{18}{5}L^2,\qquad
|K\cap\Delta|
=|K|-\frac32L^2
=\frac{21}{10}L^2. \tag{17}
\]
Let
\[
D_{\mathrm{tr}}=\frac{21}{5}L^2.
\]
Apply an orientation-preserving affine map whose area multiplier from
coordinate space to physical space is \(2/D_{\mathrm{tr}}\), in agreement
with (11).  Then the physical central area is
\[
\frac{2}{D_{\mathrm{tr}}}\frac{21}{10}L^2=1,
\]
each physical cap area is
\[
\frac{2}{D_{\mathrm{tr}}}\frac{L^2}{2}=\frac5{21},
\]
and the physical cutting-triangle area is
\[
\frac{2}{D_{\mathrm{tr}}}\frac12
=\frac1{D_{\mathrm{tr}}}.
\]
The caps remain translates, so their Brunn--Minkowski deficit is zero.
This is an exact original \(A_2\)-configuration with
\[
D=D_{\mathrm{tr}},
\]
which proves sharpness.
