## Statement

Let \(g_i,g_\ell,g_q\) be affine functions on one affine plane with
\[
g_i+g_\ell+g_q=1,
\]
and use affine coordinates
\[
x=g_i,\qquad y=g_\ell.
\]
Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\), \(a+b>0\), and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,-a),(b,-a),(-a,b)\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2\) be arbitrary nonempty two-dimensional rational convex
polygons satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{1}
\]
After interchanging the endpoint labels if necessary, write
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
Assume that \((M,m,k)\) lies in the exact strict residual area domain of
accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, and assume
\[
D_0:=3t-k-m>m.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(j\in\{i,\ell,q\}\), put
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{g_j\ge0\}|.
\]
For \(r\in\{0,2\}\) and \(j\in\{i,\ell\}\), define
\[
\alpha_{rj}=\min_{A_r}g_j,\qquad
\beta_{rj}=\max_{A_r}g_j,\qquad
w_{rj}=\beta_{rj}-\alpha_{rj},
\]
\[
R_j=\beta_{0j}+\beta_{2j},\qquad
\sigma_j=\frac{w_{0j}^2}{M}+\frac{w_{2j}^2}{m},
\]
and
\[
\Psi_j(R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&R\le0,\\
\min\{R^2/\sigma_j,m\},&R>0.
\end{cases}
\]
If
\[
c_q+m<t,
\tag{3}
\]
then
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)>2t-c_i-c_\ell.
\tag{4}
\]
The conclusion includes every support-contact and clamp boundary compatible
with the strict hypotheses.  In fact, rationality and polygonality are not
used beyond compactness, positive area, and the existence of the indicated
widths.

## Proof

Put
\[
L=a+b>0
\]
and let
\[
\Delta=\left|\det(\nabla g_i,\nabla g_\ell)\right|>0
\]
with respect to planar area on the common affine plane.  Coordinate area in
\((x,y)\) is \(\Delta\) times planar area.  Make the change of coordinates
\[
X=\frac{x}{L},\qquad Y=\frac{y}{L},
\]
and multiply every area and every value of \(\Psi_j\) by
\(\Delta/L^2\).  Indeed, the transformed data satisfy
\[
\widetilde R_j=\frac{R_j}{L},\qquad
\widetilde w_{rj}=\frac{w_{rj}}L,\qquad
\widetilde M=\frac{\Delta M}{L^2},\qquad
\widetilde m=\frac{\Delta m}{L^2},
\]
and therefore
\[
\widetilde\sigma_j=\frac{\sigma_j}{\Delta},
\qquad
\widetilde\Psi_j(\widetilde R_j)
=\frac{\Delta}{L^2}\Psi_j(R_j).
\]
Under this common positive scaling, (2), (3), and the sign of the difference
in (4) are unchanged.  Put
\[
\alpha=\frac aL,\qquad z=\frac1L.
\]
The transformed middle body is
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-\alpha,-\alpha),
(1-\alpha,-\alpha),(-\alpha,1-\alpha)\},
\tag{5}
\]
and its area is
\[
k=\frac12.
\tag{6}
\]
For the rest of the proof all bodies, areas, widths, supports, and profiles
are the transformed ones, and \(X,Y\) are again denoted by \(x,y\).  The
\(q\)-cap condition in these coordinates is
\[
x+y\le z.
\tag{6a}
\]

The strict residual domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` implies
\[
M-m<\frac12<M+m.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
M<k+m.
\tag{8}
\]

We first dispose of all clipping cells except the unique nontrivial one.  If
the \(q\)-cap is all of \(K\), then \(c_q=k\).  Equation (8) gives
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
<\frac{4(k+m)}9<k+m,
\]
which contradicts (3).  Thus every full-\(q\)-cap cell is infeasible.

If \(a\le0\), then (5) is contained in
\(\{x\ge0,y\ge0\}\), so
\[
c_i=c_\ell=k.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover,
\[
D_0=\frac{2M-m-k}{3},
\]
and (2) gives
\[
2M>k+4m.
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10) gives \(k>2m\), and hence
\[
M+m<k+2m<2k.
\]
It follows that
\[
t<\frac{2k}{3}<k.
\]
Since both profiles are nonnegative,
\[
c_i+c_\ell+\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)-2t
\ge2k-2t>0,
\tag{11}
\]
which is (4).

Suppose now that \(a>0\).  If \(b\le0\), then
\[
\max_K(x+y)=1-2\alpha=\frac{b-a}{L}<z,
\]
so the \(q\)-cap is full, which has already been excluded.  We may therefore
assume
\[
a>0,\qquad b>0.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(b-a\le1\), then \(x+y\le z\) throughout the normalized triangle, so
the \(q\)-cap is full.  Thus (3) forces
\[
b-a>1.
\tag{13}
\]
Equation (12) gives \(0<\alpha<1\), while (13) gives
\(2\alpha+z<1\).  Since \(z>0\), we have
\[
0<2\alpha+z<1.
\tag{14}
\]
Direct clipping of (5) now gives
\[
c_i=c_\ell=\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2},
\qquad
c_q=\frac{(2\alpha+z)^2}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]
These formulas include neither a missing cap nor a full cap because of
(12)--(14).

Two strict scalar estimates follow from (3) and (7).  First, (3), (6), and
(15) give
\[
(2\alpha+z)^2
<\frac{4M-14m+2}{9}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(M+m>1/2\),
\[
5M+14m=5(M+m)+9m>\frac52>2,
\]
so the right side of (16) is strictly smaller than \(M\).  Because
\(z>0\), this proves
\[
2\alpha<\sqrt M.
\tag{17}
\]
Second, \(M-m<1/2\) gives \(M<1/2+m\), while
\(c_q>2\alpha^2\) and (3) give
\[
m+2\alpha^2
<t
<\frac{2(1+2m)}9.
\]
Consequently
\[
18\alpha^2<2-5m<2,
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\alpha<\frac13.
\tag{18}
\]

We next extract from (1) exactly the support information needed for the
endpoint payment.  For \(r\in\{0,2\}\), let
\[
\underline x_r=\min_{A_r}x,\qquad
\overline x_r=\max_{A_r}x,
\]
and define \(\underline y_r,\overline y_r\) analogously.  Since the first two
facet inequalities of (5) are \(x\ge-\alpha\) and \(y\ge-\alpha\),
midpoint compatibility (1) gives
\[
\frac{\underline x_0+\underline x_2}{2}
=\min_{u\in A_0,v\in A_2}\frac{x(u)+x(v)}2
\ge-\alpha,
\tag{19}
\]
\[
\frac{\underline y_0+\underline y_2}{2}
=\min_{u\in A_0,v\in A_2}\frac{y(u)+y(v)}2
\ge-\alpha.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_i
&=\overline x_0+\overline x_2\\
&=\underline x_0+\underline x_2+w_{0i}+w_{2i}\\
&\ge w_{0i}+w_{2i}-2\alpha,
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
and similarly
\[
R_\ell\ge w_{0\ell}+w_{2\ell}-2\alpha.
\tag{22}
\]
This derivation uses no center and no central symmetry.  The upper
\(x+y\)-facet of (1) is not needed for (21)--(22).

The second endpoint-shape input is equally general.  Each \(A_r\) is
contained in its coordinate bounding rectangle
\[
[\underline x_r,\overline x_r]\times
[\underline y_r,\overline y_r].
\]
Thus
\[
w_{0i}w_{0\ell}\ge M,\qquad
w_{2i}w_{2\ell}\ge m.
\tag{23}
\]
The widths are positive because both endpoint bodies are two-dimensional.

We invoke the two-direction clamp lemma proved in accepted fact
`fc51a642f7e9ead1`.  For \(\rho\ge1\) and \(u,v>0\), let
\[
F_\rho(u,v)
=\min\left\{
\frac{[\,\rho(u-1)+v\,]_+^2}{u^2+v^2},1
\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
That lemma says
\[
u_i u_\ell\ge1,\quad v_i v_\ell\ge1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_\rho(u_i,v_i)+F_\rho(u_\ell,v_\ell)\ge1.
\tag{25}
\]
It includes equality in the two product hypotheses and all zero,
quadratic, and saturated clamp branches and their boundaries.

Put
\[
p=\sqrt M,\qquad q=\sqrt m,\qquad \rho=\frac pq\ge1,
\]
\[
u_i=\frac{w_{0i}}p,\quad
u_\ell=\frac{w_{0\ell}}p,\qquad
v_i=\frac{w_{2i}}q,\quad
v_\ell=\frac{w_{2\ell}}q.
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (23) becomes
\[
u_i u_\ell\ge1,\qquad v_i v_\ell\ge1.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(j\in\{i,\ell\}\),
\[
\sigma_j=u_j^2+v_j^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (17), (21), and (22) imply
\[
\frac{R_j}{q}
\ge \rho u_j+v_j-\frac{2\alpha}{q}
>\rho(u_j-1)+v_j.
\tag{29}
\]
For each fixed positive denominator, the function
\[
s\longmapsto
\min\left\{\frac{[s]_+^2}{u_j^2+v_j^2},1\right\}
\]
is nondecreasing.  By the definition of \(\Psi_j\), (29) therefore yields
\[
\frac{\Psi_j(R_j)}m\ge F_\rho(u_j,v_j).
\tag{30}
\]
Combining (25), (27), and (30) proves the shape-uniform payment
\[
\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)\ge m.
\tag{31}
\]

It remains only to compare areas.  Put
\[
\eta=\frac12+m-M.
\]
The left inequality in (7) says \(\eta>0\), and
\[
t=\frac{2(1+2m-\eta)}9.
\tag{32}
\]
Using (15), (18), and (31), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i+c_\ell+\Psi_i(R_i)+\Psi_\ell(R_\ell)-2t
&\ge (1-\alpha)^2+m-\frac{4(1+2m-\eta)}9\\
&=\left((1-\alpha)^2-\frac49\right)
+\frac m9+\frac{4\eta}{9}\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
This is exactly (4).

The clipping boundary \(a=0\) is included in (11).  The boundaries
\(b=0\) with \(a>0\), and \(b-a=1\) with \(a,b>0\), belong to the
infeasible full-\(q\)-cap cells.  Support equalities in (19)--(22),
width-product equalities in (23), and every zero/quadratic/plateau clamp
boundary are covered by the weak inequalities (25), (27), and (30); the
strict conclusion comes from (18) and \(\eta,m>0\) in (33).  This exhausts
all clipping, support-contact, and clamp boundaries allowed by the strict
hypotheses and completes the proof.
