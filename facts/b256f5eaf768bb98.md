## Statement

Use the threshold, optimizer, contact-set, weighted-sum, and \((CB)\) conventions of
accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`, and the full strict residual strip of accepted
fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Put
\[
\Delta=\{(\xi,\eta):\xi\ge0,\ \eta\ge0,\ \xi+\eta\le1\},
\qquad
\iota(\xi,\eta)=\left(\frac53\xi,\frac54\eta\right),
\]
\[
P=\iota(\Delta),\qquad D=|P|=\frac{25}{24},
\]
and, for \((y\in\mathbb R)\), let
\[
A_0=A_1=P,
\qquad
A_2(y)=\iota\left((0,y)+\frac25\Delta\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
q_1=(1,0),\qquad
q_+=\left(-\frac{99}{101},\frac{20}{101}\right),\qquad
q_-=\left(-\frac{99}{101},-\frac{20}{101}\right),
\tag{2}
\]
with respective weights \((198,101,101)\).

The family \((1)\) is compatible exactly for
\[
0\le y\le\frac35,
\tag{3}
\]
and every compatible member lies in the full strict residual strip.  Within
\((3)\), all three threshold allocations are genuine positive-demand interior
crossings exactly on
\[
I=\left(
\frac{\sqrt{10032}-99}{25},
\frac{99-\sqrt{7392}}{25}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]

For \((y\in I)\), set
\[
x=25y,\qquad R_+=99+x,\qquad R_-=99-x,
\]
\[
S_+=\sqrt{45144-\frac{R_+^2}{2}},\qquad
S_-=\sqrt{33264-\frac{R_-^2}{2}},
\qquad \tau=\frac{12}{25}.
\tag{5}
\]
Writing \((p_\ell=D\pi_\ell)\) and \((b_\ell=D\beta_\ell)\), the three unique
actual optimizers and thresholds are
\[
\pi_1=\frac{348-32\sqrt{21}}{625},\qquad
\beta_1=\frac{32\sqrt{21}-48}{625},\qquad
\rho_{q_1}(t)=\frac{9-\sqrt{21}}{15},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\pi_+=\frac{(R_++S_+)^2}{282150},\qquad
\beta_+=\tau-\pi_+,\qquad
\rho_{q_+}(t)=\frac{-495+R_++S_+}{303},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\pi_-=\frac{(R_-+S_-)^2}{207900},\qquad
\beta_-=\tau-\pi_-,\qquad
\rho_{q_-}(t)=\frac{-495+R_-+S_-}{303}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \((8/25<\pi_\ell<12/25)\) and \((0<\beta_\ell<4/25)\) for all three
rows.  The complete true contact sets are the singletons
\[
\mathcal U_{q_1}(b_1)=\{0\},\qquad
\mathcal U_{q_+}(b_+)=\left\{\frac{b_+}{2}\right\},\qquad
\mathcal U_{q_-}(b_-)=\left\{\frac{b_-}{2}\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

The exact weighted threshold is
\[
W(y)=\frac{66}{5}(9-\sqrt{21})-264
+\frac13(S_++S_-).
\tag{10}
\]
It has the unique maximizer
\[
y_*=\frac{99}{25}
\frac{\sqrt{45144}-\sqrt{33264}}
{\sqrt{45144}+\sqrt{33264}}
\in I,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\max_{y\in I}W(y)
=\frac{66}{5}(9-\sqrt{21})-264
+\frac13\sqrt{58806+4752\sqrt{266}}
< -\frac{363}{5}<0.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, \((W)\) is nowhere positive in the chamber.

Since \((A_0)\) is the larger outer slice, the true weighted larger-slice
contact maximum for each \((y\in I)\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(y)
&=\max_{\substack{u_1\in\mathcal U_{q_1}(b_1)\\
u_+\in\mathcal U_{q_+}(b_+)\\
u_-\in\mathcal U_{q_-}(b_-)}}
\left(198Q_{0,q_1}(u_1)+101Q_{0,q_+}(u_+)
+101Q_{0,q_-}(u_-)\right)\\
&=-363+\frac13(S_++S_-).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
It is uniquely maximized at the same \((y_*)\), with
\[
\max_{y\in I}H(y)
=-363+\frac13\sqrt{58806+4752\sqrt{266}}
<-231<0.
\tag{14}
\]

## Proof

The midpoint contribution forced at height \((1)\) by the two outer slices is
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2(y)}2
=\iota\left((0,y/2)+\frac7{10}\Delta\right).
\tag{15}
\]
This set is contained in \((A_1=\iota(\Delta))\) exactly when its second
coordinate is nonnegative and its maximum coordinate sum is at most one,
namely exactly when
\[
y\ge0,\qquad \frac y2+\frac7{10}\le1.
\]
This proves \((3)\).  For such \((y)\), the convex hull of the three displayed
slices has no integer first coordinate other than \((0,1,2)\), and \((15)\) shows
that its slice at height \((1)\) is exactly \((A_1)\).  Conversely, \((15)\) is
forced in the middle slice of every convex set having the two displayed
outer slices, so failure of either inequality prevents compatibility.

The areas do not depend on \((y)\).  They are
\[
a_0=a_1=D=\frac{25}{24},\qquad
a_2=\frac4{25}D=\frac16,\qquad
T=\frac94,\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac12.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \((M=D)\), \((m=4D/25)\), \((r=m/M=4/25)\), and \((s=a_1/M=1)\).
Since \((r<9/25)\), the lower and upper residual boundaries in accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e` are
\[
L(r)=1-r=\frac{21}{25}<1<\frac{29}{25}=1+r=U(r).
\]
Also \((r<(27-2\sqrt{26})/25)\), because the latter number is greater than
\((1/2)\), as established in that accepted fact.  Hence every compatible
member is in the full strict residual strip.  The normalized feasible
middle-allocation interval supplied by accepted fact
`58b8e7bb5c3be36f` is
\[
\lambda:=\frac{t-m}{D}=\frac8{25}
\le \pi:=\frac pD\le \frac tD=\frac{12}{25}=\tau.
\tag{17}
\]

We next derive all quantiles and contacts used below.  Normalize mass by
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q}(u)=Q_{i,q}(Du).
\tag{18}
\]
At the vertices \(((0,0),(5/3,0),(0,5/4))\) of \((P)\), the \((q_1)\)-values
are \((0,5/3,0)\), while the numerators \((101q_+\cdot z)\) are
\((0,-165,25)\), and the numerators \((101q_-\cdot z)\) are
\((0,-165,-25)\).  A triangular cap cut a support distance \((h)\) from a
vertex with adjacent full support gaps \((a,b)\) has normalized area
\((h^2/(ab))\) until the next vertex.  Applying this observation at the
minimum and maximum vertices gives, including every endpoint and branch
junction,
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_1}(u)=\frac53(1-\sqrt{1-u})
\qquad(0\le u\le1),
\tag{19}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_+}(u)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-165+\sqrt{31350u}}{101},&0\le u\le\dfrac{33}{38},\\[5pt]
\dfrac{25-\sqrt{4750(1-u)}}{101},&\dfrac{33}{38}\le u\le1,
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_-}(u)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-165+\sqrt{23100u}}{101},&0\le u\le\dfrac{28}{33},\\[5pt]
-\dfrac{\sqrt{4125(1-u)}}{101},&\dfrac{28}{33}\le u\le1,
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
\]
for \((i=0,1)\).  For example, the lower products and junction masses are
\[
165\cdot190=31350,\quad \frac{165}{190}=\frac{33}{38},
\qquad
165\cdot140=23100,\quad \frac{140}{165}=\frac{28}{33},
\]
and the complementary upper products are \((25\cdot190=4750)\) and
\((25\cdot165=4125)\), which verifies every constant in \((20)\)-\((21)\).

The small triangle is the translate by
\((\iota(0,y)=(0,5y/4))\) of \(((2/5)P)\), and has normalized area \((4/25)\).
Consequently
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q}(v)
=q\cdot\iota(0,y)+\frac25
\widehat Q_{0,q}\left(\frac{25v}{4}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
Substitution in \((19)\)-\((21)\) gives
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_1}(v)
=\frac23\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{25v}{4}}\right)
\qquad\left(0\le v\le\frac4{25}\right),
\tag{23}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_+}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x-66+\sqrt{31350v}}{101},&0\le v\le\dfrac{66}{475},\\[5pt]
\dfrac{x+10-\sqrt{760-4750v}}{101},&\dfrac{66}{475}\le v\le\dfrac4{25},
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_-}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-x-66+\sqrt{23100v}}{101},&0\le v\le\dfrac{112}{825},\\[5pt]
\dfrac{-x-\sqrt{660-4125v}}{101},&\dfrac{112}{825}\le v\le\dfrac4{25}.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]

For \((0\le\beta\le4/25)\), define the finite endpoint convolution and its
normalized contact set by
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_q(\beta)
=\max_{0\le u\le\beta}
\left(\widehat Q_{0,q}(u)+\widehat Q_{2,q}(\beta-u)\right),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_q(\beta)=D^{-1}\mathcal U_q(D\beta).
\tag{26}
\]
For \((\beta>0)\), this is the actual convolution \((E_q(D\beta))\) by
accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.  For \((q_1)\), the sum in \((26)\) is
strictly convex in the split \((u)\), so its maximum occurs at an endpoint.
For a homothetic scale \((c)\), the nonconstant endpoint value, apart from
the common factor \((5/3)\), is
\[
c\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\beta}{c^2}}\right)
=\frac{\beta}{c(1+\sqrt{1-\beta/c^2})}.
\]
For \((c=2/5)\), the denominator is at most \((4/5)\), whereas for \((c=1)\)
it is greater than \((1)\) when \((\beta>0)\).  Therefore the unique maximum
allocates all demand to \((A_2)\):
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_1}(\beta)
=\frac23\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{25\beta}{4}}\right),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_1}(\beta)=\{0\}.
\tag{27}
\]

For \((q_+)\) and \((q_-)\), the \((A_0)\) cap is always on its lower branch,
because \((u\le\beta\le4/25<28/33<33/38)\).  When the \((A_2)\) cap is also
on its lower branch, the nonconstant split-dependent part is
\[
\sqrt{\kappa u}+\sqrt{\kappa(\beta-u)},
\qquad \kappa=31350\ \hbox{or}\ 23100,
\]
which has the unique maximum \((u=\beta/2)\).  This split is strictly within
the small lower branch because
\[
\frac\beta2\le\frac2{25}
<\min\left\{\frac{66}{475},\frac{112}{825}\right\}.
\tag{28}
\]
On a small upper branch, the derivative with respect to \((u)\), apart from
the positive factor \((1/101)\), is
\[
\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{2\sqrt u}
-\frac d{2\sqrt{C-d(\beta-u)}},
\tag{29}
\]
where \(((\kappa,d,C)=(31350,4750,760))\) or
\(((23100,4125,660))\).  Squaring the positive terms shows that \((29)\) is
positive exactly when
\[
\kappa(C-d\beta)+d(\kappa-d)u>0.
\]
This holds throughout each upper-branch interior because
\((C-d\beta\ge0)\) and \((\kappa>d)\); the endpoint case follows by a
one-sided limit.  Hence an upper piece increases to its lower-branch
junction, and the lower piece continues increasing until \((u=\beta/2)\).
Thus the displayed split is the unique global contact, and
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_+}(\beta)
=\frac{-231+x+\sqrt{62700\beta}}{101},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_+}(\beta)=\left\{\frac\beta2\right\},
\tag{30}
\]
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_-}(\beta)
=\frac{-231-x+\sqrt{46200\beta}}{101},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_-}(\beta)=\left\{\frac\beta2\right\}.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations \((27)\), \((30)\), and \((31)\) hold for \((0<\beta\le4/25)\).  At
\((\beta=0)\), all three finite contact sets are \(({0})\), and the finite
extensions are
\[
0,\qquad \frac{-231+x}{101},\qquad \frac{-231-x}{101},
\tag{32}
\]
respectively.  These finite values are used only for endpoint comparison;
the actual convention remains \((E_q(0)=-\infty)\), as required by accepted
fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.

For finite comparison on \((17)\), put
\[
\delta_q(\pi)=\widehat Q_{1,q}(\pi)
-\frac12\widehat{\overline E}_q(\tau-\pi).
\tag{33}
\]
Every \((\delta_q)\) is strictly increasing: its first term is strictly
increasing in \((\pi)\), while the convolution is strictly increasing in
\((\beta=\tau-\pi)\).  Direct substitution gives
\[
\delta_{q_1}(\lambda)=\frac{4-\sqrt{17}}3<0,
\qquad
\delta_{q_1}(\tau)=\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}3>0,
\tag{34}
\]
\[
\delta_{q_+}(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{10032}-99-x}{202},
\qquad
\delta_{q_+}(\tau)=\frac{-99-x+2\sqrt{15048}}{202},
\tag{35}
\]
\[
\delta_{q_-}(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{7392}-99+x}{202},
\qquad
\delta_{q_-}(\tau)=\frac{-99+x+2\sqrt{11088}}{202}.
\tag{36}
\]
For every compatible translation \((0\le x\le15)\), the two upper values
in \((35)\)-\((36)\) are positive: \((\sqrt{15048}>122)\) and
\((\sqrt{11088}>105)\) give respective numerators greater than \((130)\) and
\((111)\).  Thus \((q_1)\) always has a unique interior crossing, \((q_+)\) has
one exactly when
\[
x>\sqrt{10032}-99,
\]
and \((q_-)\) has one exactly when
\[
x<99-\sqrt{7392}.
\]
At equality in either condition the unique optimizer is the lower endpoint
\((\pi=\lambda)\), not an interior crossing.  The interval in \((4)\) is
nonempty and is contained strictly in the compatible range, because
\[
99<\sqrt{10032}<101,
\qquad
84<\sqrt{7392}<87.
\]
This proves that \((4)\) is precisely the full compatible three-crossing
interval.

It remains to solve the three crossings.  For \((q_1)\), put
\((z=\sqrt{1-\pi_1})\).  Substitution of \((19)\) and \((27)\) into the crossing
equation reduces, with the sign selected by \((34)\), to
\[
\sqrt{17-25z^2}=10z-8,
\qquad
125z^2-160z+47=0.
\]
The only feasible root is \((z=(16+\sqrt{21})/25)\).  This gives \((6)\),
including the displayed threshold by substitution in \((19)\).  The endpoint
signs in \((34)\) also give \((8/25<\pi_1<12/25)\).

For either near-antipodal row, let
\[
(R,\kappa)=(R_+,31350)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(R_-,23100).
\]
Equations \((20)\)-\((21)\) and \((30)\)-\((31)\) turn the crossing equation into
\[
2\sqrt{\kappa\pi}-\sqrt{2\kappa(\tau-\pi)}=R.
\tag{37}
\]
Writing \((Z=\sqrt{\kappa\pi})\), the identity
\((\pi+(\tau-\pi)=\tau)\) gives
\[
3Z^2-2RZ+\frac{R^2}{2}-\kappa\tau=0.
\tag{38}
\]
The positive-demand root selected by \((35)\)-\((36)\) is
\[
Z=\frac{R+\sqrt{3\kappa\tau-R^2/2}}3.
\]
Since \((3(31350)\tau=45144)\) and
\((3(23100)\tau=33264)\), equations \((7)\)-\((8)\) follow.  The strict endpoint
signs give \((8/25<\pi_\pm<12/25)\), so \((\beta_\pm>0)\), and the actual
finite-demand contact formulas \((27)\), \((30)\), and \((31)\) prove all three
singleton identities in \((9)\).  This also confirms that none of the three
optimizers is an artifact of the finite extension at zero demand.

The directions in \((2)\) are unit because \((99^2+20^2=101^2)\), are
noncollinear, and satisfy
\[
198q_1+101q_++101q_-=0.
\]
Using \((6)\)-\((8)\) and \((R_++R_-=198)\) therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
W(y)
&=198\rho_{q_1}(t)+101\rho_{q_+}(t)+101\rho_{q_-}(t)\\
&=\frac{66}{5}(9-\sqrt{21})-264+\frac13(S_++S_-),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves \((10)\).

We now maximize this expression exactly.  On the \((x)\)-interval
corresponding to \((4)\), let
\[
F(x)=S_+(x)+S_-(x).
\]
Both radicands are positive there, and direct differentiation gives
\[
F'(x)=-\frac{99+x}{2S_+}+\frac{99-x}{2S_-}.
\tag{39}
\]
Each summand has second derivative
\[
-\frac1{2S_\pm}-\frac{(99\pm x)^2}{4S_\pm^3}<0,
\]
so \((F)\) is strictly concave.  Since all quantities in \((39)\) are positive
apart from their displayed signs, its critical equation is equivalent,
after squaring and cancelling the common mixed term, to
\[
45144(99-x)^2=33264(99+x)^2.
\]
It has the unique solution
\[
x_*=99\frac{\sqrt{45144}-\sqrt{33264}}
{\sqrt{45144}+\sqrt{33264}}.
\tag{40}
\]
This point lies strictly inside the required interval.  Indeed,
\((\sqrt{10032}-99<2)\) and \((99-\sqrt{7392}>12)\), while
\[
46^2\cdot45144-53^2\cdot33264=2086128>0,
\]
\[
107^2\cdot33264-91^2\cdot45144=7002072>0
\]
prove respectively \((x_*>7)\) and \((x_*<8)\).  Strict concavity now proves
that \((40)\) is the unique global maximizer.

At \((40)\),
\[
99+x_*=\frac{198\sqrt{45144}}
{\sqrt{45144}+\sqrt{33264}},
\qquad
99-x_*=\frac{198\sqrt{33264}}
{\sqrt{45144}+\sqrt{33264}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x_*)
&=\sqrt{(\sqrt{45144}+\sqrt{33264})^2-\frac{198^2}{2}}\\
&=\sqrt{58806+4752\sqrt{266}},
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
where
\((\sqrt{45144\cdot33264}=2376\sqrt{266})\).  Equations \((10)\), \((40)\),
and \((41)\) prove the exact maximizer and value in \((11)\)-\((12)\).  For its sign,
\[
\sqrt{45144}<213,\qquad \sqrt{33264}<183,
\qquad \sqrt{21}>\frac92,
\]
so \((F(x_*)<396)\) and
\[
\max_{y\in I}W(y)
<\frac{66}{5}\left(9-\frac92\right)-264+\frac{396}{3}
=-\frac{363}{5}<0.
\]

Finally, \((9)\) makes the larger-slice contact maximum a genuine singleton
evaluation.  Equations \((20)\)-\((21)\) give
\[
H(y)=-330
+\sqrt{\frac{31350\beta_+}{2}}
+\sqrt{\frac{23100\beta_-}{2}}.
\tag{42}
\]
For the root \((Z=(R+S)/3)\) in \((38)\), equation \((37)\) gives
\[
\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\beta}{2}}=\frac{2S-R}{6}.
\]
Using \((R_++R_-=198)\) in \((42)\) yields exactly \((13)\).  Therefore \((H)\) has
the same unique maximizer as \((F)\), and \((41)\) gives its exact value in
\((14)\).  The bound \((F(x_*)<396)\) also gives
\((\max_{y\in I}H(y)<-363+132=-231)\), completing the contact audit and
the exhaustion of the chamber.
