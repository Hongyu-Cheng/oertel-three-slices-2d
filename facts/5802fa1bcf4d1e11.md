## Statement

Equip
\[
E=\{(s_1,s_2,s_3)\in\mathbb R^3:s_1+s_2+s_3=1\}
\]
with its induced Euclidean area. Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0&=(0,0,1),&p_1&=(-10,20,-9),&p_2&=(-10,-30,41),\\
q_0&=(0,0,1),&q_1&=(-10,20,-9),&q_2&=(40,-30,-9),
\end{aligned}
\]
and define the rational triangles
\[
L=\operatorname{conv}\{p_0,p_1,p_2\},
\qquad
K=\operatorname{conv}\{q_0,q_1,q_2\}.
\]
Set
\[
\sigma=(0,0,-1),\qquad \mu=250\sqrt3,
\qquad A=500\sqrt3.
\]
Then \(A>\mu>0\), \(|L|=A-\mu\), \(|K|=\mu\), and
\(\sum_{j=1}^3\sigma_j=-1\). The six cap areas are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&j=1&j=2&j=3\\ \hline
|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|&0&100\sqrt3&\dfrac{419}{2}\sqrt3\\[2mm]
|K\cap\{s_j>\sigma_j\}|&200\sqrt3&100\sqrt3&10\sqrt3.
\end{array}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_{1\le j\le3}
\left(|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|+|K\cap\{s_j>\sigma_j\}|\right)
=\frac{439}{2}\sqrt3
<\frac{2000}{9}\sqrt3
=\frac{4A}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Use the affine coordinates \((x,y)=(s_1,s_2)\) on \(E\), so that
\(s_3=1-x-y\). The parametrization
\((x,y)\mapsto(x,y,1-x-y)\) has Gram determinant \(3\). Thus every area computed in the \((x,y)\)-plane must be multiplied by \(\sqrt3\) to obtain induced Euclidean area on \(E\).

In \((x,y)\)-coordinates, the vertices of \(L\) are
\[
(0,0),\quad(-10,20),\quad(-10,-30).
\]
The absolute determinant of its two nonzero vertex vectors is \(500\), so
\[
|L|=\frac{500}{2}\sqrt3=250\sqrt3.
\]
All three \(s_1\)-coordinates are nonpositive, hence the strict cap
\(L\cap\{s_1>0\}\) is empty and has area zero. For \(s_2>0\), the cutting line \(y=0\) contains \((0,0)\) and meets the edge joining \((-10,20)\) to \((-10,-30)\) at \((-10,0)\). The resulting triangle has coordinate area
\[
\frac12\left|\det
\begin{pmatrix}-10&-10\\20&0\end{pmatrix}\right|=100,
\]
so its Euclidean area is \(100\sqrt3\).

The condition \(s_3>0\) is \(x+y<1\). At the three vertices of \(L\), the values of \(s_3\) are respectively \(1,-9,41\). The complementary cap at \(p_1\) is bounded by \(s_3=0\), whose intersections with \([p_1,p_0]\) and \([p_1,p_2]\) are
\[
(-1,2,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(-10,11,0).
\]
In \((x,y)\)-coordinates the two edge vectors of this complementary triangle based at \((-10,20)\) are \((9,-18)\) and \((0,-9)\), so its coordinate area is \(81/2\). Therefore
\[
|L\cap\{s_3>0\}|=\left(250-\frac{81}{2}\right)\sqrt3
=\frac{419}{2}\sqrt3.
\]

In \((x,y)\)-coordinates, the vertices of \(K\) are
\[
(0,0),\quad(-10,20),\quad(40,-30).
\]
The absolute determinant of its two nonzero vertex vectors is again \(500\), so \(|K|=250\sqrt3\). For \(s_1>\sigma_1=0\), the line \(x=0\) contains \((0,0)\) and meets the edge from \((-10,20)\) to \((40,-30)\) at \((0,10)\). The cap triangle has coordinate area
\[
\frac12\left|\det
\begin{pmatrix}40&0\\-30&10\end{pmatrix}\right|=200.
\]
For \(s_2>\sigma_2=0\), the line \(y=0\) contains \((0,0)\) and meets the same edge at \((10,0)\). This cap triangle has coordinate area
\[
\frac12\left|\det
\begin{pmatrix}-10&10\\20&0\end{pmatrix}\right|=100.
\]

Finally, \(s_3>\sigma_3=-1\) is \(x+y<2\). The \(s_3\)-values at the vertices of \(K\) are \(1,-9,-9\). Hence the cutting line \(s_3=-1\) meets the two edges from \(q_0\) at the points
\[
(-2,4,-1)\quad\text{and}\quad(8,-6,-1),
\]
each one fifth of the way from \(q_0\) to the corresponding other vertex. The cap is therefore a homothetic copy of \(K\) with linear ratio \(1/5\), and its area is
\[
\frac1{25}|K|=10\sqrt3.
\]

Lines have planar area zero, so every computed value is unchanged if any strict cap is replaced by its closed version. Summing the two rows gives
\[
200\sqrt3,\qquad 200\sqrt3,
\qquad \frac{439}{2}\sqrt3.
\]
The third is the largest, and
\[
\frac{439}{2}<\frac{2000}{9}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
3951<4000.
\]
Since \(A=|L|+|K|=500\sqrt3\), this proves the strict failure of the claimed lower bound.
