## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be planar convex bodies of positive areas \(a_0,a_2\). Let \(f_i:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine and \(s_i\in\mathbb R\) for \(i=1,2,3\), and suppose that the open sets \(\{f_i<0\}\) cover \(\mathbb R^2\). Define
\[
C_{0i}=\{u\in A_0:f_i(u)-s_i\le0\},\qquad
C_{2i}=\{v\in A_2:f_i(v)+s_i\le0\},
\]
and \(x_i=|C_{0i}|\), \(y_i=|C_{2i}|\). Then
\[
A_0\times A_2\subseteq
\bigcup_{i=1}^3\bigl((C_{0i}\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times C_{2i})\bigr),
\]
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\left(\frac{x_i}{a_0}+\frac{y_i}{a_2}\right)\ge1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i+y_i)\ge\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
In the normalized certificate of accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, the sections at heights \(0\) and \(2\) of
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\le0\}
\]
are \(C_{0i}\) and \(C_{2i}\), respectively. Hence \(x_i+y_i\) is exactly the endpoint contribution of \(H_i\), and the sum of the three endpoint contributions is at least \(\min\{a_0,a_2\}\).
Moreover, if \(S=\sum_i s_i\), then at least one of the two endpoint families alone covers its whole plane by strict section halfplanes: if \(-1-S<0\), then \(\{f_i-s_i<0\}_{i=1}^3\) covers \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\sum_i x_i\ge a_0\); if \(-1+S<0\), then \(\{f_i+s_i<0\}_{i=1}^3\) covers \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\sum_i y_i\ge a_2\). At least one case always applies.

## Proof

Affineness gives, for every \(u,v\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
2f_i\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)=f_i(u)+f_i(v).
\]
Fix \((u,v)\in A_0\times A_2\). Since the open halfplanes \(\{f_i<0\}\) cover the whole plane, there is an \(i\) such that
\[
f_i\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)<0,
\]
and hence \(f_i(u)+f_i(v)<0\). If \(u\notin C_{0i}\) and \(v\notin C_{2i}\), the closed definitions of the caps give the strict inequalities
\[
f_i(u)-s_i>0,\qquad f_i(v)+s_i>0.
\]
Adding them cancels \(s_i\) and gives \(f_i(u)+f_i(v)>0\), a contradiction. Thus \(u\in C_{0i}\) or \(v\in C_{2i}\). In the first case \((u,v)\in C_{0i}\times A_2\), and in the second case \((u,v)\in A_0\times C_{2i}\). This proves the asserted product-set cover. Notice that the strict inequality furnished by the open cover is compared with strict inequalities obtained by taking complements of the closed caps, so points on either cap boundary are included and create no boundary gap.

The convex bodies and all cap sets are Borel measurable. Taking four-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the cover, using finite subadditivity, and using the product-measure identities for the six cylinders gives
\[
a_0a_2
\le
\sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(|C_{0i}\times A_2|+|A_0\times C_{2i}|\bigr)
=
\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i a_2+a_0y_i).
\]
Since \(a_0a_2>0\), division by \(a_0a_2\) proves
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\left(\frac{x_i}{a_0}+\frac{y_i}{a_2}\right)\ge1.
\]
Set \(X=\sum_i x_i\) and \(Y=\sum_i y_i\). If \(a_0\le a_2\), then
\[
1\le\frac{X}{a_0}+\frac{Y}{a_2}
\le\frac{X+Y}{a_0},
\]
so \(X+Y\ge a_0\). If \(a_2\le a_0\), the same argument with the indices exchanged gives \(X+Y\ge a_2\). Thus in both orders
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i+y_i)\ge\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]

Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` supplies affine functions satisfying \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\). For every \(w\in\mathbb R^2\), at least one \(f_i(w)\) is strictly negative, because otherwise their sum would be nonnegative. Thus its functions satisfy the cover hypothesis above. Substitution of \(h=0\) and \(h=2\) into its halfspace formula gives, respectively,
\[
f_i(w)-s_i\le0,\qquad f_i(w)+s_i\le0.
\]
After intersection with \(A_0\) and \(A_2\), these are exactly \(C_{0i}\) and \(C_{2i}\), so their planar measures \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) are exactly the two endpoint parts of the mass of \(H_i\). The preceding inequality therefore gives the claimed endpoint payment.

There is also a direct endpoint-cover consequence of the normalization. Put \(S=\sum_i s_i\). Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(f_i(w)-s_i)=-1-S,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3(f_i(w)+s_i)=-1+S
\]
for every \(w\). At least one of the two constants on the right is strictly negative, since their sum is \(-2\). If \(-1-S<0\), then for every \(w\) at least one \(f_i(w)-s_i\) is strictly negative. Thus the three strict slice-\(0\) section halfplanes cover \(\mathbb R^2\), their closed caps cover \(A_0\), and finite subadditivity gives \(\sum_i x_i\ge a_0\). If \(-1+S<0\), the identical argument on slice \(2\) gives \(\sum_i y_i\ge a_2\). This independently implies that the endpoint contribution is at least one of \(a_0,a_2\), and hence at least their minimum.

For calibration, this payment does not by itself imply the area-regime conclusion mentioned in the subgoal relation. Indeed, let \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), and let \(b_i=|A_1\cap\{f_i\le0\}|\). The same open cover implies the valid ordinary union bound \(\sum_i b_i\ge a_1\). Thus the two proved covering bounds give only
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mu(H_i)
=\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i+y_i+b_i)
\ge m+a_1.
\]
If every \(\mu(H_i)<2T/9\), summing gives \(\sum_i\mu(H_i)<2T/3\). These bounds contradict one another only when
\[
m+a_1\ge\frac{2}{3}(M+m+a_1),
\]
equivalently \(m+a_1\ge2M\). The stated regime \(M-a_1\le m/2\) does not imply this: \(M=3\), \(m=2\), \(a_1=2\) satisfies \(M-a_1=m/2\) but has \(m+a_1=4<6=2M\). Hence deriving that regime requires an additional proved bonus covering inequality; no such inequality is asserted here.
