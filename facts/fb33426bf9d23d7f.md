## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad q=\frac{29}{50},
\qquad k=\frac{21}{20},
\qquad L=\frac{k(1+q)}2=\frac{1659}{2000},
\tag{1}
\]
and let \(e\) be rational with
\[
0<e,
\qquad
e^2<
\frac{5191587}{2534105600}.
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0(e)=\operatorname{conv}\biggl\{&
(0,0),\ (2(1-e),0),\
\left(2-e,\frac e2\right),\ (0,1)\biggr\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_2(e)=q\,\operatorname{conv}\biggl\{&
(0,0),\ (2,0),\
\left(\frac{4e}{3},1-\frac{2e}{3}\right),\
(0,1-e)\biggr\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
\[
B(e)=\frac{A_0(e)+A_2(e)}2,
\qquad
A_1(e)=kB(e).
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(A_0(e)\) and \(A_2(e)\) are noncentrally symmetric,
nonhomothetic rational quadrilaterals, while \(A_1(e)\) is a
noncentrally symmetric rational pentagon.  They are compatible:
\[
\frac{A_0(e)+A_2(e)}2\subseteq A_1(e),
\tag{6}
\]
so accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` supplies a compact polytope having
exactly these three integer slices.

Their exact areas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
M(e):=|A_0(e)|&=1-\frac{e^2}{2},\\
m(e):=|A_2(e)|&=\frac{841}{2500}
-\frac{841}{3750}e^2,\\
|B(e)|&=\frac{6241}{10000}
-\frac{679}{3750}e^2,\\
|A_1(e)|&=L^2-\frac{299439}{1500000}e^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
They satisfy
\[
\frac3{10}<\frac{m(e)}{M(e)}<\frac9{25},
\tag{8}
\]
and all three strict area-triangle inequalities.  Moreover, no translate
of \(A_2(e)\) is contained in \(D_{m(e)}(A_0(e))\), so the exact closed
erosion in accepted fact `9aa8eaca3b60ecf3` is empty.

Let
\[
z_*=
L\left(\frac23,\frac13\right)
=\left(\frac{553}{1000},\frac{553}{2000}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
Then \(z_*\in A_1(e)\), and for
\[
S_e=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i(e)),
\qquad
T(e)=\sum_{i=0}^2|A_i(e)|,
\tag{10}
\]
one has the exhaustive all-direction lower bound
\[
\boxed{
h_{S_e}((1,z_*))
\geq
\frac{25998015121}{57600000000}
-\frac{1385839}{1500000}e^2.
}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{S_e}((1,z_*))-\frac{2T(e)}9
&\geq
\frac{9421769}{6400000000}
-\frac{9700873}{13500000}e^2\\
&>0
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
by (2).  Hence no halfspace of mass strictly below \(2T(e)/9\) contains
\((1,z_*)\), and no family of strict-low halfspaces, regardless of
cardinality, covers the height-\(1\) slice.

For the representative \(e=1/100\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_0|&=\frac{19999}{20000},&
|A_2|&=\frac{12614159}{37500000},\\
|B|&=\frac{23403071}{37500000},&
|A_1|&=\frac{3440251437}{5000000000},\\
\frac{2T}{9}
&=\frac{30365667911}{67500000000},
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
and (12) has the exact positive margin
\[
\frac{6049266139}{4320000000000}.
\tag{14}
\]

## Proof

We first determine the polygons and their areas.  The two endpoint
truncations remove fractions \(e^2/2\) and \(2e^2/3\) of their containing
triangles, respectively.  This gives the first two lines of (7).

Direct Minkowski addition gives the following five cyclic vertices of
\(B(e)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(0,0),\\
&(1+q-e,0),\\
&\left(1+q-\frac e2,\frac e4\right),\\
&\left(\frac{2qe}{3},
\frac{1+q-2qe/3}{2}\right),\\
&\left(0,\frac{1+q-qe}{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Condition (2) implies \(0<e<1/20\), so these vertices are distinct and
all five successive edge lengths are positive.  The shoelace formula
gives
\[
|B(e)|=\frac{(1+q)^2}{4}-\frac{679}{3750}e^2,
\tag{16}
\]
which is the third line of (7).  Dilation by \(k\) gives the fourth.

Both endpoint quadrilaterals are genuinely noncentrally symmetric.  In
the cyclic order in (3), their two diagonal sums are
\[
\left(2-e,\frac e2\right)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(2-2e,1),
\tag{17}
\]
which are unequal.  After removing the common positive factor \(q\), the
two diagonal sums in (4) are
\[
\left(\frac{4e}{3},1-\frac{2e}{3}\right)
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
(2,1-e),
\tag{18}
\]
which are also unequal.  The new truncation edge of \(A_0(e)\) has
direction \((2,1)\), while the new truncation edge of \(A_2(e)\) has
direction \((4,1)\), up to sign.  Hence their normal fans differ and the
two bodies are not homothetic.  The body \(B(e)\) has five vertices, so it
and its dilation \(A_1(e)\) cannot be centrally symmetric.

The origin lies in \(B(e)\).  Since \(k>1\), convexity gives
\[
B(e)\subseteq kB(e)=A_1(e),
\tag{19}
\]
which proves (6).  The compatibility conclusion now follows from
accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`.

For the exact residual range, (7) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_1(e)|+m(e)-M(e)
&=\frac{97881}{4000000}
+\frac{114161}{1500000}e^2>0,\\
M(e)+m(e)-|A_1(e)|
&=\frac{2593319}{4000000}
-\frac{786961}{1500000}e^2>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The second expression is positive because (2) implies \(e^2<1/400\).
The third area-triangle inequality follows from \(M(e)>m(e)>0\).

The same bound \(e^2<1/400\) gives
\[
m(e)>
\frac{841}{2500}\left(1-\frac1{600}\right)
=\frac{503759}{1500000}
>\frac3{10}.
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(M(e)<1\), this is also a lower bound for \(m(e)/M(e)\).
Conversely,
\[
\frac{m(e)}{M(e)}
<
\frac{841/2500}{1-1/800}
=\frac{6728}{19975}
=\frac9{25}-\frac{463}{19975}.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves (8).

We next verify the claimed empty erosion exactly.  Put
\[
\ell(x,y)=-5x+3y.
\tag{23}
\]
Exact clipping of \(\Delta\) gives
\[
\left|\Delta\cap\{\ell\leq-7/2\}\right|=\frac{13}{40},
\qquad
\left|\Delta\cap\{-\ell\leq2/3\}\right|=\frac{193}{585}.
\tag{24}
\]
The first cap is the triangle with vertices
\((7/10,0),(2,0),(1,1/2)\); the second is the quadrilateral with vertices
\((0,0),(2/15,0),(22/39,28/39),(0,1)\).
Because \(A_0(e)\subseteq\Delta\), its two corresponding caps are no
larger.  From (21),
\[
\frac{503759}{1500000}-\frac{13}{40}
=\frac{16259}{1500000}>0,
\tag{25}
\]
\[
\frac{503759}{1500000}-\frac{193}{585}
=\frac{346601}{58500000}>0.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus both \(A_0(e)\)-caps in (24) have area strictly below \(m(e)\).
The quantiles in accepted fact `9aa8eaca3b60ecf3` satisfy
\[
q_{A_0,m}(\ell)>-\frac72,
\qquad
q_{A_0,m}(-\ell)>\frac23.
\tag{27}
\]
Its exact halfplane representation confines \(D_{m(e)}(A_0(e))\) to an
\(\ell\)-interval of width strictly less than \(17/6\).

The minimum and maximum \(\ell\)-scores on \(A_2(e)\) are
\(-10q\) and \(3q(1-e)\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{width}_{\ell}A_2(e)
=q(13-3e)
>
\frac{29}{50}\left(13-\frac3{20}\right)
=\frac{7453}{1000}
>\frac{17}{6}.
\tag{28}
\]
Translation preserves width, proving the empty-erosion assertion.

It remains to establish the all-direction depth estimate.  The five
vertices in (15) also show that \(B(e)\) is the triangle
\(((1+q)/2)\Delta\) with its \(X\)- and \(Y\)-corners truncated.  The two
new inequalities are
\[
x-2y\leq1+q-e,
\qquad
x-4y\geq-2(1+q-qe).
\tag{29}
\]
The centroid \(((1+q)/2)(2/3,1/3)\) satisfies both under (2).  Hence
\[
z_*=k\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\left(\frac23,\frac13\right)\right)
\in kB(e)=A_1(e),
\tag{30}
\]
as required.

Compare the actual slices with the common-vertex homothetic triangles
\[
\widetilde A_0=\Delta,
\qquad
\widetilde A_1=L\Delta,
\qquad
\widetilde A_2=q\Delta.
\tag{31}
\]
Normalize them by taking \(L\Delta\) as the base triangle.  The endpoint
scale factors and their sum are
\[
a=\frac1L=\frac{2000}{1659},
\qquad
c=\frac qL=\frac{1160}{1659},
\qquad
s_0=a+c=\frac{40}{21}.
\tag{32}
\]
They satisfy the exact residual hypotheses of accepted fact
`aef70a60593c53a2`:
\[
a^2+c^2-1=\frac{2593319}{2752281}>0,
\quad
2-s_0=\frac2{21}>0,
\quad
1+c^2-a^2=\frac{59}{1659}>0.
\tag{33}
\]

We retain the stronger directionwise quantity proved in that accepted
fact.  Its exact normal form writes the normalized mass of every
nonhorizontal halfspace through the middle centroid as
\[
p(\rho)+
a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2).
\tag{34}
\]
In every one of the six projected-vertex chambers, its proof establishes
\[
p(\rho)\geq\frac49,
\qquad
a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2)
\geq\min\left\{c^2,\frac{K^2}{2}\right\},
\tag{35}
\]
and
\[
p(\rho)+\frac{K^2}{2}
\geq
\overline Q(s_0):=
\frac{(s_0+s_0^{-1})^2}{9}.
\tag{36}
\]
Here \(K\) is positive in all chambers for
\(s_0=40/21>\sqrt2\).  In the minimum-, middle-, and maximum-vertex
chambers it is, respectively,
\[
\frac{2(1+\rho)}3,
\qquad
\frac23+\rho\left(s_0-\frac43\right),
\qquad
s_0-\frac43+\frac{2\rho}{3},
\tag{37}
\]
and all three expressions are positive.
The other side of the minimum in (35) also reaches (36), because
\[
\frac49+c^2-\overline Q(s_0)
=\frac{10993223279}{39632846400}>0.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (34)-(38) therefore give
\[
\widetilde\mu(H)\geq L^2\overline Q(s_0)
\tag{39}
\]
for every nonhorizontal halfspace through \((1,z_*)\).

The two horizontal normalized masses in the same normal form are
\(a^2+1\) and \(1+c^2\).  Their exact margins above
\(\overline Q(s_0)\) are
\[
\frac{71234831279}{39632846400},
\qquad
\frac{33011471279}{39632846400}.
\tag{40}
\]
Thus (39) includes horizontal halfspaces.  If a halfspace contains
\((1,z_*)\) in its interior, translating its boundary parallel toward
that point produces a contained halfspace whose boundary passes through
the point.  Hence (39) holds for every closed halfspace containing
\((1,z_*)\).

The exact lower value in (39) is
\[
\overline Q(s_0)
=\frac{4165681}{6350400},
\qquad
L^2\overline Q(s_0)
=\frac{25998015121}{57600000000}.
\tag{41}
\]
The endpoint containments in (31) are immediate from (3)-(4).
Minkowski addition also gives
\[
B(e)\subseteq\frac{1+q}{2}\Delta,
\qquad
A_1(e)=kB(e)\subseteq L\Delta.
\]
Thus all three actual bodies are contained in their respective outer
triangles in (31).  Their total deleted area is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta(e)
&=|\widetilde A_0\setminus A_0(e)|
+|\widetilde A_1\setminus A_1(e)|
+|\widetilde A_2\setminus A_2(e)|\\
&=\left(
\frac12+\frac{299439}{1500000}
+\frac{841}{3750}\right)e^2
=\frac{1385839}{1500000}e^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
For every halfspace \(H\),
\[
\mu_{S_e}(H)\geq\widetilde\mu(H)-\delta(e).
\tag{43}
\]
Combining (39), (41), and (43) proves (11).

Finally,
\[
T(e)=
\frac{8097881}{4000000}
-\frac{1385839}{1500000}e^2.
\tag{44}
\]
Subtracting \(2T(e)/9\) from (11) gives the first line of (12), since
\[
\frac{25998015121}{57600000000}
-\frac{2}{9}\frac{8097881}{4000000}
=\frac{9421769}{6400000000}.
\tag{45}
\]
The upper endpoint in (2) is exactly the positive root condition
\[
e^2<
\frac{
(9421769/6400000000)
}{
9700873/13500000
}
=\frac{5191587}{2534105600}.
\tag{46}
\]
This proves strictness in (12), and all claims follow.  Substitution of
\(e=1/100\) in (7), (10), and (12) gives (13)-(14).
