## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle and
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times K)\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^2.
\]
For measurable \(B\subset\mathbb{R}^3\), define
\[
\mu_S(B)=\sum_{i=0}^2\operatorname{area}\{u\in K:(i,u)\in B\},
\qquad
T=\mu_S(S)=3\operatorname{area}(K),
\]
and
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu_S(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]
Then
\[
\max_{y\in S}h_S(y)=\frac{4T}{9}.
\]
A maximizer is \(y_*=(1,g)\), where \(g\) is the centroid of \(K\). If
\(\lambda_1\) is the barycentric coordinate associated with any fixed vertex
of \(K\), one minimizing halfspace at \(y_*\) is
\[
H_*=\{(z,u):\lambda_1(u)\ge1/3\}.
\]

## Proof

First prove the global upper bound. If \(y=(0,x)\), the halfspace
\(\{(z,u):z\le0\}\) has mixed measure \(T/3\). If \(y=(2,x)\), use
\(\{(z,u):z\ge2\}\).

Now let \(y=(1,x)\). Write the barycentric coordinates of \(x\) as
\(\lambda_1(x),\lambda_2(x),\lambda_3(x)\), and choose \(i\) with
\(\lambda_i(x)\ge1/3\). The vertical halfspace
\[
\{(z,u):\lambda_i(u)\ge\lambda_i(x)\}
\]
contains \(y\). On each slice it cuts a triangle similar to \(K\), with area
ratio
\[
(1-\lambda_i(x))^2\le\frac49.
\]
Thus \(h_S(y)\le4T/9\) for every \(y\in S\).

It remains to prove \(h_S(y_*)\ge4T/9\). By fact
`400272f6de6c67bb`, it suffices to consider halfspaces whose boundary
contains \(y_*\). Every such halfspace has the form
\[
H=\{(z,u):a\cdot(u-g)+b(z-1)\ge0\}.
\]
Reflection \(z\mapsto2-z\) preserves \(S\), \(\mu_S\), and \(y_*\), so
assume \(b\ge0\). If \(a=0\), then \(H\) contains at least the entire
slices at heights \(1\) and \(2\), giving \(\mu_S(H)\ge2T/3\).

Assume \(a\ne0\). Order the three values of \(a\cdot(v-g)\) at the vertices
\(v\) of \(K\). Their sum is zero. After positive rescaling, they have the
form
\[
-1,\qquad 1-p,\qquad p
\]
for some \(p\in[1/2,2]\). Let \(c\ge0\) be the correspondingly rescaled
value of \(b\), and define
\[
S_p(t)=
\frac{\operatorname{area}\{u\in K:a\cdot(u-g)\ge t\}}
{\operatorname{area}(K)}
\]
in this normalization. The fractions cut from the slices at heights
\(0,1,2\) are \(S_p(c),S_p(0),S_p(-c)\), respectively.

Set \(A=(2-p)(1+p)\) and \(B=(1+p)(2p-1)\). Similarity of triangular caps
gives
\[
S_p(t)=
\begin{cases}
1, & t\le-1,\\
1-\dfrac{(t+1)^2}{A}, & -1\le t\le1-p,\\
\dfrac{(p-t)^2}{B}, & 1-p\le t\le p,\\
0, & t\ge p,
\end{cases}
\]
with \(p=1/2,2\) understood by continuity.

First,
\[
S_p(0)\ge\frac49.
\]
Indeed, for \(p\le1\), \(S_p(0)=1-1/A\ge1/2\). For \(p\ge1\),
\[
S_p(0)=\frac{p^2}{(1+p)(2p-1)}\ge\frac49,
\]
because this inequality is equivalent to \((p-2)^2\ge0\).

Next,
\[
S_p(c)+S_p(-c)\ge\frac89.
\]
For \(p\in[1/2,1]\), put \(E(c)=S_p(c)+S_p(-c)-1\). If
\(0\le c\le1-p\), then
\[
E(c)=1-\frac{2(1+c^2)}{A}.
\]
This is decreasing, \(E(0)\in[0,1/9]\), and
\(E(1-p)\ge-1/9\), the latter reducing to
\[
(2p-1)(7p-8)\le0.
\]

If \(1-p\le c\le p\), then
\[
E(c)=\frac{(p-c)^2}{B}-\frac{(1-c)^2}{A}.
\]
This quadratic is convex. Its minimum occurs at \(c=(1+p)/3\) and equals
\[
-\frac{1-p}{3(1+p)}\ge-\frac19.
\]
Its maximum occurs at an endpoint and is at most \(1/9\) by the adjacent
cases.

If \(p\le c\le1\), then
\[
E(c)=-\frac{(1-c)^2}{A}\ge-\frac19,
\]
where the last inequality reduces to
\[
(2p-1)(5p-7)\le0.
\]
For \(c\ge1\), \(E(c)=0\). Hence
\[
\frac89\le S_p(c)+S_p(-c)\le\frac{10}{9}
\]
when \(p\le1\).

For \(p\in[1,2]\), replacing the projected variable by its negative divided
by \(p\) changes the parameter to \(1/p\) and yields
\[
S_p(c)+S_p(-c)
=
2-\left(S_{1/p}(c/p)+S_{1/p}(-c/p)\right).
\]
The same bounds therefore hold for \(p\ge1\).

Consequently,
\[
\frac{\mu_S(H)}{\operatorname{area}(K)}
=
S_p(c)+S_p(0)+S_p(-c)
\ge\frac89+\frac49=\frac43.
\]
Since \(T=3\operatorname{area}(K)\), this gives
\(\mu_S(H)\ge4T/9\), and hence \(h_S(y_*)\ge4T/9\).

Finally, \(H_*\) cuts from every slice the vertex cap
\(\{\lambda_1\ge1/3\}\), whose area ratio is
\((1-1/3)^2=4/9\). Therefore \(\mu_S(H_*)=4T/9\), proving equality.
