## Statement

Let
\[
h=\frac1{10},
\qquad
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac89,-24\right),
\left(\frac{10}{9},30\right),
\left(\frac{10}{9},31\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
A_2=\frac1{10}A_0,
\qquad
K=\frac{11}{20}A_0,
\]
and define
\[
f_i(x,y)=\frac{540}{11}x,\qquad
f_\ell(x,y)=\frac{20}{11}y,\qquad
f_j(x,y)=-1-\frac{540}{11}x-\frac{20}{11}y.
\]
Take the two homothetically aligned Alternative 1 shifts to be
\[
a_i=a_\ell=0,
\]
and put
\[
u_i=\left|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|,
\qquad
u_\ell=\left|A_0\cap\{f_\ell\leq0\}\right|,
\qquad
c_r=\left|K\cap\{f_r\leq0\}\right|.
\]
Then \(A_0,A_2,K\) are rational compact two-dimensional convex polygons, \(|A_0|=1\),
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=K,
\]
the three affine functions are nonconstant with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and sum to \(-1\), and
\[
\max\left\{
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i,\
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell,\
c_j+h^2
\right\}
<
\frac{2(1+h^2+|K|)}9.
\]

## Proof

The second and third listed vertices of \(A_0\) form a vertical edge of length \(1\), and the first vertex has horizontal distance \(2\) from that edge.  Hence \(|A_0|=1\).  Convexity gives
\[
A_0+hA_0=(1+h)A_0,
\]
so
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac{1+h}{2}A_0
=\frac{11}{20}A_0
=K.
\]
In particular,
\[
|K|=\left(\frac{11}{20}\right)^2=\frac{121}{400}.
\]
The displayed gradients are nonzero and pairwise nonparallel, and the defining formulas give
\[
f_i+f_j+f_\ell=-1.
\]

The shifts \(a_i=a_\ell=0\) are the actual homothetically aligned shifts in this example.  Indeed, \(f_i\) and \(f_\ell\) are linear, so for \(r=i,\ell\),
\[
A_2\cap\{f_r\leq-a_r\}
=\frac1{10}\left(A_0\cap\{f_r\leq a_r\}\right).
\]
Thus the corresponding \(A_2\) low-cap area is \(h^2u_r\), exactly as required by the coefficient \(1+h^2\) in the claimed inequality.

Use the rational affine coordinates
\[
X=27x,\qquad Y=y.
\]
They send \(A_0\) to the area-\(27\) triangle
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-24,-24),(30,30),(30,31)\}.
\]
Since the coordinate map has constant Jacobian, cap-area ratios may be computed in \(P\).

The line \(X=0\) meets the two edges from \((-24,-24)\) at \((0,0)\) and \((0,4/9)\).  Its low cap therefore has area
\[
\frac12\cdot24\cdot\frac49=\frac{16}{3},
\]
and hence
\[
u_i=\frac{16/3}{27}=\frac{16}{81}.
\]
Similarly, the line \(Y=0\) meets those edges at \((0,0)\) and \((-24/55,0)\).  Therefore
\[
u_\ell
=\frac{\frac12\cdot24\cdot\frac{24}{55}}{27}
=\frac{32}{165}.
\]

Write \(\lambda=11/20\).  For every \((x,y)\in A_0\), with \(X=27x\) and \(Y=y\),
\[
f_i(\lambda x,\lambda y)=X,\qquad
f_\ell(\lambda x,\lambda y)=Y,\qquad
f_j(\lambda x,\lambda y)=-1-X-Y.
\]
It follows that
\[
c_i=\frac{121}{400}u_i,\qquad
c_\ell=\frac{121}{400}u_\ell.
\]
For the \(j\)-cap, the relevant part of \(P\) is \(\{X+Y\geq-1\}\).  On the edge from \((-24,-24)\) to \((30,30)\), the line \(X+Y=-1\) occurs at the fraction \(47/108\) from the first vertex.  On the edge from \((-24,-24)\) to \((30,31)\), it occurs at the fraction \(47/109\).  Hence the complementary triangle has area ratio
\[
\frac{47}{108}\frac{47}{109}
=\frac{2209}{11772},
\]
and consequently
\[
c_j
=\frac{121}{400}
\left(1-\frac{2209}{11772}\right)
=\frac{121}{400}\frac{9563}{11772}.
\]

Now
\[
1+h^2+|K|
=1+\frac1{100}+\frac{121}{400}
=\frac{21}{16},
\]
so the claimed right-hand side equals
\[
\frac29\frac{21}{16}=\frac7{24}.
\]
The three left-hand candidates are, exactly,
\[
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i
=\left(\frac{121}{400}+\frac{101}{100}\right)\frac{16}{81}
=\frac7{27}
<\frac7{24},
\]
\[
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell
=\left(\frac{121}{400}+\frac{101}{100}\right)\frac{32}{165}
=\frac{14}{55}
<\frac7{24},
\]
and
\[
c_j+h^2
=\frac{121}{400}\frac{9563}{11772}+\frac1{100}
=\frac{1204211}{4708800}
<\frac{1373400}{4708800}
=\frac7{24}.
\]
Thus all three candidates are strictly below the asserted lower bound.

Every one of the three cutting lines meets the interior of the relevant triangle and leaves positive area on both sides.  Hence no cap in the construction is empty or full, all cutting-line boundaries have area zero, and the strict violation does not depend on a zero-cap, full-cap, support-face, or other boundary convention.
