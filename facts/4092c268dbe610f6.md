## Statement

Let
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,0),(0,3)\}\subseteq\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
C=[0,2]\times K,
\]
and let \(A_0=A_1=A_2=K\). Then \(C\) satisfies all hypotheses of the three-slice problem, and
\[
a_0=a_1=a_2=\frac92,\qquad T=\frac{27}{2},\qquad
g_0=(1,1).
\]
For \(y=(0,g_0)\),
\[
h_S(y)=2=\frac49a_0.
\]
Moreover, the closed halfspace
\[
H=\{(x,(p,q))\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2:p-3x\ge1\}
\]
contains \(y\), contributes area \(2\) on slice \(0\), and contributes zero area on slices \(1\) and \(2\). Consequently, for every \(c>0\),
\[
h_S((0,g_0))
=\frac49a_0
<\frac49a_0+c\,a_1.
\]
Thus no universal positive constant \(c\) can provide the proposed additive second-slice improvement, even in the regime \(a_i<T/2\) for every \(i\).

## Proof

The set \(C=[0,2]\times K\) is a compact polytope. Its integer-height sections are exactly \(\{i\}\times K\) for \(i=0,1,2\). Also,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac{K+K}{2}=K=A_1,
\]
so the compatibility criterion in accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` is satisfied.

The triangle \(K\) has area \(9/2\) and centroid \(g_0=(1,1)\). We first prove directly that every closed halfplane in \(\mathbb R^2\) whose boundary contains \(g_0\) cuts area at least \(2\) from \(K\).

More generally, let \(\Delta\) be a triangle with vertices \(v_0,v_1,v_2\), centroid \(g=(v_0+v_1+v_2)/3\), and let
\[
Q=\{z:\ell(z)\ge0\},
\]
where \(\ell\) is a nonzero affine functional satisfying \(\ell(g)=0\). Put \(r_i=\ell(v_i)\). Then \(r_0+r_1+r_2=0\).

Suppose first that exactly one \(r_i\) is positive. Relabel so that \(r_0>0\) and \(r_1,r_2\le0\), and write
\[
a=-\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad b=-\frac{r_2}{r_0}.
\]
Then \(a,b\ge0\) and \(a+b=1\). The line \(\ell=0\) meets the edges from \(v_0\) to \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) at respective fractions \(1/(1+a)\) and \(1/(1+b)\) from \(v_0\). Hence
\[
\frac{|Q\cap\Delta|}{|\Delta|}
=\frac1{(1+a)(1+b)}
=\frac1{2+ab}
\ge\frac49,
\]
because \(ab\le1/4\).

If exactly two \(r_i\) are positive, relabel so that \(r_0<0<r_1,r_2\), and set
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{-r_0},\qquad b=\frac{r_2}{-r_0}.
\]
Again \(a,b>0\) and \(a+b=1\). The complementary corner \(\{\ell\le0\}\cap\Delta\) has area fraction
\[
\frac1{(1+a)(1+b)}\le\frac12,
\]
since \((1+a)(1+b)=2+ab\ge2\). Therefore
\[
\frac{|Q\cap\Delta|}{|\Delta|}\ge\frac12\ge\frac49.
\]
Cases in which some \(r_i=0\) are included in the first formula or follow by continuity. Thus every such halfplane contains at least \(4/9\) of the triangle's area. Applied to \(K\), this gives the lower bound
\[
|K\cap Q|\ge\frac49\cdot\frac92=2.
\]

By the exact boundary-through-\(y\) halfspace formula in accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, every non-slice-parallel halfspace relevant to \(h_S(y)\) cuts slice \(0\) by a planar halfplane whose boundary contains \(g_0\). Its slice-\(0\) contribution is therefore at least \(2\), and all other slice contributions are nonnegative. A slice-parallel halfspace through \(y\) contains either slice \(0\) alone or all three slices, so its contribution is at least \(a_0=9/2>2\). Hence
\[
h_S(y)\ge2.
\]

For the displayed halfspace \(H\), the slice-\(0\) section is
\[
K\cap\{p\ge1\}.
\]
Its area is
\[
\int_1^3(3-p)\,dp=2.
\]
On slice \(1\), the condition is \(p\ge4\), and on slice \(2\) it is \(p\ge7\). Since every \((p,q)\in K\) has \(p\le3\), both sections are empty. Thus
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)=2,
\]
so \(h_S(y)\le2\). Combining the two inequalities yields
\[
h_S(y)=2=\frac49a_0.
\]

Finally, \(a_i/T=1/3<1/2\) for every \(i\), while for every \(c>0\),
\[
\frac49a_0+c\,a_1=2+\frac92c>2=h_S(y).
\]
This disproves the proposed universal inequality.
