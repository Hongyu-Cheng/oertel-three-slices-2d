## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3\}
=\mathbb R^2.
\tag{1}
\]
For cyclic
\[
(i,j,k)=(1,2,3),(2,3,1),(3,1,2),
\]
let \(O_i\) be the intersection of \(f_j=0\) and \(f_k=0\), and put
\[
H_i=\{f_i\leq0\},
\qquad
\mathcal T=H_1\cap H_2\cap H_3.
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
J:=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_k)|
\tag{3}
\]
is positive and independent of the cyclic pair, \(f_i(O_i)=-1\), and
\[
\mathcal T=\operatorname{conv}\{O_1,O_2,O_3\},
\qquad
|\mathcal T|=\frac1{2J}.
\tag{4}
\]

The usual finite-rank-two normalization is contained in (1) as follows. If
\[
\alpha_i>0,\qquad \sigma>0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i\phi_i=\sigma,
\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla\phi_1,\nabla\phi_2,\nabla\phi_3\}
=\mathbb R^2,
\tag{5}
\]
then
\[
f_i=-\frac{\alpha_i\phi_i}{\sigma}
\tag{6}
\]
satisfy (1), the triangle in (2) is exactly
\[
\{\phi_1\geq0,\ \phi_2\geq0,\ \phi_3\geq0\},
\]
and, writing \(n_i=\nabla\phi_i\),
\[
J=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2|\det(n_1,n_2)|}{\sigma^2},
\qquad
|\mathcal T|
=\frac{\sigma^2}
{2\alpha_1\alpha_2|\det(n_1,n_2)|}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(B,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex polytopes such that
\[
B\subseteq K,
\qquad
\mathcal T\subseteq K.
\tag{8}
\]
For each \(i\), define the forced convex core
\[
C_i=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{O_i\})\subseteq K.
\tag{9}
\]
Call the \(i\)-th corner admissible if \(O_i\) is a boundary vertex of \(C_i\), its two adjacent exposed edges are
\[
[E_i,O_i],\qquad[O_i,F_i],
\]
and
\[
f_j(F_i)<0<f_k(F_i),
\qquad
f_k(E_i)<0<f_j(E_i),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
C_i\cap\{f_j>0,\ f_k>0\}=\varnothing.
\tag{11}
\]
In addition, require the ray
\[
\{f_j=0,\ f_k>0\}
\]
to lie on the exterior side of the supporting line of \([O_i,F_i]\), and require the ray
\[
\{f_k=0,\ f_j>0\}
\]
to lie on the exterior side of the supporting line of \([E_i,O_i]\).

At an admissible corner, put
\[
a_i=-f_j(F_i),\quad b_i=f_k(F_i),\quad
c_i=f_j(E_i),\quad d_i=-f_k(E_i),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\rho_{i,j}=\frac{a_ib_i}{2J},
\qquad
\rho_{i,k}=\frac{c_id_i}{2J},
\qquad
\gamma_i=\frac{a_id_i}{J},
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
\delta_i=|K\setminus C_i|,
\qquad
e_{i,\ell}=|(K\setminus C_i)\cap H_\ell|
\quad(\ell=j,k).
\tag{14}
\]
If
\[
e_{i,j}<\rho_{i,j},
\qquad
e_{i,k}<\rho_{i,k},
\tag{15}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\gamma_i(\delta_i-e_{i,j}-e_{i,k})
\leq4e_{i,j}e_{i,k}.
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the \(i\)-th corner rules out the open parameter region
\[
e_{i,j}<\rho_{i,j},\qquad
e_{i,k}<\rho_{i,k},\qquad
\delta_i>
e_{i,j}+e_{i,k}+\frac{4e_{i,j}e_{i,k}}{\gamma_i}.
\tag{17}
\]

When all three corners are admissible, the exact simultaneous non-rejection region furnished by the three corner tests is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\bigcap_{\mathrm{cyc}(i,j,k)}
\left(
\{e_{i,j}\geq\rho_{i,j}\}
\ \cup\
\{e_{i,k}\geq\rho_{i,k}\}
\ \cup\
\left\{
\delta_i\leq e_{i,j}+e_{i,k}
+\frac{4e_{i,j}e_{i,k}}{\gamma_i}
\right\}
\right).
\quad}
\tag{18}
\]
This is the exact logical complement of the three exclusion tests, not a sufficient condition for existence of a convex extension. If a corner is not admissible, failure of admissibility is an additional geometric escape at that corner.

Suppose furthermore that the same core works at all three corners:
\[
C_1=C_2=C_3=B.
\tag{19}
\]
Write
\[
\delta=|K\setminus B|,
\qquad
e_\ell=|(K\setminus B)\cap H_\ell|,
\qquad
E=e_1+e_2+e_3,
\qquad
\gamma=\min_i\gamma_i.
\tag{20}
\]
If all six inequalities in (15) hold, then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\delta\leq\frac{2E}{3}+\frac{4E^2}{9\gamma}.
\quad}
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, at least one corner closes whenever
\[
\delta>\frac{2E}{3}+\frac{4E^2}{9\gamma}.
\tag{22}
\]

The exclusion region is nonempty. In coordinates
\[
f_2(u,v)=u,\qquad f_3(u,v)=v,\qquad
f_1(u,v)=-1-u-v,
\tag{23}
\]
let \(B\) be the rational hexagon with counterclockwise vertices
\[
(0,0),\
\left(-\frac12,\frac14\right),\
(-1,0),\
\left(-\frac34,-\frac12\right),\
(0,-1),\
\left(\frac14,-\frac34\right).
\tag{24}
\]
All three corners are admissible for this same core, and at every cyclic corner
\[
\rho_{i,j}=\frac1{16},
\qquad
\rho_{i,k}=\frac3{32},
\qquad
\gamma_i=\frac38.
\tag{25}
\]
The rational target
\[
\delta=\frac3{32},
\qquad
e_1=e_2=e_3=\frac1{32}
\tag{26}
\]
satisfies \(E=\delta\) and all six thresholds, but violates (16) at every corner. Hence no convex extension of \(B\) has (26), and a relative open neighborhood of (26) is also excluded.

## Proof

Taking gradients in (1) gives
\[
\nabla f_1+\nabla f_2+\nabla f_3=0.
\tag{27}
\]
If two cyclic gradients were dependent, (27) would put all three gradients in one line, contrary to (1). Hence every cyclic pair is independent. Also
\[
\det(\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3)
=\det(\nabla f_3,\nabla f_1)
=\det(\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2),
\tag{28}
\]
so \(J\) in (3) is well defined and positive.

For fixed cyclic \((i,j,k)\), the affine map
\[
x\longmapsto(f_j(x),f_k(x))
\tag{29}
\]
has absolute Jacobian \(J\). In these coordinates, \(f_i=-1-f_j-f_k\), so (29) maps \(\mathcal T\) bijectively to
\[
\{(r,q):r\leq0,\ q\leq0,\ r+q\geq-1\}.
\tag{30}
\]
This coordinate triangle has vertices \((0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1)\) and area \(1/2\). Its inverse image has vertices \(O_i,O_j,O_k\) and area \(1/(2J)\). This proves (3) and (4).

Under (5), definition (6) gives
\[
\sum_i f_i=-1,
\qquad
\nabla f_i=-\frac{\alpha_i n_i}{\sigma}.
\]
Taking gradients in (5) gives
\[
\alpha_1n_1+\alpha_2n_2+\alpha_3n_3=0.
\]
The rank-two hypothesis then gives the pairwise independence used above. For the pair \((1,2)\),
\[
|\det(\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2)|
=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2|\det(n_1,n_2)|}{\sigma^2}.
\]
Moreover, \(f_i\leq0\) is equivalent to \(\phi_i\geq0\). Substitution in (4) proves every assertion in (7), with no further normalization assumption.

Fix an admissible corner \(i\), and abbreviate
\[
C=C_i,\quad
\delta=\delta_i,\quad
e_j=e_{i,j},\quad e_k=e_{i,k}.
\]
Use the affine coordinates
\[
s=\frac{f_j}{\sqrt J},
\qquad
t=\frac{f_k}{\sqrt J}.
\tag{31}
\]
Their absolute Jacobian is
\[
\frac{|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_k)|}{J}=1,
\]
so they preserve planar area. They send \(O_i\) to \((0,0)\) and send the adjacent edge endpoints to
\[
F_i=(-A,B_0),\qquad E_i=(C_0,-D),
\tag{32}
\]
where
\[
A=\frac{a_i}{\sqrt J},\qquad
B_0=\frac{b_i}{\sqrt J},\qquad
C_0=\frac{c_i}{\sqrt J},\qquad
D=\frac{d_i}{\sqrt J}.
\tag{33}
\]
All four numbers are positive. The halfplanes \(H_j,H_k\) become
\[
\{s\leq0\},\qquad\{t\leq0\}.
\tag{34}
\]

Define the two nonnegative axis reaches
\[
T=\max\{t\geq0:(0,t)\in K\},
\qquad
S=\max\{s\geq0:(s,0)\in K\}.
\tag{35}
\]
The defining sets contain \(0\), and the maxima exist because \(K\) is compact. Convexity gives
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),F_i,(0,T)\}\subseteq K.
\tag{36}
\]
The positive \(t\)-axis is on the exterior side of the supporting line of \([O_i,F_i]\), while \(C\) is on the other closed side. Therefore the triangle in (36) meets \(C\) only on that supporting line, an area-zero set. The triangle lies in \(\{s\leq0\}\), and its area is \(AT/2\). Hence
\[
e_j\geq\frac{AT}{2}.
\tag{37}
\]
This remains valid when \(T=0\), when the triangle is degenerate. The identical argument at \([E_i,O_i]\) gives a triangle in \(\{t\leq0\}\), of area \(DS/2\), and hence
\[
e_k\geq\frac{DS}{2}.
\tag{38}
\]
The threshold hypotheses (15), together with
\[
\rho_{i,j}=\frac{AB_0}{2},
\qquad
\rho_{i,k}=\frac{C_0D}{2},
\tag{39}
\]
therefore imply
\[
T<B_0,\qquad S<C_0.
\tag{40}
\]

Let \(P=(s,t)\in K\) satisfy \(s>0,t>0\). The segment \([F_i,P]\) lies in \(K\) and crosses the positive \(t\)-axis at height
\[
t_P=\frac{At+B_0s}{A+s}
=\frac{A}{A+s}t+\frac{s}{A+s}B_0.
\tag{41}
\]
Thus \(t_P\leq T\). If \(t>T\), then both \(t\) and \(B_0\) would be strictly larger than \(T\) by (40), so their strict convex combination (41) would exceed \(T\), a contradiction. Hence
\[
t\leq T.
\tag{42}
\]
Similarly, the segment \([E_i,P]\) crosses the positive \(s\)-axis at
\[
s_P=\frac{Ds+C_0t}{D+t}
=\frac{D}{D+t}s+\frac{t}{D+t}C_0\leq S.
\tag{43}
\]
If \(s>S\), then (40) would make both entries in the strict convex combination in (43) larger than \(S\), again a contradiction. Therefore
\[
s\leq S.
\tag{44}
\]
The conclusions (42) and (44) also cover \(S=0\) or \(T=0\): in either case the open positive-quadrant part of \(K\) is empty. In all cases,
\[
|K\cap\{s>0,t>0\}|\leq ST.
\tag{45}
\]

Put \(W=K\setminus C\). Because \(C\) misses the open positive quadrant,
\[
W\cap\{s>0,t>0\}=K\cap\{s>0,t>0\}.
\tag{46}
\]
The complement of the open positive quadrant is exactly
\[
\{s\leq0\}\cup\{t\leq0\}.
\]
Inclusion-exclusion, with the axes included, gives the exact identity
\[
\delta-e_j-e_k
=|W\cap\{s>0,t>0\}|
-|W\cap\{s\leq0,t\leq0\}|.
\tag{47}
\]
Thus overlap of the two closed halfplanes can only decrease the left side; no boundary case has been discarded. Combining (37), (38), and (45)-(47) yields
\[
\delta-e_j-e_k
\leq ST
\leq\frac{2e_j}{A}\frac{2e_k}{D}
=\frac{4e_je_k}{AD}.
\tag{48}
\]
Finally,
\[
AD=\frac{a_id_i}{J}=\gamma_i.
\tag{49}
\]
Multiplying (48) by the positive number \(\gamma_i\) proves (16), including its factor \(4\). Rearrangement proves (17). Taking the logical complement of (17) at each of the three admissible corners gives exactly (18).

Now assume the common-core hypotheses (19)-(20), including all six thresholds. Choose \(i\) such that \(e_i=\max\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\). For its complementary cyclic pair \((j,k)\),
\[
e_j+e_k=E-e_i\leq\frac{2E}{3},
\qquad
4e_je_k\leq(e_j+e_k)^2\leq\frac{4E^2}{9}.
\tag{50}
\]
Using (16) at corner \(i\) and \(\gamma_i\geq\gamma\) gives
\[
\delta
\leq e_j+e_k+\frac{4e_je_k}{\gamma_i}
\leq\frac{2E}{3}+\frac{4E^2}{9\gamma}.
\]
This is (21), and its contrapositive is (22). Also, \(H_1\cup H_2\cup H_3=\mathbb R^2\), since three positive values cannot sum to \(-1\). Hence every common-core extension obeys the basic covering bound \(\delta\leq E\).

For the rational example, the successive edge-vector determinants of (24) are alternately \(1/4\) and \(5/16\), so the displayed order is strictly convex and counterclockwise. Its central vertices are
\[
O_1=(0,0),\qquad O_2=(-1,0),\qquad O_3=(0,-1).
\]
In each cyclic coordinate pair
\[
(f_2,f_3),\qquad(f_3,f_1),\qquad(f_1,f_2),
\]
the next and previous neighbors of the central vertex are respectively
\[
F=\left(-\frac12,\frac14\right),
\qquad
E=\left(\frac14,-\frac34\right).
\tag{51}
\]
Every vertex of \(B\) satisfies \(f_i\geq-1\) for every \(i\). By convexity the same holds throughout \(B\), and therefore
\[
f_j+f_k=-1-f_i\leq0
\]
throughout \(B\). This proves the missing-positive-quadrant condition (11) at all three corners.

Because the polygon is counterclockwise, its interior is on the left of each directed boundary edge. At \([O_i,F]\),
\[
\det(F,(0,T))=-\frac{T}{2}<0
\qquad(T>0),
\]
so the positive \(t\)-axis is exterior. At \([E,O_i]\), for \(S>0\),
\[
\det\left(
\left(-\frac14,\frac34\right),
\left(S-\frac14,\frac34\right)
\right)
=-\frac{3S}{4}<0,
\]
so the positive \(s\)-axis is exterior. Thus all three corners are admissible. Here \(J=1\), and (51) gives
\[
a_i=\frac12,\quad b_i=\frac14,\quad
c_i=\frac14,\quad d_i=\frac34,
\]
which proves (25).

For (26),
\[
e_j+e_k+\frac{4e_je_k}{\gamma_i}
=\frac1{16}
+\frac{4(1/32)^2}{3/8}
=\frac1{16}+\frac1{96}
=\frac7{96}
<\frac9{96}
=\delta.
\]
Thus every corner violates its necessary inequality (16). All threshold and reverse inequalities are strict, so the same exclusion holds on a relative open neighborhood of (26). This proves the final assertion.
