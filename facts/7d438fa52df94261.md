## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\{(x,y):0\le x+y\le20,\ |x-y|\le1/20\},
\qquad
D=\frac{11}{20}A_0.
\]
For every compact two-dimensional convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) of area \(991/1000\) containing \(D\), put
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{x\le0\}|,\qquad
c_\ell=|K\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
c_j=|K\cap\{x+y\ge-1\}|.
\]
Then
\[
c_i\ge\frac{213137}{480000}
\quad\text{or}\quad
c_\ell\ge\frac{213137}{480000}
\quad\text{or}\quad
c_j\ge\frac{163}{375}.
\]

## Proof

Use the invertible linear coordinates
\[
\sigma=x+y,\qquad \eta=x-y,
\]
whose Jacobian has absolute determinant \(2\).  Write \(P\) for the image of \(K\).  The image of \(D\) is the rectangle
\[
R=[0,11]\times[-a,a],
\qquad a=\frac{11}{400},
\]
and
\[
|P|=2|K|=\frac{991}{500}.
\tag{1}
\]

We first derive a pointwise consequence of \(R\subseteq P\).  Fix any
\(p=(\sigma,\eta)\in P\).  By convexity, \(P\) contains the convex hull
of \(p\) with each of the two horizontal edges
\[
E_-=[0,11]\times\{-a\},
\qquad
E_+=[0,11]\times\{a\}
\]
of \(R\).  These two possibly degenerate triangles have respective
\((\sigma,\eta)\)-areas
\[
\frac{11}{2}|\eta+a|,
\qquad
\frac{11}{2}|\eta-a|.
\]
Each is at most the area in (1), so
\[
|\eta+a|\le\frac{991}{2750},
\qquad
|\eta-a|\le\frac{991}{2750}.
\]
Consequently every point of \(P\) satisfies
\[
-\frac{7323}{22000}\le\eta\le\frac{7323}{22000}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(7323/22000<1\).

If \((\sigma,\eta)\in P\) and \(\sigma<-1\), then (2) gives
\[
\sigma+\eta< -1+\frac{7323}{22000}<0,
\qquad
\sigma-\eta< -1+\frac{7323}{22000}<0.
\]
Since \(x=(\sigma+\eta)/2\) and \(y=(\sigma-\eta)/2\), this proves the
pointwise inclusion
\[
K\cap\{x+y<-1\}
\subseteq
K\cap\{x<0,\ y<0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every affine line has planar area zero, including when its intersection
with \(K\) is a nontrivial support face.  Therefore (3) yields
\[
c_i\ge |K|-c_j,
\qquad
c_\ell\ge |K|-c_j.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(c_j\ge163/375\), the third alternative holds.  Otherwise (4)
implies
\[
c_i,c_\ell>
\frac{991}{1000}-\frac{163}{375}
=\frac{1669}{3000}
=\frac{213137}{480000}+\frac{53903}{480000},
\]
so in fact both side alternatives hold strictly.

For completeness, the three positions of the origin can be separated
directly.  Since \(0\in D\subseteq K\), the case \(0\notin K\) is
impossible.  If \(0\in\partial K\), choose a supporting linear form
\(u\sigma+v\eta\ge0\) for \(P\) at \(0\).  The two points
\((0,\pm a)\in R\) force \(v=0\), and \((11,0)\in R\), together with
nonzeroness of the supporting form, forces \(u>0\).  Hence
\(P\subseteq\{\sigma\ge0\}\), so
\[
c_j=|K|=\frac{991}{1000}>\frac{163}{375}.
\]
If \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\), the uniform argument (1)--(4) applies
without change.  It also applies when any cutting line meets \(K\) in
a segment or is supporting, because such a face has area zero.  The
argument uses the full horizontal edges of \(D\) and never assumes that
the simultaneous-low cell has positive area; hence it includes the
zero simultaneous-low endpoint cell.
