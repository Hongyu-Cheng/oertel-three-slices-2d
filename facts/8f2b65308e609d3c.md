## Statement

Accepted fact `4e176b68a1636efc` isolates the one-tail layer and its two
exact crossings.  Accepted fact `1299be50dd2920df` shows that an exact
convex-polytopal realization of the residual scalar calibration would
violate the proposed boundary payment, while accepted fact
`737c9ee76bf3cb91` excludes the endpoint family having only the three
common-row facets.

Let
\[
s,a,b,c,d,e,f,\delta>0,
\qquad c+d<\delta,\qquad e+f<\delta,
\]
and let \(P\) be the quadrilateral with vertices, in the displayed
clockwise order,
\[
p_0=(-a,\delta+a),\qquad
p_1=(\delta+b,-b),\qquad
p_2=(c,d),\qquad
p_3=(e,f).
\]
Assume that \(P\) is strictly convex and that
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5}.
\]
Then
\[
\delta>a+b.
\]
Consequently no such quadrilateral satisfies the strict support-order
inequality \(\delta<a+b\).  The conclusion holds a fortiori for rational
parameters.

## Proof

The hypotheses put \(p_0,p_1\) on the support line
\(x+y=\delta\), while \(p_2,p_3\) lie strictly below it.  Thus
\(\max_P(x+y)=\delta\).  Also \(p_0\) is the only vertex with negative
\(x\)-coordinate, and \(p_1\) is the only vertex with negative
\(y\)-coordinate, so both coordinate caps in the statement are
triangles.

Apply the area-preserving, orientation-reversing affine map
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(\xi,h)=(x+a,\delta-x-y).
\]
Put
\[
L=\delta+a+b,\qquad
C=a+c,\qquad E=a+e,\qquad
u=\delta-c-d,\qquad v=\delta-e-f.
\]
All five quantities are positive, and the transformed vertices are
\[
(0,0),\qquad(L,0),\qquad(C,u),\qquad(E,v)
\]
in strictly counterclockwise order.

The original line \(x=0\), which becomes \(\xi=a\), meets the two edges
incident with \(p_0\) at the fractions \(a/L\) and \(a/E\).  Hence its
triangular cap has area
\[
\frac{a^2v}{2E}=\frac{s}{8}.
\]
The original line \(y=0\), which becomes
\(\xi+h=\delta+a=L-b\), meets the two edges incident with \(p_1\) at
the fractions \(b/L\) and
\[
\frac{b}{b+d}=\frac{b}{L-C-u}.
\]
Its triangular cap therefore has area
\[
\frac{b^2u}{2(L-C-u)}=\frac{s}{5}.
\]
After clearing the positive denominators, the two cap equations are
\[
4a^2v=sE,\qquad
5b^2u=2s(L-C-u).                                      \tag{1}
\]
The shoelace formula for the whole transformed quadrilateral gives
\[
Lu+Cv-Eu=32s.                                         \tag{2}
\]

Define the positive polynomial
\[
H=2s+8a^2+5b^2.
\]
Equations (1) eliminate \(C,E\):
\[
E=\frac{8a^2v}{2s},\qquad
C=L-\frac{(2s+5b^2)u}{2s}.                           \tag{3}
\]
Substitution into (2), followed by multiplication by \(2s>0\), gives
\[
2sL(u+v)-Huv=64s^2.                                  \tag{4}
\]

Only two of the four consecutive strict-convexity determinants are
nonautomatic.  In the transformed coordinates they are
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_2&=\det\bigl((C-L,u),(E-C,v-u)\bigr),\\
D_3&=\det\bigl((E-C,v-u),(-E,-v)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using (3), they factor exactly as
\[
D_2=\frac{u}{2s}(2sL-Hv),\qquad
D_3=\frac{v}{2s}(2sL-Hu).                            \tag{5}
\]
Strict convexity and \(s,u,v>0\) therefore imply
\[
2sL-Hu>0,\qquad 2sL-Hv>0.                            \tag{6}
\]

Equations (4) and (6) now give the promised polynomial certificate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&<(2sL-Hu)(2sL-Hv)\\
&=4s^2L^2-H\bigl(2sL(u+v)-Huv\bigr)\\
&=4s^2(L^2-16H).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(L^2>16H\).  On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
16H-4(a+b)^2
&=32s+120a^2+72b^2+4(a-b)^2\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{8}
\]
It follows that \(L^2>4(a+b)^2\).  Since \(L>0\) and \(a+b>0\),
\[
L>2(a+b).
\]
Finally \(L=\delta+a+b\), so \(\delta>a+b\), as claimed.  This
contradicts the required inequality \(\delta<a+b\) without introducing
\(Q\) or \(R\).
