## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be arbitrary compact
two-dimensional convex bodies satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)
\]
and, for \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\left\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):
H\subset\mathbb R^3\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\right\}.
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
a=|A_1|,\qquad T=M+m+a,
\]
and assume
\[
M-m<a<M+m.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
\delta=\frac{2(M+m-a)}9.
\tag{3}
\]
If
\[
\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\delta}\right)^2
\geq\frac{20a}{9},
\tag{4}
\]
then the centroid \(g\) of \(A_1\) satisfies
\[
h_S((1,g))\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]

## Proof

The area inequalities (2) give
\[
0<\delta<\frac{4m}{9}<m.
\tag{6}
\]
They also imply that each slice area is strictly less than \(T/2\).
Indeed, \(a<M+m\), \(M<a+m\), and \(m<M+a\).  Therefore, with
\[
t=\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{7}
\]
one has
\[
t<\min\{|A_0|+a,a+|A_2|\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), put \(r=q\cdot g\), and define
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\leq c\}|.
\tag{9}
\]
The planar Grünbaum centroid inequality recorded in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` applies to the arbitrary convex body \(A_1\), not only
to triangles.  It gives
\[
F_{1,q}(r)\geq\frac{4a}{9}.
\tag{10}
\]
Since a line has planar area zero, the complementary open cap satisfies
\[
|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r\}|
=a-F_{1,q}(r)\leq\frac{5a}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]

We claim that the full endpoint infimal convolution obeys
\[
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(2r-c)\right)\geq\delta.
\tag{12}
\]
Suppose instead that, for some finite \(c\),
\[
u_0+u_2<\delta,\qquad
u_0=F_{0,q}(c),\qquad
u_2=F_{2,q}(2r-c).
\tag{13}
\]
Define the complementary endpoint caps
\[
U_0=A_0\cap\{x:q\cdot x>c\},\qquad
U_2=A_2\cap\{x:q\cdot x>2r-c\}.
\tag{14}
\]
By (6) and (13), both caps have positive area, and
\[
|U_0|=|A_0|-u_0,\qquad |U_2|=|A_2|-u_2.
\tag{15}
\]
The compatibility inclusion (1) retains the actual allocation \(c\):
\[
\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}
\subseteq A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r\}.
\tag{16}
\]
The planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality and (15)-(16) yield
\[
|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r\}|
\geq
\frac{\left(\sqrt{|A_0|-u_0}+
\sqrt{|A_2|-u_2}\right)^2}{4}.
\tag{17}
\]

Relabel the two losses as \(u_M,u_m\) according as they come from the
endpoint bodies of areas \(M,m\), and put \(d=u_M+u_m\).  Here
\(0\leq d<\delta<m\).  For fixed \(d\), the function
\[
x\longmapsto\sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-d+x},\qquad 0\leq x\leq d,
\]
is concave, so its minimum is at an endpoint.  Moreover,
\[
\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-d}\leq\sqrt m-\sqrt{m-d},
\]
because \(M\geq m\).  Hence the smaller endpoint value is obtained by
putting the entire loss on the body of area \(m\), and
\[
\sqrt{M-u_M}+\sqrt{m-u_m}
\geq\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-d}
>\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\delta}.
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (4), (17), and (18) gives
\[
|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r\}|>\frac{5a}{9},
\]
contrary to (11).  Thus every finite allocation satisfies the lower bound
in (12).  The compactified allocations \(c=-\infty\) and \(c=+\infty\)
have endpoint sums \(|A_2|\) and \(|A_0|\), respectively, and both are
at least \(m>\delta\) by (6).  This also covers the two limiting
allocations and proves (12).

Using the exact directional profile from accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, equations (10) and (12) give, for every
\(q\in\mathbb S^1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_q(q\cdot g)
&=F_{1,q}(r)+
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)\right)\\
&\geq\frac{4a}{9}+\delta
=\frac{2(M+m+a)}9=t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Condition (8) is precisely the range required for the exact depth-region
identity in that accepted fact.  Consequently \(g\) belongs to its region
\(D_t\), and hence \(h_S((1,g))\geq t\).

For completeness, horizontal halfspaces are included in the same exact
identity through the two terms \(|A_0|+a\) and \(a+|A_2|\).  Directly, any
horizontal halfspace containing height \(1\) contains the middle slice and
at least one endpoint slice, so (8) gives it mass strictly greater than
\(t\).

The displayed hypothesis (4) was used only after the strict inequality
\(d<\delta\) in the contradiction argument.  Thus (18) remains strict
when equality holds in (4), and the conclusion is the required
nonstrict bound.  Every other ingredient used above holds for arbitrary
compact two-dimensional convex bodies: lines have zero planar area,
halfplane intersections are measurable, (1) gives (16), the planar
Brunn-Minkowski inequality gives (17), and the planar Grünbaum inequality
gives (10).  This completes all boundary and shape cases.

## External sources

Planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality.  Exact statement used: for nonempty
bounded measurable \(X,Y\subset\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\left|\frac{X+Y}{2}\right|^{1/2}
\geq\frac{|X|^{1/2}+|Y|^{1/2}}2.
\]
Source: Richard J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of
the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1,
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2.  Here \(X=U_0\) and
\(Y=U_2\); they are nonempty bounded measurable convex sets.
