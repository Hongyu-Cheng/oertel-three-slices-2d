## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact two-dimensional convex polytopes. Put
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,\qquad 0<t<M.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), put
\[
H_i=\{f_i\le0\},\qquad c_i=|K\cap H_i|.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(L_1,L_2,L_3\in\mathbb R\), assume the support contact
\[
L_1=\min_{A_0}f_1,
\]
and, for \(i=2,3\), define
\[
I_i=\left\{\ell\in\mathbb R:
c_i+|A_0\cap\{f_i\le\ell\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-\ell\}|<t\right\}.
\]
Assume \(I_i\ne\varnothing\) and \(L_i=\inf I_i\) for \(i=2,3\). Write
\[
C=c_1,\qquad
v_1=|A_2\cap\{f_1\le-L_1\}|,\qquad
u_i=|A_0\cap\{f_i\le L_i\}|,\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-L_i\}|
\quad(i=2,3),
\tag{4}
\]
and retain the two exact crossing equalities supplied by accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`,
\[
c_i+u_i+v_i=t\qquad(i=2,3)
\tag{5}
\]
and all three canonical tail inequalities
\[
c_i+m\ge t\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{6}
\]
Assume a hypothetical strict contact failure, and define
\[
a=t-C>v_1\ge0,\qquad
d_i=u_i+v_i=t-c_i\quad(i=2,3).
\tag{7}
\]
Let
\[
0\le\lambda\le\mu\le\nu\le m
\tag{8}
\]
be the nondecreasing rearrangement of
\[
\{a,d_2,d_3\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Put
\[
\sigma=m+k-M.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\qquad
2\sigma\le4m-4\lambda-5\mu.
\qquad}
\tag{11}
\]

Under the strict residual no-half inequality
\[
M<m+k
\tag{12}
\]
from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, every contact-failure survivor therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{\qquad
4\lambda+5\mu<4m.
\qquad}
\tag{13}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mu<\frac{4m}{5}.
\tag{14}
\]

A contact or crossing row is called a tail-equality row when
\[
c_i+m=t.
\tag{15}
\]
For the contact row, (15) is equivalent to \(a=m\). For crossing row \(i=2,3\), equations (5) and (15) are equivalent to \(d_i=m\). Hence (13) implies:

1. at most one of the three rows is a tail-equality row;
2. at least two rows have strict tails;
3. the second-smallest of their three deficits is strictly below \(4m/5\).

Consequently contact failure is impossible whenever at least two tails are equalities. For a crossing row, accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` says that a tail equality permits exactly the empty-full or proper-proper cap state. Thus this conclusion closes all mixed empty-full/proper-proper assignments with two tail equalities, not merely the double-empty-full assignment covered explicitly by accepted fact `9e5ca82d9f9d55dd`.

## Proof

The tail inequalities and crossing equalities first give the complete boundary ranges. From (6)-(7),
\[
0\le a=t-C\le m,
\tag{16}
\]
and, for \(i=2,3\),
\[
0<d_i=u_i+v_i=t-c_i\le m.
\tag{17}
\]
The strict positivity in (17) follows from accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`, which gives \(v_i>0\) at every lower crossing. This proves (8).

The three halfplanes in (3) cover the plane. Indeed, a point outside all three would satisfy \(f_i>0\) for every \(i\), contradicting (2). By (7), their three \(K\)-cap areas are
\[
c_1=t-a,\qquad c_2=t-d_2,\qquad c_3=t-d_3.
\tag{18}
\]
After sorting as in (8)-(9), the largest cap in (18) has area
\[
t-\lambda,
\]
the second-largest has area
\[
t-\mu,
\]
and the third has area \(t-\nu\le t-\mu\).

Accepted fact `9e5ca82d9f9d55dd` proves the trimmed three-cap lemma, including zero caps, literal emptiness, support points, and positive-length support faces: if three proper closed halfplanes cover the plane and their cap areas have largest value \(D\), while the other two are at most \(p\le D\), then
\[
4|K|\le4D+5p.
\tag{19}
\]
Apply (19) with
\[
D=t-\lambda,\qquad p=t-\mu.
\]
Both are cap areas, so \(D\ge p\ge0\), and the third cap is at most \(p\) by construction. Therefore
\[
4k\le4(t-\lambda)+5(t-\mu)
=9t-4\lambda-5\mu.
\tag{20}
\]

The scalar identity needed to compare (20) with the no-half slack is exact. Equations (1) and (10) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
9t-4m+2\sigma
&=2(M+m+k)-4m+2(m+k-M)\\
&=4k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Combining (20) and (21) and cancelling \(9t\) gives
\[
-4m+2\sigma\le-4\lambda-5\mu,
\]
which is exactly (11).

Under (12), one has \(\sigma>0\). Inequality (11) is then strict after dropping \(2\sigma\):
\[
4\lambda+5\mu<4m,
\]
proving (13). Since \(\lambda\ge0\), equation (14) follows.

It remains to translate (13) back to the complete cap-state classification. The contact-tail equality \(C+m=t\) is exactly \(a=m\). For a crossing row, (5) gives
\[
c_i+m=t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u_i+v_i=m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d_i=m.
\tag{22}
\]
If at least two tails are equalities, at least two members of the multiset (9) equal \(m\). Because all three are at most \(m\), their second-smallest value is then
\[
\mu=m.
\]
But (13) would give
\[
4\lambda+5m<4m,
\]
which is impossible. This proves the asserted closure.

Finally, accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee` gives the exact endpoint-state interpretation of (22). At a crossing tail equality, the endpoint caps are either empty-full,
\[
u_i=0,\qquad v_i=m,
\]
or proper-proper,
\[
0<u_i<m,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad u_i+v_i=m.
\]
At a strict crossing tail, the state is empty-proper or proper-proper and \(d_i<m\). Hence the proof treats every empty/proper/full assignment and all null support boundaries. It also remains literal at a support-contact limit, because the only geometric input, (19), already includes \(p=0\), support points, and support faces. Once a strict-empty row is moved to its support-contact limit, no derivative or positive-clearance estimate is needed for any pattern covered by the two-tail-equality closure above.

The unique remaining support-contact failure class is therefore:
\[
\#\{a,d_2,d_3\text{ equal to }m\}\le1,
\qquad
4\lambda+5\mu<4m.
\tag{23}
\]
Equivalently, at least two tails are strict, and their endpoint states may be empty-proper or proper-proper. Distinguishing those states requires shared endpoint geometry; the middle-body three-cap trimming argument sees them only through the same deficits \(d_i\).
