## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex
polytopes satisfying
\[
B:=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\]
and define the total \(\mathrm{F0}\) deficit
\[
\Delta_0:=4k-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Delta_0=
\left(4|B|-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2\right)
+4\left(k-|B|\right).
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Both terms on the right are nonnegative.  In particular,
\[
0\leq4|B|-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2\leq\Delta_0,
\qquad
0\leq k-|B|\leq\frac{\Delta_0}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix any three affine cutting lines and their exact weak/strict sign-cell
convention.  Let \(r_G(K)\) and \(r_G(B)\) be the corresponding exact
cell areas in \(K\) and \(B\).  Then
\[
r_G(B)\leq r_G(K)\quad\text{for every }G,
\]
\[
\sum_G\left(r_G(K)-r_G(B)\right)
=k-|B|\leq\frac{\Delta_0}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus every middle exact-cell area changes by at most
\(\Delta_0/4\) when \(K\) is replaced by its midpoint body, uniformly
over all endpoint coincidences and cutting-line boundaries.

There is also a genuine shape modulus.  There exists a function
\(\Omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with
\(\Omega(s)\to0\) as \(s\downarrow0\), such that whenever
\[
3\leq\frac{M}{m}\leq5,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_0}{m}\leq s,
\tag{5}
\]
there are homothetic convex bodies \(H_0,H_2\) satisfying
\[
A_0\subseteq H_0,\qquad A_2\subseteq H_2,
\]
\[
|H_0\setminus A_0|+|H_2\setminus A_2|
\leq\Omega(s)m.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(s=0\), one may take
\[
H_0=A_0,\qquad H_2=A_2,\qquad \Omega(0)=0.
\]
Together, (3), (4), and (6) say that, as the normalized
\(\mathrm{F0}\) deficit tends to zero, the outer bodies approach
homothetic equality in area and the full canonical cell vector approaches
that of the minimal family
\[
(A_0,B,A_2).
\tag{7}
\]
Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` makes (7) a valid three-slice polytope,
and accepted fact `96f5513b8ab88a8f` proves the target centerpoint theorem
for it.

Now impose the complete canonical round-054 hypotheses, \(W=0\), and the
fixed critical ratios
\[
\frac{x_1}{q}=\frac{x_2}{q}=\frac{x_3}{q}=\frac5{21}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus
\[
m=\frac{12q}{7}.
\tag{9}
\]
Inside the canonical counterexample branch, the total deficit satisfies
\[
\boxed{\ \Delta_0\geq3m.\ }
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently no such counterexample can approach total
Brunn--Minkowski equality.  At the sharp scalar normalization
\[
m=1,\qquad q=\frac7{12},\qquad
M\longrightarrow4,\qquad k\longrightarrow\frac94,
\tag{11}
\]
the certified outer-centroid lower-bound margin satisfies
\[
\frac{4M}{9}-\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
\longrightarrow\frac16.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
\liminf\left(
h_S((0,g_0))-\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
\right)\geq\frac16.
\]
Hence the minimal-family conclusion transfers to a whole neighborhood
of the dangerous equality calibration, not merely to its limit.

Finally, for every \(W=0\) table under the hypotheses of accepted fact
`084bf2f472bf29c7`, the exact scalar bridge strengthens to
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
\geq
\left(
2\sqrt{r_{123}}
+
\frac{
\sqrt m+
\sqrt{120m+84q+28\eta+7\Delta_0}
}{7}
\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(c_*>0\) be the universal constant of accepted fact
`af9178be3aa6202a`, and put
\[
c_0=\max\left\{c_*,\frac67\right\}.
\]
At the fixed ratios (8), equations (10), (13), and the defect bound
recorded in `af9178be3aa6202a` give the necessary strict scale inequality
\[
D<
\tau_{\mathrm{mid}}
:=
\frac{49}{
\left(
\sqrt{12/7}
+

This is strictly stronger than the threshold in

## Proof

The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality used in row \(\mathrm{F0}\) of
accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3` gives
\[
4|B|=|A_0+A_2|\geq(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2.
\tag{15}
\]
The midpoint inclusion gives \(|B|\leq k\).  Adding
\[
4|B|-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2
\]
and \(4(k-|B|)\) proves the identity (2), and their nonnegativity proves
(3).

For the exact cells, \(B\subseteq K\) implies
\[
B\cap\mathcal C_G\subseteq K\cap\mathcal C_G
\]
for each sign chamber \(\mathcal C_G\).  The exact cells partition each
body up to the cutting lines, which have area zero.  Therefore their
area differences are nonnegative and sum to
\(|K\setminus B|=k-|B|\).  Equation (4) follows from (3).  This proves
the cell-area transfer without a generic-position assumption.

We next prove the shape modulus.  First suppose \(s=0\).  Then (5) and
\(\Delta_0\geq0\) give \(\Delta_0=0\).  Equation (2) forces
\[
|A_0+A_2|=(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2.
\]
The equality characterization in the planar Brunn--Minkowski theorem,
stated below, applies because \(A_0,A_2\) are full-dimensional convex
bodies of positive area.  It shows that \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) are
homothetic.  Taking \(H_0=A_0\), \(H_2=A_2\), and \(\Omega(0)=0\)
proves (6) in this case.

Now suppose \(s>0\).  The external near-equality theorem stated below
applies to \(A_0,A_2\), because they are Borel sets of positive finite
area and (5) confines \(M/m\) to the compact interval \([3,5]\).  By
(3),
\[
|A_0+A_2|-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2\leq\Delta_0.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{|A_0+A_2|}-\sqrt M-\sqrt m
&=
\frac{
|A_0+A_2|-(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2
}{
\sqrt{|A_0+A_2|}+\sqrt M+\sqrt m
}\\
&\leq
\frac{\Delta_0}{\sqrt{\max\{M,m\}}}\\
&\leq
s\sqrt{\max\{M,m\}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The theorem supplies homothetic convex supersets \(H_0,H_2\) with
each excess area at most
\(\varepsilon_{[3,5]}(s)\max\{M,m\}\).  Since
\(\max\{M,m\}\leq5m\), (6) follows for \(s>0\) with
\[
\Omega(s)=10\varepsilon_{[3,5]}(s).
\]
Together with the definition at zero, the theorem gives
\(\Omega(s)\to0\).

For completeness, intersecting \(A_0,H_0,A_2,H_2\) with any fixed
finite sign partition shows
\[
\sum_J\bigl||H_0\cap\mathcal C_J|
-|A_0\cap\mathcal C_J|\bigr|
=|H_0\setminus A_0|,
\]
and the analogous identity holds for \(A_2,H_2\).  Combining these
identities with (4) proves the asserted convergence of every canonical
cell area.  The replacement \(K\mapsto B\) is exactly the minimal family
covered by `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` and `96f5513b8ab88a8f`.

We now specialize to (8).  Equation (9) is immediate.  Accepted fact
`084bf2f472bf29c7` proves
\[
\sqrt{p_0}\geq
\frac{\sqrt m+\sqrt{120m+84q+28\eta}}7.
\]
Since \(\eta\geq0\) and \(q=7m/12\),
\[
\sqrt{p_0}
\geq
\frac{\sqrt m+\sqrt{169m}}7
=2\sqrt m.
\]
Thus
\[
M\geq p_0\geq4m.
\tag{17}
\]

By definition of \(\Delta_0\),
\[
k=\frac{M+m+2\sqrt{Mm}+\Delta_0}{4}.
\]
Write \(s_0=\sqrt{M/m}\geq2\).  Then
\[
M-m-k
=
\frac m4
\left(
3s_0^2-2s_0-5-\frac{\Delta_0}{m}
\right).
\tag{18}
\]
The function \(3s^2-2s-5\) is increasing for \(s\geq2\) and equals
\(3\) at \(s=2\).  Therefore
\[
\Delta_0<3m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M>m+k.
\tag{19}
\]

Let \(g_0\) be the centroid of \(A_0\).  For any closed halfspace in
\(\mathbb R^3\) containing \((0,g_0)\), its intersection with the
height-zero plane is either the whole plane or a closed planar halfplane
containing \(g_0\).  Grünbaum's planar centroid inequality, stated below,
therefore gives
\[
h_S((0,g_0))\geq\frac{4M}{9}.
\tag{20}
\]
If (19) holds, then
\[
\frac{4M}{9}
>
\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the desired centerpoint conclusion already holds with strict
certified margin.  Such a configuration cannot lie in the canonical
counterexample branch.  This proves (10).  Substitution of the limits
(11) into the lower-bound difference on the two sides of (21) gives
(12).  Combining that convergence with (20) gives the stated liminf
bound for the actual depth margin.  No matching upper bound on the
actual depth is asserted.  This is also the required stability transfer
from the minimal family: its outer-centroid witness retains a positive
certified margin when \(K\) and the scalar totals are perturbed.

It remains to derive the strengthened bridge.  Row \(\mathrm{F0}\) says
exactly
\[
4k=(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+\Delta_0.
\]
Since \(M\geq p_0\),
\[
4k\geq(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt m)^2+\Delta_0.
\tag{22}
\]
The \(W=0\) identity in `084bf2f472bf29c7` is
\[
2p_0=4m+3q+k+\eta.
\tag{23}
\]
Put \(z=\sqrt{p_0}\).  Combining (22)--(23), clearing the positive
denominator, and collecting terms gives
\[
7z^2-2\sqrt m\,z
-(17m+12q+4\eta+\Delta_0)\geq0.
\]
The other root is negative, so
\[
\sqrt{p_0}
\geq
\frac{
\sqrt m+
\sqrt{120m+84q+28\eta+7\Delta_0}
}{7}.
\tag{24}
\]
The common-plane ambient-triangle scale inequality used in
`084bf2f472bf29c7` is
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
\geq
(\sqrt{p_0}+2\sqrt{r_{123}})^2.
\]
Combining it with (24) proves (13).

At (8), accepted fact `af9178be3aa6202a` gives
\[
\frac{\eta}{q}\geq
\max\{c_*,\epsilon(5/21,5/21,5/21)\}.
\]
The eight values in the finite minimum defining \(g\), grouped by the
cardinality of \(S\), are
\[
\frac{10}{7},\qquad
\frac{20}{21},\qquad
\frac57,\qquad
\frac57.
\]
Thus \(g=5/7\), \(R=5/7\), and
\[
\epsilon=\frac{1-R}{2}+g=\frac67.
\]
Therefore \(\eta/q\geq c_0\).  Divide (13) by \(q\), use
\[
\frac mq=\frac{12}{7},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_0}{q}\geq
3\frac mq=\frac{36}{7},
\]
and drop the strictly positive \(2\sqrt{r_{123}/q}\) term.  This gives
\[
\frac1D
>
\frac1{49}
\left(
\sqrt{12/7}
+
\sqrt{
120\frac{12}{7}+84+28c_0+7\frac{36}{7}

\]
which simplifies to (14).

## External sources

Michael Christ, "Near Equality in the Two-Dimensional Brunn--Minkowski
Inequality", Theorem 1.1 and the classical equality characterization
recalled in its introduction,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1965 .  Exact statements used:

1. If \(A,C\subset\mathbb R^2\) are positive-area convex bodies, then
\[
\sqrt{|A+C|}\geq\sqrt{|A|}+\sqrt{|C|},
\]
with equality if and only if \(A\) and \(C\) are homothetic.

2. For every compact \(\Lambda\subset(0,\infty)\), there is a function
\(\varepsilon_\Lambda(\delta)\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\) such that
positive-area Borel sets \(A,C\subset\mathbb R^2\) with
\(|A|/|C|\in\Lambda\) and
\[
\sqrt{|A+C|}
\leq\sqrt{|A|}+\sqrt{|C|}
+\delta\sqrt{\max\{|A|,|C|\}}
\]
are contained in homothetic convex bodies \(H_A,H_C\), respectively,
with
\[
|H_A\setminus A|,
\ |H_C\setminus C|
<
\varepsilon_\Lambda(\delta)\max\{|A|,|C|\}.
\]
The equality statement applies in the \(s=0\) branch, and all hypotheses
of the near-equality theorem are verified in (5), (16) for \(s>0\).

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies
by Hyperplanes", Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), Theorem 2,
https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf .  Exact specialization
used: if \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a convex body with centroid \(g\),
then every closed planar halfspace containing \(g\) has intersection
with \(A\) of area at least \(4|A|/9\).  It applies to the
two-dimensional convex polytope \(A_0\) in (20).
