## Statement

Let \(\widehat\Delta\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(\widehat D\), let \(\widehat v_0\) be one of its vertices, and let
\[
\widehat A_i
=
\widehat v_0+\lambda_i(\widehat\Delta-\widehat v_0),
\qquad
\lambda_i>0,\quad i=0,1,2.
\]
Apply an invertible affine planar map sending the vertices of
\(\widehat\Delta\) to \(0,e_1,e_2\). Reset notation in the transformed coordinates by writing
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,e_2\},
\qquad
D_*:=\operatorname{area}(\Delta)=\frac12,
\]
and \(A_i=\lambda_i\Delta\). For barycentric coordinates
\(\beta_j\ge0\), \(\beta_0+\beta_1+\beta_2=1\), put
\[
z=\lambda_1(\beta_1e_1+\beta_2e_2)\in A_1.
\]

Every nonhorizontal halfspace whose boundary contains \((1,z)\) has the form
\[
H(L,\sigma)=
\{(x,w):L(w-z)\le \sigma(1-x)\},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(L\ne0\) is linear and \(\sigma\in\mathbb R\). Positive rescaling of \((L,\sigma)\) changes neither the halfspace nor its mass. Put
\[
c_j=L(v_j),\qquad c_0=0,
\]
and normalize
\[
\max_jc_j-\min_jc_j=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Choose a permutation \(\pi=(\pi_0,\pi_1,\pi_2)\) satisfying
\(c_{\pi_0}\le c_{\pi_1}\le c_{\pi_2}\). There is a unique
\(\rho\in[0,1]\) such that
\[
c_{\pi_0}=\gamma,\qquad
c_{\pi_1}=\gamma+\rho,\qquad
c_{\pi_2}=\gamma+1,
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
\gamma=
\begin{cases}
0,&\pi_0=0,\\
-\rho,&\pi_1=0,\\
-1,&\pi_2=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
Conversely, every permutation, every \(\rho\in[0,1]\), and (3)-(4) define a normalized nonzero \(L\).

Define the transformed cap fraction
\[
\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(t)
=
\frac{\operatorname{area}\{u\in\Delta:L(u)\le t\}}{D_*}.
\]
For \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\le\gamma,\\
\dfrac{(t-\gamma)^2}{\rho},
&\gamma\le t\le\gamma+\rho,\\
1-\dfrac{(\gamma+1-t)^2}{1-\rho},
&\gamma+\rho\le t\le\gamma+1,\\
1,&t\ge\gamma+1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
The exact tied-endpoint splines are
\[
\Phi_{\gamma,0}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\le\gamma,\\
1-(\gamma+1-t)^2,&\gamma\le t\le\gamma+1,\\
1,&t\ge\gamma+1,
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\Phi_{\gamma,1}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&t\le\gamma,\\
(t-\gamma)^2,&\gamma\le t\le\gamma+1,\\
1,&t\ge\gamma+1.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
m=\sum_{j=0}^2\beta_jc_j,
\qquad
t_i=\frac{\lambda_1m+\sigma(1-i)}{\lambda_i}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then the exact transformed mass is
\[
\frac{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H(L,\sigma))}{D_*}
=
M_\pi(\beta,\rho,\sigma)
:=
\sum_{i=0}^2\lambda_i^2
\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(t_i).
\tag{9}
\]
The corresponding original mass divided by \(\widehat D\) has the same right-hand side. Consequently,
\[
\frac{h_S(1,z)}{D_*}
=
\min\left\{
\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2,\
\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2,\
\inf_{\substack{\pi\in\mathfrak S_3\\
\rho\in[0,1],\ \sigma\in\mathbb R}}
M_\pi(\beta,\rho,\sigma)
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The identical formula holds for the original depth divided by \(\widehat D\).

For fixed \((\pi,\rho,\beta)\), let
\[
(b_0,b_1,b_2)=(\gamma,\gamma+\rho,\gamma+1).
\]
All finite slope breakpoints are
\[
B_{0,k}=\lambda_0b_k-\lambda_1m,\qquad
B_{2,k}=\lambda_1m-\lambda_2b_k,
\qquad k=0,1,2.
\tag{11}
\]
The two finite-slope tail values are
\[
M_\pi(\beta,\rho,-\infty)
=
\lambda_1^2\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(m)+\lambda_2^2,
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
M_\pi(\beta,\rho,+\infty)
=
\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(m).
\tag{13}
\]
Each is constant on a nonempty finite-\(\sigma\) ray. On every branch cell with \(0<\rho<1\), an interior slope-direction minimum satisfies
\[
\partial_\sigma M_\pi(\beta,\rho,\sigma)=0,
\qquad
\partial_\rho M_\pi(\beta,\rho,\sigma)=0.
\tag{14}
\]

For fixed algebraic \(\lambda_i\) and algebraic barycentric coordinates \(\beta_j\), equations (3)-(14), their branch inequalities, and the recursive boundary systems described below reduce the exact infimum in (10) to finitely many real-algebraic polynomial systems. The cases \(\rho=0\), \(\rho=1\), and the two finite-slope tails (12)-(13) are separate one-dimensional systems.

## Proof

Let \(F:\Delta\to\widehat\Delta\) be the affine map taking
\(0,e_1,e_2\) to the three original vertices. Its linear determinant has absolute value
\[
\frac{\widehat D}{D_*}=2\widehat D.
\]
Thus it maps halfspaces bijectively to halfspaces and multiplies every transformed planar slice area by \(\widehat D/D_*\). In particular, transformed mass divided by \(D_*\) equals original mass divided by \(\widehat D\). No transformed Euclidean area is divided by the original area.

By accepted lemma `400272f6de6c67bb`, the depth at \((1,z)\) may be computed from boundary-through-\((1,z)\) halfspaces, together with the two horizontal halfspaces. Every nonhorizontal such halfspace has form (1). Since \(L\ne0\), its values on a nondegenerate triangle are not all equal, so positive rescaling gives (2). Ordering the values gives a permutation \(\pi\) and
\(\rho=c_{\pi_1}-c_{\pi_0}\in[0,1]\). Because \(c_0=0\), the position of vertex \(0\) in the ordering forces precisely the three cases in (4). This proves the direction parametrization, including all six oriented chambers and their tied boundaries.

For \(0<\rho<1\), a threshold between the two lower projected vertex values cuts off a triangle similar to the minimum-vertex corner. Its area ratio is
\[
\frac{(t-\gamma)^2}
{(c_{\pi_1}-c_{\pi_0})(c_{\pi_2}-c_{\pi_0})}
=
\frac{(t-\gamma)^2}{\rho}.
\]
Above the middle value, the omitted maximum-vertex corner has area ratio
\[
\frac{(\gamma+1-t)^2}
{(c_{\pi_2}-c_{\pi_0})(c_{\pi_2}-c_{\pi_1})}
=
\frac{(\gamma+1-t)^2}{1-\rho}.
\]
This proves (5). If the two lower values coincide, only the upper formula remains and gives (6). If the two upper values coincide, only the lower formula remains and gives (7). The formulas agree at every knot. Since a cutting line has planar area zero, all closed-endpoint conventions give the same cap area.

On transformed slice \(i\), write \(w=\lambda_i u\) with \(u\in\Delta\). Since \(L(z)=\lambda_1m\), inequality (1) becomes
\[
L(u)\le
\frac{\lambda_1m+\sigma(1-i)}{\lambda_i}=t_i.
\]
The transformed slice mass is
\(D_*\lambda_i^2\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(t_i)\), proving (8)-(9). The two transformed horizontal masses are
\(D_*(\lambda_0^2+\lambda_1^2)\) and
\(D_*(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)\), so (10) follows from
`400272f6de6c67bb`. Multiplication by \(\widehat D/D_*\) gives the asserted original-coordinate formula.

For the bottom slice, a spline branch changes exactly when \(t_0=b_k\), equivalently \(\sigma=B_{0,k}\). For the top slice, it changes exactly when \(t_2=b_k\), equivalently \(\sigma=B_{2,k}\). Thus (11) is the complete finite breakpoint list.

The negative tail is the finite ray
\[
\sigma\le \min\{B_{0,0},B_{2,2}\}.
\]
There the bottom cap is empty and the top cap is full, giving (12). The positive tail is the finite ray
\[
\sigma\ge \max\{B_{0,2},B_{2,0}\}.
\]
There the bottom cap is full and the top cap is empty, giving (13). These tail values must still be minimized over \(\rho\); they are not limits requiring an infinite parameter.

For \(0<\rho<1\), assign independently to \(t_0,m,t_2\) one of the four branches in (5). Together with the six permutations this gives at most
\(6\cdot4^3=384\) branch cells in the variables \((\rho,\sigma)\). Each cell is defined by
\[
0<\rho<1
\]
and affine branch inequalities comparing \(t_0,m,t_2\) with
\(\gamma,\gamma+\rho,\gamma+1\). On a fixed cell,
\(M_\pi\) is a rational function of the two variables \((\rho,\sigma)\). Therefore an interior minimum satisfies both equations in (14). Keeping \((\rho,\sigma)\) as variables, multiply each derivative equation by a sufficiently large positive power of
\(\rho(1-\rho)\). This produces polynomial equations without solving either equation or dividing by a coefficient depending on \(\rho\).

Every finite boundary of a branch cell is obtained by setting one of its defining branch inequalities to equality. Equivalently, it is one of
\[
t_0=b_k,\qquad m=b_k,\qquad t_2=b_k.
\]
The first and third families are precisely the breakpoint equations in (11). On a codimension-one boundary \(g(\rho,\sigma)=0\), retain \(g=0\), retain all remaining branch inequalities, and impose the tangential stationary condition for the rational restriction of \(M_\pi\). This condition can be written without division by adjoining a multiplier \(\mu\), imposing
\[
\nabla M_\pi=\mu\nabla g,
\]
and clearing only the everywhere-positive powers of \(\rho(1-\rho)\). At an intersection of boundaries, set all active defining inequalities to equality and apply the same tangential condition with one multiplier for each active equality. Recursing over the finite set of defining inequalities reaches all edges and corners.

This active-boundary construction does not solve (14) for \(\sigma\). Consequently, it automatically retains cells on which a derivative coefficient vanishes, stationary points that contact a breakpoint, coincident breakpoints, and positive-dimensional stationary sets. If the active boundary gradients lose rank, the corresponding polynomial multiplier system may contain extra candidates but cannot omit a true constrained minimum; alternatively, all Jacobian rank-drop minors may be adjoined as a finite additional stratum.

The direction endpoints \(\rho=0\) and \(\rho=1\) are not obtained by clearing the interior spline denominators. They are evaluated separately with (6) and (7). For each endpoint, (11) gives a finite subdivision of the \(\sigma\)-line; on every bounded interval the mass is polynomial in \(\sigma\), so its candidates are its stationary points and interval endpoints, together with the two finite rays.

Finally, the tail functions (12)-(13) are treated separately on \(0<\rho<1\). Subdivide the \(\rho\)-interval according to the branch of
\(\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(m)\), impose its one-variable stationary equation after clearing positive powers of \(\rho(1-\rho)\), and include every branch boundary and the separately evaluated endpoints \(\rho=0,1\).

There are finitely many permutations, branch assignments, active-boundary subsets, endpoint cells, and tail cells. Every resulting equation and inequality is polynomial after the stated denominator clearing, with rational or real-algebraic coefficients whenever the input data are. Interior minima satisfy (14); every noninterior minimum lies on one of the recursively included boundaries, direction endpoints, or finite-slope tails. Hence the finite family of polynomial systems contains the exact global minimizer.
