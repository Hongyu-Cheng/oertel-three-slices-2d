## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},
\qquad
\mathcal C(w;\tau)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:w\lambda\ge\tau\}\right|,
\]
where \(|\Delta|=1\).  Fix the calibration of accepted fact `7d66445eba62df98`,
\[
A=\frac18,\qquad B=\frac{11249}{10000},\qquad
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-32877&46362&-17003\\
-67122&2&53678\\
100000&-46363&-36674
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
t=\frac{22499}{45000}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and every
\(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfying
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,qquad
|\det U|=A,qquad |\det V|=B,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{ij}+\min_jV_{ij}\ge0
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{3}
\]
one has
\[
A\mathcal C((LU)_{3*};-\sigma_3)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{3*};\sigma_3)
\ge
\eta,
\qquad
\eta:=\frac{3909493241}{50010041655000}.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover
\[
\mathcal C(L_{3*};0)
=\frac{5000000000}{10002008331},
\qquad
\eta=t-\mathcal C(L_{3*};0).
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently the third row mass in the exact system of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` is always at least \(t\).  In particular, no real,
and hence no rational, tuple \((U,V,\sigma)\) satisfies all three
strict original row conditions for the fixed data (1).

## Proof

Write
\[
g=L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),
\qquad
p=100000,\qquad a=46363,\qquad b=36674.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(-a<-b<0<p\), the one-positive cap formula in accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` gives
\[
\mathcal C(g;0)
=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}
=\frac{5000000000}{10002008331}.
\]
Direct rational subtraction from the value of \(t\) in accepted fact
`7d66445eba62df98` gives (5).  It therefore suffices to prove (4).

We first derive, without selecting any minimum-attaining column, a
lossless parameterization of every pair satisfying (2)-(3).  Put
\[
\ell_i=\min_jU_{ij},\qquad
n_i=\min_jV_{ij},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,qquad
s=\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3.
\tag{7}
\]
Every column of \(U\) sums to one, so \(s\le1\).  Equality would make
all nonnegative numbers \(U_{ij}-\ell_i\) have zero column sum, hence
all columns of \(U\) would equal \(\ell\), contradicting
\(|\det U|=A>0\).  Thus \(s<1\).  The same argument gives
\(\sum_i n_i<1\).  Summing (3) now yields
\[
s\ge-\sum_i n_i>-1.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{1-s},
\qquad
Y=\frac{V+\ell\mathbf1^\top}{1+s}.
\tag{9}
\]
The matrix \(X\) is entrywise nonnegative.  Also
\(V_{ij}+\ell_i\ge n_i+\ell_i\ge0\), so \(Y\) is entrywise
nonnegative.  Their columns sum to one, and
\[
U=\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1-s)X,
\qquad
V=-\ell\mathbf1^\top+(1+s)Y.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(\mathbf1^\top X=\mathbf1^\top Y=\mathbf1^\top\), the matrix
determinant lemma applied to
\[
U=((1-s)I+\ell\mathbf1^\top)X,
\qquad
V=((1+s)I-\ell\mathbf1^\top)Y
\]
gives
\[
\det U=(1-s)^2\det X,
\qquad
\det V=(1+s)^2\det Y.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus, with
\[
\alpha=|\det X|=\frac{A}{(1-s)^2},
\qquad
\beta=|\det Y|=\frac{B}{(1+s)^2},
\tag{12}
\]
the images \(T_X=X\Delta\) and \(T_Y=Y\Delta\) are nondegenerate
subtriangles of \(\Delta\) of normalized areas \(\alpha\) and
\(\beta\).  Indeed, entrywise nonnegativity and column stochasticity
put the images inside \(\Delta\); on the sum-zero tangent plane the
two-dimensional determinant is the full determinant because the
induced quotient map is the identity.  This also shows explicitly
that neither sign of \(\det X\) or \(\det Y\) has been excluded.

Put
\[
d=L\ell+\sigma.
\tag{13}
\]
Using (10), the two vectors defining the third endpoint caps become
\[
(LU)_{3*}+\sigma_3\mathbf1^\top
=d_3\mathbf1^\top+(1-s)gX,
\]
\[
(LV)_{3*}-\sigma_3\mathbf1^\top
=(1+s)gY-d_3\mathbf1^\top.
\tag{14}
\]
Translation by a constant multiple of \(\mathbf1^\top\), together
with positive homogeneity and \(\mathbf1^\top\lambda=1\) on
\(\Delta\), therefore gives
\[
\rho_X:=\mathcal C((LU)_{3*};-\sigma_3)
=\mathcal C\left(gX;-\frac{d_3}{1-s}\right),
\]
\[
\rho_Y:=\mathcal C((LV)_{3*};\sigma_3)
=\mathcal C\left(gY;\frac{d_3}{1+s}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Because \(X\) and \(Y\) are affine bijections from \(\Delta\) onto
\(T_X\) and \(T_Y\) with constant area multipliers \(\alpha\) and
\(\beta\), the two quantities in (15) are exactly the corresponding
upper-cap area fractions inside \(T_X\) and \(T_Y\).

We next prove a cap lemma only in the range used below.  Let \(T\) be
a nondegenerate triangle of area \(\theta\), let \(f\) be affine on
\(T\), and suppose
\[
0\le
\rho:=\frac{|T\cap\{f\ge z\}|}{\theta}
<1.
\]
Then \(T\cap\{f\le z\}\) contains a nondegenerate triangle of area at
least
\[
\theta(1-\sqrt\rho).
\tag{16}
\]
If \(\rho=0\), no point of \(T\) has \(f>z\), since such a point would
have a positive-area relative neighborhood on which \(f>z\).  Hence
\(T\subseteq\{f\le z\}\), and \(T\) itself proves (16).  Now assume
\(0<\rho<1\).  Then \(f\) is nonconstant and the cut has one of the
two nontrivial closed-cap forms in accepted fact 64760c76cadff481.
If the upper cap is, up to its boundary, a corner triangle, let
\(r_1,r_2\in(0,1]\) be its edge-intercept ratios from the upper
vertex.  The exact cap formula gives \(\rho=r_1r_2\).  The lower
quadrilateral contains a triangle on the opposite edge of area ratio
\[
1-\min\{r_1,r_2\}\ge1-\sqrt\rho.
\]
In the other nontrivial form, the lower cap is itself a corner
triangle of area ratio \(1-\rho\ge1-\sqrt\rho\).  These two forms
include distinct and repeated vertex values and cuts through a
vertex; the closed boundaries have zero area because \(f\) is
nonconstant.  Constant \(f\) can occur under \(\rho<1\) only when
\(\rho=0\), already handled.  Thus (16) is literal for every
ordering, multiplicity, and threshold boundary in its quantified
range.
For \(z\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
K_z=\{\lambda\in\Delta:g\lambda\le z\},
\]
and define its triangle capacity by
\[
m(z):=
\sup\left(
\left\{|\Theta|:
\Theta\subseteq K_z\text{ is a nondegenerate triangle}\right\}
\cup\{0\}
\right),
\]
with all areas normalized by \(|\Delta|=1\).  Thus \(m(z)=0\) by
definition when \(K_z\) is empty or contains no nondegenerate
triangle.  An exact computation gives
\[
m(z)=
\begin{cases}
0,&z\le-a,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(z+a)^2}{(a-b)(p+a)},&-a\le z\le-b,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{z+a}{p+a},&-b\le z\le p,\\[3mm]
1,&z\ge p.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]
For \(z<-a\), \(K_z\) is empty, and for \(z=-a\) it is the
single \(-a\) vertex, so the first branch is exactly the convention
in the definition of \(m\).  For \(-a<z<-b\), \(K_z\) is the similar
corner triangle at that vertex, which gives the second branch; its
values at \(z=-a\) and \(z=-b\) agree with the adjacent branches.
For \(z\ge p\), \(K_z=\Delta\), which gives the fourth branch.

For \(-b<z<p\), the set \(K_z\) is a genuine quadrilateral obtained
by cutting the \(p\) vertex.  Put
\[
r_a=\frac{z+a}{p+a},
\qquad
r_b=\frac{z+b}{p+b}.
\]
A maximum-area triangle in this compact convex polygon may be chosen
with its vertices among the polygon vertices: with two vertices fixed,
the absolute area is the absolute value of an affine function of the
third vertex and attains its maximum at a polygon vertex, and this
replacement may be repeated for all three vertices.  The four vertex
triangles have area ratios
\[
r_a,\qquad r_b,\qquad r_a(1-r_b),\qquad r_b(1-r_a).
\]
Because \(0<r_b<r_a<1\), their maximum is \(r_a\), proving the third
branch on the open interval.  At \(z=-b\) the quadrilateral degenerates
to the triangle described by either adjacent branch, and at \(z=p\)
it degenerates to \(\Delta\); the displayed formulas agree at both
endpoints.

At the only relevant join,
\[
m(-b)=\frac{a-b}{p+a}=\frac{9689}{146363}<\frac19,
\qquad
\frac19-m(-b)=\frac{59162}{1317267}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently, for every \(w>1/9\),
\[
m(z)>w\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z>(p+a)w-a.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, (18) excludes \(z\le-b\); the third branch of (17) proves
the implication for \(z<p\), while for \(z\ge p\) it follows from
\(w<1=m(z)\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that (4) fails strictly:
\[
A\rho_X+B\rho_Y<\eta.
\tag{20}
\]
Both cap fractions are nonnegative, so
\[
\rho_X<\frac\eta A,
\qquad
\rho_Y<\frac\eta B.
\tag{21}
\]
The exact rational margins are
\[
\frac1{81}-\frac\eta A
=\frac{219799490902}{18753765620625}>0,
\]
\[
\frac1{10000}-\frac\eta B
=\frac{34322726895419}{1125125917154190000}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(T_Y\subseteq\Delta\), one has \(\beta\le1\).  If
\(s\le0\), then (12) would give
\(B=(1+s)^2\beta\le1\), contrary to \(B=11249/10000>1\).
Thus \(s>0\), whence
\[
\alpha>A=\frac18,
\qquad
\beta=\frac{B}{(1+s)^2}>\frac B4>\frac14.
\tag{23}
\]

Set
\[
r=-\frac{d_3}{1-s},
\qquad
z=\frac{d_3}{1+s}.
\tag{24}
\]
By (16), the lower part \(T_X\cap K_r\) contains a triangle of area
strictly greater than
\[
\alpha\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta A}\right)
>\frac18\cdot\frac89=\frac19.
\tag{25}
\]
Similarly, \(T_Y\cap K_z\) contains a triangle of area strictly
greater than
\[
\beta\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta B}\right)
>\frac14\cdot\frac{99}{100}>\frac19.
\tag{26}
\]
Applying (19) to these two contained triangles yields
\[
r>(p+a)\alpha\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta A}\right)-a,
\]
\[
z>(p+a)\beta\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta B}\right)-a.
\tag{27}
\]

The two thresholds in (24) satisfy the exact allocation identity
\[
(1-s)r+(1+s)z=0.
\tag{28}
\]
Define
\[
\mu=A\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta A}\right),
\qquad
\nu=B\left(1-\sqrt{\frac\eta B}\right).
\tag{29}
\]
Multiplying the two inequalities in (27) by \(1-s\) and \(1+s\),
using (12) and (28), gives
\[
0>(p+a)\left(\frac\mu{1-s}+\frac\nu{1+s}\right)-2a.
\tag{30}
\]
But (22) gives
\[
\mu>\frac18\cdot\frac89=\frac19,
\qquad
\nu>\frac{99B}{100}
=\frac{1113651}{1000000}>1.
\tag{31}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, (8), and (31) imply
\[
\frac\mu{1-s}+\frac\nu{1+s}
\ge
\frac{(\sqrt\mu+\sqrt\nu)^2}{(1-s)+(1+s)}
>\frac{(1/3+1)^2}{2}=\frac89.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore the right-hand side of (30) is strictly greater than
\[
(p+a)\frac89-2a=\frac{336370}{9}>0,
\]
contradicting (30).  This proves (4).

Adding the exact middle contribution in (5) gives a third row mass at
least \(t\), whereas accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` requires every
row mass to be strictly below \(t\) in a completion.  The argument
used the actual row minima, absolute determinants, and the complete
closed-cap formulas.  It therefore covers all minimum-attainment
choices, both determinant signs, every open cap-order cell, and every
zero or repeated-value boundary stratum.  Hence the fixed real
endpoint-completion system is infeasible.
