## Statement

Normalize the area of
\(\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) to one and use the exact
cap notation \(\mathcal C\) of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.
Put
\[
p_1=\frac{567233}{1264000},
\qquad
p_2=\frac{4222159}{14284500}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every pair of nonconstant affine functions \(f,g\) on \(\Delta\)
such that
\[
\mathcal C(f;0)=p_1,
\qquad
\mathcal C(g;0)=p_2,
\tag{2}
\]
one has the strict asymmetric two-cap bound
\[
\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:f(\lambda)+g(\lambda)\leq0\}\right|
>\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]
All vertex-on-line, repeated-value, parallel-line, and constant-sum
boundaries are included.

Now put
\[
A=\frac1{10},\qquad
B=\frac{10999}{10000},\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9=\frac{7333}{15000},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
367&-265&-113\\
-260&810&-525\\
-106&-544&639
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
There are no real matrices \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and no
\(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfying (6)-(7) and (9) below:
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\geq0
\qquad(k=1,2,3),
\tag{7}
\]
For later use, write
\[
Q:=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3,
\tag{8}
\]
with no hypothesis on \(Q\).  The remaining conditions are the three
strict rows
\[
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently the frozen middle calibration (4)-(5) has no rational or
real endpoint completion in the accepted reconstruction system.

## Proof

We first prove (3).  Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives the
complete exact upper-cap formula on a triangle.  Both numbers in (1)
lie strictly between zero and one half:
\[
\frac12-p_1=\frac{64767}{1264000}>0,
\qquad
\frac12-p_2=\frac{2920091}{14284500}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(0<p<1/2\), every nonconstant affine function having zero-level
upper-cap fraction \(p\), after a positive rescaling and a vertex
permutation, has one of the following two forms:
\[
S_1(p,u)=
\left(1,\ 1-u,\ 1-\frac1{pu}\right),
\qquad
1\leq u\leq\frac1p,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
D_1(p,u)=
\left(-1,\ u-1,\ \frac1{(1-p)u}-1\right),
\qquad
1\leq u\leq\frac1{1-p}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, (11) normalizes the unique positive vertex to one.  Its two
edge-intercept ratios are \(1/u\) and \(pu\), whose product is \(p\).
Formula (12) normalizes the unique negative vertex to \(-1\).  Its
lower-cap intercept product is
\[
\frac1u\,(1-p)u=1-p,
\]
so its upper cap has area \(p\).  The interval endpoints in
(11)-(12) include a cutting line through a vertex.  Replacing \(u\)
by \(1/(pu)\) in (11), or by \(1/((1-p)u)\) in (12), exchanges the two
nondistinguished vertices.  Thus no ordering or boundary case is
lost.

Use also the cyclic representatives
\[
S_2(p,u)=
\left(1-\frac1{pu},\ 1,\ 1-u\right),
\tag{13}
\]
\[
D_2(p,u)=
\left(\frac1{(1-p)u}-1,\ -1,\ u-1\right),
\qquad
D_3(p,u)=
\left(u-1,\ \frac1{(1-p)u}-1,\ -1\right).
\tag{14}
\]
Under simultaneous vertex permutations, but without interchanging
the unequal levels \(p_1,p_2\), the eight support-incidence orbits are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{orbit}&f&g&u\text{-range}&v\text{-range}\\ \hline
SS_{\rm same}&S_1(p_1,u)&S_1(p_2,v)&[1,1/p_1]&[1,1/p_2]\\
SS_{\rm diff}&S_1(p_1,u)&S_2(p_2,v)&[1,1/p_1]&[1,1/p_2]\\
SD_{\rm in}&S_1(p_1,u)&D_3(p_2,v)&[1,1/p_1]&[1,1/(1-p_2)]\\
SD_{\rm out}&S_1(p_1,u)&D_1(p_2,v)&[1,1/p_1]&[1,1/(1-p_2)]\\
DS_{\rm in}&D_1(p_1,u)&S_2(p_2,v)&[1,1/(1-p_1)]&[1,1/p_2]\\
DS_{\rm out}&D_1(p_1,u)&S_1(p_2,v)&[1,1/(1-p_1)]&[1,1/p_2]\\
DD_{\rm same}&D_1(p_1,u)&D_1(p_2,v)&[1,1/(1-p_1)]&[1,1/(1-p_2)]\\
DD_{\rm diff}&D_1(p_1,u)&D_2(p_2,v)&[1,1/(1-p_1)]&[1,1/(1-p_2)].
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]
Here \(S\) denotes a singleton positive support and \(D\) a doubleton
nonnegative support.  In an \(SD\) or \(DS\) row, ``in'' means that
the singleton vertex lies in the doubleton support.  After the
normalization of \(f\), the remaining relative positive scale is
arbitrary, so in every row of (15) put
\[
h=f+\lambda g,\qquad \lambda>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (11)-(16) parameterize every pair in (2).

For completeness, write \(h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\).  The assertion
\[
\left|\{h\leq0\}\right|\leq\frac12
\tag{17}
\]
is exactly the disjunction of the following seven semialgebraic
predicates:

\[
\begin{split}
&h_1,h_2,h_3\geq0
\quad\text{and}\quad
(h_1,h_2,h_3)\ne(0,0,0);                                      \tag{18}\\
&h_i<0,\quad h_j,h_k\geq0,\quad
2h_i^2\leq(h_j-h_i)(h_k-h_i)
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\});                                   \tag{19}\\
&h_i,h_j\leq0<h_k,\quad
2h_k^2\geq(h_k-h_i)(h_k-h_j)
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).                                   \tag{20}
\end{split}
\]
To verify this equivalence, if exactly one value \(h_i\) is negative,
the exact cap formula gives
\[
\left|\{h\leq0\}\right|
=\frac{h_i^2}{(h_j-h_i)(h_k-h_i)}.
\]
If \(h_k\) is the unique positive value, the same formula gives
\[
\left|\{h\leq0\}\right|
=1-\frac{h_k^2}{(h_k-h_i)(h_k-h_j)}.
\]
The all-nonnegative nonzero case has zero lower-cap area.  The
all-nonpositive case has full lower-cap area, and the identically zero
case also has full lower-cap area because the cap is closed.  Thus
(18)-(20) include every zero and repeated-value boundary.

Substitute each row of (15) and (16) into the disjunction
(18)-(20).  All denominators are positive on the displayed parameter
domains.  Exact real cylindrical algebraic decomposition of
\[
\operatorname{Resolve}\!\left[
\exists(u,v,\lambda)\in\mathbb R^3:
\begin{array}{l}
u,v\text{ lie in the row's closed intervals},\\
\lambda>0,\ \text{and one of (18)-(20) holds},
\end{array}
,\mathbb R
\right]
\tag{21}
\]
returns
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
\text{orbit}&SS_{\rm same}&SS_{\rm diff}&SD_{\rm in}&SD_{\rm out}
&DS_{\rm in}&DS_{\rm out}&DD_{\rm same}&DD_{\rm diff}\\ \hline
\text{result}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F&F.
\end{array}
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(F\) means the closed existential formula is `False`.
The audit used only the literal rational numbers in (1), the
expressions (11)-(16), and the predicates (18)-(20).  Each query was
checked to contain no inexact real number.  WolframScript 1.13.0 with
the local Wolfram Language 14.3.0 kernel evaluated the eight
`Resolve[..., Reals]` calls separately and returned `False` in every
case.  Thus (17) is impossible in every orbit, proving the strict
bound (3).

We apply (3) to the endpoint system.  The columns of (5) sum to one,
its rows are nonconstant, and direct use of the one-positive formula
in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives
\[
c_1:=\mathcal C(L_{1*};0)
=\frac{367^2}{(367+265)(367+113)}
=\frac{134689}{303360},
\tag{23}
\]
\[
c_2:=\mathcal C(L_{2*};0)
=\frac{810^2}{(810+260)(810+525)}
=\frac{4374}{9523},
\tag{24}
\]
\[
c_3:=\mathcal C(L_{3*};0)
=\frac{639^2}{(639+106)(639+544)}
=\frac{408321}{881335}.
\tag{25}
\]
Consequently
\[
\alpha_i:=\frac{t-c_i}{A}
\]
satisfy
\[
\alpha_1=p_1=\frac{567233}{1264000},
\qquad
\alpha_2=p_2=\frac{4222159}{14284500},
\qquad
\alpha_3=\frac{67602911}{264400500},
\tag{26}
\]
and
\[
\frac12-\alpha_3
=\frac{64597339}{264400500}>0.
\tag{27}
\]

We record the threshold-sum consequence of (3) that will be used on
both endpoint matrices.  Let \(w_1,w_2,w_3\) be nonconstant affine
functions with
\[
w_1+w_2+w_3=\kappa,
\]
and let \(\psi_i\) be their unique thresholds at the levels in (26):
\[
\mathcal C(w_i;\psi_i)=\alpha_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Applying (3) to \(w_1-\psi_1\) and \(w_2-\psi_2\) gives
\[
\left|\{w_3\geq\kappa-\psi_1-\psi_2\}\right|
>\frac12>\alpha_3.
\]
The exact cap formula in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` makes the
cap function of nonconstant \(w_3\) strictly decreasing between its
support values.  Hence
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\psi_1+\psi_2+\psi_3>\kappa.
\qquad}
\tag{28}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that (6)-(9) hold for some arbitrary
\(\sigma\), with \(Q\) defined by (8).  We first prove that the strict
rows force \(Q>1\).  For the second endpoint matrix define
\[
\nu_k=\min_jV_{kj},\qquad
\nu=(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)^\top,\qquad
r=\mathbf1^\top\nu,\qquad
\eta=1-r.
\tag{29}
\]
Every column of \(V-\nu\mathbf1^\top\) is nonnegative and has
coordinate sum \(\eta\), so \(r\leq1\).  Equality would make all
columns of \(V\) equal to \(\nu\), contradicting
\(|\det V|=B>0\).  Thus \(\eta>0\), and
\[
Y=\frac{V-\nu\mathbf1^\top}{\eta}
\tag{30}
\]
is a nonnegative column-stochastic matrix.  Moreover
\[
V=(\eta I+\nu\mathbf1^\top)Y,
\qquad
\det V=\eta^2\det Y,
\tag{31}
\]
so \(Y\) is invertible.

Define
\[
y_i(\lambda)=\eta(LY)_{i*}\lambda
\qquad(\lambda\in\Delta,\ i=1,2,3).
\tag{32}
\]
Every \(y_i\) is nonconstant.  Indeed, if
\((LY)_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top\), then column stochasticity and
invertibility of \(Y\) give
\[
L_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top Y^{-1}=a\mathbf1^\top,
\]
contrary to (5).  The column-sum identities for \(L\) and \(Y\) give
\[
y_1+y_2+y_3=\eta.
\tag{33}
\]
Let \(\phi_i\) be the unique thresholds satisfying
\[
\mathcal C(y_i;\phi_i)=\alpha_i.
\tag{34}
\]
The threshold-sum consequence (28) gives
\[
\phi_1+\phi_2+\phi_3>\eta.
\tag{35}
\]

Put
\[
e=L\nu-\sigma.
\tag{36}
\]
Equations (30)-(32) give
\[
\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
=\mathcal C(y_i;-e_i).
\tag{37}
\]
The first-endpoint cap in (9) is nonnegative.  Therefore (9) and
\(t-c_i=A\alpha_i\) imply
\[
B\mathcal C(y_i;-e_i)<A\alpha_i.
\]
Here
\[
B-A=\frac{9999}{10000}>0,
\]
and every \(\alpha_i\) in (26) is positive.  Consequently
\[
\mathcal C(y_i;-e_i)
<\frac AB\alpha_i<\alpha_i.
\tag{38}
\]
Strict monotonicity and (34) yield
\[
-e_i>\phi_i,\qquad e_i<-\phi_i.
\tag{39}
\]
Summing (39) and using (35) gives
\[
\sum_i e_i<-\sum_i\phi_i<-\eta=r-1.
\tag{40}
\]
On the other hand, the column sums of \(L\), (8), and (36) give
\[
\sum_i e_i
=\mathbf1^\top L\nu-\sum_i\sigma_i
=r-Q.
\]
Comparison with (40) proves
\[
\boxed{Q>1.}
\tag{41}
\]

We now normalize the first endpoint matrix.  Define
\[
\ell_k=\min_jU_{kj},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s=\mathbf1^\top\ell,\qquad
\rho=1-s.
\tag{42}
\]
As above, \(\rho>0\), and
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\rho}
\tag{43}
\]
is a nonnegative invertible column-stochastic matrix with
\[
U=(\rho I+\ell\mathbf1^\top)X,
\qquad
\det U=\rho^2\det X.
\tag{44}
\]
The three nonconstant affine functions
\[
z_i(\lambda)=\rho(LX)_{i*}\lambda
\tag{45}
\]
satisfy
\[
z_1+z_2+z_3=\rho.
\tag{46}
\]
Let \(\theta_i\) be their unique thresholds at levels \(\alpha_i\):
\[
\mathcal C(z_i;\theta_i)=\alpha_i.
\tag{47}
\]
Equation (28) gives
\[
\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3>\rho.
\tag{48}
\]

Put
\[
d=L\ell+\sigma.
\tag{49}
\]
Equations (43)-(45) give
\[
\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
=\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i).
\tag{50}
\]
The second-endpoint cap in (9) is nonnegative.  Therefore (9) implies
\[
\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i)<\alpha_i.
\tag{51}
\]
Strict monotonicity and (47) yield
\[
-d_i>\theta_i,\qquad d_i<-\theta_i.
\tag{52}
\]
Summing (52) and using (48) gives
\[
\sum_i d_i<-\sum_i\theta_i<-\rho=s-1.
\tag{53}
\]
But the column sums of \(L\), (8), (41), and (49) give
\[
\sum_i d_i
=\mathbf1^\top L\ell+\sum_i\sigma_i
=s+Q>s+1,
\tag{54}
\]
contradicting (53).

Thus no value of \(Q\) permits a completion: the \(V\)-side argument
forces \(Q>1\), and the \(U\)-side argument excludes \(Q>1\).  The
proof used neither compatibility (7), the signs of the endpoint
determinants, nor any ordering of the endpoint projection values.
It therefore excludes all real compatible endpoint matrices,
allocations, determinant signs, and cap-order chambers claimed in the
statement.
