## Statement

Use the affine barycentric coordinates and the fully noncoincident endpoint convention of accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e`, but do not assume equal cell areas.  Thus
\[
\Delta=\{\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1,\ \alpha_i\geq0\}
\]
has coordinate area \(1/2\), and a convex polygon \(K\) is cut by the three lines \(\alpha_i=0\).  Its central cell is
\[
C_0=K\cap\Delta,
\]
and its three outer cells \(C_i=K\cap\{\alpha_i\leq0\}\) have pairwise disjoint interiors.  Assume the finite boundary reduction has made each \(C_i\) a triangle, and assume all six chord endpoints are noncoincident.

More explicitly, let
\[
a,b,c,d,e,f>0,
\qquad
L_1=1-a-f>0,\quad
L_2=1-b-c>0,\quad
L_3=1-d-e>0,
\]
and let the chord endpoints be
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
p_{12}=(0,a,1-a),&p_{13}=(0,1-f,f),\\
p_{21}=(b,0,1-b),&p_{23}=(1-c,0,c),\\
p_{32}=(1-d,d,0),&p_{31}=(e,1-e,0).
\end{array}
\]
The outer vertices are
\[
u_1=(-h_1,A,B),\qquad
u_2=(C,-h_2,E),\qquad
u_3=(F,G,-h_3),
\]
where all displayed capital letters and all \(h_i\) are positive and
\[
A+B=1+h_1,\qquad C+E=1+h_2,\qquad F+G=1+h_3.
\]
Assume the points occur on the counterclockwise boundary of \(K\) in the usual order
\[
p_{13},u_1,p_{12},\ldots,p_{21},u_2,p_{23},
\ldots,p_{32},u_3,p_{31},\ldots .
\]
Put
\[
D=2|C_0|,
\qquad
r_i=\frac{|C_i|}{|C_0|}.
\]
If
\[
\frac{12}{25}\leq r_i\leq\frac{13}{25}
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
then
\[
D>\frac{12}{25}.
\]
The conclusion is strict.  The hypotheses allow either endpoint value \(12/25\) or \(13/25\) for any \(r_i\), and the proof includes the putative boundary case \(D=12/25\).

## Proof

The six chord endpoints belong to the convex set \(C_0\).  Their convex hull is the central hexagon obtained from \(\Delta\) by deleting the three corner triangles of areas \(ab/2\), \(cd/2\), and \(ef/2\).  Hence, with
\[
S=ab+cd+ef,
\]
we have
\[
D=2|C_0|\geq1-S. \tag{1}
\]
Also put
\[
s_1=a+f=1-L_1,\qquad
s_2=b+c=1-L_2,\qquad
s_3=d+e=1-L_3.
\]
For fixed \(s_1,s_2,s_3\), substitute
\[
f=s_1-a,\qquad c=s_2-b,\qquad e=s_3-d.
\]
Then \(S\) is affine in each of \(a,b,d\) separately, so its maximum on the rectangular box
\[
0\leq a\leq s_1,\qquad
0\leq b\leq s_2,\qquad
0\leq d\leq s_3
\]
occurs at a vertex.  Direct evaluation at the eight vertices gives
\[
S\leq
\max\{(1-L_1)(1-L_2),(1-L_2)(1-L_3),(1-L_3)(1-L_1)\}. \tag{2}
\]

Because \(C_i\) is a triangle with base fraction \(L_i\) on a side of \(\Delta\) and barycentric height \(h_i\),
\[
|C_i|=\frac{L_i h_i}{2}.
\]
Since \(|C_0|=D/2\), the unequal-cap area equations are exactly
\[
h_i=\frac{r_iD}{L_i}. \tag{3}
\]
The cells cover \(K\) up to their boundaries, so, on writing
\[
R=1+r_1+r_2+r_3,
\]
we also have
\[
|K|=\frac{RD}{2}. \tag{4}
\]

Convexity at the two edges adjacent to each outer vertex gives
\[
b(A-a)\geq ah_1,\qquad e(B-f)\geq fh_1, \tag{5}
\]
\[
a(C-b)\geq bh_2,\qquad d(E-c)\geq ch_2, \tag{6}
\]
\[
c(G-d)\geq dh_3,\qquad f(F-e)\geq eh_3. \tag{7}
\]
For example, in \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\)-coordinates the determinant of the directed boundary edge \(u_1p_{12}\), evaluated at \(p_{21}\), is
\[
[p_{12}-u_1,p_{21}-u_1]=b(A-a)-ah_1,
\]
which is nonnegative.  The other five inequalities are the analogous supporting-edge determinants.

Add the two inequalities belonging to each cap and use (3).  Since all six endpoint parameters are positive, division is legitimate and gives
\[
\frac ab+\frac fe\leq1+\frac{L_1^2}{r_1D},
\qquad
\frac ba+\frac cd\leq1+\frac{L_2^2}{r_2D},
\qquad
\frac dc+\frac ef\leq1+\frac{L_3^2}{r_3D}. \tag{8}
\]
Summing (8) and using \(t+t^{-1}\geq2\) for the three positive reciprocal pairs yields
\[
3D\leq\frac{L_1^2}{r_1}+\frac{L_2^2}{r_2}+\frac{L_3^2}{r_3}. \tag{9}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that \(D\leq12/25\).  If every \(L_i\leq D\), then (9) and \(r_i\geq12/25\) imply
\[
3D\leq D^2\sum_{i=1}^3\frac1{r_i}
\leq\frac{25D^2}{4},
\]
so \(D=12/25\).  Equality throughout forces
\[
r_1=r_2=r_3=\frac{12}{25},
\qquad
L_1=L_2=L_3=D.
\]
But (1) gives \(S\geq1-D\), whereas (2) gives
\[
S\leq(1-D)^2<1-D,
\]
a contradiction.  Consequently some \(L_k>D\).  Relabel cyclically so that
\[
L_3>D. \tag{10}
\]

Equations (1), (2), and (10) imply
\[
(1-L_1)(1-L_2)\geq S\geq1-D. \tag{11}
\]
Indeed, each of the other two products in (2) is strictly smaller than
\[
1-L_3<1-D\leq S.
\]
Moreover, since \(c,f<1\) and \(d,e>0\),
\[
cd+ef<d+e=1-L_3.
\]
Together with \(S\geq1-D\), this gives
\[
ab>L_3-D. \tag{12}
\]

Pairwise interior-disjointness of the caps gives
\[
AC\geq h_1h_2,\qquad EG\geq h_2h_3,\qquad FB\geq h_3h_1. \tag{13}
\]
For instance, on the segment \([u_1,u_2]\subseteq K\), the zero of \(\alpha_1\) must occur no later than the zero of \(\alpha_2\).  Otherwise an open subsegment has both coordinates strictly negative; a sufficiently small relative neighborhood on the interior side of that segment then gives a positive-area overlap of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\).  Comparing the two zero parameters gives \(AC\geq h_1h_2\).  The other two inequalities are cyclic.

Let \(U=\operatorname{conv}\{u_1,u_2,u_3\}\).  Expanding the barycentric determinant gives
\[
2|U|
=h_1GE+h_3AC+h_2FB+CGB+FAE-h_1h_2h_3. \tag{14}
\]
The last two positive terms satisfy
\[
CGB+FAE
\geq2\sqrt{AC\cdot EG\cdot FB}
\geq2h_3\sqrt{AC\,h_1h_2}.
\]
Using (13) also in the first and third terms of (14) yields
\[
2|U|
\geq h_3\left(\sqrt{AC}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}\right)^2. \tag{15}
\]
Since \(U\subseteq K\), equations (3)--(4) and (15) imply
\[
\sqrt{AC}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}
\leq\sqrt{\frac{RL_3}{r_3}}. \tag{16}
\]

The first inequalities in (5) and (6) give
\[
A\geq\frac{a(b+h_1)}b,\qquad
C\geq\frac{b(a+h_2)}a.
\]
Therefore
\[
AC\geq(a+h_2)(b+h_1)
\geq\left(\sqrt{ab}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}\right)^2, \tag{17}
\]
where the last inequality follows from the exact identity
\[
(a+h_2)(b+h_1)
-\left(\sqrt{ab}+\sqrt{h_1h_2}\right)^2
=\left(\sqrt{ah_1}-\sqrt{bh_2}\right)^2.
\]
Combining (16)--(17) gives
\[
\sqrt{ab}+2\sqrt{h_1h_2}
\leq\sqrt{\frac{RL_3}{r_3}}. \tag{18}
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz and (11),
\[
\sqrt{L_1L_2}
\leq1-\sqrt{(1-L_1)(1-L_2)}
\leq1-\sqrt{1-D}.
\]
Using (3) and rationalizing,
\[
\sqrt{h_1h_2}
=\frac{\sqrt{r_1r_2}\,D}{\sqrt{L_1L_2}}
\geq
\sqrt{r_1r_2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-D}\right). \tag{19}
\]
Equations (12), (18), and (19) now force
\[
\sqrt{L_3-D}
+2\sqrt{r_1r_2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-D}\right)
<
\sqrt{\frac{R}{r_3}L_3}. \tag{20}
\]

Put
\[
\sigma=\sqrt{1-D},\qquad
A_0=2\sqrt{r_1r_2},\qquad
B_0=\frac{R}{r_3}.
\]
The ratio assumptions and \(D\leq12/25\) give the exact bounds
\[
\sigma\geq\frac{\sqrt{13}}5,\qquad
A_0\geq\frac{24}{25},\qquad
B_0\leq\frac{16}{3}. \tag{21}
\]
For \(D\leq L\leq1\), define
\[
\Phi(L)=\sqrt{L-D}+A_0(1+\sigma)-\sqrt{B_0L}.
\]
Because \(B_0>1\),
\[
2\Phi'(L)=\frac1{\sqrt{L-D}}-\frac{\sqrt{B_0}}{\sqrt L}.
\]
There is at most one critical point, namely \(L=B_0D/(B_0-1)\), and the derivative changes there from positive to negative.  Thus \(\Phi\) has no interior minimum, and its minimum on \([D,1]\) is attained at an endpoint.

At the first endpoint, (21) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(D)
&\geq
\frac{24}{25}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{13}}5\right)
-\sqrt{\frac{16}{3}\frac{12}{25}}\\
&=\frac{24\sqrt{13}-80}{125}>0,
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(\sqrt{13}>10/3\).  At the other endpoint,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(1)
&\geq
\frac{24}{25}+\frac{49\sqrt{13}}{125}
-\frac4{\sqrt3}\\
&>
\frac{1482}{625}-\frac4{\sqrt3}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sqrt{13}>18/5\), and the last strict inequality follows by squaring the two positive quantities:
\[
3\cdot1482^2-16\cdot625^2=338972>0.
\]
Hence \(\Phi(L)>0\) throughout \([D,1]\), contradicting (20) with \(L=L_3\).

The contradiction proves \(D>12/25\).  All divisions were by the explicitly positive \(D,r_i,a,b,c,d,e,f,L_i\).  The non-strict ratio endpoints were retained in (21), and the only possible equality \(D=12/25\) was handled before (10), so there is no omitted equality or ratio-boundary case.
