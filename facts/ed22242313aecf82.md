## Statement

Consider a sequence in the normalization and John gauge of accepted fact
27839a03c483c90b, with original scalar normalization
\[
|A_{2,n}|=1.
\]
Let \(d_n\in[\pi,4\pi]\) be the area multiplier of the common John affine
map, and pass to a subsequence such that
\[
d_n\longrightarrow d\in[\pi,4\pi].
\tag{1}
\]
Assume that the subsequence has the three-weight, rank-two, finite-scale
type of 27839a03c483c90b. Thus
\[
\alpha_i>0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i=1,\qquad
\sigma>0,
\tag{2}
\]
and there are nonconstant affine functions \(\phi_i\) with unit gradients
\[
n_i=\nabla\phi_i,\qquad |n_i|=1,
\tag{3}
\]
such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i\phi_i=\sigma,
\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{n_1,n_2,n_3\}=\mathbb R^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=|\det(n_1,n_2)|.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(\Delta>0\), the limiting middle central triangle is
\[
\mathcal T_1=\{w:\phi_1(w)\geq0,\ \phi_2(w)\geq0,\
\phi_3(w)\geq0\},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
|\mathcal T_1|
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\alpha_1\alpha_2\Delta}.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(r_{123,n}\) be the original \(m=1\) scalar area of the exact middle
cell \(R_{123,n}\), and pass to a further subsequence such that
\[
r_{123,n}\longrightarrow r_{123}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
r_{123}
=\frac{\sigma^2}
{2d\,\alpha_1\alpha_2\Delta}>0.
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, the middle central cell cannot disappear in a type-2
finite-scale boundary limit.

For the endpoint corollary, let \(\beta_i\) be the limiting normalized
offsets from 27839a03c483c90b and put
\[
\chi_i=\phi_i-\beta_i,
\qquad
\tau=\sigma-\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i\beta_i.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
\tau\geq2\sigma>0,
\tag{11}
\]
the three \(Q\)-threshold lines \(\chi_i=0\) are pairwise nonparallel and
nonconcurrent, and their global central triangle has area
\[
\left|\{w:\chi_1(w),\chi_2(w),\chi_3(w)\geq0\}\right|
=\frac{\tau^2}{2\alpha_1\alpha_2\Delta}.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, convex support connectivity in 27839a03c483c90b reduces the
limiting positive-area \(Q\)-support to exactly one of
\[
\boxed{\quad
\{23\},\quad
\{23,123\},\quad
\{23,123,13\},\quad
\{23,123,12\},\quad
\{23,123,13,12\}.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]

## Proof

Taking gradients in (4) gives
\[
\alpha_1n_1+\alpha_2n_2+\alpha_3n_3=0.
\tag{14}
\]
No two \(n_i\) are parallel. Indeed, if two were parallel, their positive
weighted sum would be parallel to the same line, and (14) would force the
third vector to be parallel to that line as well, contrary to the rank-two
hypothesis in (4). In particular,
\[
\Delta>0.
\tag{15}
\]

Define the affine map
\[
F:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
F(w)=
\left(
\frac{\alpha_1\phi_1(w)}{\sigma},
\frac{\alpha_2\phi_2(w)}{\sigma}
\right).
\tag{16}
\]
Its absolute Jacobian determinant is
\[
J_F
=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2}{\sigma^2}
|\det(n_1,n_2)|
=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2\Delta}{\sigma^2}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(F\) is an affine isomorphism. If
\[
y_1=\frac{\alpha_1\phi_1}{\sigma},
\qquad
y_2=\frac{\alpha_2\phi_2}{\sigma},
\qquad
y_3=\frac{\alpha_3\phi_3}{\sigma},
\]
then (4) gives
\[
y_1+y_2+y_3=1.
\tag{18}
\]
Therefore \(F\) maps \(\mathcal T_1\) bijectively onto the standard
two-dimensional simplex
\[
\{(y_1,y_2):y_1\geq0,\ y_2\geq0,\ y_1+y_2\leq1\},
\]
whose area is \(1/2\). Dividing that area by (17) proves (7). It also proves
directly that \(\mathcal T_1\) is a nondegenerate compact triangle.

At the \(n\)-th finite stage, write the normalized functions as in
27839a03c483c90b:
\[
\lambda_{i,n}=-f_{i,n}=s_{i,n}\phi_{i,n},
\qquad s_{i,n}>0.
\]
Hence
\[
f_{i,n}\leq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\phi_{i,n}\geq0.
\tag{19}
\]
Accepted fact f5bb496aa51a2cf9 gives
\[
\mathcal T_{1,n}:=\{f_{i,n}\leq0\ (i=1,2,3)\}
\subseteq K_n
\tag{20}
\]
and identifies its area, before the John map, with \(r_{123,n}\). After
the common John map this becomes
\[
|\mathcal T_{1,n}|=d_n r_{123,n}.
\tag{21}
\]

The affine functions \(\phi_{i,n}\) converge locally uniformly to
\(\phi_i\) by 27839a03c483c90b. Since the limiting three lines are
pairwise nonparallel and bound the nondegenerate compact triangle in (6),
their three vertices converge, and hence
\[
\mathcal T_{1,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal T_1
\]
in Hausdorff distance. Area is continuous under Hausdorff convergence of
compact convex bodies, so (1), (8), and (21) give
\[
|\mathcal T_1|=d\,r_{123}.
\tag{22}
\]
Substituting (7) into (22) proves (9). Every factor in its denominator is
positive, proving \(r_{123}>0\). Cutting-line boundaries have planar area
zero, so the weak exact-cell convention does not change (21) or (22).

It remains to prove the endpoint corollary. Accepted fact
27839a03c483c90b gives
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\beta_i\leq-\sigma.
\]
Thus (10) satisfies (11), and
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\chi_i=\tau.
\tag{23}
\]
The threshold functions \(\chi_i\) have the same gradients \(n_i\), so
their lines are pairwise nonparallel by (15). If all three threshold lines
were concurrent at a point \(w\), then \(\chi_i(w)=0\) for every \(i\), and
(23) would give \(0=\tau\), contrary to (11). They are therefore
nonconcurrent.

Replacing \(\phi_i,\sigma\) in (16)--(18) by \(\chi_i,\tau\) maps the
global central \(Q\)-triangle bijectively to the standard simplex, with
absolute Jacobian
\[
\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2\Delta}{\tau^2}.
\]
This proves (12). The nondegenerate-arrangement connectivity conclusion of
27839a03c483c90b now applies and gives exactly the five supports in (13).
