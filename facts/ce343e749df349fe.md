## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\). Under the affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional convex polygon and
\[
R_G=K\cap\{f_i\le0\ (i\in G),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin G)\},
\qquad
r_G=|R_G|,
\qquad
k=|K|,
\]
where the nonconstant affine functions have pairwise nonparallel linear parts and satisfy
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Put \(t=r_{123}\). For \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), define
\[
e_i=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\left(r_i-\frac{r_{ij}r_{i\ell}}{t}\right)_+,&t>0,\\[6pt]
r_i,&t=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\left(k-e_1-e_2-e_3\right)^2k\ge4e_1e_2e_3
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{e_1}+\sqrt{e_2}+\sqrt{e_3}
\le\frac32\sqrt{k}.
\tag{2}
\]
All zero-cell cases are included.

For the calibration of accepted fact `2bb72698f0248a65`,
\[
t=\frac3{20},\qquad
r_{12}=r_{13}=r_{23}=\frac1{10},\qquad
r_1=r_2=r_3=\frac{71}{60},\qquad
k=4,
\]
one has
\[
e_1=e_2=e_3
=
\frac{71}{60}-\frac{(1/10)^2}{3/20}
=
\frac{67}{60}>1.
\]
Thus the left side of (2) is strictly greater than \(3\), whereas its right side equals \(3\). Hence no convex \(K\), and therefore no compatible triple \(A_0,A_2,K\), realizes that calibration.

## Proof

Set \(\lambda_i=-f_i\). Then
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1,
\]
and the map \(x\mapsto(\lambda_1(x),\lambda_2(x),\lambda_3(x))\) is an affine isomorphism onto the plane of coordinate sum \(1\). Let
\[
T=\{\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\ge0\}
\]
and denote its vertices by \(v_i\), where \(\lambda(v_i)\) is the \(i\)-th standard basis vector.

Suppose first that \(r_1r_2r_3>0\). Choose \(x_i\) in the interior of \(R_i\). If \(M\) is the matrix whose \(i\)-th column is \(\lambda(x_i)\), then each column sums to \(1\), its diagonal entry is greater than \(1\), and its off-diagonal entries are negative. Thus \(M^T\) has row sum \(1\), positive diagonal, and nonpositive off-diagonal entries.

Write row \(p\) of \(M^T\) as
\[
(M^Ty)_p
=
y_p+\sum_{q\ne p}w_{pq}(y_p-y_q),
\qquad w_{pq}\ge0.
\]
If \(M^Ty=b\ge0\) and \(y_p<0\) is a minimum coordinate, then
\[
(M^Ty)_p\le y_p<0,
\]
a contradiction. The same minimum-maximum argument with \(b=0\) proves that \(M^T\), hence \(M\), is nonsingular. Consequently \(M^{-1}\ge0\). For each standard basis vector \(u_i\), the vector \(M^{-1}u_i\) is nonnegative and its coordinates sum to \(1\), since the columns of \(M\) sum to \(1\). Therefore
\[
v_i\in\operatorname{conv}\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}\subseteq K.
\]
It follows that
\[
T\subseteq K,\qquad R_{123}=T
\]
up to cutting-line boundaries, and hence \(t=|T|>0\).

For distinct \(i,j\), let \(p_{ij}\) be the endpoint of
\[
K\cap\operatorname{aff}\{v_i,v_j\}
\]
on the ray from \(v_i\) away from \(v_j\). Write
\[
p_{ij}=v_i+a_{ij}(v_i-v_j),
\qquad a_{ij}\ge0.
\]
For \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), the closure of \(R_i\) is bounded inside \(K\) by the two segments \(v_ip_{ij}\), \(v_ip_{i\ell}\), and the intervening outer boundary arc. The chord \(p_{ij}p_{i\ell}\) separates from \(K\) a halfplane cap \(C_i\subseteq\overline{R_i}\). Put \(z_i=|C_i|\). The remaining part of \(\overline{R_i}\) is the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{v_i,p_{ij},p_{i\ell}\},
\]
whose area is \(t\,a_{ij}a_{i\ell}\). Therefore
\[
r_i=z_i+t\,a_{ij}a_{i\ell}.
\tag{3}
\]

The triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{v_i,v_\ell,p_{ij}\}
\]
lies in the closure of \(R_{i\ell}\) and has area \(t\,a_{ij}\). Similarly,
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{v_i,v_j,p_{i\ell}\}
\subseteq\overline{R_{ij}}
\]
has area \(t\,a_{i\ell}\). Hence
\[
r_{i\ell}\ge t\,a_{ij},
\qquad
r_{ij}\ge t\,a_{i\ell}.
\]
Together with (3), this gives
\[
z_i
=
r_i-t\,a_{ij}a_{i\ell}
\ge
r_i-\frac{r_{ij}r_{i\ell}}t,
\]
and therefore
\[
z_i\ge e_i.
\tag{4}
\]

The three cap interiors are pairwise disjoint because \(C_i\subseteq\overline{R_i}\). Applying the sharp three-cap inequalities of accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031` gives
\[
(k-z_1-z_2-z_3)^2k\ge4z_1z_2z_3
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{z_1}+\sqrt{z_2}+\sqrt{z_3}
\le\frac32\sqrt{k}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(0\le e_i\le z_i\), (5) implies (1), while (6) implies (2).

It remains to cover zero cells. If some \(r_i=0\), then the corresponding \(e_i\) is zero. Thus \(e_1e_2e_3=0\), so (1) is immediate, and Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\sqrt{e_1}+\sqrt{e_2}+\sqrt{e_3}
\le
\sqrt{2(e_1+e_2+e_3)}
\le\sqrt{2k}
<\frac32\sqrt{k}.
\]
If \(t=0\), all three singleton cells cannot have positive area, because the preceding convex-hull argument would imply \(T\subseteq K\) and \(t=|T|>0\). Hence the same argument applies with the stated definition \(e_i=r_i\). Cutting-line pieces have planar area zero, so weak inequalities, collinear chord degeneracies, and zero-area caps do not alter any identity.

Finally, substituting the exact cell masses from accepted fact `2bb72698f0248a65` gives \(e_i=67/60\), and therefore contradicts (2) exactly as stated.
