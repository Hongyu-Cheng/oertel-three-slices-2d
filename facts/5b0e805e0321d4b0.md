## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be two-dimensional convex bodies, let
\(f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
and let \(L_1,L_2,L_3\in\mathbb R\). Define
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad r_i=f_i
\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3),
\]
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad
\theta=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{p_i\le0\}|,\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\{q_i\le0\}|,\qquad
c_i=|K\cap\{r_i\le0\}|.
\]
The support-contact hypotheses under consideration are
\[
\delta>0,\qquad L_1=\min_{A_0}f_1,
\]
\[
c_i+u_i+v_i=\theta\quad(i=2,3),
\qquad
c_i+m\ge\theta\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
together with the compatibility inclusion
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]

Use \((p_1,p_2-p_3)\) as coordinates on \(A_0\),
\((q_1,q_2-q_3)\) as coordinates on \(A_2\), and
\((r_1,r_2-r_3)\) as coordinates on \(K\). Write these coordinates as
\((s,y),(t,y),(z,y)\), respectively, and let
\[
I_s=\{y:(s,y)\text{ is the coordinate image of a point of }A_0\},
\]
\[
J_t=\{y:(t,y)\text{ is the coordinate image of a point of }A_2\},
\qquad
H_z=\{y:(z,y)\text{ is the coordinate image of a point of }K\}.
\]
Let \(\kappa>0\) be the common area factor, so that Euclidean area in
each original plane is \(\kappa\) times \(ds\,dy\), \(dt\,dy\), or
\(dz\,dy\), respectively. Assume the full section compatibility
\[
\frac{I_s+J_t}{2}\subseteq H_{(s+t)/2}
\tag{A}
\]
whenever \(I_s\) and \(J_t\) are nonempty.

For \(s\ge0\), define
\[
I^-(s)=I_s\cap(-\infty,\delta-s),\qquad
I^+(s)=I_s\cap(s-\delta,\infty),
\]
\[
P_-=\{s\ge0:I^-(s)\ne\varnothing\},\qquad
P_+=\{s\ge0:I^+(s)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
For \(t>0\), define
\[
J_2(t)=J_t\cap(-\infty,-2-\delta-t),\qquad
J_3(t)=J_t\cap(2+\delta+t,\infty),
\]
\[
T_2=\{t>0:J_2(t)\ne\varnothing\},\qquad
T_3=\{t>0:J_3(t)\ne\varnothing\},
\]
\[
\Pi_B=\{t>0:J_t\ne\varnothing\},\qquad
\Gamma=\Pi_B\setminus(T_2\cup T_3).
\]
Finally, define
\[
\Sigma_2=\left\{\frac{s+t}{2}:s\in P_-,\ t\in T_2\right\},
\qquad
\Sigma_3=\left\{\frac{s+t}{2}:s\in P_+,\ t\in T_3\right\},
\]
\[
\eta_2=
\sum_{i=2}^3
\left|A_2\cap\{q_1\le0,\ q_i\le0\}\right|,
\qquad
\eta_K=
\sum_{i=2}^3
\left|K\cap\{r_1\le0,\ r_i\le0\}\right|.
\]
The proposed variational inequality is
\[
u_2+u_3+\eta_2+\eta_K
+\kappa\left(
\int_\Gamma |J_t|\,dt
+
\int_{\Sigma_2\cap\Sigma_3}2(1+z)\,dz
\right)
\ge\frac{2M-m-k}{3}.
\tag{V}
\]

There is a rational rectangle example satisfying all the hypotheses
above, with constant convex/concave interval sections and with the
three cutting-line gradients pairwise nonparallel, for which the left
side of (V) is \(261/10\) and the right side is \(811/10\).

## Proof

In planar coordinates \((x,y)\), take
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad
f_2(x,y)=\frac{-1-x+y}{2},\qquad
f_3(x,y)=\frac{-1-x-y}{2},
\]
\[
L_1=0,\qquad
L_2=L_3=-\frac{3181}{300},
\]
and
\[
A_0=
\left[0,\frac{953}{75}\right]\times
\left[-\frac{15}{2},\frac{15}{2}\right],
\]
\[
A_2=
\left[-\frac1{10},\frac9{10}\right]\times
\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right],
\qquad
K=
\left[-\frac{729}{80},8\right]\times[-4,4].
\]
These are rational two-dimensional convex polygons. Directly,
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3=-\frac{3181}{150}<-1,
\]
so
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)=\frac{3031}{150}>0.
\]
Moreover, the three gradients
\[
(1,0),\qquad\left(-\frac12,\frac12\right),\qquad
\left(-\frac12,-\frac12\right)
\]
are pairwise nonparallel, and
\[
L_1=0=\min_{A_0}f_1.
\]

The auxiliary affine functions are explicitly
\[
p_1=x,\qquad
p_2=\frac{\delta-x+y}{2},\qquad
p_3=\frac{\delta-x-y}{2},
\]
\[
q_1=x,\qquad
q_2=\frac{-2-\delta-x+y}{2},\qquad
q_3=\frac{-2-\delta-x-y}{2},
\]
\[
r_1=x,\qquad
r_2=\frac{-1-x+y}{2},\qquad
r_3=\frac{-1-x-y}{2}.
\]
Thus
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3=\delta,\qquad
q_1+q_2+q_3=-2-\delta,\qquad
r_1+r_2+r_3=-1.
\]
Because \(L_1=0\) and \(L_2=L_3\), each coordinate map
\[
(p_1,p_2-p_3),\qquad
(q_1,q_2-q_3),\qquad
(r_1,r_2-r_3)
\]
is exactly \((x,y)\). Therefore \(\kappa=1\), and the sections are
\[
I_s=
\begin{cases}
[-15/2,15/2],&0\le s\le953/75,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
\[
J_t=
\begin{cases}
[-1/2,1/2],&-1/10\le t\le9/10,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
\[
H_z=
\begin{cases}
[-4,4],&-729/80\le z\le8,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The lower and upper endpoints are constant on each projection
interval, hence are respectively convex and concave.

The exact Minkowski average of the two source rectangles is
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=
\left[-\frac1{20},\frac{2041}{300}\right]\times[-4,4].
\]
Its \(x\)-interval lies in \([-729/80,8]\), so it is contained in
\(K\). More explicitly, whenever \(I_s,J_t\ne\varnothing\),
\[
-\frac1{20}
\le\frac{s+t}{2}
\le\frac{2041}{300}<8
\]
and
\[
\frac{I_s+J_t}{2}
=
\frac{[-15/2,15/2]+[-1/2,1/2]}2
=[-4,4]
=H_{(s+t)/2}.
\]
This proves the full two-parameter inclusion (A), with equality in the
section variable. The rectangles can also be realized as the three
integer slices of the rational polytope
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times K)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\bigr).
\]
Indeed, the endpoint slices are \(A_0,A_2\), and any convex
combination having first coordinate \(1\) is a convex combination of
a point of \(K\) and a point of \((A_0+A_2)/2\), hence belongs to
\(K\). Thus the middle slice is exactly \(K\).

The three areas are
\[
M=\frac{953}{75}\cdot15=\frac{953}{5},
\qquad
m=1,
\]
\[
k=
\left(8+\frac{729}{80}\right)\cdot8
=\frac{1369}{10}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\theta=
\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
=
\frac{2(953/5+1+1369/10)}9
=73.
\]

We now compute all nine cap areas. On \(A_0\),
\[
p_1\le0\iff x\le0,
\]
which has area zero. Also
\[
p_2\le0\iff y\le x-\delta,\qquad
p_3\le0\iff y\ge\delta-x.
\]
Since
\[
\delta=\frac{953}{75}+\frac{15}{2},
\]
for \(0\le x\le953/75\) one has
\[
x-\delta\le-\frac{15}{2},
\qquad
\delta-x\ge\frac{15}{2}.
\]
Each equality occurs only at \(x=953/75\), so the two cap
intersections have area zero. Hence
\[
u_1=u_2=u_3=0.
\]

On \(A_2\),
\[
q_1\le0\iff x\le0,
\]
so the contact cap is the rectangle of width \(1/10\) and height \(1\):
\[
v_1=\frac1{10}.
\]
Further,
\[
q_2\le0\iff y\le2+\delta+x.
\]
For every \(x\in[-1/10,9/10]\),
\[
2+\delta+x
\ge2+\delta-\frac1{10}
=\frac{1658}{75}
>\frac12,
\]
so \(q_2\le0\) throughout \(A_2\), and \(v_2=1\). Similarly,
\[
q_3\le0\iff y\ge-2-\delta-x,
\]
and
\[
-2-\delta-x
\le-2-\delta+\frac1{10}
=-\frac{1658}{75}
<-\frac12.
\]
Thus \(q_3\le0\) throughout \(A_2\), and \(v_3=1\).

On \(K\),
\[
r_1\le0\iff x\le0,
\]
so
\[
c_1=8\cdot\frac{729}{80}=\frac{729}{10}.
\]
Next,
\[
r_2\le0\iff y\le1+x.
\]
In the vertical section \([-4,4]\), the length satisfying this
inequality is zero for \(x\le-5\), is \(x+5\) for
\(-5\le x\le3\), and is \(8\) for \(3\le x\le8\). Therefore
\[
c_2
=
\int_{-5}^{3}(x+5)\,dx+\int_3^8 8\,dx
=32+40
=72.
\]
Reflection in \(y=0\) exchanges \(r_2\) and \(r_3\), so
\[
c_3=72.
\]

The two crossing equalities are now direct:
\[
c_i+u_i+v_i=72+0+1=73=\theta
\qquad(i=2,3).
\]
All three support-contact lower bounds also hold:
\[
c_1+m=\frac{739}{10}>73,
\qquad
c_2+m=c_3+m=73.
\]
The \(A_2\) contact cap is proper because \(v_1=1/10<m=1\). The
support-contact conclusion itself is an equality:
\[
c_1+v_1=\frac{729}{10}+\frac1{10}=73=\theta.
\]

We next determine every set entering (V). For
\(0\le s\le953/75\),
\[
\delta-s\ge\frac{15}{2},
\qquad
s-\delta\le-\frac{15}{2}.
\]
At the right endpoint the inequalities are equalities, but the
strictly cut intervals \(I^-(s)\) and \(I^+(s)\) remain nonempty.
Thus
\[
P_-=P_+=\left[0,\frac{953}{75}\right].
\]

For \(t>0\), \(J_t\ne\varnothing\) exactly when \(0<t\le9/10\).
Hence
\[
\Pi_B=\left(0,\frac9{10}\right].
\]
For such \(t\),
\[
-2-\delta-t<-2-\delta<-\frac12,
\]
so
\[
J_2(t)
=[-1/2,1/2]\cap(-\infty,-2-\delta-t)
=\varnothing.
\]
Also
\[
2+\delta+t>2+\delta>\frac12,
\]
so
\[
J_3(t)
=[-1/2,1/2]\cap(2+\delta+t,\infty)
=\varnothing.
\]
Therefore
\[
T_2=T_3=\varnothing,
\qquad
\Gamma=\left(0,\frac9{10}\right],
\qquad
\Sigma_2=\Sigma_3=\varnothing.
\]
Since every nonempty \(J_t\) has length \(1\), the two integrals in
(V) are exactly
\[
\int_\Gamma |J_t|\,dt
=
\int_0^{9/10}1\,dt
=\frac9{10},
\]
\[
\int_{\Sigma_2\cap\Sigma_3}2(1+z)\,dz
=
\int_{\varnothing}2(1+z)\,dz
=0.
\]

It remains to compute \(\eta_2,\eta_K\). On the part \(x\le0\) of
\(A_2\), both \(q_2\le0\) and \(q_3\le0\) hold everywhere, as already
proved. The area of that part is \(1/10\). Hence
\[
\eta_2
=
\left|A_2\cap\{q_1\le0,q_2\le0\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{q_1\le0,q_3\le0\}\right|
=\frac1{10}+\frac1{10}
=\frac15.
\]
On the part \(x\le0\) of \(K\), the \(r_2\)-cap has section length
zero for \(x\le-5\) and \(x+5\) for \(-5\le x\le0\). Therefore
\[
\left|K\cap\{r_1\le0,r_2\le0\}\right|
=
\int_{-5}^{0}(x+5)\,dx
=\frac{25}{2}.
\]
Reflection in \(y=0\) gives the same area for the \(r_3\)-cap, so
\[
\eta_K=\frac{25}{2}+\frac{25}{2}=25.
\]

Substituting \(\kappa=1\), \(u_2=u_3=0\), and the exact quantities
above, the left side of (V) is
\[
0+0+\frac15+25+\frac9{10}+0
=\frac{261}{10}.
\]
The right side is
\[
\frac{2M-m-k}{3}
=
\frac{2(953/5)-1-1369/10}{3}
=\frac{811}{10}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{261}{10}<\frac{811}{10},
\]
and the proposed variational inequality (V) fails by exactly \(55\).
