## Statement

Assume the low-\(M\) five-cap normal form of accepted fact
`a6fbd3f54d7ec4c7`.  Thus
\[
m=|A_0|,\qquad k=|A_1|,\qquad M=|A_2|,\qquad
T=m+k+M,\qquad q=\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
m<M,\qquad \frac T3<M<\frac{4T}{9},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\tag{3}
\]
and there are nonconstant pairwise nonparallel affine functions and real
slopes satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^3s_j>1.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(j=1,2,3\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_j&=|A_0\cap\{f_j-s_j\leq0\}|,\\
b_j&=|A_1\cap\{f_j\leq0\}|,\\
y_j&=|A_2\cap\{f_j+s_j\leq0\}|,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
and assume the three strict row inequalities
\[
b_j+x_j+y_j<q.
\tag{6}
\]
Define the closed same-index midpoint graphs and their volumes by
\[
E_j=
\left\{(z_0,z_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
f_j\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)\leq0\right\},
\qquad d_j=|E_j|,
\tag{7}
\]
and define
\[
\eta_j=Mx_j+my_j-d_j.
\tag{8}
\]
Also put
\[
N(z)=\sum_{j=1}^3\mathbf1_{\{f_j(z)\leq0\}},
\qquad
\mathcal Q=
\int_{A_0\times A_2}
\left(
N\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)-1
\right)\,dz_0\,dz_2.
\tag{9}
\]

Then
\[
\eta_j\geq0
\tag{10}
\]
and the height-shift defects of accepted fact
`a6fbd3f54d7ec4c7` have the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{\quad
\delta_j
:=x_j+y_j-\frac{d_j}{M}
=\frac{\eta_j}{M}
+\left(1-\frac mM\right)y_j.
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover, all four terms below are nonnegative and satisfy the strict
budget
\[
\boxed{\quad
\left(\sum_{j=1}^3b_j-k\right)
+\frac{\mathcal Q}{M}
+\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^3\eta_j
+\left(1-\frac mM\right)\sum_{j=1}^3y_j
<g:=M-\frac T3.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
0<g<\frac T9.
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, every such strict realization necessarily satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
\left(
\sqrt{m-\frac{2(m+M-k)}9}+\sqrt M
\right)^2
<\frac{20k}{9}.
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the reverse weak inequality in (14) excludes a low-\(M\)
realization, including its equality boundary.

## Proof

### Same-index graph inclusion and the exact defect split

Let
\[
C_{0j}=A_0\cap\{f_j-s_j\leq0\},
\qquad
C_{2j}=A_2\cap\{f_j+s_j\leq0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
If \((z_0,z_2)\in E_j\), affinity gives
\[
\frac{(f_j(z_0)-s_j)+(f_j(z_2)+s_j)}2
=f_j\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)\leq0.
\tag{16}
\]
The two summands on the left cannot both be positive.  Hence
\[
E_j\subseteq
(C_{0j}\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times C_{2j}).
\tag{17}
\]
More precisely, the pointwise function
\[
\mathbf1_{C_{0j}}(z_0)+\mathbf1_{C_{2j}}(z_2)
-\mathbf1_{E_j}(z_0,z_2)
\tag{18}
\]
is nonnegative on all of \(A_0\times A_2\).  On \(E_j\), this follows
from (17); off \(E_j\), the last indicator is zero.  Integrating (18)
gives
\[
Mx_j+my_j-d_j=\eta_j\geq0,
\]
including all points on the three cutting lines because the caps and
the graph in (15) and (7) use closed inequalities.

Now calculate directly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M\delta_j
&=M(x_j+y_j)-d_j\\
&=(Mx_j+my_j-d_j)+(M-m)y_j\\
&=\eta_j+(M-m)y_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(M>0\), division by \(M\) proves (11).  Since \(m<M\), both
terms on its right are nonnegative.

### Midpoint multiplicity and the four-defect budget

At every \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), at least one \(f_j(z)\) is strictly
negative: otherwise all three would be nonnegative, contradicting
\(\sum_jf_j(z)=-1\).  Therefore
\[
N(z)\geq1,\qquad \mathcal Q\geq0,
\tag{20}
\]
and the three closed middle caps cover \(A_1\).  Consequently
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3b_j\geq k.
\tag{21}
\]
Tonelli's theorem and (7) give the exact midpoint identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=1}^3d_j
&=
\int_{A_0\times A_2}
N\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)\,dz_0\,dz_2\\
&=mM+\mathcal Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

Using the definition of \(\delta_j\), then (22), and finally (11),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=1}^3(b_j+x_j+y_j)
&=\sum_jb_j+\frac1M\sum_jd_j+\sum_j\delta_j\\
&=\sum_jb_j+m+\frac{\mathcal Q}{M}+\sum_j\delta_j\\
&=k+m+
\left(\sum_jb_j-k\right)
+\frac{\mathcal Q}{M}
+\frac1M\sum_j\eta_j
+\left(1-\frac mM\right)\sum_jy_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Every term after \(k+m\) is nonnegative by (10), (20), (21), and
\(m<M\).  Summing the three strict inequalities (6) and using
\(3q=2T/3\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\sum_jb_j-k\right)
+\frac{\mathcal Q}{M}
+\frac1M\sum_j\eta_j
+\left(1-\frac mM\right)\sum_jy_j\\
&\qquad<
\frac{2T}{3}-k-m
=\frac{2M-k-m}{3}
=M-\frac T3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
This proves (12).  The lower inequality in (2) gives \(g>0\), while
the upper inequality gives
\[
g=M-\frac T3<\frac{4T}{9}-\frac T3=\frac T9,
\]
which proves (13).  The equality boundary \(M=4T/9\) would give
\(g=T/9\); hence the strict
inequality \(g<T/9\) uses precisely the registered strict upper bound.

For completeness, (4) also implies that the lower cap interiors cover
the entire height-zero plane.  Indeed,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3(f_j-s_j)
=-1-\sum_{j=1}^3s_j<0.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus at every point some \(f_j-s_j<0\), and hence
\[
\sum_{j=1}^3x_j\geq m.
\tag{26}
\]
This boundary-free cover is consistent with, but not needed for, the
algebraic identity (12).

### Centroid/complement necessary condition

Let \(c_1\) be the centroid of \(A_1\).  Equation (4) implies that some
index \(r\) satisfies
\[
f_r(c_1)<0.
\tag{27}
\]
Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` records the planar Grünbaum bound:
every closed halfplane containing the centroid of a planar convex body
has area at least \(4/9\) of the body's area.  Here the inequality is
strict.  To see the strictness without an equality-case assumption,
translate the line \(\{f_r=0\}\) parallel until it passes through
\(c_1\).  The translated centroid halfplane is contained in
\(\{f_r\leq0\}\) and has area at least \(4k/9\).  Since \(A_1\) is
two-dimensional, its centroid is an interior point.  Equation (27)
therefore leaves a nonempty open strip of \(A_1\) between the two
parallel lines, and that strip has positive area.  Hence
\[
b_r>\frac{4k}{9}.
\tag{28}
\]

Put
\[
D:=q-\frac{4k}{9}
=\frac{2(m+M-k)}9.
\tag{29}
\]
The \(r\)-th strict row inequality and (28) give
\[
x_r+y_r<q-b_r<D.
\tag{30}
\]
Because the left side is nonnegative, (30) proves \(D>0\).  Also
\[
D-m
=\frac{4M-2T-5m}{9}<0.
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, \(M<4T/9\) gives
\[
4M-2T<-\frac{2T}{9},
\]
and \(m>0\).  Thus
\[
0<D<m<M.
\tag{32}
\]
In particular, (30) gives \(x_r<m\) and \(y_r<M\).

Consider the strict complementary endpoint sets
\[
U_0=A_0\cap\{f_r-s_r>0\},\qquad
U_2=A_2\cap\{f_r+s_r>0\}.
\tag{33}
\]
The boundary of each cap is a line because \(f_r\) is nonconstant, so
it has planar area zero.  Therefore
\[
|U_0|=m-x_r>0,\qquad |U_2|=M-y_r>0.
\tag{34}
\]
For every \(z_0\in U_0,z_2\in U_2\), affinity gives
\[
f_r\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)
=\frac{(f_r(z_0)-s_r)+(f_r(z_2)+s_r)}2>0.
\tag{35}
\]
Compatibility (3) therefore yields the actual-body inclusion
\[
\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}
\subseteq A_1\cap\{f_r>0\}.
\tag{36}
\]
The cutting line again has area zero, so
\[
|A_1\cap\{f_r>0\}|=k-b_r.
\tag{37}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski applied to the two nonempty convex measurable
sets in (33), followed by (36)--(37), gives
\[
\left(\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}\right)^2
\leq4(k-b_r).
\tag{38}
\]

It remains to minimize the left side using its exact shared deletion
budget.  Set \(e=x_r+y_r\).  By (30) and (32),
\[
0\leq e<D<m<M.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(0\leq x\leq e\), the function
\[
h_e(x)=\sqrt{m-x}+\sqrt{M-e+x}
\tag{40}
\]
is concave, so its minimum on \([0,e]\) occurs at an endpoint.  The
endpoint corresponding to deletion from the smaller body is the smaller
one:
\[
\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
\leq\sqrt m+\sqrt{M-e}.
\tag{41}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\sqrt m-\sqrt{m-e}
=\frac{e}{\sqrt m+\sqrt{m-e}}
\geq
\frac{e}{\sqrt M+\sqrt{M-e}}
=\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-e},
\tag{42}
\]
where equality at \(e=0\) causes no problem.  Equations (40)--(42)
therefore imply
\[
\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}
\geq\sqrt{m-e}+\sqrt M
>\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M.
\tag{43}
\]
The final inequality is strict because \(e<D\).

Combining (28), (38), and (43) gives the strict chain
\[
\left(\sqrt{m-D}+\sqrt M\right)^2
<
\left(\sqrt{m-x_r}+\sqrt{M-y_r}\right)^2
\leq4(k-b_r)
<\frac{20k}{9}.
\tag{44}
\]
Substituting (29) proves (14).  The first and last inequalities in
(44) are strict, so equality in the reverse boundary of (14) is also
excluded.  This completes the proof.
