## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on
\(\mathbb R^2\), with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(B\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex polygon
containing
\[
\mathcal T=\{f_1,f_2,f_3\leq0\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the affine coordinates
\[
S=f_2,\qquad T=f_3,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac1{|\det(\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3)|}.
\tag{2}
\]
Physical area is \(\gamma\) times coordinate area, and the open
singleton-\(1\) chamber is
\[
\mathcal Q=\{S>0,\ T>0\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Start with any counterclockwise cyclic vertex list for \(B\), delete
all consecutive repeated vertices, and write the resulting reduced
list as
\[
V_j=(S_j,T_j),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus every edge vector
\[
D_j=V_{j+1}-V_j
\]
is nonzero.  For \(X=(S_X,T_X)\), define
\[
\lambda_j(X)=-\det(D_j,X-V_j).
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(\lambda_j\leq0\) on \(B\), and edge \(j\) is visible from \(X\)
exactly when \(\lambda_j(X)>0\).

For real \(a,b,c\), let
\[
a_{(1)}\leq a_{(2)}\leq a_{(3)}
\]
be their increasing rearrangement and define
\[
\vartheta(a,b,c)=
\begin{cases}
1,&a_{(3)}\leq0,\\[1mm]
0,&a_{(1)}\geq0\text{ and }a_{(3)}>0,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle
\frac{a_{(1)}^2}
{(a_{(2)}-a_{(1)})(a_{(3)}-a_{(1)})},
&a_{(1)}<0\leq a_{(2)}\text{ and }a_{(3)}>0,\\[4mm]
\displaystyle
1-\frac{a_{(3)}^2}
{(a_{(3)}-a_{(1)})(a_{(3)}-a_{(2)})},
&a_{(2)}<0<a_{(3)}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\vartheta(0,0,0)=1.
\]
For a nondegenerate triangle, \(\vartheta(a,b,c)\) is exactly the area
fraction on which an affine function with vertex values \(a,b,c\) is
nonpositive.

Let \(K\supseteq B\) be any compact convex body and let
\[
P=(S_P,T_P)\in K\cap\overline{\mathcal Q}.
\]
Put
\[
e_2=|(K\setminus B)\cap\{S\leq0\}|,\qquad
e_3=|(K\setminus B)\cap\{T\leq0\}|.
\tag{6}
\]
After the reduction of the vertex list above, the indices satisfying
\(\lambda_j(P)>0\) form one cyclic interval.  One has
\[
\boxed{\quad
e_2\geq
\frac{\gamma}{2}
\sum_{j:\lambda_j(P)>0}
\lambda_j(P)\,
\vartheta(S_j,S_{j+1},S_P),
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\boxed{\quad
e_3\geq
\frac{\gamma}{2}
\sum_{j:\lambda_j(P)>0}
\lambda_j(P)\,
\vartheta(T_j,T_{j+1},T_P).
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
Both bounds are equalities when
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{P\}).
\tag{9}
\]

If the supporting line of edge \(j\) is
\[
a_jS+b_jT=c_j,\qquad
B\subseteq\{a_jS+b_jT\leq c_j\},
\]
then
\[
\lambda_j(P)
=
\frac{|D_j|}{\sqrt{a_j^2+b_j^2}}
\left(a_jS_P+b_jT_P-c_j\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus (7)--(8) use only chain vertices, support slopes, and support
reaches.

Call an edge charging if at least one endpoint is strictly outside the
closed singleton quadrant:
\[
\mathcal J_{\rm ch}
=
\{j:\min(S_j,S_{j+1})<0
\text{ or }\min(T_j,T_{j+1})<0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal Z_B
=
\overline{\mathcal Q}\cap
\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J_{\rm ch}}
\{X:\lambda_j(X)\leq0\}.
\tag{12}
\]
For every \(P\in\mathcal Q\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\text{both right sides of (7)--(8) vanish}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P\in\mathcal Z_B.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]

The region in (12) can strictly contain \(B\cap\mathcal Q\).  In the
\((S,T)\)-plane, let
\[
B^\ast=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(2,-5),\left(4,\frac12\right),
\left(\frac52,3\right),\left(\frac12,4\right),
(-5,2),(-3,-3)
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
It contains the central triangle, and its seven exact cell areas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_1&=\frac{1015}{88},&
b_2&=\frac{86}{15},&
b_3&=\frac{86}{15},\\
b_{12}&=\frac{1322}{165},&
b_{13}&=\frac{1322}{165},&
b_{23}&=\frac{121}{10},&
b_{123}&=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
For
\[
P^\ast=\left(\frac{33}{10},\frac{89}{50}\right),
\qquad
K^\ast=\operatorname{conv}(B^\ast\cup\{P^\ast\}),
\tag{16}
\]
only the edge
\[
\left[\left(4,\frac12\right),\left(\frac52,3\right)\right]
\tag{17}
\]
is visible, and its support reach is \(17/100\).  Hence
\[
|(K^\ast\setminus B^\ast)\cap\mathcal Q|
=\frac{17}{200}>0,
\qquad
|(K^\ast\setminus B^\ast)\cap\{S\leq0\}|=0,
\qquad
|(K^\ast\setminus B^\ast)\cap\{T\leq0\}|=0.
\tag{18}
\]

For the crossing specialization, additionally let \(A_0,A_2\) be
compact two-dimensional convex bodies satisfying
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|.
\]
For endpoint offsets \(L_1,L_2,L_3\), define
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}|,\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-L_i\}|.
\tag{19}
\]
For a compact convex \(K\supseteq B\), put
\[
k=|K|,\qquad
c_i=|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,
\]
and assume all three crossing equalities
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{20}
\]
Write
\[
b=|B|,\qquad
d_i=|B\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,\qquad
\Delta=k-b,
\]
\[
e_i=|(K\setminus B)\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,
\]
\[
h_0=\frac{2(M+m+b)}9,\qquad
\rho_i=u_i+v_i+d_i-h_0,\qquad
G=\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
e_i=\frac{2\Delta}{9}-\rho_i.
\tag{22}
\]
Because (1) makes the added triple cell null, the total added
doubleton area is
\[
D_{\rm add}=-\frac{\Delta}{3}-G.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(z_1\) is the added singleton-\(1\) area, then
\[
z_1\geq e_1-D_{\rm add}
=\frac{5\Delta}{9}+\rho_2+\rho_3.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus (7)--(8) are the exact local transverse charges furnished by any
added singleton extreme point outside \(\mathcal Z_B\).  The example
(14)--(18) shows why a separate capacity bound for
\(\mathcal Z_B\setminus B\) is needed before (24) alone can force a
positive transverse charge.

Wall-parallel faces and wall vertices require no separate limiting
argument.  Equations (4), (7), and (8) contain no division by an edge
slope, and a zero wall coordinate is covered by (5).  A zero support
reach \(\lambda_j(P)=0\) contributes zero.  For an unreduced vertex
list, every zero-length edge has \(D_j=0\) and
\(\lambda_j(P)=0\); deleting a consecutive repeated vertex removes
only that zero summand and leaves all other nonzero edges and both sums
unchanged.

## Proof

The affine map in (2) is nonsingular because
\(\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3\) are not parallel.  Its Jacobian gives the
area factor \(\gamma\).  Since \(f_1=-1-S-T\), (3) is exactly the
singleton-\(1\) chamber.

For a counterclockwise edge \(D_j\), convexity gives
\[
\det(D_j,X-V_j)\geq0\qquad(X\in B),
\]
so \(\lambda_j\leq0\) on \(B\).  If \(\lambda_j(P)>0\), the open
triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{P,V_j,V_{j+1}\}
\]
apart from its base lies in the strict exterior halfplane of that
edge.  It is therefore disjoint from \(B\), and its coordinate area is
\[
\frac{\lambda_j(P)}2.
\tag{25}
\]

For the reduced polygon, the two tangent rays from \(P\) to \(B\)
bound one visible boundary arc.  Hence the visible edges form one
cyclic interval.  The triangles in (25) over this interval have
disjoint interiors and triangulate
\[
\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{P\})\setminus B
\tag{26}
\]
up to their boundaries.  Consecutive repeated vertices must be deleted
before this assertion because a zero-length edge has no tangent
direction.  Its determinant reach is identically zero, so deleting it
does not change (26) or any sum in (7)--(8).

To verify (5), first suppose exactly one vertex value is negative.
The nonpositive part is the similar corner triangle whose two linear
scale factors are
\[
\frac{-a_{(1)}}{a_{(2)}-a_{(1)}},
\qquad
\frac{-a_{(1)}}{a_{(3)}-a_{(1)}}.
\]
Their product gives the third case of (5).  If exactly two values are
negative, the positive complement is the similar corner triangle of
area fraction
\[
\frac{a_{(3)}^2}
{(a_{(3)}-a_{(1)})(a_{(3)}-a_{(2)})},
\]
giving the fourth case.  If all values are nonpositive, the whole
triangle is nonpositive, including the case \((0,0,0)\), so the
fraction is \(1\).  If all values are nonnegative and at least one is
positive, the nonpositive part has zero planar area, so the fraction
is \(0\).  This proves (5), including every zero boundary.

Apply (5) to the coordinate \(S\) on each triangle in (26), sum the
disjoint contributions, and multiply by \(\gamma\).  Since
\(\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{P\})\subseteq K\), this proves (7).
The same argument with \(T\) proves (8).  When (9) holds, (26) is the
whole added region, proving simultaneous equality.

The clockwise normal
\[
(D_{j,T},-D_{j,S})
\]
has length \(|D_j|\) and points into the exterior halfplane.  It equals
\[
\frac{|D_j|}{\sqrt{a_j^2+b_j^2}}(a_j,b_j),
\]
so taking its scalar product with \(P-V_j\) proves (10).

If \(P\in\mathcal Z_B\), every visible edge is noncharging, so its two
endpoints and \(P\) lie in the closed singleton quadrant.  Its fan
triangle has zero planar area in both transverse caps, and both sums
vanish.  Conversely, if \(P\in\mathcal Q\setminus\mathcal Z_B\), some
charging edge has positive support reach.  At least one endpoint has a
strictly negative wall coordinate while the corresponding coordinate
of \(P\) is positive.  The appropriate clipping fraction in (5) is
strictly positive.  This proves (13).

For (14), exact edge-orientation tests place
\[
(0,0),\qquad(-1,0),\qquad(0,-1)
\]
inside \(B^\ast\), and exact clipping by
\[
S=0,\qquad T=0,\qquad S+T=-1
\]
gives (15).  Substitution of \(P^\ast\) into (4), in the cyclic edge
order of (14), gives
\[
\left(
-\frac{641}{100},
\frac{17}{100},
-\frac{41}{25},
-\frac{1781}{100},
-\frac{2053}{50},
-\frac{73}{2}
\right).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus only (17) is visible.  Its endpoints and \(P^\ast\) have both
wall coordinates positive, so the fan triangle lies in
\(\mathcal Q\).  Its area is half its support reach, proving (18).

Finally, (20) and \(k=b+\Delta\) give
\[
u_i+v_i+d_i+e_i
=h_0+\frac{2\Delta}{9},
\]
which is (22).  Summing (22) gives
\[
\sum_i e_i=\frac{2\Delta}{3}-G.
\]
If \(S_{\rm add}\) denotes the total added singleton area, then
\[
\Delta=S_{\rm add}+D_{\rm add},\qquad
\sum_i e_i=S_{\rm add}+2D_{\rm add}.
\]
Eliminating \(S_{\rm add}\) proves (23).  Writing the added doubleton
areas as \(z_{12},z_{13},z_{23}\), one has
\[
e_1=z_1+z_{12}+z_{13},\qquad
D_{\rm add}=z_{12}+z_{13}+z_{23}.
\]
Hence \(z_1\geq e_1-D_{\rm add}\), and (22)--(23) give (24).
