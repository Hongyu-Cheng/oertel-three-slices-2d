## Statement

Assume the conclusion and notation of accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`. Thus \(A_0,K=A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes which are sections of one compact polytope,
\[
M=|A_0|>|A_2|=m,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) are nonconstant affine functions satisfying
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
For \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\), put
\[
c_r=|K\cap\{f_r\leq0\}|,
\]
\[
F_{0r}(a)=|A_0\cap\{f_r\leq a\}|,\qquad
F_{2r}(a)=|A_2\cap\{f_r\leq a\}|,
\]
\[
\mu_r(a)=c_r+F_{0r}(a)+F_{2r}(-a),\qquad
I_r=\{a\in\mathbb R:\mu_r(a)<t\}.
\]
Fix distinct \(i,j,\ell\) with \(\{i,j,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}\), and suppose
\[
c_j+m<t,\qquad I_i\ne\varnothing,\qquad I_\ell\ne\varnothing.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
\Delta_j=2t-k+c_j.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(\Delta_j>0\). If \(\Delta_j<m\), then
\[
c_j>\frac14\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\Delta_j}\right)^2
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
4mc_j>M(m-\Delta_j).
\tag{4}
\]
Equivalently, under \(c_j+m<t\), if \(\Delta_j<m\) and either
\[
c_j\leq\frac14\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\Delta_j}\right)^2
\]
or
\[
4mc_j\leq M(m-\Delta_j),
\]
then \(I_i=\varnothing\) or \(I_\ell=\varnothing\).

## Proof

Because the three planar bodies are sections of one compact convex polytope, convexity gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{5}
\]
This is also the compatibility implication used in accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8`.

Choose arbitrary
\[
a_i\in I_i,\qquad a_\ell\in I_\ell,
\]
and abbreviate
\[
u_r=F_{0r}(a_r),\qquad v_r=F_{2r}(-a_r)
\qquad(r=i,\ell).
\tag{6}
\]
The two strict low-mass inequalities are
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i<t,\qquad
u_\ell+v_\ell+c_\ell<t.
\tag{7}
\]

Every point of \(K\cap\{f_j>0\}\) belongs to
\[
\{f_i\leq0\}\cup\{f_\ell\leq0\},
\]
because
\[
f_i+f_\ell=-1-f_j<0.
\]
The three cutting lines have planar area zero. Therefore
\[
k-c_j\leq c_i+c_\ell.
\tag{8}
\]
Adding (7) and using (8) gives
\[
u_i+u_\ell+v_i+v_\ell
<2t-c_i-c_\ell
\leq2t-k+c_j
=\Delta_j.
\tag{9}
\]
The left side is nonnegative, so \(\Delta_j>0\).

Define the strict simultaneous complements
\[
E_0^\circ=A_0\cap\{f_i>a_i,\ f_\ell>a_\ell\},
\qquad
E_2^\circ=A_2\cap\{f_i>-a_i,\ f_\ell>-a_\ell\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The union bound gives
\[
|E_0^\circ|\geq M-u_i-u_\ell,\qquad
|E_2^\circ|\geq m-v_i-v_\ell.
\tag{11}
\]
Assume from now on that \(\Delta_j<m\). By (9), both right sides in (11) are strictly positive. Hence both strict convex sets in (10) have positive planar area and therefore contain interior points.

Let
\[
E_0=\overline{E_0^\circ},\qquad E_2=\overline{E_2^\circ}.
\tag{12}
\]
Each \(E_s\) is compact and convex. More explicitly,
\[
E_0=A_0\cap\{f_i\geq a_i,\ f_\ell\geq a_\ell\},
\qquad
E_2=A_2\cap\{f_i\geq-a_i,\ f_\ell\geq-a_\ell\}.
\tag{13}
\]
To verify these equalities, choose an interior point of the corresponding strict set. The open segment from it to any point of the right-hand closed intersection remains in the strict set except possibly at its endpoint, so every point of the closed intersection belongs to the closure. The reverse inclusions follow from continuity. The difference between each closed intersection and its strict set lies in the polygon boundary and the two affine cutting lines, all of which have planar measure zero. Therefore
\[
|E_0|=|E_0^\circ|,\qquad |E_2|=|E_2^\circ|,
\tag{14}
\]
and both \(E_0,E_2\) are compact convex bodies of positive area.

For \(x_0\in E_0\) and \(x_2\in E_2\), affinity gives
\[
2f_i\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
=f_i(x_0)+f_i(x_2)\geq a_i-a_i=0
\]
and likewise
\[
2f_\ell\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)\geq0.
\]
Equation (5) puts the midpoint in \(K\), while
\[
f_j=-1-f_i-f_\ell\leq-1
\]
at that midpoint. Hence
\[
\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_j\leq0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality recorded and used in accepted fact `d7bdfc054d5093e8` now applies to the two compact convex bodies \(E_0,E_2\). Equations (11), (14), and (15) give
\[
\sqrt{c_j}
\geq
\frac{\sqrt{|E_0|}+\sqrt{|E_2|}}2
\geq
\frac{
\sqrt{M-u_i-u_\ell}+
\sqrt{m-v_i-v_\ell}
}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]

Set
\[
A=u_i+u_\ell,\qquad B=v_i+v_\ell,\qquad R=A+B.
\]
Then \(0\leq R<\Delta_j<m\). For fixed \(R\), the concave function
\[
A\longmapsto
\sqrt{M-A}+\sqrt{m-(R-A)}
\qquad(0\leq A\leq R)
\]
attains its minimum at an endpoint. The endpoint obtained by taking \(A=0\) is the smaller one, because
\[
\sqrt m-\sqrt{m-R}
\geq
\sqrt M-\sqrt{M-R}
\]
when \(M>m\). Consequently,
\[
\sqrt{M-A}+\sqrt{m-B}
\geq
\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-R}
>
\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\Delta_j}.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (16) and (17) proves (3).

There is also a product version which retains the common-pair information. Let
\[
\mathcal D_j=
\{(x_0,x_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
f_j(x_0)+f_j(x_2)<0\},
\qquad d_j=|\mathcal D_j|.
\tag{18}
\]
For every \((x_0,x_2)\in E_0\times E_2\), the two nonnegative sums in the proof of (15), together with
\[
\sum_{r=1}^3(f_r(x_0)+f_r(x_2))=-2,
\]
give
\[
f_j(x_0)+f_j(x_2)\leq-2<0.
\]
Thus
\[
E_0\times E_2\subseteq\mathcal D_j
\]
and
\[
d_j\geq|E_0||E_2|
\geq(M-A)(m-B).
\tag{19}
\]

Write \(C_j=K\cap\{f_j<0\}\), whose area is \(c_j\) because the cutting line has area zero. Every midpoint of a pair in \(A_0\times A_2\) belongs to \(K\) by (5). Under the sum map \(y=x_0+x_2\), Fubini therefore gives
\[
d_j
=
\int_{2C_j}
(\mathbf 1_{A_0}*\mathbf 1_{A_2})(y)\,dy.
\tag{20}
\]
For every \(y\),
\[
(\mathbf 1_{A_0}*\mathbf 1_{A_2})(y)
=|A_0\cap(y-A_2)|
\leq|A_2|=m.
\]
Since \(|2C_j|=4c_j\), equation (20) gives
\[
d_j\leq4mc_j.
\tag{21}
\]

For fixed \(R=A+B<m\), the product
\[
(M-A)(m-(R-A))
\]
is concave in \(A\in[0,R]\), so its minimum is at an endpoint. The value at \(A=0\) is the smaller endpoint value because
\[
(M-R)m-M(m-R)=R(M-m)>0.
\]
Therefore, using \(R<\Delta_j\),
\[
(M-A)(m-B)
\geq M(m-R)
>M(m-\Delta_j).
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (19), (21), and (22) prove (4).

The final equivalent formulation is the contrapositive of (3)-(4).
