## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty interior,
let \(A=|K|\), and let \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) be nonzero pairwise
nonparallel linear forms such that
\[
\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0.
\]
Put
\[
H_i=\{\ell_i\leq0\},\qquad b_i=|K\cap H_i|.
\]
Then
\[
5(b_1+b_2+b_3)-6\min_i b_i\geq4A.                 \tag{1}
\]

For the induced alternating six-sector fan, let \(s_i\) be the area of the
singleton sector belonging only to \(H_i\), and let \(d_i\) be the area of
its opposite doubleton sector. Then, without any sign assumption,
\[
s_1+s_2+s_3\geq6\min_i(s_i-d_i).                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, (2) proves the registered subgoal under its hypotheses
\(s_i-d_i>0\).

## Proof

All cap ratios are invariant under a common dilation about the fan center.
Normalize \(A=1\), write
\[
p_i=b_i,\qquad
F(K)=5(p_1+p_2+p_3)-6\min_i p_i,
\]
and suppose for contradiction that
\[
F(K)<4.                                           \tag{3}
\]
Relabel so that
\[
p_1\leq p_2\leq p_3.
\]
Then
\[
F(K)=-p_1+5p_2+5p_3\geq9p_2,
\]
and hence
\[
p_1,p_2<\frac49.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Also
\[
p_2+p_3<\frac{4+p_1}{5}<\frac89<1.                \tag{5}
\]
Consequently every pair sum \(p_i+p_j\) is strictly below \(1\).

We first dispose of bodies whose fan center is not interior. This step is
included in full because it is also needed to make the compactness argument
coercive.

NONCENTERED LEMMA
Suppose \(p_1,p_2\leq1/2\) and the common zero \(O\) of the forms does not
belong to \(\operatorname{int}K\). Then
\[
p_3\geq1-p_1\quad\hbox{or}\quad p_3\geq1-p_2.     \tag{6}
\]

To prove it, use coordinates \(u=\ell_1\), \(v=\ell_2\), so that
\(\ell_3=-(u+v)\). Denote the six open-sector areas in cyclic order by
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A_0:\ u<0,\ v<0,&
B:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v<0,&
C:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v>0,\\
D:\ u>0,\ v>0,&
E:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v>0,&
F_0:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v<0
\end{array}
\]
and denote their areas by \(a,b,c,d,e,f\), respectively. Thus
\[
p_1=a+e+f,\qquad p_2=a+b+c,\qquad p_3=c+d+e.
\]
The assumptions on \(p_1,p_2\) give
\[
a\leq d.                                          \tag{7}
\]

Because \(O\notin\operatorname{int}K\), separation supplies a nonzero linear
functional which is nonnegative on \(K\) and strictly positive on
\(\operatorname{int}K\). We claim that
\[
bf=0.                                             \tag{8}
\]
If \(b,f,d>0\), choose interior points
\[
X_B=(r,-s)\in B,\qquad X_D=(x,y)\in D,\qquad
X_F=(-m,n)\in F_0,
\]
where \(s>r>0\), \(x,y>0\), and \(m>n>0\). The three cyclic determinants
\[
ry+sx,\qquad xn+ym,\qquad ms-nr
\]
are positive. The planar determinant identity therefore writes \(0\) as a
strictly positive linear combination of \(X_B,X_D,X_F\), contradicting the
strictly positive separator. Thus \(b,f>0\) would force \(d=0\).

On the other hand, the segment joining interior points of \(B\) and \(F_0\)
stays in \(u+v<0\). Along that segment \(u\) changes from positive to
negative and \(v\) from negative to positive. At the zero of \(u\), one has
\(v=u+v<0\), so the zero of \(u\) occurs before the zero of \(v\). The
intervening segment lies in \(A_0\), giving \(a>0\). This contradicts
\(a\leq d=0\), and proves (8).

If \(b=0\), then
\[
p_3=c+d+e\geq c+d=1-p_1.
\]
If \(f=0\), then
\[
p_3=c+d+e\geq d+e=1-p_2.
\]
This proves the noncentered lemma, including \(O\in\partial K\) and empty
sector cases.

Apply the lemma to (3)-(4). In the first alternative in (6),
\[
F(K)\geq-p_1+5p_2+5(1-p_1)\geq5-p_1>4,
\]
and in the second,
\[
F(K)\geq-p_1+5p_2+5(1-p_2)=5-p_1>4.
\]
Both contradict (3). Hence
\[
O\in\operatorname{int}K.                         \tag{9}
\]
Every open fan sector then has positive area, because \(K\) contains a
neighborhood of \(O\). Away from the three null cutting lines, every point
belongs to exactly one or exactly two of the \(H_i\). Therefore
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3>1.                                   \tag{10}
\]
Equations (3) and (10) give
\[
p_1>\frac16.                                      \tag{11}
\]
Since \(p_1\leq(p_1+p_2+p_3)/3\),
\[
F(K)\geq3(p_1+p_2+p_3),
\]
and hence
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3<\frac43.                              \tag{12}
\]

POLYGONAL EXTREMIZER AND COERCIVITY
Hausdorff approximation by convex polygons preserves area and the three
fixed halfplane-cut areas. Since (3) is strict and (9) holds, there is an
area-one convex polygon \(Q\), still with \(O\) in its interior, such that
\[
F(Q)<4.
\]
Let \(N\) be its number of genuine vertices, and choose
\[
F(Q)<c<4.
\]
Consider all area-one convex polygons with at most \(N\) genuine vertices
and \(F\leq c\). By the noncentered lemma, every polygon in this class
contains \(O\) in its interior.

We prove that this class has uniformly bounded diameter. Suppose instead
that \(P_n\) is a sequence in the class with diameter \(D_n\to\infty\).
Choose diameter endpoints and let \(e_n\) be the unit direction from one
endpoint to the other; pass to a subsequence with \(e_n\to e\). Let \(f_n\)
be a perpendicular unit vector and write
\[
z=x e_n+y f_n.
\]
The projection interval in the \(x\)-coordinate has length \(D_n\) and
contains \(0\).

Let \(w_n(x)\) be the length of the perpendicular section of \(P_n\) at
longitudinal coordinate \(x\). If the projection interval is
\([\alpha_n,\alpha_n+D_n]\), put
\[
q_n(t)=D_nw_n(\alpha_n+D_nt),\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
\]
Convexity makes \(q_n\) nonnegative and concave, and area normalization
gives
\[
\int_0^1q_n(t)\,dt=1.
\]
If \(q_n\) has maximum \(M_n\), concavity and nonnegativity put below its
graph the two triangles joining a maximizer to the interval endpoints.
Their total area is \(M_n/2\). Thus
\[
0\leq q_n\leq2.                                  \tag{13}
\]

The line through the diameter endpoints has distance at most \(2/D_n\)
from every point of \(P_n\): otherwise the triangle formed by the two
diameter endpoints and that point, which is contained in \(P_n\), would
have area greater than \(1\). Since \(O\in P_n\), the line itself is at
distance at most \(2/D_n\) from \(O\). Consequently
\[
|y|\leq\frac4{D_n}\qquad(z\in P_n).               \tag{14}
\]

Fix a linear form \(\ell\) with \(\ell(e)\ne0\). For all large \(n\),
\(|\ell(e_n)|\) is bounded below. By (14), the signs of
\(\ell(xe_n+yf_n)\) and \(x\ell(e_n)\) can differ only where
\[
|x|\leq\frac{C_\ell}{D_n}.
\]
By (13), the area of this region is \(O(D_n^{-2})\). After a subsequence,
let
\[
a=\lim_n|P_n\cap\{x\leq0\}|.
\]
It follows that the cap fraction for \(\{\ell\leq0\}\) tends to \(a\) if
\(\ell(e)>0\), and to \(1-a\) if \(\ell(e)<0\).

At most one of the \(\ell_i(e)\) vanishes, because the forms are pairwise
nonparallel. If none vanishes, their sum is zero, so two have one sign and
the third the opposite sign. The limiting cap vector is, after replacing
\(a\) by \(1-a\) if needed,
\[
(a,a,1-a).
\]
Its limiting functional value is
\[
5(1+a)-6\min\{a,1-a\}\geq\frac92.                \tag{15}
\]

If exactly one form vanishes on \(e\), the other two have opposite signs.
Their limiting cap fractions sum to \(1\). Passing to a further subsequence,
let the remaining cap fraction tend to \(r\in[0,1]\). Then
\[
\lim_nF(P_n)
=5(1+r)-6\min\{a,1-a,r\}
\geq5(1+r)-6\min\left\{r,\frac12\right\}
\geq\frac92.                                      \tag{16}
\]
Both (15) and (16) contradict \(F(P_n)\leq c<4\). The required diameter
bound follows.

Since every polygon contains \(O\), the diameter bound places the entire
class in one fixed ball. A polygon with at most \(N\) vertices is the convex
hull of \(N\) points after allowing repetitions. Compactness of the ball,
continuity of area and fixed-halfplane-cut area under Hausdorff convergence,
and area normalization show that the class is compact. Hence \(F\) has a
minimizer \(P\) in it. Moreover
\[
F(P)\leq F(Q)<c<4.                               \tag{17}
\]
Discard repeated and collinear listed points and retain the genuine vertices
of \(P\).

NONSMOOTH STATIONARITY AND FAN-RAY VERTICES
Small arbitrary motions of the genuine vertices preserve convexity and
\(O\in\operatorname{int}P\). Homothety about \(O\) restores area one and
does not change the cap fractions. Since (17) makes the sublevel constraint
inactive, \(P\) is an unconstrained local minimizer of \(F\) in its genuine
vertex coordinates.

Let \(h_i\in\mathbb R^6\) be the sector indicator vector of \(H_i\), and let
\(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\). Write
\[
g_i=5(p_1+p_2+p_3)-6p_i,
\qquad F=\max_i g_i.
\]
The standard finite-maximum stationarity rule is valid even when several
\(p_i\) attain the minimum: there are
\[
\theta_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_i\theta_i=1,            \tag{18}
\]
with \(\theta_i=0\) unless \(p_i=\min_jp_j\), such that
\[
\sum_i\theta_i\,dg_i=0.                           \tag{19}
\]
For completeness, if \(0\) were outside the convex hull of the active
gradients, strict separation would give one vertex velocity decreasing all
active \(g_i\); the inactive functions have a positive value gap and would
remain inactive for a short time, contradicting local minimality.

Let \(L\) be the first derivative of the six sector areas with respect to
the genuine vertices. Since \(P\) has area one,
\[
dp_i=(h_i-p_i\mathbf1)^TL.
\]
Put
\[
c_i=5-6\theta_i,\qquad
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^3c_i(h_i-p_i\mathbf1).
\]
Equations (18)-(19) say
\[
\lambda\in\mathcal W:=
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,h_1,h_2,h_3\},
\qquad
L^T\lambda=0.                                     \tag{20}
\]
This weight is nonzero. Indeed, its jump across the \(i\)-th fan line is
\(c_i\). If \(\lambda=0\), all three jumps vanish, whereas
\[
\sum_i c_i=15-6=9.                                \tag{21}
\]

Suppose a genuine vertex of \(P\) lies on a fan ray. Accepted fact
`2cf161fab8bf9e2b` includes endpoint first variations and gives
\[
\ker L^T\cap\mathcal W=\{0\},
\]
contradicting (20)-(21). Thus no genuine vertex lies on a fan ray. Since
\(O\) is interior, every ray now meets the relative interior of an edge
transversally.

RANK-FIVE INTERFACES
Apply accepted fact `1b4ea45680c75628`. Full rank is impossible by (20).
The nontriangular rank-five \(3+3\) case is also impossible: the accepted
fact's two edge-moment formulas and the two defining identities of
\(\mathcal W\) force positive parameters \(R,S\) to satisfy simultaneously
\[
S=R+1,\qquad R=S+1.
\]
Thus its annihilator line has trivial intersection with \(\mathcal W\).

The only other rank-five case is a triangle whose three edges cross two
rays each. Its vertices occupy either all three doubleton sectors or all
three singleton sectors. In the doubleton orientation, relabel the two
smallest caps as \(H_1,H_2\); by (4), both have ratios below \(1/2\).
The doubleton calculation in accepted fact `1b4ea45680c75628` applies to
any nonzero \(\mathcal W\)-annihilator. In its positive parameters \(r,s>1\)
it gives
\[
1-(1-p_1)-(1-p_2)
=\frac{(r-1)^2(s-1)^2}{(rs-1)^2}>0.
\]
Hence \(p_1+p_2>1\), contradicting (5). Therefore \(P\) is a triangle with
one vertex in each singleton sector.

TRIANGLE TERMINAL CASE
Relabel once more so that
\[
p_1\leq p_2\leq p_3,
\]
and label the singleton vertices \(V_i\in
\operatorname{int}H_i\setminus(H_j\cup H_k)\). Equations
(4)-(5), (11), and (12) give
\[
\frac16<p_1<\frac49,\qquad
p_i+p_j<1\ (i\ne j),\qquad
p_1+p_2+p_3<\frac43.                             \tag{22}
\]

We use the elementary triangle form of the planar centroid inequality.
Every halfplane containing the centroid of a triangle has at least \(4/9\)
of its area. Indeed, translate its boundary parallel toward the centroid
until the boundary contains the centroid. In barycentric coordinates, let
the values of a defining linear functional at the three vertices sum to
zero. If two values are nonnegative, normalize the negative value to
\(-1\) and write the other two as \(x,1-x\), \(0\leq x\leq1\). The
negative-side triangular cap has relative area
\[
\frac1{(1+x)(2-x)}
=\frac1{2+x(1-x)}\in\left[\frac49,\frac12\right].
\]
The other side has area between \(1/2\) and \(5/9\). The case of two
negative values is symmetric. This proves the claim, including a centroid
on the halfplane boundary.

Let \(G\) be the centroid of \(P\). If \(G\in H_1\), the centroid inequality
would give \(p_1\geq4/9\), contrary to (22). Likewise \(G\notin H_2\).
Because \(\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0\), it follows that
\[
G\in\operatorname{int}H_3,\qquad G\notin H_1\cup H_2.            \tag{23}
\]
All hypotheses of accepted fact `640eff7c0417543d` now hold. It yields
\[
5(p_2+p_3)-p_1\geq4.
\]
The left side is \(F(P)\), contradicting (17). This proves (1).

SHARPNESS
Let \(K\) be any nondegenerate triangle, let
\(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) be its barycentric affine coordinates, and
take the centroid as the origin. Put
\[
\ell_i=\frac13-\alpha_i,\qquad
H_i=\{\alpha_i\geq1/3\}.
\]
The \(\ell_i\) are linear in the centered coordinates, have distinct
pairwise nonparallel kernels, and sum to zero. Each \(K\cap H_i\) is the
vertex triangle of linear scale \(2/3\), so
\[
b_1=b_2=b_3=\frac49A.
\]
Equality holds in (1), proving sharpness and attainment.

SECTOR FORM
For the alternating singleton/doubleton labeling, orient \(H_i\) toward
the singleton sector indexed by \(i\). The three oriented normals have a
strictly positive linear dependence; positive rescaling of their defining
forms therefore gives \(\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0\) without changing any
halfplane. Put
\[
D=d_1+d_2+d_3.
\]
The cutting lines have planar area zero, and the exact sector partition
gives, for every \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
b_i=s_i+d_j+d_k=D+s_i-d_i,\qquad
b_1+b_2+b_3=A+D.                                 \tag{24}
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
&5(b_1+b_2+b_3)-6\min_i b_i-4A\\
&\qquad=A-D-6\min_i(s_i-d_i)\\
&\qquad=s_1+s_2+s_3-6\min_i(s_i-d_i).
\end{aligned}
\]
Inequality (2) is exactly (1). The equality triangle above has
\[
D=\frac A3,\qquad s_i-d_i=\frac A9,\qquad
s_1+s_2+s_3=\frac{2A}{3},
\]
so equality also occurs in the registered sector inequality with all three
differences positive. The proof used strict counterexamples only; bodies
with the fan center outside or on the boundary, fan-ray vertices, tied
minimum caps, lost vertices in polygonal limits, and zero-area sectors were
all included in the preceding alternatives.
