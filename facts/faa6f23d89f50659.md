## Statement

Work in
\[
\Pi=\{(y_1,y_2,y_3):y_1+y_2+y_3=1\},
\qquad
f_i=-200y_i+\frac{199}{3},
\qquad
L=-\frac{199}{3},
\qquad
p=\frac{199}{300}.
\]
Use the fixed polygons \(A_2,K\), the exact crossing convention, and the endpoint area
\[
M=
\frac{481\,886\,268\,000\,037\,895\,503}
     {2\,295\,616\,388\,316\,451\,200\,000}
\]
of accepted facts `0c0621b05f1db4ff`, `007cd9d294748c86`, and
`89563f2f27529ab0`. Let \(A_0\) range over all two-dimensional convex
hexagons whose six cyclic vertices occur in pairs on
\(y_3=p,y_1=p,y_2=p\), in that cyclic order, with every vertex outside
the indicated pair satisfying the corresponding strict inequality
\(y_i<p\). Assume
\[
|A_0|=M,\qquad \frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
and that all three endpoint caps have area zero.

There is no such \(A_0\) for which the three nominal equalities at \(L\)
are genuine lower crossings in the sense of accepted facts
`624b129b68e0fdee` and `eddda25c734d34bc`. This remains true after
allowing every density tie, every collinear limit, and every
support-face degeneracy in the closure of the six-vertex family.

## Proof

Put
\[
\delta=3p-1=\frac{99}{100},
\qquad
\lambda_i=\frac{p-y_i}{\delta}.
\]
On the endpoint support triangle \(D=\{y_i\leq p:i=1,2,3\}\), the
coordinates \(\lambda_i\) are nonnegative and sum to \(1\), and
\[
|D|=\frac{\delta^2}{2}=\frac{9801}{20000}
\]
in the \((y_1,y_2)\)-area convention.

Accepted fact `0c0621b05f1db4ff` gives the exact admissible endpoint
region
\[
F=\{y:y_i\leq p\ (i=1,2,3)\}
\cap\bigcap_{q\in\operatorname{vert}(A_2)}(2K-q)
=\operatorname{conv}\{F_0,\ldots,F_5\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, midpoint containment implies \(x\in2K-q\) for every
\(x\in A_0\) and every vertex \(q\) of \(A_2\), while the zero endpoint
caps and convexity imply \(A_0\subseteq D\). Hence \(A_0\subseteq F\).
In barycentric coordinates, the six counterclockwise vertices in (1)
are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\lambda_1&\lambda_2&\lambda_3\\ \hline
F_0&\dfrac{1\,604\,909}{4\,410\,450}&
     \dfrac{2\,805\,541}{4\,410\,450}&0\\[1mm]
F_1&\dfrac{54\,077}{381\,150}&
     \dfrac{327\,073}{381\,150}&0\\[1mm]
F_2&0&\dfrac{99\,373}{153\,450}&
     \dfrac{54\,077}{153\,450}\\[1mm]
F_3&0&\dfrac{437}{1782}&\dfrac{1345}{1782}\\[1mm]
F_4&\dfrac{437}{4455}&0&\dfrac{4018}{4455}\\[1mm]
F_5&\dfrac{877\,259}{3\,682\,800}&0&
     \dfrac{2\,805\,541}{3\,682\,800}.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
The support incidences and cyclic order are the fixed six-vertex fiber
isolated in accepted fact `007cd9d294748c86`; no coordinate of its
particular terminal hexagon is assumed here. After cyclic relabeling,
every member of the full fiber therefore has vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_0&=(1-f,f,0),&x_1&=(a,1-a,0),\\
x_2&=(0,1-b,b),&x_3&=(0,c,1-c),\\
x_4&=(d,0,1-d),&x_5&=(1-e,0,e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Convex cyclic order gives
\[
a+f\leq1,\qquad b+c\leq1,\qquad d+e\leq1,
\tag{4}
\]
with strict inequalities for six distinct support-edge endpoints.
Equations (1)-(2) give the six exact lower bounds
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
a\geq a_-:=\dfrac{54\,077}{381\,150},&
b\geq b_-:=\dfrac{54\,077}{153\,450},&
c\geq c_-:=\dfrac{437}{1782},\\[2mm]
d\geq d_-:=\dfrac{437}{4455},&
e\geq e_-:=\dfrac{2\,805\,541}{3\,682\,800},&
f\geq f_-:=\dfrac{2\,805\,541}{4\,410\,450}.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, (3)-(5) describe the closure of all cyclic support-line
hexagons contained in \(F\). The three omitted corner triangles of
\(D\) have relative areas \(ab,cd,ef\). Thus, including every
collinear boundary case,
\[
|A_0|=\frac{\delta^2}{2}(1-ab-cd-ef).
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to impose the genuinely one-sided information. Accepted
fact `167d064e3d9617cb` computes the fixed \(A_2\)-chord coordinate
spans at \(y_i=0\):
\[
q_1=\frac{91}{75\,090},\qquad
q_2=\frac{91}{380\,090},\qquad
q_3=\frac1{227\,500}.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(i=1,2\), chord density is the corresponding coordinate span
divided by \(200\). For \(i=3\), the Euclidean chord and gradient both
acquire the same factor \(\sqrt2\), so the density is again the
coordinate span divided by \(200\). The \(A_0\) support-edge spans are
\[
\delta(1-b-c),\qquad
\delta(1-d-e),\qquad
\delta(1-a-f)
\tag{8}
\]
for \(i=1,2,3\), respectively.

The fixed area and zero endpoint caps preserve the three exact nominal
equalities by accepted facts `0c0621b05f1db4ff` and
`89563f2f27529ab0`. At a genuine lower crossing, accepted facts
`624b129b68e0fdee` and `eddda25c734d34bc` require the one-sided
density difference to be negative, or to vanish with strictly negative
sum of slopes. In either case its first component is nonpositive.
Using (7)-(8), every candidate must therefore satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
a+f&\geq s_3:=1-\frac{q_3}{\delta}
 =\frac{225\,224}{225\,225},\\
b+c&\geq s_1:=1-\frac{q_1}{\delta}
 =\frac{742\,481}{743\,391},\\
d+e&\geq s_2:=1-\frac{q_2}{\delta}
 =\frac{3\,761\,981}{3\,762\,891}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus (9) already includes every density tie; imposing the additional
slope inequality could only shrink this relaxed feasible set.

The loss \(ab+cd+ef\) in (6) is strictly increasing in each of its six
positive variables. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_3-a_--f_-&=\frac{44\,550\,928}{200\,675\,475}>0,\\
s_1-b_--c_-&=\frac{693\,320\,273}{1\,728\,384\,075}>0,\\
s_2-d_--e_-&=\frac{58\,737\,022\,153}{419\,938\,635\,600}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently, its minimum subject to (4), (5), and (9) occurs after
decreasing variables until all three inequalities in (9) are
equalities. Write
\[
f=s_3-a,\qquad c=s_1-b,\qquad e=s_2-d.
\]
The remaining box is
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_-&\leq a\leq
a_+:=s_3-f_-=\frac{146\,044\,937}{401\,350\,950},\\
b_-&\leq b\leq
b_+:=s_1-c_-=\frac{3\,361\,075}{4\,460\,346},\\
d_-&\leq d\leq
d_+:=s_2-e_-=
\frac{33\,309\,885\,331}{139\,979\,545\,200}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
On this box the loss is
\[
\Phi(a,b,d)
=ab+(s_1-b)d+(s_2-d)(s_3-a).
\tag{12}
\]
The following exact margins determine its global minimum:
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_2-b_+-d_+
&=\frac{228\,142\,369}{27\,654\,145\,200}>0,\\
a_+-d_+
&=\frac{476\,387\,187\,746\,447}
        {3\,783\,227\,168\,120\,400}>0,\\
a_+-b_-+s_1-s_3
&=\frac{1\,754\,860\,108}{171\,110\,023\,425}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial a}=b+d-s_2<0
\]
throughout the box by the first line of (13), so a minimizer has
\(a=a_+\). With this choice,
\[
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial b}=a_+-d>0
\]
throughout the remaining box by the second line, so \(b=b_-\). With
these two choices,
\[
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial d}
=a_+-b_-+s_1-s_3>0
\]
by the third line, so \(d=d_-\). Therefore
\[
\Phi\geq
\Phi(a_+,b_-,d_-)
=
\frac{40\,351\,140\,305\,732\,756\,252\,537}
     {52\,732\,819\,270\,053\,605\,182\,500}.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (6) and (14) gives the boundary-complete area bound
\[
|A_0|
\leq
\frac{12\,381\,678\,964\,320\,848\,929\,963}
     {107\,607\,018\,202\,333\,650\,000\,000}.
\tag{15}
\]
But the prescribed area exceeds (15) by
\[
M-
\frac{12\,381\,678\,964\,320\,848\,929\,963}
     {107\,607\,018\,202\,333\,650\,000\,000}
=
\frac{11\,664\,845\,540\,778\,202\,767\,703}
     {122\,979\,449\,374\,095\,600\,000\,000}
>0.
\tag{16}
\]
This contradicts \(|A_0|=M\).

The argument optimized over the closed relaxation (3)-(5), allowed
zero support-edge lengths through (4), replaced every lexicographic
density condition by its weak first-coordinate consequence, and
included equality in all containment bounds. Hence density ties,
their slope tests, collinear vertices, support edges, support vertices,
and all translate-contact boundaries are covered. Accepted fact
`167d064e3d9617cb` excluded only the released terminal hexagon; (16)
excludes the entire fixed six-vertex fiber.
