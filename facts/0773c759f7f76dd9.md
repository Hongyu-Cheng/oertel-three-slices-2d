## Statement

Use the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on
\(\mathbb R^2\), with pairwise nonparallel linear parts, such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\), put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,
\]
and let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional
convex bodies.  Define their \(P_J,Q_J\) cells as in accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`.

Assume
\[
|P_{12}|,|P_{13}|,|P_{23}|>0
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
|Q_1|,|Q_2|,|Q_3|>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(A_0\) contains the closed bounded \(P\)-triangle
\[
\mathcal T_P=\{p_1\geq0,\ p_2\geq0,\ p_3\geq0\},
\]
and \(A_2\) contains the closed bounded \(Q\)-triangle
\[
\mathcal T_Q=\{q_1\leq0,\ q_2\leq0,\ q_3\leq0\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
|P_0|=|\mathcal T_P|,
\qquad
|Q_{123}|=|\mathcal T_Q|,
\]
and, writing
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,
\]
one has the exact common-fan identity
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{|Q_{123}|}{|P_0|}
=\left(\frac{\delta+2}{\delta}\right)^2>1.
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, no common-fan convex bodies, rational polygonal or
otherwise, can have all of the following cell masses:
\[
p_0=\frac67,\qquad
p_{12}=p_{13}=p_{23}=\frac{1129}{92400},
\]
\[
q_1=\frac{7123}{92400},\qquad
q_2=\frac{5629}{92400},\qquad
q_3=\frac{13}{100},\qquad
q_{123}=\frac{1129}{92400}.
\]

## Proof

We first prove an elementary barycentric containment lemma.  Work in
the affine plane
\[
\mathcal A=\{z\in\mathbb R^3:z_1+z_2+z_3=1\}.
\]
Suppose a convex set \(C\subset\mathcal A\) contains points
\(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},x^{(3)}\) such that, for every \(i\),
\[
x_i^{(i)}>1,\qquad x_j^{(i)}<0\quad(j\ne i).
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(X\) be the \(3\times3\) matrix whose \(i\)-th column is
\(x^{(i)}\), and let
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}
\left(x_1^{(1)},x_2^{(2)},x_3^{(3)}\right),
\qquad
C_0=D-X.
\]
The matrix \(C_0\) is entrywise nonnegative.  Put
\[
B=C_0D^{-1}.
\]
In column \(i\), the sum of the entries of \(B\) is
\[
\frac{-\sum_{j\ne i}x_j^{(i)}}{x_i^{(i)}}
=\frac{x_i^{(i)}-1}{x_i^{(i)}}<1.
\]
Hence the induced column norm satisfies \(\|B\|_1<1\), and
\[
X=(I-B)D
\]
is invertible with
\[
X^{-1}=D^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B^n
\]
entrywise nonnegative.  Every column of \(X\) sums to \(1\), so
\[
\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}X=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}X^{-1}=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus, for every standard basis vector \(e_i\), the coefficient vector
\[
\lambda^{(i)}=X^{-1}e_i
\]
is nonnegative and has coordinate sum \(1\).  Therefore
\[
e_i=X\lambda^{(i)}
\in\operatorname{conv}\{x^{(1)},x^{(2)},x^{(3)}\}
\subseteq C.
\]
Convexity now gives
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\subseteq C.
\tag{5}
\]

Apply this lemma first to \(A_0\).  Since
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3=\delta,
\]
the map
\[
x\longmapsto
\left(\frac{p_1(x)}{\delta},
\frac{p_2(x)}{\delta},
\frac{p_3(x)}{\delta}\right)
\]
is an affine isomorphism from \(\mathbb R^2\) onto \(\mathcal A\).
The positive-area hypothesis on \(P_{23}\) supplies a point away from
all three cutting lines, hence a point whose normalized coordinates
have sign pattern
\[
(+,-,-).
\]
Its first coordinate is greater than \(1\), because the coordinates
sum to \(1\).  The positive cells \(P_{13}\) and \(P_{12}\) similarly
supply strict patterns
\[
(-,+,-),\qquad(-,-,+).
\]
Equation (5), pulled back to \(\mathbb R^2\), proves
\[
\mathcal T_P\subseteq A_0.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(A_2\), put
\[
\sigma=1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)=\delta+2>0.
\]
Then
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-\sigma,
\]
so
\[
x\longmapsto
\left(-\frac{q_1(x)}{\sigma},
-\frac{q_2(x)}{\sigma},
-\frac{q_3(x)}{\sigma}\right)
\]
is another affine isomorphism onto \(\mathcal A\).  Positive area in
\(Q_i\) supplies a point off the cutting lines with normalized sign
pattern positive in coordinate \(i\) and negative in the other two
coordinates.  Its \(i\)-th coordinate is again greater than \(1\).
Using \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) in (5) proves
\[
\mathcal T_Q\subseteq A_2.
\tag{7}
\]

The open cell \(P_0\) is the interior of \(\mathcal T_P\), up to its
three null boundary lines.  Equation (6) therefore gives
\[
|P_0|=|\mathcal T_P|.
\]
Likewise, (7) gives
\[
|Q_{123}|=|\mathcal T_Q|.
\]

It remains to compare the two triangle areas.  Use
\((p_1,p_2)\) as affine coordinates.  If \(\gamma>0\) is the constant
area Jacobian from these coordinates back to the original plane, then
\(\mathcal T_P\) is the coordinate triangle with vertices
\[
(0,0),\qquad(\delta,0),\qquad(0,\delta),
\]
and hence
\[
|\mathcal T_P|=\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
The functions \(q_i\) differ from \(p_i\) only by constants, so
\((q_1,q_2)\) has the same area Jacobian.  In these coordinates,
\(\mathcal T_Q\) has vertices
\[
(0,0),\qquad(-\sigma,0),\qquad(0,-\sigma),
\]
and therefore
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
=\frac{\gamma\sigma^2}{2}
=\frac{\gamma(\delta+2)^2}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Dividing (9) by (8) proves (3).

We now apply the lemma to the proposed corrected table.  Its relevant
masses are
\[
p_{12}=p_{13}=p_{23}
=\frac{1129}{92400}>0,
\]
\[
q_1=\frac{7123}{92400}>0,\qquad
q_2=\frac{5629}{92400}>0,\qquad
q_3=\frac{13}{100}>0.
\]
Thus (1)--(2) hold.  But its bounded-cell masses are
\[
p_0=\frac67,
\qquad
q_{123}=\frac{1129}{92400},
\]
and
\[
p_0-q_{123}
=\frac{11153}{13200}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
This contradicts the necessary strict inequality
\(|Q_{123}|>|P_0|\) in (3).  Hence no common-fan convex endpoint pair
realizes the table.

The obstruction is open.  Put
\[
\varepsilon=\frac{1129}{92400}.
\]
For any cell table in the coordinatewise strict neighborhood
\[
|\widetilde p_J-p_J|<\varepsilon,\qquad
|\widetilde q_J-q_J|<\varepsilon
\tag{11}
\]
for every displayed cell, all three \(P\)-doubletons and all three
\(Q\)-singletons remain positive.  Moreover,
\[
\widetilde p_0-\widetilde q_{123}
>
p_0-q_{123}-2\varepsilon
=\frac{25271}{30800}>0.
\tag{12}
\]
Every table in (11), including every subfamily preserving all of the
previous scalar margins, therefore violates (3).

SageMath \(10.8\) audited the coordinate triangle areas over the exact
ring \(\mathbb Q[\delta]\):
\[
\frac{\delta^2}{2},
\qquad
\frac{(\delta+2)^2}{2},
\qquad
\frac{(\delta+2)^2-\delta^2}{2}=2(\delta+1)>0.
\]
It also checked (10)--(12) over \(\mathbb Q\).  No floating-point
decision or failed polygon search enters the obstruction.  Since the
endpoint pair does not exist, \(k_0\), the true opposite slab, and the
completion interval are not geometrically defined for this table.
