## Statement

Assume the surviving \(N=3\) transformed coordinate-cap model supplied by
accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `64760c76cadff481`.  Thus
\(P_0,P_1,P_2\) are nondegenerate triangles in
\[
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1+q\right\},\qquad
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1\right\},\qquad
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1-q\right\},
\]
respectively, where \(q>1\), their areas have been normalized to
\[
|P_0|:|P_1|:|P_2|=A:1:B,
\]
and
\[
1<A+B,\qquad B<1+A.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), define the closed coordinate-cap fractions
\[
u_i=\frac{|P_0\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|}{A},\qquad
c_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|,\qquad
v_i=\frac{|P_2\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|}{B}.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9
\]
and assume the three strict row inequalities
\[
A u_i+c_i+Bv_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(g_0,g_1\) be the area centroids of \(P_0,P_1\), and use the
closed-cap incidence sets
\[
I_0=\{i:(g_0)_i\ge0\},\qquad
I_1=\{i:(g_1)_i\ge0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then:

1. The two sets in (4) are nonempty and disjoint.  Up to a simultaneous
permutation of the coordinate indices, the only possibilities are
\[
(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2\}),\qquad
(\{1\},\{2,3\}),\qquad
(\{1,2\},\{3\}).
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_{i\in I_0}(g_0)_i>1+q,\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_1}(g_1)_i>1.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, a singleton \(I_0=\{i\}\) has
\((g_0)_i>1+q\), and a singleton \(I_1=\{j\}\) has
\((g_1)_j>1\).

2. The following quantitative cross-cap bounds hold:
\[
i\in I_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u_i\ge\frac49,\qquad
c_i+Bv_i<
\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
i\in I_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_i\ge\frac49,\qquad
Au_i+Bv_i<
\frac{2(A+B-1)}9<\frac{4A}{9}.
\tag{8}
\]
The inequalities include the case in which a centroid lies on the
coordinate line \(x_i=0\).

3. Suppose in addition that
\[
A+B\le\frac54,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad t\le\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
Label the vertices of \(P_1\) and write
\(\ell_{ij}\) for the \(i\)-th coordinate of its \(j\)-th vertex.  Thus
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_{ij}=1\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\tag{10}
\]
For every \(i\in I_1\), exactly one of
\(\ell_{i1},\ell_{i2},\ell_{i3}\) is positive and the other two are
strictly negative.  After a column permutation there are
\(p_i,a_i,b_i>0\) such that
\[
(\ell_{i1},\ell_{i2},\ell_{i3})=(p_i,-a_i,-b_i),
\qquad p_i\ge a_i+b_i,
\tag{11}
\]
and the exact row constraint is
\[
c_i=\frac{p_i^2}{(p_i+a_i)(p_i+b_i)}<t\le\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, on putting \(x_i=a_i/p_i\) and \(y_i=b_i/p_i\),
\[
x_i,y_i>0,\qquad x_i+y_i\le1,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
(1+x_i)(1+y_i)>\frac1t,\qquad
x_i+y_i>2\left(\frac1{\sqrt t}-1\right),
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
x_iy_i>\frac1t-1-x_i-y_i.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(|I_1|=2\), the unique positive entries of the two rows in
\(I_1\) occur in distinct columns.

The restrictions in part 3 exclude open, positive-dimensional row
chambers, not only isolated matrices.  They apply to every closed cap and
every zero-coordinate boundary stratum allowed by (2).

## Proof

We first record why (1) is automatic in the surviving system.  In the
notation of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, the normalized residual
parameters here are
\[
r=\frac AB,\qquad s=\frac1B.
\]
The strict raw residual inequalities include
\[
1-r<s<1+r.
\]
Multiplication by \(B>0\) gives exactly
\[
B<1+A,\qquad 1<A+B.
\tag{16}
\]
Accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` supplies the transformed triangles, their
coordinate-sum planes, the normalized area ratio, and the literal row
masses (3).  Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` supplies the complete closed
triangle-cap formula used below.

Because the coordinates of \(g_0\) sum to \(1+q>0\), at least one of them
is strictly positive, so \(I_0\ne\varnothing\).  Likewise the coordinates
of \(g_1\) sum to \(1\), so \(I_1\ne\varnothing\).

The planar centroid halfplane inequality recorded in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` applies to the closed coordinate halfplane on each
nondegenerate triangle.  Hence
\[
i\in I_0\Longrightarrow u_i\ge\frac49,
\qquad
i\in I_1\Longrightarrow c_i\ge\frac49.
\tag{17}
\]
If one index belonged to both sets, (17) would give
\[
A u_i+c_i+Bv_i
\ge\frac{4(A+1)}9.
\]
By (16),
\[
\frac{4(A+1)}9-t
=\frac{2(1+A-B)}9>0,
\tag{18}
\]
contradicting (3).  Thus \(I_0\cap I_1=\varnothing\).  This argument uses
closed halfplanes: equality \((g_k)_i=0\) means that the centroid is
included, not omitted.

Two nonempty disjoint subsets of a three-element set have cardinalities
\((1,1),(1,2)\), or \((2,1)\).  A simultaneous coordinate permutation
gives exactly (5).  Since the other incidence set is nonempty, each
\(I_k\) is a proper subset of \(\{1,2,3\}\).  Every coordinate outside
\(I_k\) is strictly negative.  Removing those negative coordinates from
the total coordinate sum proves (6).

For \(i\in I_0\), equations (3) and (17) give
\[
c_i+Bv_i
<t-\frac{4A}{9}
=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9.
\tag{19}
\]
The last quantity is less than \(4/9\) precisely because \(B<1+A\).
This proves (7).  For \(i\in I_1\), the same calculation gives
\[
Au_i+Bv_i
<t-\frac49
=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9.
\tag{20}
\]
It is positive by \(A+B>1\), and it is less than \(4A/9\) precisely
because \(B<1+A\).  This proves (8), including its strictness.

We now prove part 3.  Equation (9) and (3) imply
\[
c_i<t\le\frac12
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{21}
\]
Fix \(i\in I_1\).  The mean of the three vertex values
\(\ell_{i1},\ell_{i2},\ell_{i3}\) is \((g_1)_i\ge0\).
We claim that if at least two of those three values are nonnegative, then
the closed cap has fraction at least \(1/2\).

If all three values are nonnegative, the closed cap is the whole triangle.
Otherwise sort them as
\[
-u<0\le b\le c.
\]
The nonnegative mean gives \(b+c\ge u\).  The exact cap formula in
accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`, valid also when \(b=0\), gives
\[
c_i
=1-\frac{u^2}{(u+b)(u+c)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Since
\[
(u+b)(u+c)
=u^2+u(b+c)+bc
\ge2u^2,
\]
equation (22) gives \(c_i\ge1/2\), contradicting (21).  If all three
vertex values were nonpositive, their nonnegative mean would force all
three to be zero; the closed equality cap would again be the whole
triangle.  Therefore exactly one vertex value is positive and the other
two are strictly negative.  This proves the sign assertion in (11).
The nonnegative mean gives \(p_i\ge a_i+b_i\), and the unique-positive
branch of the exact triangle cap formula gives (12).

Divide (12) by \(p_i^2\).  With the notation in (13), it becomes
\[
\frac1{(1+x_i)(1+y_i)}<t,
\]
which proves the first inequality in (14) and (15).  If
\(s_i=x_i+y_i\), then
\[
(1+x_i)(1+y_i)
=1+s_i+x_iy_i
\le1+s_i+\frac{s_i^2}{4}
=\left(1+\frac{s_i}{2}\right)^2.
\]
Combining this with the strict first inequality in (14) proves its second
inequality.  This also shows directly that a surviving row must be close
to the balanced boundary \(x_i+y_i=1\) when \(t\) is close to \(4/9\).

It remains to prove that two rows in \(I_1\) cannot isolate the same
vertex.  Suppose otherwise and permute rows and columns so that their
vertex-value rows are
\[
(p,-a,-b),\qquad (r,-c,-d),
\tag{23}
\]
where all six displayed parameters are positive.  By (21) and the exact
cap formula,
\[
(p+a)(p+b)>2p^2,\qquad
(r+c)(r+d)>2r^2.
\tag{24}
\]
The column sums (10) force the third row to be
\[
(1-p-r,\ 1+a+c,\ 1+b+d).
\tag{25}
\]
If \(1-p-r\ge0\), all three entries in (25) are nonnegative, so its
closed cap is full, contradicting (21).  Hence put
\[
u=p+r-1>0,\qquad v=1+a+c>0,\qquad w=1+b+d>0.
\]
The third row is \((-u,v,w)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz and (24) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
(u+v)(u+w)
&=((p+a)+(r+c))((p+b)+(r+d))\\
&\ge
\left(
\sqrt{(p+a)(p+b)}
+\sqrt{(r+c)(r+d)}
\right)^2\\
&>2(p+r)^2
>2(p+r-1)^2
=2u^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The two-positive branch of the exact cap formula now gives
\[
c_k
=1-\frac{u^2}{(u+v)(u+w)}
>\frac12,
\tag{27}
\]
again contradicting (21).  Thus the isolated positive columns are
distinct.

For clarity, the excluded sign sets are genuinely positive-dimensional.
For example, both matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&2&1\\
5&-2&-1\\
-3&1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
22&-10&-10\\
25&-12&-11\\
-46&23&22
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{28}
\]
have column sums \((1,1,1)\), row sums \((2,2,-1)\), and determinants
\(-4\) and \(-32\), respectively.  The first has a positive-mean row
with two positive vertex values.  In the second, the first two
positive-mean rows isolate the same vertex; their cap fractions are
\[
\frac{121}{256}<\frac12,\qquad
\frac{625}{1332}<\frac12,
\]
while (27) forces the third cap above \(1/2\).  All displayed determinant,
row-sum, vertex-sign, and strict cap inequalities persist under small
column-sum-preserving perturbations.  Hence the two excluded
combinatorial types contain open sets in the affine space of row
matrices.  They are noncyclic because the positive vertex incidences are
not three distinct singleton incidences.  This completes the claimed
incidence classification and chamber exclusion.
