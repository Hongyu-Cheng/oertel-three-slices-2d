## Statement

Let
\[
\mathcal L=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x\le-|y|\},
\qquad
\mathcal R=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x\ge|y|\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex polygons such that
\[
A_0\subseteq\mathcal L,\qquad A_2\subseteq\mathcal R,\qquad
B:=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Put
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9<M,\qquad \pi=t-m.
\]
Assume that \(\delta>0\),
\[
\min_{A_0}x=-\delta,\qquad
\min_{A_2}(x+y)=0,\qquad
\min_{A_2}(x-y)=0.
\]
Define
\[
C=|K\cap\{x\le1\}|,\qquad
v_1=|A_2\cap\{x\le\delta+2\}|,\qquad
\theta=m-v_1,
\]
and assume the two strict absolute-level inequalities
\[
C+m>t,\qquad C+v_1<t.
\]
Let
\[
D(x,y)=(u,v)=(x+y,x-y),\qquad
P=-D(A_0),\qquad Q=D(A_2).
\]
Choose contact points
\[
q_u=(0,\sigma)\in Q,\qquad q_v=(\tau,0)\in Q,
\]
and set
\[
E_u=\frac{q_u-P}{2},\qquad
E_v=\frac{q_v-P}{2},\qquad
E=\operatorname{conv}(E_u\cup E_v).
\]
For \(d\in\mathbb R^2\), define
\[
\omega_P(d)=
\max_{p\in P}\det(d,p)-\min_{p\in P}\det(d,p).
\]
Then:
\[
E_u,E_v,E\subseteq D(B),
\qquad
E=\frac{[q_u,q_v]-P}{2};
\]
\[
|D^{-1}(E)|
=\frac M4+\frac{\omega_P(q_v-q_u)}8;
\]
\[
\left|D^{-1}(E_u)\cap\{x\le1\}\right|
=\frac18\left|P\cap\{p_u+p_v\ge\sigma-4\}\right|,
\]
\[
\left|D^{-1}(E_v)\cap\{x\le1\}\right|
=\frac18\left|P\cap\{p_u+p_v\ge\tau-4\}\right|;
\]
and
\[
k-C\ge\frac14(\sqrt M+\sqrt\theta)^2,
\qquad
\theta>C-\pi>0.
\]
Moreover, if
\[
R_0:=7C+2\pi-2k>0,
\]
then the exact strict consequence is
\[
k-C>
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{R_0}{2}}+\sqrt{C-\pi}
\right)^2.
\]

## Proof

The two contact points exist because \(A_2\) is compact and both displayed minima are attained. If \(a_u,a_v\in A_2\) attain the minima of \(x+y\) and \(x-y\), respectively, then
\[
q_u=D(a_u)=(0,\sigma),\qquad
q_v=D(a_v)=(\tau,0).
\]
The cone inclusion \(A_2\subseteq\mathcal R\) also gives \(\sigma,\tau\ge0\), although their signs are not needed below.

Since \(P=-D(A_0)\), one has \(-P=D(A_0)\). Linearity of \(D\) therefore gives
\[
D(B)=\frac{-P+Q}{2}.
\]
Thus, for every \(p\in P\),
\[
\frac{q_u-p}{2},\frac{q_v-p}{2}\in D(B),
\]
which proves \(E_u,E_v\subseteq D(B)\). The set \(D(B)\) is convex, so it also contains \(E\).

It remains to identify this convex hull exactly. If \(0\le\lambda\le1\) and \(p_1,p_2\in P\), convexity of \(P\) gives
\[
\lambda\frac{q_u-p_1}{2}
+(1-\lambda)\frac{q_v-p_2}{2}
=
\frac{\lambda q_u+(1-\lambda)q_v-
(\lambda p_1+(1-\lambda)p_2)}2
\in\frac{[q_u,q_v]-P}{2}.
\]
Conversely, if \(q=\lambda q_u+(1-\lambda)q_v\in[q_u,q_v]\) and \(p\in P\), then
\[
\frac{q-p}{2}
=\lambda\frac{q_u-p}{2}
+(1-\lambda)\frac{q_v-p}{2}\in E.
\]
Hence
\[
E=\frac{[q_u,q_v]-P}{2}.
\]

We next prove the segment-extrusion formula rather than assume it. Put
\[
d=q_v-q_u,\qquad R=-P.
\]
Translation does not change area, and
\[
[q_u,q_v]-P=q_u+(R+[0,d]).
\]
If \(d=0\), then \(R+[0,d]=R\) and \(\omega_P(d)=0\), so
\[
|R+[0,d]|=|P|+\omega_P(d).
\]
Suppose now that \(d\ne0\). Let \(e=d/|d|\) and let \(n=(-e_2,e_1)\). The coordinates \(z=se+rn\) are orthonormal, and
\[
\det(d,z)=|d|r.
\]
For every \(r\) in the projection of \(R\) onto the \(n\)-axis, convexity makes the slice
\[
\{s:se+rn\in R\}
\]
an interval. Adding \([0,d]\) adds \([0,|d|]\) to that interval, so its one-dimensional length increases by exactly \(|d|\). The length of the \(r\)-projection of \(R=-P\) is
\[
\frac{
\max_{z\in R}\det(d,z)-\min_{z\in R}\det(d,z)
}{|d|}
=\frac{\omega_P(d)}{|d|},
\]
because replacing \(p\) by \(-p\) negates both extrema and preserves their difference. Integrating the slice lengths in the orthonormal \((s,r)\)-coordinates yields
\[
|R+[0,d]|
=|R|+|d|\frac{\omega_P(d)}{|d|}
=|P|+\omega_P(d).
\]
This also proves the formula in the previously separated case \(d=0\).

The homothety by \(1/2\) in
\[
E=\frac{[q_u,q_v]-P}{2}
\]
has planar Jacobian \(1/4\). Hence
\[
|E|=\frac14\bigl(|P|+\omega_P(d)\bigr).
\]
The matrix of \(D\) has determinant \(-2\), while the reflection in the definition \(P=-D(A_0)\) has area factor \(1\). Consequently
\[
|P|=2|A_0|=2M.
\]
Also
\[
D^{-1}(u,v)=\left(\frac{u+v}{2},\frac{u-v}{2}\right)
\]
has determinant \(-1/2\), so its planar Jacobian is \(1/2\). Keeping the two Jacobians separate,
\[
|D^{-1}(E)|
=\frac12|E|
=\frac12\cdot\frac14
\bigl(|P|+\omega_P(d)\bigr)
=\frac M4+\frac{\omega_P(q_v-q_u)}8.
\]

For the first clipped copy, the affine bijection
\[
p\longmapsto\frac{q_u-p}{2}
\]
from \(P\) onto \(E_u\) has planar Jacobian \(1/4\). If its image is denoted by \((u,v)\), then
\[
u+v=\frac{\sigma-p_u-p_v}{2}.
\]
Under \(D^{-1}\), the condition \(x\le1\) is
\[
\frac{u+v}{2}\le1,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
u+v\le2.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\frac{\sigma-p_u-p_v}{2}\le2
\iff
\sigma-p_u-p_v\le4
\iff
p_u+p_v\ge\sigma-4.
\]
Thus the inequality points toward the upper diagonal tail of \(P\). Applying first the \(1/4\) affine Jacobian and then the \(1/2\) Jacobian of \(D^{-1}\) gives
\[
\left|D^{-1}(E_u)\cap\{x\le1\}\right|
=\frac12\cdot\frac14
\left|P\cap\{p_u+p_v\ge\sigma-4\}\right|.
\]
For \(q_v=(\tau,0)\), the same calculation starts from
\[
u+v=\frac{\tau-p_u-p_v}{2}
\]
and gives, with the same two Jacobians,
\[
\left|D^{-1}(E_v)\cap\{x\le1\}\right|
=\frac18
\left|P\cap\{p_u+p_v\ge\tau-4\}\right|.
\]

We finally prove the forced-tail bound and all strict comparisons. Since \(\pi=t-m\), the first strict absolute-level inequality gives
\[
C+m>t=m+\pi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C-\pi>0.
\]
Since \(v_1=m-\theta\), the second gives
\[
C+m-\theta=C+v_1<t=m+\pi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta>C-\pi.
\]
Therefore
\[
\theta>C-\pi>0.
\]

Let
\[
A_2^+=A_2\cap\{x\ge\delta+2\}.
\]
The line \(\{x=\delta+2\}\) has planar area zero, so
\[
|A_2^+|
=|A_2|-|A_2\cap\{x\le\delta+2\}|
=m-v_1=\theta.
\]
For every \(a_0\in A_0\) and \(a_2\in A_2^+\), the support condition
\(\min_{A_0}x=-\delta\) gives
\[
x\left(\frac{a_0+a_2}{2}\right)
\ge\frac{-\delta+(\delta+2)}2=1.
\]
Also \(A_2^+\subseteq A_2\), so
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2^+}{2}
\subseteq B\cap\{x\ge1\}
\subseteq K\cap\{x\ge1\}.
\]
The line \(\{x=1\}\) has planar area zero, hence
\[
|K\cap\{x\ge1\}|=k-C.
\]
The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality, applied to the compact convex sets \(A_0\) and \(A_2^+\), whose areas are \(M>0\) and \(\theta>0\), states
\[
|A_0+A_2^+|^{1/2}\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt\theta.
\]
Area scales by \(1/4\) under the homothety by \(1/2\). The preceding containment therefore gives
\[
k-C
\ge\left|\frac{A_0+A_2^+}{2}\right|
=\frac14|A_0+A_2^+|
\ge\frac14(\sqrt M+\sqrt\theta)^2.
\]

It remains to derive the exact strict consequence. The definitions of \(t\) and \(\pi\) give the canonical mass identity
\[
9(m+\pi)=2(M+m+k),
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
2M=7m+9\pi-2k.
\]
Because \(v_1\ge0\), the strict inequality \(C+v_1<t\) implies \(C<t=m+\pi\). Therefore, for
\[
R_0=7C+2\pi-2k,
\]
one has the strict identity comparison
\[
2M-R_0
=7m+9\pi-2k-(7C+2\pi-2k)
=7(m+\pi-C)
=7(t-C)>0.
\]
If \(R_0>0\), it follows that
\[
M>\frac{R_0}{2}>0,
\qquad
\theta>C-\pi>0.
\]
Taking square roots preserves both strict inequalities, and both sums are positive. Thus
\[
\frac14(\sqrt M+\sqrt\theta)^2
>
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{R_0}{2}}+\sqrt{C-\pi}
\right)^2.
\]
Combining this with the forced-tail bound proves
\[
k-C>
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{7C+2\pi-2k}{2}}+\sqrt{C-\pi}
\right)^2,
\]
as claimed.
