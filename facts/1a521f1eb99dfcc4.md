## Statement

Use barycentric coordinates
\[
r_1(x,y)=x,\qquad r_2(x,y)=y,\qquad r_3(x,y)=1-x-y
\]
and write \(\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3\) for the same coordinates on \(Q\).
Put
\[
s=\frac{101}{100},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{67}{200},\qquad
f_i=200(r_i-\lambda),\qquad L_i=-67.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), and the endpoint-cell convention of accepted
fact `0e51a47a9add571f` indexes the cells of \(Q\) by the nonnegative
\(\tau_i\).  Fix
\[
a=\frac{12}{125},\qquad
\varepsilon_R=\frac{51}{10000},\qquad
\varepsilon_L=\frac{49}{10000},
\]
\[
\eta_1=\frac{13}{2500},\qquad
\eta_2=\frac1{200},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(a,a),(1+\eta_1,-\varepsilon_R),
(-\varepsilon_L,1+\eta_2)
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(Q\) be any compact two-dimensional convex body whose exact
positive-area support is
\[
\mathcal S_Q=\{1,2,12,123\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the \(j=1,k=2\) single-double support identified in accepted
fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`.  Define
\[
A_2=2\lambda-sQ,\qquad
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad M=|A_0|,\quad m=|A_2|,\quad k=|B|.
\tag{5}
\]
On a point of \(A_2\) corresponding to \(\tau\in Q\), one has
\[
f_i+L_i=-202\tau_i.
\]
Thus the stated nonnegative-coordinate indexing is exactly the
accepted endpoint-cell indexing, including its weak boundary
convention.
Put
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{r_i\leq0\}|,\qquad
v_i=s^2|Q\cap\{\tau_i\geq0\}|,
\]
\[
c_i=|B\cap\{r_i\leq\lambda\}|,\qquad
e_i=u_i+v_i+c_i,\qquad
\mathcal T=M+m+k.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(i=1,2\), define the normalized endpoint profile
\[
\mathcal E_i(t)
=|A_0\cap\{r_i\leq t\}|
+s^2|Q\cap\{\tau_i\geq t/s\}|.
\tag{7}
\]
Assume
\[
\mathcal E'_{1,+}(0)<0,\qquad
\mathcal E'_{2,+}(0)<0.
\tag{8}
\]

Finally set
\[
\rho_i=e_i-\frac{2\mathcal T}{9},\qquad
\Gamma=\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3,\qquad
\widehat z_i=-\rho_i-\frac23\Gamma.
\tag{9}
\]
These are the three coordinates of the extension gate in accepted
fact `89563f2f27529ab0`.  Then
\[
\widehat z_1+\widehat z_2<-\frac{23}{240}.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular, at least one of \(\widehat z_1,\widehat z_2,\widehat
z_3\) is negative.

## Proof

All areas are measured in the \((x,y)\)-coordinates.  The outer edge
of \(A_0\) lies on
\[
x+y=\frac{10001}{10000}.
\tag{11}
\]
Direct intersection with the two coordinate axes gives
\[
A_0\cap\{x=0\}:
\quad
\frac{121188}{126125}\leq y\leq\frac{10001}{10000},
\]
\[
A_0\cap\{y=0\}:
\quad
\frac{40412}{42125}\leq x\leq\frac{10001}{10000}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the two coordinate section widths are
\[
w_1^A=\frac{395969}{10090000},\qquad
w_2^A=\frac{137377}{3370000}.
\tag{13}
\]

The positive-area cells \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\) put points of \(Q\) on
both sides of each coordinate axis.  Hence zero is in the interior of
both coordinate projection intervals of \(Q\).  The coordinate chord
lengths of a two-dimensional convex body are continuous at every
interior projection value.  Fubini's formula therefore gives
\[
\mathcal E'_{i,+}(0)=w_i^A-s w_i^Q
\qquad(i=1,2),
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
w_1^Q=\operatorname{length}_y(Q\cap\{x=0\}),\qquad
w_2^Q=\operatorname{length}_x(Q\cap\{y=0\}).
\]
Consequently (8) implies
\[
w_1^Q>\frac{w_1^A}{s}
=\frac{395969}{10190900}
>\frac{19}{500},
\]
\[
w_2^Q>\frac{w_2^A}{s}
=\frac{137377}{3403700}
>\frac1{25}.
\tag{15}
\]
The last two exact differences are
\[
\frac{395969}{10190900}-\frac{19}{500}
=\frac{21787}{25477250}>0,
\qquad
\frac{137377}{3403700}-\frac1{25}
=\frac{1229}{3403700}>0.
\tag{16}
\]

Write the two axis chords as
\[
Q\cap\{x=0\}=\{(0,y):\alpha\leq y\leq Y\},
\qquad
Q\cap\{y=0\}=\{(x,0):\beta\leq x\leq X\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The exact positive-area support (4), rather than only the vertex
signs, forces
\[
\alpha\geq1,\qquad \beta\geq1.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, each coordinate axis is a nonsupporting line of \(Q\), so a
relative-interior point of either chord is an interior point of \(Q\).
If the \(x=0\) chord had a relative-interior point \((0,y)\) with
\(y<1\), choose such a point with \(y\ne0\).  A small interior ball
would meet \(Q_3\) when \(y<0\), or \(Q_{23}\) when \(0<y<1\), in
positive area.  Both cells are absent from (4).  This proves the first
inequality in (18).  The same argument on \(y=0\) produces positive
area in \(Q_3\) or \(Q_{13}\) if \(\beta<1\), proving the second.
This argument permits cutting-line contact and does not infer support
from a vertex list.

It follows from (15), (17), and (18) that
\[
Y>\frac{519}{500},\qquad X>\frac{26}{25}.
\tag{19}
\]
Let \(H\) be the convex hull of the two chords in (17).  Convexity
gives \(H\subseteq Q\), and
\[
|H|
=\frac{XY-\alpha\beta}{2}
=\frac{\beta w_1^Q+\alpha w_2^Q+w_1^Qw_2^Q}{2}
>
\frac12\left(
\frac{19}{500}+\frac1{25}
+\frac{19}{500}\frac1{25}
\right).
\tag{20}
\]
Every point of \(H\) has \(x,y\geq0\) and \(x+y\geq1\).  Apart from
its area-zero boundary, \(H\) therefore lies in \(Q_{12}\).

Consider the weighted small-end contribution
\[
\Phi_Q=7(v_1+v_2)+4v_3-4m.
\tag{21}
\]
On the four positive-area cells \(1,2,12,123\), its area-density
coefficients before the factor \(s^2\) are, respectively,
\[
3,\quad3,\quad10,\quad14.
\tag{22}
\]
They are all nonnegative.  Equations (20)-(22) give
\[
\Phi_Q
>5s^2\left(
\frac{19}{500}+\frac1{25}
+\frac{19}{500}\frac1{25}
\right)
=\frac{5069897}{12500000}
>\frac25.
\tag{23}
\]
The positive difference in the last inequality is
\[
\frac{69897}{12500000}.
\]

We next obtain the middle contribution from a forced Minkowski
subset.  Put
\[
C=A_0-sQ.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(d=(d_1,d_2)\in C\), define
\[
d_3=1-s-d_1-d_2.
\tag{25}
\]
Since
\[
3\lambda+\frac{1-s}{2}=1,
\]
the barycentric coordinates of \(B\) are
\(\lambda+d_i/2\).  Therefore
\[
k=\frac{|C|}{4},\qquad
c_i=\frac14|C\cap\{d_i\leq0\}|.
\tag{26}
\]
Define
\[
\omega(d)
=7\mathbf 1_{\{d_1\leq0\}}
+7\mathbf 1_{\{d_2\leq0\}}
+4\mathbf 1_{\{d_3\leq0\}}-4.
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(d_1+d_2+d_3=1-s<0\), the nonempty sign types
\[
1,2,3,12,13,23,123
\]
have respective \(\omega\)-values
\[
3,3,0,10,7,7,14.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(\omega\geq0\) everywhere, up to the irrelevant assignment of
cutting-line boundaries, and
\[
\Phi_C:=7(c_1+c_2)+4c_3-4k
=\frac14\int_C\omega(d)\,dd.
\tag{29}
\]

The outer chord endpoints \((X,0)\) and \((0,Y)\) belong to \(Q\).
Set
\[
I=\operatorname{conv}\{(X,0),(0,Y)\},
\qquad
D=A_0-sI.
\tag{30}
\]
Then \(D\subseteq C\).  Every point of \(A_0\) has
\[
x+y\leq\frac{10001}{10000},
\]
whereas every point of \(I\) has coordinate sum at least
\(\min\{X,Y\}>519/500\).  Hence, throughout \(D\),
\[
d_1+d_2
<
\frac{10001}{10000}
-\frac{101}{100}\frac{519}{500}
=-\frac{1207}{25000}
<-\frac1{100}=1-s.
\tag{31}
\]
Thus \(d_3>0\) on \(D\).  At least one of \(d_1,d_2\) is nonpositive,
and (27) reduces on \(D\), outside area-zero cutting lines, to
\[
\omega(d)
=3+7\mathbf 1_{\{d_1\leq0,\ d_2\leq0\}}.
\tag{32}
\]

We now bound both terms in (32).  Translating does not change area,
and \(D\) is the Minkowski sum of \(A_0\) with a segment of vector
\[
v=(sX,-sY).
\]
For a planar convex body \(A\),
\[
|A+[0,v]|
=|A|+\max_{p\in A}\det(v,p)-\min_{p\in A}\det(v,p).
\tag{33}
\]
This identity follows directly by an affine change sending \(v\) to
a horizontal vector and integrating the added horizontal section
lengths.

For \(\ell(x,y)=Yx+Xy\), compare the two outer vertices of \(A_0\)
with \((a,a)\).  The maximum minus the minimum of \(\ell\) is at
least the larger of these two differences, hence at least their
average.  Their sum is
\[
\left(1+\eta_2-2a-\varepsilon_R\right)X
+\left(1+\eta_1-2a-\varepsilon_L\right)Y
=\frac{8079}{10000}X+\frac{8083}{10000}Y.
\tag{34}
\]
The shoelace formula gives
\[
M=|A_0|=\frac{81626181}{200000000}.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (19), (33), and (34) imply
\[
|D|
>
M+\frac{s}{2}\left(
\frac{8079}{10000}\frac{26}{25}
+\frac{8083}{10000}\frac{519}{500}
\right)
=\frac{628071381}{500000000}
>\frac54.
\tag{36}
\]
The last positive difference is
\[
\frac{3071381}{500000000}.
\]

There is also a large forced triangle in the southwest quadrant of
\(D\).  Put
\[
\kappa=s-a\left(\frac1X+\frac1Y\right)>0.
\tag{37}
\]
The segment
\[
(a,a)-sI\subseteq D
\]
meets the two coordinate axes at
\[
(-X\kappa,0),\qquad(0,-Y\kappa).
\tag{38}
\]
The outer edge (11) of \(A_0\) contains
\[
(c,c),\qquad c=\frac{10001}{20000},
\]
and \(I\) contains
\[
(h,h),\qquad h=\frac{XY}{X+Y}.
\]
Since \(X,Y>1\), one has \(h>1/2\), so
\[
d:=sh-c>0.
\]
It follows that the third point
\[
(-d,-d)=(c,c)-s(h,h)
\tag{39}
\]
also lies in \(D\).  The triangle with vertices (38)-(39) is
contained in
\[
D\cap\{d_1\leq0,\ d_2\leq0\}.
\]
Its area is
\[
J
=\frac12\left(
XY\kappa^2-d\kappa(X+Y)
\right)
=\frac{c-a}{2}(X+Y)\kappa.
\tag{40}
\]
The second equality uses \(h(X+Y)=XY\).  From (19),
\[
X+Y>\frac{1039}{500},\qquad
\kappa>s-2a=\frac{409}{500},\qquad
c-a=\frac{8081}{20000}.
\]
Therefore
\[
J
>
\frac12\frac{8081}{20000}
\frac{1039}{500}\frac{409}{500}
=\frac{3434029031}{10000000000}
>\frac13.
\tag{41}
\]
The last positive difference is
\[
\frac{302087093}{30000000000}.
\]

Since \(\omega\geq0\) on all of \(C\), the containment \(D\subseteq C\)
and (29), (32), (36), and (41) yield
\[
\Phi_C
\geq\frac14\left(3|D|+7J\right)
>
\frac14\left(3\frac54+7\frac13\right)
=\frac{73}{48}.
\tag{42}
\]

For the fixed large endpoint, nonnegativity of the three cap areas
and (35) give
\[
\Phi_A:=7(u_1+u_2)+4u_3-4M
\geq-4M
>-\frac{49}{30},
\tag{43}
\]
because
\[
\frac{49}{30}-4M
=\frac{121457}{150000000}>0.
\]
Combining (23), (42), and (43), we obtain the exact strict bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi
&:=7(e_1+e_2)+4e_3-4\mathcal T\\
&=\Phi_A+\Phi_Q+\Phi_C\\
&>
-\frac{49}{30}+\frac25+\frac{73}{48}
=\frac{23}{80}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]

The gate definition (9), equivalently the identities in accepted
fact `89563f2f27529ab0`, gives
\[
3\widehat z_1
=2\mathcal T-(5e_1+2e_2+2e_3),
\]
\[
3\widehat z_2
=2\mathcal T-(2e_1+5e_2+2e_3).
\tag{45}
\]
Adding (45) and applying (44) proves
\[
3(\widehat z_1+\widehat z_2)
=4\mathcal T-7(e_1+e_2)-4e_3
=-\Phi<-\frac{23}{80}.
\]
This is (10).  Hence
\[
\min\{\widehat z_1,\widehat z_2\}
<-\frac{23}{480}<0.
\]

Every cutting set used above is a line and has planar area zero.
The weak/strict exact-cell convention therefore changes none of the
area identities.  Positive area in (4) was used to put each
coordinate axis in the interior of the corresponding projection and
to exclude forbidden sign chambers from the relative interiors of
the axis chords.  Touching a forbidden chamber only on a cutting
line is allowed and does not affect the proof.  The strict derivative
hypotheses give the strict chord inequalities in (15), so no
zero-width or equality boundary has been discarded.
