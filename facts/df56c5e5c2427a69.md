## Statement

Equip
\[
E=\{(s_1,s_2,s_3)\in\mathbb R^3:s_1+s_2+s_3=1\}
\]
with induced Euclidean area. Define the rational parallelogram
\[
L=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(-1,-9,11),\ (0,-19/2,21/2),\ (0,21/2,-19/2),\ (-1,11,-9)
\right\}
\]
and the rational triangle
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(3,-1,-1),\ (7,-5,-1),\ (11/2,-1,-7/2)
\right\}.
\]
Set
\[
\sigma=(-1,0,0),\qquad \mu=5\sqrt3,\qquad A=25\sqrt3.
\]
Then \(L,K\subset E\) are compact two-dimensional convex bodies, \(K\) is a triangle,
\[
|L|=A-\mu,\qquad |K|=\mu,\qquad 0<\frac\mu A=\frac15<\frac9{25},
\qquad \sum_{j=1}^3\sigma_j=-1,
\]
and their six cap areas are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&j=1&j=2&j=3\\ \hline
|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|&0&\dfrac{43}{4}\sqrt3&\dfrac{43}{4}\sqrt3\\[2mm]
|K\cap\{s_j>\sigma_j\}|&5\sqrt3&0&0.
\end{array}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_{1\le j\le3}\left(
|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|+|K\cap\{s_j>\sigma_j\}|
\right)
=\frac{43}{4}\sqrt3
<\frac{100}{9}\sqrt3
=\frac{4A}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Use affine coordinates \((x,y)=(s_1,s_2)\) on \(E\), so \(s_3=1-x-y\). The parametrization \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y,1-x-y)\) has Gram determinant \(3\), hence induced Euclidean area is \(\sqrt3\) times coordinate area.

For \(L\), introduce the linear coordinate \(t=s_2-s_3\). Its displayed four vertices are precisely the images of the rectangle
\[
-1\le x\le0,\qquad -20\le t\le20
\]
under
\[
(x,t)\longmapsto
\left(x,\frac{1-x+t}{2},\frac{1-x-t}{2}\right).
\]
The Jacobian from \((x,t)\) to \((x,y)\) has absolute value \(1/2\). Thus the coordinate area of \(L\) is
\[
\frac12\cdot1\cdot40=20,
\]
so \(|L|=20\sqrt3=A-\mu\). Since \(-1\le s_1=x\le0\) throughout \(L\), the strict cap \(L\cap\{s_1>0\}\) is empty.

The condition \(s_2>0\) is \(t>x-1\). For every \(x\in[-1,0]\), this threshold lies strictly between \(-20\) and \(20\). Therefore the \((x,t)\)-area of this cap is
\[
\int_{-1}^{0}\bigl(20-(x-1)\bigr)\,dx
=\int_{-1}^{0}(21-x)\,dx
=\frac{43}{2}.
\]
After multiplying by the Jacobian \(1/2\) and then by \(\sqrt3\),
\[
|L\cap\{s_2>0\}|=\frac{43}{4}\sqrt3.
\]
Likewise, \(s_3>0\) is \(t<1-x\), whose interval length inside \([-20,20]\) is again \(21-x\). Hence
\[
|L\cap\{s_3>0\}|=\frac{43}{4}\sqrt3.
\]

For \(K\), put \(u=-s_2\) and \(v=-s_3\), so that
\[
(u,v)\longmapsto(s_1,s_2,s_3)=(1+u+v,-u,-v).
\]
The displayed vertices of \(K\) correspond to
\[
(u,v)=(1,1),\qquad(5,1),\qquad(1,7/2).
\]
The map from \((u,v)\) to \((x,y)\) has absolute Jacobian \(1\), so the coordinate area of \(K\) is
\[
\frac12(5-1)(7/2-1)=5.
\]
Thus \(|K|=5\sqrt3=\mu\). Throughout \(K\), one has \(s_1\ge3>-1=\sigma_1\), while \(s_2\le-1<0=\sigma_2\) and \(s_3\le-1<0=\sigma_3\). It follows that
\[
|K\cap\{s_1>\sigma_1\}|=|K|=5\sqrt3,
\qquad
|K\cap\{s_2>\sigma_2\}|=|K\cap\{s_3>\sigma_3\}|=0.
\]
This proves the table. The three paired sums are \(5\sqrt3,43\sqrt3/4,43\sqrt3/4\), and the last two are largest because \(43/4>5\). Finally,
\[
\frac{43}{4}<\frac{100}{9}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
387<400,
\]
which gives the strict failure claimed.
