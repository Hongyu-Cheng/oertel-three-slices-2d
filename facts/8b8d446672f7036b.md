## Statement

Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1/2)\},\\
A_1&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/2,1/2),(0,0),(3/4,0),
                         (1/2,1/4),(-1/2,3/4)\},\\
A_2&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(-1,1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their areas are \(1/8,13/32,1/2\), respectively, and the strict target is
\(11/48\).  For
\[
u_1=(1,1),\qquad u_2=(-1,0),\qquad u_3=(0,-1),
\]
define
\[
M_r(c)=\inf_{k\in\mathbb R}\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|A_i\cap\{w:u_r\cdot w\le c+k(i-1)\}\right|
\]
and
\[
R_r=\sup\{c\in\mathbb R:M_r(c)<11/48\}.
\]
Then
\[
R_1=\frac{\sqrt{366}-6}{60},\qquad
R_2=-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12},\qquad
R_3=-\frac56+\frac{\sqrt{26}}{12}.
\]
Their Farkas-weighted sum satisfies
\[
R_1+R_2+R_3
=\frac{\sqrt{366}+5\sqrt{78}+5\sqrt{26}-116}{60}
<-\frac7{20}<0.
\]
Consequently, there do not exist real \(c_r,k_r\), \(r=1,2,3\), such that
the three halfspaces
\[
H_r=\{(h,w):u_r\cdot w\le c_r+k_r(h-1)\}
\]
all have total three-slice mass strictly below \(11/48\) and their
height-\(1\) trace interiors cover \(\mathbb R^2\).

## Proof

For \(r=1,2,3\), \(i=0,1,2\), set
\[
F_{ri}(t)=|A_i\cap\{w:u_r\cdot w\le t\}|.
\]
Here is the complete exact clipping table.  For an increasing breakpoint
list \(B=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)\), write
\[
I_0=(-\infty,b_1),\qquad I_j=(b_j,b_{j+1})\ (1\le j<m),
\qquad I_m=(b_m,\infty).
\]
The last column lists the values of \(F_{ri}\) on \(I_0,\ldots,I_m\), in
that order:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|l}
r&i&B_{ri}&(F_{ri}|_{I_0},\ldots,F_{ri}|_{I_m})\\ \hline
1&0&(0,\frac12)&
\left(0,\frac{t^2}{2},\frac18\right)\\
1&1&(0,\frac14,\frac34)&
\left(0,\frac{t^2+t}{2},
\frac{-8t^2+16t-1}{16},\frac{13}{32}\right)\\
1&2&(0,1)&
\left(0,\frac{-t^2+2t}{2},\frac12\right)\\[2pt] \hline
2&0&(-\frac12,0)&
\left(0,\frac{(2t+1)^2}{8},\frac18\right)\\
2&1&(-\frac34,-\frac12,0,\frac12)&
\left(0,\frac{(4t+3)^2}{32},\frac{8t^2+16t+7}{32},
\frac{-8t^2+16t+7}{32},\frac{13}{32}\right)\\
2&2&(-1,0,1)&
\left(0,\frac{(t+1)^2}{4},
\frac{-t^2+2t+1}{4},\frac12\right)\\[2pt] \hline
3&0&(-\frac12,0)&
\left(0,\frac{(2t+1)^2}{8},\frac18\right)\\
3&1&(-\frac34,-\frac12,-\frac14,0)&
\left(0,\frac{(4t+3)^2}{16},\frac{8t^2+16t+7}{16},
\frac{24t+13}{32},\frac{13}{32}\right)\\
3&2&(-1,0)&
\left(0,\frac{(t+1)^2}{2},\frac12\right).
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
The \(r=2,3\) rows are the corresponding clipping subcomputations in
accepted fact `3c143dfcc90fab8e`; they are included here to make the present
cell audit explicit.  For the new \(r=1\) rows, use the area-preserving
coordinates
\[
s=x+y,\qquad z=x.
\]
At level \(s\), the three section widths, in increasing breakpoint order,
are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
A_0&s\quad(0<s<1/2)\\
A_1&s+1/2\quad(0<s<1/4),\qquad
    1-s\quad(1/4<s<3/4)\\
A_2&1-s\quad(0<s<1).
\end{array}
\]
Integrating these widths gives the first three rows of (1).  Equivalently,
on every breakpoint interval one may insert the exact edge intersection
\[
p+\frac{t-u_r\cdot p}{u_r\cdot(q-p)}(q-p)
\]
into the shoelace formula.  Adjacent entries in (1) agree at every listed
breakpoint, including the constant values at the unbounded ends.  Thus (1)
also gives all one-dimensional clipping boundaries exactly.

We next enumerate every open cell in the \((c,k)\)-plane.  Let
\(I_{ri,j}\) denote the intervals determined by \(B_{ri}\) in (1), and put
\[
\mathcal C^{(r)}_{abd}
=\{(c,k):c-k\in I_{r0,a},\ c\in I_{r1,b},\
                 c+k\in I_{r2,d}\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For each \(r\) and middle-interval index \(b\), the nonempty cells are
exactly the following:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
r&b&\{(a,d):\mathcal C^{(r)}_{abd}\ne\varnothing\}\\ \hline
1&0&\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(2,0)\}\\
1&1&\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)\}\\
1&2&\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}\\
1&3&\{(0,2),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)\}\\[2pt] \hline
2&0&\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(2,0)\}\\
2&1&\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)\}\\
2&2&\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}\\
2&3&\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,2),(1,3),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)\}\\
2&4&\{(0,3),(1,3),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2),(2,3)\}\\[2pt] \hline
3&0&\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(2,0)\}\\
3&1&\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)\}\\
3&2&\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1)\}\\
3&3&\{(0,2),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}\\
3&4&\{(0,2),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)\}.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the bijective affine change of variables
\[
a=c-k,\qquad d'=c+k,\qquad
c=\frac{a+d'}2,\qquad k=\frac{d'-a}2
\tag{4}
\]
shows that \(\mathcal C^{(r)}_{abd}\) is nonempty exactly when
\[
(I_{r0,a}+I_{r2,d})\cap2I_{r1,b}\ne\varnothing.
\]
Direct interval addition gives every entry in (3), so no open cell is
missing.  Every lower-dimensional cell is obtained by replacing one or
more of the three interval conditions in (2) by equality to a breakpoint
from (1).  Because the adjacent formulas in (1) agree there, every such
edge and vertex has the common boundary value of its incident cells.  This
accounts for all lower-dimensional clipping cells as well.

It remains to minimize the cell polynomials.  With
\[
a=c-k,\qquad c+k=2c-a,
\]
define
\[
G_r(c)=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_{r0}(a)+F_{r2}(2c-a)\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
M_r(c)=F_{r1}(c)+G_r(c).
\tag{6}
\]
The three claimed endpoint values lie in the rational intervals
\[
\frac15<\frac{\sqrt{366}-6}{60}<\frac14,\qquad
-\frac3{10}<-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12}<-\frac14,\qquad
-\frac5{12}<-\frac56+\frac{\sqrt{26}}{12}<-\frac25.
\tag{7}
\]
These follow by squaring positive quantities in
\[
18<\sqrt{366}<21,\qquad
\frac{42}{5}<\sqrt{78}<9,\qquad
5<\sqrt{26}<\frac{26}{5}.
\]
We therefore audit \(G_r\) on the three intervals in (7).

For \(r=1\), take \(1/5<c<1/4\) and set \(s=2c\), so
\(2/5<s<1/2\).  The breakpoints for the expression in (5), in increasing
order, are
\[
s-1,\qquad0,\qquad s,\qquad\frac12.
\]
On all five resulting intervals, including every open cell that meets this
endpoint strip, the exact expressions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{interval for }a&F_{10}(a)+F_{12}(s-a)\\ \hline
(-\infty,s-1)&\frac12\\
(s-1,0)&(s-a)-\frac{(s-a)^2}{2}\\
(0,s)&\frac{a^2}{2}+(s-a)-\frac{(s-a)^2}{2}\\
(s,\frac12)&\frac{a^2}{2}\\
(\frac12,\infty)&\frac18.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
The second entry decreases to the third at \(a=0\).  The third simplifies
to
\[
s-\frac{s^2}{2}+(s-1)a
\]
and is strictly decreasing because \(s<1\).  The fourth is strictly
increasing.  Hence the minimum among the middle three intervals occurs at
their common boundary \(a=s\), where its value is \(s^2/2\).  Since
\(s<1/2\), this is strictly below the constant competitors \(1/8\) and
\(1/2\).  The boundary agreement already checked after (1) shows that this
boundary minimum is attained, so
\[
G_1(c)=\frac{s^2}{2}=2c^2
\qquad(1/5<c<1/4).
\tag{9}
\]

For \(r=2\), take \(-3/10<c<-1/4\), so
\(-3/5<s=2c<-1/2\).  The ordered breakpoints are
\[
s-1,\qquad s,\qquad-\frac12,\qquad0,\qquad s+1.
\]
The complete vertical cell audit is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{interval for }a&F_{20}(a)+F_{22}(s-a)\\ \hline
(-\infty,s-1)&\frac12\\
(s-1,s)&\frac{-(s-a)^2+2(s-a)+1}{4}\\
(s,-\frac12)&\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{4}\\
(-\frac12,0)&
\frac{(2a+1)^2}{8}+\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{4}\\
(0,s+1)&\frac18+\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{4}\\
(s+1,\infty)&\frac18.
\end{array}
\tag{10}
\]
The second and third entries decrease toward the fourth interval.  The
fourth is strictly convex, with its unique minimum at \(a=s/3\), of value
\[
\frac{(2s+3)^2}{24}=\frac{(4c+3)^2}{24}.
\]
The fifth decreases to the last constant value \(1/8\).  Thus, including
all boundary values,
\[
G_2(c)=\min\left\{\frac{(4c+3)^2}{24},\frac18\right\}.
\]
Here \(4c+3>9/5\), and hence
\[
\frac{(4c+3)^2}{24}>\frac{(9/5)^2}{24}
=\frac{27}{200}>\frac18.
\]
Therefore
\[
G_2(c)=\frac18
\qquad(-3/10<c<-1/4).
\tag{11}
\]

For \(r=3\), take \(-5/12<c<-2/5\), so
\(-5/6<s=2c<-4/5\).  The ordered breakpoints are
\[
s,\qquad-\frac12,\qquad0,\qquad s+1.
\]
The complete vertical cell audit is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{interval for }a&F_{30}(a)+F_{32}(s-a)\\ \hline
(-\infty,s)&\frac12\\
(s,-\frac12)&\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{2}\\
(-\frac12,0)&
\frac{(2a+1)^2}{8}+\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{2}\\
(0,s+1)&\frac18+\frac{(s-a+1)^2}{2}\\
(s+1,\infty)&\frac18.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
The second entry decreases toward the third interval.  The third is
strictly convex, with its unique minimum at
\[
a=\frac{s}{2}+\frac14,
\]
of value
\[
\frac{(2s+3)^2}{16}=\frac{(4c+3)^2}{16}.
\]
The fourth decreases to the last constant value \(1/8\).  Consequently,
including all boundary values,
\[
G_3(c)=\min\left\{\frac{(4c+3)^2}{16},\frac18\right\}.
\]
On this interval \(4c+3<7/5\), so
\[
\frac{(4c+3)^2}{16}<\frac{(7/5)^2}{16}
=\frac{49}{400}<\frac18.
\]
It follows that
\[
G_3(c)=\frac{(4c+3)^2}{16}
\qquad(-5/12<c<-2/5).
\tag{13}
\]

Combining (1), (6), (9), (11), and (13) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&\text{interval}&M_r(c)\\ \hline
1&(1/5,1/4)&\displaystyle\frac{5c^2+c}{2}\\[3pt]
2&(-3/10,-1/4)&\displaystyle\frac{8c^2+16c+11}{32}\\[3pt]
3&(-5/12,-2/5)&\displaystyle\frac{(c+1)(3c+2)}{2}.
\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]
Each function in (14) is strictly increasing on its listed interval.  Its
equation \(M_r(c)=11/48\), after clearing positive denominators, is,
respectively,
\[
120c^2+24c-11=0,\qquad
24c^2+48c+11=0,\qquad
72c^2+120c+37=0.
\tag{15}
\]
The unique roots of (15) in the intervals in (14) are
\[
\frac{\sqrt{366}-6}{60},\qquad
-1+\frac{\sqrt{78}}{12},\qquad
-\frac56+\frac{\sqrt{26}}{12}.
\tag{16}
\]

For each fixed \(k\), all three slice thresholds in the definition of
\(M_r(c)\) increase with \(c\).  Hence the corresponding total mass is
nondecreasing in \(c\), and taking the infimum over \(k\) shows that every
\(M_r\) is nondecreasing.  By (14), immediately to the left of the
corresponding root in (16) the mass is strictly below \(11/48\), equality
holds at that root, and immediately to its right the mass is strictly
larger.  Global monotonicity extends these inequalities to all smaller and
larger \(c\), respectively.  Thus the roots in (16) are exactly
\(R_1,R_2,R_3\).

Their all-ones Farkas sum is
\[
R_1+R_2+R_3
=\frac{\sqrt{366}+5\sqrt{78}+5\sqrt{26}-116}{60}.
\]
The exact rational comparisons
\[
\sqrt{366}<20,\qquad \sqrt{78}<9,\qquad\sqrt{26}<6
\]
give
\[
R_1+R_2+R_3
<\frac{20+45+30-116}{60}
=-\frac7{20}<0.
\tag{17}
\]

Finally, suppose all three halfspaces in the statement have mass strictly
below \(11/48\).  Since \(M_r(c_r)\) is the infimum over slopes,
\[
M_r(c_r)<\frac{11}{48},
\]
so the endpoint characterization gives \(c_r<R_r\).  Equation (17) then
implies
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3<R_1+R_2+R_3<0.
\tag{18}
\]
The closed complements of the height-\(1\) trace interiors have the system
\[
x+y\ge c_1,\qquad x\le-c_2,\qquad y\le-c_3.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(u_1+u_2+u_3=0\), this is the all-ones Farkas system.  It is
feasible exactly when \(c_1+c_2+c_3\le0\): necessity follows by adding the
three inequalities, and sufficiency follows from the explicit point
\[
(x,y)=(-c_2,-c_3).
\]
Thus (18) makes the intersection of the three closed complements nonempty.
The three trace interiors cannot cover the middle plane.  This excludes the
entire fixed diagonal Farkas normal cell.
