## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\tag{1}
\]
and write \(a_\ell=|A_\ell|\).  For a unit vector \(q\), put
\[
F_{\ell,q}(c)
=\left|A_\ell\cap\{x:q\cdot x\leq c\}\right|
\qquad(\ell=0,2).
\tag{2}
\]
There are a point \(z\in\operatorname{int}A_1\) and unit normals
\(q_1,q_2,q_3\) such that the three lines
\[
L_i=\{x:q_i\cdot x=q_i\cdot z\}
\tag{3}
\]
divide \(A_1\) into six cyclic sectors of area \(a_1/6\), and, on writing
\[
D_i=\{x:q_i\cdot x\leq q_i\cdot z\},\qquad
U_i=\{x:q_i\cdot x\geq q_i\cdot z\},
\tag{4}
\]
one has
\[
D_1\cup D_2\cup D_3=\mathbb R^2,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\left|A_1\cap D_i\right|=\frac{a_1}{2},
\qquad
\left|A_1\cap U_i\cap U_j\right|=\frac{a_1}{6}
\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{6}
\]
More precisely, the three pairwise intersections in (6) are the closures
of three alternating sectors.

Define
\[
J_i=\inf_{c_{0i}+c_{2i}=2q_i\cdot z}
\left(F_{0,q_i}(c_{0i})+F_{2,q_i}(c_{2i})\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Then, for every \(i\ne j\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
J_i+J_j\ge L_*:=
\min\left\{m,\ M+m-\frac{2a_1}{3}\right\},
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
where
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(R_i=a_1/2+J_i\) and \(R_{(1)}\le R_{(2)}\le R_{(3)}\)
are the ordered row masses, then the exact order-statistic consequence of
(8) is
\[
R_{(2)}\ge \frac{a_1+L_*}{2}.
\tag{10}
\]

Suppose additionally that the areas lie in the strict residual strip of
accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Set
\[
T=M+m+a_1,\qquad
\delta=\frac{2T}{9}-\frac{a_1}{2}
=\frac{4(M+m)-5a_1}{18}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
L_*>2\delta,
\tag{12}
\]
so
\[
R_{(2)}>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus at least two fan rows strictly exceed the target and at most one
index can satisfy \(R_i\le2T/9\).  If \(\delta<0\), all three rows
strictly exceed the target.  If \(\delta=0\), all three meet the target
and at most one is equal to it.

## Proof

We first prove the six-sector equipartition directly.

Normalize planar area on \(A_1\) to a probability measure \(\mu\).  It
vanishes on every line.  For a directed unit vector \(v\), there is a
unique line \(L(v)\), directed by \(v\), whose two open sides both have
\(\mu\)-mass \(1/2\).  Indeed, translate a line parallel to \(v\) across
\(A_1\).  The mass on one side is continuous, and it is strictly
increasing while the line crosses the interior of the convex body.
The median line and its offset depend continuously on \(v\).

Fix \(v\), direct \(L(v)\) by \(v\), and let \(P\) vary on \(L(v)\).
Starting with the positive ray \(r_0(P)\) in direction \(v\), sweep
counterclockwise.  In the first open halfplane choose the unique rays
\(r_1(P),r_2(P)\) for which the sectors from \(r_0(P)\) have masses
\(1/6\) and \(1/3\).  Put \(r_3(P)=-r_0(P)\).  In the other open
halfplane choose the unique rays \(r_4(P),r_5(P)\) for which the sectors
from \(r_3(P)\) have masses \(1/6\) and \(1/3\).  The six consecutive
sectors bounded by
\[
r_0(P),r_1(P),\ldots,r_5(P)
\tag{14}
\]
therefore all have mass \(1/6\).

Here uniqueness and continuity do not require a positive density outside
\(A_1\).  From a point on \(L(v)\), the directions meeting
\(\operatorname{int}A_1\) in either open halfplane form an interval.
Every nonempty angular subinterval in its interior cuts positive area.
Consequently the angular distribution is strictly increasing at the
levels \(1/6\) and \(1/3\), and the four quantile rays depend
continuously on \(P\) and \(v\).

We next find the unique \(P=P_*(v)\) for which \(r_1(P)\) and \(r_4(P)\)
are opposite.  Use coordinates in which \(L(v)\) is the \(x\)-axis,
positive \(x\) points along \(v\), and \(P=(t,0)\).  If
\(\theta_1(t)\in(0,\pi)\) is the angle of \(r_1(P)\), put
\(s_1(t)=\cot\theta_1(t)\).  Its defining upper sector is
\[
A_1\cap\{y\ge0,\ x-s_1(t)y\ge t\},
\tag{15}
\]
and has area \(a_1/6\).  If \(t_2>t_1\) and
\(s_1(t_2)\ge s_1(t_1)\), the cap in (15) for \(t_2\) is strictly
contained in the cap for \(t_1\).  Strictness in area follows because
both that cap and its complement in the upper half-body have positive
area and the upper half-body is convex.  This contradicts equality of
their areas.  Hence \(s_1(t)\) strictly decreases and
\(\theta_1(t)\) strictly increases.

Similarly, if \(\theta_4(t)\in(\pi,2\pi)\) is the angle of \(r_4(P)\)
and \(s_4(t)=\cot\theta_4(t)\), its defining lower sector is
\[
A_1\cap\{y\le0,\ x-s_4(t)y\le t\},
\tag{16}
\]
again of area \(a_1/6\).  The same strict-containment argument shows
that \(s_4(t)\) strictly increases and hence
\(\theta_4(t)\) strictly decreases.  Compactness of \(A_1\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta_1(t)&\longrightarrow0,&
\theta_4(t)&\longrightarrow2\pi
&&\text{as }t\longrightarrow-\infty,\\
\theta_1(t)&\longrightarrow\pi,&
\theta_4(t)&\longrightarrow\pi
&&\text{as }t\longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the continuous strictly decreasing function
\[
\theta_4(t)-\theta_1(t)-\pi
\tag{18}
\]
has a unique zero.  This defines \(P_*(v)\).  The preceding continuity,
the uniform form of the limits in (17) for \(v\) in a small arc, and
uniqueness of the zero show that \(P_*(v)\) depends continuously on
\(v\).

At \(P_*(v)\), choose the angular lifts
\[
0<\theta_1<\theta_2<\pi<
\theta_4=\theta_1+\pi<\theta_5<2\pi
\tag{19}
\]
of \(r_1,r_2,r_4,r_5\), and define
\[
\phi(v)=\theta_5-\theta_2-\pi.
\tag{20}
\]
This is continuous.  Reversing \(v\) reverses the directed median line
but not its underlying set.  The six rays are relabelled by
\[
r_1\leftrightarrow r_4,\qquad
r_2\leftrightarrow r_5,\qquad
r_0\leftrightarrow r_3.
\tag{21}
\]
The pair condition defining \(P_*\) is unchanged, so uniqueness gives
\(P_*(-v)=P_*(v)\), and (20)--(21) give
\[
\phi(-v)=-\phi(v).
\tag{22}
\]
Along any half-circle from \(v\) to \(-v\), the intermediate value
theorem therefore gives a direction \(v_*\) with \(\phi(v_*)=0\).
At \(z=P_*(v_*)\), the three pairs
\[
(r_0,r_3),\qquad(r_1,r_4),\qquad(r_2,r_5)
\tag{23}
\]
are three concurrent lines, and their six cyclic sectors all have area
\(a_1/6\).  Their intersections with the interior of \(A_1\) are all
nonempty.  If \(z\notin\operatorname{int}A_1\), the three line traces
inside the convex body have no common interior point and can create at
most four interior regions, a contradiction.  Hence
\[
z\in\operatorname{int}A_1.
\tag{24}
\]

It remains to choose the required signs of the three normals.  Let
\(C_k\) be the closed sector from \(r_{k-1}\) to \(r_k\), with indices
modulo \(6\), so that each \(A_1\cap C_k\) has area \(a_1/6\).
Label the lines in (23) in their displayed order.  Choose their upper
sides to be
\[
U_1=C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3,\qquad
U_2=C_5\cup C_6\cup C_1,\qquad
U_3=C_3\cup C_4\cup C_5.
\tag{25}
\]
Equivalently, choose the unit normal \(q_i\) so that \(U_i\) is the
upper halfplane in (4).  Then
\[
U_1\cap U_2=C_1,\qquad
U_2\cap U_3=C_5,\qquad
U_3\cap U_1=C_3.
\tag{26}
\]
The lower sides are
\[
D_1=C_4\cup C_5\cup C_6,\qquad
D_2=C_2\cup C_3\cup C_4,\qquad
D_3=C_6\cup C_1\cup C_2.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (25)--(27), with their common boundary rays understood, prove
(5)--(6) and the asserted exact orientation.

We now prove the endpoint ledger.  Fix \(i\ne j\).  Accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240` shows that both infima in (7) are attained.  Choose
attaining thresholds
\[
c_{0k}+c_{2k}=2q_k\cdot z
\qquad(k=i,j)
\tag{28}
\]
and define
\[
B_{\ell k}
=A_\ell\cap\{x:q_k\cdot x\le c_{\ell k}\},
\qquad
b_{\ell k}=|B_{\ell k}|
\tag{29}
\]
for \(\ell=0,2\).  Thus
\[
J_i+J_j=b_{0i}+b_{2i}+b_{0j}+b_{2j}.
\tag{30}
\]
Put
\[
E_\ell
=A_\ell\cap\{x:q_i\cdot x\ge c_{\ell i}\}
          \cap\{x:q_j\cdot x\ge c_{\ell j}\},
\qquad e_\ell=|E_\ell|.
\tag{31}
\]
Lines have zero planar area, so the union bound gives
\[
e_\ell\ge a_\ell-b_{\ell i}-b_{\ell j}.
\tag{32}
\]

If \(J_i+J_j\ge m\), (8) is immediate.  Suppose
\(J_i+J_j<m\).  Then (30) gives
\[
b_{\ell i}+b_{\ell j}<m\le a_\ell
\qquad(\ell=0,2),
\tag{33}
\]
so both \(E_0,E_2\) have positive area.  If \(x_\ell\in E_\ell\), then
(28) and (31) imply, for \(k=i,j\),
\[
q_k\cdot\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}
\ge\frac{c_{0k}+c_{2k}}2
=q_k\cdot z.
\tag{34}
\]
By (1), which is also the necessary compatibility statement in accepted
fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`,
\[
\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}
\subseteq A_1\cap U_i\cap U_j.
\tag{35}
\]
The last body has area \(a_1/6\) by (6).  Planar
Brunn--Minkowski therefore yields
\[
\frac{\sqrt{e_0}+\sqrt{e_2}}2
\le
\sqrt{\left|\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\right|}
\le\sqrt{\frac{a_1}{6}},
\tag{36}
\]
and hence
\[
e_0+e_2
\le(\sqrt{e_0}+\sqrt{e_2})^2
\le\frac{2a_1}{3}.
\tag{37}
\]
On the other hand, (30) and (32) give
\[
e_0+e_2\ge M+m-(J_i+J_j).
\tag{38}
\]
Thus
\[
J_i+J_j\ge M+m-\frac{2a_1}{3}.
\tag{39}
\]
Together with the first case, this proves (8), including equality in the
case split and all cutting-line boundary ties.

For (10), order the \(J_i\) increasingly.  Applying (8) to the two
smallest gives
\[
J_{(1)}+J_{(2)}\ge L_*,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
J_{(2)}\ge\frac{L_*}{2}.
\tag{40}
\]
Adding \(a_1/2\) proves (10).

It remains to make the residual comparison.  The strict residual strip
in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` includes
\[
M-m<a_1<M+m.
\tag{41}
\]
From (11),
\[
2\delta=\frac{4(M+m)-5a_1}{9}.
\tag{42}
\]
The two entries in the minimum defining \(L_*\) satisfy
\[
m-2\delta
=\frac{5m-4M+5a_1}{9}
>\frac{M}{9}>0
\tag{43}
\]
by the left inequality in (41), and
\[
\left(M+m-\frac{2a_1}{3}\right)-2\delta
=\frac{5(M+m)-a_1}{9}
>\frac{4(M+m)}9>0
\tag{44}
\]
by the right inequality in (41).  Hence (12) holds.  Equations
(10)--(12) give (13).  Finally, each \(J_i\ge0\).  This proves the two
refinements according as \(\delta<0\) or \(\delta=0\), and completes the
claimed starvation ledger.

REMAINING ALL-DIRECTION GAP
For an arbitrary unit normal \(u\), put
\[
J(u)=
\inf_{c_0+c_2=2u\cdot z}
\left(F_{0,u}(c_0)+F_{2,u}(c_2)\right).
\tag{45}
\]
Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` gives the exact tilted depth profile
\[
h_S((1,z))
=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
\left|A_1\cap\{x:u\cdot x\le u\cdot z\}\right|+J(u)
\right].
\tag{46}
\]
The argument above proves the target row bound for at least two of the
three selected normals \(q_i\).  Reversing all three chosen normals and
repeating the same pairwise argument also controls two of the three
oppositely oriented fan rows: by (25)--(27), the old upper halfplanes
cover the plane, while the pairwise intersections of the old lower
halfplanes are \(C_4,C_2,C_6\).  It gives no lower bound for (46) at a
normal strictly between consecutive fan normals.  In particular, the
set-theoretic cover \(D_1\cup D_2\cup D_3=\mathbb R^2\) supplies no
angular convexity or interpolation inequality for the infimal
convolution \(J(u)\).

Thus the precise remaining statement needed at this concurrency point is
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{x:u\cdot x\le u\cdot z\}\right|+J(u)
\ge\frac{2T}{9}
\qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathbb S^1.
\tag{47}
\]
No assertion in this packet proves (47) away from the six signed fan
normals.  Consequently the packet stops at the fan-row starvation
ledger and does not claim that \(z\) is a full-depth witness.
