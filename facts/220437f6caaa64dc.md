## Statement

Use the coordinate and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `989c9b585d2b42cc`.  Let \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex polygon, let coordinate area in \((X,Y)\) equal \(\Delta>0\) times physical area, and put
\[
p_j=X,\qquad p_\ell=Y,\qquad p_k=\delta-X-Y.
\]
Suppose
\[
A\cap\{X=0\}
=\{(0,y):\alpha\le y\le\alpha+J\},
\]
\[
A\cap\{Y=0\}
=\{(x,0):\beta\le x\le\beta+H\},
\]
the strict cell
\[
P_\ell^\circ
=\operatorname{int}A\cap
\{X>0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y<\delta\}
\]
has physical area \(c>0\), and
\[
0<\alpha<\alpha+J<\beta<\beta+H<\delta.
\]
If
\[
\frac{(\alpha+J)(\delta-\beta-H)}
{\beta+H-\alpha-J}
\le
\frac{\alpha(\delta-\beta)}{\beta-\alpha},
\tag{1}
\]
then every such polygon necessarily satisfies
\[
2\Delta c
\le
\frac{\alpha(\delta-\beta)^2}{\beta-\alpha}
-
\frac{(\alpha+J)(\delta-\beta-H)^2}
{\beta+H-\alpha-J}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, no compact two-dimensional convex polygon realizes the complete \(A_0\) data in accepted fact `aa79870c25ab40a5`:
\[
\Delta=1,\qquad \delta=100,
\]
\[
|P_j|=|P_{k\ell}|=\frac1{10000},\qquad
|P_0|=\frac{286001}{4000},\qquad
|P_\ell|=\frac3{10},
\]
\[
A\cap\{X=0\}
=\{(0,y):52\le y\le521/10\},
\]
\[
A\cap\{Y=0\}
=\{(x,0):99\le x\le991/10\},
\]
\[
A\cap\{X+Y=100\}
=\{(100+t,-t):1\le t\le11/10\}.
\]

## Proof

Take any \((x,y)\in P_\ell^\circ\).  The segment from \((0,\alpha)\) to \((x,y)\) lies in \(A\) and meets \(Y=0\) at
\[
\left(\frac{\alpha x}{\alpha-y},0\right).
\]
This point belongs to the full horizontal chord, so its first coordinate is at least \(\beta\).  Since \(\alpha-y>0\),
\[
\alpha x+\beta y\ge\alpha\beta.
\tag{3}
\]
Likewise, the segment from \((0,\alpha+J)\) to \((x,y)\) meets \(Y=0\) at
\[
\left(\frac{(\alpha+J)x}{\alpha+J-y},0\right).
\]
Its first coordinate is at most \(\beta+H\), and hence
\[
(\alpha+J)x+(\beta+H)y
\le(\alpha+J)(\beta+H).
\tag{4}
\]
These two conclusions use arbitrary points of \(P_\ell^\circ\), not a prescribed polygonal ansatz.

Introduce
\[
r=-y,\qquad s=\delta-x-y.
\]
The absolute Jacobian of this affine change is one, and \(r,s>0\) on \(P_\ell^\circ\).  Inequalities (3) and (4) become
\[
s\le
\delta-\beta-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\alpha}r,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
s\ge
\delta-\beta-H
-
\frac{\beta+H-\alpha-J}{\alpha+J}r.
\tag{6}
\]
The upper boundary in (5) meets \(s=0\) at
\[
r_-=\frac{\alpha(\delta-\beta)}{\beta-\alpha},
\]
and the lower boundary in (6) meets \(s=0\) at
\[
r_+=
\frac{(\alpha+J)(\delta-\beta-H)}
{\beta+H-\alpha-J}.
\]
Under (1), the closed region cut out by \(r,s\ge0\), (5), and (6) is the quadrilateral with vertices
\[
(0,\delta-\beta),\quad
(r_-,0),\quad
(r_+,0),\quad
(0,\delta-\beta-H)
\]
in \((r,s)\)-coordinates.  Its coordinate area is
\[
\frac12\left(
(\delta-\beta)r_-
-
(\delta-\beta-H)r_+
\right)
\]
\[
=\frac12\left(
\frac{\alpha(\delta-\beta)^2}{\beta-\alpha}
-
\frac{(\alpha+J)(\delta-\beta-H)^2}
{\beta+H-\alpha-J}
\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The strict \(P_\ell\)-cell is contained in this quadrilateral.  Cutting-line boundaries have area zero, while its coordinate area is \(\Delta c\).  Comparing with (7) proves (2).

For the data of accepted fact `aa79870c25ab40a5`,
\[
r_-=\frac{52}{47}=\frac{5200}{4700},
\qquad
r_+=\frac{4689}{4700},
\]
so (1) holds.  The right side of (2) is
\[
\frac{52}{47}
-
\frac{(521/10)(9/10)^2}{47}
=\frac{9799}{47000}.
\]
Thus (2) would require
\[
\frac35=2|P_\ell|
\le\frac{9799}{47000},
\]
whereas
\[
\frac35-\frac{9799}{47000}
=\frac{18401}{47000}>0.
\]
Equivalently, the two-chord lens has coordinate area only
\[
\frac{9799}{94000}
<
\frac3{10}.
\]

For completeness, on \(X+Y=100\) the lens permits
\[
\frac{4689}{4700}\le -Y\le\frac{52}{47},
\]
and
\[
\frac{4689}{4700}<1<\frac{11}{10}<\frac{52}{47}.
\]
Hence the prescribed third full chord itself lies inside the permitted diagonal interval.  The first incompatibility is the required \(P_\ell\)-area, not an endpoint ordering.  The proof makes no assumption at all on the outer boundary inside \(P_j\) or \(P_{k\ell}\); arbitrary points and arbitrary polygonal arcs in those exterior cells cannot repair the contradiction.
