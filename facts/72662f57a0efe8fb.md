## Statement

Assume all hypotheses and notation of accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c`.  Put
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad
p=|P|=p_\varnothing,
\]
\[
E=\overline{Q_{\{j\}}},\qquad
F=\overline{Q_{\{k,\ell\}}},\qquad
H=\operatorname{conv}(E\cup F),
\]
\[
U=\frac{P+E}{2},\qquad
V=\frac{P+F}{2},\qquad
Z=\frac{P+H}{2},
\]
and
\[
D=Z\cap\{-1<f_j<0\},\qquad s_j=|D|.
\]
Then, up to the null boundaries introduced by taking closures,
\[
\boxed{\quad Z=\operatorname{conv}(U\cup V).\quad}
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\mathcal V(P,H)\) denotes planar mixed area, normalized by
\[
|P+tH|=p+2t\mathcal V(P,H)+t^2|H|
\qquad(t\geq0),
\tag{2}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\quad
|Z|=\frac14\bigl(p+|H|+2\mathcal V(P,H)\bigr).
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
Every realization satisfying the hypotheses of accepted fact
`a2a9387275a2c97c` obeys the simultaneous necessary budget
\[
\boxed{\quad
2p\geq
q_{\{j\}}+4q_{\{k,\ell\}}+|Z|+3s_j.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Z|+3s_j
&\geq |U|+|V|+4s_j\\
&\geq
\frac14\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt{q_{\{j\}}}\right)^2
+\frac14\left(\sqrt p+\sqrt{q_{\{k,\ell\}}}\right)^2
+4s_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (4) contains the separate endpoint-plus-slab budget of the
accepted predecessor as a consequence.

The coefficient-minimum whole-body charge in (4) is not, by itself, a
geometric contradiction.  More precisely, for every integer \(n\geq3\)
there are two exact rational common-plane configurations, one with
positive \(Q\)-support
\[
\{3,13,1,12\}
\tag{6a}
\]
and one with positive \(Q\)-support
\[
\{3,13,123,12\},
\tag{6b}
\]
such that
\[
p_\varnothing=1,\qquad
q_3=q_{12}=\frac1{16},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
|U|=\frac{25}{64},\qquad
|V|=\frac{25}{64}+O(n^{-2}),\qquad
0<s_3\leq\frac5{32n},
\tag{8}
\]
\[
|Z|=\frac{35}{32}+O(n^{-1}),
\tag{9}
\]
and, exactly for every \(n\geq3\),
\[
q_3+4q_{12}+|Z|+3s_3<2p_\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]
Both configurations have
\[
A_0=P_\varnothing,\qquad
H=A_2,\qquad
K=Z=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\tag{11}
\]
so they realize the common summand and the simultaneous placement in
one compatible convex triple.  They do not satisfy, and are not claimed
to satisfy, the crossings, tails, or \(W=0\).  Consequently they are not
counterexamples to the locked theorem or to (4); they prove only that
the strict inequality opposite to (4) cannot follow from the common
summand, either minimal \(Q\)-path type, section concavity of \(Z\), and
the endpoint/slab data alone.

## Proof

We first prove the simultaneous identities and budget.  Convexity of
\(P\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
P+\operatorname{conv}(E\cup F)
&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((P+E)\cup(P+F)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, a point on the left has the form
\[
p_0+(1-t)e+tf
=(1-t)(p_0+e)+t(p_0+f),
\]
and a point on the right has the form
\[
(1-t)(p_1+e)+t(p_2+f)
=\bigl((1-t)p_1+tp_2\bigr)+(1-t)e+tf,
\]
where the first summand on the last line belongs to \(P\).
Division by two proves (1).  Equation (3) is (2) at \(t=1\), followed
by the planar area scaling under division by two.

Every point of \(Z\) belongs to \(K\), because
\[
P\subseteq A_0,\qquad H\subseteq A_2,\qquad
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{13}
\]
The exact \(R\)-cell \(R_\varnothing\) is empty.  Hence every point of
\(Z\), outside cutting-line boundaries of area zero, lies in an
\(R_G\) whose coefficient \(3|G|-2\) is at least \(1\).
If \(z\in D\), then
\[
-1<f_j(z)<0,\qquad
f_k(z)+f_\ell(z)=-1-f_j(z)<0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(j\in G(z)\) and at least one of \(k,\ell\) also belongs to
\(G(z)\).  The coefficient on \(D\) is therefore at least \(4\).
Consequently the weighted \(R\)-contribution coming from \(Z\) is at
least
\[
|Z|+3|D|=|Z|+3s_j.
\tag{15}
\]
The two displayed \(Q\)-cells contribute
\[
q_{\{j\}}+4q_{\{k,\ell\}}.
\tag{16}
\]
Under the hypotheses of accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c`, the only
negative term in \(W\) is \(-2p_\varnothing=-2p\), while every
uncounted term is nonnegative.  Since \(W=0\), (15)--(16) prove (4).

The endpoint bodies \(U,V\), and the slab \(D\), are pairwise disjoint
up to cutting-line boundaries, and all three lie in \(Z\).  Hence
\[
|Z|\geq|U|+|V|+s_j.
\tag{17}
\]
Adding \(3s_j\) and applying planar Brunn--Minkowski separately to
\((P,E)\) and \((P,F)\) proves (5).

We now give the two exact calibrating families.  Work in rational
coordinates \((r,w)\), put
\[
\delta=4n+3,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{8n},
\tag{18}
\]
and use \(j=3,k=1,\ell=2\).  For the outer-path family define
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_1&=-r-4n-6,&
q_2&=-r+1+w,&
q_3&=2r-w,\\
L_1&=-\frac{6n+7}{2},&
L_2&=-n,&
L_3&=-\frac12,
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
and put
\[
A_{\mathrm o}=-\frac{9n}{2}-6,\qquad
B_{\mathrm o}=1+\frac n2,\qquad
c_{\mathrm o}=1,\qquad d_{\mathrm o}=7.
\tag{20}
\]
For the central-path family define
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_1&=-r-4n-3,&
q_2&=-r+w,&
q_3&=2r-2-w,\\
L_1&=-(3n+2),&
L_2&=-\frac{2n+1}{2},&
L_3&=-\frac32,
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
and put
\[
A_{\mathrm c}=-\frac{9n}{2}-3,\qquad
B_{\mathrm c}=1+\frac n2,\qquad
c_{\mathrm c}=\frac12,\qquad d_{\mathrm c}=4.
\tag{22}
\]
In either case set
\[
f_i=q_i-L_i.
\tag{23}
\]
The three linear parts are respectively
\[
(-1,0),\qquad(-1,1),\qquad(2,-1),
\]
so they are pairwise nonparallel.  In both cases
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-4n-5=-\delta-2,
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3=-4n-4=-\delta-1,
\tag{24}
\]
and therefore
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{25}
\]

Take
\[
A_0=P=[0,2n]\times\left[0,\frac1{2n}\right].
\tag{26}
\]
In both families the three \(P\)-coordinates \(p_i=f_i-L_i=q_i-2L_i\)
are
\[
p_1=2n+1-r,\qquad
p_2=2n+1-r+w,\qquad
p_3=1+2r-w.
\tag{27}
\]
They are strictly positive throughout \(P\):
\[
p_1\geq1,\qquad
p_2\geq1,\qquad
p_3\geq1-\frac1{2n}>0.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(A_0=P_\varnothing=P\) and
\[
p_\varnothing=|P|=1.
\tag{29}
\]

For \(\tau\in\{\mathrm o,\mathrm c\}\), define
\[
A_{2,\tau}
=
\left\{(r,w):
0\leq w\leq\varepsilon,\quad
A_\tau\leq r\leq B_\tau+c_\tau w
\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
In the outer family, for fixed \(w\), the three zero lines are met in
the order
\[
r=-4n-6,\qquad r=\frac w2,\qquad r=1+w.
\tag{31}
\]
The left and right boundaries in (30) lie respectively \(n/2\) before
the first crossing and \(n/2\) after the last crossing.  Hence the
exact positive-area support is
\[
\{3,13,1,12\}.
\tag{32}
\]
In the central family the crossing order is
\[
r=-4n-3,\qquad r=w,\qquad r=1+\frac w2,
\tag{33}
\]
so the exact positive-area support is
\[
\{3,13,123,12\}.
\tag{34}
\]
All omitted sign cells have zero area, because every horizontal chord
has precisely the displayed strict chamber order.

In either family, \(E=\overline{Q_3}\) is the rectangle of dimensions
\[
\frac n2\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac1{8n},
\]
whereas \(F=\overline{Q_{12}}\) is the parallelogram
\[
F=(\text{translation})
+\left[0,\frac n2\right](1,0)
+\left[0,\frac1{8n}\right](c_\tau,1).
\tag{35}
\]
Therefore
\[
|E|=|F|=\frac1{16}.
\tag{36}
\]
The two sets contain the two left and the two right vertices,
respectively, of the quadrilateral \(A_{2,\tau}\).  Hence
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}(E\cup F)=A_{2,\tau}.
\tag{37}
\]
Set
\[
K=Z=\frac{P+A_{2,\tau}}2.
\tag{38}
\]
This gives midpoint compatibility with equality.

The rectangle \(E\) is a translate of \(\frac14P\), so
\[
|U|
=\frac14\left(\sqrt{|P|}+\sqrt{|E|}\right)^2
=\frac{25}{64}.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(V\), combine the two horizontal generators into one segment of
length \(5n/2\).  The other two generators are
\[
\left(0,\frac1{2n}\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac{c_\tau}{8n},\frac1{8n}\right).
\]
Summing the three pairwise determinant areas of this zonotope gives
\[
|P+F|
=\frac{25}{16}+\frac{c_\tau}{16n^2},
\]
and hence
\[
|V|
=\frac{25}{64}+\frac{c_\tau}{64n^2}.
\tag{40}
\]
Thus both endpoint Brunn--Minkowski bounds are exact in the limit, and
the first is exact for every \(n\).

It remains to compute \(|Z|\).  Direct integration in (30) gives
\[
|A_{2,\tau}|
=\varepsilon(B_\tau-A_\tau)
+\frac{c_\tau\varepsilon^2}{2}.
\tag{41}
\]
Adding the horizontal segment of \(P\) increases the area by
\(2n\varepsilon\).  Adding its vertical segment increases the area by
\[
\frac1{2n}
\left(B_\tau-A_\tau+2n+c_\tau\varepsilon\right).
\tag{42}
\]
Since
\[
B_\tau-A_\tau=5n+d_\tau,
\tag{43}
\]
equations (41)--(42) give
\[
|P+A_{2,\tau}|
=\frac{35}{8}
+\frac{5d_\tau}{8n}
+\frac{9c_\tau}{128n^2}.
\tag{44}
\]
Therefore
\[
|Z|
=\frac{35}{32}
+\frac{5d_\tau}{32n}
+\frac{9c_\tau}{512n^2}.
\tag{45}
\]

The \(w\)-projection of \(Z\) has length
\[
\frac12\left(\frac1{2n}+\frac1{8n}\right)
=\frac5{16n}.
\tag{46}
\]
In the outer family
\[
f_3=2r-w+\frac12,
\]
and in the central family
\[
f_3=2r-w-\frac12.
\]
For each fixed \(w\), the strip \(-1<f_3<0\) cuts an \(r\)-interval
of length \(1/2\).  Consequently
\[
0<s_3\leq\frac5{32n}.
\tag{47}
\]
Positivity also follows directly from the fact that the convex body
\(Z=\operatorname{conv}(U\cup V)\) has one positive-area endpoint
strictly on each side of the strip.

Finally, \(d_\tau\leq7\) and \(c_\tau\leq1\).  Equations
(36), (45), and (47) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_3+4q_{12}+|Z|+3s_3
&\leq
\frac5{16}
+\frac{35}{32}
+\frac{35}{32n}
+\frac9{512n^2}
+\frac{15}{32n}\\
&=
\frac{45}{32}
+\frac{25}{16n}
+\frac9{512n^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
For \(n\geq3\), the last expression is strictly smaller than \(2\):
after multiplication by \(512n^2\), the difference is
\[
304n^2-800n-9>0,
\tag{49}
\]
which is positive at \(n=3\) and strictly increasing thereafter.
This proves (10).

Equations (39)--(40) and (47) also give
\[
|U|+|V|+4s_3
\leq
\frac{25}{32}
+\frac5{8n}
+\frac1{64n^2}
\longrightarrow\frac{25}{32},
\tag{50}
\]
which is exactly the sum of the two separate endpoint
Brunn--Minkowski lower bounds.  Thus neither path type forces a
positive uniform surplus in the three pieces \(U,V,D\).  Equations
(45) and (48) show that charging the entire convex hull \(Z\) at the
minimum \(R\)-coefficient still does not force the strict reverse of
(4).  What remains unmeasured is the weighted allocation of the
intermediate \(Q\)-path cells and of \(Z\setminus D\) among the
doubleton and tripleton \(R\)-cells, together with the crossings and
tails.  The families make no assertion about those data, so they do
not furnish a full canonical realization.  This completes the proof.
