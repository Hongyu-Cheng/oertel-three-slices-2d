## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Write \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and \(t=2T/9\). For every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), define
\[
\mu(H)
=
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
\{z\in A_i:(i,z)\in H\}
\right|.
\]
Suppose proper closed halfspaces
\[
H_r=\{p\in\mathbb R^3:\ell_r(p)\le0\},
\qquad 1\le r\le q\le3,
\]
satisfy
\[
\mu(H_r)<t
\]
and their interiors cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\). Define
\[
B_{ri}=\{z\in A_i:\ell_r(i,z)\le0\},
\qquad b_{ri}=|B_{ri}|,
\]
so
\[
\mu(H_r)=\sum_{i=0}^2b_{ri}.
\]
For \(z_0\in A_0,z_2\in A_2\), define
\[
X_r(z_0)=\ell_r(0,z_0),
\qquad
Y_r(z_2)=\ell_r(2,z_2).
\]
Set
\[
D_r=\{(z_0,z_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
X_r(z_0)+Y_r(z_2)<0\},
\qquad d_r=|D_r|,
\]
and
\[
E_r=
\left|
\left((B_{r0}\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times B_{r2})\right)
\setminus D_r
\right|.
\]
Then
\[
a_0a_2
\le
\sum_{r=1}^q d_r
=
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(
a_2b_{r0}+a_0b_{r2}
-b_{r0}b_{r2}-E_r
\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
Every such cover satisfies
\[
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(b_{r0}b_{r2}+E_r\right)
<
\frac{M(2M-m-a_1)}{3}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, no such cover exists whenever its actual affine geometry forces the reverse weak inequality in (2).

For
\[
F_{r2}(s)
=
\left|
\{z_2\in A_2:Y_r(z_2)<s\}
\right|,
\]
the new statistic has the exact representation
\[
d_r
=
\int_{A_0}
F_{r2}\bigl(-X_r(z_0)\bigr)\,dz_0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(d_r\), equivalently \(E_r\), is an affine-slack convolution statistic rather than a cap-area statistic.

The inclusion-exclusion terms \(b_{r0}b_{r2}\) alone yield no contradiction under the exact scalar hypothesis
\[
a_1+m<2M.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, after relabeling so \(a_0=M\) and \(a_2=m\), the three formal cap vectors
\[
(b_{r0},b_{r1},b_{r2})
=
\left(0,\frac{a_1}{3},\frac m3\right),
\qquad r=1,2,3,
\tag{5}
\]
satisfy the strict mass bounds, the middle-cover area inequality, and (1) with every \(E_r\) omitted.

Moreover, the affine-slack statistic is not determined by total slice areas and cap areas, even among compatible triples and strict low-mass halfspaces. Let
\[
A_1=[-3/10,6/5]\times[0,1],
\qquad
H=\{(i,x,y):x\le0\}.
\]
Consider the two triples
\[
A_0=A_2=[-1/5,4/5]\times[0,1]
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
A'_0=[-2/5,8/5]\times[0,1/2],
\qquad
A'_2=A_2.
\tag{7}
\]
Both triples are compatible, both have slice areas
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)=\left(1,\frac32,1\right),
\]
and \(H\) has cap vector
\[
(b_0,b_1,b_2)
=
\left(\frac15,\frac3{10},\frac15\right)
\]
and mass \(7/10<7/9=2T/9\) in both. Nevertheless, the two affine-slack convolution masses are respectively
\[
d=\frac2{25},
\qquad
d'=\frac9{100},
\]
and the corresponding defects are respectively
\[
E=\frac7{25},
\qquad
E'=\frac{27}{100}.
\]

## Proof

Fix \((z_0,z_2)\in A_0\times A_2\). The assumed inclusion gives
\[
\left(1,\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)
\in
\{1\}\times A_1.
\]
The interior cover supplies an index \(r\) such that
\[
\ell_r\left(1,\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)<0.
\]
Since \(\ell_r\) is affine,
\[
2\ell_r\left(1,\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)
=
X_r(z_0)+Y_r(z_2).
\]
Hence \((z_0,z_2)\in D_r\), proving
\[
A_0\times A_2=\bigcup_{r=1}^qD_r
\]
and therefore
\[
a_0a_2\le\sum_{r=1}^qd_r.
\tag{8}
\]

If \(X_r(z_0)+Y_r(z_2)<0\), then at least one of \(X_r(z_0)\) and \(Y_r(z_2)\) is negative. Consequently,
\[
D_r
\subseteq
(B_{r0}\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times B_{r2}).
\]
The measure of the latter union is
\[
a_2b_{r0}+a_0b_{r2}-b_{r0}b_{r2}.
\]
By the definition of \(E_r\),
\[
d_r
=
a_2b_{r0}+a_0b_{r2}
-b_{r0}b_{r2}-E_r.
\]
Together with (8), this proves (1).

Formula (3) follows directly from Fubini:
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_r
&=
\int_{A_0}
\left|
\{z_2\in A_2:
Y_r(z_2)<-X_r(z_0)\}
\right|\,dz_0\\
&=
\int_{A_0}
F_{r2}\bigl(-X_r(z_0)\bigr)\,dz_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Relabel the outer slices so \(a_0=M\) and \(a_2=m\). From (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Mm
&\le
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(
mb_{r0}+Mb_{r2}
-b_{r0}b_{r2}-E_r
\right)\\
&\le
M\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2})
-
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(b_{r0}b_{r2}+E_r\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The strict mass bounds, \(q\le3\), and the middle-slice cover give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2})
&=
\sum_{r=1}^q\mu(H_r)-\sum_{r=1}^qb_{r1}\\
&<
qt-\sum_{r=1}^qb_{r1}\\
&\le
3t-a_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (9) and (10) yields
\[
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(b_{r0}b_{r2}+E_r\right)
<
M(3t-a_1-m).
\]
Since
\[
3t-a_1-m
=
\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}3-a_1-m
=
\frac{2M-m-a_1}{3},
\]
this proves (2).

For the formal vectors (5),
\[
b_{r0}+b_{r1}+b_{r2}
=
\frac{a_1+m}{3}
<
\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}9=t,
\]
where the strict inequality is exactly (4). Also,
\[
\sum_{r=1}^3b_{r1}=a_1
\]
and
\[
\sum_{r=1}^3
\left(
a_2b_{r0}+a_0b_{r2}-b_{r0}b_{r2}
\right)
=
3M\frac m3
=
Mm.
\]
Thus the cap-area part of (1) remains feasible whenever (4) holds.

It remains to verify the independence example. In (6),
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=A_0\subseteq A_1.
\]
In (7),
\[
\frac{A'_0+A'_2}{2}
=
[-3/10,6/5]\times[0,3/4]
\subseteq A_1.
\]
Thus both triples are compatible. Direct rectangle-area calculations give the common slice areas and cap vector stated above. Their common total area is \(T=7/2\), while
\[
\mu(H)
=
\frac15+\frac3{10}+\frac15
=
\frac7{10}
<
\frac79
=
\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

For (6), put
\[
u=x_0+\frac15,
\qquad
v=x_2+\frac15.
\]
The induced area coordinates are uniform on \([0,1]^2\), and
\[
x_0+x_2<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u+v<\frac25.
\]
Therefore
\[
d
=
\frac12\left(\frac25\right)^2
=
\frac2{25}.
\]

For (7), put
\[
u=\frac{x_0+2/5}{2},
\qquad
v=x_2+\frac15.
\]
Because the height of \(A'_0\) is \(1/2\), its projected area element satisfies
\[
\frac12\,dx_0=du,
\]
so \(u,v\) are again uniform area coordinates on \([0,1]^2\). Now
\[
x_0+x_2<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2u+v<\frac35.
\]
Hence
\[
d'
=
\int_0^{3/10}\left(\frac35-2u\right)\,du
=
\frac9{100}.
\]

In both examples, the cylinder-union measure is
\[
a_2b_0+a_0b_2-b_0b_2
=
\frac15+\frac15-\frac1{25}
=
\frac9{25}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
E=\frac9{25}-\frac2{25}=\frac7{25},
\qquad
E'=\frac9{25}-\frac9{100}=\frac{27}{100}.
\]
Thus the cap data do not determine \(d_r\) or \(E_r\).
