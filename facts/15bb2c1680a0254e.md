## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be compatible nonempty two-dimensional convex-polytopal slices, let
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\]
and define
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac{a_1}{M}.
\]
Suppose the depth conclusion is not already certified by the large-slice criterion in accepted fact \(2f9be517307232c7\), the outer-centroid area criterion in accepted fact \(9fa81a67fd2eaafc\), or the middle-cover criterion in accepted fact \(87035f4e1344144e\). Compatibility, characterized at the area level by accepted fact \(d7bdfc054d5093e8\), and failure of those three criteria give the exact raw necessary system
\[
0<r\le1,\qquad s>0,
\]
\[
s\ge \frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4},
\]
\[
1-r<s<1+r,
\]
\[
s<2-r,
\]
and
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\]
Equivalently, set
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}.
\]
The residual area domain is
\[
0<r<\rho,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),
\]
where
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le \dfrac9{25},\\[4pt]
\dfrac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2},&\dfrac9{25}<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le \dfrac12,\\[4pt]
2-r,&\dfrac12<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
All inequalities defining this simplified strip are strict. The number \(\rho\) is sharp for this area-only synthesis: for every \(0<r<\rho\), some \(s\) lies in the displayed strip and the corresponding positive area triple is compatible, while none of the three stated sufficient area criteria applies. This does not assert that any compatible geometric triple in the strip is a counterexample.

## Proof

Since \(M,m>0\) and \(m\le M\), one has \(0<r\le1\), while \(a_1>0\) gives \(s>0\).

Accepted fact \(d7bdfc054d5093e8\) says that compatibility is equivalent at the area level to
\[
\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2}\le2\sqrt{a_1}.
\]
Dividing by \(\sqrt M\) and squaring the resulting nonnegative quantities gives the weak inequality
\[
1+\sqrt r\le2\sqrt s,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
s\ge\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]

Accepted fact \(2f9be517307232c7\) certifies the conclusion whenever any slice has area at least \(T/2\). Therefore every residual triple must satisfy
\[
a_i<\frac T2\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\]
For the outer slice of area \(M\), this is
\[
2M<M+m+a_1,
\]
or
\[
s>1-r.
\tag{2}
\]
For the middle slice it is
\[
2a_1<M+m+a_1,
\]
or
\[
s<1+r.
\tag{3}
\]
The inequality for the outer slice of area \(m\) is automatic because
\[
m<M+a_1
\]
follows from \(m\le M\) and \(a_1>0\).

Under these strict large-slice failures, all hypotheses of accepted fact \(9fa81a67fd2eaafc\) hold. Its sufficient area condition is
\[
(\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2})^2+4m\ge2T.
\]
Equality is included in that criterion, so a residual triple must satisfy the strict reverse inequality
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m<2(M+m+a_1).
\]
After division by \(M\), this becomes
\[
(1+\sqrt r)^2+4r<2(1+r+s).
\]
Expanding and rearranging yields
\[
1+2\sqrt r+5r<2+2r+2s,
\]
hence
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Accepted fact \(87035f4e1344144e\) certifies the conclusion whenever
\[
2M\le a_1+m.
\]
Again equality is certified, so every residual triple satisfies the strict failure
\[
2M>a_1+m,
\]
equivalently
\[
s<2-r.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (1) through (5) prove the raw system, including every weak or strict endpoint.

It remains to simplify that system. Write
\[
B(r)=1-r,\qquad
C(r)=\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4},\qquad
D(r)=\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\]
Direct subtraction gives
\[
C(r)-B(r)=D(r)-C(r)
=\frac{5r+2\sqrt r-3}{4},
\]
and
\[
D(r)-B(r)=\frac{5r+2\sqrt r-3}{2}.
\]
The function \(5r+2\sqrt r-3\) is strictly increasing for \(r>0\), and it vanishes at
\[
r=\frac9{25}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
D(r)<C(r)<B(r)\quad\text{for }0<r<\frac9{25},
\]
all three quantities agree at \(r=9/25\), and
\[
B(r)<C(r)<D(r)\quad\text{for }r>\frac9{25}.
\]
Thus the three lower conditions \(s>B(r)\), \(s\ge C(r)\), and \(s>D(r)\) are exactly equivalent to
\[
s>L(r),
\]
with the stated piecewise \(L\). In particular, although compatibility initially gives a weak inequality, one of the two strict failure inequalities always dominates it, including at \(r=9/25\).

Similarly,
\[
(1+r)-(2-r)=2r-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\min\{1+r,2-r\}
=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\
2-r,&r>1/2,
\end{cases}
\]
and the two strict upper conditions are exactly \(s<U(r)\).

The raw system also requires
\[
D(r)<2-r.
\]
This is equivalent to
\[
5r+2\sqrt r-5<0.
\]
Putting \(x=\sqrt r>0\), the inequality becomes
\[
5x^2+2x-5<0.
\]
The positive root of the quadratic is
\[
x_0=\frac{\sqrt{26}-1}{5},
\]
while its other root is negative. Hence the inequality holds exactly when
\[
0<x<x_0,
\]
or
\[
0<r<x_0^2
=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}
=\rho.
\]
The inequality is strict because at \(r=\rho\) the strict requirements \(s>D(r)\) and \(s<2-r\) have the same endpoint. Moreover \(1/2<\rho<1\), since \(5r+2\sqrt r-5\) is strictly increasing and is negative at \(r=1/2\) and positive at \(r=1\).

For completeness, the strip is nonempty for every \(0<r<\rho\). If \(0<r\le9/25\), then
\[
L(r)=1-r<1+r=U(r).
\]
If \(9/25<r\le1/2\), then
\[
2\bigl(U(r)-L(r)\bigr)
=3-r-2\sqrt r
=4-(1+\sqrt r)^2>0,
\]
because \(r<1\). If \(1/2<r<\rho\), then \(L(r)<U(r)\) is precisely
\[
5r+2\sqrt r-5<0.
\]
Thus one may choose, for example,
\[
s=\frac{L(r)+U(r)}2.
\]
The established ordering of \(B,C,D\) shows that this \(s\) satisfies the raw compatibility inequality and every strict failure inequality. Taking \(M=1\), \(m=r\), and \(a_1=s\), accepted fact \(d7bdfc054d5093e8\) realizes these positive areas by compatible slices. The strict reverse inequalities show that none of accepted facts \(2f9be517307232c7\), \(9fa81a67fd2eaafc\), or \(87035f4e1344144e\) certifies such an area triple. Hence \(\rho\) is the sharp upper bound obtainable from exactly these area constraints, without any claim of a geometric counterexample.
