## Statement

Assume the complete above-half singleton-\(I_1\) transformed triangular system and the common-column support conventions of accepted fact `b8734f5137d2af29`. Thus the middle cap fractions satisfy
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge1,
\]
every \(I_0\)-row satisfies
\[
c_i<\kappa<\frac49,
\]
and the unique \(I_1\)-row satisfies
\[
\frac49\le c_h<\frac12.
\]
For all-nine-nonzero matrices, retain the common vertex labeling
\[
J_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}>0\}
\]
and the orbitwise row stabilizer of `b8734f5137d2af29`. For zero-entry matrices, retain its weak-sign data
\[
P_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}>0\},\qquad
N_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}<0\},\qquad
Z_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}=0\}.
\]
Then the following exact exclusions hold.

1. In \(\mathcal O_{11}\), where \((I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2\})\), there is no all-nine-nonzero system with
\[
(J_1,J_2,J_3)=(\{2,3\},\{1\},\varnothing).
\]
There is also no permitted zero-entry boundary with \(P_3=\varnothing\).

2. In \(\mathcal O_{21}\), where \((I_0,I_1)=(\{1,2\},\{3\})\), there is no all-nine-nonzero system with
\[
(J_1,J_2,J_3)=(\varnothing,\{2,3\},\{1\}).
\]
There is also no permitted zero-entry boundary with \(P_1=\varnothing\) or \(P_2=\varnothing\).

These conclusions use only the displayed common labeling and the allowed row swap \(1\leftrightarrow2\) in \(\mathcal O_{21}\). They make no assertion about any other support representative or weak-sign boundary.

## Proof

Accepted fact `b8734f5137d2af29` gives the exact middle cover inequality
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]
It also gives \(c_i<\kappa<4/9\) for each \(i\in I_0\), and \(c_h<1/2\) for every \(h\in I_1\). Finally, it gives the exact cap value \(c_i=0\) whenever a non-\(I_1\) row has empty positive support and at least one negative entry. In the weak-sign catalog every row has \(N_i\ne\varnothing\), so this zero-cap conclusion applies to every boundary considered below, whether or not that particular row itself contains a zero.

Consider first \(\mathcal O_{11}\). Here row 1 is the \(I_0\)-row and row 2 is the unique \(I_1\)-row, so
\[
c_1<\frac49,\qquad c_2<\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
In the all-nine-nonzero representative
\[
(J_1,J_2,J_3)=(\{2,3\},\{1\},\varnothing),
\]
row 3 is all negative. The all-negative-row formula in `b8734f5137d2af29` gives \(c_3=0\). Equations (1)-(2) would then give
\[
1\le c_1+c_2+c_3<\frac49+\frac12=\frac{17}{18}<1,
\]
a contradiction. The column labels have not been changed. If instead an actual permitted weak-sign boundary has \(P_3=\varnothing\), then \(N_3\ne\varnothing\) and the same accepted zero-cap formula again gives \(c_3=0\). The identical contradiction excludes every such boundary.

Now consider \(\mathcal O_{21}\). Rows 1 and 2 are the \(I_0\)-rows, and row 3 is the unique \(I_1\)-row. Hence
\[
c_1<\frac49,\qquad c_2<\frac49,\qquad c_3<\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]
For the listed all-nine-nonzero representative
\[
(J_1,J_2,J_3)=(\varnothing,\{2,3\},\{1\}),
\]
row 1 is all negative, so \(c_1=0\). Equations (1) and (3) give
\[
1\le c_1+c_2+c_3<\frac49+\frac12=\frac{17}{18}<1,
\]
again a contradiction. The allowed \(\mathcal O_{21}\) row stabilizer interchanges rows 1 and 2, so this is exactly the representative orbit with an empty positive support on either \(I_0\)-row. More directly, on a permitted weak-sign boundary, \(P_1=\varnothing\) gives \(c_1=0\), while \(P_2=\varnothing\) gives \(c_2=0\). In either case, the remaining \(I_0\)-cap is less than \(4/9\), the \(I_1\)-cap is less than \(1/2\), and the same \(17/18\) contradiction to (1) follows.

The proof used no property of the other nonempty-support representatives. Therefore it establishes exactly the stated support and boundary exclusions and nothing beyond them.
