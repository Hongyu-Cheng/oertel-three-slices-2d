## Statement

Work in affine coordinates
\[
 r_1+r_2+r_3=1,\qquad \tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3=1,
\]
related by
\[
 r_i=2\lambda_i-s\tau_i,\qquad
 s>0,\qquad
 \lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=\frac{1+s}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
All areas use the common \(r\)-coordinate normalization; hence the
area of a set specified in \(\tau\)-coordinates is multiplied by
\(s^2\).
The middle cutting line in row \(j\) is \(r_j=\lambda_j\).
Let \(P\) and \(C_\tau\) be planar compact convex sets in the
\(r\)- and \(\tau\)-planes, respectively, and define
\[
 C_r=2\lambda-sC_\tau,\qquad
 H=\frac{P+C_r}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose \(q^-,q^+\in C_\tau\) satisfy
\[
 \tau_j(q^-)=b>0,\qquad \tau_j(q^+)=-\delta<0.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(0\le\eta<sb\), put
\[
 P_\eta=P\cap\{r_j\le\eta\}.
\]
Assume explicitly that \(P_\eta\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
 t_\eta=\frac{b-\eta/s}{b+\delta},
\tag{4}
\]
and, for \(\zeta=q^+-q^-\), define the determinant width
\[
 D_\zeta(P_\eta)=
 \max_{x\in P_\eta}\det(\zeta,x)
 -\min_{x\in P_\eta}\det(\zeta,x).
\tag{5}
\]
Then the following rowwise extrusion inequality holds:
\[
 \boxed{
 \left|H\cap\{r_j\le\lambda_j\}\right|
 \ge
 \frac14\left(
 |P_\eta|+s\,t_\eta D_\zeta(P_\eta)
 \right).
 }
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently, suppose \(H\subseteq K\), write
\[
 k=|K|,\qquad
 c_j=|K\cap\{r_j\le\lambda_j\}|,
\tag{7}
\]
and assume one crossing and the no-half inequality
\[
 u_j+v_j+c_j=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\qquad k<M+m.
\tag{8}
\]
If the right side of (6) is at least
\[
 \frac{4(M+m)}9-u_j-v_j,
\tag{9}
\]
then no such \(K\) exists.

Here is a nontrivial two-parameter application.  Set
\[
 s=\frac{21}{20},\qquad
 \lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac{41}{120},
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\alpha=\frac1{10^6},\quad
a=\frac{99999}{100000},\quad
c=\frac{499}{500},\quad
d=\frac59,\quad
e=\frac{124991}{225000}=d-\frac1{25000}.
\tag{11}
\]
Let
\[
\begin{split}
A_0=\{\,r_1+r_2+r_3=1:\;&r_i\ge-\alpha\ (i=1,2,3),\\
&r_1/e+r_2/a\ge1,\\
&(1-c)r_1+(d-c)r_2\le d(1-c)\,\},
\end{split}
\tag{12}
\]
and put
\[
 P=A_0\cap\{r_1,r_2,r_3\ge0\}.
\tag{13}
\]
For arbitrary rational
\[
 0<t_1\le1,\qquad 0<t_2\le1,
\tag{14}
\]
let \(A_2(t_1,t_2)\) be the \(\tau\)-quadrilateral with cyclic
vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_1&=(1+\rho+\omega,-\rho,-\omega),\\
y_2&=(-\chi,1+\chi+\omega,-\omega),\\
z_2&=e_2+t_2(e_2-y_2),\\
z_1&=e_1+t_1(e_1-y_1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
 \rho=\frac1{100},\qquad
 \chi=\frac1{25},\qquad
 \omega=\frac1{10000}.
\tag{16}
\]
Let \(Q_J\) be its sign cells, with \(i\in J\) meaning
\(\tau_i\ge0\), and define
\[
 C_\tau=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2}),
\qquad C_r=2\lambda-sC_\tau.
\tag{17}
\]

There is no compact convex \(K\) satisfying
\[
 H:=\frac{P+C_r}{2}\subseteq K,
\tag{18}
\]
\[
 u_2+v_2+c_2=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,\qquad k<M+m,
\tag{19}
\]
where
\[
 M=|A_0|,\quad m=|A_2(t_1,t_2)|,\quad
 u_2=|A_0\cap\{r_2\le0\}|,
\]
\[
 v_2=|A_2(t_1,t_2)\cap\{\tau_2\ge0\}|,\quad
 c_2=|K\cap\{r_2\le\lambda_2\}|.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus every member of the rational two-parameter family (14) is
excluded by the rowwise inequality.

## Proof

We first prove (6).  Parametrize
\[
 q(t)=q^-+t(q^+-q^-),\qquad 0\le t\le t_\eta.
\]
By (3)-(4),
\[
 \tau_j(q(t))
 =b-t(b+\delta)\ge\frac{\eta}{s}.
\tag{21}
\]
Hence, for every \(p\in P_\eta\),
\[
 p_j\le\eta\le s\tau_j(q(t)).
\tag{22}
\]
The midpoint of \(p\) and \(2\lambda-sq(t)\) has \(j\)-th
\(r\)-coordinate at most \(\lambda_j\).  Therefore
\[
 \frac12\left(
 P_\eta+2\lambda-s[q^-,q(t_\eta)]
 \right)
 \subseteq H\cap\{r_j\le\lambda_j\}.
\tag{23}
\]

For any nonempty planar compact convex \(E\) and vector \(w\),
\[
 |E+[0,w]|
 =|E|+
 \max_{x\in E}\det(w,x)
 -\min_{x\in E}\det(w,x).
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed, after an invertible linear change sending \(w\) to a vertical
vector, every nonempty vertical section is lengthened by the length
of that vector; integration over the horizontal projection proves
(24), with the determinant range supplying exactly the Jacobian
factor.  Apply (24) to
\[
 E=P_\eta,\qquad
 w=-s\,t_\eta(q^+-q^-).
\]
Translation does not change area, and division by \(2\) multiplies
area by \(1/4\).  Equation (23) now gives (6).

If (8) holds, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_j
&=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9-u_j-v_j\\
&<\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_j-v_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
But \(H\subseteq K\) and (6) give the reverse weak inequality whenever
(9) holds.  This proves the general exclusion criterion.

We now audit the two-parameter family.  The points \(e_1,e_2\) lie,
respectively, on \([y_1,z_1]\) and \([y_2,z_2]\).  Since
\[
t_1(\rho+\omega)<1,\qquad
t_2(\chi+\omega)<1,
\]
both \(z_1,z_2\) have three positive coordinates.  The quadrilateral
therefore has exactly the positive sign cells
\[
 Q_1,\quad Q_2,\quad Q_{12},\quad Q_{123}.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, the two side edges pass through \(e_1,e_2\), respectively.
Below \(\tau_3=0\), the \(Q_1,Q_2\) corner triangles have
\(\tau\)-areas \(\rho\omega/2,\chi\omega/2\), while the intervening
trapezoid has area \(\omega+\omega^2/2\).  Above that line,
\(Q_{123}\) is the quadrilateral
\(\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,z_2,z_1\}\).  Shoelace gives it
\(\tau\)-area
\[
\frac{\omega}{2}\left(
t_1+t_2-t_1t_2(\rho+\chi+\omega)
\right).
\]
Thus, including the \(s^2\) factor, the cell areas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_1&=\frac{441}{800000000},&
q_2&=\frac{441}{200000000},\\
q_{12}&=\frac{8820441}{80000000000},&
q_{123}&=\frac{441}{8000000}
\left(t_1+t_2-\frac{501}{10000}t_1t_2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The last quantity is positive for (14): here \(501/10000<1\) and
\(t_1t_2\le\min\{t_1,t_2\}<t_1+t_2\).  Consequently
\[
m=q_1+q_2+q_{12}+q_{123},\qquad
v_2=m-q_1,
\tag{28}
\]
and
\[
\sigma_0:=q_1+q_2+q_{12}
=\frac{9040941}{80000000000}
\le m.
\tag{29}
\]

The halfplane intersections in (12) give
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{
(e,0),(d,0),(c,1-c),(0,1),(0,a)\},
\tag{30}
\]
\[
M=
\frac{199205788224789533079666379817}
{898126208756748000000000000000},
\qquad
u_2=\frac{899991}{249497466250000000}.
\tag{31}
\]
For an exact clipping certificate, the cyclic vertices of \(A_0\)
are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(
\frac{24947954742919}{44909543925000},
-\frac{899991}{4989949325000}
\right),\\
&\left(
\frac{1996001991}{2000000000},
\frac{4000009}{2000000000}
\right),\\
&\left(-\frac1{1000000},\frac{500001}{500000}\right),\\
&\left(
-\frac1{1000000},
\frac{499959900351}{499964000000}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Its \(r_2\le0\) cap is the triangle with vertices
\[
(e,0),\quad(d,0),\quad
\left(
\frac{24947954742919}{44909543925000},
-\frac{899991}{4989949325000}
\right),
\]
which gives the displayed \(u_2\).  Shoelace on (32) gives \(M\).

Use (6) in row \(j=2\) with
\[
q^-=e_2,\qquad q^+=y_1,\qquad
b=1,\qquad \delta=\rho,\qquad
\eta=\frac1{20}.
\tag{33}
\]
The segment \([e_2,y_1]\) lies in \(C_\tau\), because
\(e_2\in\overline{Q_2}\) and \(y_1\in Q_1\).  Put
\[
P_\eta=P\cap\{r_2\le\eta\},\qquad
\zeta=y_1-e_2=(1+\rho+\omega,-1-\rho).
\]
Since
\[
1-c<\eta<a,
\]
the point \((e,0)\) belongs to \(P_\eta\).  Thus the nonemptiness
hypothesis preceding (4) holds for every member of the family.  The
left and right section endpoints of \(P_\eta\) are
\[
x_L(y)=e\left(1-\frac ya\right),
\tag{34}
\]
\[
x_R(y)=
\begin{cases}
d+\dfrac{c-d}{1-c}y,&0\le y\le1-c,\\[2mm]
1-y,&1-c\le y\le\eta.
\end{cases}
\tag{35}
\]
Integration gives
\[
|P_\eta|
=\frac{2387696279}{112498875000}.
\tag{36}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\det(\zeta,(x,y))
=(1+\rho+\omega)y+(1+\rho)x.
\]
On the polygon \(P_\eta\), its minimum is attained at \((e,0)\) and
its maximum at \((1-\eta,\eta)\).  Hence
\[
D_\zeta(P_\eta)
=(1+\rho)(1-e)+\omega\eta
=\frac{20202043}{45000000}.
\tag{37}
\]
Finally,
\[
t_\eta=\frac{1-\eta/s}{1+\rho}=\frac{2000}{2121}.
\tag{38}
\]
Substitution in (6) gives the exact row-2 lower bound
\[
\left|H\cap\{r_2\le\lambda_2\}\right|
\ge
\frac{10583235138143}{90899091000000}
=\frac{11}{100}
+\frac{584335128143}{90899091000000}
>\frac{11}{100}.
\tag{39}
\]

It remains to compare one crossing.  Equations (28)-(29) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_2-v_2
&=\frac{4M}{9}-u_2+q_1-\frac{5m}{9}\\
&\le
\frac{4M}{9}-u_2+q_1-\frac{5\sigma_0}{9}\\
&=\frac1{10}
-\frac{2998368327853674323139107683}
{2020783969702683000000000000000}
<\frac1{10}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
The containment in (18) gives \(H\subseteq K\), so (39) yields
\[
c_2>\frac{11}{100}.
\]
The crossing and \(k<M+m\), through (25) and (40), instead give
\[
c_2<\frac1{10}.
\]
This contradiction is uniform over every rational pair
\((t_1,t_2)\) in (14).  No aggregate mixed-area estimate is used.
