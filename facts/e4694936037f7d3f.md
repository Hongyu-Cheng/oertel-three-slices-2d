## Statement

Assume the actual dominant all-double common-plane hypotheses, exact-cell convention, and rank-two normalization of accepted facts \(174b54e9843ef8fc\), \(29dfdfc94477e532\), \(95ac9a8137777d40\), and \(bd8c4d0245cddf92\).  Thus
\[
B_0=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,\qquad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3\}=\mathbb R^2,
\]
and, for every \(\ell\),
\[
h_{B_0}(f_\ell)>0.
\tag{1}
\]
For cyclic \((i,j,k)\), let
\[
O_i=\{f_j=f_k=0\},\qquad
\Psi_i(r)=h_{B_0}(f_k+r f_j),\qquad
I_i=\{r>0:\Psi_i(r)\leq0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
\mathcal R_i^\circ=\{f_i<0,\ f_j>0,\ f_k>0\},\qquad
\beta_i=|B_0\cap\mathcal R_i^\circ|.
\tag{3}
\]

Then \(\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R}I_i=\varnothing\) if and only if exactly one of the following alternatives holds.

1. Empty interval:
\[
I_i=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_0\cap\{f_j>0,f_k>0\}\neq\varnothing.
\tag{4}
\]
In this case \(\beta_i>0\), and
\[
h_{A_0}(f_k+r f_j)+h_{A_2}(f_k+r f_j)>0
\qquad(r>0).
\tag{5}
\]

2. Singleton tangent:
\[
I_i=\{\tau_i\}\qquad\text{for a unique }\tau_i>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
B_0\subseteq\{f_k+\tau_i f_j\leq0\},
\tag{7}
\]
and the exposed face
\[
\mathcal F_i
=B_0\cap\{f_k+\tau_i f_j=0\}
\tag{8}
\]
is a nondegenerate segment containing \(O_i\) in its relative interior.  Its two sides contain points \(F_i,E_i\) satisfying
\[
f_j(F_i)<0<f_k(F_i),\qquad
f_k(E_i)<0<f_j(E_i).
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, \(O_i\in B_0\), \(\beta_i=0\), and, with
\[
F_X(g)=\{x\in X:g(x)=h_X(g)\},
\]
the tangent has the exact endpoint form
\[
h_{A_0}(f_k+\tau_i f_j)+h_{A_2}(f_k+\tau_i f_j)=0,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\mathcal F_i
=\frac{F_{A_0}(f_k+\tau_i f_j)
+F_{A_2}(f_k+\tau_i f_j)}2,
\qquad
2O_i\in
F_{A_0}(f_k+\tau_i f_j)
+F_{A_2}(f_k+\tau_i f_j).
\tag{11}
\]

The empty case has an exact crossing-capacity obstruction.  Write
\[
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and let \(r_J\) be the area of the exact middle sign cell whose negative-index set is \(J\).  The three crossings and the all-double identity \(v_i=m-x_i\) give
\[
r_i
=h-u_i-m+x_i-r_{ij}-r_{ik}-r_{123}.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, (4) implies
\[
0<\beta_i
\leq r_i
=h-u_i-m+x_i-r_{ij}-r_{ik}-r_{123}
\leq h-u_i-m+x_i.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus either strict reverse inequality in (13) rules out the empty failure; in particular, \(r_i=0\) leaves only the singleton-tangent alternative.

There is also a simultaneous-collapse obstruction.  Let
\[
\mathcal T=\{f_1\leq0,\ f_2\leq0,\ f_3\leq0\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{O_1,O_2,O_3\}.
\tag{14}
\]
If
\[
\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R}I_i=\varnothing
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{15}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal T\subseteq B_0.}
\tag{16}
\]

Retain the dominant bodies \(P\subseteq A_0\), \(C\subseteq A_2\), and
\[
p=|P|,\qquad d=|C|,\qquad
S_1=\frac{P+C}{2}\subseteq B_0,
\]
from accepted fact \(29dfdfc94477e532\).  Thus
\[
\min_{S_1}f_1\geq\frac{\eta_1}{2},\qquad
\lambda_1=\frac{r_{123}\eta_1}{2},
\tag{17}
\]
and, writing planar mixed area as
\[
|X+tY|=|X|+2t\,\mathcal V(X,Y)+t^2|Y|,
\]
\[
|S_1|=\frac{p+d+2\mathcal V(P,C)}4,\qquad
\sqrt{pd}\leq\mathcal V(P,C)\leq\mathscr B,
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
\mathscr B
=\frac{13p}{6}
-\frac{14}{3}(m+q+E_R)
-2x_1-2(u_2+u_3)
-\frac d2-2\lambda_1.
\tag{19}
\]
Under (15),
\[
\boxed{
r_{123}+\lambda_1+|S_1|
\leq |B_0|
=\frac{M+m+2\mathcal V(A_0,A_2)}4.
}
\tag{20}
\]
Equivalently, total corner collapse adds the upper endpoint
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V(P,C)
\leq
2\bigl(|B_0|-r_{123}-\lambda_1\bigr)
-\frac{p+d}{2}.
}
\tag{21}
\]
Hence the accepted mixed-area window sharpens under total collapse to
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{pd}\leq\mathcal V(P,C)
\leq
\min\left\{
\mathscr B,\,
2\bigl(|B_0|-r_{123}-\lambda_1\bigr)-\frac{p+d}{2}
\right\}.
}
\tag{22}
\]
In particular, the strict, endpoint-computable region
\[
\boxed{
|B_0|
<
r_{123}+\lambda_1
+\frac{(\sqrt p+\sqrt d)^2}{4}
}
\tag{23}
\]
rules out (15), so at least one corner is admissible.  In full-endpoint mixed-area form, (23) is
\[
M+m+2\mathcal V(A_0,A_2)
<
4r_{123}+4\lambda_1+(\sqrt p+\sqrt d)^2.
\tag{24}
\]
The no-half constraint \(k<T/2\), with \(T=M+m+k\), also gives the necessary chain
\[
r_{123}+\lambda_1
+\frac{p+d+2\mathcal V(P,C)}4
\leq |B_0|\leq k<\frac T2
\tag{25}
\]
under total collapse.

Finally, in the strict region (23), compute the admissible-corner parameters \(p_i<q_i\), \(a_i,c_i\), \(J\), \(\delta\), and \(e_\ell\) as in accepted fact \(bd8c4d0245cddf92\).  If every corner for which \(p_i<q_i\) satisfies
\[
e_j<\frac{p_i a_i^2}{2J},\qquad
e_k<\frac{q_i c_i^2}{2J},\qquad
\delta>e_j+e_k+\frac{4J\,e_je_k}{q_i a_i c_i},
\tag{26}
\]
then no actual realization exists, by the corner exclusion of accepted fact \(b4a2962b4691ff67\).

If equality holds in (23) while all three corners fail, then
\[
\mathcal V(P,C)=\sqrt{pd},
\tag{27}
\]
so \(P\) and \(C\) are positively homothetic, and the three area pieces used to prove (20) fill \(B_0\) up to area zero.  Thus after the strict region (23), the next invariant is the full-midpoint packing defect
\[
|B_0|-r_{123}-\lambda_1-|S_1|,
\tag{28}
\]
together with the three tangent support normals in (10), rather than any positive-reach datum.

## Proof

Fix a cyclic \((i,j,k)\) and use the coordinates
\[
s=f_j,\qquad t=f_k.
\tag{29}
\]
Their Jacobian is nonzero by rank two.  Accepted fact \(95ac9a8137777d40\) gives \(h_{B_0}(s)>0\) and \(h_{B_0}(t)>0\), and accepted fact \(bd8c4d0245cddf92\) says that the only remaining admissibility condition is that \(I_i\) have nonempty interior.

Suppose first that \(B_0\) contains a point with \(s>0,t>0\).  At that point \(t+rs>0\) for every \(r>0\), so \(\Psi_i(r)>0\) for every \(r>0\), and \(I_i=\varnothing\).

Conversely, suppose that \(B_0\) misses the open positive quadrant.  A point of \(B_0\) with \(s>0\) must then have \(t<0\).  Indeed, equality \(t=0\), together with any point having \(t>0\), would by convexity produce points with both coordinates positive.  The symmetric assertion holds for points with \(t>0\).  Define
\[
L=\sup_{\substack{x\in B_0\\s(x)<0<t(x)}}
\frac{t(x)}{-s(x)},
\qquad
U=\inf_{\substack{x\in B_0\\t(x)<0<s(x)}}
\frac{-t(x)}{s(x)}.
\tag{30}
\]
The two indexing sets are nonempty by (1), and \(0<L,U<\infty\).  For
\[
x=(-a,b),\qquad y=(c,-d),\qquad a,b,c,d>0,
\]
convexity and avoidance of the positive quadrant force
\[
\frac ba\leq\frac dc.
\tag{31}
\]
Otherwise the segment \([x,y]\) has a nonempty portion in the open positive quadrant.  Taking the supremum and infimum gives \(L\leq U\).  Every \(r\in[L,U]\) satisfies \(t+rs\leq0\) throughout \(B_0\), by considering separately \(s>0\), \(s<0\), and \(s=0\).  Hence \(I_i\neq\varnothing\).  This proves (4).  Since \(B_0\) is two-dimensional and the quadrant is open, a nonempty intersection in (4) has positive area: move any intersection point slightly toward an interior point of \(B_0\).  This proves \(\beta_i>0\).  The support identity of accepted fact \(bd8c4d0245cddf92\) then gives (5).

Now assume \(I_i\) has empty interior but is nonempty.  Convexity and continuity of \(\Psi_i\), together with (1), imply that \(I_i=\{\tau_i\}\) for a unique \(0<\tau_i<\infty\), and \(\Psi_i(\tau_i)=0\).  Take \(r_n\uparrow\tau_i\) with \(r_n<\tau_i\).  Since \(\Psi_i(r_n)>0\), a fixed vertex \(F_i\) of \(B_0\) maximizes \(t+r_ns\) along a subsequence.  Passing to the limit gives
\[
t(F_i)+\tau_i s(F_i)=0,
\]
and
\[
0<t(F_i)+r_ns(F_i)
=(r_n-\tau_i)s(F_i)
\]
forces \(s(F_i)<0<t(F_i)\).  A sequence decreasing to \(\tau_i\) from above gives an active vertex \(E_i\) with \(t(E_i)<0<s(E_i)\).  Both lie in the exposed face (8).  Their segment contains \((0,0)=O_i\) in its relative interior, proving (7)-(9).

Conversely, (7)-(9) imply \(\Psi_i(\tau_i)=0\).  Writing
\[
F_i=(-a,\tau_i a),\qquad
E_i=(c,-\tau_i c),\qquad a,c>0,
\]
gives
\[
(t+rs)(F_i)=a(\tau_i-r)>0\quad(r<\tau_i),
\]
\[
(t+rs)(E_i)=c(r-\tau_i)>0\quad(r>\tau_i).
\]
Thus \(I_i=\{\tau_i\}\).  The exposed-face identity for a Minkowski average proves (10)-(11).  The open positive quadrant is disjoint from (7), so \(\beta_i=0\).  This completes the residual dichotomy.

For the crossing consequence, boundaries of affine sign cells have area zero.  Therefore
\[
c_i:=|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|
=r_i+r_{ij}+r_{ik}+r_{123}.
\tag{32}
\]
The \(i\)-th crossing in the all-double branch is
\[
u_i+c_i+(m-x_i)=h.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (32)-(33) give (12).  In the empty case,
\[
B_0\cap\mathcal R_i^\circ\subseteq K\cap\mathcal R_i^\circ,
\]
so \(\beta_i\leq r_i\), proving (13).  This is the exact point at which the crossing data constrain the empty failure.  The no-half inequalities of accepted fact \(174b54e9843ef8fc\) do not by themselves remove a tangent edge, which has zero sign-cell area.

It remains to prove (16).  Define affine barycentric coordinates
\[
\lambda_\ell(x)=-f_\ell(x),\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.
\tag{34}
\]
They identify the plane affinely with the plane of triples summing to one, and \(\lambda(O_i)\) is the \(i\)-th standard basis vector.  For every failed corner choose \(X_i\in B_0\) as follows.  In the empty case choose
\[
X_i\in B_0\cap\mathcal R_i^\circ;
\]
in the singleton case choose \(X_i=O_i\), which belongs to \(B_0\) by (8)-(9).  Let \(A\) be the matrix whose \(i\)-th column is \(\lambda(X_i)\).  Every column sums to one, every off-diagonal entry is nonpositive, and
\[
A_{ii}=1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}|A_{\ell i}|.
\tag{35}
\]
Indeed, an empty-corner choice has \(f_j,f_k>0\) and
\(-f_i=1+f_j+f_k>1\), while a singleton-corner choice gives exactly the \(i\)-th basis vector.

The transpose \(A^\mathsf T\) has row sum one, positive diagonal, nonpositive off-diagonal entries, and strict diagonal dominance.  It is invertible and its inverse is entrywise nonnegative.  To see both assertions directly, let \(A^\mathsf Ty=b\) with \(b\geq0\).  If \(y_p<0\) is a least component, then the signs of the off-diagonal entries and the row-sum identity give
\[
b_p
\leq y_p\sum_q(A^\mathsf T)_{pq}
=y_p<0,
\]
a contradiction.  Applying the same argument to a vector in the kernel and to its negative proves invertibility.  Hence \(A^{-1}\) is also entrywise nonnegative.  Since \(\mathbf1^\mathsf TA=\mathbf1^\mathsf T\),
\[
\mathbf1^\mathsf TA^{-1}=\mathbf1^\mathsf T.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus every standard basis vector is a convex combination of the columns of \(A\).  By injectivity of the affine coordinates (34), every \(O_i\) lies in
\(\operatorname{conv}\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}\).  All \(X_i\) lie in the convex body \(B_0\), so
\[
\mathcal T
=\operatorname{conv}\{O_1,O_2,O_3\}
\subseteq\operatorname{conv}\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}
\subseteq B_0.
\]
This proves (16).

By accepted fact \(b4a2962b4691ff67\),
\[
|\mathcal T|=\frac1{2J}.
\]
Under the exact-cell convention and \(\mathcal T\subseteq K\), this is exactly \(r_{123}\).  Choose \(y\in S_1\) minimizing \(f_1\), and write
\[
a=f_1(y)\geq\frac{\eta_1}{2}.
\tag{37}
\]
Under total collapse, both the edge \([O_2,O_3]\subseteq\mathcal T\) and \(y\) lie in \(B_0\).  Hence
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{O_2,O_3,y\}\subseteq B_0.
\]
The triangle \(\Delta\) has area
\[
|\Delta|=\frac{a}{2J}=a\,r_{123}\geq\lambda_1.
\tag{38}
\]
Its portion with \(0<f_1<a\) is area-disjoint from \(S_1\), because \(a=\min_{S_1}f_1\), and is area-disjoint from \(\mathcal T\), whose interior has \(f_1<0\).  Since \(\mathcal T,S_1\subseteq B_0\), summing these three area-disjoint pieces gives
\[
|B_0|\geq r_{123}+\lambda_1+|S_1|,
\]
which is (20).  Substituting the exact area formula in (18) and solving for \(\mathcal V(P,C)\) proves (21)-(22).  The lower endpoint \(\mathcal V(P,C)\geq\sqrt{pd}\), also from accepted fact \(29dfdfc94477e532\), gives (23)-(24).  Inclusion \(B_0\subseteq K\) and the no-half inequality \(k<T/2\) from accepted fact \(174b54e9843ef8fc\) give (25).

In region (23), (15) is impossible, so some corner has \(p_i<q_i\).  At any such corner, accepted facts \(bd8c4d0245cddf92\) and \(b4a2962b4691ff67\) turn (26) into the contradictory corner inequality, proving the final exclusion assertion.

Finally, equality in (23) forces equality in every step
\[
|B_0|
\geq r_{123}+\lambda_1+|S_1|
\geq r_{123}+\lambda_1+\frac{(\sqrt p+\sqrt d)^2}{4}.
\]
Thus (27) holds, \(a=\eta_1/2\), and the area-disjoint pieces
\(\mathcal T\), \(S_1\), and the open part of \(\Delta\) exhaust \(B_0\) up to area zero.  The equality case recorded in accepted fact \(29dfdfc94477e532\) makes \(P\) and \(C\) positively homothetic.  This proves the boundary statement and identifies (28) and the tangent normals as the remaining invariants.
