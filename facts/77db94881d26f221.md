## Statement

Consider any surviving transformed triangular N=3 system in the canonical orientation of accepted facts 63975845ca0e2926 and 6600e139055be506. Thus, after normalizing the common planar area measure, there are nondegenerate triangles
\[
P_0\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1+q\},\qquad
P_1\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1\},\qquad
P_2\subset\{\mathbf 1^\top z=1-q\}
\]
with \(q>1\), areas
\[
|P_0|=A,\qquad |P_1|=1,\qquad |P_2|=B,
\]
and
\[
0<A<B,\qquad B<1+A,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9>\frac12,\qquad
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
For each coordinate \(k\), put
\[
u_k=\frac{|P_0\cap\{z_k\geq0\}|}{A},\qquad
c_k=|P_1\cap\{z_k\geq0\}|,\qquad
v_k=\frac{|P_2\cap\{z_k\geq0\}|}{B},
\]
and assume the literal strict inequalities
\[
Au_k+c_k+Bv_k<t\qquad(k=1,2,3).
\]
Let the columns of \(L\) be the vertices of \(P_1\), and let \(I_1\) be the set of coordinates whose value at the centroid of \(P_1\) is nonnegative. Then no \(i\in I_1\) can have, up to the common vertex labeling, a row
\[
L_{i*}=(-d,x,y),\qquad d,x,y>0,\qquad x+y\geq d.
\]
Equivalently, no compatible endpoint matrices and independent shifts make all three literal row masses strictly below \(t\) in any of the orbits
\[
(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2\}),\quad
(\{1\},\{2,3\}),\quad
(\{1,2\},\{3\})
\]
if an \(I_1\)-row has exactly two positive vertex values. No sign, support, nonzero-entry, or strict-mean hypothesis is imposed on either of the other rows.

## Proof

Accepted fact 63975845ca0e2926 gives the endpoint orientation, the three transformed triangles, their common area normalization, compatibility, and preservation of every literal row mass under the transformation. Accepted fact 6600e139055be506 gives the three above-half incidence orbits, \(A<B\), the exact row cap formulas, and
\[
\beta=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9<\frac{4A}{9}.
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to \(B<1+A\), so all numerical assumptions in the statement are consequences of the two cited accepted facts.

Fix a putative two-positive \(I_1\)-row \(i\). Its exact middle cap fraction is
\[
c:=c_i=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)}.
\]
Since \(x+y\geq d\) and \(xy>0\),
\[
(d+x)(d+y)=d^2+d(x+y)+xy>2d^2,
\]
and hence \(c>1/2\). Put
\[
X:=Au_i,\qquad Y:=Bv_i,\qquad
\delta:=t-c,\qquad
\delta_0:=t-\frac12=\frac{4(A+B)-5}{18}.
\]
The strict row inequality gives
\[
X+Y<\delta,
\]
so in particular \(0<\delta<\delta_0\). Moreover \(\delta_0<A\). Indeed,
\[
18(A-\delta_0)=14A-4B+5
>14A-4(1+A)+5=10A+1>0.
\]
Thus
\[
0\leq X,Y,\qquad X+Y<\delta<\delta_0<A<B.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(j=0,1,2\), let
\[
K_j:=P_j\cap\{z_i\leq0\}.
\]
The row of \(P_1\) has both signs, so its zero section is a line and has planar area zero. Equation (1) also gives \(u_i<1\) and \(v_i<1\). Therefore neither endpoint coordinate functional is identically zero or identically nonnegative; if it is nonconstant its zero section has area zero, while if it is constant it is strictly negative. Consequently the three complement areas are exactly
\[
|K_0|=A-X,\qquad |K_1|=1-c,\qquad |K_2|=B-Y.
\tag{2}
\]
Compatibility gives the literal complement containment
\[
\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\subseteq K_1,
\tag{3}
\]
because the midpoint of two points with nonpositive \(i\)-th coordinate again has nonpositive \(i\)-th coordinate. Identify the three parallel slice planes with their common two-dimensional translation space. The planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality, in the form
\[
\left|\frac{E+F}{2}\right|^{1/2}
\geq\frac{|E|^{1/2}+|F|^{1/2}}2
\]
for nonempty measurable planar sets \(E,F\), applies to \(K_0,K_2\), whose areas are positive by (1). Equations (2)-(3) imply the necessary cross-row inequality
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
\leq2\sqrt{1-c}
=2\sqrt{1-t+\delta}.
\tag{4}
\]

We now show that the two sides of (4) lie strictly on opposite sides of \(\sqrt2\). Let \(E=X+Y\). For fixed \(E<A\), the function
\[
f(r)=\sqrt{A-r}+\sqrt{B-E+r},\qquad 0\leq r\leq E,
\]
is concave, so its minimum is at an endpoint. Since \(A<B\),
\[
\sqrt A-\sqrt{A-E}
\geq \sqrt B-\sqrt{B-E},
\]
and hence \(f(E)\leq f(0)\). It follows from \(E<\delta\) that
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
\geq\sqrt{A-E}+\sqrt B
>\sqrt{A-\delta}+\sqrt B
>\sqrt{A-\delta_0}+\sqrt B.
\tag{5}
\]

It remains to evaluate the last expression exactly at the limiting budget. Put
\[
s:=A+B,\qquad \eta:=B-A,
\qquad a_*:=A-\delta_0.
\]
Here \(s>5/4\) because \(t>1/2\), and \(0<\eta<1\) because \(A<B<1+A\). Direct substitution gives
\[
a_*=\frac{5s+5-9\eta}{18},\qquad
B=\frac{s+\eta}{2},\qquad
a_*+B=\frac{14s+5}{18}>\frac54.
\tag{6}
\]
Also
\[
\begin{aligned}
36a_*B
&=(5s+5-9\eta)(s+\eta)\\
&=(5s-4)(s+1)+(1-\eta)(4+4s+9\eta)\\
&>(5s-4)(s+1)
>\left(\frac94\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(a_*B>9/64\). Equations (6)-(7) yield
\[
(\sqrt{a_*}+\sqrt B)^2
=a_*+B+2\sqrt{a_*B}
>\frac54+\frac34=2.
\]
Together with (5), this proves
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}>\sqrt2.
\tag{8}
\]
On the other hand, \(\delta<\delta_0=t-1/2\) gives
\[
2\sqrt{1-t+\delta}<2\sqrt{1-t+\delta_0}=\sqrt2,
\tag{9}
\]
contradicting (4).

The proof used no property of the other two rows and no endpoint support ordering. Accepted fact 63975845ca0e2926 transports this contradiction back to the original \(U,V,L,\sigma\) system without changing compatibility or any literal row mass. Hence it covers all three incidence orbits, both endpoint determinant signs before canonical orientation, every independent shift, and all zero, repeated-value, and mean-equality boundaries of the other rows.
