## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty interior, area \(D\), and centroid \(g\). Let \(p\in K\), and let
\[
a\ge c>0,\qquad 1<a^2+c^2,\qquad a+c<2,\qquad a^2<1+c^2.
\]
Set
\[
A_0=p+a(K-p),\qquad A_1=K,\qquad A_2=p+c(K-p),
\]
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),\qquad
T=D(a^2+1+c^2).
\]
Assume \(S\) is in the three-slice setting of accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`.

For \(L_h>0\), an anchored concave probability density on \([0,L_h]\) is a nonnegative concave function
\[
h:[0,L_h]\to[0,\infty)
\]
satisfying
\[
\int_0^{L_h}h(y)\,dy=1.
\]
Endpoint values at \(0\) and \(L_h\) are allowed. For integration outside \([0,L_h]\), extend \(h\) by zero, but no concavity of this extension across either endpoint is required. Write
\[
G_h(w)=\int_w^\infty h(y)\,dy.
\]
using this zero extension.

Assume that for every \(L_h>0\) and every anchored concave probability density \(h\) on \([0,L_h]\) with mean
\[
\int_0^{L_h}y h(y)\,dy=1,
\]
one has
\[
G_h(1)+a^2G_h(w_0)+c^2G_h(w_2)
\ge \frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9
\tag{1}
\]
for every \(w_0,w_2\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
aw_0+cw_2=2.
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge \frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

More precisely, for each \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), let
\[
L=\min_{k\in K}\langle q,k\rangle,\qquad
U=\max_{k\in K}\langle q,k\rangle,\qquad
\mu=\langle q,g\rangle,
\]
and let \(F\) be the distribution function of the normalized \(q\)-projection of area on \(K\). For \(r\in[L,U]\), define
\[
\Psi_q(r)=
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left[
a^2F\!\left(r+\frac{\mu-r+\lambda}{a}\right)
+c^2F\!\left(r+\frac{\mu-r-\lambda}{c}\right)
\right].
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(\Psi_q\) is nonincreasing on \([L,U]\). At \(r=U\), the change of variables below gives a bijection between \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) and all pairs satisfying (2).

## Proof

The centroid \(g\) belongs to \(K\): otherwise a linear functional strictly separating \(g\) from the compact convex set \(K\) would take at \(g\) a value strictly larger than its average over \(K\), a contradiction. Thus \((1,g)\in S\).

The three slice areas are
\[
|A_0|=a^2D,\qquad |A_1|=D,\qquad |A_2|=c^2D.
\]
Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, applied at \((1,g)\), states that the depth is the minimum of the infimum over all \(q\in\mathbb S^1,\lambda\in\mathbb R\) and the two horizontal masses
\[
D(a^2+1),\qquad D(1+c^2).
\tag{5}
\]

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), and choose a unit vector \(v\perp q\). For \(t\in[L,U]\), let
\[
I_t=\{s\in\mathbb R:tq+sv\in K\},\qquad f(t)=|I_t|,
\]
and put \(f(t)=0\) outside \([L,U]\) only when it appears in an integral. Each \(I_t\) is a compact interval, possibly a point at an endpoint. Convexity of \(K\) gives
\[
(1-\theta)I_{t_1}+\theta I_{t_2}
\subseteq I_{(1-\theta)t_1+\theta t_2}
\]
for \(t_1,t_2\in[L,U]\) and \(0\le\theta\le1\). Since the length of a Minkowski convex combination of intervals is the same convex combination of their lengths,
\[
f((1-\theta)t_1+\theta t_2)
\ge (1-\theta)f(t_1)+\theta f(t_2).
\]
Thus \(f\) is nonnegative and concave on \([L,U]\). No concavity of its zero extension is asserted.

Fubini's theorem gives
\[
D=\int_L^U f(t)\,dt,\qquad
\mu=\frac1D\int_L^U t f(t)\,dt.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
F(u)=\frac1D\int_{-\infty}^u f(t)\,dt.
\tag{7}
\]
using the zero extension for this integral. Then, for every real \(u\),
\[
F(u)=\frac1D\left|K\cap\{k:\langle q,k\rangle\le u\}\right|.
\tag{8}
\]
The equality remains exact when \(u=L\) or \(u=U\), even if the corresponding support face is a segment, because that segment has planar area zero.

Since \(K\) has nonempty interior,
\[
R:=U-\mu
=\frac1D\int_K\bigl(U-\langle q,k\rangle\bigr)\,dk>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Similarly \(\mu>L\), so
\[
B:=\frac{U-L}{R}>1.
\]
Define, on the anchored interval \([0,B]\),
\[
h(y)=\frac{R}{D}f(U-Ry).
\tag{10}
\]
This function is nonnegative and concave on \([0,B]\), including its endpoint values. Its zero extension outside \([0,B]\) will be used only in integrals. Substitution \(t=U-Ry\), together with (6), yields
\[
\int_0^B h(y)\,dy=1
\]
and
\[
\int_0^B y h(y)\,dy
=\frac1{DR}\int_L^U(U-t)f(t)\,dt
=\frac{U-\mu}{R}=1.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(h\) is one of the anchored densities quantified in (1), with \(L_h=B\).

Let \(G=G_h\). For every \(u\in\mathbb R\), setting
\[
w=\frac{U-u}{R}
\tag{12}
\]
gives
\[
F(u)=G(w).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(L<u<U\), this follows directly by substitution in the integral. If \(u\ge U\), then \(w\le0\) and both sides of (13) equal \(1\). If \(u\le L\), then \(w\ge B\) and both sides equal \(0\). Endpoint values of \(f\) or \(h\) contribute no mass to these one-dimensional integrals. Thus (13) covers both support endpoints and every cap threshold outside the support. In particular,
\[
F(\mu)=G(1).
\tag{14}
\]

Write
\[
r=\langle q,p\rangle.
\]
Because \(p\in K\), one has \(L\le r\le U\), including the possible endpoint case \(r=U\). A nonhorizontal halfspace from accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` has, on slice \(i\), the cap condition
\[
\langle q,w-g\rangle\le\lambda(1-i).
\tag{15}
\]
The middle slice condition is \(\langle q,k\rangle\le\mu\).

For \(w=p+a(k-p)\in A_0\), condition (15) is equivalent, since \(a>0\), to
\[
\langle q,k\rangle
\le u_0:=r+\frac{\mu-r+\lambda}{a}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(w=p+c(k-p)\in A_2\), it is equivalent to
\[
\langle q,k\rangle
\le u_2:=r+\frac{\mu-r-\lambda}{c}.
\tag{17}
\]
The two homotheties multiply planar area by \(a^2\) and \(c^2\). Hence the exact mass of this halfspace is
\[
D\bigl(F(\mu)+a^2F(u_0)+c^2F(u_2)\bigr).
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (16) and (17) imply
\[
a(u_0-r)+c(u_2-r)=2(\mu-r),
\tag{19}
\]
or equivalently, with \(\beta=2-a-c>0\),
\[
a(u_0-\mu)+c(u_2-\mu)=\beta(\mu-r).
\tag{20}
\]
Conversely, every pair \((u_0,u_2)\) satisfying (19) comes from exactly one \(\lambda\): set
\[
\lambda=a(u_0-r)-(\mu-r),
\]
and (19) then gives
\[
c(u_2-r)=\mu-r-\lambda.
\]
Thus the line constraint is exact, not merely necessary.

We next prove the claimed monotonicity. Let \(L\le r_1\le r_2\le U\), and let \((u_0,u_2)\) satisfy (20) with \(r=r_1\). Put
\[
\Delta=\frac{\beta(r_2-r_1)}{a+c}\ge0,\qquad
\widetilde u_0=u_0-\Delta,\qquad
\widetilde u_2=u_2-\Delta.
\]
Then
\[
a(\widetilde u_0-\mu)+c(\widetilde u_2-\mu)
=\beta(\mu-r_2),
\]
so \((\widetilde u_0,\widetilde u_2)\) is exactly a threshold pair for \(r_2\). This translation is bijective between the two constraint lines. Since \(F\) is nondecreasing,
\[
a^2F(u_0)+c^2F(u_2)
\ge a^2F(\widetilde u_0)+c^2F(\widetilde u_2).
\]
Taking infima over the two bijectively corresponding lines proves
\[
\Psi_q(r_1)\ge\Psi_q(r_2).
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the worst projected homothety center is \(r=U\).

Apply this comparison directly to the actual pair (16), (17), with \(r_2=U\). Set
\[
\Delta=\frac{(2-a-c)(U-r)}{a+c},\qquad
\widehat u_i=u_i-\Delta\quad(i=0,2).
\tag{22}
\]
Then \(\widehat u_i\le u_i\), and the shifted pair satisfies
\[
a(\widehat u_0-U)+c(\widehat u_2-U)=2(\mu-U)=-2R.
\tag{23}
\]
Define
\[
\widehat w_i=\frac{U-\widehat u_i}{R}.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation (23) is exactly
\[
a\widehat w_0+c\widehat w_2=2.
\tag{25}
\]

The endpoint parametrization is also exact. If \(r=U\), then (16), (17) read
\[
u_0=U+\frac{-R+\lambda}{a},\qquad
u_2=U+\frac{-R-\lambda}{c},
\]
so
\[
w_0=\frac{R-\lambda}{aR},\qquad
w_2=\frac{R+\lambda}{cR}.
\tag{26}
\]
These satisfy \(aw_0+cw_2=2\). Conversely, every real pair satisfying that equation is obtained from
\[
\lambda=R(1-aw_0)=R(cw_2-1).
\]
Hence no endpoint slopes or admissible tail pairs are omitted.

By monotonicity of \(F\), (13), (14), and (22),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\mu)+a^2F(u_0)+c^2F(u_2)
&\ge G(1)+a^2F(\widehat u_0)+c^2F(\widehat u_2)\\
&=G(1)+a^2G(\widehat w_0)+c^2G(\widehat w_2).
\end{aligned}
\]
The pair in the last line satisfies (25), so hypothesis (1) gives
\[
F(\mu)+a^2F(u_0)+c^2F(u_2)
\ge \frac{2(1+a^2+c^2)}9.
\tag{27}
\]
This holds for every \(q\) and every finite \(\lambda\), without any attainment assumption. It includes \(r=U\), where \(\Delta=0\), and all thresholds on or beyond the projection endpoints by (13). Combining (18) and (27), every nonhorizontal halfspace through \((1,g)\) has mass at least \(2T/9\).

It remains to check the genuinely horizontal halfspaces, which are separate in accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` because their boundary contains the full middle slice. Their exact masses are those in (5). For \(x\le1\),
\[
9(a^2+1)-2(1+a^2+c^2)
=7+7a^2-2c^2
\ge 7+5a^2>0,
\]
where \(c\le a\). For \(x\ge1\),
\[
9(1+c^2)-2(1+a^2+c^2)
=7+7c^2-2a^2
>5+5c^2>0,
\]
where \(a^2<1+c^2\). Thus both horizontal masses are also at least \(2T/9\). The closed-halfspace convention is essential here and, as stated in `400272f6de6c67bb`, includes the whole middle boundary mass \(D\).

Taking the minimum over the nonhorizontal infimum and these two horizontal masses proves (3).
