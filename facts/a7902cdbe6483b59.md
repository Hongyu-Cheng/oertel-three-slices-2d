## Statement

Set \(A=\frac1{100}\), \(B=\frac{99}{100}\), \(K=\frac{49}{100}\), \(p=\frac{99}{10000}\), \(q=\frac1{100}\), and \(r=\frac{49}{225}\). Then \(A,B,K>0\), \(0<p<A\), \(0<q<B\), and \(0<r<K\). Moreover,
\(K\ge\frac{(\sqrt A+\sqrt B)^2}{4}\), \(r\ge\frac{4K}{9}\), \((\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\le4r\), and \((\sqrt{A-p}+\sqrt{B-q})^2\le4(K-r)\), whereas
\(p+q+r<\frac{2(A+B+K)}9\).
Consequently, the stated scalar implication is false.

## Proof

All six assigned numbers are positive. The endpoint caps are nonempty and non-full because
\(A-p=\frac1{10000}>0\) and \(B-q=\frac{49}{50}>0\). The middle cap is also nonempty and non-full because
\(K-r=\frac{49}{180}>0\). Thus \(0<p<A\), \(0<q<B\), and \(0<r<K\).

Since \(99<100\), positivity gives \(\sqrt{99}<10\). Hence
\[
\frac{(\sqrt A+\sqrt B)^2}{4}
=\frac{(1+\sqrt{99})^2}{400}
=\frac{50+\sqrt{99}}{200}
<\frac3{10}
<\frac{49}{100}=K.
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{4K}{9}=\frac{4}{9}\frac{49}{100}=\frac{49}{225}=r.
\]

For the chosen endpoint caps, the same exact radical bound gives
\[
(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2
=\frac{(\sqrt{99}+10)^2}{10000}
=\frac{199+20\sqrt{99}}{10000}
<\frac{399}{10000}
<\frac1{25}
<\frac{196}{225}=4r,
\]
where the last rational comparison is \(225<4900\).

For the complementary endpoint caps,
\[
A-p=\frac1{10000},\qquad B-q=\frac{49}{50},\qquad K-r=\frac{49}{180}.
\]
The rational comparison \(2\cdot140^2=39200<39601=199^2\) implies
\(\sqrt2<\frac{199}{140}\). Therefore
\[
(\sqrt{A-p}+\sqrt{B-q})^2
=\left(\frac1{100}+\frac{7\sqrt2}{10}\right)^2
=\frac{9801+140\sqrt2}{10000}
<1
<\frac{49}{45}=4(K-r).
\]

Finally,
\[
p+q+r
=\frac{199}{10000}+\frac{49}{225}
=\frac{21391}{90000},
\qquad
\frac{2(A+B+K)}9
=\frac{149}{450}
=\frac{29800}{90000}.
\]
Since \(21391<29800\), the target bound fails strictly. This completes the exact counterexample to the scalar implication, without making any geometric realizability claim.
