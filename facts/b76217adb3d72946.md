## Statement

Use all hypotheses and notation of accepted facts
`9374e5c5a811bd3d` and `d288193baa0edc6b`.  In particular,
\[
\Delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>2,\qquad
s=\frac{\Delta+2}{\Delta},
\]
\[
r_i=\frac{f_i-L_i}{\Delta},\qquad
\tau_i=-\frac{f_i+L_i}{\Delta+2},\qquad
\lambda_i=-\frac{L_i}{\Delta},
\]
\[
\sum_i r_i=\sum_i\tau_i=1,\qquad
r_i=2\lambda_i-s\tau_i,
\tag{1}
\]
and all areas use the common \(r\)-coordinate normalization, so a
\(\tau\)-coordinate area is multiplied by \(s^2\).

Let
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},
\qquad
D_\tau=A_{2,\tau}\cap\{\tau_3\leq0\},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
H_D=\frac12(P+2\lambda-sD_\tau)\subseteq K.
\tag{3}
\]
Assume the two active \(P\)-faces are present:
\[
0\in X_1\cap X_2,
\tag{4}
\]
and write
\[
\ell_i=w_i^P(0)
\qquad(i=1,2)
\tag{5}
\]
as in accepted fact `9374e5c5a811bd3d`.

For \(i=1,2\), let \(\widehat T_i\) be the
\(\tau_i\)-projection interval of \(D_\tau\), write its nonempty
sections in the corresponding transverse area coordinate as
\[
(D_\tau)_{i,t}
=[L_i^D(t),R_i^D(t)]
\qquad(t\in\widehat T_i),
\]
and put
\[
w_i^D(t)=R_i^D(t)-L_i^D(t),
\qquad
\widehat\beta_i=\max\widehat T_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Retain the distinct notation
\[
\beta_i=\max T_i
\tag{7}
\]
for the \(\tau_i\)-projection maximum of
\[
C_\tau=
\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
\overline{Q_1}^{\,\tau}\cup
\overline{Q_2}^{\,\tau}\bigr)
\]
from accepted fact `9374e5c5a811bd3d`.

Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
[0,\widehat\beta_i]\subseteq\widehat T_i,
\qquad
\widehat\beta_i\geq\beta_i>1.
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\left|H_D\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|
\geq
\frac14\left(
s\widehat\beta_i\ell_i+q_i+q_{12}
\right)
\geq
\frac14\left(
s\beta_i\ell_i+q_i+q_{12}
\right).
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]

The row crossing and \(\kappa<M+m\) give
\[
c_i=
\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9-u_i-v_i
<
\frac{4(M+m)}9-u_i-v_i
=U_i.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(H_D\subseteq K\), equations (9)-(10) force
\[
\boxed{\quad
s\widehat\beta_i\ell_i+q_i+q_{12}<4U_i
\qquad(i=1,2).
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, define
\[
\widehat B=
\min_{i=1,2}
\{4U_i-s\widehat\beta_i\ell_i-q_i\},
\]
\[
B=
\min_{i=1,2}
\{4U_i-s\beta_i\ell_i-q_i\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
B\geq\widehat B>q_{12}.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently no compatible canonical configuration satisfying (4)
can lie in the chamber \(B\leq q_{12}\).

## Proof

Fix \(i\in\{1,2\}\).  Every strict point of \(Q_i\) has
\[
\tau_i>1,
\tag{14}
\]
because its other two \(\tau\)-coordinates are negative and the
coordinates sum to one.  Since
\[
Q_i^\tau\subseteq D_\tau,
\]
equation (14) gives
\[
\widehat\beta_i>1.
\tag{15}
\]
The opposite singleton \(Q_{3-i}\) contains a strict point with
\(\tau_i<0\), also in \(D_\tau\).  The projection
\(\widehat T_i\) of the compact convex set \(D_\tau\) is a compact
interval containing both that negative value and
\(\widehat\beta_i\).  Hence
\[
[0,\widehat\beta_i]\subseteq\widehat T_i.
\tag{16}
\]
Also
\[
C_\tau\subseteq D_\tau,
\]
so their projection maxima satisfy
\[
\widehat\beta_i\geq\beta_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Accepted fact `9374e5c5a811bd3d` gives \(\beta_i>1\).
This proves (8) while keeping the two projection maxima distinct.

Apply the exact two-rail identity in accepted fact
`cb3cba490b10885c` to \(P\) and \(D_\tau\).  The resulting body is
exactly \(H_D\) from (3), and
\[
f_i\leq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_i\leq\lambda_i.
\tag{18}
\]
For
\[
-s\widehat\beta_i\leq u\leq0
\]
choose the equal rails
\[
t_-(u)=t_+(u)=-\frac{u}{s}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equation (16) gives
\[
t_\pm(u)\in\widehat T_i,
\]
and (4) gives
\[
u+st_\pm(u)=0\in X_i.
\]
Thus (19) is an admissible continuous, hence Borel, selector pair on
the whole displayed \(u\)-interval.  Its two-rail integrand is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_i^D(u;t_-(u),t_+(u))
&=w_i^P(0)
+s\,w_i^D\!\left(-\frac{u}{s}\right)\\
&=\ell_i
+s\,w_i^D\!\left(-\frac{u}{s}\right)
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The exact two-rail lower bound therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|H_D\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|
&\geq
\frac14
\int_{-s\widehat\beta_i}^{0}
\left[
\ell_i+s\,w_i^D\!\left(-\frac{u}{s}\right)
\right]du\\
&=
\frac14\left[
s\widehat\beta_i\ell_i
+s^2\int_0^{\widehat\beta_i}w_i^D(t)\,dt
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
The factor \(s^2\) is exact: one factor \(s\) comes from the
transverse interval in the integrand and the second from
\(du=-s\,dt\).

Up to the null coordinate lines, the exact positive support
\[
\{J:q_J>0\}=\{1,2,12,123\}
\]
and the defining inequality \(\tau_3\leq0\) give
\[
D_\tau\cap\{\tau_i\geq0\}
=Q_i^\tau\mathbin{\dot\cup}Q_{12}^\tau
\qquad(i=1,2).
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, \(Q_{123}\) has \(\tau_3>0\), the opposite singleton has
\(\tau_i<0\), and there are no other positive-area cells.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
s^2\int_0^{\widehat\beta_i}w_i^D(t)\,dt
&=s^2|D_\tau\cap\{\tau_i\geq0\}|_\tau\\
&=q_i+q_{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Substitution of (23) in (21), followed by (17), proves (9).

Compatibility proves the containment in (3) directly:
\[
P\subseteq A_0,\qquad
2\lambda-sD_\tau\subseteq A_2,
\]
so
\[
H_D\subseteq\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{24}
\]
Therefore
\[
\left|H_D\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|\leq c_i.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (9), (10), and (25) prove (11).

Taking the minimum of the strict inequalities (11) gives
\[
\widehat B>q_{12}.
\]
Equation (17) and \(\ell_i\geq0\) give, row by row,
\[
4U_i-s\beta_i\ell_i-q_i
\geq
4U_i-s\widehat\beta_i\ell_i-q_i,
\]
so \(B\geq\widehat B\).  This proves (13).

There is no support-face or breakpoint exception.  The two-rail
identity applies to arbitrary compact convex section intervals.
The endpoint levels \(t=0,\widehat\beta_i\) have zero measure in
(21), even when a terminal section is a nondegenerate support face.
All weak-versus-strict cell differences in (22) lie on the three
coordinate lines and have zero planar area.  Thus neither the
projection argument nor the Jacobian calculation loses a boundary
term.
