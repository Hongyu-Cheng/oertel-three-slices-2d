## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional
convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
s=|A_1|,\qquad a_0=|A_0|,\qquad a_2=|A_2|,\qquad
T=a_0+s+a_2,
\tag{2}
\]
and, for \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), define
\[
G_i^u(r)
=
\left|A_i\cap\{w\in\mathbb R^2:\langle u,w\rangle\le r\}\right|
\qquad(i=0,1,2),
\tag{3}
\]
\[
(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(r)
=
\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(G_0^u(\tau)+G_2^u(r-\tau)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]
For
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)
\tag{5}
\]
and every \(z\in A_1\), the halfspace depth is exactly
\[
h_S(1,z)
=
\min\left\{
a_0+s,\ s+a_2,\
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2\langle u,z\rangle)
\right]
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal M(A_0,A_1,A_2)
=
\max_{z\in A_1}\min_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2\langle u,z\rangle)
\right].
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
\max_{z\in A_1}h_S(1,z)
=
\min\{a_0+s,\ s+a_2,\ \mathcal M(A_0,A_1,A_2)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The accepted large-slice lemma already proves the locked theorem whenever
at least one of
\[
a_0\ge s+a_2,\qquad
s\ge a_0+a_2,\qquad
a_2\ge a_0+s
\tag{9}
\]
holds. If none holds, then
\[
a_0<s+a_2,\qquad
s<a_0+a_2,\qquad
a_2<a_0+s,
\tag{10}
\]
and both horizontal terms in (8) are strictly larger than \(T/2\).
Consequently, the locked theorem follows from the following weakest
corrected global statement:

For every triple satisfying (1) and (10),
\[
\mathcal M(A_0,A_1,A_2)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equivalently, for every such triple there exists \(z\in A_1\) such that,
for every \(u\in\mathbb S^1\),
\[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2\langle u,z\rangle)
\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{12}
\]
The proposed decomposition has the correct normalization:
\[
\frac{4s}{9}+\frac{2(a_0+a_2-s)}9
=\frac{2(a_0+a_2+s)}9
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{13}
\]
Only the total bound (12) is required. No separate lower bounds by the two
summands in (13) are part of this reduction.

## Proof

Fix \(z\in A_1\). Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, specialized to
\(j=1\), says that the nonhorizontal closed halfspaces whose boundary
contains \((1,z)\) are parametrized by
\((u,\lambda)\in\mathbb S^1\times\mathbb R\), with the condition on slice
\(x=i\)
\[
\langle u,w-z\rangle\le\lambda(1-i).
\tag{14}
\]
Put \(t=\langle u,z\rangle\). The three slice conditions in (14) become
\[
\langle u,w\rangle\le t+\lambda\quad(i=0),\qquad
\langle u,w\rangle\le t\quad(i=1),\qquad
\langle u,w\rangle\le t-\lambda\quad(i=2).
\tag{15}
\]
Hence the measure of this halfspace is
\[
G_0^u(t+\lambda)+G_1^u(t)+G_2^u(t-\lambda).
\tag{16}
\]
Substitute \(\tau=t+\lambda\). Since
\(t-\lambda=2t-\tau\), taking the infimum over \(\lambda\) in (16)
gives
\[
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(G_0^u(t+\lambda)+G_1^u(t)+G_2^u(t-\lambda)\bigr)
=
G_1^u(t)+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2t).
\tag{17}
\]
The parameter set \(\mathbb S^1\) contains both \(u\) and \(-u\), so
(17) includes both sides of every nonhorizontal hyperplane through
\((1,z)\).

The two horizontal halfspaces through \((1,z)\) are \(x\le1\) and
\(x\ge1\). They are closed, so both include the entire middle slice.
Their measures are
\[
a_0+s=T-a_2,\qquad s+a_2=T-a_0.
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (17), (18), and the exact halfspace parametrization in accepted
fact `400272f6de6c67bb` proves (6).

The extrema used in (4) and (7) are attained. To see this directly, choose
\(R>0\) such that
\[
|\langle u,w\rangle|\le R
\tag{19}
\]
for every \(u\in\mathbb S^1\) and every
\(w\in A_0\cup A_1\cup A_2\). If \(t=\langle u,z\rangle\), then
\(|t|\le R\). For \(\tau\le-4R\), the two outer terms in (4), evaluated at
\(r=2t\), equal \(0+a_2\); for \(\tau\ge4R\), they equal \(a_0+0\).
Thus the infimum in (4) can be restricted to \([-4R,4R]\).
Each cap profile in (3) is jointly continuous in \((u,r)\), because every
affine line has zero planar area in a two-dimensional convex body.
Compactness now shows that the infimum in (4), the minimum over
\(\mathbb S^1\), and the maximum over \(A_1\) are attained. Maximizing
(6) over \(z\), while observing that the first two entries of its minimum
are independent of \(z\), proves (8).

Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` applies whenever one slice has area at
least \(T/2\). The three alternatives are exactly (9), because, for
example,
\[
a_0\ge\frac T2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a_0\ge s+a_2,
\tag{20}
\]
and similarly for the other two slices. Equality is included. If no
alternative in (9) holds, all three inequalities reverse strictly, giving
(10), equivalently
\[
\max\{a_0,s,a_2\}<\frac T2.
\tag{21}
\]
In that remaining region, (18) yields
\[
a_0+s=T-a_2>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9},\qquad
s+a_2=T-a_0>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]
Therefore (8) shows that (11), or equivalently (12), produces a
middle-slice point of depth at least \(2T/9\).

Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` shows that condition (1) is exactly the
admissibility condition for triples of convex polytopes arising as the
three integer-height sections of a compact polytope. Thus the proof covers
every instance of the locked problem: first apply
`2f9be517307232c7` if (9) holds, and otherwise apply (11) in (10).

Finally, this explains why the proposed domain \(s\le a_0+a_2\) is
stronger than necessary. It contains the large-\(A_0\) and large-\(A_2\)
parts of (9), which are already settled. Moreover, before those regimes
are removed, a tilted bound alone need not certify a middle-slice point.
From (18), the exact horizontal requirements at the target \(2T/9\) are
\[
a_2\le\frac{7T}{9},\qquad a_0\le\frac{7T}{9}.
\tag{23}
\]
For instance, if \(a_2>7T/9\), then the horizontal halfspace \(x\le1\)
has measure \(T-a_2<2T/9\), independently of \(z\). After all
large-slice cases (9) are removed, (22) makes the horizontal requirements
automatic. The algebra in (13) is immediate, so there is no normalization
error. This completes the exact reduction without proving the remaining
global inequality (11).
