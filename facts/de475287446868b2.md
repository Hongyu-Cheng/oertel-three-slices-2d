## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies satisfying the compatibility inclusion
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
a=|A_1|,\qquad T=M+m+a,
\tag{2}
\]
and suppose
\[
M-m<a<M+m.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(H\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) is a nonhorizontal closed
halfspace and the boundary line of its height-one trace bisects \(A_1\),
then
\[
\mathcal H_2\!\left(
H\cap\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i)
\right)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]

More precisely, fix a unit vector \(q\) and \(s\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le s\}\right|=\frac a2,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
G_i(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}\right|,
\qquad
J_q(s)=\min_{c_0+c_2=2s}\bigl(G_0(c_0)+G_2(c_2)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
J_q(s)\ge \Phi_{M,m}(a),
\tag{7}
\]
where the sharp lower envelope furnished by complementary-cap
Brunn--Minkowski and the two capacity bounds, including empty caps, is
\[
\Phi_{M,m}(a)=
\begin{cases}
m,
&\sqrt{2a}\le\sqrt M,\\[2mm]
m-\bigl(\sqrt{2a}-\sqrt M\bigr)^2,
&\sqrt M\le\sqrt{2a}\le\sqrt M+\sqrt m,\\[2mm]
0,
&\sqrt{2a}\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt m.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
The formulas agree at both branch boundaries, and throughout (1)--(3),
\[
\frac a2+\Phi_{M,m}(a)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]
The two horizontal closed halfspaces with boundary at height one do not
have a bisecting-line trace, but their masses are also strictly larger than
\(2T/9\).

## Proof

The compatibility condition (1) is the condition characterized for the
locked polytopal slices in accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`.  We use the
inclusion itself, so the argument below also applies verbatim to convex
bodies.

Write a point of \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) as \((t,x)\).  After
orienting and rescaling its defining inequality, every nonhorizontal closed
halfspace has the form
\[
H=\{(t,x):q\cdot x+\lambda t\le b\}
\tag{10}
\]
for a unit vector \(q\) and real numbers \(\lambda,b\).  Put \(s=b-\lambda\)
and
\[
c_i=b-i\lambda\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(c_1=s\) and
\[
c_0+c_2=2s.
\tag{12}
\]
The height-\(i\) contribution is \(G_i(c_i)\).  Since an affine line has
planar area zero, the bisection hypothesis gives \(G_1(s)=a/2\).  Thus the
mass of (10) is
\[
\frac a2+G_0(c_0)+G_2(c_2).
\tag{13}
\]
This is the direct halfspace normal form underlying accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240`.

For completeness, the minimum in (6) is genuine.  Each \(G_i\) is
continuous because the level lines of \(q\cdot x\) have planar area zero.
As \(c_0\) tends sufficiently far to the left, the two terms in
\[
c_0\longmapsto G_0(c_0)+G_2(2s-c_0)
\tag{14}
\]
are exactly \(0\) and \(|A_2|\); sufficiently far to the right they are
exactly \(|A_0|\) and \(0\).  Compact support therefore makes (14) constant
on both tails, so its global infimum is attained on a compact interval.
This also verifies directly the endpoint-attainment assertion in accepted
fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240`; no limiting threshold is being inserted into (6).

Relabel the two outer bodies, only within this proof, as \(A_M,A_m\), with
areas \(M,m\).  Fix any allocation \(c_M+c_m=2s\), and let
\[
E_M=A_M\cap\{x:q\cdot x\ge c_M\},\qquad
E_m=A_m\cap\{x:q\cdot x\ge c_m\},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
X=|E_M|,\qquad Y=|E_m|.
\tag{16}
\]
The endpoint contribution for this allocation is
\[
M+m-X-Y,
\tag{17}
\]
again because cutting lines have zero planar area.

If \(X,Y>0\), then both caps are nonempty.  From (1), (12), and (15),
\[
\frac{E_M+E_m}{2}
\subseteq A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x\ge s\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The right side has area \(a/2\) by (5).  Planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt X+\sqrt Y}{2}
\le
\sqrt{\left|\frac{E_M+E_m}{2}\right|}
\le\sqrt{\frac a2},
\tag{19}
\]
and hence, on setting \(B=\sqrt{2a}\),
\[
\sqrt X+\sqrt Y\le B.
\tag{20}
\]

The empty-cap cases have to be separated before using (19).  If \(X=0\),
(17) is at least \(M\), while if \(Y=0\), it is at least \(m\).  Thus in
either case
\[
M+m-X-Y\ge m.
\tag{21}
\]
This is the point at which fixing the endpoint allocation independently,
or applying Brunn--Minkowski to an empty Minkowski summand, would give an
incorrect first branch.

We now solve the capacity problem exactly.  For positive caps put
\[
p=\sqrt X,\qquad r=\sqrt Y.
\tag{22}
\]
Relaxing to nonnegative variables can only enlarge their possible sum of
squares, and the relevant maximum is
\[
K(B)=\max\{p^2+r^2:
0\le p\le\sqrt M,\ 0\le r\le\sqrt m,\ p+r\le B\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(M\ge m\),
\[
K(B)=
\begin{cases}
B^2,&0\le B\le\sqrt M,\\[1mm]
M+(B-\sqrt M)^2,
&\sqrt M\le B\le\sqrt M+\sqrt m,\\[1mm]
M+m,&B\ge\sqrt M+\sqrt m.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed, the first line follows from
\(p^2+r^2\le(p+r)^2\le B^2\).  In the middle range a maximizer has
\(p+r=B\), and convexity makes it one of the two capacity endpoints.
The endpoint filling the larger capacity first is no worse, since
\[
\begin{aligned}
&M+(B-\sqrt M)^2
-\left(m+(B-\sqrt m)^2\right)\\
&\qquad
=2(\sqrt M-\sqrt m)(\sqrt M+\sqrt m-B)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
The last line of (24) is immediate when both capacities can be filled.

If \(B\le\sqrt M\), (17), (20), and the first line of (24) give
\[
M+m-X-Y\ge M+m-B^2\ge m;
\tag{26}
\]
together with (21), the universal bound is \(m\).  In the middle range,
(17), (20), and (24) give
\[
M+m-X-Y\ge m-(B-\sqrt M)^2,
\tag{27}
\]
and (21) is no smaller.  In the last range nonnegativity gives the bound
zero.  This proves (7)--(8).  It also proves sharpness relative to precisely
these constraints: the abstract cap-area allocations
\[
(X,Y)=(M,0),\quad
(X,Y)=\left(M,(B-\sqrt M)^2\right),\quad
(X,Y)=(M,m)
\tag{28}
\]
attain the three respective formulas whenever their branch applies.  No
geometric realization beyond the stated capacity and Brunn--Minkowski data
is asserted.  At \(B=\sqrt M\), the first two formulas both equal \(m\);
at \(B=\sqrt M+\sqrt m\), the last two both equal zero.  Thus all branch
boundaries and the zero/full endpoint caps are included.

It remains to prove (9).  Brunn--Minkowski applied to the whole compatible
outer bodies gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt M+\sqrt m}{2}
\le\sqrt{\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right|}
\le\sqrt a.
\tag{29}
\]
Normalize by \(M\), writing
\[
x=\sqrt{\frac mM}\in(0,1],\qquad s=\frac aM.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (3) and (29) become
\[
s>1-x^2,\qquad s<1+x^2,\qquad
s\ge\frac{(1+x)^2}{4}.
\tag{31}
\]

The first branch of (8) would require \(s\le1/2\), and is actually
incompatible with (31).  If \(x<1/\sqrt2\), then
\(s>1-x^2>1/2\); if \(x\ge1/\sqrt2\), then
\[
s\ge\frac{(1+x)^2}{4}
\ge\frac{(1+1/\sqrt2)^2}{4}>\frac12.
\tag{32}
\]
This checks the whole first branch, including \(s=1/2\).

In the middle branch,
\[
1\le\sqrt{2s}\le1+x
\tag{33}
\]
and (8) reads
\[
\frac{\Phi_{M,m}(a)}M
=x^2-\bigl(\sqrt{2s}-1\bigr)^2.
\tag{34}
\]
After multiplying the difference between the two sides of (9) by
\(18/M\), inequality (9) is equivalent to
\[
F_x(s):=14x^2-22-31s+36\sqrt{2s}\ge0.
\tag{35}
\]
The feasible upper endpoint is
\[
U_x=\frac{(1+x)^2}{2},
\tag{36}
\]
since \(U_x\le1+x^2\).  The strict inequality
\(1-x^2<s\le U_x\) forces \(x>1/3\).  The lower endpoint supplied by
(31) is
\[
L_x=
\begin{cases}
1-x^2,&1/3\le x\le3/5,\\[1mm]
(1+x)^2/4,&3/5\le x\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{37}
\]
Strictness at a lower endpoint only removes that endpoint and does not
affect the following lower bound.

The function \(s\mapsto F_x(s)\) is concave for \(s>0\), so its minimum on
\([L_x,U_x]\) is attained at an endpoint.  At the upper endpoint,
\[
F_x(U_x)=\frac{(3x-1)(3-x)}2\ge0
\qquad(1/3\le x\le1).
\tag{38}
\]
For \(1/3\le x\le3/5\), put \(u=x^2\).  At the first lower endpoint,
\[
F_x(1-x^2)=45u-53+36\sqrt{2(1-u)}.
\tag{39}
\]
Here \(53-45u>0\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
2592(1-u)-(53-45u)^2
&=-(9u-1)(225u-217)\\
&\ge0
\qquad\left(\frac19\le u\le\frac9{25}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
Thus (39) is nonnegative.  For \(3/5\le x\le1\), the other lower endpoint
gives
\[
F_x\!\left(\frac{(1+x)^2}{4}\right)
=\frac{25x^2-62x-119}{4}+18\sqrt2(1+x).
\tag{41}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\frac{25x-31}{2}+18\sqrt2>0
\qquad(3/5\le x\le1),
\tag{42}
\]
and at \(x=3/5\) its value is
\[
\frac{144\sqrt2-184}{5}>0.
\tag{43}
\]
Equations (38)--(43) prove (35) throughout the middle branch, including
both capacity boundaries.

In the third branch,
\[
s\ge\frac{(1+x)^2}{2}.
\tag{44}
\]
If \(0<x\le1/3\), then (31) and
\[
1-x^2\ge\frac{4(1+x^2)}5
\tag{45}
\]
give \(s>4(1+x^2)/5\).  If \(1/3\le x\le1\), then
\[
\frac{(1+x)^2}{2}-\frac{4(1+x^2)}5
=\frac{(3x-1)(3-x)}{10}\ge0.
\tag{46}
\]
Hence in every case
\[
s\ge\frac{4(1+x^2)}5,
\tag{47}
\]
which is exactly
\[
\frac s2\ge\frac{2(1+x^2+s)}9.
\tag{48}
\]
Since the endpoint envelope is zero in this branch, (48) proves (9).
Together with (13), this proves (4).

The exact zero/full endpoint tails are also safe directly.  Their endpoint
mass is at least \(m\), and the left inequality in (3) gives
\[
\frac a2+m-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{5a+14m-4M}{18}
>\frac{M+9m}{18}>0.
\tag{49}
\]
Thus no limiting slope has been omitted.

Finally, (3) implies that each of \(M,m,a\) is strictly less than \(T/2\).
The two horizontal halfspaces with boundary at height one have masses
\[
|A_0|+a=T-|A_2|,
\qquad
a+|A_2|=T-|A_0|,
\tag{50}
\]
so both are strictly larger than \(T/2\), hence larger than \(2T/9\).
As emphasized in accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240`, these horizontal
halfspaces are separate from the tilted limit because their boundary
contains the entire middle slice.

Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` places the unresolved residual area strip
inside the hypotheses (1)--(3).  Accepted fact `7694bc07b24d1290`
constructs an equal-six-sector point whose three selected lines bisect
\(A_1\), so the present lemma upgrades its selected-row conclusion from at
least two safe rows to all three.  Nothing above applies to a direction
whose trace does not bisect \(A_1\), and no angular interpolation has been
used.  Accepted fact `d5ee798890c7e1b4` gives an exact failure of such an
interpolation from the selected rows, so no unselected-direction conclusion
is claimed.
