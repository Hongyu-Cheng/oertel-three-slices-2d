## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nondegenerate triangles satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Define
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\]
and, for \(y\in S\),
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\left\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):
H\subset\mathbb R^3\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\right\}.
\]
Put
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
let \(g_i\) be the centroid of \(A_i\), and set
\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}.
\]
Write the barycentric coordinates of \(z\) in \(A_1\), in increasing order,
as
\[
0\le\beta\le\gamma\le 1-\beta-\gamma.
\]
If
\[
(18\beta\gamma-1)a_1\ge M-m,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]

The criterion gives a new open class in the strict residual strip of accepted
fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. Explicitly, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),\qquad
z=\left(\frac{17}{50},\frac{37}{100}\right),
\]
and define
\[
A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_0=z+\frac{20}{21}(\Delta-g),\qquad
A_2=z+\frac{8}{21}(\Delta-g).
\tag{3}
\]
This rational triple is strictly compatible, lies strictly in that residual
strip, satisfies (1) strictly, and lies strictly outside both accepted open
classes `1e612c16bc508e32` and `a6808da3e0b737ca`. These properties persist
on a full Euclidean neighborhood of the nine vertices in (3).

## Proof

We first derive the exact quadratic cap formula used below. Let \(A\) be a
nondegenerate triangle of area \(a\), and let \(x\in A\) have barycentric
coordinates
\[
0\le\beta\le\gamma\le\delta,\qquad \beta+\gamma+\delta=1.
\]
Then its planar halfspace depth is
\[
d_A(x)=4\beta\gamma a.
\tag{4}
\]

It is enough to consider closed halfplanes whose boundary passes through
\(x\): translating the boundary of any halfplane containing \(x\) parallel
toward \(x\) produces a contained halfplane. By an affine normalization, it
is then enough to work with a reference triangle. Consider a line through
\(x\) which does not pass through a vertex. It cuts off a triangle at one
vertex \(v_i\). If the other vertices are \(v_j,v_k\), write the intersections
with the incident sides as
\[
(1-s)v_i+sv_j,\qquad (1-u)v_i+uv_k,
\qquad 0<s,u\le1.
\]
Writing the barycentric coordinates of \(x\) as
\(\lambda_i,\lambda_j,\lambda_k\), there is a number \(X\in(0,1)\) such that
\[
X=\frac{\lambda_j}{s},\qquad
1-X=\frac{\lambda_k}{u}.
\]
The relative area of the isolated vertex triangle is therefore
\[
su=\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{X(1-X)}
\ge4\lambda_j\lambda_k
\ge4\beta\gamma.
\tag{5}
\]

The complementary quadrilateral also has relative area at least
\(4\beta\gamma\). Indeed, \(s,u\le1\) gives
\[
\lambda_j\le X\le1-\lambda_k.
\]
The minimum of \(X(1-X)\) on this interval occurs at an endpoint, so
\[
su\le
\max\left\{
\frac{\lambda_k}{1-\lambda_j},
\frac{\lambda_j}{1-\lambda_k}
\right\}.
\]
Consequently the complementary relative area is at least
\[
\frac{\lambda_i}{1-\min\{\lambda_j,\lambda_k\}}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\lambda_i=\beta\), the right side of (6) is
\(\beta/(1-\gamma)\), which is at least \(4\beta\gamma\) because
\(4\gamma(1-\gamma)\le1\). If \(\lambda_i=\gamma\), it is
\(\gamma/(1-\beta)\), which is at least \(4\beta\gamma\) because
\(4\beta(1-\beta)\le1\). If \(\lambda_i=\delta\), it is at least
\(\gamma/(1-\beta)\), so the same inequality applies. Thus both closed
sides of every line through \(x\) have area at least \(4\beta\gamma a\).
Lines through a vertex follow by continuity.

Equality is attained. Isolate the vertex whose barycentric coordinate is
\(\delta\), and intersect its two incident sides at parameters
\(s=2\beta\) and \(u=2\gamma\). These parameters belong to \([0,1]\), and
the midpoint of the two intersection points is \(x\). The isolated triangle
has relative area \(su=4\beta\gamma\). This proves (4). In particular, at
the centroid of any triangle,
\[
d_A(g_A)=\frac{4|A|}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]

Compatibility puts \(z=(g_0+g_2)/2\) in \(A_1\). We now prove (2) by checking
an arbitrary closed halfspace \(H\) containing \((1,z)\).

First suppose \(H\) is nonhorizontal. Orient its planar normal so that
\[
H=\{(h,w):ah+q\cdot w\le b\}
\]
for some \(q\ne0\). Since \(H\) contains \((1,z)\),
\[
a+q\cdot z\le b.
\]
Replacing \(b\) by \(a+q\cdot z\) shrinks \(H\) to a contained closed
halfspace whose boundary passes through \((1,z)\). It is therefore enough
to bound the mass of
\[
H'=\{(h,w):ah+q\cdot w\le a+q\cdot z\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Its middle section is the closed cap
\[
A_1\cap\{w:q\cdot w\le q\cdot z\},
\]
which contains \(z\). Formula (4) gives the lower bound
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{w:q\cdot w\le q\cdot z\}\right|
\ge4\beta\gamma a_1.
\tag{9}
\]

The endpoint sections of (8) have thresholds
\[
c_0=q\cdot z+a,\qquad c_2=q\cdot z-a,
\]
and hence
\[
c_0+c_2=2q\cdot z=q\cdot g_0+q\cdot g_2.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(c_0\ge q\cdot g_0\), then the \(A_0\)-cap
\[
A_0\cap\{w:q\cdot w\le c_0\}
\]
contains \(g_0\), so (7) gives it area at least \(4a_0/9\). If
\(c_0<q\cdot g_0\), equation (10) gives
\[
c_2=q\cdot g_0+q\cdot g_2-c_0>q\cdot g_2,
\]
so the \(A_2\)-cap contains \(g_2\) and has area at least \(4a_2/9\).
The other endpoint contribution is nonnegative. Thus every nonhorizontal
closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\) has mass at least
\[
4\beta\gamma a_1+\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{11}
\]
Condition (1) is exactly the statement that the right side of (11) is at
least
\[
\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}9=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to check horizontal halfspaces separately. The two smallest
horizontal masses for a halfspace containing height \(1\) are
\[
a_0+a_1,\qquad a_1+a_2.
\tag{13}
\]
Their minimum is \(a_1+m=T-M\). Since
\(\beta\le\gamma\le1-\beta-\gamma\), one has
\(\gamma\le(1-\beta)/2\) and \(\beta\le1/3\), hence
\[
\beta\gamma\le\frac{\beta(1-\beta)}2\le\frac19.
\]
Condition (1), whose right side is nonnegative,
therefore implies
\[
0\le18\beta\gamma-1\le1,\qquad M-m\le a_1.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that
\[
T-M=a_1+m\ge M,
\tag{15}
\]
and hence \(T-M\ge T/2>2T/9\).
Thus every horizontal halfspace containing \((1,z)\) also has mass above
the target. Equations (8)-(15) prove the pointwise conclusion (2) for every
closed halfspace in its definition.

We now verify that (3) removes a genuinely new open residual branch. The
three barycentric coordinates of \(z\) in \(\Delta\) are
\[
\left(\frac{29}{100},\frac{17}{50},\frac{37}{100}\right),
\]
so
\[
\beta=\frac{29}{100},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{17}{50}.
\tag{16}
\]
The common centroid of \(A_0,A_2\) is \(z\), and convexity gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=z+\frac23(\Delta-g).
\tag{17}
\]
Every barycentric coordinate on the right side of (17) is at least
\[
\frac{29}{100}-\frac29=\frac{61}{900}>0.
\]
Thus (17) is contained in the interior of \(\Delta\), proving strict
compatibility.

The areas are
\[
a_1=\frac12,\qquad
M=a_0=\frac{200}{441},\qquad
m=a_2=\frac{32}{441}.
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently
\[
r=\frac mM=\frac4{25},\qquad
s=\frac{a_1}{M}=\frac{441}{400}.
\]
Here \(r<9/25\) and
\[
1-r=\frac{21}{25}
<\frac{441}{400}
<\frac{29}{25}=1+r.
\tag{19}
\]
The piecewise characterization in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`
therefore places (18) strictly in the full residual strip.

The new criterion has the strict margin
\[
(18\beta\gamma-1)a_1-(M-m)
=\frac{1937}{5000}-\frac8{21}
=\frac{677}{105000}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
By contrast, the criterion of accepted fact `1e612c16bc508e32` fails
strictly:
\[
(18\beta^2-1)a_1
=\frac{2569}{10000}
<\frac8{21}=M-m.
\tag{21}
\]

Finally, the normalized area condition of accepted fact
`a6808da3e0b737ca` also fails strictly. For the values above,
\[
r-\frac{2(1+r-s)}9=\frac{53}{360},
\qquad
\frac{20s}{9}=\frac{49}{20},
\]
and
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{53}{360}}\right)^2<\frac{49}{20}.
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, after subtracting \(1+53/360\), both sides are positive and
squaring the required inequality reduces to
\[
76320<219961.
\]

The strict containment margin in (17), the strict residual inequalities
in (19), the new positive margin in (20), the strict failure in (21), and
the strict reverse inequality in (22) all depend continuously on the nine
vertices. The three barycentric coordinates in (16) are distinct, so their
ordering also persists under small perturbations. Hence a full Euclidean
neighborhood of (3) remains strictly compatible, remains in the residual
strip, satisfies the new criterion, and remains outside both previously
accepted open classes. Independent perturbations in this neighborhood
destroy all parallel-edge and homothetic relations, so the gain is not an
aligned special family.
