## Statement

Assume, without omission, all hypotheses and notation in the statement
of accepted fact `1e269d4c02c94338`.  In particular, assume the common
affine-plane and exact-cell hypotheses of accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`, the exact positive \(Q\)-support
\[
\{J:q_J>0\}=\{123,1,2,12\},
\]
all finite-interior conditions, all three crossing equalities, both
canonical tails in every row, \(W=0\), and all actual-counterexample
hypotheses used there.  Thus
\[
p:=p_\varnothing>0,\qquad q_1>0,\qquad q_2>0,
\]
and the quantities \(M,m,k,\mathcal P^\star,\mathcal Q^\star\), and
\(\mathcal R^\star_K\) below have exactly the meanings fixed in that
accepted fact:
\[
\mathcal P^\star
=\frac32(p_1+p_2)+5p_{12}
+\frac72(p_{13}+p_{23}),
\]
\[
\mathcal Q^\star
=\frac32(q_1+q_2)+5q_{12}+7q_{123},
\]
\[
\mathcal R^\star_K
=\frac32(r_1+r_2)+5r_{12}
+\frac72(r_{13}+r_{23})+7r_{123}.
\tag{1}
\]

Define the exact midpoint images
\[
U_1=\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_1}{2},\qquad
U_2=\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_2}{2},\qquad
U_3=\frac{P_3+Q_{123}}2,
\]
and put
\[
B_1=\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_1}}2,\qquad
B_2=\frac{\overline{P_\varnothing}+\overline{Q_2}}2,\qquad
B_3=\frac{\overline{P_3}+\overline{Q_{123}}}2.
\]
Then
\[
B_i=\overline{U_i}\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{2}
\]
where the assertion is also valid if \(P_3=\varnothing\).  Consequently
\[
H:=
\operatorname{conv}(B_1\cup B_2\cup B_3)
=
\overline{\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2\cup U_3)}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, the closed and exact three-body hulls have the same
planar area and \(H\subseteq K\).

Put
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad
C=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2}),
\qquad \sigma=|C|.
\]
Let \(V(P,C)\) be planar mixed area in the normalization
\[
|P+C|=p+\sigma+2V(P,C).
\tag{4}
\]
This is well-defined because \(P\) and \(C\) are compact convex bodies
of positive area.  Define
\[
H_{12}=\operatorname{conv}(B_1\cup B_2),
\qquad
\Gamma=\left|H_{12}\cap R_{12}\right|.
\]
Then
\[
H_{12}=\frac{P+C}{2},
\qquad
|H|\ge |H_{12}|
=\frac{p+\sigma+2V(P,C)}4,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal R^\star_K
\ge
\frac38\bigl(p+\sigma+2V(P,C)\bigr)
+\frac72\Gamma.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]

It follows that either of the following gates excludes the branch:
\[
p+\sigma+2V(P,C)\ge4(M+m)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k\ge M+m,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\frac38\bigl(p+\sigma+2V(P,C)\bigr)+\frac72\Gamma
>
2p-\mathcal P^\star-\mathcal Q^\star
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W^\star>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The full hypotheses inherited from accepted fact
`1e269d4c02c94338` give \(W^\star=W=0\), so (8) is impossible.

Both gates are affine invariant.  Indeed, set

\mu=\frac mM,\qquad

Then (7) and (8) are, respectively,

\[
\frac38(\pi+\chi+2\nu)+\frac72\gamma

\tag{10}

In particular, if

\frac pM\ge1-\varepsilon,\qquad
\frac mM\le\mu_0,\qquad
\frac{\sigma}{M}\ge\chi_0,\qquad
\frac{V(P,C)}M\ge\nu_0,

and

\tag{11}
\]
then \(k/M\ge1+m/M\).  Thus (11) excludes any no-half scalar escape
with \(p/M\) near \(1\) and \(k/M<1+m/M\).

## Proof

We first prove the closure assertions, including the point omitted in the
rejected version.  If \(E,F\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) are bounded, then
\[
\overline{E+F}=\overline E+\overline F.
\tag{17}
\]
The right-hand side is compact, hence closed, and contains \(E+F\);
therefore it contains \(\overline{E+F}\).  Conversely, for
\(e\in\overline E\) and \(f\in\overline F\), choose
\(e_n\in E\) and \(f_n\in F\) with \(e_n\to e\) and \(f_n\to f\).
Then \(e_n+f_n\to e+f\), proving the reverse inclusion in (17).
Scaling commutes with closure, so (17) proves (2) for every nonempty
pair.  If \(P_3=\varnothing\), both \(U_3\) and \(B_3\) are empty, and
(2) remains literal.

More generally, for finitely many bounded sets \(E_1,\ldots,E_s\),
\[
\overline{\operatorname{conv}(E_1\cup\cdots\cup E_s)}
=
\operatorname{conv}(\overline E_1\cup\cdots\cup\overline E_s).
\tag{18}
\]
The right-hand side is compact and contains the exact finite hull, which
gives one inclusion.  For the reverse inclusion, write a point of the
right-hand side as a finite convex combination
\(\sum_j\lambda_j e_j\), with each \(e_j\) in some
\(\overline E_i\).  Approximate every \(e_j\) by a sequence from the
corresponding \(E_i\), keeping the coefficients \(\lambda_j\) fixed.
The resulting exact convex combinations converge to the original
point.  Applying (18) to \(U_1,U_2,U_3\), and then using (2), proves
(3).  A bounded planar convex set and its closure differ by a subset of
the boundary of its closure, which has planar area zero.  Thus the two
hulls in (3) have the same area.  Every exact \(U_i\) lies in
\((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\); since \(K\) is closed and convex, (2)--(3)
also give \(H\subseteq K\).

For arbitrary convex sets \(P,Q_1,Q_2\),
\[
\operatorname{conv}\bigl((P+Q_1)\cup(P+Q_2)\bigr)
=P+\operatorname{conv}(Q_1\cup Q_2).
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, a convex combination of \(p_1+q_1\) and \(p_2+q_2\) equals
\[
\bigl((1-\lambda)p_1+\lambda p_2\bigr)
+\bigl((1-\lambda)q_1+\lambda q_2\bigr)
\]
and belongs to the right-hand side.  Conversely,
\[
p+(1-\lambda)q_1+\lambda q_2
=(1-\lambda)(p+q_1)+\lambda(p+q_2)
\]
belongs to the left-hand side.  Applying (19) to the closed cells and
scaling by \(1/2\) gives the first identity in (5).  Formula (4) and
quadratic area scaling give the second identity.  The inclusions
\(H_{12}\subseteq H\subseteq K\) complete (5) and prove (7).

We next prove the charge bound geometrically.  Accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f` gives the exact singleton incidences
\[
U_1\subseteq R_1,\qquad U_2\subseteq R_2.
\]
For \(x\in P\) and
\(y\in\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2}\),
\[
2f_3\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)
=\bigl(f_3(x)-L_3\bigr)+\bigl(f_3(y)+L_3\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus, apart from null pieces on the three middle cutting lines,
\(H_{12}\) is partitioned only into \(R_1,R_2,R_{12}\).  The
\(R^\star\)-coefficients on those cells are \(3/2,3/2,5\).
Consequently the exact charge carried by \(H_{12}\) is
\[
\frac32\bigl(|H_{12}|-\Gamma\bigr)+5\Gamma
=\frac32|H_{12}|+\frac72\Gamma.
\tag{21}
\]
All coefficients in \(\mathcal R^\star_K\) are nonnegative, and
\(H_{12}\subseteq K\).  Combining (21) with (5) proves (6).

Under the full hypotheses assumed here, accepted fact
`1e269d4c02c94338` applies without any weakening and gives
\[
W^\star
=-2p+\mathcal P^\star+\mathcal Q^\star+\mathcal R^\star_K.
\tag{22}
\]
The same full hypotheses give \(W^\star=W=0\).  Combining (6) and
(22) proves (8).

Under any nonsingular affine change of the common plane, every ordinary
area in (7)--(10) is multiplied by the same absolute determinant.
The mixed area \(V(P,C)\) is multiplied by that determinant as well,
as follows by applying the change of variables to (4).  Hence the gates
and all six normalized quantities are affine invariant.  Division by
\(M>0\) proves (9)--(11).

It remains to verify the claimed calibration and neighborhood.  The
source polygons in accepted fact `70ae511e2c57d140` show that
\(C=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2})\) is the
trapezoid with vertices
\[
(-2(D+2),-D-4),\quad(2(D+2),-D-4),
\]
\[
(-(D+2),-D-2),\quad(D+2,-D-2).
\]
Its area is \(6(D+2)\), proving the first formula in (12).

Pruning the vertices of
\(\operatorname{conv}(B_1\cup B_2)\) from the same accepted polygon
lists gives the symmetric octagon with vertex pairs
\[
\left(\pm\frac{5D+7}{4},-\frac{D+9}{4}\right),\qquad
\left(\pm\frac{5D+9}{4},-\frac{D+7}{4}\right),
\]
\[
\left(\pm\frac{2D+5}{2},-2\right),\qquad
\left(\pm\frac{D+3}{2},-1\right).
\]
The shoelace formula gives (13).  Substitution of
\[
p_D=\frac{D^2+6D-3}{4},\qquad \sigma_D=6D+12
\]
and (13) into the mixed-area identity (5) gives
\[
V_D=D^2+3D+4,
\]
which completes (12).  Subtracting
\[
M_D+m_D=\frac{D^2+38D+73}{4}
\]
from (13) gives (14).  Its numerator is positive at \(D=28\) and
strictly increasing for \(D\ge28\).

Finally, the left side of (15) equals
\[
\frac{4(|H_{12}|-M_D-m_D)}{M_D}
=\frac{5D^2-98D-215}{(D+3)^2}.
\]
Subtracting \(1\) gives
\[
\frac{4(D^2-26D-56)}{(D+3)^2}\ge0
\qquad(D\ge28),
\]
with equality at \(D=28\).  The triangle inequality shows that (16)
changes the left side of (15) by less than \(1\), and therefore makes
the area gate (9) strict.
