## Statement

Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=(63/1940,-63/1940),&
E_0&=(97/105,0),\\
R_0&=(291/280,49/60),&
T_0&=(97/120,21/20),\\
F_0&=(0,14/15),&
N_0&=(-9/280,9/280),
\end{aligned}
\]
and define the rational polygon
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{L_0,E_0,R_0,T_0,F_0,N_0\},
\]
where the vertices are in counterclockwise order.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_2&=(0,-1/16),&
E_2&=(1/5,0),\\
R_2&=(9/40,7/40),&
T_2&=(7/40,9/40),\\
F_2&=(0,1/5),&
C_2&=(-1/16,0),\\

\end{aligned}
\]
and define the rational polygon
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}
\{D_2,E_2,R_2,T_2,F_2,C_2,S_2\},
\]
again in counterclockwise order.  With quadrant boundaries ignored
as null sets, these polygons satisfy
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&(+,+)&(-,+)&(+,-)&(-,-)\\ \hline
A_0&97/100&3/200&3/200&0\\
A_2&9/200&1/160&1/160&1/200.
\end{array}
\]
If
\[
P=A_0\cap\{x\geq0,y\geq0\},\qquad
Q=A_2\cap\{x\geq0,y\geq0\},
\]
then
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|=\frac{2293}{6400}
<\frac{29}{80}.
\]
Thus the complete hypotheses of accepted fact
`bc026780453747b8` hold, and accepted fact
`92a1b537d7e7876c` supplies its translated inner and outer
homothetic containments for this same pair \(P,Q\).

After normalizing the two full positive axis traces of \(P\), write
them as
\[
[a,A]\times\{0\},\qquad \{0\}\times[b,B].
\]
Then
\[
a=b=0,\qquad A=B=1,
\]
\[
(W,H)\notin P,
\]
and the unique rightmost and topmost points are
\[
R=(W,Y)=(9/8,7/8),\qquad
T=(X,H)=(7/8,9/8).
\]
In particular, \(X<A\) and \(Y<B\).

\]

column headed “vertices” gives all other listed vertices in the order
corresponding to the entries in the last column.  The positive
multiplier in the middle column only clears denominators.

We have now checked, before invoking any homothetic conclusion, every

It remains to check the support order.  The full positive axis traces
of \(P\) are
\[
[0,97/105]\times\{0\},\qquad
\{0\}\times[0,14/15].
\]
Apply the positive diagonal normalization
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac{105}{97}x,\frac{15}{14}y\right).
\]
It sends \(P\) to
\[
\widehat P=\operatorname{conv}
\{(0,0),(1,0),(9/8,7/8),(7/8,9/8),(0,1)\}.
\]
Thus the normalized traces are
\[
[a,A]\times\{0\}=[0,1]\times\{0\},\qquad
\{0\}\times[b,B]=\{0\}\times[0,1],
\]
so explicitly
\[
a=b=0,\qquad A=B=1.
\]
The point \((9/8,7/8)\) is the unique rightmost point of
\(\widehat P\), and \((7/8,9/8)\) is its unique topmost point.
Therefore \((W,H)=(9/8,9/8)\notin\widehat P\), while the only possible
support-point choices have
\[
X=Y=\frac78<1=A=B.
\]
This proves the claimed full Alternative 1 counterexample to the
transverse-support dichotomy.
