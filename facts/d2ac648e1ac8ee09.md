## Statement

Let
\[
a=\lambda_1,\qquad b=\lambda_2,\qquad c=\lambda_3,
\qquad s=b+c,\qquad p=bc,
\qquad R=a+s,
\]
and assume the zero-defect face
\[
\mathcal A_1:\qquad a\geq1+3s.
\tag{1}
\]
By accepted fact `edf57cbc053c4a13`, \(a\) is the unique dominant
coordinate, while \(b,c\) remain interchangeable.  Assume the accepted
three-cap constraints
\[
4abc\leq1+a+b+c,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\sqrt a+\sqrt b+\sqrt c
\leq\frac32\sqrt{1+a+b+c}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then (2) alone implies
\[
p<\frac12,
\tag{4}
\]
and it implies (3) strictly.  Thus (1)-(3) are equivalent to (1)-(2).
More precisely, for fixed \(b,c>0\), the full cap-compatible
\(\mathcal A_1\)-fiber is exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
a\in[\,1+3s,\infty),&0<p\leq\frac14,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle
a\in\left[
1+3s,\frac{1+s}{4p-1}
\right],
&\displaystyle
\frac14<p\leq
\frac{1+2s}{2(1+3s)}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
The second interval is empty when
\[
p>\frac{1+2s}{2(1+3s)}.
\]

Use the cap lower-bound functions of accepted fact
`6aa12d1b76699246`.  For \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), put
\[
A_k=\frac{1+R}{\lambda_k},
\qquad
c_k=2\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j},
\]
\[
d_{1k}=\frac{4A_kc_k^2}{(A_k+c_k^2)^2},
\qquad
d_{2k}
=1-\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}
\right)^2,
\]
\[
d_0=\min\left\{
1,\frac3{a^{-1}+b^{-1}+c^{-1}}
\right\},
\qquad
\delta=\min\{d_0,d_{11},d_{12},d_{13},d_{21},d_{22},d_{23}\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
\theta=\tau(R)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{1+R}+\sqrt{204+120R}\right)^2},
\qquad
\sigma=\sqrt{1-\theta},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\alpha=\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}
=\frac{\theta}{(1+\sigma)^2},
\qquad
z=\frac{4abc}{1+R}.
\tag{8}
\]
On the cap-compatible face (1)-(2), one has
\[
0<\theta<1,\qquad0<z\leq1.
\]
The closed survivor condition
\[
\delta\leq\tau(R)=\theta
\tag{9}
\]
is equivalent to the union of the following five exact cases:
\[
\mathsf H:\qquad
\frac3{a^{-1}+b^{-1}+c^{-1}}\leq\theta,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\mathsf P:\qquad z\leq\alpha,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
\mathsf E_k:\qquad
\sqrt{\frac{1+R}{\lambda_k}}
\geq
\sigma+2(1+\sigma)\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j},
\qquad k=1,2,3.
\tag{12}
\]
All equality boundaries in (10)-(12) are included.  These cases have the
following immediate exact consequences:
\[
\mathsf H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\min\{b,c\}\leq\theta,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\mathsf P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z\leq\alpha<\theta,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\mathsf E_k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z<\frac1{(1+\sigma)^2},
\tag{15}
\]
and the dominant endpoint-gap case satisfies the sharper minor-product
bound
\[
\mathsf E_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p<
\left(
\frac{\sqrt2-\sigma}{2(1+\sigma)}
\right)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

Every \(A_2\)-geometry on this face belongs to one of the eight exact
endpoint-pair strata of accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8`.  Consequently
(10)-(12), crossed with those eight strata, form an exhaustive finite
cover by at most \(5\cdot8=40\) analytic common-plane cases.  The cover
includes zero limiting side gaps, coincident chord endpoints, vanishing
normalized cap areas, \(D=0\) limits, collinear labels, redundant labels,
and every equality in (1), (2), and (9)-(12).

Now restrict to full common-plane positive-middle \(W=0\) realizations
satisfying the hypotheses of accepted facts `0009896ebff12f31` and
`084bf2f472bf29c7`, whose ratios lie on (1) and satisfy (9).  There exists
a constant
\[
\kappa_{\mathcal A}>0
\tag{17}
\]
such that every such realization satisfies
\[
\eta\geq\kappa_{\mathcal A}q.
\tag{18}
\]
If the class is empty, any positive constant is admissible.  Otherwise
\(\kappa_{\mathcal A}\) may be taken as its positive infimum of
\(\eta/q\).

Define the corresponding strengthened threshold
\[
\tau_{\mathcal A}(R)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+
\sqrt{204+120R+28\kappa_{\mathcal A}}
\right)^2}.
\tag{19}
\]
Then every actual realization on the face satisfies the strict
common-plane restriction
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\delta<\tau_{\mathcal A}(R)<\tau(R).
\qquad}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular, the exact ratio region
\[
\tau_{\mathcal A}(R)\leq\delta\leq\tau(R)
\tag{21}
\]
contains no such \(W=0\) realization.  Moreover, no one of the forty
cases above supports a sequence with
\[
\frac{r_{123}}q\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\eta}q\longrightarrow0.
\tag{22}
\]

## Proof

We first solve the three-cap constraints exactly on \(\mathcal A_1\).
Equation (2) is equivalent to
\[
a(4p-1)\leq1+s.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(p\geq1/2\), then \(4p-1\geq1\), so (23) gives
\[
a\leq1+s,
\]
contrary to \(a\geq1+3s>1+s\).  This proves (4).

If \(p\leq1/4\), the left side of (23) is nonpositive, so (23) holds for
every \(a\geq1+3s\).  If \(p>1/4\), equation (23) is exactly
\[
a\leq\frac{1+s}{4p-1}.
\]
This upper bound is compatible with (1) exactly when
\[
(1+3s)(4p-1)\leq1+s,
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
p\leq\frac{1+2s}{2(1+3s)}.
\]
This proves the fiber description (5), including its boundary cases.

We next prove that (3) is automatic and strict.  Put
\[
u=\sqrt a,\qquad d=\sqrt b+\sqrt c,\qquad r=\sqrt p.
\]
Because both sides of (3) are positive, squaring shows that (3) is
equivalent to
\[
Q(u):=
5u^2-8du+9+5s-8r\geq0.
\tag{24}
\]
On the face (1),
\[
u\geq u_0:=\sqrt{1+3s}.
\]
Also \(d\leq\sqrt{2s}\), so
\[
Q'(u)=10u-8d
\geq10\sqrt{1+3s}-8\sqrt{2s}>0.
\tag{25}
\]
The last inequality follows after squaring from
\[
100(1+3s)>128s.
\]
Thus \(Q\) is strictly increasing on the allowed ray and it is enough to
bound \(Q(u_0)\).

Using
\[
d^2=s+2r,\qquad u_0^2=1+3s,
\]
the elementary inequality
\[
8du_0\leq4d^2+4u_0^2
=4+16s+8r
\]
gives
\[
Q(u_0)
\geq10+4s-16r.
\]
Since \(s=b+c\geq2\sqrt{bc}=2r\), and (4) gives
\(r<1/\sqrt2\),
\[
Q(u_0)
\geq10-8r
>
10-4\sqrt2
>0.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (24)-(26) prove (3) strictly for every cap-compatible point of
\(\mathcal A_1\).  This proves the first part of the statement.

We now derive the exact five-case survivor cover.  Since \(R>1\),
equation (7) gives
\[
0<\theta<\frac{49}{225}<1.
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(\theta<1\), the condition \(d_0\leq\theta\) is equivalent to
\(\mathsf H\).

For every \(k\),
\[
\frac{c_k^2}{A_k}
=
\frac{4\lambda_i\lambda_j\lambda_k}{1+R}
=z.
\tag{28}
\]
Therefore all three \(d_{1k}\) are the same number:
\[
d_{1k}=\frac{4z}{(1+z)^2}.
\tag{29}
\]
The product inequality (2) says exactly \(z\leq1\).  On \(0<z\leq1\),
solving
\[
\frac{4z}{(1+z)^2}\leq\theta
\]
gives
\[
z\leq
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\theta}}{1+\sqrt{1-\theta}}
=\alpha.
\]
Thus any one of the three inequalities \(d_{1k}\leq\theta\) is
equivalent to the single case \(\mathsf P\).

Finally, put
\[
y_k=\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}.
\]
Since \(0<\theta<1\),
\[
d_{2k}\leq\theta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{clip}(y_k)\geq\sqrt{1-\theta}=\sigma
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y_k\geq\sigma.
\]
The last inequality is exactly \(\mathsf E_k\).  Since \(\delta\) is the
minimum of \(d_0\), the three equal \(d_{1k}\), and the three \(d_{2k}\),
this proves the exact equivalence of (9) with the five cases
(10)-(12).

For the consequences, \(\mathsf H\) gives
\[
a^{-1}+b^{-1}+c^{-1}\geq\frac3\theta.
\]
At least one coordinate is therefore at most \(\theta\).  Equations
(1) and (27) give \(a>1>\theta\), so that coordinate is one of \(b,c\),
proving (13).  Equation (14) follows from
\[
\alpha=\frac{\theta}{(1+\sigma)^2}<\theta.
\]

In case \(\mathsf E_k\),
\[
\sqrt{A_k}
\geq
\sigma+(1+\sigma)c_k
>
(1+\sigma)c_k.
\]
Using (28) gives (15).  In case \(\mathsf E_1\), equations (1) and
\(s>0\) give
\[
\sqrt{A_1}
=
\sqrt{1+\frac{1+s}{a}}
\leq
\sqrt{1+\frac{1+s}{1+3s}}
<\sqrt2.
\]
Since \(c_1=2\sqrt p\), the \(\mathsf E_1\) inequality now yields
\[
\sigma+2(1+\sigma)\sqrt p<\sqrt2,
\]
which is exactly (16).

Accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8` supplies the eight endpoint-pair strata
and a complete polynomial convex-order system on each.  Crossing that
finite list with the exact five-case cover just proved gives the asserted
forty case combinations.  The same accepted normal form explicitly
allows coincident endpoints, collinear and redundant labels, and every
weak convex-order equality.  The compact normalization in accepted fact
`444fb905d0cc5122` permits \(L_i,D,D\lambda_i\) to tend to zero, so the
listed boundary regimes are retained rather than divided away.

It remains to use the common plane.  Suppose (18) were false.  Then there
would be a sequence of realizations in the stated class with
\[
\frac{\eta_n}{q_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Because
\[
\eta_n\geq6r_{123,n},
\]
the same sequence would satisfy
\[
\frac{r_{123,n}}{q_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is excluded by accepted fact `444fb905d0cc5122`, which covers every
unequal-cap endpoint stratum and all unbounded or vanishing ratio limits.
Thus the positive constant in (17)-(18) exists.  The same accepted fact
also proves directly that no one of the forty cases supports a sequence
as in (22).

Finally put
\[
D=\frac q{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
Accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246` gives
\[
D\geq\delta.
\tag{30}
\]
Apply the strengthened scalar bridge of accepted fact
`084bf2f472bf29c7` with the exact upper bound
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|=\frac qD.
\]
Using (18) and \(r_{123}>0\), that bridge gives
\[
\frac qD
\geq
\left(
2\sqrt{r_{123}}
+
\frac{
\sqrt{q(1+R)}
+
\sqrt{120q(1+R)+84q+28\eta}
}{7}
\right)^2
>
\frac q{49}
\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+
\sqrt{204+120R+28\kappa_{\mathcal A}}
\right)^2
=\frac q{\tau_{\mathcal A}(R)}.
\tag{31}
\]
Hence
\[
D<\tau_{\mathcal A}(R).
\]
Combining this with (30) proves the first strict inequality in (20).  The
second follows from \(\kappa_{\mathcal A}>0\) by comparing the positive
denominators in (7) and (19).  This proves (20)-(21) and completes the
exhaustive face theorem.
