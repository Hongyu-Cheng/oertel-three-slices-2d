## Statement

Assume the normalized original \(A_2\)-geometry of accepted facts
`c49613ac26c0c4a8` and `d0a445c133525448`:
\[
q=1,\qquad
C_i=A_2\cap\{\alpha_i\leq0\},\qquad
|C_i|=\frac5{21}\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1,
\]
the global cutting triangle is affinely normalized to the barycentric
triangle
\[
\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\geq0\},
\]
the three cutting lines are pairwise nonparallel, \(A_2\) is a
two-dimensional convex polygon, the central cell has physical area \(1\),
and the cap interiors are pairwise disjoint.  Let \(L_i>0\) be the affine
side fraction of the
actual cutting-base chord
\[
C_i\cap\{\alpha_i=0\}
\]
in the corresponding side of \(\Delta\).  Thus either of the other two
barycentric coordinates varies by exactly \(L_i\) between the two endpoints
of this chord.  Put
\[
S_Q=C_1+C_2+C_3,
\qquad
\mathcal V(A,B)=\frac{|A+B|-|A|-|B|}{2}.
\]
Then, for every distinct \(i,j\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal V(C_i,C_j)
\geq
\max\left\{\frac5{21},\frac{L_iL_j}{D}\right\}.
\quad}
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\quad
|S_Q|
\geq
\frac57+
2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq3}
\max\left\{\frac5{21},\frac{L_iL_j}{D}\right\}.
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Equivalently, the original-cap Brunn--Minkowski deficit satisfies
\[
\sqrt{|S_Q|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}
\geq
\sqrt{
\frac57+
2\sum_{i<j}
\max\left\{\frac5{21},\frac{L_iL_j}{D}\right\}}
-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, (1) is strictly stronger than ordinary Minkowski's inequality
for the pair \((i,j)\) whenever
\[
L_iL_j>\frac{5D}{21}.
\tag{4}
\]
If the actual cap-sum deficit vanishes, then accepted fact
`d0a445c133525448` gives
\[
D\geq
\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
>\frac19.
\tag{5}
\]
All assertions use the original caps.  They include every
endpoint-coincidence stratum, every collinear or redundant boundary label,
and every weak cutting-line assignment allowed by accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`.

## Proof

Use the original barycentric normalization of accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`, without applying its endpoint-preserving boundary
reduction.  In the coordinate plane
\[
x=\alpha_1,\qquad y=\alpha_2,\qquad \alpha_3=1-x-y,
\]
the normalized cutting triangle has coordinate area \(1/2\).  Its physical
area is \(1/D\), so the common affine map from coordinate space to the
physical plane has area multiplier
\[
J=\frac{2}{D}.
\tag{6}
\]
The coordinate image \(\widehat C_i\) of each original cap therefore has
area
\[
|\widehat C_i|
=\frac{|C_i|}{J}
=\frac{5D}{42}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(i\ne j\).  We first prove the base-edge estimate in coordinate
space.  For a convex polygon \(A\) and a convex body \(B\) containing the
origin, the polygonal edge-support formula is
\[
2\mathcal V_{\rm coord}(A,B)
=
\sum_{e\subset\partial A}|e|\,h_B(n_e),
\tag{8}
\]
where \(n_e\) is the outward unit normal to \(e\).  Formula (8) follows by
moving each supporting line of \(A\) outward by
\(t h_B(n_e)\): the derivative at \(t=0\) of
\(|A+tB|\) is the displayed sum, while
\[
|A+tB|
=|A|+2t\mathcal V_{\rm coord}(A,B)+t^2|B|.
\]

Choose a point \(z\in\widehat C_j\) minimizing \(\alpha_i\), and replace
\(\widehat C_j\) by \(\widehat C_j-z\).  Mixed area is translation
invariant, and the translated body contains the origin.  Hence every term
on the right side of (8) is nonnegative.  The support value in the outward
normal of the cutting-base edge of \(\widehat C_i\) is the
\(\alpha_i\)-width of \(\widehat C_j\), with the Euclidean normalization of
the normal included.

The resulting contribution of that one edge is exactly
\[
L_i\,\operatorname{width}_{\alpha_i}(\widehat C_j).
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, for \(i=1\) or \(i=2\), the base edge has Euclidean length \(L_i\)
and its outward unit normal is the corresponding coordinate unit normal.
For \(i=3\), the base edge has direction \((1,-1)\), hence Euclidean length
\(\sqrt2L_i\), while the unit normal in the direction of increasing
\(\alpha_3=1-x-y\) divides the \(\alpha_3\)-support value by \(\sqrt2\).
The two factors cancel and again give (9).

The base chord of \(\widehat C_j\) lies on \(\alpha_j=0\).  Along that side,
the two remaining barycentric coordinates sum to \(1\); by the definition
of \(L_j\), each of them varies by exactly \(L_j\) between the chord
endpoints.  Since \(i\ne j\),
\[
\operatorname{width}_{\alpha_i}(\widehat C_j)\geq L_j.
\tag{10}
\]
Dropping all other nonnegative edge terms in (8) and using (9)--(10) gives
\[
\mathcal V_{\rm coord}(\widehat C_i,\widehat C_j)
\geq\frac{L_iL_j}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]

The planar Minkowski inequality gives, independently,
\[
\mathcal V_{\rm coord}(\widehat C_i,\widehat C_j)
\geq
\sqrt{|\widehat C_i|\,|\widehat C_j|}
=\frac{5D}{42}.
\tag{12}
\]
Mixed area is multiplied by the same determinant \(J\) under a common
affine map.  Multiplying the maximum of (11) and (12) by \(J=2/D\) proves
\[
\mathcal V(C_i,C_j)
\geq
\max\left\{
\frac{2}{D}\frac{5D}{42},
\frac{2}{D}\frac{L_iL_j}{2}
\right\}
=
\max\left\{\frac5{21},\frac{L_iL_j}{D}\right\},
\]
which is (1).

Mixed area is additive in either Minkowski argument.  Equivalently, expanding
the planar area polynomial for three summands gives
\[
|C_1+C_2+C_3|
=
\sum_i|C_i|+2\sum_{i<j}\mathcal V(C_i,C_j).
\tag{13}
\]
Substitution of \(|C_i|=5/21\) and (1) proves (2), and taking square roots
proves (3).  Condition (4) is exactly the condition under which the second
entry in the maximum in (1) is strictly larger than the first.

If the actual deficit on the left side of (3) is zero, then
\(|S_Q|=9(5/21)\).  Since each pairwise mixed area is at least \(5/21\),
(13) forces equality in all three pairwise Minkowski inequalities.  The
accepted original-cap equality classification `d0a445c133525448` applies
and yields (5).

Finally, positive cap area makes each actual cutting-base chord
nondegenerate, so \(L_i>0\).  The proof never divides by a chord-endpoint
parameter, never changes an original cap, and depends only on its actual
base edge and width.  Coincident endpoints belonging to different cutting
lines, collinear or redundant polygon vertices, and weak assignments on the
null cutting lines therefore leave every step unchanged.  This proves the
claimed endpoint coverage.
