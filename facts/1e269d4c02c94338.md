## Statement

Assume the common affine-plane and exact-cell hypotheses of accepted
facts `0e51a47a9add571f` and `91cb5bfc71da49ac`, relabeled so that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,
\]
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,\qquad
\{H:q_H>0\}=\{123,1,2,12\}.
\]
Use the notation and all the finite-interior, crossing, canonical-tail,
and \(W=0\) hypotheses of accepted fact `05153059dfd604c8`.  Thus
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h:=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,\qquad
c_i+m\ge h,\qquad c_i+M\ge h,
\tag{1}
\]
and, writing
\[
p=p_\varnothing,\qquad
q=q_{123},\qquad a=q_1,\qquad b=q_2,\qquad
\gamma=q_{12},
\]
all five displayed masses are positive and
\[
m=q+a+b+\gamma.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume also the actual-counterexample hypotheses used in accepted fact
`e51585de76810353`, so in particular
\[
\min\{q,m-a,m-b\}<\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(g_0,g_2\) be the area centroids of \(A_0,A_2\), and put
\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},\qquad
\mathcal B=\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
f_r(g_0)>L_r\qquad(r=1,2,3),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
f_3(z)>0,
\tag{6}
\]
and there is an index \(i\in\{1,2\}\) for which
\[
f_i(z)<-\frac12,\qquad
f_i(g_2)+L_i<-1,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
v_i>\frac{4m}{9},\qquad c_i>\mathcal B.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular the same canonical row satisfies the strict common-level
bound
\[
h=u_i+v_i+c_i>
\frac{4m}{9}+\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{9}.
\tag{8a}
\]
In the uncharged third direction one has the complementary strict
bound
\[
k-c_3>\mathcal B.
\tag{9}
\]

Define
\[
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123}
\tag{10}
\]
and let \(\mathcal R^\star\) be the complete \(R\)-cell contribution to
\[
W^\star=W+\frac12D_1+\frac12D_2-D_3,
\qquad D_r=u_r+v_r+c_r.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal R^\star
=\frac32(r_1+r_2)+5r_{12}
+\frac72(r_{13}+r_{23})+7r_{123}
=\frac32(k-c_3)+\frac72E_R.
\tag{11}
\]
For the bridge \(\Gamma_{12}\) of accepted fact
`d3afd91c459bcce8`,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal R^\star
>
\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{6}
+\frac72\Gamma_{12}.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]
With the notation
\(\Delta,\alpha,\Lambda,\lambda\) of accepted fact
`f1fcc678305bbd1c`, this also gives
\[
\mathcal R^\star
>
\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{6}
+
\frac{7p\Lambda}
{2\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})}.
\tag{13}
\]

Finally put
\[
\mathcal P^\star
=\frac32(p_1+p_2)+5p_{12}
+\frac72(p_{13}+p_{23}),
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\mathcal Q^\star
=\frac32(a+b)+5\gamma+7q.
\tag{15}
\]
The exact endpoint/middle decomposition is
\[
W^\star=-2p+\mathcal P^\star+\mathcal Q^\star
+\mathcal R^\star.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently the remaining endpoint comparison
\[
\boxed{\quad
2p\le
\mathcal P^\star+\mathcal Q^\star
+\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{6}
+\frac52\Gamma_{12}
\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
would imply \(W^\star>\Gamma_{12}\).  Under the three crossing
equalities \(W^\star=W=0\), so (17) would close the registered
single-double branch.  The centroid-cover mechanism proves (5)--(13);
it does not by itself prove (17).

## Proof

The left canonical tail in (1), combined with the crossing in the same
row, gives
\[
u_r+v_r\le m\qquad(r=1,2,3).
\tag{18}
\]
Finite interior gives \(v_r>0\), hence \(u_r<m\).  Accepted fact
`05153059dfd604c8` gives
\[
\frac pm>\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}>\frac94.
\]
Since \(p\le M\), it follows that
\[
u_r<m<\frac{4M}{9}.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(f_r(g_0)\le L_r\), the closed lower cap of \(A_0\) in row \(r\)
contains its centroid.  The planar centroid inequality recorded in
accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` would give
\[
u_r\ge\frac{4M}{9},
\]
contrary to (19).  This proves (5).

Write
\[
\eta_r=f_r+L_r.
\]
For any \(r\) with \(f_r(z)\le0\), affinity and (5) give
\[
\eta_r(g_2)
=2f_r(z)-\bigl(f_r(g_0)-L_r\bigr)<0.
\tag{20}
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(f_3(z)\le0\).  Then
\(\eta_3(g_2)<0\).  The centroid \(g_2\) is an interior point of the
two-dimensional convex body \(A_2\).  We claim that
\[
\eta_1(g_2)<0,\qquad\eta_2(g_2)<0.
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, if \(\eta_1(g_2)\ge0\), a sufficiently small disk about \(g_2\)
inside \(A_2\) contains a positive-area open subset on which
\(\eta_1>0\) and \(\eta_3<0\).  Apart from the null line
\(\{\eta_2=0\}\), that subset lies in \(Q_3\cup Q_{23}\).
Both cells have zero area under the exact support
\(\{123,1,2,12\}\), a contradiction.  Thus
\(\eta_1(g_2)<0\); the same argument using
\(Q_3\cup Q_{13}\) proves the second inequality in (21).

Equations (20)--(21) put \(g_2\) strictly inside all three lower
endpoint halfplanes.  Translating each cutting line parallel until it
passes through \(g_2\), applying the centroid inequality, and then
using the positive strip between the two lines gives
\[
v_1>\frac{4m}{9},\qquad
v_2>\frac{4m}{9},\qquad
v_3>\frac{4m}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]
In the exact support (2),
\[
v_1=m-b,\qquad v_2=m-a,\qquad v_3=q.
\]
Thus (22) contradicts (3).  This proves (6).

Now
\[
f_1(z)+f_2(z)=-1-f_3(z)<-1.
\]
Hence at least one \(i\in\{1,2\}\) satisfies
\[
f_i(z)<-\frac12.
\tag{23}
\]
For this \(i\), equation (20) sharpens to
\[
f_i(g_2)+L_i
<2f_i(z)<-1,
\]
which proves (7).  The same translated-centroid-line argument used in
(22) gives the first inequality in (8).

We next derive the middle cap bounds without identifying \(z\) as the
centroid of any Minkowski sum.  Choose
\(\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_2>0\) with
\[
\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_2<-2f_i(z)
\]
and put
\[
C_s=A_s\cap
\{f_i\le f_i(g_s)+\varepsilon_s\},
\qquad s=0,2.
\]
Each \(C_s\) has area strictly larger than \(4|A_s|/9\): its
centroidal subcap has area at least \(4|A_s|/9\), and the positive
parallel displacement adds a positive-area strip unless the enlarged
cap is already all of \(A_s\), in which case strictness is immediate.
For \(x_s\in C_s\),
\[
2f_i\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
\le 2f_i(z)+\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_2<0.
\]
Compatibility therefore gives
\[
\frac{C_0+C_2}{2}\subseteq K\cap\{f_i<0\}.
\]
The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality recorded in accepted fact
`d7bdfc054d5093e8` yields
\[
c_i
\ge\left|\frac{C_0+C_2}{2}\right|
\ge\frac{(\sqrt{|C_0|}+\sqrt{|C_2|})^2}{4}
>\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{9}
=\mathcal B.
\]
This proves the second inequality in (8).
Adding the first inequality in (8), using \(u_i\ge0\), and invoking the
crossing equality in row \(i\) proves (8a).

Apply the same argument to the upper centroid caps in row \(3\).
Choose \(\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_2>0\) with
\(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_2<2f_3(z)\) and set
\[
D_s=A_s\cap
\{f_3\ge f_3(g_s)-\varepsilon_s\}.
\]
Their areas are strictly larger than \(4|A_s|/9\), and
\[
\frac{D_0+D_2}{2}\subseteq K\cap\{f_3>0\}.
\]
Brunn--Minkowski now gives
\[
|K\cap\{f_3>0\}|>\mathcal B.
\]
The cutting line has area zero, so
\[
|K\cap\{f_3>0\}|=k-c_3,
\]
proving (9).

For a cell indexed by \(G\), its coefficient in \(W^\star\) is
\[
3|G|-2+\frac12\mathbf1_{\{1\in G\}}
+\frac12\mathbf1_{\{2\in G\}}
-\mathbf1_{\{3\in G\}}.
\]
In the index order
\[
\varnothing,1,2,3,12,13,23,123
\]
the coefficients are
\[
-2,\quad\frac32,\quad\frac32,\quad0,\quad
5,\quad\frac72,\quad\frac72,\quad7.
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(R_\varnothing\) is empty and
\[
k-c_3=r_1+r_2+r_{12},
\]
equation (24) gives the two equal expressions in (11).  Accepted fact
`d3afd91c459bcce8` gives a set of area \(\Gamma_{12}\) contained in
\(R_{12}\), so
\[
E_R\ge r_{12}\ge\Gamma_{12}.
\]
Combining this with (9) and (11) proves (12).  The strict quantitative
bridge estimate in accepted fact `f1fcc678305bbd1c` is
\[
\Gamma_{12}>
\frac{p\Lambda}
{\Delta\lambda(1+\sqrt{1-\alpha})},
\]
and substitution into (12) proves (13).

Finally, applying the same coefficient row (24) to the \(P\)- and
\(Q\)-families, using \(P_{123}=Q_\varnothing=\varnothing\) and the
exact \(Q\)-support, gives (14)--(16).  Combining (12) and (16) gives
\[
W^\star>
-2p+\mathcal P^\star+\mathcal Q^\star
+\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2}{6}
+\frac72\Gamma_{12}.
\]
Under (17), the right-hand side is at least \(\Gamma_{12}\), proving
the asserted reduction.  The three crossings make
\(\frac12D_1+\frac12D_2-D_3=0\), and \(W=0\) is assumed, so
\(W^\star=0\).  Since accepted fact `d3afd91c459bcce8` gives
\(\Gamma_{12}>0\), proving (17) would therefore contradict the
single-double branch.
