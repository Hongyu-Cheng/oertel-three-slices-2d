## Statement

Let \(v:[0,1]\to(0,\infty)\) be nondecreasing and concave, with its
endpoint values equal to its one-sided limits.  Fix
\[
0<\lambda<r<1,
\]
and assume the left clipping point is the endpoint
\[
\alpha=0,\qquad a:=v(0)\geq\lambda.
\tag{1}
\]
Define the right clipping point by one of the two alternatives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(R_i)}&
\lambda<\beta<1,\qquad v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda,\\
\mathrm{(R_1)}&
\beta=1,\qquad v(1)\geq1-\lambda.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,
&
R&=\int_0^\lambda v(u)\,du,\\
T&=\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du,
&
C&=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2}{2},\\
J&=C+T,
&
A&=I+9J,\\
K&=5J+8R-3I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
A<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, for the exact crossing and contact ratios
\[
p=\frac12+\frac{J}{2I},
\qquad
q(s)=\frac{\int_0^s v(u)\,du}{I},
\tag{5}
\]
one has
\[
p<\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q(r)>p,\qquad 4p+5q(r)>4.
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

We first audit the endpoint representatives and clipping points.  A
finite concave function on \((0,1)\) has finite one-sided limits.
Changing isolated endpoint values to those limits changes none of the
integrals in (3) or (5).  We use this canonical representative.  If a
noncanonical width was initially given, its clipping alternative is
recomputed from the canonical endpoint value.

The function
\[
f(u)=v(u)+u-\lambda
\]
is strictly increasing.  Assumption (1) gives \(f(0)\geq0\), so the
left clipping point is indeed \(0\), including equality
\(v(0)=\lambda\).

On the right, let
\[
g(u)=v(u)-u+\lambda.
\]
This function is concave and \(g(\lambda)=v(\lambda)>0\).  If
\(g(1)\geq0\), concavity gives \(g\geq0\) throughout
\([\lambda,1]\), and the right clipping point is \(\beta=1\).  This
includes the weak boundary \(v(1)=1-\lambda\).  If \(g(1)<0\), its
nonnegative superlevel set in \([\lambda,1]\) is a closed interval
\[
\{u:g(u)\geq0\}=[\lambda,\beta]
\]
for some \(\lambda<\beta<1\).  Continuity gives \(g(\beta)=0\).
Concavity between \(g(\lambda)>0\) and \(g(\beta)=0\) makes \(g>0\)
on \((\lambda,\beta)\); the secant-slope inequality makes \(g<0\) on
\((\beta,1]\).  Thus this \(\beta\) is unique and satisfies the first
alternative in (2).

These sign facts also verify the crossing integral.  The clipped
integrand
\[
\max\{-v(u),\min\{u-\lambda,v(u)\}\}
\]
equals \(u-\lambda\) on \([0,\beta]\) and \(v(u)\) on
\([\beta,1]\), apart from irrelevant endpoints.  Hence its integral
is
\[
\int_0^\beta(u-\lambda)\,du+\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du
=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2}{2}+T
=J.
\tag{7}
\]

We now prove (4), first in the full-right case \(\beta=1\).  Here
\[
T=0,\qquad
J=\frac{(1-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2}{2}
=\frac12-\lambda.
\tag{8}
\]
Put
\[
\delta=\lambda-\frac12.
\]
The premise \(A<0\) becomes
\[
I<9\delta.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(I>0\), this implies \(\delta>0\).

Write
\[
w(u)=v(u)-a.
\]
The function \(w\) is nonnegative and concave, with \(w(0)=0\).
Therefore, for every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
w(\lambda x)
\geq\lambda w(x)+(1-\lambda)w(0)
=\lambda w(x).
\tag{10}
\]
Changing variables in \(R\), using (1), and then using (10), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R
&=a\lambda+\lambda\int_0^1w(\lambda x)\,dx\\
&\geq a\lambda+\lambda^2\int_0^1w(x)\,dx\\
&=\lambda^2I+a\lambda(1-\lambda)\\
&\geq\lambda^2I+\lambda^2(1-\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (8) and (11),
\[
K\geq(8\lambda^2-3)I
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta.
\tag{12}
\]

If \(8\lambda^2-3\geq0\), then \(I\geq a\geq\lambda\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
K
&\geq(8\lambda^2-3)\lambda
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta\\
&=8\lambda^2-8\lambda+\frac52\\
&=8\left(\lambda-\frac12\right)^2+\frac12>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

If \(8\lambda^2-3<0\), the strict upper bound (9) reverses after
multiplication by this negative coefficient.  Equation (12) gives
\[
K>4(16\lambda^3-7\lambda^2-8\lambda+4).
\tag{14}
\]
For \(y=2\lambda-1\in(0,1)\), the cubic in (14) is
\[
2y^3+\frac{17}{4}y^2-\frac32y+\frac14.
\]
Its quadratic part has positive leading coefficient and discriminant
\[
\left(-\frac32\right)^2
{}-4\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)\left(\frac14\right)
=-2<0.
\]
The quadratic and \(2y^3\) are both strictly positive.  Thus (14)
also gives \(K>0\).  This proves (4) when \(\beta=1\), without using
the right endpoint inequality beyond identifying the clipping cell.

Now suppose \(\lambda<\beta<1\).  Truncate at \(\beta\) by setting
\[
I_c=I-T,\qquad J_c=J-T=C.
\]
The integral \(R\) is unchanged, and direct substitution gives
\[
A=(I_c+9J_c)+10T,
\qquad
K=(5J_c+8R-3I_c)+2T.
\tag{15}
\]
Define the isotropically rescaled width
\[
\widehat v(u)=\frac{v(\beta u)}{\beta},
\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{\beta}.
\tag{16}
\]
It is again positive, nondecreasing, and concave.  Its left clipping
point is \(0\), because
\[
\widehat v(0)=\frac a\beta
\geq\frac\lambda\beta=\widehat\lambda,
\]
and the interior right clipping equality becomes
\[
\widehat v(1)=1-\widehat\lambda.
\]
All area quantities scale by \(\beta^{-2}\):
\[
\widehat I=\frac{I_c}{\beta^2},\qquad
\widehat R=\frac{R}{\beta^2},\qquad
\widehat J=\frac{J_c}{\beta^2}.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(A<0\), then
\[
\widehat I+9\widehat J
=\frac{A-10T}{\beta^2}<0.
\]
The full-right result applied to \(\widehat v\) gives
\[
5J_c+8R-3I_c>0.
\]
Equation (15), together with \(T>0\), proves \(K>0\).  At the full
boundary \(\beta=1\), \(T=0\) and the direct proof above applies, so
strictness is not lost at the transition.

Finally, (5) gives the exact identities
\[
p-\frac49=\frac{A}{18I},
\qquad
5p+4q(\lambda)-4=\frac{K}{2I}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(p<4/9\) implies \(K>0\) and
\[
5p+4q(\lambda)>4.
\]
If \(q(\lambda)\leq p\), the left side would be at most
\(9p<4\), a contradiction.  Therefore
\[
q(\lambda)>p,
\]
and
\[
4p+5q(\lambda)
=\bigl(5p+4q(\lambda)\bigr)+\bigl(q(\lambda)-p\bigr)>4.
\]
Since \(v>0\) and \(r>\lambda\),
\[
q(r)-q(\lambda)
=\frac{\int_\lambda^r v(u)\,du}{I}>0.
\]
This proves (6).  The maximal exact class established here is all
positive nondecreasing concave canonical widths with
\(\alpha=0\), for both right clipping alternatives and all weak/full
right boundaries.
