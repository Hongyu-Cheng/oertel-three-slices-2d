## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a compact polytope such that
\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\]
where the \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes. Suppose
\[
A_1=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\]
and suppose \(A_0,A_2\) are centrally symmetric about their respective centroids \(g_0,g_2\). Set
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}.
\]
Then \(A_1\) is centrally symmetric with centroid \(g_1=z\). If some, necessarily unique, \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\) satisfies \(a_k\ge T/2\), define
\[
y^*=(k,g_k).
\]
If instead \(a_i<T/2\) for every \(i\), define
\[
y^*=(1,z).
\]
In both cases \(y^*\in S\), and
\[
h_S(y^*)\ge\frac{2T}{9},
\]
where
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\left\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y
\right\}.
\]

## Proof

Write
\[
K_i=A_i-g_i\qquad(i=0,2).
\]
Central symmetry gives \(K_i=-K_i\). With
\[
K_1=\frac{K_0+K_2}{2},
\]
the minimal-family identity becomes
\[
A_1=z+K_1.
\]
Since \(K_1=-K_1\), the body \(A_1\) is centrally symmetric about \(z\). The change of variables \(x\mapsto-x\) gives
\[
\int_{K_1}x\,dx=-\int_{K_1}x\,dx=0,
\]
so its centroid is \(g_1=z\). In particular, all three points \(g_i\) belong to their respective slices.

First suppose \(a_k\ge T/2\) for some \(k\). Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` applies to the \(k\)-th slice centroid and gives
\[
h_S(k,g_k)\ge\frac49a_k
\ge\frac49\frac T2
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
This treats every closed halfspace, including tilted and slice-parallel ones, in the large-slice regime.

Now suppose
\[
a_0,a_1,a_2<\frac T2.
\]
Set
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
and take \(y^*=(1,z)\).

Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` shows that every closed halfspace containing \(y^*\) contains a parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through \(y^*\), and the latter has no larger \(S\)-mass. It therefore suffices to check every boundary-through-\(y^*\) halfspace. The same accepted fact separates these into slice-parallel and tilted halfspaces.

For the two slice-parallel choices, closedness includes the entire middle slice, and the exact masses are
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap\{x\le1\})=a_0+a_1=T-a_2>\frac T2
\]
and
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap\{x\ge1\})=a_1+a_2=T-a_0>\frac T2.
\]
Both are greater than \(2T/9\).

Fix a tilted halfspace through \((1,z)\). By the exact parametrization in accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, there are \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) such that its section on slice \(i\) is
\[
A_i\cap\{w:\langle q,w-z\rangle\le\lambda(1-i)\}.
\]
Put
\[
d=\langle q,g_0-z\rangle,\qquad \tau=\lambda-d.
\]
Because \(g_2-z=-(g_0-z)\), writing points of the outer slices as \(g_i+x\) gives the exact outer cap conditions
\[
x\in K_0,\quad \langle q,x\rangle\le\tau,
\]
and
\[
x\in K_2,\quad \langle q,x\rangle\le-\tau.
\]
For \(i=0,2\), define
\[
F_i(t)=\left|K_i\cap\{x:\langle q,x\rangle\le t\}\right|.
\]
Since \(K_i=-K_i\), reflection \(x\mapsto-x\) interchanges the two halfplanes bounded by \(\langle q,x\rangle=0\). That line has planar area zero, so
\[
F_i(0)=\frac{a_i}{2}.
\]
Similarly, the middle section is the halfplane through the symmetry center of \(A_1\), so its exact area is \(a_1/2\). Hence the tilted halfspace has exact mass
\[
F_0(\tau)+\frac{a_1}{2}+F_2(-\tau).
\]
If \(\tau\ge0\), monotonicity gives \(F_0(\tau)\ge a_0/2\), and therefore the mass is at least
\[
\frac{a_0+a_1}{2}\ge\frac{m+a_1}{2}.
\]
If \(\tau\le0\), then \(-\tau\ge0\), so \(F_2(-\tau)\ge a_2/2\), and the mass is at least
\[
\frac{a_2+a_1}{2}\ge\frac{m+a_1}{2}.
\]
Thus every tilted halfspace through \(y^*\) has mass at least \((m+a_1)/2\).

Because \(M<T/2\),
\[
m+a_1=T-M>\frac T2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{m+a_1}{2}>\frac T4>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
All boundary-through-\(y^*\) halfspaces, tilted or slice-parallel, therefore have mass at least \(2T/9\). The boundary reduction from accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` extends the same bound to every closed halfspace containing \(y^*\). Hence
\[
h_S(y^*)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
