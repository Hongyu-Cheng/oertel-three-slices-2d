## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex
polygons satisfying midpoint compatibility
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Choose \(j\in\{0,2\}\) with \(a_j=M\), and put \(k=2-j\), so
\(a_k=m\).  Assume the residual hypotheses of accepted fact
`58b8e7bb5c3be36f`; in particular \(0<t<a_1+m\).

For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), set
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}|
\]
and let \(Q_{i,q}:[0,a_i]\to\mathbb R\) be the endpoint-normalized cap
quantile.  Define the finite endpoint convolution
\[
\overline E_q(b)=
\max_{0\le u\le b}
\bigl(Q_{j,q}(u)+Q_{k,q}(b-u)\bigr)
\qquad(0\le b\le m).
\tag{2}
\]
Retain the actual conventions
\[
D_{1,q}(0)=-\infty,\qquad E_q(0)=-\infty,
\]
\[
D_{1,q}(a)=Q_{1,q}(a)\quad(0<a\le a_1),\qquad
E_q(b)=\overline E_q(b)\quad(0<b\le m),
\]
and \(D_{1,q}(a)=+\infty\) for \(a>a_1\),
\(E_q(b)=+\infty\) for \(b>m\).  Let
\[
\rho_q(t)=
\min_{0\le a\le t}
\max\left\{D_{1,q}(a),\frac12E_q(t-a)\right\},
\]
let \(p=a_*(q)\) be its unique optimizer, and put \(b=t-p\).  Then
\(0\le b\le m\), and the true endpoint contact set is
\[
\mathcal U_q(b)=
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{0\le u\le b}
\bigl(Q_{j,q}(u)+Q_{k,q}(b-u)\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

For fixed \(q\), define
\[
m_i(q)=\min_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,\qquad
M_i(q)=\max_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,
\]
\[
\lambda_{i,q}(c)=
\mathcal H^1(A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x=c\}),\qquad
\ell_{i,q}(u)=\lambda_{i,q}(Q_{i,q}(u)).
\tag{4}
\]
The function \(\lambda_{i,q}\) is continuous, concave, and piecewise
affine on \([m_i(q),M_i(q)]\), positive in the interior, and
\[
Q_{i,q}'(u)=\frac1{\ell_{i,q}(u)}
\qquad(0<u<a_i).
\tag{5}
\]
At \(u=0\) and \(u=a_i\), the corresponding one-sided derivative is
\(1/\ell_{i,q}(u)\) in the extended interval \((0,+\infty]\), with
\(1/0=+\infty\).

If \(b>0\), then \(u\in\mathcal U_q(b)\) if and only if
\[
\int_u^v
\left(
\frac1{\ell_{j,q}(s)}
-\frac1{\ell_{k,q}(b-s)}
\right)\,ds\le0
\qquad\text{for every }v\in[0,b],
\tag{6}
\]
where the integral is oriented and is interpreted improperly at a
zero-chord endpoint.  In particular:

1. If \(0<u<b\) and \(u\in\mathcal U_q(b)\), then
   \[
   \ell_{j,q}(u)=\ell_{k,q}(b-u).
   \tag{7}
   \]

2. One has \(0\in\mathcal U_q(b)\) if and only if
   \[
   Q_{j,q}(v)-Q_{j,q}(0)
   \le
   Q_{k,q}(b)-Q_{k,q}(b-v)
   \qquad(0\le v\le b).
   \tag{8}
   \]
   This implies
   \[
   \lambda_{j,q}(m_j(q))
   \ge
   \lambda_{k,q}(Q_{k,q}(b)).
   \tag{9}
   \]

3. One has \(b\in\mathcal U_q(b)\) if and only if
   \[
   Q_{j,q}(b)-Q_{j,q}(b-v)
   \ge
   Q_{k,q}(v)-Q_{k,q}(0)
   \qquad(0\le v\le b).
   \tag{10}
   \]
   This implies
   \[
   \lambda_{j,q}(Q_{j,q}(b))
   \le
   \lambda_{k,q}(m_k(q)).
   \tag{11}
   \]

Whenever the two marginal derivatives are finite, (7), (9), and (11)
are the normal-cone KKT condition
\[
\frac1{\ell_{j,q}(u)}
-\frac1{\ell_{k,q}(b-u)}
\in N_{[0,b]}(u),
\tag{12}
\]
where
\[
N_{[0,b]}(0)=(-\infty,0],\qquad
N_{[0,b]}(u)=\{0\}\ (0<u<b),\qquad
N_{[0,b]}(b)=[0,\infty).
\]
If both relevant endpoint derivatives are \(+\infty\), (12) is not
defined; the exact conditions are (8) and (10), not a formal subtraction
of infinities.  Every interior point of a nonunique contact set satisfies
(7), and if an interval is contained in \(\mathcal U_q(b)\), then (7)
holds throughout its interior.  For \(b=0\),
\(\mathcal U_q(0)=\{0\}\), with no KKT equation and without changing the
actual value \(E_q(0)=-\infty\).

For \(u\in\mathcal U_q(b)\), put \(v=b-u\) and
\[
c_j=Q_{j,q}(u),\qquad c_k=Q_{k,q}(v).
\]
Then \(c_j+c_k=\overline E_q(b)\), and compatibility gives
\[
F_{1,q}\!\left(\frac{c_j+c_k}{2}\right)
\ge
\frac{(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2}{4},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\lambda_{1,q}\!\left(\frac{c_j+c_k}{2}\right)
\ge
\frac{\lambda_{j,q}(c_j)+\lambda_{k,q}(c_k)}2.
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover,
\[
p=F_{1,q}(\rho_q(t))
\ge
\frac{(\sqrt u+\sqrt{b-u})^2}{4}.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, \(p>0\).  At an interior contact, the right side of (14)
is the common chord length in (7).

Now fix a noncollinear positive-spanning triple
\[
q_1,q_2,q_3\in\mathbb S^1,\qquad
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell=0.
\tag{16}
\]
For each \(\ell\), write
\[
\rho_\ell=\rho_{q_\ell}(t),\qquad
p_\ell=a_*(q_\ell),\qquad
b_\ell=t-p_\ell,\qquad
W=\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_\ell,
\tag{17}
\]
and define
\[
\eta_\ell
=2\rho_\ell-\overline E_{q_\ell}(b_\ell),
\qquad
d_\ell(u)
=Q_{j,q_\ell}(u)
-Q_{k,q_\ell}(b_\ell-u).
\tag{18}
\]
The endpoint classification gives \(\eta_\ell=0\) in the crossing
regime, at \(p_\ell=0\), and at the middle-capacity endpoint;
\(\eta_\ell\ge0\) at the outer-capacity endpoint \(p_\ell=L>0\); and
\(\eta_\ell\le0\) at the zero outer-demand endpoint \(b_\ell=0\).
Compatibility excludes the listed case \(p_\ell=0\).

Because each contact set is compact, define its rightmost point
\[
u_\ell^+=\max\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell).
\tag{19}
\]
For every contact \(u\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\),
\[
Q_{j,q_\ell}(u)
=\rho_\ell+\frac12\bigl(d_\ell(u)-\eta_\ell\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(Q_{j,q_\ell}\) is strictly increasing and the contact choices are
independent,
\[
\boxed{
\max_{\substack{u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\\ \ell=1,2,3}}
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)
=
W+\frac12\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell\bigl(d_\ell(u_\ell^+)-\eta_\ell\bigr).}
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, under the antecedent \(W>0\), the consequent of (CB) is
equivalent to the exact cross-direction remainder
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
\alpha_\ell\bigl(d_\ell(u_\ell^+)-\eta_\ell\bigr)
\ge-2W.}
\tag{XD}
\]
Equivalently, (CB) is the conditional implication
\(W>0\Longrightarrow\text{(XD)}\).  Neither (XD) nor (CB) is asserted.

A stronger sufficient condition, also not asserted, is the existence of
contacts \(u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\) and one
\(z\in\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)-\rho_\ell=q_\ell\cdot z
\qquad(\ell=1,2,3).
\tag{COH}
\]
Condition (COH) would give a strictly positive weighted contact sum
whenever \(W>0\).

## Proof

Fix \(q\) and \(i\).  Every section of \(A_i\) perpendicular to \(q\) is
an interval.  If \(c_0,c_1\in[m_i(q),M_i(q)]\), convexity sends the
corresponding intervals by Minkowski interpolation into the section at
\((1-\theta)c_0+\theta c_1\).  Length in one dimension is additive under
Minkowski sums, so
\[
\lambda_{i,q}((1-\theta)c_0+\theta c_1)
\ge
(1-\theta)\lambda_{i,q}(c_0)
+\theta\lambda_{i,q}(c_1).
\]
Thus \(\lambda_{i,q}\) is concave.  For a polygon it is piecewise affine,
and direct inspection at a support edge or support vertex gives
continuity at both endpoints.  It is positive at every interior level.

Cavalieri's formula gives
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=
\int_{m_i(q)}^c\lambda_{i,q}(s)\,ds.
\]
Thus \(F_{i,q}\) is continuously differentiable in the open support
interval with positive derivative \(\lambda_{i,q}\).  Accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878` identifies \(Q_{i,q}\) as its strictly increasing
continuous inverse, proving (5).

The endpoint derivatives require separate checking.  If
\(L=\lambda_{i,q}(m_i(q))>0\), then
\[
F_{i,q}(m_i(q)+x)=Lx+o(x),
\]
so
\[
\frac{Q_{i,q}(u)-Q_{i,q}(0)}u\longrightarrow\frac1L.
\]
If \(L=0\), then
\(F_{i,q}(m_i(q)+x)/x\to0\), and inversion gives the extended derivative
\(+\infty\).  The upper endpoint is identical after replacing \(F\) by
\(a_i-F\).  This proves the endpoint assertion without subtracting two
extended derivatives.

For \(b>0\), define
\[
G_b(u)=Q_{j,q}(u)+Q_{k,q}(b-u).
\]
For \(u,v\in[0,b]\), the fundamental theorem of calculus, interpreted
improperly at an endpoint when necessary, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_b(v)-G_b(u)
&=Q_{j,q}(v)-Q_{j,q}(u)
  +Q_{k,q}(b-v)-Q_{k,q}(b-u)\\
&=\int_u^v
\left(
\frac1{\ell_{j,q}(s)}
-\frac1{\ell_{k,q}(b-s)}
\right)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
The improper integral is finite because it equals the displayed finite
quantile difference.  By definition, \(u\) is a global maximizer exactly
when this difference is nonpositive for every \(v\), proving (6).

At an interior maximizer, differentiability gives zero derivative and
hence (7).  Substituting \(u=0\) into the global maximizing inequality
gives (8), and substituting \(u=b\), with \(b-v\) as the competitor,
gives (10).  Taking the appropriate one-sided difference quotients gives
(9) and (11).  If both endpoint chords are positive, this is ordinary
reciprocal comparison.  If exactly one endpoint chord is zero, the
extended derivative shows directly which endpoint can be maximizing.  If
both are zero, the chord inequalities reduce to \(0=0\) and contain no
information; (8) or (10) still gives the exact comparison of the two
quantile increments.  For example, at two polygonal support vertices with
\[
\lambda_j(m_j+x)=\gamma_jx,\qquad
\lambda_k(M_k-x)=\gamma_kx
\]
near the endpoints, (8) first requires
\(\sqrt{2v/\gamma_j}\le\sqrt{2v/\gamma_k}\), equivalently
\(\gamma_j\ge\gamma_k\).  This verifies the zero-chord endpoint case
without the undefined expression \(+\infty-(+\infty)\).

When both marginal derivatives are finite, the three derivative signs are
exactly (12).  If a contact interval is nontrivial, \(G_b\) is constant on
it, so its derivative vanishes throughout the interior and (7) follows
there.  Multiple isolated contacts are still characterized by (6);
local KKT is not sufficient because \(G_b\) is not generally concave.
Accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` gives compactness of the contact set.
Strict increase of \(Q_{j,q}\) then shows that its rightmost point uniquely
maximizes \(Q_{j,q}\) over that set.  At \(b=0\), (2) has only the split
\(u=0\), while the actual convention remains \(E_q(0)=-\infty\).  This
proves all contact assertions.

Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` identifies compatibility with (1).
For
\[
K_j=A_j\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c_j\},\qquad
K_k=A_k\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c_k\},
\]
one has \(|K_j|=u\) and \(|K_k|=v\), including zero and full-capacity
endpoints.  Compatibility gives
\[
\frac{K_j+K_k}{2}
\subseteq
A_1\cap
\left\{x:q\cdot x\le\frac{c_j+c_k}{2}\right\}.
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski, which also permits one cap to have zero area,
proves (13).  Applying compatibility to the two boundary-section
intervals instead gives their Minkowski average inside the middle
boundary section.  The average interval has length
\((\lambda_{j,q}(c_j)+\lambda_{k,q}(c_k))/2\), proving (14).

Accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f` gives
\[
F_{1,q}(\rho_q(t))=p.
\tag{22}
\]
The exact allocation classification in accepted fact
`65512ded3a06d971` shows the following.  In the crossing regime and at
the middle-capacity endpoint,
\(\rho_q(t)=\overline E_q(b)/2\).  At the outer-capacity endpoint
\(p=L>0\), one has
\(\rho_q(t)\ge\overline E_q(b)/2\).  At the left endpoint \(p=L=0\),
one again has equality.  The only reverse inequality occurs at
\(b=0\), when the contact masses are both zero.  Therefore, for \(b>0\),
monotonicity of \(F_{1,q}\), (13), and (22) give (15); for \(b=0\),
(15) is the trivial inequality \(p\ge0\).

If \(p=0\), then \(b=t>0\), and equality holds in the preceding threshold
comparison.  Since \(u+(b-u)=t>0\), the right side of (15) is positive,
contradicting \(p=0\).  Thus compatibility excludes \(p=0\).  At an
interior contact, (7) makes the two outer chord lengths in (14) equal.
This proves all compatibility assertions.

For the triple, (2) and the definition of a contact give
\[
Q_{j,q_\ell}(u)+Q_{k,q_\ell}(b_\ell-u)
=\overline E_{q_\ell}(b_\ell).
\]
Subtracting and adding this equality, and using (18), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{j,q_\ell}(u)
&=\frac12\left(
\overline E_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)+d_\ell(u)\right)\\
&=\rho_\ell+\frac12\left(d_\ell(u)-\eta_\ell\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (20).  The signs and zero cases for \(\eta_\ell\) follow
directly from the same allocation classification just used.

Because \(\alpha_\ell>0\), the product maximization separates by
direction.  Strict increase of each \(Q_{j,q_\ell}\) selects the rightmost
contact \(u_\ell^+\).  Summing (20) with weights \(\alpha_\ell\) proves
(21).  Moving \(W\) to the other side proves that the consequent of
(CB) is equivalent to (XD).  Hence the whole statement (CB) is precisely
the conditional implication \(W>0\Longrightarrow\text{(XD)}\).

Under \(W>0\), accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f` also gives the middle-cap
cover and \(\sum_\ell p_\ell\ge a_1\).  Together with (15), these are
mass inequalities.  The KKT equations control marginal chord lengths,
and (13)-(15) control sum levels and cap masses, but none of them relates
the three signed normal differences \(d_\ell\).  Thus they do not prove
(XD).

Finally, if (COH) held, then (16) would give
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)
=
W+z\cdot\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell
=W>0.
\]
For a noncollinear triple, the \(3\times2\) matrix with rows
\(q_\ell^{\mathsf T}\) has rank two and left kernel spanned by
\((\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)\).  Hence existence of \(z\) in (COH) is
equivalent to zero weighted sum of the three offsets
\(Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)-\rho_\ell\).  Interior KKT matches each pair of
outer chord lengths separately, but compatibility does not make the
normal offsets of those three chord pairs arise from one common vector.
This is exactly the first cross-direction condition missing from the
variational route.  No assertion of (COH), (XD), or (CB) has been made.
