## Statement

Let \(\mathscr R_q\) be the complete fixed critical-ratio residual-counterexample class of accepted facts `0435696483192d31` and `d721506a274e22da`, at arbitrary scale \(q>0\).  Thus
\[
q=|Q_{123}|,\qquad
|Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}|=\frac{5q}{21}\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
the only positive \(Q\)-cells are \(Q_{123},Q_{12},Q_{13},Q_{23}\), all three middle singleton cells and the middle central cell are positive, the full common-plane midpoint inclusion holds, and the realization is in the no-half-slice residual branch.  Put
\[
C_i=\overline{Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}},\qquad
D=\frac{q}{|\mathcal T_Q|},
\]
where \(\mathcal T_Q\) is the bounded triangle cut out by the three central \(Q\)-halfplanes.

Let \(D_0>0\) be the universal constant supplied by accepted fact `0435696483192d31`.  There is a universal constant \(\epsilon_{\rm cap}>0\), depending only on this normalized residual class, such that every member of \(\mathscr R_q\) satisfies
\[
D_0\leq D<\frac19
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}
-3\sqrt{\frac{5q}{21}}
\geq \epsilon_{\rm cap}\sqrt q.
\tag{1}
\]

More precisely, every sequence in \(\mathscr R_1\) has representatives obtained by common planar affine maps and slice-preserving affine height shears for which the following simultaneous precompactness holds.

1. The three full slice bodies \(A_{0,n},K_n,A_{2,n}\) lie in one fixed Euclidean ball and have Hausdorff-convergent subsequences.
2. For each of the three common cutting hyperplanes, its oriented unit planar normal and its restrictions to the \(P\)-, \(R\)-, and \(Q\)-planes have convergent line offsets.
3. The central \(Q\)-cells and the three original caps converge in Hausdorff distance to the intersections of the limiting body with the limiting halfplanes.  Their limiting areas are respectively \(q_\infty\) and \(5q_\infty/21\), where \(q_\infty>0\).  Every exact \(P\)-, \(R\)-, and \(Q\)-cell closure is precompact, every exact-cell area converges to the area of the corresponding limiting sign intersection, and a cell with positive limiting area converges in Hausdorff distance to that whole intersection.  Consequently all finite cell-mass aggregates, and all finite union and intersection areas of the midpoint bodies used in the canonical ledger, converge.
4. The limiting three central \(Q\)-halfplanes cut out a bounded nondegenerate triangle, the global triangles converge in Hausdorff distance, and \(D_n\to D_\infty=q_\infty/|\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}|\).

Consequently the normalized left side of (1) is continuous on the compact sequential closure of these representatives.

## Proof

It is enough first to treat \(q=1\).  Accepted fact `0435696483192d31` gives \(D\geq D_0>0\), while accepted fact `d721506a274e22da` gives \(D<1/9\) throughout the residual branch.

We first obtain a compact gauge.  Use the planar John normalization appearing in accepted fact `3ff81f067c99b5ad`: after a common planar affine map,
\[
B\subseteq A_{2,n}\subseteq2B,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(B\) is the unit disk.  Write primes only in this paragraph for the transformed areas.  If \(J_n>0\) is the absolute determinant of this planar map, then \(q'_n=J_n\).  Since the four positive \(Q\)-cells partition \(A'_{2,n}\) up to cutting lines,
\[
|A'_{2,n}|=\frac{12q'_n}{7}.
\]
Equation (2) therefore gives the uniform bounds
\[
q_-:=\frac{7\pi}{12}\leq q'_n\leq
\frac{7\pi}{3}=:q_+.
\tag{3}
\]
The normalized cap deficit divided by \(\sqrt q\), and also \(D=q/|\mathcal T_Q|\), are unchanged by this map.

Choose \(z_n\in A'_{0,n}\), put \(v_n=z_n/2\), and apply
\[
(h,z)\longmapsto (h,z-(2-h)v_n).
\tag{4}
\]
This is the height shear of accepted fact `ecee03aff61db31e` followed by a common planar translation.  It fixes \(A'_{2,n}\), sends \(A'_{0,n}\) to \(A'_{0,n}-z_n\), sends \(K'_n\) to \(K'_n-z_n/2\), and preserves every cell area, cutting incidence, residual condition, and midpoint inclusion.  Suppress the primes and hats from now on.  We then have
\[
0\in A_{0,n},\qquad
B\subseteq A_{2,n}\subseteq2B,\qquad
\frac{A_{0,n}+A_{2,n}}2\subseteq K_n.
\tag{5}
\]

Put \(p_n=|P_{\varnothing,n}|\), \(r_n=|R_{123,n}|\), \(M_n=|A_{0,n}|\), \(m_n=|A_{2,n}|=12q'_n/7\), and \(k_n=|K_n|\).  The full-common-plane scale inequality in accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8` is homogeneous and gives
\[
D_n\leq
\frac{q'_n}{(\sqrt{p_n}+2\sqrt{r_n})^2}.
\]
Using (3) and \(D_n\geq D_0\),
\[
p_n\leq\frac{q_+}{D_0}.
\tag{6}
\]
The canonical-tail calculation in accepted fact `d721506a274e22da`, rescaled by \(q'_n\), gives
\[
M_n-p_n\leq\frac{5q'_n}{7}.
\tag{7}
\]
The no-half-slice residual condition gives \(k_n<M_n+m_n\).  Hence
\[
k_n\leq K_*:=
\frac{q_+}{D_0}+\frac{17q_+}{7}.
\tag{8}
\]

The area bound (8) is a diameter bound because (5) contains a fixed disk.  Indeed, (5) implies \(B/2\subseteq K_n\).  For \(x\in K_n\), take the diameter of \(B/2\) perpendicular to \(x\).  Its convex hull with \(x\) is a triangle in \(K_n\) of area \(|x|/2\).  Thus
\[
|x|\leq2K_*,
\qquad K_n\subseteq 2K_*B.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(0\in A_{2,n}\), (5) also gives \(x/2\in K_n\) for every \(x\in A_{0,n}\), and therefore
\[
A_{0,n}\subseteq4K_*B.
\tag{10}
\]
Together with \(A_{2,n}\subseteq2B\), this proves the asserted uniform containment of all three bodies.  Notice that no endpoint-preserving boundary reduction has been made.

For completeness, uniform containment gives sequential Hausdorff compactness directly.  If \(L\) is a compact convex subset of \(RB\), its support function on the unit circle takes values in \([-R,R]\) and is \(R\)-Lipschitz.  A diagonal equicontinuity argument gives a uniformly convergent subsequence of support functions, and the limit is the support function of a compact convex set.  Applying this simultaneously to the finite list of bodies and cell closures gives the required Hausdorff subsequences.  The limits of \(A_{2,n}\) and \(K_n\) remain two-dimensional because they contain \(B\) and \(B/2\).  The scaled inequality \(p_n>9q'_n\) from accepted fact `d721506a274e22da`, together with (3), also keeps the \(A_{0,n}\)-limits two-dimensional.

We next include the lines.  For the \(i\)-th common cutting hyperplane choose its oriented planar unit normal \(u_{i,n}\).  Write its restrictions to the \(Q\)- and \(R\)-planes as
\[
\ell^Q_{i,n}=\{u_{i,n}\cdot z=b^Q_{i,n}\},
\qquad
\ell^R_{i,n}=\{u_{i,n}\cdot z=b^R_{i,n}\},
\]
with the orientation chosen so that the central \(Q\)-halfplane is
\(H^Q_{i,n}=\{u_{i,n}\cdot z\geq b^Q_{i,n}\}\).
The \(Q\)-line meets the interior of \(A_{2,n}\), because the central cell and the cap \(C_{i,n}\) have positive area on its two sides.  Thus
\[
|b^Q_{i,n}|\leq2.
\tag{11}
\]
The \(R\)-line meets the interior of \(K_n\), because \(r_{123,n}>0\) and, for either \(j\neq i\), \(r_{j,n}>0\) put positive-area cells on its two sides.  By (9),
\[
|b^R_{i,n}|\leq2K_*.
\tag{12}
\]
The restriction of an affine hyperplane to parallel height planes has affine line offset in the height.  Hence its \(P\)-offset satisfies the exact reflection identity
\[
b^P_{i,n}=2b^R_{i,n}-b^Q_{i,n}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (11)--(13), compactness of the unit circle, and a finite diagonal extraction give simultaneous convergence of all normals and all \(P,R,Q\) line offsets.

We record the halfspace-limit assertion carefully.  Suppose
\[
L_n\longrightarrow L,\qquad
u_n\longrightarrow u,\qquad b_n\longrightarrow b,
\]
where the \(L_n\) are convex bodies in one fixed ball, and suppose the areas of
\[
E_n=L_n\cap\{u_n\cdot z\leq b_n\}
\]
converge to a positive number.  Every Hausdorff limit \(\widetilde E\) of the \(E_n\) is contained in
\(L\cap\{u\cdot z\leq b\}\).  Conversely, every point in the interior of that latter intersection satisfies the defining inequalities strictly after an arbitrarily small inward perturbation and belongs to \(E_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since \(\widetilde E\) has positive area, the limiting intersection has nonempty interior, whose closure is the whole intersection.  Therefore
\[
E_n\longrightarrow L\cap\{u\cdot z\leq b\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The same argument works for a finite intersection of halfspaces.  Applying (14) to the original caps and central cells, and passing also to \(q'_n\to q_\infty\), gives
\[
C_{i,n}\longrightarrow
C_{i,\infty}:=
A_{2,\infty}\cap\{u_{i,\infty}\cdot z\leq b^Q_{i,\infty}\},
\qquad
|C_{i,\infty}|=\frac{5q_\infty}{21},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
Q_{123,n}\longrightarrow
Q_{123,\infty}:=
A_{2,\infty}\cap\bigcap_iH^Q_{i,\infty},
\qquad
|Q_{123,\infty}|=q_\infty.
\tag{16}
\]
Here \(q_\infty\in[q_-,q_+]\).  The area identities follow from continuity of planar area under Hausdorff convergence.  One elementary quantitative proof of that continuity is to use
\[
L_n\subseteq L+\delta_nB,\qquad
L\subseteq L_n+\delta_nB,\qquad \delta_n=d_H(L_n,L),
\]
and the planar parallel-body formula; perimeters of convex subsets of \(RB\) are at most \(2\pi R\), so the two area differences tend to zero.

For an arbitrary exact sign cell \(E_n\), any Hausdorff limit is contained in its limiting sign intersection \(E\).  If \(E\) has nonempty interior, the interior-point argument in (14) gives \(E_n\to E\).  If \(E\) has empty interior, every subsequential Hausdorff limit of \(E_n\) has area zero, and area continuity gives \(|E_n|\to0=|E|\).  Thus every exact-cell area converges to the area of its limiting sign intersection, including collapsing cells.  Finite sums of these masses, such as the singleton, doubleton, tail, and total-slice aggregates, therefore converge.

The central \(Q\)-cell and the three \(Q\)-caps stay uniformly positive by (3), with respective lower area bounds \(q_-\) and \(5q_-/21\).  The scaled residual bound \(p_n>9q'_n\) also keeps \(P_{\varnothing,n}\) uniformly positive.  Hence
\[
P_{\varnothing,n}\longrightarrow P_{\varnothing,\infty}
\]
as a full two-dimensional cell.  The middle cells \(R_{\{i\},n}\) and \(R_{123,n}\) are positive in each realization, but the accepted hypotheses give no uniform lower bound for them; they may collapse to zero area on the closure.  The same is true of any other cell required only to be positive member by member.

Each midpoint body
\[
B_{i,n}=\frac{P_{\varnothing,n}+C_{i,n}}2
\]
converges by continuity of Minkowski addition.  The same compactness argument applied to every finite intersection of the \(B_{i,n}\) proves convergence of its area: a positive-area limiting intersection has interior and is obtained exactly, while an empty-interior limiting intersection has area zero.  Inclusion-exclusion then gives convergence of all finite union areas.  This includes the overlap and coverage aggregates used in the canonical ledger, without asserting that their limiting strict support pattern remains unchanged.

The cap interiors remain pairwise disjoint in the limit.  Otherwise a small disk would lie in the interiors of two limiting caps.  Hausdorff convergence of two-dimensional convex bodies would put a smaller disk in both corresponding caps for all large \(n\), contradicting the exact all-double \(Q\)-support.  Thus the limiting \(Q\)-geometry has exactly the disjoint-cap incidence needed below.

Let
\[
\mathcal T_{Q,n}=\bigcap_iH^Q_{i,n}.
\]
Its area is \(q'_n/D_n\), and hence
\[
|\mathcal T_{Q,n}|\leq\frac{q_+}{D_0}.
\tag{17}
\]
The limiting halfplane intersection \(\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}\) contains the positive-area body in (16), so it has nonempty interior.  It cannot be unbounded.  Indeed, an unbounded planar convex set with nonempty interior contains compact subsets of arbitrarily large area; every compact subset of its interior lies in \(\mathcal T_{Q,n}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), contradicting (17).  Therefore \(\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}\) is bounded.  An intersection of three halfplanes which is bounded and has nonempty interior is a nondegenerate triangle, so the three limiting lines are pairwise nonparallel.  Convergence of its three vertices now follows from convergence of the line equations, and hence
\[
\mathcal T_{Q,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal T_{Q,\infty},
\qquad
|\mathcal T_{Q,n}|\longrightarrow|\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}|.
\tag{18}
\]
After also taking \(D_n\to D_\infty\), equations (3) and (18) give
\[
D_\infty=\frac{q_\infty}{|\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}|}
\in\left[D_0,\frac19\right].
\tag{19}
\]
This proves all asserted precompactness and continuity statements.

Minkowski addition is continuous for Hausdorff distance:
\[
d_H(A_n+B_n,A+B)
\leq d_H(A_n,A)+d_H(B_n,B).
\]
Together with area continuity and (15), it follows that
\[
\mathfrak d(C_1,C_2,C_3;q):=
\frac{\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}
-3\sqrt{5q/21}}{\sqrt q}
\tag{20}
\]
is continuous along every normalized subsequence above.  It is nonnegative by the iterated planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality, as recorded in accepted fact `0a44ad1c2be95e40`.

We now exclude zero on the sequential closure.  Suppose a sequence in \(\mathscr R_1\) had \(\mathfrak d\to0\), and take the simultaneous limit just constructed.  Equation (20) and continuity give equality for the three limiting caps.  The equality statement in accepted fact `0a44ad1c2be95e40` makes the three equal-area limiting caps translates.

There is one closure point to check: a Hausdorff limit of polytopes need not itself be a polygon.  The equality exclusion of accepted fact `d0a445c133525448` nevertheless applies to this limit.  Here is a direct verification.  First apply a common planar map of area multiplier \(1/q_\infty\), so that the limiting central-cell area is \(1\) and every limiting cap has area \(5/21\); this leaves \(D_\infty\) unchanged.  Then use the original cutting lines of the resulting nondegenerate triangle from (18) as barycentric coordinates
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1,
\qquad
\mathcal T_{Q,\infty}=\{\alpha_i\geq0:i=1,2,3\}.
\]
Write the caps as \(B+t_i\).  Each cutting line is a nondegenerate chord, so \(B\) has the three cutting-base segments with the three distinct normals used in `d0a445c133525448`.  The support observation in the proof of that fact, applied to each base segment in the other two translated copies, gives
\[
n_1\cdot t_2=n_1\cdot t_3,\qquad
n_2\cdot t_1=n_2\cdot t_3,\qquad
n_3\cdot t_1=n_3\cdot t_2.
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, after a translation of all \(t_i\),
\[
t_1-t_3=(\lambda,0),\qquad
t_2-t_3=(0,\lambda)
\tag{22}
\]
for some \(\lambda\neq0\); otherwise the positive-area caps would coincide, contrary to their disjoint interiors.

If the boundary of the convex body \(B\) had any part beyond its three base segments, one connected component of the complement of those segments in \(\partial B\) would contain a nonempty relatively open boundary arc.  In local coordinates that arc is the graph of a one-variable convex function.  If the graph is affine on a subinterval, \(B\) has an additional boundary segment.  Otherwise choose an interior differentiability point of the convex function; such points exist because its one-sided derivatives are monotone and have only countably many jumps.  The supporting line there cannot meet the arc at a second point, since convexity would then make the intervening graph affine.  Thus in either case there is a nonzero supporting normal \(w\) whose support face is contained in the extra arc and is disjoint from all three base segments.

In every translated cap this support face lies strictly on the outer side of that cap's cutting base.  A neighborhood of the face in the cap is therefore also a neighborhood in \(A_{2,\infty}\).  Its supporting line is a local support of the convex body \(A_{2,\infty}\), hence a global support.  The support observation consequently gives
\[
h_{A_{2,\infty}}(w)=h_B(w)+w\cdot t_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Thus \(w\) is orthogonal to both vectors in (22), a contradiction.  Hence \(B\) is exactly the triangle bounded by its three base segments.  The remaining affine and area calculation in accepted fact `d0a445c133525448` applies verbatim and yields
\[
D_\infty\geq
D_{\rm tr}:=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
>\frac19.
\tag{23}
\]
This contradicts (19).

It follows that zero is absent from the compact sequential closure of the normalized residual cap geometries.  If the residual class is nonempty, define
\[
\epsilon_{\rm cap}:=
\min \mathfrak d(C_1,C_2,C_3;q)
\]
over that compact closure.  The preceding contradiction proves
\(\epsilon_{\rm cap}>0\).  If the class is empty, take any positive constant.

Finally, for a realization at arbitrary scale \(q>0\), apply a common planar homothety of area multiplier \(1/q\).  It sends \(q\) to \(1\), leaves \(D\) unchanged, and divides the cap deficit by \(\sqrt q\).  Undoing the homothety proves (1).  The argument never uses the endpoint-preserving triangular reduction.  It proves no numerical lower bound on \(\epsilon_{\rm cap}\) and no inequality comparing it with the separate ledger demand.
