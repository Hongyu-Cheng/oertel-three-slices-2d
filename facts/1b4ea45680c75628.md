## Statement

Let \(\ell _1,\ell _2,\ell _3\) be nonzero pairwise nonparallel linear forms on \(\mathbb R^2\) with
\[
\ell _1+\ell _2+\ell _3=0,
\]
and let \(O\) be their common zero. Put \(H_i=\{\ell_i\leq0\}\). In the coordinates
\[
u=\ell _1,\qquad v=\ell _2
\]
label the six open sectors in cyclic order by
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A:\ u<0,\ v<0,&
B:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v<0,&
C:\ u>0,\ v<0,\ u+v>0,\\
D:\ u>0,\ v>0,&
E:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v>0,&
F:\ u<0,\ v>0,\ u+v<0.
\end{array}
\]
Thus, up to the fan lines,
\[
H_1=A\cup E\cup F,\qquad
H_2=A\cup B\cup C,\qquad
H_3=C\cup D\cup E.
\]

Let \(P\) be a convex polygon with \(n\geq3\) genuine vertices, with \(O\in\operatorname{int}P\), no vertex on a fan ray, and every boundary intersection with a fan ray transversal. Let
\[
L_P:\mathbb R^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^6
\]
send vertex velocities to the six signed first derivatives of the sector areas.

Then \(\operatorname{rank}L_P\geq5\), and \(\operatorname{rank}L_P=5\) exactly in one of the following two cases.

1. Exactly two boundary edges meet fan rays, each of them crosses three consecutive rays, all remaining vertices lie in one open sector, and the one remaining vertex lies in the opposite sector. Equivalently, all but one vertices lie in a sector \(S\), the last vertex lies in \(S+3\), and the two edges incident with the last vertex cross all six rays, three each.

2. \(P\) is a triangle, every edge crosses exactly two consecutive fan rays, and, if the two crossing parameters on its three cyclically oriented edges are
\[
0<a_i<b_i<1\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
then
\[
\prod_{i=1}^3
\frac{a_i b_i}{(1-a_i)(1-b_i)}=1.
\]

In every other centered fan-transverse combinatorial case, \(\operatorname{rank}L_P=6\).

Now write
\[
T=|P|,\qquad p_i=\frac{|P\cap H_i|}{T}.
\]
Within the smooth stratum of polygons having the same number of vertices and the same transversal fan-crossing pattern, suppose \(P\) is a local minimizer of \(p_3\) subject to fixed values of \(p_1,p_2\), where
\[
p_1\leq\frac12,\qquad p_2\leq\frac12.
\]
Then \(P\) has exactly three vertices, one in each of \(B,D,F\). Moreover its three edges cross two rays each and its crossing parameters satisfy the product identity above. In particular, its vertices lie in the three singleton sectors:
\[
B\subset H_2\setminus(H_1\cup H_3),\quad
D\subset H_3\setminus(H_1\cup H_2),\quad
F\subset H_1\setminus(H_2\cup H_3).
\]

The centered hypothesis is necessary. For the canonical forms
\[
\ell _1=z+\tau,\qquad \ell _2=\tau-z,\qquad \ell _3=-2\tau,
\]
in the conventional ordered coordinates \((z,\tau)\), the rational rectangle
\[
R=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/4,2),(1/4,2),(1/4,3),(-1/4,3)\}
\]
lies in \(D\), has \((p_1,p_2,p_3)=(0,0,1)\), and is a nontriangular local, indeed global, minimizer of \(p_3\) subject to \(p_1=p_2=0\). Thus a statement using “fan-transverse” only to exclude tangencies, without requiring the fan center inside the polygon, is false; the smallest correction is to require \(O\in\operatorname{int}P\), equivalently that all six rays exit through the boundary.

## Proof

Orient the vertices \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) counterclockwise, put
\[
d_i=P_{i+1}-P_i
\]
with cyclic indices, and give \(P_i\) velocity \(V_i\). On the \(i\)-th edge use the parameterization
\[
x_i(t)=P_i+t d_i,\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
The velocity there is the affine interpolation
\[
V_i(t)=(1-t)V_i+tV_{i+1}.
\]
A moving boundary element sweeps signed outward area
\[
\det(V_i(t),d_i)\,dt.
\]
At a transversal crossing with a fixed fan ray, replacing the moving crossing point by the old one changes the swept region by \(O(s^2)\). Hence, if \(\mathbf 1_j\) is the indicator of the \(j\)-th open sector, the exact first-variation map is
\[
(L_PV)_j
=\sum_{i=1}^n\int_0^1
\mathbf 1_j(x_i(t))
\det\bigl((1-t)V_i+tV_{i+1},d_i\bigr)\,dt.
\tag{1}
\]

We first give a dual characterization of its rank. For
\(\lambda=(\lambda_A,\ldots,\lambda_F)\), let
\(\lambda_i(t)\) be the value \(\lambda_j\) of the sector containing
\(x_i(t)\). Expanding \(\lambda\cdot L_PV\) from (1) and collecting
the coefficient of each \(V_i\) shows that it vanishes for every
vertex velocity if and only if
\[
\left(\int_0^1(1-t)\lambda_i(t)\,dt\right)d_i
+
\left(\int_0^1t\lambda_{i-1}(t)\,dt\right)d_{i-1}=0
\tag{2}
\]
at every vertex. The two incident edge directions are nonparallel
because every listed vertex is genuine. Therefore (2) is equivalent
to the two scalar conditions
\[
\int_0^1(1-t)\lambda_i(t)\,dt=0,\qquad
\int_0^1t\lambda_i(t)\,dt=0
\tag{3}
\]
on every edge. Consequently
\[
\ker L_P^{\,T}
=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^6:\text{\((3)\) holds on every edge}\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the requested exact rank test.

For a nonempty interval \(I\subset(0,1)\), put
\[
m(I)=\left(\int_I(1-t)\,dt,\int_I t\,dt\right).
\]
If \(I\) lies strictly before a disjoint interval \(J\), then
\[
\det(m(I),m(J))
=|I|\,|J|\,(\overline t_J-\overline t_I)>0,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\overline t_I,\overline t_J\) are their midpoints. Thus the
moment columns of any two disjoint positive-length edge pieces are
linearly independent.

Because \(O\in\operatorname{int}P\), the six fan rays meet the boundary
once each and in cyclic order. Let \(k_i\) be the number met on the
\(i\)-th edge. Then
\[
0\leq k_i\leq3,\qquad \sum_i k_i=6.
\tag{6}
\]
An edge with \(k_i=0\) forces its one sector weight to be zero by
(3). An edge with \(k_i=1\) forces both of its sector weights to be
zero by (5). An edge with \(k_i=2\) has three sector pieces; if any
one of their weights is zero, (5) forces the other two to be zero.
An edge with \(k_i=3\) has four sector pieces; if two of their weights
are zero, (5) forces the other two to be zero.

Suppose a nonzero \(\lambda\) satisfies (3). There can be no edge with
\(k_i=1\). Indeed, its two consecutive zero weights propagate across
every adjacent edge with at most two crossings. There cannot be two
three-crossing edges in addition to it because their crossing counts
would already exceed six; hence the propagation reaches both ends of
the possible unique three-crossing edge and kills it as well.

Thus every \(k_i\) is \(0,2\), or \(3\). If a three-crossing edge
occurs, parity in (6) forces exactly two of them and all remaining
edges have zero crossings. A zero-crossing edge in each of the two
opposite endpoint sectors would set both endpoint weights to zero and
then kill both three-crossing edges. Hence all zero-crossing edges
must lie in just one endpoint sector. This is case 1. Conversely, in
case 1 that sector weight is zero, and after prescribing the opposite
sector weight, (5) uniquely solves for the two interior weights on
each three-crossing edge. This gives a one-dimensional kernel.

If no three-crossing edge occurs, (6) gives exactly three
two-crossing edges. Any zero-crossing edge would provide a zero weight
which propagates through all three two-crossing edges, so there are no
other edges. The polygon is a triangle with two crossings on every
edge. On an oriented edge whose sector weights in order are
\(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\), direct solution of (3) gives
\[
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}
=-\frac{a(1+b-a)}{(b-a)(1-a)},
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_0}
=\frac{ab}{(1-a)(1-b)}.
\tag{7}
\]
All three components of this edge null vector are nonzero. Going
around the triangle, compatibility is exactly the product identity
in case 2. If it holds, the kernel is one-dimensional; if it fails,
the kernel is zero. This proves the rank classification and also
\(\operatorname{rank}L_P\geq5\).

We next impose the normalized-cap constraints. In sector coordinates
let
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_1&=(1,0,0,0,1,1),\\
h_2&=(1,1,1,0,0,0),\\
h_3&=(0,0,1,1,1,0),
\end{aligned}
\qquad \mathbf 1=(1,1,1,1,1,1),
\]
and put
\[
u_i=h_i-p_i\mathbf 1.
\tag{8}
\]
Up to the common positive factor \(1/T\), the first derivative of
\(p_i\) is \(u_i\cdot L_PV\). In every rank-five case a nonzero vector
in \(\ker L_P^T\) has unequal values in \(B\) and \(C\): on each
relevant edge the moment-null signs alternate, and in case 1 a zero
endpoint is followed by a nonzero value. Since every linear
combination of \(u_1,u_2\) has equal \(B\)- and \(C\)-components, the
two constraint differentials are independent. Indeed, if
\(\alpha u_1+\beta u_2\in\ker L_P^T\), comparison at \(B,C\) first
forces the kernel coefficient to vanish; comparison at \(A,B\) and
\(C,D\) then gives \(\alpha=\beta=0\). The same independence is
immediate when \(L_P\) is onto.

The ordinary equality-constrained multiplier rule therefore applies.
At a local minimum there must be \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R\) such
that
\[
L_P^T(u_3-\alpha u_1-\beta u_2)=0.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(L_P\) is onto, (9) would say
\(u_3=\alpha u_1+\beta u_2\), which is impossible because the
\(B,C\) components of the right side are equal and those of \(u_3\)
differ by \(1\). Thus only the two rank-five cases can remain.

We now exclude the nontriangular rank-five case, in fact also the
three-vertex \(3,3,0\) case. A permutation of the three forms, and if
necessary simultaneous replacement \(\ell_i\mapsto-\ell_i\), sends
its opposite pair of sectors to \(A,D\). These operations preserve
\[
\mathcal W=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,h_1,h_2,h_3\}.
\tag{10}
\]
We may therefore suppose that the unique vertex is in \(A\), all
other vertices are in \(D\), and the two incident edges run through
\[
A,B,C,D\qquad\text{and}\qquad A,F,E,D.
\]
Every other edge lies in \(D\), so (3) gives \(\lambda_D=0\).
Normalize the one-dimensional annihilator by \(\lambda_A=1\).

Write the unique \(A\)-vertex in form coordinates as
\[
(-x,-y),\qquad x,y>0,\qquad
\theta=\frac{x}{x+y}.
\]
On the first incident edge let its \(D\)-endpoint have normalized
positive form values
\[
X=\frac{\ell_1}{x},\qquad Y=\frac{\ell_2}{y},\qquad X>Y>0,
\]
and put
\[
R=\frac{1+Y}{X-Y}>0.
\]
Its three crossing parameters are
\[
\frac1{1+X},\qquad
\frac1{1+Y+\theta(X-Y)},\qquad
\frac1{1+Y}.
\]
Solving its two moment equations gives
\[
\lambda_B=-\frac{R^2+2R+\theta}{1-\theta},
\qquad
\lambda_C=\frac{R^2}{\theta}.
\tag{11}
\]
On the other incident edge write its normalized endpoint values as
\[
X'=\frac{\ell_1}{x},\qquad Y'=\frac{\ell_2}{y},\qquad Y'>X'>0,
\qquad
S=\frac{1+X'}{Y'-X'}>0.
\]
The same calculation, with \(\theta\) replaced by \(1-\theta\), gives
\[
\lambda_F=-\frac{S^2+2S+1-\theta}{\theta},
\qquad
\lambda_E=\frac{S^2}{1-\theta}.
\tag{12}
\]

If (9) held, its vector
\(u_3-\alpha u_1-\beta u_2\) would be nonzero by the same
\(B,C\)-component comparison, so it would span the annihilator line
and that line would belong to \(\mathcal W\). Every
\(w\in\mathcal W\) satisfies
\[
w_A-w_B=w_E-w_D,\qquad
w_C-w_D=w_A-w_F.
\tag{13}
\]
With \(w_A=1,w_D=0\), equations (11)-(13) first give
\[
S^2=(R+1)^2,
\]
so \(S=R+1\). The second relation then gives
\[
\theta-(R+2)^2=\theta-R^2,
\]
which would force \(R=-1\), contrary to \(R>0\). Hence the
rank-deficient \(3+3\) polygon cannot satisfy KKT.

It remains to classify the two-crossings-per-edge triangle. Its
vertices occupy either \(A,C,E\) or \(B,D,F\). We show that the first
orientation is incompatible with (9) and \(p_1,p_2\leq1/2\).
In the \((u,v)=(\ell_1,\ell_2)\) coordinates write its vertices as
\[
P_A=(-x,-y),\qquad
P_C=(a,-b),\qquad
P_E=(-c,d),
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
x,y,a,b,c,d>0,\qquad a>b,\qquad d>c.
\]
Uniform dilation lets us put \(x=1\). Define
\[
r=\frac ab>1,\qquad s=\frac dc>1.
\tag{15}
\]
The three ordered pairs of crossing parameters on
\(P_AP_C,P_CP_E,P_EP_A\) are
\[
\left(\frac{x}{x+a},
\frac{x+y}{x+y+a-b}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{b}{b+d},
\frac{a}{a+c}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{d-c}{d-c+x+y},
\frac{d}{d+y}\right).
\]
Substitution of these six parameters in (7) shows that the
rank-five product identity is
\[
c\,y(a-b)=b\,x(d-c),
\]
and hence
\[
y=\frac{s-1}{r-1}.
\tag{16}
\]

Normalize the annihilator by \(w_A=1\). If (9) holds then
\(w\in\mathcal W\), so it satisfies the two relations (13).
Substituting (14)-(16) into the edge moment equations (3), and then
into (13), cancels positive common factors and gives exactly
\[
bc\,r(r-1)-br+c(r-2)=0,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
bc\,s(r-1)+b(s-2)-cs=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Solving these two equations gives the unique positive possibility
\[
b=\frac{r+s-2}{r(r-1)(s-1)},
\qquad
c=\frac{r+s-2}{s(r-1)^2}.
\tag{19}
\]
For completeness, (17) gives
\(c[br(r-1)+r-2]=br\); substituting this in (18) and simplifying gives
the first formula in (19), and the displayed expression for \(c\)
then follows back from (17).

In this \(A,C,E\) triangle, the complement of \(H_1\) is the triangular
cap at \(P_C\), while the complement of \(H_2\) is the triangular cap
at \(P_E\). Therefore
\[
1-p_1=\frac{a^2}{(a+1)(a+c)},\qquad
1-p_2=\frac{d^2}{(d+b)(d+y)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Using (15), (16), and (19), these become
\[
1-p_1=
\frac{s(r-1)(r+s-2)}{(rs-1)^2},
\qquad
1-p_2=
\frac{r(s-1)(r+s-2)}{(rs-1)^2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Their sum is strictly less than \(1\), because
\[
1-(1-p_1)-(1-p_2)
=\frac{(r-1)^2(s-1)^2}{(rs-1)^2}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(p_1+p_2>1\), contradicting
\(p_1,p_2\leq1/2\). The \(A,C,E\) orientation is impossible.

The only remaining possibility is therefore a rank-five triangle
with vertices in \(B,D,F\), as asserted.

Finally, the vertices of the rational rectangle in the statement are
listed in \((z,\tau)\)-coordinates. They satisfy
\[
2\leq\tau\leq3,\qquad |z|\leq\frac14<\tau,
\]
so the rectangle lies strictly in
\[
D=\{\tau>0,\ -\tau<z<\tau\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\ell_1=z+\tau>0,\qquad
\ell_2=\tau-z>0,\qquad
\ell_3=-2\tau<0
\]
throughout the rectangle. Thus \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) have zero area in
it, while \(H_3\) contains all of it, giving
\((p_1,p_2,p_3)=(0,0,1)\). More generally, any convex body with
\(p_1=p_2=0\) lies up to a null set in
\(\{\ell_1>0,\ell_2>0\}=D\), where \(\ell_3<0\); hence \(p_3=1\).
This proves both the stated ratios and the global-minimizer claim,
completing the proof.
