## Statement

For functions \(f,g:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\), use the convention
\[
(f\square g)(s):=\inf_{r\in\mathbb R}\bigl(f(r)+g(s-r)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(u\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(z\in A_1\), the endpoint contribution
to a boundary-through-\((1,z)\) halfspace with planar direction \(u\),
optimized over its slope, is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left[
\gamma_0^u\bigl(\langle u,z\rangle+\lambda-m_0(u)\bigr)
+\gamma_2^u\bigl(\langle u,z\rangle-\lambda-m_2(u)\bigr)
\right]\\
&\hspace{35mm}
=(\gamma_0^u\square\gamma_2^u)\bigl(d_u(z)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the whole fixed-direction middle profile is
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{w:\langle u,w-z\rangle\leq0\}\right|
+(\gamma_0^u\square\gamma_2^u)\bigl(d_u(z)\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

Put \(K:=-(A_0+A_2)\).  If \(u_1,u_2,u_3\in\mathbb S^1\) and
\(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3>0\) satisfy
\(\sum_{i=1}^3\nu_i u_i=0\), then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\nu_i d_{u_i}(z)
=\sum_{i=1}^3\nu_i h_K(u_i)\geq0.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(L:=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,u_2,u_3\}\), let
\[
F_K(v):=\{x\in K:\langle v,x\rangle=h_K(v)\},
\]
and use the outer normal cone
\[
N_K(x):=\{v:\langle v,y-x\rangle\leq0\text{ for every }y\in K\}.
\]
The following are equivalent:
\[
\begin{split}
&\sum_{i=1}^3\nu_i h_K(u_i)=0; \tag{5a}\\
&\bigcap_{i=1}^3F_K(u_i)\neq\varnothing; \tag{5b}\\
&\text{there is }x\in K\text{ such that }u_i\in N_K(x)
  \text{ for all }i; \tag{5c}\\
&\text{there is }x\in K\text{ such that }K\subseteq x+L^\perp.
  \tag{5d}
\end{split}
\]
In particular, (5) forces \(K\) to be a singleton when \(L=\mathbb R^2\),
but only forces containment in a line when \(\dim L=1\).  If \(K\) is
full-dimensional, neither case is possible, and (4) is strict.

For a two-dimensional endpoint body, its cap function satisfies
\(\gamma_i^u(r)>0\) for every \(r>0\).  Consequently the individual bonus
has the exact zero set
\[
(\gamma_0^u\square\gamma_2^u)\bigl(d_u(z)\bigr)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d_u(z)\leq0.
\tag{6}
\]
Under the strict-cap property above, in particular for the present
two-dimensional endpoint bodies, all three bonuses in the directions
\(u_i\) vanish simultaneously if and only if there is \(x\in K\) such that
\[
u_i\in N_K(x)\quad(i=1,2,3),
\qquad
-2z-x\in L^\perp.
\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently, support equality holds and \(d_{u_i}(z)=0\) for every \(i\).
For the two-dimensional endpoint bodies in the locked problem, \(K\) is
full-dimensional, so (7) cannot occur.  Hence at least one of the three
bonuses is strictly positive.

## Proof

Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb`, specialized to \(j=1\), says that a
non-slice-parallel boundary-through-\((1,z)\) halfspace with planar
direction \(u\) and slope \(\lambda\) cuts from the endpoint slices the
sets
\[
A_0\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle+\lambda\},
\qquad
A_2\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle-\lambda\}.
\]
Their areas are respectively
\[
\gamma_0^u\bigl(\langle u,z\rangle+\lambda-m_0(u)\bigr),
\qquad
\gamma_2^u\bigl(\langle u,z\rangle-\lambda-m_2(u)\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]
Set
\[
r:=\langle u,z\rangle+\lambda-m_0(u).
\]
As \(\lambda\) runs through \(\mathbb R\), so does \(r\), and the second
argument in (8) becomes
\[
\langle u,z\rangle-\lambda-m_2(u)
=2\langle u,z\rangle-m_0(u)-m_2(u)-r
=d_u(z)-r.
\]
Taking the infimum proves (2).  The middle-slice inequality is
\(\langle u,w-z\rangle\leq0\), independent of \(\lambda\), which proves
(3).  At argument zero, the actual closed cap is contained in a support
line and therefore has planar area zero, so the convention
\(\gamma_i^u(r)=0\) for \(r\leq0\) matches the halfspace cap exactly.

For the closing identity, support functions and minimum supports give
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_K(u)
&=\max_{a_0\in A_0,\,a_2\in A_2}
  \langle u,-a_0-a_2\rangle\\
&=-m_0(u)-m_2(u).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Hence
\[
d_u(z)=2\langle u,z\rangle+h_K(u)
=h_K(u)-\langle u,-2z\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]
After multiplying (10) by \(\nu_i\) and summing, the \(z\)-term vanishes
because \(\sum_i\nu_i u_i=0\), proving the identity in (4).  Sublinearity
and positive homogeneity of the support function give
\[
0=h_K(0)
=h_K\left(\sum_i\nu_i u_i\right)
\leq\sum_i\nu_i h_K(u_i),
\]
which proves its inequality.

We next audit equality without assuming that the directions span
\(\mathbb R^2\).  For any \(x\in K\), put
\[
g_i(x):=h_K(u_i)-\langle u_i,x\rangle\geq0.
\]
The positive dependence among the directions yields
\[
\sum_i\nu_i g_i(x)
=\sum_i\nu_i h_K(u_i)
-\left\langle\sum_i\nu_i u_i,x\right\rangle
=\sum_i\nu_i h_K(u_i).
\tag{11}
\]
If the last expression is zero, positivity of every \(\nu_i\) forces
\(g_i(x)=0\) for every \(i\).  Thus \(x\in F_K(u_i)\), equivalently
\(u_i\in N_K(x)\), for every \(i\).  Conversely, one common support point
gives
\[
\sum_i\nu_i h_K(u_i)
=\left\langle\sum_i\nu_i u_i,x\right\rangle=0.
\]
This proves (5a)--(5c).

Suppose (5c) holds and \(y\in K\).  Then
\(\langle u_i,x-y\rangle\geq0\) for every \(i\), while
\[
\sum_i\nu_i\langle u_i,x-y\rangle
=\left\langle\sum_i\nu_i u_i,x-y\right\rangle=0.
\]
Again positivity of the weights forces every summand to vanish.  Hence
\(\langle u_i,y-x\rangle=0\) for all \(i\), or
\(y-x\in L^\perp\).  This proves (5d).  Conversely, (5d) makes each
\(\langle u_i,\cdot\rangle\) constant on \(K\), so every \(u_i\) lies in
\(N_K(x)\), proving (5c).  This also proves the stated distinction between
\(L=\mathbb R^2\) and \(\dim L=1\).  Since the \(u_i\) are nonzero,
\(\dim L\geq1\), so (5d) is incompatible with a full-dimensional planar
body.

It remains to prove the zero assertions.  Let \(A\) be a planar convex body,
let \(m=\min_A\langle u,\cdot\rangle\), and fix \(r>0\).  Choose
\(p\in A\) with \(\langle u,p\rangle=m\) and \(y\in\operatorname{int}A\).
Then
\(\delta:=\langle u,y-p\rangle>0\).  Choose \(t\in(0,1)\) with
\(t\delta<r\).  If \(B(y,\varepsilon)\subseteq A\), convexity gives
\[
B\bigl((1-t)p+ty,t\varepsilon\bigr)
=(1-t)p+tB(y,\varepsilon)\subseteq A.
\]
After decreasing its radius, this ball also lies in
\(\{\langle u,\cdot\rangle<m+r\}\).  The cap therefore has positive area.
This proves
\[
\gamma_A^u(r)>0\qquad(r>0).
\tag{12}
\]

If \(d\leq0\), choose any \(r\in[d,0]\).  Then \(r\leq0\) and
\(d-r\leq0\), so both cap terms vanish and
\((\gamma_0^u\square\gamma_2^u)(d)=0\).  If \(d>0\), then for every
\(r\in\mathbb R\), either \(r\geq d/2\), in which case
\(\gamma_0^u(r)\geq\gamma_0^u(d/2)>0\), or \(r\leq d/2\), in which case
\(\gamma_2^u(d-r)\geq\gamma_2^u(d/2)>0\).  In fact every sum in (1) is at
least
\[
\min\{\gamma_0^u(d/2),\gamma_2^u(d/2)\}>0.
\]
This proves (6), including strict positivity rather than mere nonattainment
of zero.

Finally, suppose all three bonuses vanish.  By (6),
\(d_{u_i}(z)\leq0\) for all \(i\).  Their positively weighted sum is the
nonnegative quantity in (4), so it must be zero; positivity of the weights
then forces
\[
d_{u_i}(z)=0\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{13}
\]
Support equality gives an \(x\in K\) satisfying the normal-cone condition
in (7).  Combining (10) and (13) gives
\[
\langle u_i,-2z-x\rangle=0\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
which is exactly \(-2z-x\in L^\perp\).  Conversely, (7) gives
\(h_K(u_i)=\langle u_i,x\rangle=\langle u_i,-2z\rangle\), hence
\(d_{u_i}(z)=0\), and (6) makes every bonus zero.  This proves (7).
Because \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) have nonempty interior, so do
\(A_0+A_2\) and \(K\); the full-dimensional conclusion follows.
