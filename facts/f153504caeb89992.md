## Statement

Let a sequence satisfy the actual dominant all-double residual-counterexample hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted facts `0e51a47a9add571f`, `2f637c4da2df28f1`, and `174b54e9843ef8fc`.  Normalize
\[
m_n=|A_{2,n}|=1.
\]
Write
\[
M_n=|A_{0,n}|,\qquad k_n=|K_n|,
\qquad
\Xi_n=U_n+q_n+E_{R,n}.
\]
Let
\[
c_{i,n}
=\left|K_n\cap\{f_{i,n}\leq0\}\right|.
\]
Assume that, after the stated \(A_2\)-John normalization, height shear, and passage to a subsequence, the three bodies and the normalized middle cutting functions converge:
\[
K_n\longrightarrow K,\qquad
\phi_{i,n}\longrightarrow\phi_i,\qquad
\alpha_{i,n}\longrightarrow\alpha_i,
\]
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\phi_i=\sigma,\qquad
\sigma\geq0,\qquad
\alpha_i\geq0,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i=1,
\tag{1}
\]
where every \(\phi_i\) has unit gradient.  The boundedness needed to take the scalar subsequence follows from accepted fact `366887b2b328c518`.  Write
\[
M_n\to M,\quad k_n\to k,\quad
\Xi_n\to\Xi,\quad c_{i,n}\to c_i.
\]
Then for every distinct \(i,j\),
\[
k-c_i-c_j
\geq
\frac{1+\Xi}{3}
\geq\frac13.                                             \tag{2}
\]
For the two subordinate indices,
\[
k-c_2-c_3
\geq
\frac89+\frac{\Xi}{3}.                                  \tag{3}
\]

Consequently, neither of the following limiting types can occur.

1. Exactly two weights are positive.
2. All three weights are positive and the three limiting gradients are collinear.

Thus every such normalized limit satisfies
\[
\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla\phi_1,\nabla\phi_2,\nabla\phi_3\}
=\mathbb R^2.                                            \tag{4}
\]

## Proof

At every finite stage, accepted facts `174b54e9843ef8fc` and `67b6f734f1157b4c` give
\[
2M_n=4+k_n+3\Xi_n,
\]
and hence
\[
k_n=2M_n-4-3\Xi_n,\qquad
h_n=\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9
=\frac{2(M_n-1-\Xi_n)}3.                                \tag{5}
\]
The crossings and all-double identity \(v_{i,n}=1-x_{i,n}\) give
\[
c_{i,n}=h_n-u_{i,n}-1+x_{i,n}.                          \tag{6}
\]
Combining (5)-(6) yields, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
k_n-c_{i,n}-c_{j,n}
=\frac{1}{3}\bigl(
&2M_n-2-5\Xi_n\\
&-3(x_{i,n}+x_{j,n})
+3(u_{i,n}+u_{j,n})
\bigr).
\end{aligned}                                            \tag{7}
\]

Accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives
\[
M_n>3+3\Xi_n.                                            \tag{8}
\]
Every \(u_{i,n}\) is nonnegative, while the exact positive \(Q\)-partition gives
\[
x_{i,n}+x_{j,n}<1.
\]
Substitution in (7) proves the uniform finite-stage gap
\[
k_n-c_{i,n}-c_{j,n}>
\frac{1+\Xi_n}{3}.                                       \tag{9}
\]
For \(\{i,j\}=\{2,3\}\), the persistent dominant-cell inequality in accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` is
\[
q_n+x_{2,n}+x_{3,n}<\frac49.
\]
Using \(x_{2,n}+x_{3,n}<4/9\) in (7) gives
\[
k_n-c_{2,n}-c_{3,n}>
\frac89+\frac{\Xi_n}{3}.                                \tag{10}
\]
Passing to the convergent scalar subsequence proves (2)-(3).

The exact-cell convention in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, Hausdorff convergence of \(K_n\), and local uniform convergence of the affine functions give
\[
c_i=\left|K\cap\{\phi_i\geq0\}\right|.                  \tag{11}
\]
Indeed, \(\lambda_{i,n}=-f_{i,n}\) and
\(\phi_{i,n}\) is a positive normalization of \(\lambda_{i,n}\), while a nonconstant affine zero line has planar area zero.

Suppose exactly two limiting weights, say \(\alpha_i,\alpha_j\), are positive.  Taking gradients in (1) shows that their unit gradients are opposite and their weights are equal:
\[
\alpha_i=\alpha_j=\frac12.
\]
Therefore
\[
\phi_j=2\sigma-\phi_i.
\]
Since \(\sigma\geq0\),
\[
\{\phi_i\geq0\}\cup\{\phi_j\geq0\}
=\{\phi_i\geq0\}\cup\{\phi_i\leq2\sigma\}
=\mathbb R^2.
\]
Equation (11) would then give
\[
k=|K|
\leq c_i+c_j,
\]
contrary to (2).  This excludes the two-weight type.  It strengthens the two-weight exclusion mechanism recorded in accepted fact `366887b2b328c518`: no \(4/9\) equality limit is needed.

A single positive limiting weight is impossible directly from (1), because it would make one nonconstant affine function identically equal to the constant \(\sigma\).  Therefore exclusion of the two-weight type leaves all three weights positive.

Now suppose all three weights are positive and the gradients are collinear.  Choose a unit linear coordinate \(t\).  Since their positive weighted sum has zero gradient, both orientations occur.  Relabel so that one orientation occurs only at index \(a\):
\[
\phi_a=r_a-t,\qquad
\phi_b=t-r_b,\qquad
\phi_c=t-r_c.
\]
The gradient part of (1) gives
\[
\alpha_a=\alpha_b+\alpha_c=\frac12.
\]
The constant part gives
\[
\alpha_a r_a-\alpha_b r_b-\alpha_c r_c=\sigma\geq0,
\]
and hence
\[
r_a\geq
\frac{\alpha_b}{\alpha_a}r_b
+
\frac{\alpha_c}{\alpha_a}r_c
\geq\min\{r_b,r_c\}.                                    \tag{12}
\]
Choose \(b\) so that \(r_b=\min\{r_b,r_c\}\).  Then
\[
\{\phi_a\geq0\}\cup\{\phi_b\geq0\}
=\{t\leq r_a\}\cup\{t\geq r_b\}
=\mathbb R^2.
\]
Again (11) gives \(k\leq c_a+c_b\), contradicting (2).  This excludes the three-weight rank-one type and proves (4).

For clarity, the endpoint \(Q\)-geometry alone would not exclude rank one.  The accepted dominant and centroid facts persist as
\[
|Q_{23,\infty}|\geq\frac59,\qquad
g_{2,\infty}\in\overline{Q_{23,\infty}},
\]
and convexity makes the positive limiting sign support connected along the projected coordinate.  For example, on
\[
A_2=[0,1]\times[0,1],
\]
the collinear signed \(Q\)-functions
\[
d_1=4-20t,\qquad d_2=10t,\qquad d_3=10t-1
\]
sum to \(3\) and have connected positive support
\[
Q_{12}:\ 0<t<\frac1{10},\qquad
Q_{123}:\ \frac1{10}<t<\frac15,\qquad
Q_{23}:\ \frac15<t<1.
\]
Thus \(Q_{23}\) has area \(4/5\), and the centroid of \(A_2\) lies strictly in it.  Choosing constants \(L_i\) with sum \(-2\) and putting
\[
\lambda_i=d_i+L_i,\qquad f_i=-\lambda_i
\]
gives \(\sum_i f_i=-1\) and the required endpoint threshold sum.  What prevents this exact rank-one endpoint picture from extending to a residual limit is precisely the middle pairwise gap (2), not endpoint connectedness by itself.

Accepted fact `67b6f734f1157b4c` gives additional finite-stage individual cap bounds according to whether the midpoint \(z\) lies in \(R_2,R_3\), or \(R_{23}\); their weak forms persist on convergent sign-pattern subsequences.  They are compatible with (2) but are not needed once a parallel dependence forces a covering pair.  Likewise, accepted fact `1ba5106642a3b8bd` gives
\[
r_{23}\geq
\left[\theta_2^2+\theta_3^2-1\right]_+\sigma_1.
\]
The parallel hypotheses alone do not force
\(\theta_2^2+\theta_3^2>1\), and no accepted predecessor supplies that additional inequality.  The pairwise uncovered-area gap therefore supplies the required boundary exclusion without an extra transverse-support assumption.
