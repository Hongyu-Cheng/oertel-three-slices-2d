## Statement

Let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).  Use the common-plane
exact-cell convention of accepted facts `8b9c428a36c45d68` and
`816813cf31d6daf5`.  Put
\[
\pi_s=f_s-L_s,\qquad
\theta_s=f_s+L_s,
\]
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,\qquad
\sigma=\delta+2,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\sigma}{\delta}=1+\frac2\delta.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\gamma>0\) be the inverse area Jacobian of
\((\pi_i,\pi_j)\), equivalently of \((\theta_i,\theta_j)\).
Assume
\[
\overline{P_0}=T_\delta
:=\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq\delta\},
\qquad
p_0=\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{2},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
q_j=0,\qquad
q_iq_kq_{ij}q_{ik}q_{jk}>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(B,K\) be compact convex bodies such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq B\subseteq K.
\tag{4}
\]
Write \(\rho_G\) and \(r_G\) for the exact cells of \(B\) and
\(K\), respectively, and assume \(W=0\) in the convention of
`8b9c428a36c45d68`.  Put
\[
a_2=|A_2|=\sum_Gq_G,\qquad
b=|B|=\sum_G\rho_G.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(s\in\{i,k\}\), define the accepted singleton support
allowance
\[
\Lambda_s=\frac{4\rho_s-p_0-q_s}{2p_0}.
\tag{6}
\]
Every realization has
\[
\Lambda_s\geq\sqrt{\frac{q_s}{p_0}}.
\tag{7}
\]

Then \(q_{123}>0\).  Define
\[
T=\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^2-\frac{q_{123}}{p_0}}.
\tag{8}
\]
The radical is real, and for at least one
\(s\in\{i,k\}\) both inequalities
\[
\boxed{\quad
q_{sj}\leq
2p_0\Lambda_sT+
\frac{p_0^2}{q_s}\Lambda_s^2T^2
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
q_{sj}\leq
(4b-p_0-a_2)T
\leq
(7p_0-a_2)T
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
hold.  Here \(q_{sj}=q_{ij}\) when \(s=i\), and
\(q_{sj}=q_{jk}\) when \(s=k\).  The conclusion includes a
terminating horizontal support face of positive length; no
zero-length endpoint section is assumed.

Now fix all \(P,\rho,r\) data from accepted fact
`9aea6365aa810898`, but allow the \(Q\)-table to vary subject to
\[
q_2=0,\qquad
\sum_Gq_G=\frac1{100},
\tag{11}
\]
\[
\left(\sum_{G\ni1}q_G,\sum_{G\ni2}q_G,
\sum_{G\ni3}q_G\right)
=\left(\frac1{125},\frac7{2000},\frac3{1000}\right),
\tag{12}
\]
with every other non-forced \(Q\)-cell positive.  There is no
compact convex common-plane realization of any table on this
face together with the locked middle data.  Equivalently, the
entire two-parameter positive face (11)--(12), including the
specific table of `9aea6365aa810898`, is excluded.

## Proof

Relabel to \((i,j,k)=(1,2,3)\); the general statement follows by
permutation.  Work in
\[
u=\theta_1,\qquad
v=-\theta_2,\qquad
\theta_3=v-\sigma-u,
\tag{13}
\]
and write every nonempty horizontal section of the coordinate
image \(Q\) of \(A_2\) as
\[
Q_v=\{u:\ell(v)\leq u\leq r(v)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The function \(\ell\) is convex and \(r\) is concave.  Put
\[
c(v)=v-\sigma
\]
and define the two convex tail bodies
\[
\mathcal L=Q\cap\{u\leq c(v)\},\qquad
\mathcal R=Q\cap\{u\geq0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Let their vertical projection intervals be
\[
[\alpha_1,\beta_1],\qquad
[\alpha_3,\beta_3],
\tag{16}
\]
and let \(f_1,f_3\) be their horizontal section-length
functions.  These functions are nonnegative and concave.

For \(v<0\), the left, middle, and right chambers are
\[
Q_1,\quad Q_{13},\quad Q_3.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(0<v<\sigma\), they are
\[
Q_{12},\quad Q_{123},\quad Q_{23}.
\tag{18}
\]
The four positive cells \(Q_1,Q_3,Q_{12},Q_{23}\) imply
\[
\alpha_s<0<\beta_s\qquad(s=1,3).
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
\beta=\min\{\beta_1,\beta_3\}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\beta>\sigma\), choose
\(\sigma<v<\beta\).  Both tail sections then have positive
length.  The interval \(Q_v\) contains a point strictly left of
\(c(v)>0\) and a point strictly right of \(0\), so it contains
\((0,c(v))\).  This gives \(Q_2\) positive area on an interval of
heights, contrary to \(q_2=0\).  Hence
\[
\beta\leq\sigma.
\tag{21}
\]

For every \(0<v<\beta\), the interval \(Q_v\) contains
\([c(v),0]\).  Its interior is in \(Q_{123}\), so
\[
\frac{q_{123}}{\gamma}
\geq
\int_0^\beta(\sigma-v)\,dv
=\sigma\beta-\frac{\beta^2}{2}.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves \(q_{123}>0\).  The coefficient \(7\) of
\(q_{123}\) in \(W\), nonnegativity of every other positive
contribution, and \(W=0\) give
\[
7q_{123}\leq2p_0.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus the radical in (8) is real.  Divide (22) by
\(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\) and put \(y=\beta/\delta\).  Since
\(0<y\leq\lambda\),
\[
\lambda y-\frac{y^2}{2}
\leq\frac{q_{123}}{2p_0}.
\]
The left side is increasing on \([0,\lambda]\), and solving the
quadratic gives
\[
\frac{\beta}{\delta}\leq T.
\tag{24}
\]

At least one tail, indexed by \(s\in\{1,3\}\), has
\(\beta_s=\beta\).  Fix such an \(s\), put
\[
A_-=\int_{\alpha_s}^0f_s(v)\,dv=\frac{q_s}{\gamma},
\qquad
A_+=\int_0^\beta f_s(v)\,dv=\frac{q_{s2}}{\gamma},
\tag{25}
\]
and let
\[
a=f_s(0)>0,\qquad d=f'_{s,-}(0).
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (25) is unchanged if the terminal section has positive
length, because one horizontal section has planar area zero.
Concavity gives the global supporting line
\[
f_s(v)\leq a+dv.
\tag{27}
\]
If \(d>0\), integration of its positive part on the negative
half-line gives
\[
A_-\leq\frac{a^2}{2d},
\qquad
d\leq\frac{a^2}{2A_-}.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(d\leq0\), use \(d_+=0\).  In both cases, integration on
\([0,\beta]\) yields
\[
A_+\leq
a\beta+\frac{a^2\beta^2}{4A_-}.
\tag{29}
\]

Let \(t_s\) be the singleton support parameter in accepted fact
`816813cf31d6daf5`.  The section at \(v=0\) belongs to the
closure of the negative-height singleton tail: join any point of
that section to a strict point of \(Q_s\), and approach the section
point along the resulting segment from \(v<0\).  Therefore
\[
a\leq t_s.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (21)--(22) of that accepted fact give
\[
\frac{t_s}{\delta}\leq\Lambda_s.
\tag{31}
\]
Substitute (24)--(25) and (30)--(31) into (29), multiply by
\(\gamma\), and use \(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\).  The result is
exactly (9).

For the global estimate, define
\[
w_Q=
\max_Q(\theta_1+\theta_2)
-\min_Q\theta_1-\min_Q\theta_2.
\tag{32}
\]
The exact triangle Minkowski identity is
\[
|T_\delta+Q|_{\rm coord}
=\frac{\delta^2}{2}+|Q|_{\rm coord}+\delta w_Q.
\tag{33}
\]
Since \((P_0+A_2)/2\subseteq B\), equation (33) gives
\[
b\geq
\frac14\left(p_0+a_2+\gamma\delta w_Q\right).
\tag{34}
\]
Every nonempty middle cell has \(W\)-coefficient at least \(1\).
Also \(B\subseteq K\) gives \(\rho_G\leq r_G\) cell by cell.
Therefore
\[
b=\sum_G\rho_G
\leq
\sum_G(3|G|-2)r_G
\leq2p_0,
\tag{35}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(W=0\) after discarding
the nonnegative endpoint contributions.  Equations (34)--(35)
imply
\[
\frac{w_Q}{\delta}
\leq
\frac{4b-p_0-a_2}{2p_0}
\leq
\frac{7p_0-a_2}{2p_0}.
\tag{36}
\]
Every horizontal section length of \(Q\), and hence every
\(f_s(v)\), is at most \(w_Q\).  Indeed, if
\((u_-,y),(u_+,y)\in Q\), then the definition (32) gives
\[
w_Q\geq(u_++y)-u_--y=u_+-u_-.
\]
Consequently
\[
q_{s2}
=\gamma A_+
\leq\gamma w_Q\beta.
\tag{37}
\]
Combining (24), (36), and
\(\gamma\delta^2=2p_0\) proves (10).  Since the same terminating
tail satisfies (9) and (10), the common-breakpoint theorem is
proved.

We now audit the locked face.  From `9aea6365aa810898`,
\[
D=16072081,\qquad
p_0=\frac{16000000}{D},\qquad
\delta=10,\qquad
\lambda=\frac65,
\tag{38}
\]
\[
\rho_1=\frac{278173684303}{964324860000},
\qquad
\rho_3=\frac{83927775767}{321441620000}.
\tag{39}
\]
Put
\[
x=q_3,\qquad z=q_{123}.
\]
Solving the total and three cap-sum equations (11)--(12) gives
\[
q_1=\frac{11}{2000}-x+z,\qquad
q_{12}=\frac3{2000}+x-z,
\tag{40}
\]
\[
q_{13}=\frac1{1000}-z,\qquad
q_{23}=\frac1{500}-x.
\tag{41}
\]
Conversely, (40)--(41) has exactly the locked total and cap sums.
Positivity gives
\[
0<x<\frac1{500},\qquad
0\leq z<\frac1{1000}.
\tag{42}
\]
The case \(z=0\) contradicts (22), so henceforth \(z>0\).
Moreover, the weighted \(Q\)-contribution is
\[
\sum_G(3|G|-2)q_G
=3\sum_{s=1}^3\sum_{G\ni s}q_G-2\sum_Gq_G,
\]
so it depends only on (11)--(12).  Since the locked
\(P,\rho,r\) data and the table in `9aea6365aa810898` have
\(W=0\), every table on this face has the same \(W=0\).  Thus all
hypotheses of the common-breakpoint theorem apply to any putative
realization on the face.

For \(s=1,3\), define
\[
\Delta_s(q)=(4\rho_s-p_0-q)^2-4p_0q.
\tag{43}
\]
The singleton condition (7) implies
\(\Delta_s(q_s)\geq0\).  On all ranges in (42),
\[
\Delta_s'(q)=2(q-4\rho_s-p_0)<0.
\tag{44}
\]
For example, \(q<1/100\), while
\[
p_0-\frac{99}{100}
=\frac{8863981}{1607208100}>0,
\]
so the sign in (44) is strict without numerical approximation.
Exact substitution gives
\[
\Delta_3\left(\frac3{5000}\right)
=-\frac{23638478733805839}
{403612168235251562500}<0,
\tag{45}
\]
\[
\Delta_1\left(\frac{5841}{1000000}\right)
=-\frac{5001780810161632853831}
{2324806089035049000000000000}<0.
\tag{46}
\]
Thus
\[
x<\frac3{5000},\qquad
q_1<\frac{5841}{1000000}.
\tag{47}
\]

The exact upper allowances are
\[
\frac2{25}
-\frac{4\rho_1-p_0}{2p_0}
=\frac{226315697}{480000000000}>0,
\tag{48}
\]
\[
\frac1{40}
-\frac{4\rho_3-p_0}{2p_0}
=\frac{72224233}{160000000000}>0.
\tag{49}
\]
Since \(q_s>0\), equations (6), (48)--(49) give
\[
\Lambda_1<\frac2{25},\qquad
\Lambda_3<\frac1{40}.
\tag{50}
\]
Also \(p_0<1\), with exact gap
\[
1-p_0=\frac{72081}{D}>0.
\tag{51}
\]

Using \(z<1/1000\), the exact worst-case radical gap is
\[
\lambda^2-\frac{(1/1000)}{p_0}
-\left(\frac{2399}{2000}\right)^2
=\frac{3123919}{16000000000}>0.
\tag{52}
\]
Therefore
\[
T<\frac1{2000}.
\tag{53}
\]

Suppose first that the left tail \(s=1\) terminates at the common
breakpoint.  Equations (40), (42), and (47) give
\[
q_{12}>\frac1{2000},\qquad
q_1>\frac{49}{10000}.
\tag{54}
\]
The local bound (9), together with (50)--(53), would give
\[
q_{12}
<
2\left(\frac2{25}\right)\left(\frac1{2000}\right)
+
\frac{(2/25)^2(1/2000)^2}{49/10000}
=\frac{123}{1531250}.
\tag{55}
\]
But
\[
\frac1{2000}-\frac{123}{1531250}
=\frac{5141}{12250000}>0,
\tag{56}
\]
contradicting (54).  Hence the left tail cannot terminate.

The right tail \(s=3\) must therefore terminate.  If
\[
x\geq\frac1{1000000},
\tag{57}
\]
then (9), (47), and (50)--(53) give
\[
q_{23}
<
2\left(\frac1{40}\right)\left(\frac1{2000}\right)
+
\frac{(1/40)^2(1/2000)^2}{1/1000000}
=\frac{29}{160000}.
\tag{58}
\]
On the other hand, (41) and (47) give
\[
q_{23}>\frac7{5000},
\]
and the exact gap is
\[

\tag{59}
\]
This excludes (57).

It remains that
\[

\tag{60}
\]
Equations (40) and (47) imply
\[
z<\frac{341}{1000000}+x
<\frac{342}{1000000}
=\frac{171}{500000}.
\tag{61}
\]
The rationalized identity
\[
T=
\frac{z/p_0}
{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2-z/p_0}}
<\frac{z}{p_0\lambda}
\tag{62}
\]
and the \(W\)-corollary in (10) give
\[
q_{23}
<(7p_0)T
<\frac{7z}{\lambda}
=\frac{35z}{6}
<\frac{399}{200000}.
\tag{63}
\]
But (41) and (60) give
\[
q_{23}>\frac{1999}{1000000},
\]
whereas
\[
\frac{1999}{1000000}-\frac{399}{200000}
=\frac1{250000}>0.
\tag{64}
\]
This is the final contradiction.

All alternatives for the common terminating tail and for
\((x,z)\) have been exhausted.  Equations (40)--(41) are an exact
parameterization of the full face, so every positive table on
(11)--(12) is excluded.  All numerical comparisons
(45)--(46), (48)--(49), (51)--(52), (55)--(56),
(58)--(59), and (61)--(64) are exact rational identities and
inequalities.
