## Statement

Assume the normalized homothetic Regime-A setting of accepted fact
`f37aced9de558c8d`.  Thus
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad A_2=hA_0,\qquad |K|=s,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{1+h}{2},\qquad D=\lambda A_0\subseteq K.
\]
Assume that the endpoint body has the trapezoid form
\[
A_0=\{(\sigma,\eta):0\leq\sigma\leq L,\
|\eta-c\sigma|\leq a+b\sigma\},
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
L>0,\qquad a>0,\qquad a+bL>0,\qquad
L(2a+bL)=2.
\tag{2}
\]
If all three original Regime-A row masses are strictly below
\[
t=\frac{2(1+h^2+s)}9,
\tag{3}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{s>\frac32.}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently no strict Regime-A hit exists in the family (1)-(2).
This conclusion holds for every real \(b,c\), including every rational
nonconstant width \(b\ne0\), and for arbitrary compact
two-dimensional convex \(K\).  It therefore includes every orientation
and incidence chamber of a rational middle trapezoid, every nonzero
middle-width slope, and all support contacts.

## Proof

The physical area element in \((\sigma,\eta)\)-coordinates is
\(\frac12\,d\sigma\,d\eta\).  The vertical projection of \(D\) is
\([0,Q]\), where
\[
Q=\lambda L>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Its vertical widths at the two endpoints are
\[
r_0=2\lambda a>0,\qquad
r_Q=2\lambda(a+bL)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
d=|D|=\lambda^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The normalization (2) gives the exact identity
\[
Q(r_0+r_Q)=4d.
\tag{8}
\]
The shear \(c\) does not enter this width identity.

Accepted fact `51ee862220ddc7e4` applied to the strict row failure (3)
gives
\[
X=K\cap\{\sigma<-1,\ \eta<\sigma\},\qquad
Y=K\cap\{\sigma<-1,\ \eta>-\sigma\},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
|X|>\frac19,\qquad |Y|>\frac19.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \([p,q]\) be the \(\sigma\)-projection of \(K\).  Equation (10)
gives \(p<-1\).  Put
\[
\delta=-1-p>0.
\tag{11}
\]
For an interior \(\sigma\), write the vertical section of \(K\) as
\[
K_\sigma=[\ell(\sigma),u(\sigma)]
\tag{12}
\]
and define
\[
g_X(\sigma)=\sigma-\ell(\sigma),\qquad
g_Y(\sigma)=u(\sigma)+\sigma,\qquad
w(\sigma)=u(\sigma)-\ell(\sigma).
\tag{13}
\]
Convexity of \(K\) makes \(\ell\) convex and \(u\) concave.  Hence
\(g_X,g_Y,w\) are concave.

The section of \(D\) at \(\sigma=0\) contains
\((0,-\lambda a)\) and \((0,\lambda a)\), so
\[
g_X(0)\geq\lambda a>0,\qquad
g_Y(0)\geq\lambda a>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Every point of \(X\), together with \((0,-\lambda a)\), lies in the
open halfplane \(\eta<\sigma\).  The segment between them crosses
\(\sigma=-1\), remains in \(K\), and remains in that halfplane.
Likewise, a point of \(Y\) can be joined to \((0,\lambda a)\) inside
\(\eta>-\sigma\).  Therefore
\[
H_X:=g_X(-1)>0,\qquad H_Y:=g_Y(-1)>0.
\tag{15}
\]

For a \(\sigma\)-aligned middle trapezoid, (14)-(15) give the exact
support-gap incidence enumeration.  Each of \(g_X,g_Y\) either has one
root in \([p,-1)\), denoted \(C\), or is positive on all of
\([p,-1]\), denoted \(F\).  Hence the four chambers are
\[
CC,\qquad CF,\qquad FC,\qquad FF,
\tag{16}
\]
with \(g_X(p)=0\) and \(g_Y(p)=0\) assigned to their contact
boundaries.  The following argument eliminates all four chambers
together.  It also covers every further subdivision according to
whether a cap line crosses the opposite side, because only an upper
bound on each clipped section is used.

For \(0\leq r\leq\delta\), concavity and the ordered points
\(-1-r<-1<0\) give
\[
g_X(-1-r)
\leq (1+r)H_X-rg_X(0)
\leq(1+r)H_X.
\tag{17}
\]
At each \(\sigma\), the vertical length of the \(X\)-section is at most
\((g_X(\sigma))_+\).  Thus, including the physical Jacobian factor,
\[
|X|
\leq\frac12\int_p^{-1}(g_X(\sigma))_+\,d\sigma
\leq\frac{H_X\delta(\delta+2)}4.
\tag{18}
\]
The identical argument for \(g_Y\) gives
\[
|Y|\leq\frac{H_Y\delta(\delta+2)}4.
\tag{19}
\]
Combining (10), (18), and (19),
\[
H_X>\frac4{9\delta(\delta+2)},\qquad
H_Y>\frac4{9\delta(\delta+2)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently the vertical width at the root line satisfies
\[
W:=w(-1)=2+H_X+H_Y
>
W_0(\delta):=
2+\frac8{9\delta(\delta+2)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (17)-(21) use only convexity, so they remain valid when \(K\)
is not a trapezoid.

Concavity of \(w\) and nonnegativity of the left support section give
\[
\frac12\int_p^{-1}w(\sigma)\,d\sigma
\geq\frac{\delta W}{4}.
\tag{22}
\]
There are two independent lower bounds for the physical area on
\(-1\leq\sigma\leq Q\).  The chord between the sections at \(-1\)
and \(0\), followed by the contained body \(D\), gives
\[
R_1=d+\frac{W+r_0}{4}.
\tag{23}
\]
The chord between the sections at \(-1\) and \(Q\) gives
\[
R_2=\frac{(Q+1)(W+r_Q)}4.
\tag{24}
\]
Hence the area on this interval is at least
\(\max\{R_1,R_2\}\).

For fixed \(d,W,Q\), minimize this maximum over nonnegative
\(r_0,r_Q\) subject to (8).  Enlarging the original positive-width
domain to include zero can only lower the minimum.  This is solely an
auxiliary relaxation for a lower bound on the strict domain
\(r_0,r_Q>0\); it does not extend the theorem to endpoint bodies with
zero endpoint width.  The expression \(R_1\) is increasing in \(r_0\),
while \(R_2\) is decreasing in \(r_0\).  Their intersection is
\[
r_0^*=\frac{WQ+4d/Q}{Q+2}.
\tag{25}
\]
It lies in \(0\leq r_0^*\leq4d/Q\) exactly when
\[
WQ^2\leq4d(Q+1).
\tag{26}
\]
Therefore (22)-(25) give the exhaustive two-case lower bounds
\[
s\geq
\frac{\delta W}{4}
+d+\frac W4+
\frac{WQ+4d/Q}{4(Q+2)}
\quad\text{if (26) holds},
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
s\geq\frac{W(\delta+Q+1)}4
\quad\text{if}\quad
WQ^2\geq4d(Q+1).
\tag{28}
\]
Equality in (26) makes (27) and (28) agree.

Since \(h>0\),
\[
d=\left(\frac{1+h}{2}\right)^2>\frac14.
\tag{29}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(\delta,Q):={}&
\frac{\delta W_0}{4}+\frac14+\frac{W_0}{4}
+\frac{W_0Q+Q^{-1}}{4(Q+2)},\\
&W_0=2+\frac8{9\delta(\delta+2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
In case (27), monotonicity in \(d\) and \(W\), together with
(21) and (29), gives
\[
s>B(\delta,Q).
\tag{31}
\]

In case (28), first use the same \(W\) in (30), before replacing it by
\(W_0\).  The exact difference is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{W(\delta+Q+1)}4
-\left(
\frac{\delta W}{4}+\frac14+\frac W4+
\frac{WQ+Q^{-1}}{4(Q+2)}
\right)\\
&\qquad=
\frac{(Q+1)(WQ^2-(Q+1))}{4Q(Q+2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
The case condition and (29) imply
\[
WQ^2\geq4d(Q+1)>Q+1,
\tag{33}
\]
so (32) is positive.  The expression in parentheses is increasing in
\(W\), and (21) again gives (31).  Thus (31) holds in both exhaustive
connector cases.

Since \(W_0>2\), AM-GM gives
\[
(W_0-1)Q+Q^{-1}\geq2\sqrt{W_0-1}>2.
\tag{34}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{W_0Q+Q^{-1}}{4(Q+2)}>\frac14.
\tag{35}
\]
Therefore
\[
B(\delta,Q)>
\frac{W_0(\delta+1)}4+\frac12.
\tag{36}
\]
The one-variable function in (36) has the exact minimum
\[
\min_{\delta>0}\frac{W_0(\delta+1)}4
=
\frac{\sqrt{169+38\sqrt{19}}}{18}
>1,
\tag{37}
\]
attained at
\[
\delta=\frac{-3+\sqrt{11+2\sqrt{19}}}{3}.
\tag{38}
\]
Indeed, after setting \(x=\delta+1\), differentiate
\[
\frac{x}{2}+\frac{2x}{9(x^2-1)}
\]
and solve the unique critical equation
\[
9(x^2-1)^2=4(x^2+1).
\tag{39}
\]
The strict inequality in (37) follows after squaring from
\[
38^2\cdot19=27436>24025=155^2.
\tag{40}
\]
Equations (31), (36), and (37) prove (4).

It remains to compare (4) with the exact Regime-A parameter range.
Accepted fact `f37aced9de558c8d` leaves only
\[
0<h<h_0:=\frac{2\sqrt{71}-9}{29}.
\tag{41}
\]
The polynomial \(29h^2+18h-7\) is increasing for \(h>0\) and is
positive at \(h=1/3\), so
\[
h_0<\frac13.
\tag{42}
\]
Accepted fact `776f5d2aa96794fd`, with
\(M=1\), \(m=h^2\), and \(k=s\), forces every counterexample to
satisfy
\[
s<1+\frac{h^2}{2}
<1+\frac1{18}
=\frac{19}{18}.
\tag{43}
\]
This contradicts (4), so no strict counterexample exists in (1)-(2).

The proof never divides by \(b\), a cap area, a chord length, or a
contact angle.  It allows \(b=0\), arbitrary shear \(c\), support
