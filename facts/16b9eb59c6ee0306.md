## Statement

Assume all hypotheses and notation of accepted fact
`904839a1c6967954`.  In particular, the complete canonical
single-double hypotheses hold, all three levels are actual lower
crossings, all three crossing equalities and both tails hold, and
\[
 p=p_\varnothing,\qquad
 \frac pm>\beta:=\frac{93+12\sqrt{21}}{50}>1,\qquad
 \Delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>2.
\tag{1}
\]

Consider the following additional rational triangle/trapezoid
restriction.  After a common affine change of the plane and a common
area normalization, assume
\[
 L_1=L_2=L_3=-\frac{\Delta+1}{3},
 \qquad
 s:=\frac{\Delta+2}{\Delta}=1+\frac2\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Use the affine coordinates
\[
 r_i=\frac{f_i-L_i}{\Delta},
 \qquad r_1+r_2+r_3=1,
\tag{3}
\]
and, on the smaller endpoint, the equivalent coordinates
\[
 \tau_i=-\frac{f_i+L_i}{\Delta+2},
 \qquad \tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
 f_i\leq L_i\iff r_i\leq0,
 \qquad
 f_i\leq-L_i\iff\tau_i\geq0.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\alpha _1,\alpha _2,\alpha _3,b,u,v,w\) be positive rational
numbers with \(0<b<1\), and put
\[
 d=u+v+w.
\tag{6}
\]
In the \(r\)-plane let the larger endpoint be the separator-normal
trapezoid
\[
 A_0=
 \left\{
 r_1+r_2+r_3=1:
 r_i\geq-\alpha_i\ (i=1,2,3),\ r_3\leq b
 \right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
In the \(\tau\)-plane let the smaller endpoint be the triangle with
vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
 y_1&=(1+u+w,-u,-w),\\
 y_2&=(-v,1+v+w,-w),\\
 y_3&=\left(\frac vd,\frac ud,\frac wd\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The affine relation between the two coordinate systems is
\[
 r_i=\frac{2(\Delta+1)}{3\Delta}-s\tau_i.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(K\) be any two-dimensional rational convex polytope satisfying
all the compatibility, crossing, tail, finite-interior, and
actual-counterexample hypotheses assumed in (1).  In particular, the
conclusion below also applies when \(K\) is restricted to a rational
triangle or separator-normal trapezoid.

Then no such \(A_0,A_2,K\) exist.

More explicitly, before imposing the impossible canonical scalar
conditions, the endpoint class has exact positive \(Q\)-support
\[
 \{J:q_J>0\}=\{123,1,2,12\},
\tag{10}
\]
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_1&=\frac{s^2uw}{2},&
q_2&=\frac{s^2vw}{2},\\
q_{12}&=s^2\left(w+\frac{w^2}{2}\right),&
q_{123}&=\frac{s^2w}{2d},
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
 m=\frac{s^2w(1+d)^2}{2d}.
\tag{12}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 P=\overline{P_\varnothing}
 =\operatorname{conv}\{(1-b,0),(1,0),(0,1),(0,1-b)\}
\tag{13}
\]
in \((r_1,r_2)\)-coordinates, so
\[
 p=b-\frac{b^2}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]
The hull
\[
 C=\operatorname{conv}(\overline{Q_1}\cup\overline{Q_2})
\]
is a trapezoid of area
\[
 \sigma=\frac{s^2w(d+2)}2,
\tag{15}
\]
and its exact mixed area with \(P\) is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 2V(P,C)=s\bigl[w+b(1+d)\bigr].
 \quad}
\tag{16}
\]

## Proof

The coordinate identities are exact.  Equation (3) follows from
\(\sum_i f_i=-1\), (2), and the definition of \(\Delta\).  Equations
(2)--(4) give
\[
 f_i+L_i
 =\Delta r_i+2L_i
 =-(\Delta+2)\tau_i,
\]
which proves (5) and (9).  The linear part of (9) is the homothety
\(-sI\), so areas in the \(r\)-plane are \(s^2\) times the
corresponding areas in the \(\tau\)-plane.

We first audit the exact cells of the triangle (8).  Put
\[
 e_1=(1,0,0),\qquad e_2=(0,1,0).
\]
The segment \([y_1,y_3]\) passes through \(e_1\), and
\([y_2,y_3]\) passes through \(e_2\).  For example, the parameter
\(d/(1+d)\) from \(y_1\) toward \(y_3\) simultaneously makes the
second and third coordinates zero and makes the first coordinate one.
The analogous statement holds after interchanging the first two
coordinates.

Conversely, this parametrizes the whole declared asymmetric chamber.
Indeed, singleton vertices on the common base
\(\tau_3=-w\) have the first two forms in (8), for unique
\(u,v>0\).  The two lines joining them through \(e_1,e_2\)
intersect at the third point in (8), so the triple vertex is forced.

The base \([y_1,y_2]\) lies on \(\tau_3=-w\).  Its intersections
with \(\tau_2=0\) and \(\tau_1=0\) are, respectively,
\[
 (1+w,0,-w),\qquad(0,1+w,-w).
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the \(Q_1\)-cell is the triangle with vertex \(y_1\), transverse
height \(u\), and base length \(w\); the \(Q_2\)-cell is the analogous
triangle with height \(v\).  The \(Q_{123}\)-cell is
\(\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,y_3\}\).  Consequently, in the
\(\tau\)-area normalization,
\[
 |Q_1|_\tau=\frac{uw}{2},\qquad
 |Q_2|_\tau=\frac{vw}{2},\qquad
 |Q_{123}|_\tau=\frac{w}{2d}.
\tag{18}
\]
The side crossings at \(e_1,e_2\) are simultaneous crossings of the
two relevant coordinate lines.  Hence no \(Q_{13}\)- or
\(Q_{23}\)-region occurs.  The remaining positive region below
\(\tau_3=0\) is exactly \(Q_{12}\).

A determinant using the vertices in (8) gives
\[
 |A_2|_\tau=\frac{w(1+d)^2}{2d}.
\tag{19}
\]
Subtracting the three quantities in (18) from (19) gives
\[
 |Q_{12}|_\tau=w+\frac{w^2}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
Multiplication by the common area factor \(s^2\) proves
(10)--(12).

On \(A_0\), the conditions in (5) show that the closure of the empty
cell is
\[
 \{r_i\geq0,\ r_3\leq b\}.
\]
Its cyclic vertex list in \((r_1,r_2)\)-coordinates is exactly (13).
Shoelace gives (14).  Notice also that all three \(A_0\)-caps are
proper and have positive area.  Direct integration gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 u_1&=\alpha_1(b+\alpha_3),\\
 u_2&=\alpha_2(b+\alpha_3),\\
 u_3&=\alpha_3(1+\alpha_1+\alpha_2)
      +
      \frac{\alpha_3^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the obstruction below is not a zero-cap boundary artifact.

In the \(\tau\)-plane,
\[
 C_\tau=\operatorname{conv}\{y_1,y_2,e_2,e_1\}.
\tag{22}
\]
It is the union, up to null coordinate lines, of
\(Q_1,Q_2,Q_{12}\).  Equations (18) and (20) give
\[
 |C_\tau|
 =\frac{uw}{2}+\frac{vw}{2}
  +w+\frac{w^2}{2}
 =\frac{w(d+2)}2.
\]
After the homothety in (9), this proves (15).

We next compute the polygonal support formula entirely in rational
coordinates.  For a counterclockwise edge vector
\(e=(\delta x,\delta y)\), put
\[
 N_e=(\delta y,-\delta x).
\]
By positive homogeneity of the support function,
\[
 |e|h_C(n_e)=h_C(N_e),
\]
so the usual polygonal formula is equivalently
\[
 2V(P,C)=\sum_{e\subset\partial P}h_C(N_e).
\tag{23}
\]
For the cyclic list in (13), the four nonunit outward normals are
\[
 (0,-b),\quad(1,1),\quad(-b,0),\quad
 (-(1-b),-(1-b)).
\tag{24}
\]
Translation does not change their total support sum because the four
vectors in (24) add to zero.  We may therefore replace the actual
trapezoid \(C\) by \(-sC_\tau\).  From (8) and (22), its four support
values in the order (24) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &sb(1+v+w),\qquad -s,\\
 &sb(1+u+w),\qquad s(1-b)(1+w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Their sum is
\[
 s\bigl[w+b(1+u+v+w)\bigr]
 =s\bigl[w+b(1+d)\bigr],
\]
which proves (16).

It remains to impose the actual lower-crossing condition.  Define the
first endpoint distribution
\[
 \Phi_1(t)=
 |A_0\cap\{f_1\leq t\}|
 +|A_2\cap\{f_1\leq-t\}|.
\tag{26}
\]
At \(t=L_1\), neither cutting line passes through a vertex of the
corresponding endpoint polygon.  Thus \(\Phi_1\) is differentiable
there.  If \(t=L_1+\eta\), the first cap in (26) is cut at
\[
 r_1=\frac{\eta}{\Delta}.
\]
At \(r_1=0\), its section has \(r_3\)-length
\(b+\alpha_3\).  Its derivative with respect to \(\eta\) is therefore
\[
 \frac{b+\alpha_3}{\Delta}.
\tag{27}
\]

For the second cap in (26), equation (4) changes the threshold to
\[
 \tau_1\geq\frac{\eta}{\Delta+2}
 =\frac{\eta}{\Delta s}.
\]
The section of the triangle (8) at \(\tau_1=0\) joins
\((0,1,0)\) to \((0,1+w,-w)\), so its
\(\tau_2\)-length is exactly \(w\).  The \(\tau\)-area derivative is
therefore \(-w/(\Delta s)\), and the \(r\)-area factor \(s^2\)
turns it into
\[
 -\frac{sw}{\Delta}.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27)--(28) gives the exact one-sided derivative
\[
 \Phi_1'(L_1+)
 =\frac{b+\alpha_3-sw}{\Delta}.
\tag{29}
\]

Because \(L_1\) is an actual lower crossing, with the fixed middle
cap \(c_1\) and common level \(h\),
\[
 L_1=\inf\{t:c_1+\Phi_1(t)<h\},
 \qquad c_1+\Phi_1(L_1)=h.
\tag{30}
\]
There are therefore points \(t>L_1\), arbitrarily close to \(L_1\),
for which \(\Phi_1(t)<\Phi_1(L_1)\).  Differentiability and (29)
force
\[
 b+\alpha_3\leq sw.
\tag{31}
\]

The remaining contradiction is an exact scalar certificate.  From
(12),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 m-2s^2w
 &=\frac{s^2w\bigl((1+d)^2-4d\bigr)}{2d}\\
 &=\frac{s^2w(d-1)^2}{2d}\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(s>1\), equations (14), (31), and (32) give the strict chain
\[
 p<b<b+\alpha_3
 \leq sw
 <\frac m2.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus \(2p<m\), contradicting (1).  This contradiction precedes the
choice of \(K\).  In particular, imposing the three crossing
equalities, both tails, \(W=0\), compatibility, or either gate from
accepted fact `7706987b44b0132f` cannot restore feasibility.
