## Statement

Use the normalized \(A_2\) coordinates and coordinate-area convention of accepted facts `2b42506f5de09be1` and `fa0e57b225178b3b`.  For the strict rational data of accepted fact `6090beea12ef2770`, put
\[
 S=102,\qquad T=S-U+V,
\]
so that the three cutting lines are \(U=0\), \(V=0\), and \(T=0\), and \(\Delta=1\).  There is a compact two-dimensional convex rational polygon \(\mathcal P\) whose full chords are exactly
\[
 \mathcal P\cap\{U=0\}
 =\{(0,V):3/10\le V\le13/25\},
\]
\[
 \mathcal P\cap\{V=0\}
 =\{(U,0):3/10\le U\le11/20\},
\]
\[
 \mathcal P\cap\{T=0\}
 =\{(102-t,-t):50\le t\le251/5\}.
\]
Its only positive-area strict cells are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 &U&V&T&\text{area}\\ \hline
Q_j&<0&>0&>0&1/5\\
Q_{jk}&>0&>0&>0&1/10\\
Q_N&>0&<0&>0&20\\
Q_{k\ell}&>0&<0&<0&3/20.
\end{array}
\]
Thus all four areas, all three offsets, and all three chord lengths \(J_2=11/50\), \(H_2=1/4\), and \(K_2=1/5\) are realized simultaneously by one polygon.

## Proof

In counterclockwise cyclic order, let \(\mathcal P\) have the following eleven vertices:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
i&U_i&V_i\\ \hline
0&527/10&-254/5\\
1&52&-50\\
2&132091/5000&-25\\
3&11/20&0\\
4&73/260&13/50\\
5&0&13/25\\
6&-20/11&11/5\\
7&0&3/10\\
8&11/75&3/20\\
9&3/10&0\\
10&259/5&-251/5.
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
Write these vertices as \(v_0,\ldots,v_{10}\), with indices modulo \(11\).  Convexity can be checked without a drawing.  For every oriented edge \(v_iv_{i+1}\), direct rational determinant evaluation gives the following minimum over all other listed vertices:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{edge}&\text{vertex attaining the minimum}&
\min_{r\ne i,i+1}\det(v_{i+1}-v_i,v_r-v_i)\\ \hline
v_0v_1&v_{10}&3/10\\
v_1v_2&v_0&9267/3125\\
v_2v_3&v_4&16371/3250000\\
v_3v_4&v_5&3/1000\\
v_4v_5&v_6&37/35750\\
v_5v_6&v_4&37/35750\\
v_6v_7&v_8&49/8250\\
v_7v_8&v_9&1/1000\\
v_8v_9&v_7&1/1000\\
v_9v_{10}&v_8&83/3000\\
v_{10}v_0&v_1&3/10.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Every entry is positive.  Thus every oriented edge strictly supports all nonincident vertices on its left, so (1) is the vertex cycle of a compact two-dimensional convex polygon.

The values of the three affine chamber coordinates at the vertices are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
i&U_i&V_i&T_i=102-U_i+V_i\\ \hline
0&527/10&-254/5&-3/2\\
1&52&-50&0\\
2&132091/5000&-25&252909/5000\\
3&11/20&0&2029/20\\
4&73/260&13/50&132573/1300\\
5&0&13/25&2563/25\\
6&-20/11&11/5&5831/55\\
7&0&3/10&1023/10\\
8&11/75&3/20&30601/300\\
9&3/10&0&1017/10\\
10&259/5&-251/5&0.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
For \(U\), the only negative vertex is \(v_6\), and the two zero vertices are its boundary neighbors \(v_5,v_7\).  For \(V\), the boundary changes sign only through \(v_3,v_9\).  For \(T\), the only negative vertex is \(v_0\), and the two zero vertices are its boundary neighbors \(v_{10},v_1\).  Convexity and (3) therefore give the full, not merely contained, chords
\[
 \mathcal P\cap\{U=0\}=[v_7,v_5],\qquad
 \mathcal P\cap\{V=0\}=[v_9,v_3],\qquad
 \mathcal P\cap\{T=0\}=[v_1,v_{10}].
\tag{4}
\]
The first two segments in (4) have the intervals stated in the claim.  Moreover
\[
v_1=(102-50,-50),\qquad
v_{10}=(102-251/5,-251/5),
\]
so the third segment also has exactly the asserted parameter interval.

Clipping the full polygon by the three lines in (4) gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline{Q_{k\ell}}\cap\mathcal P
 &=\operatorname{conv}(v_{10},v_0,v_1),\\
\overline{Q_N}\cap\mathcal P
 &=\operatorname{conv}(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_9,v_{10}),\\
\overline{Q_{jk}}\cap\mathcal P
 &=\operatorname{conv}(v_3,v_4,v_5,v_7,v_8,v_9),\\
\overline{Q_j}\cap\mathcal P
 &=\operatorname{conv}(v_5,v_6,v_7).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The sign table (3) shows that every polygon in (5) lies in its indicated closed chamber and that its interior lies in the corresponding strict chamber.  The four polygons cover \(\mathcal P\), meeting only along the three chords.  Hence every other strict chamber is empty.

It remains to audit the areas.  Let \([w_1,\ldots,w_r]\) denote the area of the polygon with those cyclic vertices.  Exact shoelace determinants give
\[
\begin{aligned}
[v_{10},v_0,v_1]&=\frac3{20},\\
[v_1,v_3,v_9,v_{10}]&=\frac{821}{50},
& [v_1,v_2,v_3]&=\frac{179}{50},\\
[v_3,v_5,v_7,v_9]&=\frac{49}{500},
& [v_3,v_4,v_5]&=\frac3{2000},
& [v_7,v_8,v_9]&=\frac1{2000},\\
[v_5,v_6,v_7]&=\frac15.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The two bulge triangles in each relevant line of (6) have disjoint interiors from the displayed base polygon and share only their base edge.  Consequently (5)-(6) yield
\[
 |Q_{k\ell}|=\frac3{20},\qquad
 |Q_N|=\frac{821}{50}+\frac{179}{50}=20,
\]
\[
 |Q_{jk}|=\frac{49}{500}+\frac3{2000}+\frac1{2000}
 =\frac1{10},\qquad
 |Q_j|=\frac15.
\tag{7}
\]
Their sum is \(409/20\), also the shoelace area of the full polygon.  Because \(\Delta=1\), these coordinate areas are the prescribed physical areas.  Equations (2), (4), (5), and (7) complete the global convexity, full-chord, chamber, and area audit.
