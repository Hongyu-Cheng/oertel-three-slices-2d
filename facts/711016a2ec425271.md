## Statement

Use the exact \(Q\)-datum, common affine coordinates, and inverse
area Jacobian of accepted fact `9aa5e8e4ddcd1c62`.  Thus
\[
\delta=10,\qquad
\gamma=\frac{320000}{16072081},
\qquad
\theta_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad
\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3=-12.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
D=16072081,\qquad
E=\frac{D}{640000000}.
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
\tau=
\frac{E\bigl(\sqrt{72+E}-6\bigr)}{36+E},
\qquad
\sigma=\frac{2E-12\tau}{12+\tau},
\qquad
\beta=12-\sqrt{144-2E},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
H=\frac{6E}{\beta},\qquad
H_-=\frac{22E}{\sigma}-H,
\qquad
R=\frac{12+\sigma}{\sigma-\tau},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
r_0=R\tau,\qquad r_+=H-r_0.
\tag{5}
\]
In the affine coordinates
\[
u=\theta_1,\qquad v=-\theta_2,\qquad
\theta_3=v-12-u,
\tag{6}
\]
let \(\mathcal Q\) be the polygon with the following counterclockwise
vertices:
\[
\begin{split}
V_1&=(-12-\sigma-H_-,-\sigma),\\
V_2&=(-12-\sigma,-\sigma),\\
V_3&=(r_0,0),\\
V_4&=(r_+,\beta),\\
V_5&=(\beta-12,\beta),\\
V_6&=(-12-H,0).
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
Then \(\mathcal Q\) is a compact two-dimensional convex polygon.
Let \(A_2\) be its inverse image under the affine isomorphism (6).
Using the exact-cell convention in which an index records
\(\theta_i\leq0\), the physical cell areas of \(A_2\) are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
J&1&2&3&12&13&23&123\\ \hline
q_J&
\dfrac{11}{2000}&0&\dfrac1{2000}&
\dfrac3{2000}&\dfrac1{2000}&
\dfrac3{2000}&\dfrac1{2000}.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
Every positive entry in (8) is realized by a set with nonempty
interior.  The weak/strict assignment on the three cutting lines
does not change any area.

## Proof

The number \(\tau\) in (3) is the unique positive root of
\[
(36+E)z^2+12Ez-E^2=0.
\tag{9}
\]
The polynomial in (9) is strictly increasing for \(z\geq0\).
Exact rational substitution in (9), in the formula for \(\sigma\),
and in \(12z-z^2/2-E\) gives
\[
\frac{1733}{10^6}<\tau<\frac{1734}{10^6},
\qquad
\frac{2451}{10^6}<\sigma<\frac{2453}{10^6},
\qquad
\frac{2092}{10^6}<\beta<\frac{2094}{10^6}.
\tag{10}
\]
For a literal sign audit, the values of the left side of (9) at the
two endpoints of its interval are
\[
-\frac{266248190354307}
        {1280000000000000000000},
\qquad
\frac{279352557455347}
       {1280000000000000000000}.
\tag{11}
\]
The two residuals bracketing the small root of
\(12z-z^2/2=E\) are
\[
E-\left(12\frac{2092}{10^6}
-\frac12\left(\frac{2092}{10^6}\right)^2\right)
=\frac{21629589}{2000000000000}>0,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\left(12\frac{2094}{10^6}
-\frac12\left(\frac{2094}{10^6}\right)^2\right)-E
=\frac{26362039}{2000000000000}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
The map defining \(\sigma\) is strictly decreasing in \(\tau\);
substitution of the two bounds in (10) proves its displayed
interval.  Thus (10) is a fully rational isolation of all three
algebraic parameters.

The definitions in (3) and equation (9) give the three exact
identities
\[
12(\sigma+\tau)+\sigma\tau=2E,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
(12+\sigma)\tau^2=2E(\sigma-\tau),
\tag{15}
\]
\[
12\beta-\frac{\beta^2}{2}=E.
\tag{16}
\]
The brackets (10) imply
\[
0<\tau<\beta<3\tau,\qquad
\tau<\sigma,\qquad
11\beta>3\sigma.
\tag{17}
\]
They also give the exact convexity margin
\[
3\sigma^2+6\beta\sigma-11\beta^2
>
3\left(\frac{2451}{10^6}\right)^2
+6\frac{2094}{10^6}\frac{2451}{10^6}
-11\left(\frac{2094}{10^6}\right)^2
=\frac{583371}{10^{12}}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Here the quadratic is increasing in \(\sigma\) and decreasing in
\(\beta\) throughout the rational box (10).  In particular,
\[
H>0,\qquad H_->0,\qquad R>0,\qquad r_0>0,\qquad r_+>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, \(H_->0\) is equivalent to \(11\beta>3\sigma\).
By (15),
\[
\frac{R\tau^2}{2}=E,
\tag{20}
\]
so \(r_+=6E/\beta-2E/\tau>0\) is equivalent to
\(\beta<3\tau\).

We now verify convexity without relying on the vertex picture.
For \(-\sigma\leq v\leq\beta\), define the left-tail profile
\[
L(v)=
\begin{cases}
H_-+\dfrac{H-H_-}{\sigma}(v+\sigma),
&-\sigma\leq v\leq0,\\[2mm]
H\left(1-\dfrac v\beta\right),
&0\leq v\leq\beta,
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
\]
and the right endpoint
\[
\rho(v)=
\begin{cases}
R(v+\tau),&-\sigma\leq v\leq0,\\[1mm]
r_0+\dfrac{r_+-r_0}{\beta}v,
&0\leq v\leq\beta.
\end{cases}
\tag{22}
\]
The polygon in (7) is exactly
\[
\mathcal Q=
\{(u,v):-\sigma\leq v\leq\beta,\
v-12-L(v)\leq u\leq\rho(v)\}.
\tag{23}
\]
The left endpoint in (23) is convex if and only if \(L\) is
concave.  The slope condition at \(v=0\) is
\[
\frac{H-H_-}{\sigma}\geq-\frac H\beta.
\tag{24}
\]
After substituting (4), condition (24) is exactly (18).  Hence the
left endpoint is convex.  The right endpoint is concave because
\[
R\geq\frac{r_+-r_0}{\beta}.
\tag{25}
\]
Using (20), inequality (25) reduces to
\[
\beta(\beta+2\tau)\geq3\tau^2,
\]
whose left side minus its right side is
\[
(\beta-\tau)(\beta+3\tau)>0
\tag{26}
\]
by (17).  Finally, the section width in (23) is concave and is
strictly positive at \(v=-\sigma,0,\beta\), by (19).  It is
therefore positive throughout.  This proves that (23), equivalently
(7), is a compact two-dimensional convex polygon.  It also proves
that all collinear or endpoint boundary cases used below are
legitimate convex sections.

It remains to compute its cells.  In the \((u,v)\)-plane the three
cutting lines are
\[
u=0,\qquad v=0,\qquad u=v-12.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(v<0\), the three possible cells from left to right are
\[
Q_1,\qquad Q_{13},\qquad Q_3.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(0<v<12\), they are
\[
Q_{12},\qquad Q_{123},\qquad Q_{23}.
\tag{29}
\]
The missing cell \(Q_2\) would require
\[
v\geq12,\qquad 0<u<v-12.
\tag{30}
\]
But (10) gives \(v\leq\beta<12\) throughout \(\mathcal Q\), so
\(Q_2=\varnothing\).

On the lower interval, the left endpoint is
\(v-12-L(v)\), with \(L(v)>0\).  The right endpoint satisfies
\[
\rho(-\sigma)=-12-\sigma,\qquad
\rho(-\tau)=0,
\tag{31}
\]
and it lies weakly to the right of \(u=v-12\) after
\(v=-\sigma\), because
\[
R-1=\frac{12+\tau}{\sigma-\tau}>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
|Q_1|_{\rm coord}
=\int_{-\sigma}^{0}L(v)\,dv
=\frac{\sigma(H_-+H)}2
=11E.
\tag{32}
\]
Between \(v=-\sigma\) and \(v=-\tau\), the middle section grows
linearly from zero to length \(12+\tau\).  Between
\(v=-\tau\) and \(v=0\), it has the full length \(12-v\).
Consequently, using (14),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Q_{13}|_{\rm coord}
&=\frac{(\sigma-\tau)(12+\tau)}2
+\int_{-\tau}^{0}(12-v)\,dv\\
&=6(\sigma+\tau)+\frac{\sigma\tau}{2}
=E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
The right tail is a triangle, so (20) gives
\[
|Q_3|_{\rm coord}
=\frac{R\tau^2}{2}=E.
\tag{34}
\]

On the upper interval, \(L(v)\geq0\), the right endpoint is
positive, and \(L(\beta)=0\).  Thus the left and right tails and the
full central strip have coordinate areas
\[
|Q_{12}|_{\rm coord}
=\int_0^\beta L(v)\,dv
=\frac{\beta H}{2}=3E,
\tag{35}
\]
\[
|Q_{123}|_{\rm coord}
=\int_0^\beta(12-v)\,dv
=12\beta-\frac{\beta^2}{2}=E,
\tag{36}
\]
\[
|Q_{23}|_{\rm coord}
=\int_0^\beta\rho(v)\,dv
=\frac{\beta(r_0+r_+)}2
=\frac{\beta H}{2}=3E.
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (32)--(37) also show directly that every listed cell has
nonempty interior.

The change from \((u,v)\)-area to physical area has factor
\(\gamma\): changing the sign of the second common affine coordinate
does not change the absolute Jacobian.  By (1)--(2),
\[
\gamma E
=\frac{320000}{D}\frac{D}{640000000}
=\frac1{2000}.
\tag{38}
\]
Multiplying (32)--(37) by (38) proves exactly the table (8), whose
total is \(20/2000=1/100\).  Each cutting set in (27) is a line.
Its intersection with the two-dimensional polygon has planar area
zero, so assigning equality to the indexed cell gives the exact
disjoint convention without changing any displayed area.

All identities (14)--(16), the brackets (10), the convexity signs
(18), (25), and the six area formulas (32)--(37) were independently
checked in the real algebraic field with SageMath \(10.8\).  The
construction and proof use no floating-point feasibility decision.
