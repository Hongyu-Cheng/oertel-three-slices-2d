## Statement

We first state the endpoint lemma.  Let \(s>0\), and let
\(P,Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex bodies.  Use
coordinates \((x,y)\) for a point of \(P\), and write the same ambient
coordinate functions as \((X,Y)\) when the point lies in \(Q\).  Put
\[
\delta=\max_{p\in P}(x(p)+y(p)).
\tag{1}
\]
Assume
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad |Q|=s,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=|Q\cap\{X\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=|Q\cap\{Y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
G_x(r)=|P\cap\{x\leq r\}|+|Q\cap\{X\leq-r\}|,
\]
\[
G_y(r)=|P\cap\{y\leq r\}|+|Q\cap\{Y\leq-r\}|.
\tag{5}
\]
Suppose
\[
G_x(r)\geq\frac{s}{4},\qquad
G_y(r)\geq\frac{2s}{5}\qquad(r<0).
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\left|Q\cap\{X+Y\geq-2-\delta\}\right|=s.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, (1)--(6) are incompatible with
\[
\left|Q\cap\{X+Y\geq-2-\delta\}\right|=\frac{3s}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently, the following full no-realization assertion holds.  There do
not exist \(s>0\) and rational two-dimensional convex polygons
\(P,Q,R\subset\mathbb R^2\) satisfying all of the following conditions.
The three bodies use the same ambient coordinate basis, with coordinates of
a point of \(Q\) denoted by \((X,Y)\):
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad |Q|=s,\qquad |R|=\frac{151}{10}s,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=|Q\cap\{X\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},
\tag{11}
\]
\[
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=|Q\cap\{Y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\delta=\max_{p\in P}(x(p)+y(p)),
\qquad
|Q\cap\{X+Y\geq-2-\delta\}|=\frac{3s}{4},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
|R\cap\{x+y\geq-1\}|=\frac{92}{15}s,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
|R\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{413}{60}s,\qquad
|R\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{101}{15}s.
\tag{15}
\]
For these polygons, define \(G_x,G_y\) by (5).  Require both genuine exact
lower crossings at zero:
\[
G_x(r)\geq\frac{s}{4},\qquad
G_y(r)\geq\frac{2s}{5}\qquad(r<0),
\tag{16}
\]
and, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), require the existence of
\(r_x,r_y\in(0,\varepsilon)\) such that
\[
G_x(r_x)<\frac{s}{4},\qquad
G_y(r_y)<\frac{2s}{5}.
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, put
\[
F_P=P\cap\{x+y=\delta\},\qquad
d_x=-\min_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
d_y=-\min_{p\in P}y(p),
\tag{18}
\]
and require
\[
F_P\cap\mathbb R_{>0}^2=\varnothing
\quad\text{or}\quad
\delta\geq d_x+d_y.
\tag{19}
\]
No \(P,Q,R,s,\delta\) satisfy (9)--(19).

## Proof

We prove the endpoint lemma first.  Put
\[
d_x=-\min_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
d_y=-\min_{p\in P}y(p),
\]
\[
M_x=\max_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
M_y=\max_{p\in P}y(p).
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) show that each coordinate cap of \(P\) has positive
area strictly smaller than \(|P|\).  Hence
\[
d_x,d_y,M_x,M_y>0.
\tag{21}
\]

Choose \(p_x\in P\) with \(x(p_x)=-d_x\), and set
\[
\lambda_x=\frac{d_x}{d_x+M_x}.
\tag{22}
\]
Convexity gives
\[
p_x+\lambda_x(P-p_x)\subseteq P.
\tag{23}
\]
Every point of this homothetic copy has \(x\)-coordinate at most zero,
so (2)--(3) imply
\[
16s\lambda_x^2\leq\frac{s}{8}.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(M_x\leq10d_x\), then \(\lambda_x\geq1/11\), and (24) would give
\[
\frac{16s}{121}\leq\frac{s}{8},
\]
contrary to \(128>121\).  Therefore
\[
M_x>10d_x.
\tag{25}
\]
The same homothety based at a point minimizing \(y\) gives
\[
M_y>7d_y.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, \(M_y\leq7d_y\) would put a homothetic copy of area at least
\(16s/8^2=s/4\) inside the \(y\)-cap of area \(s/5\).

By (1), the body \(P\) is contained in
\(\{x+y\leq\delta\}\).  At a point maximizing \(x\), one has
\(y\geq-d_y\), while at a point maximizing \(y\), one has
\(x\geq-d_x\).  Thus
\[
M_x\leq\delta+d_y,\qquad M_y\leq\delta+d_x.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (25)--(27) yield
\[
\delta>10d_x-d_y,\qquad
\delta>7d_y-d_x.
\tag{28}
\]
Taking respectively \(8/19\) and \(11/19\) of these two strict
inequalities gives
\[
\delta>\frac{69}{19}(d_x+d_y)>3(d_x+d_y)>0.
\tag{29}
\]

Put
\[
D_X=-\min_{q\in Q}X(q),\qquad
D_Y=-\min_{q\in Q}Y(q).
\tag{30}
\]
The proper positive-area caps in (3)--(4) imply \(D_X,D_Y>0\).
Substitute \(r=-d_x<0\) in the first inequality of (6).  The set
\(P\cap\{x\leq-d_x\}\) is contained in a supporting line and has
planar area zero.  Hence
\[
|Q\cap\{X\leq d_x\}|\geq\frac{s}{4}.
\tag{31}
\]
After subtracting the \(Q\)-cap in (3), whose cutting line is planar
null, (31) gives
\[
|Q\cap\{0<X\leq d_x\}|\geq\frac{s}{8}.
\tag{32}
\]

Let \(\ell_X(t)\) be the length of the vertical section
\(Q\cap\{X=t\}\).  On the projection interval of \(Q\), the function
\(\ell_X\) is nonnegative and concave.  Indeed, convexity of \(Q\)
contains the Minkowski interpolation of any two vertical section
intervals in the section at the interpolated coordinate, and the
length of that interpolated interval is the corresponding interpolation
of the two lengths.  Since the cap in (3) is proper, zero is in the
interior of this projection interval and \(\ell_X(0)>0\).
Cavalieri's formula and concavity on \([-D_X,0]\) give
\[
\frac{s}{8}
=\int_{-D_X}^{0}\ell_X(t)\,dt
\geq\frac{D_X}{2}\bigl(\ell_X(-D_X)+\ell_X(0)\bigr)
\geq\frac{D_X\ell_X(0)}{2}.
\tag{33}
\]
For every positive \(t\) in the projection interval, monotonicity of
secant slopes of a concave function gives
\[
\frac{\ell_X(t)-\ell_X(0)}{t}
\leq
\frac{\ell_X(0)-\ell_X(-D_X)}{D_X}
\leq\frac{\ell_X(0)}{D_X}.
\tag{34}
\]
Writing \(A_X=s/8\), equations (33)--(34) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Q\cap\{0<X\leq d_x\}|
&\leq
\ell_X(0)\left(d_x+\frac{d_x^2}{2D_X}\right)\\
&\leq
A_X\left(
\frac{2d_x}{D_X}+\frac{d_x^2}{D_X^2}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
If the positive \(X\)-support of \(Q\) occurs before \(d_x\), the
left side is integrated only to that support; extending the upper
integration bound to \(d_x\) in (35) still gives the displayed upper
bound.

Combining (32) and (35), and cancelling \(A_X>0\), yields
\[
1\leq
\frac{2d_x}{D_X}+\frac{d_x^2}{D_X^2}.
\tag{36}
\]
If \(D_X\geq3d_x\), the right side of (36) is at most
\(2/3+1/9=7/9\), a contradiction.  Thus
\[
D_X<3d_x.
\tag{37}
\]

The identical argument in the \(Y\)-coordinate starts from
\(r=-d_y\) in (6), uses the common cap area \(s/5\), and gives
\[
D_Y<3d_y.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (29), (37), and (38) imply
\[
D_X+D_Y<3(d_x+d_y)<\delta.
\tag{39}
\]
Every \(q\in Q\) consequently satisfies
\[
X(q)+Y(q)\geq-D_X-D_Y>-\delta>-2-\delta.
\tag{40}
\]
Thus all of \(Q\) lies in the halfplane in (7), and its intersection
with that halfplane has area \(|Q|=s\).  This proves (7), and (8) is
impossible because \(s>0\).

It remains to derive the full no-realization assertion without omitting
any hypothesis.  Suppose polygons \(P,Q,R\) and \(s>0\) satisfied
(9)--(19).  The first two total-area identities in (10) are exactly
(2).  The four coordinate-cap identities (11)--(12) are exactly
(3)--(4).  The definition of \(\delta\) in (13) is exactly (1).
The left inequalities in the two genuine lower crossings (16) are
exactly (6).  Therefore these \(P,Q,s,\delta\) satisfy every hypothesis
of the endpoint lemma.  The lemma forces
\[
|Q\cap\{X+Y\geq-2-\delta\}|=s,
\]
whereas the second condition in (13) requires the same area to equal
\(3s/4\), a contradiction.

The compatibility inclusion (9), the area and three cap identities for
\(R\) in (10), (14), and (15), the arbitrary-small positive crossing
witnesses (17), and the support-depth alternative (18)--(19) were all
retained in the quantified assertion.  The endpoint contradiction does
not need them, so none can repair it.  Hence no data satisfying the
complete list (9)--(19) exist.
