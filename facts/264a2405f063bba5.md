## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},
\tag{1}
\]
put
\[
a=\left(\frac34,\frac14\right),\qquad
d=\left(-\frac1{160},\frac3{160}\right),
\]
and define
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{\epsilon a+\delta d:
\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\qquad
K_s=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{(0,s)\}),
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
\frac{361}{480}\le s\le\frac{241}{320}.
\tag{4}
\]
In \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\), consider the three-slice set
\[
S_s=
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup
(\{1\}\times K_s)\cup
(\{2\}\times A_2),
\tag{5}
\]
equipped with the measure
\[
\mu_s(E)=
\sum_{j=0}^2
\left|\{z\in A_j:(j,z)\in E\}\right|,
\qquad A_1:=K_s.
\tag{6}
\]
Write
\[
T_s=|A_0|+|K_s|+|A_2|,
\qquad
t_s=\frac{2T_s}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
p=\left(\frac13,\frac14\right),
\qquad
\widehat p=(1,p).
\tag{8}
\]
For every nonzero planar linear form \(n\) and every \(L\in\mathbb R\),
the tilted closed halfspace through \(\widehat p\),
\[
H(n,L)=
\{(x,z):n\mathbin{\cdot}z+Lx
\le n\mathbin{\cdot}p+L\},
\tag{9}
\]
satisfies
\[
\mu_s(S_s\cap H(n,L))
\ge
\frac{85129}{184320}
\ge
t_s+\frac{211}{61440}.
\tag{10}
\]
The two slice-parallel closed halfspaces through \(\widehat p\) are
\[
H_-=\{x\le1\},
\qquad
H_+=\{x\ge1\},
\tag{11}
\]
and they also have mass strictly larger than the right side of (10).
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
d_{S_s}(\widehat p)
\ge t_s+\frac{211}{61440}
>t_s.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Here the depth is taken with respect to \(\mu_s\) over all closed
halfspaces containing \(\widehat p\).

Equivalently, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_n(u)=\min_{L\in\mathbb R}\bigl(
&|A_0\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u+L\}|\\
&+|A_2\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u-L\}|
\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
\[
G_{n,s}(u)
=|K_s\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u\}|+E_n(u),
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
U_s(n)=\sup\{u:G_{n,s}(u)<t_s\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then, for every \(n\ne0\),
\[
U_s(n)\le n\mathbin{\cdot}p.
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore every positive Farkas balance
\(\sum_i\alpha_i r_i=0\) obeys
\[
\sum_i\alpha_iU_s(r_i)\le0.
\tag{17}
\]

## Proof

The endpoint areas follow directly from (1)-(2):
\[
|A_0|=1,
\qquad
|A_2|=4|\det(a,d)|=\frac1{16}.
\tag{18}
\]
Put
\[
s_0=\frac{361}{480},
\qquad
s_1=\frac{241}{320},
\qquad
K_0=K_{s_0}.
\]
The cyclic vertices of \(K_0\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=(-121/320,-37/320),&
v_1&=(-119/320,-43/320),\\
v_2&=(201/320,-43/320),&
v_3&=(441/320,37/320),\\
v_4&=(439/320,43/320),&
v_5&=(119/320,203/320),\\
v_6&=(0,361/480),&
v_7&=(-121/320,123/320).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
For general \(s\in[s_0,s_1]\), the same cyclic list holds with
\(v_6=(0,s)\).  Indeed, the eight consecutive edge determinants at the
two parameter endpoints are respectively
\[
\left(
\frac3{160},\frac14,\frac1{64},\frac1{64},
\frac{131}{1920},\frac{29}{160},\frac{121}{640},\frac1{320}
\right)
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
\left(
\frac3{160},\frac14,\frac1{64},\frac1{64},
\frac{43}{640},\frac{233}{1280},\frac{121}{640},\frac1{320}
\right).
\tag{21}
\]
The varying determinants are affine in \(s\), so all remain positive.
Shoelace evaluation gives
\[
|K_0|=1.
\]
Only the two shoelace terms adjacent to \(v_6\) vary, and their combined
area derivative is
\[
\frac12\left(\frac{119}{320}+\frac{121}{320}\right)
=\frac38.
\]
Thus
\[
|K_s|=1+\frac38(s-s_0).
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(s_1-s_0=1/960\), equations (7), (18), and (22) give
\[
t_s
=\frac{11}{24}+\frac{s-s_0}{12}
\le\frac{5281}{11520}.
\tag{23}
\]

We next prove a lower bound for the two endpoint caps which is independent
of \(n\).  The point
\[
2p=\left(\frac23,\frac12\right)
\]
is in the interior of \(A_0\).  The set
\[
C=A_0\cap(4p-A_0)
\tag{24}
\]
is the parallelogram with cyclic vertices
\[
\left(0,\frac23\right),\quad
\left(\frac43,0\right),\quad
\left(\frac43,\frac13\right),\quad
(0,1).
\tag{25}
\]
It is centrally symmetric about \(2p\), and
\[
|C|=\frac49.
\tag{26}
\]
Every line through \(2p\) bisects \(C\).  Hence, for every \(n\ne0\),
\[
\left|A_0\cap
\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le2n\mathbin{\cdot}p\}\right|
\ge\frac29.
\tag{27}
\]
The body \(A_2\) is centrally symmetric about the origin, so every line
through the origin bisects it:
\[
\left|A_2\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le0\}\right|
=\frac1{32}.
\tag{28}
\]

Fix \(n\ne0\), and write \(u=n\mathbin{\cdot}p\).  If, for some \(L\),
the endpoint sum in (13) were smaller than \(1/32\), then each
nonnegative summand would be smaller than \(1/32\).  By cap monotonicity,
(27) would force
\[
u+L<2u,
\]
while (28) would force
\[
u-L<0.
\]
The first inequality says \(L<u\), and the second says \(L>u\), a
contradiction.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
E_n(n\mathbin{\cdot}p)\ge\frac1{32}
}
\qquad(n\ne0).
\tag{29}
\]
This bound already minimizes over every real height level.

We now compute the exact planar halfspace depth of \(p\) in \(K_0\).
The membership \(p\in\operatorname{int}B\) is immediate from
\[
p=\frac{2p+0}{2},
\]
because \(2p\in\operatorname{int}A_0\) and
\(0\in\operatorname{int}A_2\).

For a nonvertical line through \(p\), let \(m\) be its slope and define
\[
A(m)=
\left|K_0\cap
\{y-p_y\le m(x-p_x)\}\right|.
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(|K_0|=1\), the opposite oriented cap has area \(1-A(m)\).
The only projective events occur when the line passes through one of the
vertices in (19).  Their slopes, in increasing order, are
\[
-\frac{241}{160},\
-\frac{369}{283},\
-\frac{129}{683},\
-\frac{129}{1003},\
-\frac{111}{997},\
\frac{351}{683},\
\frac{369}{677},\
\frac{369}{37}.
\tag{31}
\]
Between consecutive event slopes, the line intersects a fixed pair of
edges.  Exact intersection followed by the shoelace formula makes
\(A(m)\) a rational function with a quadratic numerator and at most two
linear denominator factors.

The complete nine-sector audit is as follows.  The last column is
\[
d_I=\min\{\min_I A,\ 1-\max_I A\},
\]
the least area of either oriented cap in that sector.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
I&\min_I A&\max_I A&d_I\\ \hline
(-\infty,-241/160]
&233860699/444211200
&357644983/658022400
&300377417/658022400\\
[-241/160,-369/283]
&3904911/7483072
&233860699/444211200
&210350501/444211200\\
[-369/283,-129/683]
&25471/50112
&18139/34240
&16101/34240\\
[-129/683,-129/1003]
&18139/34240
&34895/64192
&29297/64192\\
[-129/1003,-111/997]
&34895/64192
&175027/319040
&144013/319040\\
[-111/997,351/683]
&46059/88640
&104951/184320
&79369/184320\\
[351/683,369/677]
&23283/45280
&46059/88640
&42581/88640\\
[369/677,369/37]
&73/160
&23283/45280
&73/160\\
[369/37,\infty)
&73/160
&300377417/658022400
&73/160.
\end{array}
\tag{32}
\]
Here the two unbounded sectors use one-sided vertical limits.
Differentiating the nine rational functions shows that only two sectors
have an interior stationary point:
\[
m=-\frac{25343}{44901}
\quad\text{in the third row of (32),}
\]
\[
m=\frac{46}{683}
\quad\text{in the sixth row of (32).}
\tag{33}
\]
All other extrema in (32) occur at an event slope from (31) or at a
vertical limit.

For an explicit audit of the globally tight sector, when
\[
-\frac{111}{997}\le m\le\frac{351}{683},
\]
exact clipping gives
\[
A(m)=
\frac{-466489m^2+1112346m+522639}
{1843200(m+1/2)},
\tag{34}
\]
\[
A'(m)=
-\frac{466489}{1843200}
\frac{(m-46/683)(m+729/683)}{(m+1/2)^2}.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus its maximum occurs at \(m=46/683\), where
\[
A(46/683)=\frac{104951}{184320},
\]
and the opposite cap has area
\[
1-A(46/683)=\frac{79369}{184320}.
\tag{36}
\]
Exact comparison of the final column in (32) shows that (36) is the
global minimum.  The vertical cases are already the limits of the first
and ninth sectors.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
d_{K_0}(p)=\frac{79369}{184320}.
}
\tag{37}
\]

The middle polygons are nested.  Indeed, \(0\in B\), and for
\(s_0\le s\), the point \((0,s_0)\) lies on the segment from \(0\) to
\((0,s)\).  Hence
\[
K_0\subseteq K_s.
\tag{38}
\]
Every closed halfplane through \(p\) therefore satisfies
\[
|K_s\cap H|\ge\frac{79369}{184320}.
\tag{39}
\]

For the tilted halfspace \(H(n,L)\) in (9), its intersections with the
three slices are exactly
\[
A_0\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u+L\},
\quad
K_s\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u\},
\quad
A_2\cap\{n\mathbin{\cdot}z\le u-L\},
\]
where \(u=n\mathbin{\cdot}p\).  Equations (29) and (39) give
\[
\mu_s(S_s\cap H(n,L))
\ge
\frac{79369}{184320}+\frac1{32}
=\frac{85129}{184320}.
\tag{40}
\]
Moreover, (23) gives
\[
\frac{85129}{184320}
-t_s
\ge
\frac{85129-84496}{184320}
=\frac{211}{61440}.
\tag{41}
\]
This proves (10) for every tilted halfspace.

It remains to audit the two horizontal halfspaces.  Directly from the
slice locations,
\[
\mu_s(S_s\cap H_-)
=|A_0|+|K_s|
\ge2,
\tag{42}
\]
\[
\mu_s(S_s\cap H_+)
=|K_s|+|A_2|
\ge\frac{17}{16}.
\tag{43}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
t_s+\frac{211}{61440}
\le\frac{85129}{184320}<1.
\tag{44}
\]
Thus (42)-(43) are strictly larger than the claimed depth bound.

Every closed halfspace containing \(\widehat p\) contains the parallel
tight halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\widehat p\).
Measure monotonicity reduces the depth infimum to the tilted and
horizontal through-point cases already audited.  Equations (40)-(44)
therefore prove (12).

For completeness, \(G_{n,s}\) in (14) is nondecreasing in \(u\).  The
middle cap is nondecreasing, and increasing \(u\) with \(L\) fixed
increases both endpoint caps in (13); taking the minimum preserves the
inequality.  Equation (10) then gives (16).  Positive homogeneity,
\[
U_s(\gamma n)=\gamma U_s(n)\qquad(\gamma>0),
\]
follows by replacing \(u,L\) with \(\gamma u,\gamma L\) in
(13)-(15).  This identity
and (16) imply, whenever \(\alpha_i>0\) and
\(\sum_i\alpha_i r_i=0\),
\[
\sum_i\alpha_iU_s(r_i)
\le
p\mathbin{\cdot}\sum_i\alpha_i r_i
=0.
\]
This proves (17) and completes the proof.

All polygon intersections, areas, event slopes, rational cap functions,
derivatives, and comparisons in (18)-(44) were independently checked in
SageMath \(10.8\) using exact `QQ` arithmetic.  No floating optimizer or
random step was used.
