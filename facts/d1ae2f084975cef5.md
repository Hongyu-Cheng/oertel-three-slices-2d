## Statement

Assume the \(A_2\)-only unequal-cap normal form of accepted fact
`c49613ac26c0c4a8`, with
\[
q=1,\qquad x_1=x_2=x_3=\frac5{21}.
\]
Let \(C_i\) be the three physical triangular outer caps and put
\[
\Delta_Q=\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}-3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}.
\]
In the barycentric coordinate triangle of area \(1/2\), let
\(\widehat C_i\) be the corresponding coordinate caps, let \(L_i\) be
their base fractions, and let \(h_i\) be their transverse barycentric
heights.  Thus
\[
|\widehat C_i|=\frac{5D}{42},\qquad
L_i h_i=t,\qquad t:=\frac{5D}{21},
\tag{1}
\]
and the physical affine area multiplier is \(2/D\).

After relabeling so that \(L_1\leq L_2\leq L_3\), every endpoint stratum
and every cyclic edge-order chamber satisfies
\[
\frac{L_1^2}{t}+\frac{L_2^2}{t}
\geq
\frac{4}{1+L_3^2/t}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, for every \(0<D\leq5/21\),
\[
|C_1+C_2+C_3|
\geq
\frac53+\frac{10}{21}\sqrt{2-\frac{21D}{5}},
\tag{3}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Delta_Q\geq
\sqrt{\frac53+\frac{10}{21}\sqrt{2-\frac{21D}{5}}}
-\sqrt{\frac{15}{7}}.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
The right side of (4) is positive for \(D<5/21\).  In particular,
\[
0<D\leq\frac18
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta_Q\geq
\sqrt{\frac53+\frac{10}{21}\sqrt{\frac{59}{40}}}
-\sqrt{\frac{15}{7}}>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Moreover, \(\Delta_Q=0\) holds if and only if, up to an
orientation-preserving affine map and redundant collinear boundary
labels, the coordinate normal-form parameters satisfy
\[
L_1=L_2=L_3=h_1=h_2=h_3=L,
\qquad
L=\frac{5+4\sqrt5}{33},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
a_{ij}=a_{ji}=\frac{1-L}{2}\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
\rho_{12}=\rho_{23}=\rho_{31}=0,
\qquad
\tau_1=\tau_2=\tau_3=\frac12.
\tag{7}
\]
The parameters \(\theta_{12},\theta_{23},\theta_{31}\) are arbitrary in
\([0,1]\), because they only place redundant labels on the three straight
central boundary segments.  In every equality configuration
\[
D=D_{\rm eq}
=\frac{21L^2}{5}
=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}
>\frac18.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus none of the other seven endpoint strata contains an equality
configuration, and equality is bounded far away from the dangerous
small-\(D\) range.

## Proof

We first prove the shape inequality (2) in the stratum where all three
cyclic endpoint pairs are positive.  Use the endpoint notation from the
proof of accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246`:
\[
a=a_{12},\quad b=a_{21},\quad
c=a_{23},\quad d=a_{32},\quad
e=a_{31},\quad f=a_{13}.
\]
Write the three outer vertices in barycentric coordinates as
\[
u_1=(-h_1,A,B),\qquad
u_2=(C,-h_2,E),\qquad
u_3=(F,G,-h_3),
\]
where
\[
A+B=1+h_1,\qquad
C+E=1+h_2,\qquad
F+G=1+h_3.
\]
All six displayed positive coordinates are positive.  The six supporting
determinants at the two outer edges of each cap are
\[
b(A-a)\geq ah_1,\qquad
e(B-f)\geq fh_1,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
a(C-b)\geq bh_2,\qquad
d(E-c)\geq ch_2,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
f(F-e)\geq eh_3,\qquad
c(G-d)\geq dh_3.
\tag{11}
\]
These are direct determinants of consecutive edges of the common convex
boundary.  They do not assume any strict ordering among nonconsecutive
edge directions.

Define the six positive overhangs
\[
\xi_1=A-a,\quad \eta_1=B-f,\qquad
\xi_2=C-b,\quad \eta_2=E-c,
\]
\[
\xi_3=F-e,\quad \eta_3=G-d.
\]
Because
\[
a+f=1-L_1,\qquad b+c=1-L_2,\qquad d+e=1-L_3,
\]
they satisfy
\[
\xi_i+\eta_i=L_i+h_i\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{12}
\]
Multiplying the two determinant inequalities at each of the three
corners, which is legitimate because \(a,b,c,d,e,f>0\), gives
\[
\xi_1\xi_2\geq h_1h_2,\qquad
\eta_2\eta_3\geq h_2h_3,\qquad
\eta_1\xi_3\geq h_1h_3.
\tag{13}
\]

Put
\[
\sigma_i=\frac{L_i}{h_i}=\frac{L_i^2}{t}.
\]
Normalize (12)-(13) by the heights.  Set
\[
X=\frac{\xi_1}{h_1},\quad X'=\frac{\eta_1}{h_1},
\quad
Y=\frac{\xi_2}{h_2},\quad Y'=\frac{\eta_2}{h_2},
\]
\[
Z=\frac{\xi_3}{h_3},\quad Z'=\frac{\eta_3}{h_3}.
\]
Then
\[
X+X'=1+\sigma_1,\qquad
Y+Y'=1+\sigma_2,\qquad
Z+Z'=1+\sigma_3,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
XY\geq1,\qquad Y'Z'\geq1,\qquad X'Z\geq1.
\tag{15}
\]
All six quantities are positive.  Therefore \(XY\geq1\) gives
\[
X+Y\geq2,
\]
while the other two inequalities in (15) give
\[
X'\geq\frac1Z,\qquad Y'\geq\frac1{Z'}.
\]
Using (14),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1+\sigma_2
&=X+X'+Y+Y'-2\\
&\geq\frac1Z+\frac1{Z'}\\
&\geq\frac4{Z+Z'}
=\frac4{1+\sigma_3},
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
where the penultimate inequality is equivalent to
\((Z-Z')^2\geq0\).  This proves (2) in the positive endpoint stratum.

We now include all eight endpoint-pair strata.  At a coincident pair,
accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246` supplies the following
endpoint-preserving limiting argument: truncate the polygon by a
supporting line moved a positive distance into the polygon at every
shared chord endpoint.  For sufficiently small simultaneous truncations,
all formerly shared endpoint pairs become positive, no cap overlap is
created, every positive cell stays positive, and the resulting polygons
converge in area and in all six chord endpoints to the original polygon.
The derivation of (16) did not use equality of the three cap areas, so it
applies to every truncated polygon with its own values of \(L_i/h_i\).
Passing to the limit proves (16), and hence (2), on each of the eight
strata.  Since (9)-(11) are the only edge-direction information used,
this also proves (2) simultaneously in every strict cyclic edge-order
chamber.  Collinear chamber walls follow by the same continuity argument.

Next we combine (2) with the central-cell geometry.  Put
\[
s_i=1-L_i.
\]
The central-cell formula in accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8` gives
\[
D
=1-\sum_{(i,j)=(1,2),(2,3),(3,1)}
 (1-\rho_{ij})a_{ij}a_{ji}
\geq1-\sum_{(i,j)=(1,2),(2,3),(3,1)}a_{ij}a_{ji}.
\tag{17}
\]
For fixed \(s_1,s_2,s_3\), substitute
\[
a_{13}=s_1-a_{12},\quad
a_{23}=s_2-a_{21},\quad
a_{31}=s_3-a_{32}.
\]
The sum of the three products in (17) is affine in each of
\(a_{12},a_{21},a_{32}\) separately.  Its maximum on the containing box
is therefore attained at a box vertex, where it is one of
\[
0,\qquad s_1s_2,\qquad s_2s_3,\qquad s_3s_1.
\]
Because \(L_1\leq L_2\leq L_3\), one has
\(s_1\geq s_2\geq s_3\), and hence
\[
D\geq1-s_1s_2
=L_1+L_2-L_1L_2
\geq L_2.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, for \(0<D\leq5/21\),
\[
\sigma_2=\frac{L_2^2}{t}
\leq\frac{D^2}{5D/21}
=\frac{21D}{5}
\leq1.
\tag{19}
\]

Since \(\sigma_1\leq\sigma_2\), inequality (2) implies
\[
2\sigma_2\geq\frac4{1+\sigma_3},
\qquad
\sigma_2\sigma_3\geq2-\sigma_2.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{L_2L_3}{t}
=\sqrt{\sigma_2\sigma_3}
\geq\sqrt{2-\sigma_2}
\geq\sqrt{2-\frac{21D}{5}}.
\tag{21}
\]

We record all mixed-area facts used in the final step.  For compact convex
polygons \(K,L\), define \(V(K,L)\) by
\[
|aK+bL|=a^2|K|+2ab\,V(K,L)+b^2|L|
\qquad(a,b\geq0).
\tag{22}
\]
Merging counterclockwise edge-vector lists proves (22), symmetry,
additivity, and translation invariance.  The polygonal support formula
\[
2V(K,L)=\sum_{e\in E(L)}|e|\,h_K(n_e)
\]
for the outward unit edge normals \(n_e\) proves monotonicity in \(K\);
the weighted sum of the normals is zero, so the formula is translation
invariant.  Symmetry gives monotonicity in both arguments, and continuity
includes segments.

For positive-area compact convex planar bodies, planar
Brunn--Minkowski says
\[
\sqrt{|K+L|}\geq\sqrt{|K|}+\sqrt{|L|},
\]
or equivalently
\[
V(K,L)\geq\sqrt{|K||L|}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equality in (23) holds exactly when \(K,L\) are positively homothetic.
Here every \(\widehat C_i\) is a positive-area triangle of area \(t/2\),
so
\[
2V(\widehat C_i,\widehat C_j)\geq t
\qquad(i\ne j).
\tag{24}
\]

The bases of \(\widehat C_2,\widehat C_3\) are segments with coordinate
edge vectors \((-L_2,0)\) and \((L_3,-L_3)\).  Their determinant has
absolute value \(L_2L_3\).  By mixed-area monotonicity, or directly by
applying (22) to these two contained segments,
\[
2V(\widehat C_2,\widehat C_3)\geq L_2L_3.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (21), (24), and (25), together with additivity in (22), give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat C_1+\widehat C_2+\widehat C_3|
&=\frac{3t}{2}
 +2\sum_{i<j}V(\widehat C_i,\widehat C_j)\\
&\geq
\frac{3t}{2}+t+t+
t\sqrt{2-\frac{21D}{5}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Multiplying by the physical affine area multiplier \(2/D\), and using
\(t=5D/21\), proves
\[
|C_1+C_2+C_3|
\geq
\frac53+\frac{10}{21}\sqrt{2-\frac{21D}{5}},
\]
which is (3).  Taking positive square roots proves (4).  Since
\[
\frac53+\frac{10}{21}
=\frac{15}{7},
\]
the right side of (4) is positive exactly when its inner square root is
larger than \(1\), in particular for every \(D<5/21\).  The right side
decreases with \(D\), so substituting \(D=1/8\) proves (5), including its
strict positivity.

It remains to classify equality \(\Delta_Q=0\).  Repeated application of
planar Brunn--Minkowski to the three positive-area triangles gives
\[
\sqrt{|C_1+C_2+C_3|}
\geq\sqrt{|C_1|}+\sqrt{|C_2|}+\sqrt{|C_3|}
=3\sqrt{\frac5{21}}.
\tag{27}
\]
Equality in (27) forces equality at both two-body
Brunn--Minkowski steps.  By the equality statement following (23), all
three caps are positively homothetic.  Their areas are equal, so their
homothety ratios are \(1\); hence they are translates.  Conversely, three
translated equal-area copies give equality in (27).

The base of \(C_i\) is parallel to the \(i\)-th side of the barycentric
triangle.  If the three caps are translates, their common triangular
shape therefore has one side in each of the three nonparallel barycentric
side directions.  Consider cap \(1\):
\[
p_{13}=(0,1-a_{13},a_{13}),\qquad
p_{12}=(0,a_{12},1-a_{12}),
\]
\[
u_1=(-h_1,U_2,U_3),\qquad U_2+U_3=1+h_1.
\]
The two nonbase edges must be parallel to the other two barycentric
sides.  The only pairing compatible with \(h_1>0\) is
\[
U_2=1-a_{13},\qquad U_3=1-a_{12}.
\]
The opposite pairing would give
\(U_2+U_3=a_{12}+a_{13}=1-L_1<1\), contradicting
\(U_2+U_3=1+h_1>1\).  Therefore
\[
h_1=L_1.
\]
Cyclically, \(h_i=L_i\) for every \(i\).  Equation (1) now gives
\[
L_1=L_2=L_3=\sqrt t=:L.
\tag{28}
\]

At the corner shared by caps \(1\) and \(2\), the directed boundary edge
from \(u_1\) to \(p_{12}\) and the directed boundary edge from \(p_{21}\)
to \(u_2\) are both \(L(e_1-e_2)\).  On a convex counterclockwise
boundary, every intervening edge direction lies between these two equal
directions, so the whole intervening central arc is parallel to
\(e_1-e_2\).  Hence
\[
a_{12}=a_{21}.
\]
The same argument at the other two corners gives
\[
a_{ij}=a_{ji}\qquad(i\ne j).
\tag{29}
\]
Write the three common values as \(b_{12},b_{23},b_{31}\).  The three
equal gap equations are
\[
b_{12}+b_{31}=b_{12}+b_{23}=b_{23}+b_{31}=1-L,
\]
so
\[
b_{12}=b_{23}=b_{31}=\frac{1-L}{2}>0.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus equality belongs only to the all-positive endpoint stratum.
The three intervening central arcs are straight, so the normal-form
parameters have \(\rho_{12}=\rho_{23}=\rho_{31}=0\), while each
\(\theta_{ij}\) only chooses a redundant point on the corresponding
segment.  The outer vertices have the two positive barycentric
coordinates equal to \((1+L)/2\), which is exactly
\(\tau_i=1/2\).

Finally, the central-area formula and (1) become
\[
D=1-\frac{3(1-L)^2}{4},
\qquad
D=\frac{21L^2}{5}.
\]
Eliminating \(D\) gives
\[
99L^2-30L-5=0.
\]
Its unique positive root is
\[
L=\frac{5+4\sqrt5}{33},
\]
and substitution gives
\[
D=\frac{147+56\sqrt5}{363}.
\]
The converse is immediate from the displayed parameters: the convex
boundary is the triangle with vertices \(u_1,u_2,u_3\), its three caps
are translates, and all area formulas hold.  Finally
\[
8(147+56\sqrt5)>8\cdot147>363,
\]
so \(D>1/8\).  This proves (6)-(8) and completes the equality
classification.
