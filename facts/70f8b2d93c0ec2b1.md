## Statement

Use the exact reference objects of accepted fact `9eb5170d42a00426`, explicitly
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
and the quadrilateral \(Q\) with cyclic vertices
\[
\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
Put
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/20\\[2pt]1/20&401/400\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B=g+M(\Delta-g),
\]
and, for \(v=(a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\), set
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=B+v,\qquad A_2=Q.
\]
Then the exact midpoint-compatibility region is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V
&=\left\{v:\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}\subseteq B+v\right\}\\
&=\left\{(a,b):
48120a-2400b\leq703,\quad
-12a+240b\leq1,\quad
1524a+1520b\geq-327
\right\}\\
&=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac{713}{48000},\frac{1571}{320000}\right),
\left(\frac{3547}{960000},-\frac{1400551}{6400000}\right),
\left(-\frac5{24},-\frac1{160}\right)
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every \(v\in\mathcal V\), define
\[
S_v=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),\qquad
T=\sum_{i=0}^2|A_i|=\frac{16027}{16000},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000}.
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), put
\[
\mu_v(H)=\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|\{z\in A_i:(i,z)\in H\}\right|,
\qquad
h_{S_v}(y)=\inf\{\mu_v(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_{S_v}((1,g+v))\geq\frac{49}{220}
=t+\frac{103}{792000}>t.
\]
Consequently, for every compatible translation \(v\), no collection of closed halfspaces of \(S_v\)-mass strictly below \(t\), even one of arbitrary cardinality, covers \(\{1\}\times(B+v)\) by its interiors.

## Proof

The inverse matrix is
\[
M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}401/400&-1/20\\[2pt]-1/20&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The barycentric coordinates of \(z=(x,y)\) relative to \(B\), indexed in the order \(X,Y,Z\), are therefore
\[
X(z)=\frac{401}{400}x-\frac1{20}y+\frac{19}{1200},\qquad
Y(z)=-\frac1{20}x+y+\frac1{60},
\]
\[
Z(z)=1-X(z)-Y(z)
=\frac{387}{400}-\frac{381}{400}x-\frac{19}{20}y.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(B=\{X\geq0,Y\geq0,Z\geq0\}\). Direct evaluation at the vertices of \(\Delta\) and \(Q\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_\Delta X&=-\frac{41}{1200},&
\min_QX&=\frac{1523}{24000},\\
\min_\Delta Y&=-\frac1{30},&
\min_QY&=\frac1{24},\\
\min_\Delta Z&=\frac3{200},&
\min_QZ&=\frac{63}{160}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
An affine functional has on \((\Delta+Q)/2\) the average of its separate minima. Hence (2) gives
\[
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}X=\frac{703}{48000},\qquad
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}Y=\frac1{240},\qquad
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}Z=\frac{327}{1600}.
\tag{3}
\]

Write
\[
s_1=\frac{401}{400}a-\frac1{20}b,qquad
s_2=-\frac1{20}a+b.
\]
By (1), membership of \(z\) in \(B+v\) is equivalent to
\[
X(z)\geq s_1,qquad Y(z)\geq s_2,qquad
Z(z)\geq-s_1-s_2.
\]
Equation (3) therefore proves that compatibility is exactly
\[
s_1\leq\frac{703}{48000},\qquad
s_2\leq\frac1{240},\qquad
s_1+s_2\geq-\frac{327}{1600}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \((s_1,s_2)^\mathsf T=M^{-1}(a,b)^\mathsf T\), the three vertices in \((s_1,s_2)\)-coordinates are
\[
\left(\frac{703}{48000},\frac1{240}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{703}{48000},-\frac{10513}{48000}\right),\qquad
\left(-\frac{1001}{4800},\frac1{240}\right).
\]
Multiplication by \(M\) gives the three displayed vertices of \(\mathcal V\), while clearing denominators in (4) gives its displayed halfplane description. This proves the exact compatibility claim.

We next record the consequence of compatibility used in the depth estimate. For any \(d\in\Delta\), \(q\in Q\), and any one of the affine functions \(F=X,Y,Z\),
\[
2F\left(\frac{d+q}{2}-v\right)=F(d)+F(q-2v).
\tag{5}
\]
It follows from (2) and (5) that compatibility is equivalently
\[
Q-2v\subseteq R,
\qquad
R=\left\{z:X(z)\geq\frac{41}{1200},\quad
Y(z)\geq\frac{40}{1200},\quad
Z(z)\geq-\frac{18}{1200}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
\mathbf r=\frac1{1200}(41,40,-18),
\qquad \kappa=1-\sum_i\mathbf r_i=\frac{1137}{1200}=\frac{379}{400}.
\]
In the \((X,Y,Z)\) barycentric coordinates of \(B\), (6) says precisely
\[
R=\{\mathbf r+\kappa\mu:\mu_i\geq0,\ \sum_i\mu_i=1\}.
\tag{7}
\]

The determinant of \(M\) is one, so \(|B|=|\Delta|=1/2\). Shoelace evaluation of the displayed vertices of \(Q\) gives \(|Q|=27/16000\), proving the asserted values of \(T\) and \(t\).

Fix \(v\in\mathcal V\), and consider a closed halfspace \(H\) containing \((1,g+v)\). If \(H\) is proper and its boundary does not contain this point, move the boundary parallel toward the point until it does. The resulting halfspace is contained in \(H\), so it suffices to bound boundary-through-point halfspaces. First suppose the planar normal is nonzero. Then for a nonzero linear functional \(\ell\) and some \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
H=\{(h,z):\ell(z-g-v)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\}.
\tag{8}
\]
At height one, after translating by \(v\), its contribution is
\[
|B\cap\{z:\ell(z-g)\geq0\}|.
\tag{9}
\]

If the height-two section of (8) contains all of \(Q\), the centroid-cap bound proved below gives mass at least
\[
\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}>\frac{49}{220},
\qquad
\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}-\frac{49}{220}
=\frac{1873}{1584000}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Otherwise choose \(q\in Q\) outside that closed section. Then
\[
\ell(q-g-v)+\beta<0.
\]
The height-zero section of (8) is
\[
\Delta\cap\{z:\ell(z-g)\geq\ell(v)+\beta\},
\]
and the preceding strict inequality gives
\[
\ell(v)+\beta<2\ell(v)-\ell(q-g)
=-\ell(q-2v-g).
\tag{11}
\]
By (6), \(q-2v\in R\). If
\[
A_R=-\min_{z\in R}\ell(z-g),
\tag{12}
\]
then (11) shows that the height-zero section contains
\[
\Delta\cap\{z:\ell(z-g)\geq A_R\}.
\tag{13}
\]

Index the vertices of \(B\) by their barycentric coordinates \(X,Y,Z\), and let \(\lambda_i\) be the values of \(z\mapsto\ell(z-g)\) at those three vertices. Since \(g\) is their centroid,
\[
\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\lambda_2=0.
\]
Order the values, divide by the absolute value of the minimum, and write the ordered triple as
\[
-1,\qquad 1-\rho,\qquad \rho,
\qquad \frac12\leq\rho\leq2.
\tag{14}
\]
This positive rescaling changes neither the cap (9) nor the cap (13), because \(A_R\) scales by the same factor.
Similar triangles give the exact area of the cap (9). If \(1/2\leq\rho\leq1\), it is
\[
\frac12\left(1-\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}\right)\geq\frac14.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(1\leq\rho\leq2\), it is
\[
f(\rho)=\frac{\rho^2}{2(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Here
\[
f'(\rho)=\frac{\rho(\rho-2)}{2(1+\rho)^2(2\rho-1)^2}\leq0,
\qquad
f(\rho)-\frac29
=\frac{(\rho-2)^2}{18(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}\geq0.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(1/4>49/220\), whenever \(\rho\leq7/4\) the middle contribution alone is at least
\[
f(7/4)=\frac{49}{220}.
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to treat \(7/4<\rho\leq2\) when the height-two section does not contain \(Q\). In this range the normalized values in (14) all lie in \([-1,2]\), with one high value \(\rho\), one minimum value \(-1\), and the remaining value \(1-\rho\). Let \((h,m,o)\) be their indices in that order. From (7),
\[
A_R=\kappa-\mathbf r\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda.
\tag{19}
\]
The \((X,Y,Z)\) barycentric coordinates of the three vertices \(v_0=(0,0)\), \(v_1=(1,0)\), \(v_2=(0,1)\) of \(\Delta\) are
\[
\frac1{1200}
\begin{pmatrix}
19&20&1161\\
1222&-40&18\\
-41&1220&21
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]
For \(\eta_j=\ell(v_j-g)-A_R\), substitution of (19) and (20) gives the following complete six-order audit:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(h,m,o)&\eta_0&\eta_1&\eta_2\\ \hline
(0,1,2)&-\dfrac{361\rho+18}{400}&\dfrac{421\rho-379}{400}&-\dfrac{\rho+798}{400}\\[4pt]
(0,2,1)&-\dfrac{37}{20}&\dfrac{421\rho-379}{400}&-\dfrac{21\rho-2}{20}\\[4pt]
(1,0,2)&-\dfrac{361\rho+18}{400}&-2&\dfrac{419\rho-378}{400}\\[4pt]
(1,2,0)&-\dfrac{37}{20}&-\dfrac{421\rho-42}{400}&\dfrac{21\rho-19}{20}\\[4pt]
(2,0,1)&\dfrac{361\rho-379}{400}&-2&-\dfrac{419\rho-41}{400}\\[4pt]
(2,1,0)&\dfrac{361\rho-379}{400}&-\dfrac{421\rho-42}{400}&\dfrac{\rho-799}{400}
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]
For \(7/4<\rho\leq2\), every row of (21) has exactly one positive entry, and that entry is at least
\[
\frac{1011}{1600}>\frac35.
\tag{22}
\]
The same matrix (20), together with \(-1\leq\lambda_i\leq2\), gives
\[
-\frac{441}{400}\leq\ell(v_j-g)\leq\frac{841}{400}
\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\tag{23}
\]
Hence any two of these three vertex values differ by at most \(641/200<4\).

By (21), the cap (13) is a one-vertex corner triangle. If \(k\) is its positive vertex and \(i,j\) are the other two vertices, similar triangles, (22), and (23) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\Delta\cap\{z:\ell(z-g)\geq A_R\}\right|
&=\frac12
\frac{\eta_k^2}
{(\ell(v_k-g)-\ell(v_i-g))(\ell(v_k-g)-\ell(v_j-g))}\\
&>\frac12\frac{(3/5)^2}{4^2}
=\frac9{800}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (17) and (24) show that in this last directional range the height-zero and height-one contributions have total mass greater than
\[
\frac29+\frac9{800}>\frac{49}{220},
\qquad
\frac29+\frac9{800}-\frac{49}{220}
=\frac{851}{79200}>0.
\tag{25}
\]

If the planar normal in a proper boundary-through-point halfspace vanishes, the halfspace contains either the height-zero and height-one slices, of mass \(1\), or the height-one and height-two slices, of mass \(1/2+27/16000\). Both exceed \(49/220\). The whole-space halfspace, if allowed, has mass \(T\). Equations (10), (18), and (25) therefore show that every closed halfspace containing \((1,g+v)\) has mass at least \(49/220\). Taking the infimum proves the asserted depth bound.

Finally, \(g+v\) lies in the interior of \(B+v\). An interior cover of the middle slice by strict-low closed halfspaces would place \((1,g+v)\) in one of their interiors, hence in a closed halfspace of mass below \(t\), contradicting the depth bound. This proves the uniform no-cover conclusion.
