## Statement

`7706987b44b0132f` and `05153059dfd604c8`.  Thus the full canonical

in every row, \(W=0\), and
\[
\{J:q_J>0\}=\{123,1,2,12\}.
\]

p=p_\varnothing,\qquad
a=q_1,\qquad b=q_2,\qquad
\gamma=q_{12},\qquad q=q_{123},

\[

\Delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3),

and put
\[

\]
Accepted fact `05153059dfd604c8` gives

\frac pm>\beta>1,\qquad \Delta>2.
\tag{1}
\]

Use the notation of accepted fact `7706987b44b0132f`:

P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad

\qquad \sigma=|C|,

\[

\[

With the notation of accepted fact `f1fcc678305bbd1c`, set

The following directional mixed-area inequality holds:

## Proof

We first prove the directional mixed-area inequality used below.  Let
\(K,L\) be planar convex bodies, let \(\varphi\) be a nonconstant
affine functional, and write
\[
w_\varphi(E)=\max_E\varphi-\min_E\varphi.
\]
Set
\[
r=\frac{w_\varphi(L)}{w_\varphi(K)}>0.
\]
Then
\[
2V(K,L)\ge r|K|+\frac{|L|}{r}.
\tag{10}
\]

To prove (10), first suppose the two \(\varphi\)-widths are equal.
Translate the bodies so that both \(\varphi\)-projections are the same
interval \(I\).  Choose an affine functional \(\psi\) whose linear part
is independent of that of \(\varphi\).  In the
\((\varphi,\psi)\)-plane, the section of each body over \(t\in I\) is
an interval.  Denote the two section lengths, in the common
\(\psi\)-coordinate, by \(\ell_K(t)\) and \(\ell_L(t)\).
For \(0\le s\le1\), the section of
\((1-s)K+sL\) over the same \(t\) contains
\[
(1-s)K_t+sL_t,
\]
whose length is
\[
(1-s)\ell_K(t)+s\ell_L(t).
\]
Integrating over \(I\), with the same constant Jacobian for both
bodies, gives
\[
|(1-s)K+sL|
\ge(1-s)|K|+s|L|.
\tag{11}
\]
The mixed-area polynomial gives
\[
|(1-s)K+sL|
=(1-s)^2|K|+2s(1-s)V(K,L)+s^2|L|.
\tag{12}
\]
Subtracting the right side of (11) from (12) and dividing by
\(s(1-s)>0\) proves
\[
2V(K,L)\ge|K|+|L|
\]
in the equal-width case.

For general \(r\), translate \(L\) arbitrarily and apply a uniform
homothety of ratio \(1/r\), obtaining \(L'\).  Then
\[
w_\varphi(L')=w_\varphi(K),\qquad
|L'|=\frac{|L|}{r^2},\qquad
V(K,L')=\frac{V(K,L)}r.
\]
The equal-width inequality for \(K,L'\), multiplied by \(r\), is
exactly (10).

Return to \(P,C\).  Define the normalized affine simplex coordinates
\[
\xi_i(x)=\frac{f_i(x)-L_i}{\Delta}.
\]
On \(P\), one has \(\xi_i\ge0\) and
\(\xi_1+\xi_2+\xi_3=1\).  Hence
\[
w_{\xi_1}(P)\le1,\qquad w_{\xi_2}(P)\le1.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(y_1,y_2\in C\), their coordinate differences give
\[
w_{\xi_1}(C)
\ge \xi_1(y_2)-\xi_1(y_1)=A,
\]
\[
w_{\xi_2}(C)
\ge \xi_2(y_1)-\xi_2(y_2)=B.
\tag{14}
\]
Choose \(i\in\{1,2\}\) attaining
\(\Lambda=\max\{A,B\}\), and put
\[
r=\frac{w_{\xi_i}(C)}{w_{\xi_i}(P)}.
\]
The body \(P\) has positive area, so its \(\xi_i\)-width is positive.
Equations (13)--(14) give
\[
r\ge\Lambda>1.
\tag{15}
\]

Convexity of \(A_2\) gives \(C\subseteq A_2\), so
\[
\sigma\le m<p
\tag{16}
\]
by (1).  Therefore
\[
g(t)=pt+\frac{\sigma}{t}
\]
is strictly increasing for \(t\ge1\), since
\[
g'(t)=p-\frac{\sigma}{t^2}>0.
\]
Apply (10) with \(K=P,L=C,\varphi=\xi_i\), and then use (15):
\[
2V(P,C)\ge g(r)\ge g(\Lambda)
=p\Lambda+\frac{\sigma}{\Lambda}.
\]
This proves (3).  The proof also shows directly that (3) is invariant
under every nonsingular affine change of the common plane: widths in
the pulled-back functional have the same ratio, while all areas and
mixed areas scale by the same absolute determinant.

Accepted fact `7706987b44b0132f` gives
\[
k\ge|H|
\ge\frac14\bigl(p+\sigma+2V(P,C)\bigr)
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal R^\star_K
\ge
\frac38\bigl(p+\sigma+2V(P,C)\bigr)+\frac72\Gamma.
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (3) with (17) proves (4).  Combining (2), (3), and (18)
proves the strict bound (5).  Here \(\Delta>2\), \(\lambda>1\), and
\(d_\alpha\ge1\), so the denominator is positive.  A large
\(\Delta\) weakens the bridge term, as it must; it is not replaced by
a false uniform constant.

We next use all three tails.  In row \(i\), the crossing equality and
the left tail give
\[
h=u_i+v_i+c_i,\qquad c_i+m\ge h,
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
u_i+v_i\le m.
\tag{19}
\]
Under the exact \(Q\)-support,
\[
v_1=m-b,\qquad v_2=m-a,\qquad v_3=q.
\]
Substitution in (19) gives (6).  The sets \(Q_1,Q_2\) are disjoint up
to null cutting lines and lie in \(C\), so
\[
\sigma\ge a+b\ge U.
\tag{20}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathcal Q^\star
=\frac32(a+b)+5\gamma+7q
\ge\frac32(a+b)\ge\frac32U,
\]
while the third inequality in (6) gives
\[
\mathcal Q^\star
\ge\frac32(a+b+\gamma)\ge\frac32u_3.
\]
This proves (7).

Since \(m<p/\beta\) by (1), condition (8), inequalities (4) and (7),
give
\[
k\ge|H|
\ge M+\frac p\beta>M+m.
\]
Under (9), inequalities (5) and (7) give
\[
\mathcal R^\star_K
>
2p-\mathcal P^\star-\underline{\mathcal Q}
\ge
2p-\mathcal P^\star-\mathcal Q^\star.
\]
Accepted fact `7706987b44b0132f` supplies the exact common-level
identity
\[
W^\star
=-2p+\mathcal P^\star+\mathcal Q^\star+\mathcal R^\star_K,
\]
so \(W^\star>0\), proving (9).

Finally, (10) survives the scalar and thin-strip tests sharply.  For
arbitrary \(p,\sigma,r,R>0\), take
\[
P_R=[0,R]\times\left[0,\frac pR\right],
\qquad
C_R=[0,rR]\times\left[0,\frac{\sigma}{rR}\right],
\]
and use horizontal projection.  The width ratio is exactly \(r\), and
\[
P_R+C_R
=[0,(1+r)R]\times
\left[0,\frac pR+\frac{\sigma}{rR}\right].
\]
Consequently
\[
|P_R+C_R|
=p+\sigma+pr+\frac{\sigma}{r},
\]
so
\[
2V(P_R,C_R)=pr+\frac{\sigma}{r}.
\]
The aspect ratio \(R^2/p\) may be arbitrarily large.  Thus no stronger
directional lower bound follows from the area data and one width ratio,
and neither (8) nor (9) is silently implied by a scalar table lacking
the required common-plane width profile.
