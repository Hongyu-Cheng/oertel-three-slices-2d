## Statement

Let
\[
A_1=H:=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(1,1),(0,1),
(-1,0),(-1,-1),(0,-1)\},
\tag{1}
\]
and let
\[
K:=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac1{12},-\frac{11}{12}\right),(1,0),(1,1),
\left(\frac16,1\right),\left(\frac1{12},\frac{11}{12}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Define the two outer slices by
\[
A_0=\frac32K,\qquad A_2=\frac12K.
\tag{3}
\]
Then the three slices are nondegenerate rational polygons,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=K\subseteq A_1,
\tag{4}
\]
and their areas are
\[
a_0=3,\qquad a_1=3,\qquad a_2=\frac13.
\tag{5}
\]

At \(z=0\), take the three unit fan normals
\[
q_1=(0,1),\qquad q_2=\frac{(1,-1)}{\sqrt2},
\qquad q_3=(-1,0).
\tag{6}
\]
They form a fan satisfying every orientation conclusion of accepted fact
`7694bc07b24d1290`.  Nevertheless, with \(\Psi\) denoting the exact middle
row from accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240`,
\[
\Psi(0,q_1)=\Psi(0,q_2)=\Psi(0,q_3)=\frac{11}{6},
\qquad
\Psi(0,(1,0))=\frac32.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(R_{(2)}=11/6\), whereas
\[
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}\Psi(0,u)\le\frac32<\frac{11}{6}=R_{(2)}.
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

The halfspace description of the middle hexagon is
\[
H=\{(x,y):-1\le x\le1,\ -1\le y\le1,\ -1\le x-y\le1\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Every vertex in (2) satisfies (9), so \(K\subseteq H\).  Since \(K\) is
convex,
\[
\frac{(3K/2)+(K/2)}2=K,
\]
which proves (4) and hence compatibility by accepted fact
`8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`.  Direct shoelace calculation gives
\[
|H|=3,\qquad |K|=\frac43.
\tag{10}
\]
For example, in the cyclic order displayed in (2), the two shoelace sums
for \(K\) are \(299/144\) and \(-85/144\), respectively.  Thus
\[
|K|=\frac12\left(\frac{299}{144}+\frac{85}{144}\right)=\frac43,
\]
and scaling gives (5).

Here \(M=3\), \(m=1/3\), and therefore
\[
r=\frac mM=\frac19,\qquad s=\frac{a_1}{M}=1.
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(r\le9/25\), accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
L(r)=1-r=\frac89<1<\frac{10}{9}=1+r=U(r).
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the area triple lies strictly inside the full residual strip.

The three lines in (6) are
\[
y=0,\qquad x-y=0,\qquad x=0.
\tag{13}
\]
The origin lies in the interior of \(H\).
They join the three opposite vertex pairs of \(H\).  Their six cyclic
sectors inside \(H\) are the triangles from \(0\) to consecutive vertices
in (1).  Every consecutive determinant is \(1\), so every sector has area
\(1/2=a_1/6\).  With
\[
D_i=\{x:q_i\cdot x\le0\},\qquad U_i=\{x:q_i\cdot x\ge0\},
\]
one has
\[
D_1=\{y\le0\},\qquad D_2=\{x-y\le0\},\qquad D_3=\{x\ge0\}.
\tag{14}
\]
These three lower halfplanes cover the plane: a point outside all three
would satisfy \(x>y>0\) and \(x<0\).  Each pairwise intersection
\(U_i\cap U_j\) is one of the three alternating sectors and hence cuts
area \(1/2=a_1/6\) from \(H\).  This verifies the complete fan convention
used in accepted fact `7694bc07b24d1290`.

For a nonzero vector \(v\), put
\[
F_v(t):=|K\cap\{x:v\cdot x\le t\}|.
\tag{15}
\]
Positive rescaling of \(v\) does not change the corresponding row.  The
outer infimal convolution at \(z=0\) is, by (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(v)
&=\inf_{c_0+c_2=0}
\left(
\left|\frac32K\cap\{v\cdot x\le c_0\}\right|
+\left|\frac12K\cap\{v\cdot x\le c_2\}\right|
\right)\\
&=\inf_{x\in\mathbb R}
\left(\frac94F_v(x)+\frac14F_v(-3x)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, use \(x=2c_0/3\), so \(2c_2=-2c_0=-3x\).

We now calculate (16) in the three fan directions.  For \(v_1=(0,1)\),
the support interval is \([-11/12,1]\), and direct horizontal slicing gives
\[
F_{v_1}(t)=\frac12\left(t+\frac{11}{12}\right)^2
\quad\left(-\frac{11}{12}\le t\le0\right).
\tag{17}
\]
In particular,
\[
F_{v_1}\left(-\frac13\right)=\frac{49}{288}.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(x\le-1/3\), then \(F_{v_1}(-3x)=4/3\), so the expression in (16)
is at least \(1/3\).  If \(x\ge-1/3\), its first term alone is at least
\[
\frac94\frac{49}{288}=\frac{49}{128}>\frac13.
\tag{19}
\]
Equality in the first case is attained at \(x=-11/12\), and hence
\[
J(v_1)=\frac13.
\tag{20}
\]

For \(v_2=(1,-1)\), the support interval is \([-5/6,1]\), and direct
slicing gives
\[
F_{v_2}(t)=\frac12t^2+\frac{11}{12}t+\frac5{12}
\quad\left(-\frac56\le t\le0\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Thus \(F_{v_2}(-1/3)=1/6\).  If \(x\le-1/3\), the second term of (16)
is \(1/3\); if \(x\ge-1/3\), the first term is at least
\[
\frac94\frac16=\frac38>\frac13.
\tag{22}
\]
Equality is attained at \(x=-5/6\), so
\[
J(v_2)=\frac13.
\tag{23}
\]

For \(v_3=(-1,0)\), the support interval is \([-1,-1/12]\).  If
\(x\le1/36\), then \(-3x\ge-1/12\), so the second term in (16) is
\(1/3\).  If \(x>1/36\), then \(x>-1/12\), so the first term in (16)
is \(3\).  Equality is attained at \(x=-1\), and therefore
\[
J(v_3)=\frac13.
\tag{24}
\]

The hexagon \(H\) is centrally symmetric about \(0\), so every line
through \(0\) bisects its area.  Equations (20), (23), and (24), together
with the harmless normalization of \(v_2\), now give
\[
\Psi(0,q_i)=\frac{|H|}{2}+J(q_i)
=\frac32+\frac13=\frac{11}{6}
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{25}
\]

Finally take \(u=(1,0)\).  Every point of \(K\) has first coordinate at
least \(1/12\), so
\[
F_u(0)=0.
\]
Choosing \(x=0\) in (16) makes both endpoint caps empty, whence
\[
J(u)=0.
\tag{26}
\]
The middle cap still has area \(3/2\), proving
\[
\Psi(0,u)=\frac32.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (25)--(27) prove the exact failure (8).

It remains only to calibrate this failure against the locked theorem.
Here
\[
T=\frac{19}{3},\qquad \frac{2T}{9}=\frac{38}{27},
\qquad
\frac32-\frac{38}{27}=\frac5{54}>0.
\tag{28}
\]
For every direction \(v\), central symmetry gives a middle contribution
exactly \(3/2\), while the endpoint infimal convolution is nonnegative.
Together with equality in (27), the exact profile of accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240` yields
\[
h_S((1,0))=\inf_{v\in\mathbb S^1}\Psi(0,v)=\frac32
>\frac{38}{27}.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus the true target-depth region is not empty.

For completeness, neither outer slice has a target-depth point.  Put
\[
d=\frac49\frac{38}{27}=\frac{152}{243}.
\tag{30}
\]
The following three halfplanes cover \(\mathbb R^2\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=\{(x,y):-x\le-1/2\},\\
P_2&=\{(x,y):12x-7y\le17/4\},\\
P_3&=\{(x,y):3x+4y\le5/2\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, their normals and thresholds satisfy the exact positive
dependence
\[
69(-1,0)+4(12,-7)+7(3,4)=(0,0),
\tag{32}
\]
\[
69\left(-\frac12\right)+4\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)
+7\left(\frac52\right)=0.
\tag{33}
\]
A point outside all three halfplanes would make the weighted left side of
(33) strictly larger than itself, a contradiction.

Exact edge intersections give
\[
K\cap P_1
=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(1,1),(1/2,1),(1/2,-1/2)\},
\]
\[
K\cap P_2
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac16,1\right),\left(\frac{15}{16},1\right),
\left(\frac1{12},-\frac{13}{28}\right),
\left(\frac1{12},\frac{11}{12}\right)\right\},
\]
\[
K\cap P_3
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac{13}{14},-\frac1{14}\right),
\left(\frac1{12},-\frac{11}{12}\right),
\left(\frac1{12},\frac9{16}\right)\right\}.
\tag{34}
\]
Shoelace calculation gives the respective cap areas
\[
\frac58,\qquad \frac{5015}{8064},\qquad \frac{5041}{8064}.
\tag{35}
\]
All three are strictly below \(d\), with exact gaps
\[
d-\frac58=\frac1{1944},\qquad
d-\frac{5015}{8064}=\frac{787}{217728},\qquad
d-\frac{5041}{8064}=\frac{85}{217728}.
\tag{36}
\]
Hence every point of \(K\) lies in a halfplane whose \(K\)-mass is less
than \(d\).  Scaling (31) by \(3/2\) shows that every point of \(A_0\)
lies in a halfplane whose \(A_0\)-mass is less than
\[
\frac94d=\frac{38}{27}.
\tag{37}
\]
Also \(|A_2|=1/3<38/27\).  By the endpoint identities in accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240`, neither outer slice contains a mixed-depth witness at
the target.  The exact middle witness (29), however, shows why the present
triple refutes only the fan interpolation step.
