## Statement

Let
\[
v(u)=a+hu+d\min\{u,t\},
\qquad
a>0,\quad h\geq0,\quad d>0,\quad 0<t<1.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(0<\lambda<1\), define the clipped crossing integrand
\[
\psi_\lambda(u)
=\max\{-v(u),\,\min\{u-\lambda,v(u)\}\},
\]
and put
\[
I=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,\qquad
p=\frac12+\frac{1}{2I}\int_0^1\psi_\lambda(u)\,du.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(\lambda<r<1\), define the contact ratio
\[
q(r)=\frac{\int_0^r v(u)\,du}{I}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
p<\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q(r)>p
\quad\text{and}\quad
4p+5q(r)>4.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular,
\[
p<\frac49,\qquad q(r)\geq p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4p+5q(r)\geq4.
\]

## Proof

The slopes of \(v\) are \(h+d>0\) on \([0,t]\) and \(h\geq0\) on
\([t,1]\).  Thus \(v\) is positive, nondecreasing, and concave, and
\(I>0\).

We first construct the clipping points.  On the left, consider
\[
f(u)=v(u)+u-\lambda.
\]
This function is strictly increasing.  If \(a=v(0)\geq\lambda\), set
\(\alpha=0\).  If \(a<\lambda\), then
\[
f(0)<0,\qquad f(\lambda)=v(\lambda)>0,
\]
so there is a unique \(\alpha\in(0,\lambda)\) satisfying
\[
v(\alpha)=\lambda-\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]

On the right, put
\[
g(u)=v(u)-u+\lambda.
\]
The function \(g\) is concave and \(g(\lambda)=v(\lambda)>0\).  If
\(g(1)\geq0\), equivalently \(v(1)\geq1-\lambda\), set
\(\beta=1\); concavity then gives \(g(u)\geq0\) throughout
\([\lambda,1]\).  If \(g(1)<0\), the nonnegative superlevel set of
\(g\) in \([\lambda,1]\) is a closed interval
\[
\{u\in[\lambda,1]:g(u)\geq0\}=[\lambda,\beta]
\]
for a unique \(\beta\in(\lambda,1)\).  Continuity gives
\[
v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, this is the only zero of \(g\) in \((\lambda,1)\): for
\(\lambda<u<\beta\), concavity between \(g(\lambda)>0\) and
\(g(\beta)=0\) gives \(g(u)>0\), while \(g(u)<0\) for \(u>\beta\).
This proves existence and uniqueness in every endpoint case.

The two sign tests \(f(u)\) and \(g(u)\) now give, apart from the
endpoints of intervals,
\[
\psi_\lambda(u)=
\begin{cases}
{}-v(u),&0\leq u\leq\alpha,\\
u-\lambda,&\alpha\leq u\leq\beta,\\
v(u),&\beta\leq u\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=\int_0^\lambda v(u)\,du,
&
X&=\int_0^\alpha v(u)\,du,\\
T&=\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du,
&
C&=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-(\lambda-\alpha)^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Integrating (7) gives
\[
J:=\int_0^1\psi_\lambda(u)\,du=-X+C+T.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus
\[
p=\frac12+\frac{J}{2I},
\qquad
q(\lambda)=\frac RI.
\tag{10}
\]

We next invoke the accepted cell lemmas, after checking that their
coverage is exhaustive.  The construction above gives exactly two
left endpoint alternatives,
\[
\alpha=0,\ a\geq\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
0<\alpha<\lambda,\ v(\alpha)=\lambda-\alpha,
\]
and exactly two right endpoint alternatives,
\[
\beta=1,\ v(1)\geq1-\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\lambda<\beta<1,\ v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda.
\]
These choices give four endpoint combinations.  Since
\[
0\leq\alpha<\lambda<\beta\leq1,
\]
every kink position belongs to at least one of the four closed order
regions
\[
t\leq\alpha,\qquad
\alpha\leq t\leq\lambda,\qquad
\lambda\leq t\leq\beta,\qquad
\beta\leq t.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence (11), crossed with the four endpoint combinations, is the
sixteen-cell decomposition.

Accepted fact `d651564643c4883d` proves
\[
I+9J<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5J+8R-3I>0
\tag{12}
\]
for both extreme weak orders \(t\leq\alpha\) and
\(\beta\leq t\), for all four endpoint combinations.  This covers
eight cells, including the shared boundaries \(t=\alpha\) and
\(t=\beta\).

Accepted fact `c17339e50a2c4109` proves (12) when \(\alpha=0\) in
both central weak orders \(\alpha\leq t\leq\lambda\) and
\(\lambda\leq t\leq\beta\), for either right endpoint alternative.
This covers four more cells, including \(t=\lambda\).

Accepted fact `ecc568083f998fa7` proves (12) when
\(0<\alpha<\lambda\) for the two strict central orders
\[
\alpha<t<\lambda,\qquad \lambda<t<\beta,
\]
again for either right endpoint alternative.  Its accepted proof
uses closed kink intervals in its Bernstein certificate and explicitly
proves the common boundary \(t=\lambda\) from both sides.  The
remaining central boundaries \(t=\alpha\) and \(t=\beta\) were already
covered by `d651564643c4883d`.  Thus these three accepted facts prove
(12) for every one of the sixteen cells and every shared boundary.

It remains only to translate (12).  Because \(I>0\), equation (10)
gives the exact equivalence
\[
p-\frac49
=\frac{I+9J}{18I}.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore \(p<4/9\) is exactly \(I+9J<0\).  The exhaustive cell result
then gives
\[
K:=5J+8R-3I>0.
\]
Again by (10),
\[
5p+4q(\lambda)-4
=\frac{K}{2I}>0.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(q(\lambda)\leq p\), then
\[
5p+4q(\lambda)\leq9p<4,
\]
contradicting (14).  Hence
\[
q(\lambda)>p.
\tag{15}
\]
Combining (14) and (15),
\[
4p+5q(\lambda)
=\bigl(5p+4q(\lambda)\bigr)+\bigl(q(\lambda)-p\bigr)>4.
\tag{16}
\]
Finally, \(v>0\) and \(r>\lambda\) imply
\[
q(r)-q(\lambda)
=\frac{\int_\lambda^r v(u)\,du}{I}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (15)--(17) prove \(q(r)>p\) and
\(4p+5q(r)>4\), completing the theorem.
