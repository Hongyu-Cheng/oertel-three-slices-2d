## Statement

Let \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty compact convex polygon, use
coordinates \((X,Y)\), and let \(\delta>0\).  Let \(B\) be a measurable
central cell contained in
\[
A\cap\{0<Y<\delta\}.
\]
Write
\[
b=|B|,\qquad
a_-=\left|A\cap\{Y<0\}\right|,
\]
and let \(\lambda\ge0\) be the chord trace at \(Y=0\), with the
one-sided interior convention when \(Y=0\) is a support line.  Then
\[
a_-\bigl(b-\lambda\delta\bigr)_+
\le
\frac{\lambda^2\delta^2}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, if \(b>\lambda\delta\), then
\[
a_-\le
\frac{\lambda^2\delta^2}
{4(b-\lambda\delta)}.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(b\le\lambda\delta\), chord concavity alone gives no finite upper
bound on \(a_-\).  If \(\lambda=0\), (1) reduces to
\[
a_-b=0.
\tag{3}
\]
The estimate and every boundary case are sharp using nonnegative concave
piecewise-linear chord profiles, including when \(Y=0\) is a support edge
or support vertex.

For the canonical specialization, let
\[
N=\{j,k,\ell\},
\]
let \(A_0\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional compact
convex polygon, and let \(p_j,p_k,p_\ell\) be affine functions satisfying
\[
p_j+p_k+p_\ell=\delta>0,
\qquad
\Delta=|\det(\nabla p_j,\nabla p_k)|>0.
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(I\subseteq N\), define the exact sign cell
\[
P_I=
A_0\cap
\left\{
p_h\le0\ (h\in I),\quad
p_h>0\ (h\notin I)
\right\},
\tag{5}
\]
and write
\[
P_0=P_\varnothing.
\]
All three cutting sets \(A_0\cap\{p_i=0\}\) have planar area zero:
indeed, (4) makes every \(\nabla p_i\) nonzero, so each cutting set lies
in an affine line.  Thus changing strictness on those lines does not
change any cell area, while (5) gives an exact disjoint partition.

Assume that the only positive-area cells are
\[
P_j,\qquad P_0,\qquad P_\ell,\qquad P_{k\ell},
\]
with respective physical areas
\[
a,\qquad b,\qquad c,\qquad d.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(\lambda_{0j},\lambda_{0k},\lambda_{0\ell}\) be the three
coordinate chord traces at \(p_i=0\).  Then
\[
\Delta a\bigl(\Delta b-\lambda_{0j}\delta\bigr)_+
\le\frac{\lambda_{0j}^2\delta^2}{4},
\tag{7j}
\]
\[
\Delta d\bigl(\Delta b-\lambda_{0k}\delta\bigr)_+
\le\frac{\lambda_{0k}^2\delta^2}{4},
\tag{7k}
\]
\[
\Delta(c+d)\bigl(\Delta b-\lambda_{0\ell}\delta\bigr)_+
\le\frac{\lambda_{0\ell}^2\delta^2}{4}.
\tag{7l}
\]
Writing
\[
u_j=a,\qquad u_k=d,\qquad u_\ell=c+d,
\tag{8}
\]
each inequality with \(u_i>0\) is equivalently
\[
\lambda_{0i}\delta
\ge
2\Delta\left(\sqrt{u_i(u_i+b)}-u_i\right).
\tag{9}
\]

## Proof

Let
\[
w(y)=\mathcal H^1\{X:(X,y)\in A\}
\]
be the horizontal chord length.  It is concave on the vertical support
interval of \(A\).

First suppose \(a_->0\) and \(b>0\).  Then \(Y=0\) lies in the
interior of that interval, the actual chord length equals \(\lambda\),
and the finite right derivative
\[
g=w'_+(0)
\]
is a supergradient.  Hence
\[
w(y)\le\lambda+gy
\tag{10}
\]
throughout the support interval.  Since
\(B\subset\{0<Y<\delta\}\),
\[
b\le\int_0^\delta w(y)\,dy.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(b>\lambda\delta\), (10)-(11) force \(g>0\) and give
\[
g\ge\frac{2(b-\lambda\delta)}{\delta^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(y<0\), nonnegativity and (10) show that the support cannot extend
below \(-\lambda/g\).  Therefore
\[
a_-
\le\int_{-\lambda/g}^0(\lambda+gy)\,dy
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2g}
\le
\frac{\lambda^2\delta^2}
{4(b-\lambda\delta)}.
\]
This proves (1) when its left side is nonzero.

If \(b\le\lambda\delta\), the left side of (1) vanishes.  If
\(a_-=0\), the inequality is immediate; in this case \(Y=0\) may be
a lower support line, and \(\lambda\) is its trace from the interior
side.  If \(\lambda=0\) and \(a_->0\), concavity and nonnegativity
prevent positive width on both sides of zero, so \(b=0\).  If
\(\lambda=0\), \(b>0\), and \(a_-=0\), the line \(Y=0\) may instead
be a support vertex.  Thus (3) is exact and all boundary configurations
are covered.

It remains to prove sharpness.  Suppose first that
\(\lambda>0\) and \(b>\lambda\delta\).  Put
\[
\gamma=\frac{2(b-\lambda\delta)}{\delta^2}.
\]
For any
\[
0<a_-\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2\gamma},
\]
put
\[
\eta=\frac{\lambda^2}{2a_-}\ge\gamma
\]
and take a chord profile whose consecutive affine pieces are
\[
w(y)=\lambda+\eta y
\quad\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\eta}\le y\le0\right),
\qquad
w(y)=\lambda+\gamma y
\quad(0\le y\le\delta),
\]
followed by an affine descent to zero with slope at most \(\gamma\).
The slopes are nonincreasing, so \(w\) is concave.  The region
\[
\{(X,Y):0\le X\le w(Y)\}
\]
is a convex polygon, its area below zero is \(a_-\), and its strip area
on \(0<Y<\delta\) is exactly \(b\).  Equality in (2) occurs when
\(\eta=\gamma\).  For \(a_-=0\), begin the support at \(Y=0\) with
the same positive-side profile.

If \(\lambda>0\) and \(b\le\lambda\delta\), use an arbitrary
\(\eta>0\) below zero and the constant profile \(w=\lambda\) on
\([0,\delta]\).  Then
\[
a_-=\frac{\lambda^2}{2\eta}
\]
can be any positive number, while a central subcell of any area
\(0\le b\le\lambda\delta\) fits in the strip.  The choice
\(a_-=0\) again starts the support at zero.

If \(\lambda=0\) and \(b>0\), the triangular profile
\[
w(y)=\frac{2b}{\delta^2}y
\qquad(0\le y\le\delta),
\]
followed by an affine descent to zero, realizes \(a_-=0\) and strip
area \(b\).  Reflecting a triangle below zero realizes
\(a_->0,b=0\).  The case \(a_-=b=0\) is immediate.  Hence (1) is the
strongest relation among \(a_-,b,\lambda,\delta\) forced by chord
concavity.

We now prove the canonical specialization.  From (4),
\[
\nabla p_j+\nabla p_k+\nabla p_\ell=0.
\]
Therefore all three pairwise determinant magnitudes equal \(\Delta\),
and every \(\nabla p_i\) is nonzero.  On \(P_0\), all three
\(p\)-coordinates are positive and sum to \(\delta\), so
\[
P_0\subset\{0<p_i<\delta\}
\qquad(i=j,k,\ell).
\tag{13}
\]

The exact sign definition (5), the support assumption (6), and the null
cutting lines give
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{p_j\le0\}\right|=|P_j|=a,
\tag{14j}
\]
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{p_k\le0\}\right|=|P_{k\ell}|=d,
\tag{14k}
\]
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{p_\ell\le0\}\right|
=|P_\ell|+|P_{k\ell}|=c+d.
\tag{14l}
\]
For each \(i\), use \(Y=p_i\) and either one of the other
\(p\)-coordinates as the transverse coordinate.  The coordinate
Jacobian has magnitude \(\Delta\), so coordinate area is
\(\Delta\) times physical area.  Hence the coordinate area of \(P_0\)
is \(\Delta b\), while the coordinate areas below \(p_i=0\) are
\[
\Delta a,\qquad \Delta d,\qquad \Delta(c+d)
\]
by (14j)-(14l).  The coordinate chord traces are
\(\lambda_{0i}\).  Substitution in (1) proves (7j)-(7l).

To obtain (9), put
\[
r=\lambda_{0i}\delta,\qquad u=u_i.
\]
In the nonvacuous regime, the corresponding inequality is
\[
r^2+4\Delta u\,r-4\Delta^2ub\ge0.
\]
Its nonnegative root is
\[
2\Delta\left(\sqrt{u(u+b)}-u\right).
\]
This root is strictly below \(\Delta b\) when \(u,b>0\), so the same
lower bound also includes the vacuous regime
\(r\ge\Delta b\).  This proves (9).
