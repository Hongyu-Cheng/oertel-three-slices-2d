## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a two-dimensional convex polygon, and let
\(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) be pairwise nonparallel affine lines.  Choose a
central closed halfplane \(H_i^-\) and its complementary outer closed
halfplane \(H_i^+\) for each \(i\).  Suppose that
\[
|K\cap H_i^+|>0,\qquad
\left|K\cap H_1^-\cap H_2^-\cap H_3^-\right|>0,
\]
and that the interiors of the three outer caps \(K\cap H_i^+\) are
pairwise disjoint.  The endpoints of chords belonging to different
\(\ell_i\) are allowed to coincide.

Then there is a two-dimensional convex polygon \(K'\) with at most twelve
vertices such that:

1. the three cutting lines and every geometric chord endpoint are unchanged;
2. every boundary arc between consecutive chord-endpoint labels remains in
   its original closed sign chamber;
3. the areas of the three outer caps and of the central remainder are
   unchanged.

Consequently, if the strict rational table is fully realized, then its
\(A_2\)-geometry has an exact replacement \(B\) with
\[
4\leq |\operatorname{vert}B|\leq12,
\qquad
|B|=1,
\]
\[
|B\cap H_i^+|=\frac15\quad(i=1,2,3),
\qquad
\left|B\cap H_1^-\cap H_2^-\cap H_3^-\right|=\frac25,
\]
and the unchanged global central triangle
\[
\mathcal T_Q=H_1^-\cap H_2^-\cap H_3^-
\]
satisfies
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\geq\frac{169}{10}.
\]
This replacement is asserted only for the \(A_2\)-geometry; no replacement
of \(A_2\) inside the full midpoint-inclusion realization is claimed.

## Proof

Each \(\ell_i\) meets the interior of \(K\).  Indeed, its outer side has
positive area by hypothesis.  Its central side also has positive area
because it contains the positive-area central remainder.  Hence
\(K\cap\ell_i\) is a nondegenerate chord.  In particular, the two endpoints
of the chord on any one \(\ell_i\) are distinct.  The only missing case from
accepted fact `2ec8b7d91f50db55` is therefore that an endpoint of the chord
on \(\ell_i\) may equal an endpoint of the chord on \(\ell_j\), where
\(i\ne j\).

Insert the six chord-endpoint labels into the cyclic boundary list, retaining
both labels when two endpoints coincide geometrically.  Between consecutive
labels the strict sign vector is constant.  A nonconstant arc cannot lie in
two outer open halfplanes: a relative neighborhood immediately inside a
nontrivial polygonal boundary arc would then give a positive-area
intersection of the corresponding two cap interiors.  Thus every
nonconstant labeled arc lies in the closure of either one cap chamber or the
central chamber.  A pair of consecutive labels at the same geometric point
simply determines a zero-length, zero-area arc.  Hence the six labeled arcs,
with zero arcs retained, have exactly the chamber structure needed by the
local slides in accepted fact `2ec8b7d91f50db55`.

For completeness, the only point in that proof which used global
distinctness of all six marks was the claim that a moving unmarked vertex
cannot first leave its chamber through a cutting line.  That claim remains
valid here.  During one of the accepted parallel slides, suppose the moving
vertex first meets \(\ell_i\).  Until that time the slide stays in one
chamber and preserves its signed segment area, so the polygon continues to
have positive area on both sides of \(\ell_i\).  For a two-dimensional
convex polygon with interior points on both sides of a line, the two
endpoints of its chord on that line are the only boundary points on the
line.  Those two geometric endpoints stayed fixed throughout the slide.
Therefore the moving vertex cannot create a third boundary contact.  It can
only collide with one of the fixed chord endpoints.  As in accepted fact
`2ec8b7d91f50db55`, keep the fixed marked occurrence and delete the
coincident unmarked occurrence together with any resulting zero-length or
collinear unmarked chain.  If the fixed point carries two endpoint labels,
both labels remain at that same geometric point.  Thus a coincidence between
different chords creates no new obstruction to the slide.

Apply the accepted parallel slide on every nonzero labeled arc until that
arc has at most one unmarked vertex.  Zero arcs already have none.  Each
slide fixes all chord endpoints, stays in the same closed chamber, and
preserves the signed area between the affected arc and its fixed endpoint
chord.  It therefore preserves all three cap areas and the central area.
There are at most six distinct geometric marked points and at most one
unmarked vertex on each of the six labeled arcs.  The resulting polygon
\(K'\) consequently has at most twelve vertices.  This proves the
degeneracy extension.

Now assume a full realization of the strict rational table and apply the
extension to \(K=A_2\) with
\[
\ell_i=\{x:f_i(x)+L_i=0\}.
\]
The exact \(Q\)-cell table gives three pairwise interior-disjoint caps of
area \(1/5\), central remainder \(2/5\), and total area one.  The reduced
polygon \(B\) has the same data.  Its cutting lines are unchanged, so its
global central triangle is the original \(\mathcal T_Q\).  Accepted fact
`b1eaab0ae37367d1` gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\geq\frac{169}{10}.
\]
If \(B\) had three vertices, the triangular endpoint bound in accepted fact
`68717bfc506e2bb9` would instead give
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\leq16|B|=16,
\]
a contradiction.  A two-dimensional polygon has at least three vertices,
so \(B\) must have between four and twelve vertices.  No step replaces
\(A_2\) in the full compatibility constraint; the conclusion is exactly the
claimed \(A_2\)-only extremal reduction.
