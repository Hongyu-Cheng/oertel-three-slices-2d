## Statement

Let \(P,Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex bodies with
\[
|P|=M,\qquad |Q|=m.
\tag{1}
\]
Use coordinates \((x,y)\) on \(P\) and \((X,Y)\) on \(Q\).  Define
\[
u_2=|P\cap\{x\le0\}|,\qquad
v_2=|Q\cap\{X\le0\}|,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
u_3=|P\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
v_3=|Q\cap\{Y\le0\}|,
\tag{3}
\]
and assume
\[
u_2,u_3,v_2,v_3>0,\qquad u_2,u_3<M.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
\delta=\max_{p\in P}(x(p)+y(p)),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
G_2(r)=|P\cap\{x\le r\}|+|Q\cap\{X\le-r\}|,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
G_3(r)=|P\cap\{y\le r\}|+|Q\cap\{Y\le-r\}|,
\tag{7}
\]
and assume the full lower-crossing left inequalities
\[
G_2(r)\ge u_2+v_2,\qquad
G_3(r)\ge u_3+v_3
\quad\text{for every }r<0.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
p=\sqrt{\frac{M}{u_2}},\qquad
q=\sqrt{\frac{M}{u_3}},\qquad
C=\frac{pq-p-q}{p+q},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
B_i=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u_i/v_i}-1}
\quad(i=2,3).
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
C\ge\max\{B_2,B_3\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q\subseteq\{X+Y\ge-\delta\}
\subseteq\{X+Y\ge-2-\delta\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently, suppose additionally that \(c_1,t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
v_1:=|Q\cap\{X+Y\ge-2-\delta\}|,
\qquad c_1+m\ge t.
\tag{12}
\]
Under the premise in (11), one has \(v_1=m\), and hence the strict contact
failure inequality \(c_1+v_1<t\) is impossible.

## Proof

Define the negative coordinate depths and positive supports of \(P\) by
\[
d_2=-\min_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
d_3=-\min_{p\in P}y(p),
\tag{13}
\]
\[
s_2=\max_{p\in P}x(p),\qquad
s_3=\max_{p\in P}y(p).
\tag{14}
\]
Every affine line has planar area zero.  Thus \(u_2,u_3>0\) imply
\(d_2,d_3>0\), while \(u_2,u_3<M\) imply \(s_2,s_3>0\).

Choose \(a_2\in P\) with \(x(a_2)=-d_2\), and put
\[
\lambda_2=\frac{d_2}{d_2+s_2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Convexity gives
\[
a_2+\lambda_2(P-a_2)\subseteq P.
\tag{16}
\]
The \(x\)-coordinate of every point in this homothetic copy is at most
\[
-d_2+\lambda_2(s_2+d_2)=0.
\]
The copy therefore lies in \(P\cap\{x\le0\}\), and comparison of planar
areas gives
\[
M\lambda_2^2\le u_2.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(\sqrt{u_2/M}=1/p\), equations (15)--(17) yield
\[
\frac{d_2}{d_2+s_2}\le\frac1p,
\qquad
s_2\ge(p-1)d_2.
\tag{18}
\]
The same argument based at a point minimizing \(y\) gives
\[
s_3\ge(q-1)d_3.
\tag{19}
\]
These weak inequalities include equality and do not require either minimizing
set to be a single point.

At a point of \(P\) maximizing \(x\), the \(y\)-coordinate is at least
\(-d_3\); at a point maximizing \(y\), the \(x\)-coordinate is at least
\(-d_2\).  Hence (5), (18), and (19) imply
\[
\delta\ge(p-1)d_2-d_3,
\qquad
\delta\ge-d_2+(q-1)d_3.
\tag{20}
\]
Take \(q/(p+q)\) times the first inequality and \(p/(p+q)\) times the
second.  Both weights are positive and sum to one, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta
&\ge
\frac{q\bigl((p-1)d_2-d_3\bigr)+p\bigl(-d_2+(q-1)d_3\bigr)}{p+q}\\
&=\frac{pq-p-q}{p+q}(d_2+d_3)
=C(d_2+d_3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
This proves the support-depth bound with the exact constant \(C\).

We next derive the endpoint depth bound in the \(X\)-coordinate.  Substituting
\(r=-d_2<0\) into the first inequality in (8), the \(P\)-term has area zero
because it is contained in the supporting line \(\{x=-d_2\}\).  Therefore
\[
|Q\cap\{X\le d_2\}|\ge u_2+v_2.
\tag{22}
\]
Subtracting (2) gives
\[
|Q\cap\{0<X\le d_2\}|\ge u_2>0.
\tag{23}
\]
In particular, \(Q\) has points with positive \(X\)-coordinate and
\(v_2<m\).  Since \(v_2>0\), it also has points with negative
\(X\)-coordinate.  Thus
\[
D_2=-\min_{q\in Q}X(q)>0
\tag{24}
\]
and \(0\) is in the interior of the \(X\)-projection of \(Q\).

Let \(\ell_2(t)\) be the length of the vertical section
\[
Q\cap\{X=t\}.
\tag{25}
\]
The function \(\ell_2\) is nonnegative and concave on the projection interval
of \(Q\): the Minkowski interpolation of two section intervals is contained
in the intervening section, and interval lengths interpolate linearly.
Moreover, \(\ell_2(0)>0\).  Indeed, the positive-area parts of \(Q\) on both
sides of \(\{X=0\}\) contain interior points; the convexity of
\(\operatorname{int}Q\) puts an interior point of \(Q\) on \(\{X=0\}\).
Cavalieri's formula and the chord inequality for this concave function on
\([-D_2,0]\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_2
&=\int_{-D_2}^{0}\ell_2(t)\,dt\\
&\ge \frac{D_2}{2}\bigl(\ell_2(-D_2)+\ell_2(0)\bigr)
\ge\frac{D_2\ell_2(0)}2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
For every \(s>0\) in the \(X\)-projection of \(Q\), concavity also gives the
secant-slope inequality
\[
\frac{\ell_2(s)-\ell_2(0)}s
\le
\frac{\ell_2(0)-\ell_2(-D_2)}{D_2}
\le\frac{\ell_2(0)}{D_2}.
\tag{27}
\]
If the positive \(X\)-support of \(Q\) is smaller than \(d_2\), the integral
below stops at that support.  In either case, (26)--(27) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Q\cap\{0<X\le d_2\}|
&\le \ell_2(0)\left(d_2+\frac{d_2^2}{2D_2}\right)\\
&\le v_2\left(
\frac{2d_2}{D_2}+\frac{d_2^2}{D_2^2}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (23) and (28), and putting \(z=d_2/D_2>0\), yields
\[
\frac{u_2}{v_2}\le2z+z^2.
\tag{29}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
z\ge\sqrt{1+\frac{u_2}{v_2}}-1,
\]
and hence
\[
D_2\le
\frac{d_2}{\sqrt{1+u_2/v_2}-1}
=B_2d_2.
\tag{30}
\]

Repeating (22)--(30) with \(y,Y,u_3,v_3,d_3\) in place of
\(x,X,u_2,v_2,d_2\) gives
\[
D_3:=-\min_{q\in Q}Y(q)>0,
\qquad
D_3\le B_3d_3.
\tag{31}
\]

Now assume \(C\ge\max\{B_2,B_3\}\).  Each \(B_i\) is strictly positive, so
this premise also gives \(C>0\).  Equations (21), (30), and (31) imply
\[
D_2+D_3
\le B_2d_2+B_3d_3
\le C(d_2+d_3)
\le\delta.
\tag{32}
\]
Every \(q\in Q\) satisfies \(X(q)\ge-D_2\) and \(Y(q)\ge-D_3\), even though
the two minima need not occur at the same point.  Therefore
\[
X(q)+Y(q)\ge-D_2-D_3\ge-\delta>-2-\delta.
\tag{33}
\]
This proves both containments in (11).

All boundary cases used above are harmless.  A point or positive-length
support face of \(P\) has planar area zero in (22).  A positive-length
support face of \(Q\) merely makes \(\ell_i(-D_i)>0\), which is retained in
(26) and only strengthens that estimate.  The cutting lines at zero are
planar null.  If a positive support of \(Q\) occurs before \(d_i\), the
corresponding strip integral is truncated, as explicitly allowed in (28).
All constant comparisons are weak, so equality in a homothetic cap estimate,
a secant estimate, or \(C=B_i\) is included.

Finally, (11) gives \(v_1=m\) under definition (12).  The tail inequality in
(12) then gives \(c_1+v_1=c_1+m\ge t\), contradicting
\(c_1+v_1<t\).  This is the stated strict-contact consequence.
