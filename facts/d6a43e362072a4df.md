## Statement

Let \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) be nonzero pairwise nonparallel linear forms on \(\mathbb R^2\) satisfying
\[
\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0,
\]
and let \(O\) be their common zero.  In the coordinates
\[
x=\ell_1,\qquad y=\ell_2
\]
put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline B&=\{x\geq0,\ y\leq0,\ x+y\leq0\},\\
\overline D&=\{x\geq0,\ y\geq0\},\\
\overline F&=\{x\leq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq0\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{V_1,V_2,V_3\}
\]
be a nondegenerate triangle with
\[
V_1\in\overline F,\qquad
V_2\in\overline B,\qquad
V_3\in\overline D.
\]
Set
\[
p_i=\frac{|T\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|}{|T|}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
If
\[
p_1\leq\frac12,\qquad p_2\leq\frac12,
\]
then
\[
p_3\geq(1-p_1)(1-p_2).
\]

## Proof

We first assume that the three vertices lie in the open sectors \(F,B,D\), respectively.  Thus \(V_i\) lies in the singleton sector of \(H_i=\{\ell_i\leq0\}\): \(V_1\in F\), \(V_2\in B\), and \(V_3\in D\).  The three vertex directions have every cyclic angular gap strictly smaller than \(\pi\).  Hence \(O\in\operatorname{int}T\).  Let
\[
(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)
\]
be the positive barycentric coordinates of \(O\).

Fix \(i\), and let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).  A line through \(O\) which separates \(V_i\) from \(V_j,V_k\) meets \(V_iV_j\) and \(V_iV_k\) at edge ratios \(s,t\in(0,1)\) measured from \(V_i\).  In barycentric coordinates its equation is
\[
\frac{z_j}{s}+\frac{z_k}{t}=1.
\]
Writing \(q=\lambda_j/s\), passage through \(O\) gives
\[
s=\frac{\lambda_j}{q},\qquad
t=\frac{\lambda_k}{1-q},\qquad
\lambda_j<q<1-\lambda_k.
\]
The separated corner triangle therefore has area fraction
\[
\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{q(1-q)}.
\tag{13}
\]
Its infimum over all such lines is
\[
m_i=
\begin{cases}
4\lambda_j\lambda_k,&\lambda_j,\lambda_k\leq1/2,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_i+\lambda_k},
&\lambda_j>1/2,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j},
&\lambda_k>1/2.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, \(q(1-q)\) is maximized at \(q=1/2\) when \(1/2\) belongs to the closed interval \([\lambda_j,1-\lambda_k]\); otherwise it is maximized at the endpoint nearest \(1/2\).  Since \(H_i\) cuts off \(V_i\), (13) gives
\[
p_i\geq m_i.
\]

Suppose first that \(\lambda_1>1/2\).  Formula (14) gives
\[
m_2=\frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_2+\lambda_3},
\qquad
m_3=\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_2+\lambda_3}=1-m_2.
\]
Consequently
\[
(1-p_1)(1-p_2)
\leq1-p_2
\leq1-m_2
=m_3
\leq p_3.
\]
The case \(\lambda_2>1/2\) is symmetric.

Suppose next that \(\lambda_3>1/2\).  Then
\[
m_1=\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2},
\qquad
m_2=\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2},
\qquad
m_1+m_2=1.
\]
Because \(p_i\geq m_i\) and \(p_1,p_2\leq1/2\), equality is forced throughout:
\[
\lambda_1=\lambda_2,\qquad
p_1=m_1=p_2=m_2=\frac12.
\]
For \(m_1\), the maximizing endpoint of \(q(1-q)\) in (13) is \(q=1-\lambda_3\); equality means \(t=1\), so \(\partial H_1\) passes through \(V_3\).  The identical argument for \(m_2\) says that \(\partial H_2\) also passes through \(V_3\).  Both lines already pass through \(O\), so they coincide, contradicting pairwise nonparallelity.  Thus \(\lambda_3>1/2\) is impossible.

It remains that
\[
\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\leq\frac12.
\]
Put
\[
u=\lambda_1,\qquad v=\lambda_2,\qquad w=\lambda_3.
\]
Then (14) gives
\[
m_1=4vw,\qquad m_2=4uw,\qquad m_3=4uv.
\]
The exact factorization is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_3-(1-m_1)(1-m_2)
={}&-(2u+2v-1)\\
&\quad\cdot
\bigl(8u^2v+8uv^2-12uv+2u+2v-1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The first factor before the minus sign is
\[
2u+2v-1=1-2w\geq0.
\]
The second factor equals
\[
1-2w-4uv(1+2w).
\]
Since \(u,v\leq1/2\) and \(u+v=1-w\), their product is minimized, on the closed admissible interval, at
\[
\{u,v\}=\left\{\frac12,\frac12-w\right\}.
\]
Hence
\[
uv\geq\frac{1-2w}{4},
\]
so the second factor in (15) is nonpositive.  Therefore
\[
m_3\geq(1-m_1)(1-m_2).
\]
Using \(p_i\geq m_i\) now yields
\[
p_3\geq m_3
\geq(1-m_1)(1-m_2)
\geq(1-p_1)(1-p_2).
\]
This proves the result for vertices in the open sectors.

We now pass to the closed sectors while retaining the two half-mass inequalities.  Choose fixed points
\[
U_1\in F,\qquad U_2\in B,\qquad U_3\in D
\]
and a vector \(W\in D\).  For \(\varepsilon>0\), first put
\[
\widehat V_i(\varepsilon)
=(1-\varepsilon)V_i+\varepsilon U_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
For all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), these three points form a nondegenerate triangle, lie in \(F,B,D\), respectively, and converge to \(V_1,V_2,V_3\).

For the triangle with vertices
\[
\widehat V_1(\varepsilon),\quad
\widehat V_2(\varepsilon),\quad
\widehat V_3(\varepsilon)+\delta W,
\]
write the \(\ell_1\)-values as
\[
-r_1,\ s_1,\ t_1+\delta\ell_1(W)
\]
and the \(\ell_2\)-values, in the vertex order
\(\widehat V_2,\widehat V_1,\widehat V_3+\delta W\), as
\[
-r_2,\ s_2,\ t_2+\delta\ell_2(W).
\]
All \(r_i,s_i,t_i\) are positive.  The corner-cap formula gives
\[
p_i(\varepsilon,\delta)
=\frac{r_i^2}
{(r_i+s_i)(r_i+t_i+\delta\ell_i(W))}
\qquad(i=1,2).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(p_i(\varepsilon,\delta)\leq1/2\) whenever
\[
\delta\geq
\delta_i(\varepsilon):=
\max\left\{
0,\
\frac{2r_i^2/(r_i+s_i)-r_i-t_i}{\ell_i(W)}
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(r_i^0,s_i^0,t_i^0\) be the limiting nonnegative values.  If \(r_i^0+s_i^0>0\), the limiting corner-cap formula and \(p_i\leq1/2\) give
\[
\frac{2(r_i^0)^2}{r_i^0+s_i^0}-r_i^0-t_i^0\leq0.
\]
If \(r_i^0+s_i^0=0\), then \(r_i^0=s_i^0=0\) and \(t_i^0>0\), since a nonzero linear form cannot vanish at all three vertices of a nondegenerate triangle.  The numerator in (17) then tends to \(-t_i^0<0\).  Consequently
\[
\delta_i(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]
Choose
\[
\delta(\varepsilon)
=\max\{\delta_1(\varepsilon),\delta_2(\varepsilon)\}+\varepsilon.
\]
Then \(\delta(\varepsilon)>0\), \(\delta(\varepsilon)\to0\), the third vertex remains in \(D\), and both first cap fractions are at most \(1/2\).

The open-sector result applies to these perturbed triangles.  Triangle area and halfplane-section area are continuous functions of the vertices as long as the limiting triangle is nondegenerate.  Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) therefore gives
\[
p_3\geq(1-p_1)(1-p_2)
\]
for the original triangle.  This approximation stays inside the assigned singleton-sector closures and covers vertices on fan rays, a vertex equal to \(O\), an edge on a fan line, empty caps, and the half-full cases \(p_1=1/2\) or \(p_2=1/2\).
