## Statement

Let
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
and define, for \(0<r<\rho\),
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le 9/25,\\[2pt]
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\[2pt]
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

There exist nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Writing
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac kM,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
their areas satisfy the exact residual conditions
\[
0<r<\rho,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r).
\]

There also exist nonconstant affine functions \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1
\]
such that, for
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|
\]
and the centroid \(g\) of \(K\), one has
\[
c_1+m<t,\qquad f_1(g)>0,
\]
but
\[
\max_{\substack{j\ne1\\ f_j(g)<0}}
\left[
c_j+\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|+
|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|
\right)
\right]<t.
\]
Thus the registered joint two-direction centroid-clearance assertion is false.

## Proof

Let
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,2)\}.
\]
The determinant of the two nonzero vertices is \(2\), so
\[
k=|K|=1.
\]
Define the barycentric affine functions
\[
\beta_0(x,y)=1-x-\frac y2,\qquad \beta_1(x,y)=x.
\]
Put
\[
p=\left(\frac{279}{800},\frac{279}{400}\right),
\qquad
B=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(1,0),(0,2),p\right\}.
\]
The barycentric coordinates of \(p\) relative to \(K\) are
\[
\beta_0(p)=\frac{121}{400},
\qquad
\beta_1(p)=\frac{279}{800},
\qquad
\frac{p_y}{2}=\frac{279}{800}.
\]
They are positive and sum to \(1\), so \(p\in K\) and hence \(B\subseteq K\).

Moreover,
\[
\det\left(
(-1,2),
\left(-\frac{521}{800},\frac{279}{400}\right)
\right)
=\frac{121}{200}.
\]
Therefore
\[
|B|=\frac{121}{400}.
\]
For \(\alpha>0\), write \(\alpha B=\{\alpha x:x\in B\}\), and define
\[
A_0=\frac{20}{11}B,
\qquad
A_2=\frac{2}{11}B.
\]
These are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, and
\[
M=|A_0|
=\left(\frac{20}{11}\right)^2\frac{121}{400}
=1,
\]
while
\[
m=|A_2|
=\left(\frac{2}{11}\right)^2\frac{121}{400}
=\frac1{100}.
\]
For every convex set \(B\) and positive \(\alpha,\gamma\),
\[
\alpha B+\gamma B=(\alpha+\gamma)B:
\]
one inclusion follows from convexity, and the reverse inclusion follows by taking the same point of \(B\) in both summands. Consequently
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=
\frac{(20/11+2/11)B}{2}
=B
\subseteq K.
\]
Thus compatibility holds.

The normalized areas are
\[
r=\frac1{100},\qquad s=1.
\]
Since \(81>26\),
\[
\rho-\frac9{25}
=\frac{18-2\sqrt{26}}{25}>0.
\]
Hence
\[
0<\frac1{100}<\frac9{25}<\rho.
\]
The relevant branches of the residual bounds are therefore
\[
L(r)=1-r=\frac{99}{100},
\qquad
U(r)=1+r=\frac{101}{100},
\]
and
\[
L(r)<s<U(r).
\]
All residual-domain inequalities are strict. The target threshold is
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9
=\frac{2(1+1/100+1)}9
=\frac{67}{150}.
\]

Now set \((q,i,\ell)=(1,2,3)\) and define
\[
\lambda_1
=25\left(\beta_1-\frac{17}{50}\right),
\qquad
\lambda_2
=1000\left(\beta_0-\frac{83}{250}\right),
\qquad
\lambda_3=1-\lambda_1-\lambda_2,
\]
and
\[
f_j=-\lambda_j\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\]
Then
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
The linear parts of \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) are respectively
\[
(25,0),\qquad (-1000,-500),\qquad (975,500).
\]
Their pairwise determinants are
\[
-12500,\qquad 12500,\qquad -12500,
\]
so all three functions are nonconstant and their linear parts are pairwise nonparallel. The same holds for the \(f_j\).

The centroid of \(K\) is
\[
g=\left(\frac13,\frac23\right),
\]
at which
\[
\beta_0(g)=\beta_1(g)=\frac13.
\]
Therefore
\[
\lambda_1(g)
=25\left(\frac13-\frac{17}{50}\right)
=-\frac16,
\]
\[
\lambda_2(g)
=1000\left(\frac13-\frac{83}{250}\right)
=\frac43,
\]
and
\[
\lambda_3(g)
=1+\frac16-\frac43
=-\frac16.
\]
It follows that
\[
f_1(g)=\frac16>0,\qquad
f_2(g)=-\frac43<0,\qquad
f_3(g)=\frac16>0.
\]
Thus the index set in the asserted maximum is exactly \(\{2\}\).

For \(0\le\tau\le1\), the cap of \(K\) defined by a barycentric coordinate being at least \(\tau\) is a copy of \(K\), homothetic about the corresponding vertex with linear ratio \(1-\tau\). Since \(|K|=1\), its area is \((1-\tau)^2\).

Because \(f_1\le0\) is equivalent to \(\lambda_1\ge0\), hence to \(\beta_1\ge17/50\),
\[
c_1
=\left(1-\frac{17}{50}\right)^2
=\frac{1089}{2500}.
\]
Consequently
\[
c_1+m
=\frac{1089}{2500}+\frac1{100}
=\frac{557}{1250},
\]
and
\[
t-(c_1+m)
=\frac{67}{150}-\frac{557}{1250}
=\frac{2}{1875}>0.
\]
Thus the strict tail hypothesis \(c_1+m<t\) holds.

Similarly, \(f_2\le0\) is equivalent to \(\beta_0\ge83/250\), so
\[
c_2
=\left(1-\frac{83}{250}\right)^2
=\frac{27889}{62500}.
\]
The exact deficit is
\[
t-c_2
=\frac{67}{150}-\frac{27889}{62500}
=\frac{83}{187500}>0.
\]

It remains to verify that the two outer caps in direction \(2\) can simultaneously be empty. Since \(\beta_0\) is affine and vanishes at the first two vertices of \(B\),
\[
\sup_B\beta_0=\beta_0(p)=\frac{121}{400}.
\]
For every \(\alpha>0\) and \(z\in B\),
\[
\beta_0(\alpha z)
=1-\alpha\bigl(1-\beta_0(z)\bigr).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sup_{A_0}\beta_0
=1-\frac{20}{11}\left(1-\frac{121}{400}\right)
=-\frac{59}{220},
\]
and
\[
\sup_{A_2}\beta_0
=1-\frac{2}{11}\left(1-\frac{121}{400}\right)
=\frac{1921}{2200}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\sup_{A_0}\lambda_2
=1000\left(-\frac{59}{220}-\frac{83}{250}\right)
=-\frac{6602}{11}
<-600,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{A_2}\lambda_2
=1000\left(\frac{1921}{2200}-\frac{83}{250}\right)
=\frac{5953}{11}
<600.
\]
At \(a=600\),
\[
A_0\cap\{f_2\le a\}
=A_0\cap\{\lambda_2\ge-600\}
=\varnothing,
\]
and
\[
A_2\cap\{f_2\le-a\}
=A_2\cap\{\lambda_2\ge600\}
=\varnothing.
\]
Since cap areas are nonnegative,
\[
\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_2\le a\}|+
|A_2\cap\{f_2\le-a\}|
\right)=0.
\]
The proposed maximum consequently equals
\[
c_2=\frac{27889}{62500}
=\frac{67}{150}-\frac{83}{187500}
<t.
\]
This verifies every hypothesis and the strict failure of the claimed conclusion.
