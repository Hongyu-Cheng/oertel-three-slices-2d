## Statement

Use the common affine fan and exact-cell convention of accepted facts
`9aea6365aa810898` and `9fdba5fffd591cda`.  Put
\[
D=16072081,\qquad
\gamma=\frac{320000}{D},\qquad
E=\frac{D}{640000000},\qquad
e=\frac{E}{50}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(\gamma E=1/2000\) and \(\gamma e=1/100000\).
There is no compact two-dimensional convex body \(Q\) in the
\((\theta_1,\theta_2)\)-plane with physical exact-cell table
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
J&1&2&3&12&13&23&123\\ \hline
q_J&
\frac{11}{2000}&0&\frac1{100000}&
\frac3{2000}&\frac{99}{100000}&
\frac{199}{100000}&\frac1{100000},
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
and whose actual support widths \(r_1,r_3\), as defined in
`9fdba5fffd591cda`, satisfy the two target midpoint inequalities
\[
\rho_i\geq
\frac{\gamma}{4}
\left(50+\frac{q_i}{\gamma}+10r_i\right)
\qquad(i=1,3),
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
\rho_1=\frac{4327}{15000}
-\frac{126243}{64288324000},
\qquad
\rho_3=\frac{2611}{10000}
-\frac{126243}{64288324000}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently there is no such \(Q\) whose actual
\((r_1,h_1,r_3,h_3)\) satisfy all the symbolic support-height and
midpoint constraints displayed in `9aea6365aa810898`.

## Proof

Work first in coordinate area.  Set
\[
u=\theta_1,\qquad v=-\theta_2,\qquad
\theta_3=v-12-u.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(v<0\), the three chambers from left to right are
\[
Q_1,\quad Q_{13},\quad Q_3,
\tag{6}
\]
while for \(0<v<12\) they are
\[
Q_{12},\quad Q_{123},\quad Q_{23}.
\tag{7}
\]
Write every nonempty horizontal section of \(Q\) as
\[
Q\cap\{v=\text{constant}\}
=\{(u,v):\ell(v)\leq u\leq r(v)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Convexity of \(Q\) says that \(\ell\) is convex and \(r\) is
concave on the projection interval.  Define the two potential
outer-tail profiles
\[
g(v)=v-12-\ell(v),\qquad p(v)=r(v).
\tag{9}
\]
Both \(g\) and \(p\) are concave.

The coordinate areas supplied by (2) are
\[
|Q_1|=11E,\quad |Q_3|=e,\quad
|Q_{12}|=3E,\quad |Q_{23}|=\frac{199}{50}E,\quad
|Q_{123}|=e.
\tag{10}
\]
At each height, the left outer-cell length is at most \(g(v)_+\),
and the right outer-cell length is at most \(p(v)_+\).  Hence
\[
|Q_1|\leq\int_{v<0}g(v)_+\,dv,\qquad
|Q_3|\leq\int_{v<0}p(v)_+\,dv,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
|Q_{12}|\leq\int_{v>0}g(v)_+\,dv,\qquad
|Q_{23}|\leq\int_{v>0}p(v)_+\,dv.
\tag{12}
\]

The positivity of \(Q_1,Q_{12}\) and concavity imply \(g(0)>0\):
choose a negative height and a positive height at which \(g>0\), and
apply concavity at their convex combination \(0\).  Likewise the
positivity of \(Q_3,Q_{23}\) gives \(p(0)>0\).

Let \(C_1=\overline{Q_1}\) and \(C_3=\overline{Q_3}\).  The support
incidences proved in `9fdba5fffd591cda` give
\[
g(0)\leq r_1,\qquad p(0)\leq r_3.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, a point \((u,0)\) with \(u<-12\) belongs to \(C_1\), and its
distance from \(u=-12\) is bounded by
\[
r_1=\max_{C_1}(\theta_1+\theta_2)
-\min_{C_1}\theta_1-\min_{C_1}\theta_2.
\]
Similarly, a point \((u,0)\) with \(u>0\) belongs to \(C_3\), and its
positive \(u\)-coordinate is bounded by \(r_3\).

We need one elementary derivative bound.  Let \(\phi\) be either
\(g\) or \(p\), put \(\phi_0=\phi(0)>0\), and let
\(s=\phi'_-(0)\), which is finite because \(0\) is in the interior
of the projection interval.  Concavity gives the global supporting
line
\[
\phi(v)\leq\phi_0+sv.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(s>0\), the positive part of the right side on the negative
half-line is a triangle of area \(\phi_0^2/(2s)\).  If \(s\leq0\),
its positive part cannot increase to the right of \(0\).  Applying
(11) therefore gives
\[
(g'_-(0))_+\leq\frac{g(0)^2}{2|Q_1|}
\leq\frac{r_1^2}{22E},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
(p'_-(0))_+\leq\frac{p(0)^2}{2|Q_3|}
\leq\frac{r_3^2}{2e}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (13)--(16) now imply, for every \(v>0\),
\[
g(v)_+\leq r_1+\frac{r_1^2}{22E}v,
\qquad
p(v)_+\leq r_3+\frac{r_3^2}{2e}v.
\tag{17}
\]

We next use the tiny common central cell.  Since the positive sets of
the concave functions \(g,p\) are intervals containing \(0\), let
\(\tau>0\) be the smaller of their two positive-side endpoints.  For
every \(0<v<\min\{\tau,12\}\), one has
\[
\ell(v)<v-12<0<r(v).
\]
The section of \(Q_{123}\) therefore contains the full interval
\([v-12,0]\), of length \(12-v\).  If \(\tau\geq1\), this would give
\[
e=|Q_{123}|\geq\int_0^1(12-v)\,dv=\frac{23}{2},
\]
which is impossible because \(e<1\).  Hence \(\tau<1\), and
\[
e=|Q_{123}|
\geq\int_0^\tau(12-v)\,dv
\geq11\tau.
\]
Thus
\[
\tau\leq\tau_0:=\frac e{11}
=\frac{E}{550}
=\frac{16072081}{352000000000}.
\tag{18}
\]

At least one outer tail terminates at \(\tau\).  Suppose first that
the left tail terminates there.  Then (12), (17), and (18) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Q_{12}|
&\leq r_1\tau+\frac{r_1^2}{44E}\tau^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The \(i=1\) instance of (3), reduced exactly as in
`9fdba5fffd591cda`, gives
\[
r_1\leq
\frac{73695475241}{96000000000}<1.
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently
\[
|Q_{12}|
<
\tau_0+\frac{\tau_0^2}{44E}.
\tag{21}
\]
Exact subtraction gives
\[
3E-
\left(\tau_0+\frac{\tau_0^2}{44E}\right)
=
\frac{641369233897719}{8518400000000\cdot1000}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
This contradicts \(|Q_{12}|=3E\).

Suppose instead that the right tail terminates first.  The \(i=3\)
instance of (3), now using \(q_3/\gamma=e\), gives
\[
r_3\leq
\frac{78539443259}{320000000000}
<\frac14,
\tag{23}
\]
where the exact gap from \(1/4\) is
\[
\frac14-
\frac{78539443259}{320000000000}
=\frac{1460556741}{320000000000}>0.
\]
Equations (12), (17), and (18) then give
\[
|Q_{23}|
<
\frac{\tau_0}{4}
+\frac{\tau_0^2}{64e}.
\tag{24}
\]
But exact subtraction gives
\[
\frac{199}{50}E-
\left(
\frac{\tau_0}{4}
+\frac{\tau_0^2}{64e}
\right)
=
\frac{24765132099199}{247808000000000}>0,
\tag{25}
\]
contradicting \(|Q_{23}|=199E/50\).

The two alternatives are exhaustive, including the case in which
both tails terminate at the same height.  Therefore no convex
\(Q\) can realize (2) while its actual support widths satisfy the
midpoint inequalities (3).  In particular, no choices of the actual
heights \(h_1,h_3\) can make all the support-height inequalities in
`9aea6365aa810898` hold: the obstruction occurs earlier, at the
common concave section profile.  The displayed placeholder quadruple
in that accepted fact is therefore scalar-only and is not induced by
one realizing convex endpoint.

All rational reductions in (18), (20), (22), (23), and (25) were
independently checked in SageMath \(10.8\) over \(\mathbb Q\).  No
floating-point infeasibility decision is used.
