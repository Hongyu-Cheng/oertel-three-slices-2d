## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) be compact two-dimensional convex polygons in the plane, with \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1\). Use the canonical quantities \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\), \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), and \(t=2T/9\). Let \(S\) be their three integer-height slices, and let \(\mu\) be the sum of their planar area measures. Suppose \(z\) is an interior middle point attaining \(d=\max_{w\in A_1}h_S((1,w))\), and a genuine minimizing cut through \((1,z)\) is

\[
\widehat H_j=\{(h,w):q_j\cdot(w-z)+\lambda_j(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad \lVert q_j\rVert=1,
\qquad \mu(\widehat H_j)=d.
\tag{1}
\]

Define \(L_{ij}=A_i\cap\{w:q_j\cdot(w-z)+\lambda_j(i-1)=0\}\). Assume that all three boundary sections \(L_{ij}\) are nondegenerate transverse chords, and that every chord endpoint lies in the relative interior of a polygon edge. Thus the cut is in an open polygon cell, with no boundary vertex on its plane. The first-order conclusions of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346` use the full outer chords in this case: their lengths are the same positive \(\ell_j\), the middle length is \(b_j\), and the total section first moment is zero. These conclusions also follow directly from genuine minimality under the present differentiability hypotheses.

Choose a unit tangent \(p_j\) perpendicular to \(q_j\). In the coordinates \(\xi=q_j\cdot(w-z)\) and \(\upsilon=p_j\cdot(w-z)\), write \(\upsilon_{ij}^-<\upsilon_{ij}^+\) for the endpoints of \(L_{ij}\). Near these endpoints the two polygon edges have equations

\[
\upsilon=\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}
+k_{ij}^{\pm}\bigl(\xi+\lambda_j(i-1)\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]

The edge slopes \(k_{ij}^{\pm}\) are ordinary finite slopes \(d\upsilon/d\xi\); they are not selected lengths at supporting edges. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_j={}&(k_{0j}^+-k_{0j}^-)+(k_{2j}^+-k_{2j}^-),\\
\Gamma_j={}&(k_{2j}^+\upsilon_{2j}^+-k_{2j}^-\upsilon_{2j}^-)
            -(k_{0j}^+\upsilon_{0j}^+-k_{0j}^-\upsilon_{0j}^-),\\
\Theta_j={}&\sum_{i=0}^2
\left[k_{ij}^+(\upsilon_{ij}^+)^2-k_{ij}^-(\upsilon_{ij}^-)^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Then the necessary second-order conditions are

\[
\Delta_j\ge0,\qquad \Theta_j\ge0,\qquad
\Gamma_j^2\le\Delta_j\Theta_j.
\tag{4}
\]

There is the following geometric specialization. Suppose additionally that the middle chord is centered at \(z\) and its width is locally constant in the normal coordinate, that is, \(\upsilon_{1j}^++\upsilon_{1j}^-=0\) and \(k_{1j}^+=k_{1j}^-\). Then

\[
k_{0j}^+\ge k_{2j}^-,\qquad k_{2j}^+\ge k_{0j}^-.
\tag{5}
\]

If also \(k_{ij}^+\ge k_{ij}^-\) for \(i=0,2\), the intervals \([k_{0j}^-,k_{0j}^+]\) and \([k_{2j}^-,k_{2j}^+]\) overlap. If both outer widths are locally constant, (5) forces \(k_{0j}^+=k_{0j}^-=k_{2j}^+=k_{2j}^-\).

Conditions (4)-(5) apply in particular to every cut of accepted fact `e4a8e2c026dd7346` meeting the stated open-cell hypotheses. They exclude the explicit first-order configuration verified below, although its scalar height-slope curvature is strictly positive. No assertion about second derivatives at a supporting edge or a vertex is made.

## Proof

Fix \(j\). Nearby cuts can be parameterized, without requiring a unit normal, by

\[
\{(h,w):(q_j+\eta\beta p_j)\cdot(w-z)
+(\lambda_j+\eta\tau)(h-1)\le0\},
\tag{6}
\]

where \(\beta\) and \(\tau\) are arbitrary real numbers. Dividing both the normal and the height slope by \(\sqrt{1+\eta^2\beta^2}\) gives the same halfspace with unit normal. Thus (6) is a valid local coordinate system for rotations and height-slope changes; no normalization term has been discarded.

For small \(\eta\) every chord continues to cross the same two edges. Indeed, in the two free parameters \(\eta\beta\) and \(\eta\tau\), a cut passing through a specified polygon vertex is a linear equation. Finitely many such equations divide parameter space into finitely many cells. The hypotheses put the initial parameters in the interior of one cell.

Let \(F_i(\eta)\) be the area from slice \(i\) in (6), and denote its two moving tangent coordinates by \(\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}(\eta)\). Solving the cut equation together with (2) gives

\[
\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}(\eta)
=\frac{\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}-\eta k_{ij}^{\pm}\tau(i-1)}
       {1+\eta k_{ij}^{\pm}\beta},
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{d\eta}\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}(\eta)\right|_{\eta=0}
=-k_{ij}^{\pm}\bigl(\beta\upsilon_{ij}^{\pm}+\tau(i-1)\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]

The swept-area formula, parameterizing the moving chord by \(\upsilon\), is

\[
F_i'(\eta)
=-\int_{\upsilon_{ij}^-(\eta)}^{\upsilon_{ij}^+(\eta)}
  \bigl(\beta\upsilon+\tau(i-1)\bigr)\,d\upsilon.
\tag{8}
\]

For clarity, its normal speed is \(-(\beta\upsilon+\tau(i-1))/\sqrt{1+\eta^2\beta^2}\), while arclength along the moving line is \(\sqrt{1+\eta^2\beta^2}\,d\upsilon\). These factors cancel exactly. Inside the fixed cell all endpoints vary smoothly, so differentiating (8) and using (7) is legitimate. It yields

\[
\left.\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}\sum_iF_i(\eta)\right|_{\eta=0}
=\sum_{i=0}^2\left[
k_{ij}^+\bigl(\beta\upsilon_{ij}^++\tau(i-1)\bigr)^2
-k_{ij}^-\bigl(\beta\upsilon_{ij}^-+\tau(i-1)\bigr)^2
\right]
=\Theta_j\beta^2+2\Gamma_j\beta\tau+\Delta_j\tau^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Genuine minimality in both signs of \(\eta\) makes the first derivative vanish and the quadratic form in (9) nonnegative for every \(\beta,\tau\). The first derivative for \(\beta=0\) gives equality of the two full outer lengths. Its rotational component gives the tangential first-moment equation. The normal first moment vanishes as well because the two outer normal coordinates are \(\lambda_j\) and \(-\lambda_j\) and their lengths agree, while the middle coordinate is zero. This independently recovers the first-order identities used here, consistently with `e4a8e2c026dd7346`. Nonnegativity of a real symmetric two-by-two form is equivalent to (4), including the zero diagonal cases.

For (5), put \(\kappa_{ij}=(k_{ij}^++k_{ij}^-)/2\) for \(i=0,2\). The middle chord is centered, so the first-moment equation and equal outer lengths imply that the two outer tangent midpoints are opposite. Denote the height-zero midpoint coordinate by \(c\). The outer endpoint coordinates are therefore \(c\pm\ell_j/2\) and \(-c\pm\ell_j/2\). Substitute \(\tau'=\tau-\beta c\) into (9). The middle summand vanishes because its endpoint coordinates are opposite and its two edge slopes agree. The remaining form is

\[
\Delta_j(\tau')^2
+2\ell_j(\kappa_{2j}-\kappa_{0j})\beta\tau'
+\frac{\ell_j^2\Delta_j}{4}\beta^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Because \(\ell_j>0\), its nonnegativity is equivalent to \(\Delta_j\ge0\) and \(|\kappa_{2j}-\kappa_{0j}|\le\Delta_j/2\). Expanding the two sides of the latter absolute-value inequality gives precisely the two cross inequalities (5). Conversely, their sum gives \(\Delta_j\ge0\), so there is no extra sign assumption hidden in this equivalence. When each individual outer width is nondecreasing, (5) says exactly that the two ordered edge-slope intervals overlap. With both outer widths constant, the intervals are singletons and must agree.

We next verify an excluded configuration with strictly positive scalar curvature. The point in this calibration is denoted \(w_0=(0,0)\); it is not asserted to be the attained maximizer \(z\) of (1). Define rational polygons

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0={}&\{(x,y):-1/8\le x\le15/8,\ -1+x/8\le y\le1+3x/8\},\\
A_2={}&\{(x,y):-1/8\le x\le3/8,\ -1-3x/8\le y\le1-x/8\},\\
A_1={}&[-1/8,3/2]\times[-11/8,11/8].
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The coordinate bounds of \((A_0+A_2)/2\) are \(x\in[-1/8,9/8]\) and \(y\in[-69/64,87/64]\). They lie within the defining bounds of \(A_1\), proving compatibility. Direct integration of the outer widths \(2+x/4\) gives

\[
a_0=\frac{71}{16},\qquad a_2=\frac{65}{64},\qquad
a_1=\frac{143}{32},\qquad T=\frac{635}{64}.
\tag{12}
\]

In the notation of the residual strip in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, \(r=m/M=65/284<9/25\) and \(s=a_1/M=286/284\). Its strict bounds are \(219/284<s<349/284\), which hold. Thus no violation of that necessary area strip explains the exclusion.

Consider the cut \(x\le0\) through \((1,w_0)\), with \(q=(1,0)\) and \(\lambda=0\). Its outer chord lengths are both \(2\), its middle chord length is \(11/4\), and all three chord midpoints are \(w_0\). Every section endpoint is in an edge interior. It therefore satisfies the equal-length and full first-moment conditions, as well as the selected-segment containment and midpoint bounds of the accepted first-order statement. Its mass is

\[
\mu(\{x\le0\})
=2\int_{-1/8}^{0}(2+x/4)\,dx+\frac{11}{4}\frac18
=\frac{215}{256}<\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{635}{288}.
\tag{13}
\]

Suppressing the single cut index \(j\), its outer edge slopes are \((k_0^-,k_0^+)=(1/8,3/8)\) and \((k_2^-,k_2^+)=(-3/8,-1/8)\), while both middle slopes are zero. The outer intervals are disjoint, violating (5). More explicitly, (9) is

\[
\frac12\beta^2-2\beta\tau+\frac12\tau^2.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus the scalar height-slope curvature is \(1/2>0\), but \(\beta=\tau=1\) gives negative curvature \(-1\).

There is an exact finite decrease, not only a formal Hessian test. For \(0<\eta\le1/100\) set \(H_\eta=\{x+\eta y+\eta(h-1)\le0\}\). On \(A_0\) the endpoint \((0,1)\) stays fixed and the other endpoint has \(x\)-coordinate \(2\eta/(1+\eta/8)\); the added triangle has that area. On \(A_2\) the endpoint \((0,-1)\) stays fixed and the other endpoint has \(x\)-coordinate \(-2\eta/(1-\eta/8)\); the removed triangle has area \(2\eta/(1-\eta/8)\). Both statements follow by intersecting the new cut with the two indicated edges, and the small bound on \(\eta\) keeps the intersections on those edges. The middle cap keeps its area because its tangent interval is symmetric. Consequently

\[
\mu(H_\eta)
=\frac{215}{256}
+\frac{2\eta}{1+\eta/8}-\frac{2\eta}{1-\eta/8}
=\frac{215}{256}-\frac{\eta^2}{2(1-\eta^2/64)}
<\frac{215}{256}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(\eta=1/100\) the exact decrease is \(32/639999\). Thus this configuration cannot be a genuine active minimizing cut, despite all the stated first-order checks and the strict scalar second-order check. This is the promised concrete exclusion.

Finally, the following calibration limits what a single-cut second-variation argument can prove. For \(0<\varepsilon<1/8\) let

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0={}&[-\varepsilon,2-\varepsilon]\times[-1,1],\\
A_2={}&[-\varepsilon,1/2-\varepsilon]\times[-1,1],\\
A_1={}&[-\varepsilon,8/5-\varepsilon]\times[-5/4,5/4].
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Their midpoint rectangle is \([-\varepsilon,5/4-\varepsilon]\times[-1,1]\), contained in \(A_1\). Their areas are \(a_0=4\), \(a_2=1\), \(a_1=4\), so \(T=9\) and \(t=2\). Again the strict residual strip of `15bb2c1680a0254e` holds, now with \(r=1/4\) and \(s=1\).

At \(w_0=(0,0)\), the cut \(x\le0\) has full outer chord lengths \(2\), middle length \(5/2\), and all first moments zero. Every edge slope \(k_i^{\pm}\) is zero. More strongly, for all sufficiently small real \(\beta,\tau\) the mass of \(\{x+\beta y+\tau(h-1)\le0\}\) is exactly

\[
(2\varepsilon+2\tau)+\frac52\varepsilon
+(2\varepsilon-2\tau)=\frac{13}{2}\varepsilon.
\tag{17}
\]

The cut is genuinely globally minimizing through \((1,w_0)\). To prove this, both outer rectangles contain the centrally symmetric rectangle \(P=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[-1,1]\). For every unit planar normal and every height slope, the two corresponding caps of \(P\) have opposite thresholds and their areas sum to \(|P|=4\varepsilon\). Hence the actual outer contribution is at least \(4\varepsilon\). The middle rectangle contains the centrally symmetric rectangle \(R=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[-5/4,5/4]\), so every middle halfplane through \(w_0\) has area at least \(|R|/2=5\varepsilon/2\). The exact tilted-halfspace description in accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` therefore gives

\[
h_S((1,w_0))=\frac{13}{2}\varepsilon,
\tag{18}
\]

with equality supplied by \(x\le0\). Equivalently, the two horizontal masses are \(5\) and \(8\) and exceed this value, so they cannot alter the conclusion. In particular the mass-to-\(T\) ratio tends to zero, while the full local two-parameter function is constant and all chord sections are positive and transverse.

This does not meet the attained-maximum hypothesis. At the point \((\varepsilon,0)\), both outer rectangles instead contain the symmetric rectangle \([-\varepsilon,3\varepsilon]\times[-1,1]\), and the middle contains \([-\varepsilon,3\varepsilon]\times[-5/4,5/4]\). These inclusions hold because \(\varepsilon<1/8\). The same lower-bound argument, with equality from \(x\le\varepsilon\), gives \(h_S((1,(\varepsilon,0)))=13\varepsilon\), strictly greater than (18). Thus the additional concurrent directions of `e4a8e2c026dd7346` cannot be omitted when seeking a bound on \(d\). The calibration falsifies only an individual-cut mass bound from local second-order data; it does not falsify the locked target or the full active-family reduction.

All derivatives above were taken at full transverse chords inside fixed polygon cells. The argument neither chooses a supporting-edge subsegment nor identifies such a selection with a one-sided derivative.
