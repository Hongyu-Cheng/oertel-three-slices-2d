## Statement

Use the exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on
\(\mathbb R^2\), with pairwise nonparallel linear parts, such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\), put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,
\]
and let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact
two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
B:=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\]
Write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,\qquad q_J=|Q_J|,\qquad
m=|A_2|,\qquad \kappa=|K|,
\]
and abbreviate \(p_0=p_{\varnothing}\).  If
\[
p_{12},p_{13},p_{23},q_1,q_3,q_{12}>0,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\quad
|B|>
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}+\frac{p_0}{\delta}
>
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}.
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently every compatible \(K\) satisfies
\[
\kappa>\frac{3p_0+m}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]
No hypothesis on \(q_2\) is needed.  In particular, (2) applies to
the one-zero class \(q_2=0\) in which all other non-forced endpoint
cells are positive.

For the exact proposed one-zero datum, the relevant values are
\[
p_0=\frac45,\qquad
p_{12}=p_{13}=p_{23}=\frac{43}{4600},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
q_1=\frac{461}{1725},\qquad
q_2=0,\qquad
q_3=\frac1{50},\qquad
q_{12}=\frac1{100},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
m=\frac13,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{9329}{13800}.
\tag{6}
\]
There are no common-fan convex endpoints and compatible \(K\)
realizing (4)--(6), since
\[
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}
=\frac{41}{60}
=\frac{9329}{13800}+\frac{101}{13800}
>\kappa.
\tag{7}
\]

This is an open obstruction.  Put
\[
\eta=\frac{101}{55200}.
\tag{8}
\]
Any nonnegative datum whose \(p_0,m,\kappa\) and the six positive
cell masses displayed in (4)--(5) differ coordinatewise from the
corresponding values there by less than \(\eta\) still satisfies
(1) and violates (3).  This remains true in the exact subfamily
\(q_2=0\).

## Proof

The affine map
\[
F:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,\qquad
F(z)=(p_1(z),p_2(z))
\tag{9}
\]
is an affine isomorphism.  Indeed, the linear parts of \(p_1,p_2\)
are the pairwise nonparallel linear parts of \(f_1,f_2\).  Let
\(\gamma>0\) be its constant inverse area Jacobian, so physical area
equals \(\gamma\) times area in the coordinates
\[
x=p_1,\qquad y=p_2,\qquad p_3=\delta-x-y.
\tag{10}
\]

We first show that \(A_0\) contains the closed \(P\)-triangle
\[
\mathcal T_P=\{p_1\geq0,\ p_2\geq0,\ p_3\geq0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Positive area in \(P_{23},P_{13},P_{12}\), respectively, supplies
strict points whose normalized \(p\)-coordinate vectors in the
affine plane
\[
\mathcal A=\{(z_1,z_2,z_3):z_1+z_2+z_3=1\}
\]
have sign patterns
\[
(+,-,-),\qquad(-,+,-),\qquad(-,-,+).
\tag{12}
\]
In each vector the positive coordinate is greater than \(1\).
For completeness, put these three vectors in the columns of a matrix
\(X\), with the positive coordinate on the diagonal, and let \(D\)
be the positive diagonal of \(X\).  Then
\[
C_0=D-X\geq0,\qquad H=C_0D^{-1}\geq0,
\]
and every column sum of \(H\) is
\[
\frac{X_{ii}-1}{X_{ii}}<1.
\]
Thus
\[
X=(I-H)D,\qquad
X^{-1}=D^{-1}\sum_{n\geq0}H^n\geq0.
\]
Every column of \(X\) has coordinate sum \(1\), so
\(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}X^{-1}=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\).
For each standard basis vector \(e_i\), the vector
\(X^{-1}e_i\) is therefore nonnegative and has coordinate sum
\(1\).  Hence every \(e_i\) lies in the convex hull of the three
vectors in (12).  Pulling this conclusion back to the plane proves
(11).  This is the \(P\)-side barycentric argument of accepted fact
`0773c759f7f76dd9`; importantly, it uses only the three positive
\(P\)-doubletons in (1).

In the coordinates (10), \(\mathcal T_P\) is
\[
T_\delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(\delta,0),(0,\delta)\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The exact cell \(P_0\) is the interior of this triangle up to its
three null boundary lines.  Since (11) holds,
\[
p_0=|\mathcal T_P|=\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]

We next extract the width forced on \(A_2\) by the three positive
\(Q\)-cells.  Put
\[
a_i=-2L_i,\qquad \sigma=\delta+2.
\]
Then
\[
a_1+a_2+a_3=2\delta+2
\]
and, in (10),
\[
q_1=x-a_1,\qquad
q_2=y-a_2,\qquad
q_3=\delta-x-y-a_3.
\tag{15}
\]
The zero threshold for the last coordinate is
\[
\delta-a_3=a_1+a_2-\sigma.
\tag{16}
\]
Because affine cutting lines have area zero, each positive-area cell
in (1) contains a point at which all three signs are strict.
A strict point of \(Q_1\) satisfies
\[
y>a_2,\qquad
x+y<a_1+a_2-\sigma,
\]
and hence
\[
x<a_1-\sigma.
\tag{17}
\]
A strict point of \(Q_3\) satisfies
\[
x>a_1,\qquad y>a_2,
\]
and hence
\[
x+y>a_1+a_2.
\tag{18}
\]
Finally, a strict point of \(Q_{12}\) has
\[
y<a_2.
\tag{19}
\]
For the coordinate image \(C=F(A_2)\), define
\[
D(C)=
\max_C(x+y)-\min_C x-\min_C y.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (17)--(19) give the strict support-width bound
\[
D(C)>\sigma=\delta+2.
\tag{21}
\]

We use the following exact polygonal Minkowski identity:
\[
|T_\delta+C|_{\rm coord}
=\frac{\delta^2}{2}+|C|_{\rm coord}+\delta D(C).
\tag{22}
\]
Here is a direct derivation.  For convex polygons \(P,C\), merging
their counterclockwise edge lists and applying the shoelace formula
to \(P+tC\) gives
\[
|P+tC|
=|P|+
t\sum_{e\in E(P)}|e|h_C(n_e)+t^2|C|,
\tag{23}
\]
where \(n_e\) is the outward unit normal of \(e\) and
\(h_C\) is the support function.  Parallel edges follow by merging
equal directions, or by continuity.  At \(t=1\), the three edges of
\(T_\delta\) have outward normals
\[
(0,-1),\qquad
\frac{(1,1)}{\sqrt2},\qquad
(-1,0)
\]
and lengths
\[
\delta,\qquad\delta\sqrt2,\qquad\delta.
\]
Thus the middle coefficient in (23) is exactly
\[
\delta\left(
-\min_C y+\max_C(x+y)-\min_C x
\right)=\delta D(C),
\]
which proves (22).

Affine translations in (9) do not affect area, support widths, or
Minkowski-sum area.  Therefore
\[
|A_0+A_2|
=\gamma\,|F(A_0)+F(A_2)|_{\rm coord}.
\tag{24}
\]
By (11), \(T_\delta\subseteq F(A_0)\), while
\[
|C|_{\rm coord}=\frac m\gamma.
\]
Monotonicity of area, followed by (22), (21), and (14), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|B|
&=\frac14|A_0+A_2|\\
&\geq
\frac{\gamma}{4}|T_\delta+C|_{\rm coord}\\
&=
\frac{\gamma}{4}
\left(
\frac{\delta^2}{2}+\frac m\gamma+\delta D(C)
\right)\\
&>
\frac{\gamma}{4}
\left(
\frac{\delta^2}{2}+\frac m\gamma+\delta(\delta+2)
\right)\\
&=
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}+\frac{\gamma\delta}{2}\\
&=
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}+\frac{p_0}{\delta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
This proves (2).  Since \(B\subseteq K\), (3) follows.

The values in (4)--(5) satisfy (1), so (7) proves the claimed exact
nonrealizability.  For the neighborhood assertion, every one of the
six positive cell masses in (4)--(5) is greater than \(\eta\).
Moreover, if tildes denote perturbed values, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3\widetilde p_0+\widetilde m}{4}
-\widetilde\kappa
&>
\left(
\frac{3p_0+m}{4}-\kappa
\right)-2\eta\\
&=
\frac{101}{13800}-\frac{202}{55200}
=\frac{101}{27600}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus every datum in the stated neighborhood violates (3).

SageMath \(10.8\) independently audited the exact geometry over
\(\mathbb Q\).  With \(\delta=1\), \(a_1=a_2=a_3=4/3\),
\[
T_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\]
and
\[
C=
\left[-\frac{23}{3},\frac{13}{3}\right]
\times
\left[\frac{13}{12},\frac{19}{12}\right],
\]
exact rational halfspace clipping returned
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
J&1&2&3&12&13&23&123\\ \hline
|C\cap Q_J|&
\frac{47}{32}&0&\frac34&\frac{49}{32}&
\frac{25}{32}&\frac34&\frac{23}{32}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus this is a genuine \(q_2=0\) common-fan support model.
Exact Minkowski addition returned
\[
|C|=6,\qquad
D(C)=\frac{25}{2},\qquad
|T_1+C|=19
=\frac12+6+\frac{25}{2},
\]
verifying (22) on that rational model.  The same exact audit checked
(7), (8), and (26).  The universal inequality (2) is proved by
(9)--(25), not inferred from this computational check.
