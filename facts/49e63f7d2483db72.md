## Statement

Let \(B\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body,
let \(K\supseteq B\) be a compact convex body, and put \(D=K\setminus B\).
Let \(f,g\) be affine functions with linearly independent linear parts,
and let \(o\) be the unique point satisfying
\[
f(o)=g(o)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
e_f=|D\cap\{f\leq0\}|,\qquad
e_g=|D\cap\{g\leq0\}|.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose that \(B\) has the opposite-ray anchor property
\[
B\cap\{o+t(o-z):t\geq0\}\ne\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }z\in\{f>0,\ g<0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
e_g>e_f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad o\in K.
\tag{4}
\]
In fact, (3) is equivalent to
\[
o\in\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{z\})
\quad\text{for every }z\in\{f>0,\ g<0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
A convenient sufficient condition for (3) is the existence of
\[
b\in B\cap\{g=0,\ f<0\},
\qquad
c\in B\cap\{f>0,\ g>0\}.
\tag{6}
\]

The exact cap bonus forced by (4) is
\[
\Lambda_f(B,o)=
\left|
\bigl(\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{o\})\setminus B\bigr)
\cap\{f\leq0\}
\right|,
\tag{7}
\]
namely
\[
e_g>e_f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_f\geq\Lambda_f(B,o).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, suppose that an affine function \(\ell\), a number \(h\),
and points \(v,p\in B\) satisfy
\[
B\subseteq\{\ell\geq h\},\qquad
\ell(o)<h,\qquad
\ell(v)=\ell(p)=h,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
f(v)<0,\qquad f(p)=0.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
e_g>e_f
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e_f\geq|\operatorname{conv}\{o,v,p\}|>0.
\tag{11}
\]
This is the affine-invariant fixed third-support triangle.

There is also a quantitative pairwise support inequality which does not
assume (3).  Let
\[
\Psi(x)=(f(x),g(x))=(u,v),\qquad
J=|\det D\Psi|>0,
\tag{12}
\]
and write \(\widetilde B=\Psi(B)\), \(\widetilde K=\Psi(K)\).  Suppose
that for some \(a>0\) and \(b,s\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\widetilde B\subseteq\{(u,v):v\geq b+su\},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\{(u,b+su):-a\leq u\leq0\}\subseteq\widetilde B.
\tag{14}
\]
Put
\[
E_f=Je_f,\qquad E_g=Je_g,\qquad
c_0=b-\frac{2E_f}{a},\qquad
d_0=s-\frac{2E_f}{a^2}.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(d_0>0\), then
\[
\boxed{\quad
E_g-E_f\leq
\frac{(-c_0)_+^2}{2d_0}.
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
All quantities in (12)-(16) are coordinate areas and support data of
the affine image \(\Psi(B)\), so (16) is invariant under an invertible
affine change of the original plane.

The anchor in (3) cannot be omitted, even if both cutting lines cross
the interior of \(B\) and the third-support triangle in (9)-(10) is
present.  Indeed, put
\[
f(x,y)=x,\qquad g(x,y)=y,\qquad o=(0,0),
\tag{17}
\]
\[
r=\left(-\frac1{10},1\right),\qquad
q=\left(1,-\frac1{10}\right),\qquad
w=(1,1),\qquad
z=\left(2,-\frac12\right),
\tag{18}
\]
\[
B=\operatorname{conv}\{r,q,w\},\qquad
K=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{z\}).
\tag{19}
\]
Then both \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) cross \(\operatorname{int}B\), and
\[
B\subseteq\{x+y\geq9/10\}.
\tag{20}
\]
The exposed edge \([r,q]\) contains
\[
p=(0,9/10),
\qquad
|\operatorname{conv}\{o,r,p\}|=\frac9{200}.
\tag{21}
\]
Nevertheless
\[
e_f=0,\qquad e_g=\frac{13}{60}>e_f,\qquad o\notin K.
\tag{22}
\]
Here (3) fails, since
\[
B\cap\{(-2t,t/2):t\geq0\}=\varnothing.
\tag{23}
\]
The simpler anchor in (6) also fails because
\(B\cap\{y=0,\ x<0\}=\varnothing\).
For the profile (13)-(14), one has \(a=1/10\), \(b=9/10\),
\(s=-1\), and \(E_f=0\), hence \(d_0=-1\).  Thus this example also
shows why the positive-slope hypothesis \(d_0>0\) is needed in (16).

For the explicit midpoint body \(B\) in accepted fact
`50ba22f0dcebcebd`, the hypotheses (6) and (9)-(10) hold for

\ell(x,y)=3020y-19x,\qquad

\[

More strongly, write \(F_1,F_2,U\) as in equations (29), (30), and

## Proof

Cutting lines have planar area zero.  Decomposing \(D\) into the four
open sign chambers gives
\[
e_g-e_f
=|D\cap\{f>0,g<0\}|
 -|D\cap\{f<0,g>0\}|.
\tag{30}
\]
If \(e_g>e_f\), the first term on the right is positive, so there is
\(z\in D\cap\{f>0,g<0\}\).  By (3), there are \(q\in B\) and
\(t\geq0\) such that
\[
q=o+t(o-z).
\]
If \(t=0\), then \(o=q\in B\).  If \(t>0\), then
\[
o=\frac1{1+t}q+\frac{t}{1+t}z\in[q,z].
\tag{31}
\]
In either case \(o\in K\), proving (4).

Conversely, for a fixed \(z\), the equality
\(o\in\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{z\})\), together with convexity of
\(B\), is equivalent to the existence of \(q\in B\) on the ray
\(\{o+t(o-z):t\geq0\}\).  Applying this to every chamber point proves
the equivalence of (3) and (5).

To prove the sufficient condition (6), fix
\(z\in\{f>0,g<0\}\).  The segment \([z,c]\) crosses \(\{g=0\}\) at a
point \(w\) with \(f(w)>0\).  Both \(b,w\) lie on \(\{g=0\}\), with
opposite strict \(f\)-signs, so \(o\in[b,w]\).  Hence
\[
o\in\operatorname{conv}\{b,c,z\}
\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{z\}),
\]
which proves (3).

Once \(o\in K\),
\[
\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{o\})\subseteq K,
\]
so intersecting the difference from \(B\) with \(\{f\leq0\}\) proves
(8).  Under (9)-(10), the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{o,v,p\}\) lies in \(K\cap\{f\leq0\}\).  Its
edge \([v,p]\) lies on \(\{\ell=h\}\), while every other point of the
triangle outside this zero-area edge has \(\ell<h\), and hence lies
outside \(B\).  The triangle is nondegenerate because \(v\ne p\) and
\(\ell(o)<h\).  This proves (11).

We next prove (16).  Work in the \((u,v)\)-plane and let
\(\kappa(u)\) be the lower boundary of \(\widetilde K\).  On
\([-a,0]\), put
\[
r(u)=b+su-\kappa(u).
\tag{32}
\]
The support assumptions (13)-(14) imply that \(r\geq0\), and the
vertical strip between \(\kappa(u)\) and \(b+su\) belongs to
\(\widetilde K\setminus\widetilde B\).  Since \(\kappa\) is convex,
\(r\) is concave, and therefore
\[
\int_{-a}^0r(u)\,du\leq E_f.
\tag{33}
\]
If the projection of \(\widetilde K\) contains no positive \(u\),
then \(E_g-E_f\leq0\) by the chamber identity, and (16) is immediate.
Otherwise \(0\) is an interior point of that projection.
Concavity and nonnegativity give
\[
r(0)\leq\frac{2E_f}{a},
\qquad
r'_-(0)\leq
\frac{r(0)-r(-a)}a
\leq\frac{2E_f}{a^2}.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus
\[
\kappa(0)\geq c_0,\qquad
\kappa'_-(0)=s-r'_-(0)\geq d_0.
\tag{35}
\]
The left derivative of a convex function is a subgradient at an
interior point of its domain.  Hence, for every \(u\geq0\) in the
projection of \(\widetilde K\),
\[
\kappa(u)\geq c_0+d_0u.
\tag{36}
\]
Using (30) in \((u,v)\)-coordinates and then (36),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_g-E_f
&\leq
\left|
(\widetilde K\setminus\widetilde B)
\cap\{u>0,v<0\}
\right|\\
&\leq
\int_{\operatorname{proj}_u(\widetilde K)\cap(0,\infty)}
(-\kappa(u))_+\,du\\
&\leq
\int_0^\infty(-c_0-d_0u)_+\,du
=\frac{(-c_0)_+^2}{2d_0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
This proves (16).  The change-of-variables identity
\(|\Psi(E)|=J|E|\) proves the asserted affine invariance.

For the rational sharpness example, the vertices
\(r,q,z,w\) occur counterclockwise, and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{r,q,z,w\},\qquad
\overline{K\setminus B}=\operatorname{conv}\{q,z,w\}
\tag{38}
\]
because the two sets differ only on their shared edge.  This extension
triangle lies in
\(\{x\geq1\}\), so \(e_f=0\).  Its full area is \(11/20\).  Its part
above \(y=0\) is the triangle with vertices
\[
w,\qquad (1,0),\qquad (5/3,0),
\]
of area \(1/3\).  Therefore
\[
e_g=\frac{11}{20}-\frac13=\frac{13}{60}.
\tag{39}
\]
The line \(x=0\) meets \(\operatorname{int}B\) for
\(9/10<y<1\), and \(y=0\) meets it for \(9/10<x<1\).
The edge \([r,q]\) is \(x+y=9/10\), while
\(z_x+z_y=3/2\), so all of \(K\) satisfies
\(x+y\geq9/10\).  Thus \(o\notin K\).  Moreover, the opposite ray
to \(z\) is \(\{(-2t,t/2):t\geq0\}\); its nonzero points have
coordinate sum \(-3t/2<0\), so it is disjoint from \(B\).
This proves (20)-(23).
This also shows that the one-direction strict support-clearance premise
of accepted fact `cf1a8ff16a0da82a` is absent for both cuts.
