## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),\qquad
m=\left(\frac38,\frac38\right),
\]
and define the rational nondegenerate triangles
\[
A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_0=m+\frac15(\Delta-g),\qquad
A_2=m+\frac65(\Delta-g).
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac{37}{120},\frac{37}{120}\right),
\left(\frac{61}{120},\frac{37}{120}\right),
\left(\frac{37}{120},\frac{61}{120}\right)\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac1{40},-\frac1{40}\right),
\left(\frac{47}{40},-\frac1{40}\right),
\left(-\frac1{40},\frac{47}{40}\right)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
These triangles satisfy the strict compatibility
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subset\operatorname{int}A_1.
\tag{4}
\]
Let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\bigr)
\]
and
\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2).
\]
If \(g_i\) is the centroid of \(A_i\), then for every \(s\in[0,1]\),
\[
z_s=(1-s)\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}+s g_1
\]
satisfies
\[
h_S((1,z_s))<\frac{2}{9}\bigl(|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
More precisely, with the exact sum profile of accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240`, the \(q=(1,1)\) directional inequality at the target
holds exactly for
\[
s\in\left[0,\,9-12\sqrt{\frac{23}{45}}\right],
\tag{6}
\]
whereas the \(-q\) directional inequality holds exactly for
\[
s\in\left[9-12\sqrt{\frac{22}{45}},\,1\right].
\tag{7}
\]

## Proof

First verify compatibility and the locked three-slice setting.  For every
convex set \(K\) and positive \(a,b\), one has \(aK+bK=(a+b)K\).
Therefore (1) gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=m+\frac7{10}(\Delta-g).
\tag{8}
\]
The three vertices of (8) are
\[
\left(\frac{17}{120},\frac{17}{120}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{101}{120},\frac{17}{120}\right),\qquad
\left(\frac{17}{120},\frac{101}{120}\right).
\]
Every coordinate is positive, and the sum of the coordinates at either of
the last two vertices is \(59/60<1\).  This proves the strict containment
(4).

The polytope \(C\) lies between heights \(0\) and \(2\), and its sections at
heights \(0\) and \(2\) are \(A_0\) and \(A_2\).  A point of its section at
height \(1\) has planar component
\[
\tau x_0+(1-2\tau)x_1+\tau x_2
=(1-2\tau)x_1+2\tau\frac{x_0+x_2}{2},
\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq\frac12,
\]
with \(x_i\in A_i\).  By (4), this belongs to \(A_1\).  Conversely the
whole of \(A_1\) is present at height \(1\).  Hence
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\tag{9}
\]

The three areas and the target are
\[
a_0=\frac1{50},\qquad a_1=\frac12,\qquad
a_2=\frac{18}{25},\qquad
T=\frac{31}{25},\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{62}{225}.
\tag{10}
\]
The centroids of \(A_0,A_2\) are both \(m\), while the centroid of \(A_1\)
is \(g\).  If \(u(x,y)=x+y\), then
\[
r_s:=u(z_s)=\frac34-\frac{s}{12},
\qquad \frac23\leq r_s\leq\frac34.
\tag{11}
\]
We use \(q=(1,1)\) as a nonunit representative of its direction; positive
normalization rescales all thresholds and does not change any cap or
halfspace.

We record the exact cap formula used twice.  For
\[
B_\ell=m+\ell(\Delta-g),
\]
the \(u\)-support interval is
\[
\left[\frac34-\frac{2\ell}{3},\,
      \frac34+\frac{\ell}{3}\right].
\]
If \(c\) lies in this interval, direct homothety from
\(\Delta\cap\{x+y\leq v\}\), whose area is \(v^2/2\), gives
\[
\left|B_\ell\cap\{u\leq c\}\right|
=\frac12\left(c-\frac34+\frac{2\ell}{3}\right)^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Below the interval the cap has area \(0\), and above it the cap has the full
area \(\ell^2/2\).  Consequently the upper cap inside the support interval
has area
\[
\left|B_\ell\cap\{u\geq c\}\right|
=\frac{\ell^2}{2}
-\frac12\left(c-\frac34+\frac{2\ell}{3}\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
All support-line boundary pieces have planar area zero, so the endpoint
equalities in (12)-(13) are exact.

For direction \(q\), the support intervals of \(A_0,A_2\) are
\[
\left[\frac{37}{60},\frac{49}{60}\right],
\qquad
\left[-\frac1{20},\frac{23}{20}\right].
\tag{14}
\]
For \(2/3\leq r\leq3/4\), put
\[
I_q(r)=
\inf_{c_0+c_2=2r}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{u\leq c_0\}|
+|A_2\cap\{u\leq c_2\}|
\right).
\tag{15}
\]
We claim
\[
I_q(r)=\frac1{50}.
\tag{16}
\]
For the upper bound, take
\[
c_2=-\frac1{20},\qquad c_0=2r+\frac1{20}.
\]
Then the \(A_2\)-cap has area zero, while
\(c_0\geq83/60>49/60\), so the \(A_0\)-cap is full.

For the reverse inequality, consider any \(c_0+c_2=2r\).  If
\(c_0\geq49/60\), the \(A_0\)-cap already has area \(1/50\).  If
\(c_0\leq37/60\), then
\[
c_2\geq\frac43-\frac{37}{60}=\frac{43}{60}.
\]
If this threshold is still inside the \(A_2\)-support, (12) gives its cap
area at least
\[
\frac12\left(\frac{43}{60}+\frac1{20}\right)^2
=\frac{529}{1800}>\frac1{50};
\]
if it is beyond the support, the cap is full and the same conclusion is
immediate.  Finally suppose
\[
c_0=\frac{37}{60}+x,\qquad 0<x<\frac15.
\]
Then
\[
c_2+\frac1{20}
=2r-\frac{17}{30}-x
>\frac{17}{30}.
\]
Formula (12), or the full-cap alternative, makes the \(A_2\)-contribution
strictly larger than
\[
\frac12\left(\frac{17}{30}\right)^2
=\frac{289}{1800}>\frac1{50}.
\]
These cases prove (16), with the full endpoint allocation retained.

The \(A_1\)-cap in direction \(q\) has area \(r^2/2\).  Thus the exact sum
profile from accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240`, specialized using (16), is
\[
\Phi_q(r)=\frac{r^2}{2}+\frac1{50}
\qquad\left(\frac23\leq r\leq\frac34\right).
\tag{17}
\]
By (10),
\[
\Phi_q(r)\geq t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r^2\geq\frac{23}{45}.
\tag{18}
\]
Substitution of (11) into (18) gives exactly (6).

Now use the opposite direction \(-q\).  At the point whose \(q\)-coordinate
is \(r\), its middle cap is the upper cap
\[
|A_1\cap\{u\geq r\}|=\frac{1-r^2}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]
Write the two endpoint thresholds in \(u\)-coordinates as \(k_0,k_2\).
The opposite-direction allocation condition is \(k_0+k_2=2r\), so its
endpoint infimum is
\[
J(r)=
\inf_{k_0+k_2=2r}
\left(
|A_0\cap\{u\geq k_0\}|
+|A_2\cap\{u\geq k_2\}|
\right).
\tag{20}
\]
Again, throughout \(2/3\leq r\leq3/4\),
\[
J(r)=\frac1{50}.
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, take \(k_2=23/20\) and \(k_0=2r-23/20\).  Then the \(A_2\)-cap
has area zero, while
\[
k_0\leq\frac7{20}<\frac{37}{60},
\]
so the \(A_0\)-cap is full.  Conversely, if
\(k_0\leq37/60\), that \(A_0\)-cap is already full.  If
\(k_0>37/60\), then
\[
k_2=2r-k_0<\frac32-\frac{37}{60}=\frac{53}{60}.
\]
If \(k_2\leq-1/20\), the \(A_2\)-upper cap is full.  Otherwise (13) gives
\[
|A_2\cap\{u\geq k_2\}|
>
\frac{18}{25}
-\frac12\left(\frac{53}{60}+\frac1{20}\right)^2
=\frac{64}{225}
>\frac1{50}.
\]
This proves (21) for every allocation, not merely for a support scalar
bound.

Combining (19) and (21), the exact opposite profile is
\[
\Phi_{-q}(-r)=\frac{1-r^2}{2}+\frac1{50}
\qquad\left(\frac23\leq r\leq\frac34\right).
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (10) and (22) give
\[
\Phi_{-q}(-r)\geq t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r^2\leq\frac{22}{45}.
\tag{23}
\]
Substitution of (11) into (23) gives exactly (7).

Both interval endpoints lie strictly between \(0\) and \(1\): this follows
from
\[
\frac49<\frac{22}{45}<\frac{23}{45}<\frac9{16}.
\tag{24}
\]
Moreover,
\[
9-12\sqrt{\frac{23}{45}}
<
9-12\sqrt{\frac{22}{45}},
\tag{25}
\]
so (6) and (7) are disjoint.  For every \(s\in[0,1]\), at least one of the
two exact directional profiles is therefore strictly below \(t\).
Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` identifies the mixed depth with the
infimum of these sum profiles over all directions.  Hence (5) follows.

Finally, \(a_2=18/25>T/2=31/50\).  Thus the counterexample is intentionally
only to the unrestricted middle-slice centroid-segment assertion.  For
example, the centroid of \(A_2\) has planar triangle depth
\(4a_2/9=8/25>62/225\); the endpoint identity in accepted fact
`949ce2fdf2e1e240` therefore shows that the locked existence conclusion
itself holds for this triple.
