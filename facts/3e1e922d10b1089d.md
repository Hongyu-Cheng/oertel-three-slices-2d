## Statement

Let three distinct lines through \(0\) divide \(\mathbb R^2\) into six open sectors, and let \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) be alternate sectors. Let
\[
K_i\subseteq\overline{Q_i}
\]
be compact convex sets containing \(0\), put \(a_i=|K_i|\), and set
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3).
\]
Then
\[
|H|\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2. \tag{1}
\]
The constant is sharp for every fixed three-line fan.

If at least one \(a_i\) is zero, equality in (1) holds exactly when
\[
a_1=a_2=a_3=0,\qquad |H|=0.
\]
If all \(a_i>0\), equality implies
\[
a_1=a_2=a_3
\]
and the given triple is an area-minimizing triple for these prescribed areas. Conversely, every minimizing triple with three equal positive prescribed areas is an equality case.

For a minimizing triangle, equality holds exactly when, after normalization to area \(1\), the parameters of accepted fact `55870afe6d5fc27f` satisfy
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,
\]
and its three selected-sector areas are all \(2/9\). No stronger geometric classification of arbitrary positive-area minimizing triples is asserted.

## Proof

If at least one \(a_i\) is zero, accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9` proves (1) directly and proves that it is strict unless all three areas and the hull area are zero. Hence assume
\[
a_1,a_2,a_3>0.
\]

Let \(M(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) be the minimum hull area from accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9`. That fact supplies a minimizing triangle \(T\) with one vertex in each \(\overline{Q_i}\), containing \(0\), and satisfying
\[
|T\cap Q_i|=a_i,\qquad |T|=M(a_1,a_2,a_3). \tag{2}
\]
Write \(m=|T|>0\) and dilate \(T\) about \(0\) by \(m^{-1/2}\). The fan sectors are cones, so the normalized triangle
\[
\widehat T=m^{-1/2}T
\]
still has one vertex in each selected-sector closure and satisfies
\[
|\widehat T|=1,\qquad
A_i:=|\widehat T\cap Q_i|=\frac{a_i}{m}. \tag{3}
\]

Accepted fact `55870afe6d5fc27f` gives positive parameters
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\rho,\sigma,\tau
\]
for \(\widehat T\), satisfying all endpoint and side-order hypotheses of accepted fact `81e7a2c179deb1c8`, and identifies its three \(A_i\) and complementary area
\[
b=1-A_1-A_2-A_3
\]
with the formulas in that theorem. Apply accepted fact `81e7a2c179deb1c8` with \(d_i=A_i\). It gives
\[
b\ge
\sqrt{A_1A_2}+\sqrt{A_2A_3}+\sqrt{A_3A_1}
-\frac12(A_1+A_2+A_3).
\]
Using \(b=1-\sum_iA_i\), this rearranges to
\[
1\ge
\frac12(A_1+A_2+A_3)
+\sqrt{A_1A_2}+\sqrt{A_2A_3}+\sqrt{A_3A_1}
=\frac12\left(\sqrt{A_1}+\sqrt{A_2}+\sqrt{A_3}\right)^2.
\]
Substitution from (3) yields
\[
m\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2. \tag{4}
\]
The original triple is feasible in the minimization defining \(M\), so
\[
|H|\ge M(a_1,a_2,a_3)=m.
\]
Together with (4), this proves (1).

We next establish sharpness for an arbitrary fixed fan. Label its rays cyclically so that
\[
Q_1=\operatorname{cone}(r_1,r_2),\quad
Q_2=\operatorname{cone}(r_3,r_4),\quad
Q_3=\operatorname{cone}(r_5,r_6),\quad
r_{j+3}=-r_j.
\]
Choose representatives \(u\in r_1\) and \(w\in r_3\) so that \(u+w\in r_2\); this is possible because \(r_2\) lies strictly inside \(\operatorname{cone}(r_1,r_3)\). In the basis \(u,w\), the six rays are represented by
\[
(1,0),\ (1,1),\ (0,1),\ (-1,0),\ (-1,-1),\ (0,-1).
\]
Let
\[
P_1=(2,1),\qquad P_2=(-1,1),\qquad P_3=(-1,-2)
\]
in this basis, and put \(T_0=\operatorname{conv}\{P_1,P_2,P_3\}\). Each \(P_i\) lies in \(Q_i\), and
\[
P_1+P_2+P_3=0,
\]
so \(0\) is the centroid and lies in the interior of \(T_0\).

The rays \(r_2,r_3\) meet \(P_1P_2\) at its \(1/3\) and \(2/3\) points; \(r_4,r_5\) do the same on \(P_2P_3\); and \(r_6,r_1\) do the same on \(P_3P_1\). Therefore the coordinates in accepted fact `55870afe6d5fc27f` are
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1.
\]
Its area formulas give
\[
|T_0\cap Q_1|=|T_0\cap Q_2|=|T_0\cap Q_3|
=\frac{2}{9}|T_0|.
\]
Taking \(K_i=T_0\cap\overline{Q_i}\), their convex hull is \(T_0\), because it contains the three vertices. Hence equality holds in (1). A common dilation realizes every positive common value of the three \(a_i\), proving sharpness for the fixed fan.

It remains to record exactly what the accepted equality statements imply. If \(T\) is a minimizing triangle and equality holds, equality must hold in the application of accepted fact `81e7a2c179deb1c8`. Its equality classification gives
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,\qquad
A_1=A_2=A_3=\frac29. \tag{5}
\]
Conversely, (5) is an equality case by the same accepted theorem. This completely classifies equality among minimizing triangles.

Now suppose the original positive-area triple is an equality case. Both inequalities in
\[
|H|\ge M(a_1,a_2,a_3)\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2
\]
must be equalities. Thus the given triple is minimizing, while its minimizing triangle satisfies (5). By (3),
\[
\frac{a_1}{m}=\frac{a_2}{m}=\frac{a_3}{m}=\frac29,
\]
so \(a_1=a_2=a_3\).

Conversely, let \(a_1=a_2=a_3=a>0\) and suppose the given triple is minimizing. The sharpness construction, scaled so that each selected area is \(a\), shows
\[
M(a,a,a)\le\frac92a.
\]
Inequality (1), already proved, gives the reverse inequality. Therefore
\[
|H|=M(a,a,a)=\frac92a
=\frac12(3\sqrt a)^2,
\]
so the triple is an equality case. The three accepted facts establish existence and calibration of a minimizing triangle, but they do not prove that every arbitrary minimizing hull is that triangle. The remaining positive-area equality issue is precisely the geometric classification of all minimizing triples with equal prescribed areas.
