## Statement

Let \(L>0\), and let \(h\) be a nonnegative concave Lebesgue probability density on \([0,L]\) satisfying
\[
\int_0^L xh(x)\,dx=1.
\]
Since changing endpoint values does not change the density, its tails, or its moments, set
\[
h(0):=\lim_{x\downarrow0}h(x),\qquad h(L):=\lim_{x\uparrow L}h(x).
\]
These limits are finite, and this gives the canonical concave representative on \([0,L]\).

For \(0\leq\tau\leq L\), define the normalized tent with common support \([0,L]\) and peak \(\tau\) by
\[
h_{L,\tau}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2(L-x)}{L^2},&\tau=0,\ 0\leq x\leq L,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2x}{L^2},&\tau=L,\ 0\leq x\leq L,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2x}{L\tau},&0<\tau<L,\ 0\leq x\leq\tau,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2(L-x)}{L(L-\tau)},&0<\tau<L,\ \tau\leq x\leq L,\\[4pt]
0,&x\notin[0,L].
\end{cases}
\]
There is a unique Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,L]\) such that
\[
h(x)=\int_{[0,L]}h_{L,\tau}(x)\,\nu(d\tau),\qquad 0\leq x\leq L.
\]
If \(\kappa=-D^2h\) is the nonnegative distributional curvature measure on \((0,L)\), then
\[
\nu(\{0\})=\frac{Lh(0)}2,\qquad
\nu(\{L\})=\frac{Lh(L)}2,\qquad
\nu(d\tau)=\frac{\tau(L-\tau)}2\,\kappa(d\tau)\quad(0<\tau<L).
\]
Thus positive endpoint-face values are represented exactly by atoms at the corresponding endpoint peaks.

Every tent satisfies
\[
\int_0^L h_{L,\tau}(x)\,dx=1,\qquad
\int_0^L xh_{L,\tau}(x)\,dx=\frac{L+\tau}{3}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\int_{[0,L]}\tau\,\nu(d\tau)=3-L,
\qquad
\frac32\leq L\leq3.
\]
At the endpoint lengths, \(L=3\) forces \(\nu=\delta_0\) and \(h=h_{3,0}\), while \(L=3/2\) forces \(\nu=\delta_L\) and \(h=h_{3/2,L}\).

Conversely, for any \(3/2\leq L\leq3\) and any probability measure \(\nu\) on \([0,L]\) with
\[
\int\tau\,\nu(d\tau)=3-L,
\]
the displayed mixture is a nonnegative concave probability density on \([0,L]\) with mean \(1\).

Extend each density by zero outside \([0,L]\), and write
\[
G_h(t):=\int_t^\infty h(x)\,dx.
\]
For a tent, put \(Q_{L,\tau}(t):=G_{h_{L,\tau}}(t)\). Explicitly,
\[
Q_{L,\tau}(t)=
\begin{cases}
1,&t\leq0,\\[3pt]
1-\dfrac{t^2}{L\tau},&0<t\leq\tau\leq L,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{(L-t)^2}{L(L-\tau)},&0\leq\tau\leq t<L,\\[7pt]
0,&t\geq L.
\end{cases}
\]
The two middle expressions agree when \(t=\tau\), and their stated domains include the endpoint peaks without a zero denominator.

For every \(n\geq1\), every \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\in\mathbb R\), and every \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\Phi(\tau):=\sum_{j=1}^n c_jQ_{L,\tau}(t_j),\qquad m:=3-L.
\]
Then
\[
\min_h\sum_{j=1}^n c_jG_h(t_j)
=
\underline{\operatorname{co}}_{[0,L]}\Phi(m),
\]
where the minimum is over all nonnegative concave probability densities on \([0,L]\) with mean \(1\), and \(\underline{\operatorname{co}}_{[0,L]}\Phi\) is the greatest convex function on \([0,L]\) lying below \(\Phi\). Equivalently,
\[
\underline{\operatorname{co}}_{[0,L]}\Phi(m)
=
\min_{\substack{0\leq a\leq m\leq b\leq L}}
\left[
\frac{b-m}{b-a}\Phi(a)
+
\frac{m-a}{b-a}\Phi(b)
\right],
\]
where \(a=b=m\) is allowed and its value is defined to be \(\Phi(m)\). Hence an optimizer always exists and is a mixture of at most two common-support tents.

## Proof

A nonnegative integrable concave function on \((0,L)\) has finite one-sided endpoint limits. Indeed, concave functions have extended one-sided limits, secant-slope inequalities through two fixed interior points bound the function above near either endpoint, and nonnegativity rules out a limit of \(-\infty\). Resetting the two point values to these limits preserves concavity and changes no integral.

Let
\[
\kappa:=-D^2h
\]
on \((0,L)\). Concavity makes \(\kappa\) a nonnegative Radon measure. Define
\[
K(x,\tau):=\frac{\min\{x,\tau\}\bigl(L-\max\{x,\tau\}\bigr)}{L}.
\]
We first prove the Green representation
\[
h(x)=\frac{L-x}{L}h(0)+\frac{x}{L}h(L)
+\int_{(0,L)}K(x,\tau)\,\kappa(d\tau).
\]
Fix \(0<a<x<b<L\), and set
\[
K_{a,b}(x,\tau)
=
\frac{(\min\{x,\tau\}-a)(b-\max\{x,\tau\})}{b-a}.
\]
As a function of \(x\), \(K_{a,b}(x,\tau)\) vanishes at \(a,b\) and has distributional second derivative \(-\delta_\tau\). Therefore
\[
\int_{(a,b)}K_{a,b}(x,\tau)\,\kappa(d\tau)
\]
has second derivative \(-\kappa=D^2h\) on \((a,b)\) and vanishes at both endpoints. Their difference is affine, so
\[
h(x)=
\frac{b-x}{b-a}h(a)+\frac{x-a}{b-a}h(b)
+\int_{(a,b)}K_{a,b}(x,\tau)\,\kappa(d\tau).
\]
Choose nested intervals with \(a\downarrow0\) and \(b\uparrow L\). Extended by zero outside \((a,b)\), the nonnegative kernels \(K_{a,b}(x,\tau)\) increase pointwise to \(K(x,\tau)\). The affine term converges to the chord through the canonical endpoint values. Monotone convergence proves the claimed Green representation, including possible infinite total curvature near an endpoint.

For \(0<\tau<L\),
\[
h_{L,\tau}(x)=\frac{2K(x,\tau)}{\tau(L-\tau)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{Lh(0)}2h_{L,0}(x)=\frac{L-x}{L}h(0),\qquad
\frac{Lh(L)}2h_{L,L}(x)=\frac{x}{L}h(L).
\]
Substitution in the Green representation gives
\[
h(x)=\int_{[0,L]}h_{L,\tau}(x)\,\nu(d\tau)
\]
for the measure \(\nu\) stated above.

The interior part of \(\nu\) is finite. Indeed, Tonelli's theorem and a direct integration give
\[
\int_0^L K(x,\tau)\,dx=\frac{\tau(L-\tau)}2,
\]
so integrating the Green representation yields
\[
1=\int_0^Lh(x)\,dx
=
\frac{Lh(0)}2+\frac{Lh(L)}2
+\int_{(0,L)}\frac{\tau(L-\tau)}2\,\kappa(d\tau)
=\nu([0,L]).
\]
Thus \(\nu\) is a probability measure. This also verifies all normalization constants.

Uniqueness follows directly from endpoint values and curvature. In any tent mixture, dominated convergence, using \(0\leq h_{L,\tau}\leq2/L\), gives
\[
h(0)=\frac2L\nu(\{0\}),\qquad
h(L)=\frac2L\nu(\{L\}).
\]
On \((0,L)\),
\[
-D^2h_{L,\tau}=\frac{2}{\tau(L-\tau)}\delta_\tau
\]
for \(0<\tau<L\), while the endpoint tents are affine there. Hence \(-D^2h\) recovers
\[
\nu(d\tau)=\frac{\tau(L-\tau)}2(-D^2h)(d\tau)
\]
on the interior. No two distinct mixing measures can represent the same canonical density.

Direct integration of the two affine pieces of a tent gives
\[
\int_0^Lh_{L,\tau}=1,\qquad
\int_0^Lxh_{L,\tau}(x)\,dx=\frac{L+\tau}{3},
\]
also for \(\tau=0,L\). Applying Tonelli to the mixture and using the assumed mean gives
\[
1=\frac{L+\int\tau\,\nu(d\tau)}3,
\]
so \(\int\tau\,d\nu=3-L\). Since \(0\leq\tau\leq L\),
\[
0\leq3-L\leq L,
\]
which is equivalent to \(3/2\leq L\leq3\). If \(3-L=0\), nonnegativity of \(\tau\) forces \(\nu=\delta_0\). If \(3-L=L\), the bound \(\tau\leq L\) forces \(\nu=\delta_L\).

Conversely, each \(h_{L,\tau}\) is nonnegative and concave on \([0,L]\), and it is bounded by \(2/L\). Therefore the pointwise integral against any probability measure \(\nu\) is finite, nonnegative, and concave. Tonelli gives total mass \(1\) and mean
\[
\frac{L+\int\tau\,d\nu}{3}.
\]
The barycenter condition makes this mean \(1\). Every tent is positive on \((0,L)\), so the mixture has common support \([0,L]\). Dominated convergence at the endpoints supplies its canonical endpoint values.

Integrating the explicit tent pieces from \(t\) to \(L\) gives the stated formula for \(Q_{L,\tau}(t)\). Consequently every mixture satisfies
\[
G_h(t)=\int_{[0,L]}Q_{L,\tau}(t)\,\nu(d\tau),
\]
and hence
\[
\sum_{j=1}^nc_jG_h(t_j)=\int_{[0,L]}\Phi(\tau)\,\nu(d\tau).
\]
The displayed endpoint-safe formula shows that \(\Phi\) is continuous on the compact interval \([0,L]\).

By the representation and its converse, minimizing over admissible \(h\) is exactly the same as minimizing \(\int\Phi\,d\nu\) over probability measures on \([0,L]\) with barycenter \(m\). The pair
\[
\left(\int\tau\,d\nu,\int\Phi(\tau)\,d\nu\right)
\]
lies in the compact convex hull of the graph
\[
\{(\tau,\Phi(\tau)):0\leq\tau\leq L\},
\]
and every finite convex combination of graph points is produced by the corresponding finitely supported \(\nu\). The lowest ordinate of this convex hull above \(m\) is precisely the greatest convex minorant \(\underline{\operatorname{co}}_{[0,L]}\Phi(m)\). A lower boundary point of a compact planar convex hull is either a graph point or lies on a segment joining two graph points. Thus there are \(0\leq a\leq m\leq b\leq L\) for which the lower boundary value is
\[
\frac{b-m}{b-a}\Phi(a)+\frac{m-a}{b-a}\Phi(b),
\]
with the one-point convention when \(a=b=m\). The probability measure with these two weights has barycenter \(m\), so its corresponding two-tent mixture attains the lower-envelope value. This proves both the exact formula and attainment.
