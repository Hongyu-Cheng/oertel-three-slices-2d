## Statement

Let three distinct lines through \(0\) divide \(\mathbb R^2\) into six open
sectors, and let \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) be alternate sectors. Let
\(K_i\subseteq\overline{Q_i}\) be compact convex sets containing \(0\), set
\[
a_i=|K_i|,\qquad
H=\operatorname{conv}(K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3),
\]
and, for \(i<j\), set
\[
H_{ij}=\operatorname{conv}(K_i\cup K_j).
\]
Then
\[
|H|\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2. \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, one always has the exact identity
\[
\sum_{i<j}|H_{ij}|=|H|+a_1+a_2+a_3. \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, (1) is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{i<j}|H_{ij}|
\ge
\frac32(a_1+a_2+a_3)
+\sqrt{a_1a_2}+\sqrt{a_2a_3}+\sqrt{a_3a_1}. \tag{3}
\]

If at least one \(a_i\) is zero, equality in (1), equivalently in (3), holds
if and only if all \(a_i=0\) and \(|H|=0\). If all \(a_i>0\), equality holds
if and only if \(H\) is a nondegenerate triangle whose centroid is \(0\), the
three fan lines are parallel to its three sides, and, after cyclic labeling,
\[
K_i=H\cap\overline{Q_i}\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
In this case
\[
a_1=a_2=a_3=\frac{2|H|}{9}.
\]
Such triangles exist for every choice of the three fan lines, so the constant
\(1/2\) is sharp.

## Proof

We first prove the pair-hull identity (2), including lower-dimensional pieces.
We use the following elementary convex-position property of alternate
sectors: if \(x_i\in\overline{Q_i}\setminus\{0\}\), then
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}. \tag{4}
\]
For points in the open sectors, this is the positive-determinant calculation
in accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9`. Points on fan rays follow by taking
limits from the open sectors. In particular, no open halfplane bounded by a
line through \(0\) can contain one nonzero point from each
\(\overline{Q_i}\).

Every point \(x\in H\) can be written, after grouping a convex combination
according to the three convex sets, as
\[
x=\lambda_1x_1+\lambda_2x_2+\lambda_3x_3,
\qquad x_i\in K_i,\quad
\lambda_i\ge0,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i=1.
\]
If a coefficient vanishes or one \(x_i\) is \(0\), then \(x\) belongs to one
of the \(H_{ij}\). Otherwise, (4) implies that the triangle
\(\operatorname{conv}\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}\) is the union of the three triangles
\(\operatorname{conv}\{0,x_i,x_j\}\). Each of the latter is contained in
\(H_{ij}\). Hence
\[
H=H_{12}\cup H_{23}\cup H_{31}. \tag{5}
\]

We next show
\[
H_{12}\cap H_{13}=K_1, \tag{6}
\]
with the cyclic analogues. The closed halfplane spanned by
\(Q_1\), the intervening sector between \(Q_1,Q_2\), and \(Q_2\) contains
\(H_{12}\). The analogous halfplane for \(Q_1,Q_3\) contains \(H_{13}\).
Their intersection is \(\overline{Q_1}\), so every point in the left side of
(6) belongs to \(\overline{Q_1}\).

Suppose that \(x\in H_{12}\cap H_{13}\) but \(x\notin K_1\). Strict
separation from the compact convex set \(K_1\) gives a linear functional
\(\ell\) such that
\[
\ell(x)>h_{K_1}(\ell)\ge0,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(0\in K_1\). Membership in \(H_{12}\) forces
\(h_{K_2}(\ell)>\!0\), and membership in \(H_{13}\) forces
\(h_{K_3}(\ell)>\!0\). Thus there are nonzero
\(y_i\in\overline{Q_i}\), namely \(y_1=x\), \(y_2\in K_2\), and
\(y_3\in K_3\), with \(\ell(y_i)>0\) for every \(i\). This contradicts (4).
Therefore (6) holds. The three pairwise intersections of the sets in (5)
are consequently \(K_1,K_2,K_3\), while their triple intersection is
\(\{0\}\), because alternate closed sectors meet pairwise only at \(0\).
Inclusion-exclusion for planar area now gives (2). This proof also covers
fan-ray contacts and zero-area \(K_i\).

We prove (1). If at least one \(a_i\) vanishes, the zero-area branch of
accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9` gives (1) directly and states that it is
strict unless all three areas and the hull area are zero.

Assume henceforth that \(a_i>0\) for all \(i\). Accepted fact
`e16916ffe774b4a9` gives a minimizing triangle \(T\), with
\(0\in\operatorname{int}T\), one vertex in each
\(\overline{Q_i}\), and
\[
|T\cap Q_i|=a_i,\qquad
|T|\le |H|. \tag{7}
\]
Put \(s=|T|>0\) and apply the homothety \(x\mapsto s^{-1/2}x\). The resulting
triangle \(\widehat T\) has area \(1\), the same fan lines and sector
assignments, and
\[
A_i:=|\widehat T\cap Q_i|=\frac{a_i}{s},\qquad
b:=1-A_1-A_2-A_3. \tag{8}
\]
Accepted fact `55870afe6d5fc27f` supplies finite positive parameters
\(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\rho,\sigma,\tau\) satisfying every endpoint and
side-order constraint, with its four formulas equal exactly to the quantities
in (8). This remains true when a triangle vertex lies on a fan ray.

Apply accepted fact `81e7a2c179deb1c8` with \(d_i=A_i\). It gives
\[
b\ge
\sqrt{A_1A_2}+\sqrt{A_2A_3}+\sqrt{A_3A_1}
-\frac12(A_1+A_2+A_3).
\]
Using \(b=1-\sum_iA_i\) and rearranging,
\[
1\ge
\frac12\sum_iA_i+\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{A_iA_j}
=\frac12\left(\sqrt{A_1}+\sqrt{A_2}+\sqrt{A_3}\right)^2. \tag{9}
\]
Multiplication by \(s\), followed by (7) and (8), yields
\[
|H|\ge s\ge
\frac12\left(\sqrt{a_1}+\sqrt{a_2}+\sqrt{a_3}\right)^2,
\]
which proves (1). Substituting (2) into (1) gives (3), and conversely, so the
two inequalities are genuinely equivalent rather than merely comparable.

It remains to verify equality and sharpness. The zero-area equality statement
was already supplied by accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9`. Suppose all
\(a_i>0\) and equality holds in (1). Then every inequality above is an
equality. In particular, the original triple is minimizing. The saturation
part of accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9` gives
\[
K_i=H\cap\overline{Q_i}. \tag{10}
\]
Its area-preserving compression keeps the six fan-ray contacts and the three
bridge segments fixed and produces a minimizing triangle \(T\) of the same
area.

Equality in (9), by accepted fact `81e7a2c179deb1c8`, is possible exactly when
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,\qquad
A_1=A_2=A_3=\frac29. \tag{11}
\]
Accepted fact `55870afe6d5fc27f` identifies \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) as the
barycentric coordinates of \(0\), so (11) says that \(0\) is the centroid of
\(T\). Its endpoint formulas put the two fan-ray contacts on every side at
the one-third and two-thirds points. Hence each fan line is parallel to the
opposite triangle side. More explicitly, if the vertices are
\(P_1,P_2,P_3\), the two contacts of one fan line are
\[
\frac23P_1+\frac13P_3
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac23P_2+\frac13P_3,
\]
whose difference is \(\frac23(P_2-P_1)\); the other two lines are cyclic.
Scaling (11) back by \(s\) gives \(a_i=2s/9\).

The compression in accepted fact `e16916ffe774b4a9` did not move the bridge
segments. Their three support lines are now the three side lines of \(T\).
The original hull \(H\) lies in all three corresponding support halfplanes,
so \(H\subseteq T\). Compression preserved total area, hence
\(|H|=|T|\). A proper closed convex subset of a nondegenerate triangle has
strictly smaller area, so \(H=T\). Together with (10), this proves necessity
of the stated geometric equality conditions.

Conversely, let \(H\) be a triangle with centroid \(0\), take the three fan
lines through \(0\) parallel to its sides, and put
\(K_i=H\cap\overline{Q_i}\) for the alternate sectors containing the three
vertices. The side contacts are the trisection points, so the parameters in
(11) apply and each \(a_i=2|H|/9\). Direct substitution gives equality in
(1). For any prescribed three distinct fan-line directions, choose a
triangle with sides parallel to those directions and translate it so that
its centroid is \(0\). Thus equality examples exist for every fan and the
constant is sharp.
