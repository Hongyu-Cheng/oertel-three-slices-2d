## Statement

Let
\[
v(u)=a+hu+d\min\{u,t\},
\qquad
a>0,\quad h\geq0,\quad d>0,\quad 0<t<1,
\]
and fix \(0<\lambda<1\).  Suppose the left clipping point is
\(\alpha=0\), equivalently
\[
a=v(0)\geq\lambda.
\tag{1}
\]
On the right, suppose either
\[
\lambda<\beta<1,\qquad v(\beta)=\beta-\lambda,
\tag{2}
\]
or
\[
\beta=1,\qquad v(1)\geq1-\lambda.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
I&=\int_0^1v(u)\,du,
&
R&=\int_0^\lambda v(u)\,du,\\
X&=\int_0^\alpha v(u)\,du,
&
T&=\int_\beta^1v(u)\,du,\\
C&=\frac{(\beta-\lambda)^2-(\lambda-\alpha)^2}{2},
&
J&=-X+C+T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
If either \(0=\alpha\leq t\leq\lambda\) or
\(\lambda\leq t\leq\beta\), then
\[
I+9J<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5J+8R-3I>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the required weak conclusion holds in all four cells.

## Proof

First prove (5) when \(\beta=1\).  Since \(\alpha=0\),
\[
X=T=0,\qquad
J=C=\frac{(1-\lambda)^2-\lambda^2}{2}
=\frac12-\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
\delta=\lambda-\frac12.
\]
The premise in (5) is
\[
I-9\delta<0.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(I>0\), (7) implies
\[
\delta>0,\qquad I<9\delta.
\tag{8}
\]

Write
\[
w(u)=v(u)-a,\qquad W=\int_0^1w(u)\,du=I-a.
\]
The function \(w\) is nonnegative, nondecreasing, and concave, with
\(w(0)=0\).  Concavity therefore gives, for every \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
w(\lambda x)\geq\lambda w(x)+(1-\lambda)w(0)
=\lambda w(x).
\tag{9}
\]
After the change of variables \(u=\lambda x\), (9) and (1) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
R
&=a\lambda+\lambda\int_0^1w(\lambda x)\,dx\\
&\geq a\lambda+\lambda^2W\\
&=\lambda^2I+a\lambda(1-\lambda)\\
&\geq\lambda^2I+\lambda^2(1-\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Using (6) in \(K=5J+8R-3I\), inequality (10) gives
\[
K\geq(8\lambda^2-3)I
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta.
\tag{11}
\]

If \(8\lambda^2-3\geq0\), then \(I\geq a\geq\lambda\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
K
&\geq(8\lambda^2-3)\lambda
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta\\
&=8\lambda^2-8\lambda+\frac52\\
&=8\left(\lambda-\frac12\right)^2+\frac12>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

If \(8\lambda^2-3<0\), the strict upper bound in (8) reverses after
multiplication by \(8\lambda^2-3\).  Hence (11) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
K
&>9(8\lambda^2-3)\delta
{}+8\lambda^2(1-\lambda)-5\delta\\
&=4(16\lambda^3-7\lambda^2-8\lambda+4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
To check the last sign without approximation, put
\[
y=2\lambda-1.
\]
By (8), \(0<y<1\), and the cubic in (13) is exactly
\[
16\lambda^3-7\lambda^2-8\lambda+4
=2y^3+\frac{17}{4}y^2-\frac32y+\frac14.
\tag{14}
\]
The quadratic
\[
\frac{17}{4}y^2-\frac32y+\frac14
\]
has positive leading coefficient and discriminant
\[
\left(-\frac32\right)^2
{}-4\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)\left(\frac14\right)
=-2<0.
\]
It is therefore strictly positive, and \(2y^3>0\).  Equations
(13)--(14) prove \(K>0\).  This completes the full-right case.  Notice
that condition (3) was not used, so the proof holds on a larger
parameter set.

It remains to reduce (2) to the case just proved.  Put
\[
I_c=I-T,\qquad J_c=J-T=-X+C.
\]
The integral \(R\) is unchanged by truncation at \(\beta\).  Direct
substitution gives
\[
I+9J=(I_c+9J_c)+10T,
\tag{15}
\]
\[
5J+8R-3I=(5J_c+8R-3I_c)+2T.
\tag{16}
\]
Define the isotropically rescaled width and parameters by
\[
\widehat v(u)=\frac{v(\beta u)}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{\beta},\qquad
\widehat t=\frac{t}{\beta}.
\tag{17}
\]
The rescaled left clipping point is still zero, because
\[
\widehat v(0)=\frac a\beta
\geq\frac\lambda\beta=\widehat\lambda.
\tag{18}
\]
The right clipping equality in (2) becomes
\[
\widehat v(1)=1-\widehat\lambda.
\tag{19}
\]
All area integrals and the quadratic clipping term scale by
\(\beta^{-2}\).  Thus the rescaled full-right quantities are
\[
\widehat I=\frac{I_c}{\beta^2},\qquad
\widehat R=\frac{R}{\beta^2},\qquad
\widehat J=\frac{J_c}{\beta^2}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(I+9J<0\), then (15) and \(T>0\) imply
\[
\widehat I+9\widehat J
=\frac{I+9J-10T}{\beta^2}<0.
\]
The full-right result gives
\[
5J_c+8R-3I_c>0.
\]
Equation (16) now proves \(5J+8R-3I>0\).

The proof uses only concavity of \(w\), so every weak kink boundary is
included.  In particular, \(t=\lambda\) is covered from both listed
orders; \(t=\beta\) becomes \(\widehat t=1\) after (17), where (9)
remains valid; and the formal boundary \(t=0\) is also harmless.
Equality \(a=\lambda\) is included in (1).  The full endpoint
\(\beta=1\) is handled directly, while (15)--(20) handle every
\(\beta<1\), with the transition corresponding to \(T=0\).  Hence no
kink-order or clipping-endpoint boundary among the four cells is
omitted.
