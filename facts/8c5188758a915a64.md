## Statement

Assume the surviving exact triangular \(N=3\) barycentric system supplied by accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `64760c76cadff481`.  Thus
\[
0<A\le B,\qquad q:=\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i>1,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9,
\]
\[
\frac{U\Delta+V\Delta}{2}\subseteq\Delta,\qquad
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\]
and the literal row masses are
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(R_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((RU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((RV)_{i*};\sigma_i).
\tag{1}
\]
The residual area inequalities include
\[
B<1+A.
\tag{2}
\]
Accepted fact `291c24bb45bd4946` permits restriction of every remaining counterexample to \(A/B<3/8\).

Suppose, after a row permutation and a common column permutation, that
\[
R=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&-a&-a\\
-a&p&-a\\
1-p+a&1-p+a&1+2a
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
a>0,\qquad p>2a,\qquad p-2a>\frac32,\qquad p>a+1.
\tag{4}
\]
The columns of \(R\) sum to \(1\).  Suppose also that the centroid incidence sets of accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` are
\[
I_1=\{1,2\},\qquad I_0=\{3\}.
\tag{5}
\]
If
\[
\boxed{\qquad \frac14\le\frac AB<\frac38,\qquad}
\tag{6}
\]
then the three strict inequalities
\[
M_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{7}
\]
are impossible.  This conclusion holds for all real endpoint matrices \(U,V\) satisfying the exact compatibility condition, all determinant signs, and all real height allocations \(\sigma\) with \(q>1\).  In particular, it is not a cyclic-row exclusion: the first two middle rows isolate distinct vertices, while the third row has negative mean and isolates the third vertex.

## Proof

Put
\[
d=p-2a,\qquad
\alpha=\frac{p}{p+a},\qquad
\beta=\frac{1+2a}{p+a}.
\tag{8}
\]
The row sums in (3) are
\[
d,\qquad d,\qquad 3-2d.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(d>3/2\) gives exactly the middle-centroid incidence
\(I_1=\{1,2\}\).  The complete triangle-cap formula of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` gives
\[
c_1=c_2=\alpha^2,\qquad c_3=\beta^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the first two rows have one positive value \(p\) and two
equal negative values \(-a\).  The third row has the two negative
values \(1-p+a\) and the positive value \(1+2a\), and the difference
between these values is \(p+a\).

The parameters in (8) satisfy
\[
\frac23<\alpha<1,\qquad 0<\beta<\frac23,
\qquad
\beta>2(1-\alpha).
\tag{11}
\]
The first inequality follows from \(p>2a\).  The inequality
\(\beta<2/3\) is equivalent to \(3<2(p-2a)=2d\).  Finally,
\[
\beta-2(1-\alpha)
=\frac{1+2a}{p+a}-\frac{2a}{p+a}
=\frac1{p+a}>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Write
\[
r=\frac AB,\qquad
k=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9,\qquad z=\sqrt{k},\qquad
\alpha_0=1-\frac z2.
\tag{13}
\]
By (2), \(0<z<2/3\).  Since \(3\in I_0\), the quantitative centroid
incidence bound in accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` says that the
absolute small-end cap area in row \(3\) is at least \(4A/9\).
The large-end cap area is nonnegative, so (7) in row \(3\) would give
\[
\beta^2+\frac{4A}{9}<t,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\beta^2< t-\frac{4A}{9}=k=z^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (11), (12), and (14) imply
\[
2(1-\alpha)<\beta<z,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\alpha>\alpha_0.
\tag{15}
\]

We next derive the rowwise complement inequality directly, without
assuming that the three middle cap fractions are equal.  Let \(X,Y\)
be the absolute endpoint cap areas in row \(1\):
\[
X=A\mathcal C((RU)_{1*};-\sigma_1),\qquad
Y=B\mathcal C((RV)_{1*};\sigma_1).
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\alpha^2>4/9\), (2) gives
\[
\alpha^2+A-t
>
\frac49+A-\frac{2(1+A+B)}9
=\frac{2+7A-2B}{9}
>\frac{5A}{9}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus row \(1\) in a strict system cannot have \(X=A\) or \(Y=B\).

Consider the closed lower complementary caps in \(U\Delta,V\Delta\)
and in the middle triangle:
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_0&=U\Delta\cap\{w:R_{1*}w\le-\sigma_1\},\\
D_2&=V\Delta\cap\{w:R_{1*}w\le \sigma_1\},\\
D_1&=\Delta\cap\{w:R_{1*}w\le0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The relevant functionals are nonconstant unless an endpoint cap is
empty or full; the full case was excluded in (17), and the empty case
only makes its lower complement the whole endpoint triangle.  Hence
\[
|D_0|=A-X,\qquad |D_2|=B-Y,\qquad |D_1|=1-\alpha^2.
\tag{19}
\]
Exact compatibility from accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives
\[
\frac{D_0+D_2}{2}\subseteq D_1.
\tag{20}
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski applied to (19)--(20) yields the literal
rowwise constraint
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
\le 2\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}.
\tag{21}
\]

If \(\alpha_0^2\ge t\), then (15) gives
\(\alpha^2>t\), contradicting row \(1\) of (7) immediately.  It remains
to consider
\[
b_0:=t-\alpha_0^2>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Because \(z<2/3\), one has \(\alpha_0>2/3\).  Equation (2) gives
\[
t<\frac{4(1+A)}9,
\]
and therefore
\[
0<b_0<t-\frac49<\frac{4A}{9}<A.
\tag{23}
\]
Row \(1\) of (7), together with (15), implies
\[
X+Y<t-\alpha^2<t-\alpha_0^2=b_0.
\tag{24}
\]

We record a two-variable allocation observation.  If
\[
0\le X,\quad0\le Y,\quad s=X+Y<b_0<A\le B,
\]
then concavity of
\[
\xi\longmapsto
\sqrt{A-\xi}+\sqrt{B-s+\xi}
\qquad(0\le\xi\le s)
\]
puts its minimum at an endpoint.  The smaller endpoint value is
\(\sqrt{A-s}+\sqrt B\), because
\[
\sqrt A-\sqrt{A-s}
\ge \sqrt B-\sqrt{B-s}.
\]
Consequently (24) gives
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
>
\sqrt{A-b_0}+\sqrt B.
\tag{25}
\]
Combining (15), (21), and (25), every putative strict system would
have to satisfy
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\sqrt{A-b_0}+\sqrt B
<
2\sqrt{1-\alpha_0^2}.
\qquad}
\tag{26}
\]

We now prove the exact opposite inequality on the whole chamber (6).
This is the only algebraic step.  From (13),
\[
B=\frac{9z^2-2}{2(1-r)}.
\tag{27}
\]
Direct substitution into (22) gives
\[
b_0=\frac{N(r,z)}{36(1-r)},
\tag{28}
\]
where
\[
N(r,z)
=(45r+27)z^2+36(1-r)z+20r-36.
\tag{29}
\]
For fixed \(r\), \(N(r,z)\) is strictly increasing for \(z>0\).  Also
\[
N\left(r,\frac7{12}\right)=\frac{229r-93}{16}<0,
\qquad
N\left(r,\frac23\right)=16r>0
\tag{30}
\]
throughout (6).  Let \(\zeta(r)\in(7/12,2/3)\) be its unique zero.
Equation (28) and (22) give
\[
z>\zeta(r).
\tag{31}
\]

Put
\[
P=A-b_0,\qquad W=1-\alpha_0^2,\qquad
D=4W-P-B,
\qquad
E=4PB-D^2.
\tag{32}
\]
All of \(P,B,W\) are positive by (13), (22), and (23).  We prove
\[
E>0.
\tag{33}
\]

First, \(E\) is strictly increasing in \(z\) on the rectangle relevant
to (6), (30).  Define
\[
u=8r-2,\qquad v=12z-7
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal B_i^m(s)=\binom mi s^i(1-s)^{m-i}
\]
denote the Bernstein basis.  Thus \(0\le u<1\) and \(0<v<1\).
Exact differentiation and collection in this basis gives
\[
36(1-r)^2\frac{\partial E}{\partial z}
=
\sum_{i=0}^2\sum_{j=0}^3
q_{ij}\mathcal B_i^2(u)\mathcal B_j^3(v),
\tag{34}
\]
where the coefficient matrix \((q_{ij})\), with rows indexed by
\(i=0,1,2\), is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{709657}{3072}&\frac{30845}{144}&
\frac{27097}{144}&\frac{452}{3}\\[1mm]
\frac{538997}{2048}&\frac{72881}{288}&
\frac{67591}{288}&206\\[1mm]
\frac{3405161}{12288}&\frac{52139}{192}&
\frac{49651}{192}&\frac{709}{3}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{35}
\]
Every entry is positive.  The Bernstein basis functions are
nonnegative and their double sum is \(1\), so (34)--(35) prove
\[
\frac{\partial E}{\partial z}>0.
\tag{36}
\]

It remains to evaluate the lower boundary \(z=\zeta(r)\).  On
\(N(r,z)=0\), direct elimination of \(r\) from (27)--(32) gives
\[
E=E_0(z):=
-\frac{47}{2}z^4+26z^3-50z^2+28z-1.
\tag{37}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{\partial N}{\partial r}
=45z^2-36z+20>0
\tag{38}
\]
because the quadratic in (38) has negative discriminant.  Hence
\(\zeta(r)\le\zeta(1/4)\) for \(r\ge1/4\).  The latter root is
\[
\zeta_*:=\zeta\left(\frac14\right)
=\frac{-18+8\sqrt{38}}{51}.
\tag{39}
\]

The polynomial \(E_0\) is strictly decreasing for
\(z\ge7/12\).  Indeed,
\[
E_0''(z)=-282z^2+156z-100<0
\]
because its discriminant is \(-88464\), while
\[
E_0'\left(\frac7{12}\right)=-\frac{19397}{864}<0.
\tag{40}
\]
Therefore
\[
E_0(\zeta(r))\ge E_0(\zeta_*).
\tag{41}
\]
An exact reduction using \(153\zeta_*^2+108\zeta_*-124=0\) gives
\[
E_0(\zeta_*)
=
\frac{140583072\sqrt{38}-866477353}{6765201}>0.
\tag{42}
\]
The last sign is certified without approximation by
\[
140583072^2\cdot38-866477353^2
=233801790486383>0.
\tag{43}
\]
Equations (31), (36), and (37)--(43) prove (33).

Finally, (32)--(33) imply
\[
2\sqrt{PB}>|D|.
\]
Since \(D=4W-P-B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sqrt P+\sqrt B\right)^2-4W
&=P+B+2\sqrt{PB}-4W\\
&=2\sqrt{PB}-D\\
&>|D|-D\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt{A-b_0}+\sqrt B
>2\sqrt{1-\alpha_0^2},
\tag{45}
\]
which contradicts the necessary strict inequality (26).

This contradiction used no parametrization or restriction of \(U,V\),
no determinant sign, and no restriction on the individual
\(\sigma_i\) beyond the surviving system.  It therefore excludes the
entire distorted noncyclic family (3)--(6), as claimed.
