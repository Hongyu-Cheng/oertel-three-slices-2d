## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and areas \(a_0,a_1,a_2\), and put \(\mu=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\). Fix
\[
0<t<a_1+\mu.
\]
For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), use the exact notation of accepted facts `17ca54faa05ad878`, `65512ded3a06d971`, and `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`:
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}\right|,
\qquad
I_q(s)=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(s-c)\bigr),
\]
\[
\Phi_q(r)=F_{1,q}(r)+I_q(2r),
\qquad
\rho_q(t)=\inf\{r:\Phi_q(r)\ge t\},
\]
and
\[
D_t=A_1\cap\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}
\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q(t)\}.
\]
Retain the exact zero-demand and over-capacity conventions
\[
D_{1,q}(a)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&a=0,\\
Q_{1,q}(a),&0<a\le a_1,\\
+\infty,&a>a_1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
E_q(b)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&b=0,\\
\inf\{s:I_q(s)\ge b\},&0<b\le\mu,\\
+\infty,&b>\mu,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(Q_{1,q}\) is the endpoint-normalized inverse of \(F_{1,q}\).

If \(z\in D_t\) and \(q\cdot z=\rho_q(t)\), then
\[
J_q(z)=
\left[
\max\{0,t-I_q(2q\cdot z)\},
\min\{t,F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)\}
\right]
=\{F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)\}.
\]
Moreover, the unique directionwise optimizer \(a_*(q)\) from accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` is
\[
a_*(q)=F_{1,q}(q\cdot z).
\]

For \(a\in[0,t]\), say that \(a\) certifies \(z\) in direction \(q\) if
\[
q\cdot z\ge D_{1,q}(a),
\qquad
q\cdot z\ge\frac12E_q(t-a).
\]
If \(q_1,q_2,q_3\in\mathbb S^1\) are tight at \(z\), meaning
\[
q_i\cdot z=\rho_{q_i}(t)\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
and one fixed \(a\in[0,t]\) certifies \(z\) in all three directions, then
\[
F_{1,q_1}(q_1\cdot z)
=F_{1,q_2}(q_2\cdot z)
=F_{1,q_3}(q_3\cdot z)
=a.
\]
This conclusion includes middle-demand zero, endpoint-demand zero, and either capacity-active endpoint.

## Proof

Fix a tight direction \(q\) and write
\[
r=q\cdot z=\rho_q(t),\qquad
p=F_{1,q}(r),\qquad
e=I_q(2r).
\]
The slope form in accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` agrees exactly with this notation, because the substitution \(c=r+\lambda\) gives
\[
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)\bigr)
=I_q(2r).
\]
Accepted facts `17ca54faa05ad878` and `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` give profile attainment at the threshold:
\[
\Phi_q(\rho_q(t))=t.
\]
Consequently,
\[
p+e=t.
\]
Both quantities are actual closed-cap profile masses, so
\[
0\le p\le a_1,\qquad 0\le e\le\mu.
\]
It follows directly that
\[
\max\{0,t-e\}=\max\{0,p\}=p,
\qquad
\min\{t,p\}=p,
\]
where \(p\le t\) follows from \(e\ge0\). These are precisely the two endpoints of \(J_q(z)\), proving
\[
J_q(z)=\{p\}=\{F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)\}.
\]
No open cap or limiting support value has been substituted here: \(p\) is the area of the closed cap appearing in the definition of \(F_{1,q}\), and \(e\) is the attained profile value \(I_q(2r)\).

For completeness, the interval is exactly the set of fixed allocations certifying \(z\) in direction \(q\), including all endpoint conventions. For \(a\in[0,t]\), endpoint-normalized inversion gives
\[
r\ge D_{1,q}(a)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_{1,q}(r)\ge a.
\]
At \(a=0\), both sides are automatic because \(D_{1,q}(0)=-\infty\); at \(a>a_1\), both sides are false for finite \(r\); and positive demands, including \(a=a_1\), use the closed-cap quantile inverse. Likewise, the exact superlevel threshold in accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` gives
\[
2r\ge E_q(b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
I_q(2r)\ge b
\]
for \(b\ge0\), with both sides automatic at \(b=0\) because \(E_q(0)=-\infty\), both false when \(b>\mu\), and the full-capacity case \(b=\mu\) included. Therefore \(a\) certifies \(z\) exactly when
\[
a\le p,\qquad t-a\le e,
\]
or equivalently \(a\in J_q(z)\). Since \(J_q(z)=\{p\}\), every certifying allocation equals \(p\).

This also identifies the outer optimizer. Accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` gives
\[
\rho_q(t)=
\min_{0\le a\le t}
\max\left\{D_{1,q}(a),\frac12E_q(t-a)\right\}.
\]
The certifying allocation \(a=p\) makes both entries in the maximum at most \(r=\rho_q(t)\), so it is an optimizer. Accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` proves that this optimizer is unique under the stated hypotheses, including zero-demand and capacity-active cases. Hence \(a_*(q)=p\).

The endpoint cases can also be read directly from \(p+e=t\). If \(p=0\), then \(e=t\) and \(J_q(z)=\{0\}\); the middle demand is zero and uses \(D_{1,q}(0)=-\infty\), with \(e=\mu\) allowed. If \(e=0\), then \(p=t\) and \(J_q(z)=\{t\}\); the endpoint demand is zero and uses \(E_q(0)=-\infty\), with \(p=a_1\) allowed. If \(p=a_1\), then \(e=t-a_1\) and the singleton is \(\{a_1\}\). If \(e=\mu\), then \(p=t-\mu\) and the singleton is \(\{t-\mu\}\). The simultaneous case \(p=a_1\) and \(e=\mu\) would give \(t=a_1+\mu\), excluded by the strict hypothesis.

Finally, suppose one \(a\) certifies \(z\) in the three tight directions \(q_i\). Applying the singleton identity separately to each direction gives
\[
a=F_{1,q_i}(q_i\cdot z)\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
which is the claimed equal-cap consequence. The argument is conditional on the existence of this one common \(a\) and proves no such existence statement.
