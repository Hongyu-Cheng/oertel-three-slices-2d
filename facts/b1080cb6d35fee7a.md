## Statement

Use all notation and hypotheses of accepted fact `8815656d889bfbde`, and
specialize to
\[
\mu=\frac25,\qquad \lambda=\frac85,\qquad
k=\frac{13}{5}\Delta,\qquad \Delta=\frac{pq}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus
\[
t=\frac{266}{225}\Delta,\qquad
m=\frac4{25}\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
There is no strictly convex octagon with positive parameters and cyclic
vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_\alpha&=(a,b),&
Z_v&=(-c,c+d),&
Z_\beta&=(-e-f,e),&
A&=(-p,0),\\
Z_a&=(-g,-h),&
B&=(0,-q),&
Z_\gamma&=(i,-i-j),&
Z_u&=(\ell+n,-\ell)
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
that satisfies
\[
c_i<t,\qquad c_\ell<t,\qquad c_q+m<t.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the calibrated asymmetric-octagon chamber contains no rational
counterexample to
\[
\mathcal E_K=c_i+c_\ell+c_q-k\ge D_0.
\]

## Proof

Put
\[
C=c+d,\qquad L=\ell+n,
\]
and retain the actual open-cone areas \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) from accepted
fact `8815656d889bfbde`.  Define the two remaining positive-sum side-cone
areas
\[
u=\left|K\cap\{x>0,\ y<0,\ x+y>0\}\right|,
\]
\[
v=\left|K\cap\{x<0,\ y>0,\ x+y>0\}\right|,
\tag{5}
\]
the negative-quadrant area
\[
N=\left|K\cap\{x<0,\ y<0\}\right|,
\tag{6}
\]
and the inhomogeneous strip area
\[
\eta=\left|K\cap\{0<x+y\leq1\}\right|.
\tag{7}
\]

The scalar calibration really lies in the residual chamber.  Indeed,
\[
r=\frac1{16}<\frac9{25},\qquad
s=\frac{65}{64},
\]
and the first branch of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
\frac{15}{16}=1-r<\frac{65}{64}<1+r=\frac{17}{16}.
\tag{8}
\]
Accepted fact `8815656d889bfbde` also verifies \(D_0>m\), midpoint
containment, the zero profiles, and the support condition at (1).

For completeness, retain both parts of the accepted side-cone gate.  Since
\(D\subseteq K\), accepted fact `c3aabe018c39779d` gives the exhaustive
alternative
\[
\alpha\leq\eta
\tag{9}
\]
or
\[
2\beta\left(\frac1{p^2}+\frac1p\right)
+2\gamma\left(\frac1{q^2}+\frac1q\right)>1.
\tag{10}
\]
Also, with
\[
H=CL-c\ell=cn+d\ell+dn>0,
\]
the positive-quadrant payment in accepted fact `8815656d889bfbde` is
\[
\alpha\geq
\frac{H^2}{2(C+\ell)(c+L)}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
The contradiction below holds on the common semialgebraic part of both
branches (9) and (10), and therefore closes the full accepted dichotomy; no
independent cone-area assignment is made.

The two side-cone payments in accepted fact `8815656d889bfbde` are retained
as well:
\[
\beta\geq\frac{p^2C}{2(p+d)},\qquad
\gamma\geq\frac{q^2L}{2(q+n)}.
\tag{11a}
\]

Accepted fact `8815656d889bfbde` places the origin in the interior of \(K\).
The six concurrent cone cells and the strip cell therefore give the exact
null-boundary decompositions
\[
k=N+u+v+\alpha+\beta+\gamma,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
c_i=u+\alpha+\gamma,\qquad
c_\ell=v+\alpha+\beta,\qquad
c_q=N+\beta+\gamma+\eta.
\tag{13}
\]
The strict turn at \(Z_a=(-g,-h)\) is
\[
\det(Z_a-A,B-Z_a)=ph+gq-pq>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence
\[
N=\frac{ph+gq}{2}>\frac{pq}{2}=\Delta.
\tag{15}
\]
Using (2), the strict \(q\)-row in (4) and (13) now give
\[
N+\beta+\gamma+\eta
<t-m
=\frac{46}{45}\Delta,
\]
and consequently
\[
\beta+\gamma+\eta<\frac{\Delta}{45}.
\tag{16}
\]

The other two rows give exact lower bounds for the positive-sum side cells.
Indeed, (12)-(13) imply
\[
c_\ell+c_q=k-u+\beta+\eta,
\qquad
c_i+c_q=k-v+\gamma+\eta.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(c_\ell<t\), \(c_i<t\), and \(c_q<t-m\), equations (1), (2), and
(17) yield
\[
u>k-2t+m+\beta+\eta
=\frac{89}{225}\Delta+\beta+\eta
>\frac{89}{225}\Delta,
\tag{18}
\]
\[
v>k-2t+m+\gamma+\eta
=\frac{89}{225}\Delta+\gamma+\eta
>\frac{89}{225}\Delta.
\tag{19}
\]

We next record the exact radial reaches supplied by the strict cyclic
order.  The edge \(Z_vZ_\beta\) crosses \(x+y=0\) at a unique point
\[
W_\beta=(-\rho_\beta,\rho_\beta),\qquad \rho_\beta>0,
\]
and the edge \(Z_\gamma Z_u\) crosses it at
\[
W_\gamma=(\rho_\gamma,-\rho_\gamma),\qquad \rho_\gamma>0.
\tag{20}
\]
The \(\beta\)-cell contains
\(\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,W_\beta\}\), while the \(\gamma\)-cell
contains \(\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),B,W_\gamma\}\).  Therefore
\[
\beta\geq\frac{p\rho_\beta}{2},
\qquad
\gamma\geq\frac{q\rho_\gamma}{2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Together with (16), these give
\[
\rho_\beta<\frac q{45},\qquad
\rho_\gamma<\frac p{45}.
\tag{22}
\]

Convexity also puts four elementary triangles inside the two axis clips:
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),B,Z_\alpha\},
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),B,Z_u\}
\subseteq K\cap\{x\geq0\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,Z_\alpha\},
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),A,Z_v\}
\subseteq K\cap\{y\geq0\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Their areas and (4) imply, with
\[
A_0=\frac{266}{225},
\]
that
\[
a<A_0p,\qquad L<A_0p,\qquad
b<A_0q,\qquad C<A_0q.
\tag{24}
\]

The closure of the \(u\)-cell has outer boundary
\[
W_\gamma,\quad Z_u,\quad
[Z_u,Z_\alpha]\cap\{y=0\},
\]
together with the origin.  Thus every point in that cell has
\[
0<x<\max\{\rho_\gamma,L,a\}<A_0p,\qquad -x<y<0.
\]
It follows that
\[
u<\frac{A_0^2p^2}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Similarly, the closure of the \(v\)-cell has outer boundary
\[
W_\beta,\quad Z_v,\quad
[Z_v,Z_\alpha]\cap\{x=0\},
\]
together with the origin.  Hence
\[
v<\frac{A_0^2q^2}{2}.
\tag{26}
\]
Combining (19) and (26) gives
\[
\frac{89pq}{450}
<\frac{A_0^2q^2}{2},
\]
or
\[
\frac pq<
\frac{225A_0^2}{89}
=\frac{70756}{20025}
<4.
\tag{27}
\]
Consequently
\[
z:=\frac qp>\frac14.
\tag{28}
\]

It remains to sharpen the three \(x\)-reaches of the \(u\)-cell.  Apply the
pointwise forced-triangle estimate from accepted fact
`c3aabe018c39779d` to the positive-quadrant vertex
\(Z_\alpha=(a,b)\).  The segment \([B,Z_\alpha]\) crosses \(x+y=0\), and
the resulting triangle in the \(\gamma\)-cone has area
\[
\frac{q^2a}{2(q+a+b)}.
\]
Thus
\[
\gamma\geq\frac{q^2a}{2(q+a+b)}.
\tag{29}
\]
By (24),
\[
a+b<A_0(p+q).
\]
Combining this strict inequality, (16), and (29) yields
\[
\frac ap
<
\frac{1+A_0(1+1/z)}{45}.
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(z>1/4\),
\[
\frac ap
<
\frac{1+5A_0}{45}
=\frac{311}{2025}
<\frac16.
\tag{31}
\]

The forced \(\gamma\)-payment for \(Z_u\) from accepted fact
`8815656d889bfbde` is
\[
\gamma\geq\frac{q^2L}{2(q+n)}.
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(0<n<L\), equations (16) and (32) give
\[
\frac{q^2L}{2(q+L)}
<\frac{pq}{90}.
\]
Using \(z>1/4\), this is equivalent to
\[
\frac Lp<\frac{z}{45z-1}
<\frac{1/4}{45/4-1}
=\frac1{41}
<\frac16.
\tag{33}
\]
Finally, (22) gives \(\rho_\gamma<p/45<p/6\).

Equations (31), (33), and (22) show that the whole \(u\)-cell is contained,
up to its null boundary, in
\[
\left\{(x,y):0<x<\frac p6,\ -x<y<0\right\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
u<\int_0^{p/6}x\,dx=\frac{p^2}{72}.
\tag{34}
\]
On the other hand, (18) and (28) give
\[
u>\frac{89pq}{450}
=\frac{89z}{450}p^2
>\frac{89}{1800}p^2
>\frac1{72}p^2,
\tag{35}
\]
where the last comparison is \(89>25\).  Equations (34)-(35) contradict
each other.

Thus no positive rational parameter tuple satisfies the calibrated strict
octagon system.  All inequalities used above are strict at the required
boundaries, and every cutting line and polygon edge has zero planar area.
