## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nondegenerate triangles satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\]
and, for \(y\in S\), let
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\left\{\mathcal H_2(S\cap H):
H\subset\mathbb R^3\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\right\}.
\]
Write
\[
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
and assume the strict area-triangle inequalities
\[
M-m<a_1<M+m.
\tag{2}
\]
Set
\[
\delta=\frac{2(M+m-a_1)}9.
\]
If
\[
\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\delta}\right)^2
\geq \frac{20a_1}{9},
\tag{3}
\]
then, for the centroid \(g_1\) of \(A_1\),
\[
h_S((1,g_1))\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, after putting \(r=m/M\) and \(s=a_1/M\), this proves the locked conclusion for every compatible triangular triple satisfying
\[
1-r<s<1+r,\qquad
\left(1+\sqrt{r-\frac{2(1+r-s)}9}\right)^2
\geq\frac{20s}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]
The inequality in (5) is strict at \((r,s)=(1/2,1)\), because there it becomes
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{\frac7{18}}\right)^2-\frac{20}{9}
=\frac{2\sqrt{14}-5}{6}>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence (5) contains an open two-dimensional area class.  The assertion is uniform over all compatible relative positions in that class.

## Proof

The inequalities in (2) imply
\[
0<\delta<\frac{4m}{9}<m.
\tag{7}
\]
They also say that each one of \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) is less than \(T/2\).  Consequently, with
\[
t=\frac{2T}{9},
\]
one has
\[
0<t<\min\{a_0+a_1,a_1+a_2\},
\tag{8}
\]
so the exact middle-profile identity of accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` applies.

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), put \(r_q=q\cdot g_1\), and define
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\leq c\}\right|.
\]
We claim that the full endpoint infimal convolution satisfies
\[
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(2r_q-c)\right)\geq\delta.
\tag{9}
\]
This is a statement about every allocation \(c\), not about either support-extreme routing.

Suppose, to the contrary, that some \(c\) gives
\[
u_0+u_2<\delta,\qquad
u_0=F_{0,q}(c),\qquad
u_2=F_{2,q}(2r_q-c).
\tag{10}
\]
Let
\[
U_0=A_0\cap\{x:q\cdot x>c\},\qquad
U_2=A_2\cap\{x:q\cdot x>2r_q-c\}.
\]
Lines have planar area zero, so
\[
|U_0|=a_0-u_0,\qquad |U_2|=a_2-u_2.
\tag{11}
\]
Both sets have positive area by (7) and (10).  Compatibility (1) preserves the same allocation and the same direction:
\[
\frac{U_0+U_2}{2}
\subseteq
A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r_q\}.
\tag{12}
\]
The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality and (11)-(12) give
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r_q\}\right|
\geq
\frac{\left(\sqrt{a_0-u_0}+\sqrt{a_2-u_2}\right)^2}{4}.
\tag{13}
\]

Relabel the two endpoint losses as \(u_M,u_m\), according as they belong to the endpoint of area \(M,m\), and put \(d=u_M+u_m\).  For fixed \(0\leq d<m\), the concave function
\[
x\longmapsto \sqrt{M-x}+\sqrt{m-d+x},
\qquad 0\leq x\leq d,
\]
has its minimum at an endpoint.  Removing a fixed amount decreases the square root of the smaller initial number at least as much as it decreases the square root of the larger one.  Therefore the smaller endpoint value is obtained by putting the entire loss on \(m\), and
\[
\sqrt{M-u_M}+\sqrt{m-u_m}
\geq \sqrt M+\sqrt{m-d}
>\sqrt M+\sqrt{m-\delta}.
\tag{14}
\]
The last inequality is strict because \(d<\delta\).

Accepted fact `2b809ac6ae61991f` proves that every closed halfplane through the centroid of a nondegenerate triangle has area at least \(4a_1/9\).  Applying this to both sides of the line \(q\cdot x=r_q\), whose intersection with \(A_1\) has area zero, gives
\[
\left|A_1\cap\{x:q\cdot x>r_q\}\right|\leq\frac{5a_1}{9}.
\tag{15}
\]
On the other hand, (3), (13), and (14) make the left side of (15) strictly greater than \(5a_1/9\).  This contradiction proves (9).

The same centroid estimate gives
\[
F_{1,q}(r_q)\geq\frac{4a_1}{9}.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (9) and (16), for every direction \(q\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_q(r_q)
&=
F_{1,q}(r_q)
+\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(r_q+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r_q-\lambda)\right)\\
&=
F_{1,q}(r_q)
+\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(2r_q-c)\right)\\
&\geq \frac{4a_1}{9}+\delta
=\frac{2T}{9}=t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the direction-dependent optimizer is retained inside the infimum in every direction.  By the exact identity
\[
D_t=A_1\cap\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}
\{z:q\cdot z\geq\rho_q(t)\}
\]
in accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, equations (8) and (17) imply \(g_1\in D_t\), proving (4).

It remains to check the claimed calibration and nontriviality.  Formula (5) is obtained from (2)-(3) by division by \(M\), and (6) is direct arithmetic.  Strictness at \((1/2,1)\) persists on an open neighborhood.  Compatible triples exist there with slack: for a triangle \(\Delta\), the homothetic choice with endpoint linear ratios \(1,\sqrt{1/2}\) has midpoint linear ratio \((1+\sqrt{1/2})/2<1\) inside the middle copy, and the strict containment persists under small independent nonparallel perturbations.  Since the theorem itself uses no alignment, it covers every compatible perturbation, not only those examples.

Finally, the explicit accepted calibration `74e60bee4b401289` has
\[
M=\frac12,\qquad m=\frac{15}{56},\qquad
a_1=\frac{35}{64},\qquad \delta=\frac{11}{224},
\qquad m-\delta=\frac7{32}.
\]
For these values the margin in (3) is
\[
\left(\sqrt{\frac12}+\sqrt{\frac7{32}}\right)^2
-\frac{20}{9}\frac{35}{64}
=\frac{72\sqrt7-143}{288}>0,
\]
because \(72^2\cdot7>143^2\).  Hence the full-profile argument above proves that its middle centroid is a valid \(2T/9\) center.  This uses all endpoint allocations and is consistent with, rather than contradicted by, the failure of the two-routing maximum recorded in `74e60bee4b401289`.

## External sources

Planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality.  Exact statement used: if \(X,Y\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets and \(0<\lambda<1\), with \((1-\lambda)X+\lambda Y\) measurable, then
\[
\left|(1-\lambda)X+\lambda Y\right|^{1/2}
\geq
(1-\lambda)|X|^{1/2}+\lambda|Y|^{1/2}.
\]
Source: Richard J. Gardner, “The Brunn--Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2.  Here \(X=U_0\), \(Y=U_2\), and \(\lambda=1/2\); the sets are bounded measurable convex subsets of the endpoint triangles, and their midpoint sum in (12) is measurable.
