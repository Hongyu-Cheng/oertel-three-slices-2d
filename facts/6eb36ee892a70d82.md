## Statement

Assume the locked problem, assume that the areas lie in the full strict residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, and assume that \(A_1\) is a nondegenerate triangle. Write
\[
a_\ell=|A_\ell|,\qquad
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad
d=\frac{2(M+m-a_1)}9.
\]
Let \(g_1\) be the centroid of \(A_1\), and let
\(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) be its barycentric coordinates. For
\(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), orient the unit vector \(q_i\) so that
\[
A_1\cap\{x:q_i\cdot x\le q_i\cdot g_1\}
=\{x\in A_1:\beta_i(x)\ge 1/3\}.
\]
For \(\ell\in\{0,2\}\), put
\[
F_{\ell,q}(c)
=|A_\ell\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}|
\]
and define
\[
J_i=
\inf_{c_{0i}+c_{2i}=2q_i\cdot g_1}
\left(F_{0,q_i}(c_{0i})+F_{2,q_i}(c_{2i})\right).
\]
Then, for every two distinct \(i,j\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
\boxed{
J_i+J_j\ge
\min\left\{m,\ M+m-\frac{8a_1}{9}\right\}>2d.}
\]
Consequently, at most one index satisfies \(J_i\le d\), and in particular at most one index satisfies \(J_i<d\).

## Proof

We first verify the triangle cap geometry with the stated orientation. The affine barycentric-coordinate domain is
\[
D=\{(x,y):x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\},
\]
whose coordinate area is \(1/2\); affine area ratios in \(D\) are the same as those in \(A_1\). The vertex cap
\[
C_i=\{\beta_i\ge1/3\}
\]
is a triangle of linear scale \(2/3\), so
\[
|C_i|=\frac49a_1.
\]
For \(i\ne j\), the region
\[
C_i\cap C_j=\{\beta_i\ge1/3,\ \beta_j\ge1/3\}
\]
becomes, after subtracting \(1/3\) from both displayed coordinates, a triangle of linear scale \(1/3\). Hence
\[
|C_i\cap C_j|=\frac19a_1.
\]
Thus the advisory value \(a_1/9\) is correct for the intersection of the two lower vertex caps. It is not the area needed below. The common region on the upper sides of their two boundary lines is
\[
R_{ij}
=\{\beta_i\le1/3,\ \beta_j\le1/3\}.
\]
In the \((\beta_i,\beta_j)\)-domain this is the square
\([0,1/3]^2\), which lies in \(D\). Its coordinate area is \(1/9\), and therefore
\[
|R_{ij}|=\frac29a_1.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, \(C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3=A_1\), since at least one barycentric coordinate is at least \(1/3\), and their triple intersection is the singleton \(\{g_1\}\). Their pairwise intersections have the positive area \(a_1/9\) computed above, so this is a cover with positive-area overlaps.

Fix distinct \(i,j\). By accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240`, each of the two endpoint infima is attained. Choose thresholds
\[
c_{0k}+c_{2k}=2q_k\cdot g_1
\qquad(k=i,j)
\tag{2}
\]
that attain \(J_k\), and write
\[
B_{\ell k}
=A_\ell\cap\{x:q_k\cdot x\le c_{\ell k}\},
\qquad
b_{\ell k}=|B_{\ell k}|
\qquad(\ell=0,2;\ k=i,j).
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
J_i+J_j
=b_{0i}+b_{2i}+b_{0j}+b_{2j}.
\tag{4}
\]

Set
\[
E_\ell
=A_\ell\cap\{q_i\cdot x\ge c_{\ell i}\}
\cap\{q_j\cdot x\ge c_{\ell j}\},
\qquad e_\ell=|E_\ell|
\qquad(\ell=0,2).
\tag{5}
\]
Each \(E_\ell\) is an actual compact convex endpoint body. Since a line has planar area zero, the union bound applied to the two lower caps in (3) gives
\[
e_\ell\ge a_\ell-b_{\ell i}-b_{\ell j}.
\tag{6}
\]

Convexity of the locked polytope gives the midpoint containment
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1:
\]
indeed, the midpoint of \((0,x_0)\in C\) and \((2,x_2)\in C\) belongs to the height-one slice of \(C\). If \(x_0\in E_0\) and \(x_2\in E_2\), then (2) and (5) give, for \(k=i,j\),
\[
q_k\cdot\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}
\ge\frac{c_{0k}+c_{2k}}2
=q_k\cdot g_1.
\]
By the chosen orientation of \(q_k\), this says
\(\beta_k((x_0+x_2)/2)\le1/3\). Therefore
\[
\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\subseteq R_{ij}.
\tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
L=M+m-\frac{8a_1}{9}.
\]
We prove
\[
J_i+J_j\ge\min\{m,L\}.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(J_i+J_j\ge m\), this is immediate. Suppose instead that
\(J_i+J_j<m\). Then (4) implies
\[
b_{\ell i}+b_{\ell j}<m\le a_\ell
\qquad(\ell=0,2),
\]
so (6) shows that both \(E_0,E_2\) have positive area. The planar Brunn--Minkowski inequality and (1), (7) yield
\[
\frac{\sqrt{e_0}+\sqrt{e_2}}2
\le
\sqrt{\left|\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\right|}
\le\sqrt{\frac{2a_1}{9}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
e_0+e_2
\le(\sqrt{e_0}+\sqrt{e_2})^2
\le\frac{8a_1}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]
On the other hand, summing (6) and using (4) gives
\[
e_0+e_2\ge M+m-(J_i+J_j).
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) imply \(J_i+J_j\ge L\), proving (8) also in the second case.

It remains to compare this ledger with \(2d\). The strict residual inequalities in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` include
\[
M-m<a_1<M+m.
\tag{11}
\]
The left inequality gives
\[
d=\frac{2(M+m-a_1)}9<\frac{4m}{9},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
m>2d.
\tag{12}
\]
The right inequality gives
\[
L-2d
=M+m-\frac{8a_1}{9}
-\frac{4(M+m-a_1)}9
=\frac{5(M+m)-4a_1}{9}
>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus both entries in the minimum in (8) are strictly larger than \(2d\). This proves the boxed pairwise ledger. If two indices satisfied \(J_i\le d\), their sum would be at most \(2d\), contradicting that ledger. The asserted starvation claim follows, including all equality and boundary cases.
