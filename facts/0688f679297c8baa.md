## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex body with centroid \(g\) and area \(s\). Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Suppose
\[
f_1(g)>0,\qquad f_2(g)>0,
\]
and put
\[
c_i=\left|K\cap\{f_i\le0\}\right|
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Then
\[
\max\{c_1,c_2\}\ge2\sqrt{s\,c_3}-2c_3.
\]

## Proof

We use the planar centroid halfplane inequality recorded in accepted fact `6e68b44550cac3c8`: every closed halfplane containing the centroid of a planar convex body of area \(a\) cuts area at least \(4a/9\).

Assume for contradiction that \(K\) is a strict counterexample. Write
\[
M=\max\{c_1,c_2\},\qquad
R=2\sqrt{s\,c_3}-2c_3,
\]
so that
\[
M<R.                                                             \tag{1}
\]
The affine relation and the two strict centroid signs give
\[
f_3(g)=-1-f_1(g)-f_2(g)<-1.
\]
Thus the halfplane \(\{f_3\le0\}\) contains \(g\), and the centroid halfplane inequality gives
\[
c_3\ge\frac{4s}{9}.                                              \tag{2}
\]
For \(u\ge4s/9\), the function
\[
u\longmapsto2\sqrt{su}-2u
\]
is decreasing and equals \(4s/9\) at \(u=4s/9\). Hence
\[
R\le\frac{4s}{9}.                                                \tag{3}
\]
In particular, (1) is a strict inequality below the centroid halfplane threshold.

Choose inscribed convex polygons \(K_m\) converging to \(K\) in Hausdorff distance. Let \(s_m=|K_m|\), let \(g_m\) be the centroid of \(K_m\), and put
\[
c_{i,m}=\left|K_m\cap\{f_i\le0\}\right|.
\]
Bounded convex Hausdorff convergence gives
\[
s_m\to s,\qquad g_m\to g,\qquad c_{i,m}\to c_i.
\]
The cap convergence follows because every fixed affine line has planar area zero. For all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
f_1(g_m)>0,\qquad f_2(g_m)>0                                   \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
M_m:=\max\{c_{1,m},c_{2,m}\}
<
R_m:=2\sqrt{s_m c_{3,m}}-2c_{3,m}.                              \tag{5}
\]
Fix one such \(m\), and suppress the subscript \(m\) until the end of the proof.

Again,
\[
f_3(g)=-1-f_1(g)-f_2(g)<-1,
\]
so the centroid halfplane inequality applied to the polygon gives
\[
d:=c_3\ge\frac{4s}{9}.                                           \tag{6}
\]
Consequently
\[
R=2\sqrt{sd}-2d\le\frac{4s}{9},
\qquad
c_1,c_2\le M<R\le\frac{4s}{9}.                                  \tag{7}
\]

We now impose exact balance without moving a vertex of the polygon and without changing the third cap. For \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(\alpha)&=\left|K\cap\{f_1\le-\alpha\}\right|,\\
B(\alpha)&=\left|K\cap\{f_2\le\alpha-1\}\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both functions are continuous; \(A\) is nonincreasing and \(B\) is nondecreasing. Set
\[
\alpha_-=-f_1(g),\qquad
\alpha_+=1+f_2(g).
\]
At the lower endpoint, the first cutting line passes through the centroid:
\[
A(\alpha_-)
=\left|K\cap\{f_1\le f_1(g)\}\right|
\ge\frac{4s}{9}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\alpha_--1=-1-f_1(g)<0,
\]
so
\[
B(\alpha_-)\le c_2<\frac{4s}{9}.
\]
Thus
\[
A(\alpha_-)>B(\alpha_-).                                        \tag{8}
\]
At the upper endpoint,
\[
-\alpha_+=-1-f_2(g)<0,
\]
and hence
\[
A(\alpha_+)\le c_1<\frac{4s}{9},
\]
whereas the second cutting line passes through the centroid:
\[
B(\alpha_+)
=\left|K\cap\{f_2\le f_2(g)\}\right|
\ge\frac{4s}{9}.
\]
Therefore
\[
A(\alpha_+)<B(\alpha_+).                                        \tag{9}
\]
By continuity, there is
\[
\alpha\in(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)
\]
such that
\[
A(\alpha)=B(\alpha)=:C.                                         \tag{10}
\]

Define two affine coordinates
\[
x=f_1+\alpha,\qquad y=f_2+1-\alpha.
\]
Their linear parts are independent because those of \(f_1,f_2\) are nonparallel. The choice of the open interval in (10) gives
\[
x(g)=f_1(g)+\alpha>0,\qquad
y(g)=f_2(g)+1-\alpha>0.                                         \tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
x+y=f_1+f_2+1=-f_3,                                             \tag{12}
\]
so the third cap is unchanged:
\[
\left|K\cap\{x+y\ge0\}\right|
=\left|K\cap\{f_3\le0\}\right|
=d.                                                             \tag{13}
\]
Thus equality in the threshold \(d=4s/9\), if it occurs, is preserved exactly. No translation of \(K\), vertex correction, or perturbation of \(d\) is being used.

The common side-cap area in (10) does not exceed the larger original cap. If \(\alpha\ge0\), then
\[
C=A(\alpha)\le A(0)=c_1\le M.
\]
If \(\alpha\le0\), then \(\alpha-1<0\), so
\[
C=B(\alpha)\le B(1)=c_2\le M.
\]
Hence in all cases
\[
C\le M<R.                                                       \tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
e=s-C-d.
\]
Since \(C+2d<2\sqrt{sd}\) by (14), squaring gives
\[
C^2<4d(s-C-d)=4de.                                               \tag{15}
\]
In particular,
\[
e>0.
\]

Let \(T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2\) be the invertible affine map
\[
T(z)=(x(z),y(z)),
\]
and let \(\kappa>0\) be the absolute determinant of its linear part. We next show that the fan point \(T^{-1}(0)\) lies in the interior of \(K\).

Suppose not. Then the convex polygon \(T(K)\) is contained in a closed halfplane through \(0\), so the directions of its nonzero points lie in a closed semicircle. Use the six canonical sectors of accepted fact `d97bf9695b3393c1`. Equality of the two side-cap areas in (10) is exactly the balance relation
\[
N_x-W_y=N_y-W_x=e.
\]
Because \(e>0\),
\[
N_x=e+W_y>0,\qquad N_y=e+W_x>0.                                 \tag{16}
\]
A closed semicircle containing interior directions of both \(N_x\) and \(N_y\) must use the arc through \(O\), and it cannot contain an interior direction of \(P\). Hence
\[
P=0.
\]
In the unscaled areas of \(K\), this gives
\[
s=2d+2e+O,
\]
so
\[
d+e\le\frac{s}{2},\qquad C=s-d-e\ge\frac{s}{2}.                  \tag{17}
\]
Together with \(d\ge4s/9\),
\[
4de\le4d\left(\frac{s}{2}-d\right)\le\frac{8s^2}{81},
\]
whereas \(C^2\ge s^2/4\). Therefore
\[
C^2-4de\ge\frac{s^2}{4}-\frac{8s^2}{81}
=\frac{49s^2}{324}>0,
\]
contradicting (15). Thus
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}T(K).                                    \tag{18}
\]

The canonical polygon \(T(K)\) has area \(\kappa s\), equal side-cap areas \(\kappa C\), third-cap area \(\kappa d\), and balance excess \(\kappa e\). Its centroid is \(T(g)\), whose two coordinates are strictly positive by (11); equivalently, both canonical centroid moments are strictly positive. Equation (6) gives
\[
\kappa d\ge\frac{4\kappa s}{9}.
\]
All hypotheses of accepted polygon theorem `d97bf9695b3393c1` therefore hold, and it yields
\[
(\kappa C)^2\ge4(\kappa e)(\kappa d).
\]
After division by \(\kappa^2\),
\[
C^2\ge4ed.                                                       \tag{19}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
(C+2d)^2\ge4sd,
\]
and all quantities are nonnegative, so
\[
C\ge2\sqrt{sd}-2d=R.
\]
This contradicts (14).

We have obtained a contradiction from one sufficiently accurate polygonal approximation of the alleged strict convex-body counterexample. Hence no strict counterexample exists, proving
\[
\max\{c_1,c_2\}\ge2\sqrt{s\,c_3}-2c_3
\]
for every compact convex body in the statement. Since the argument excludes strict violation, it includes equality in the asserted inequality and the boundary case \(c_3=4s/9\).
