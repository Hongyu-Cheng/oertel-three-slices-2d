## Statement

Work in the \((y_1,y_2)\)-coordinates on \(y_1+y_2+y_3=1\), with
\[
f_i=-200y_i+\frac{199}{3},\qquad
L_i=L=-\frac{199}{3},\qquad p=\frac{199}{300}.
\]
Use the fixed \(A_2,K\) of accepted fact `0c0621b05f1db4ff` and the
terminal hexagon \(A_0^\star\) obtained in accepted fact
`007cd9d294748c86`.  Put
\[
M=|A_0^\star|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
H=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
\[
C_i=|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|,\qquad
\mu_i(a)=C_i+|A_0^\star\cap\{f_i\leq a\}|
                 +|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-a\}|,
\]
\[
I_i=\{a:\mu_i(a)<H\}.
\]
The crossing framework of accepted fact `89563f2f27529ab0`, the exact
equalities of accepted fact `0c0621b05f1db4ff`, and their terminal
preservation in accepted fact `007cd9d294748c86` give
\(\mu_i(L)=H\) for \(i=1,2,3\).

For the chord densities and one-sided slopes in the convention of
accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc`, the three exact pairs
\[
\left(
\rho_{0i}^{+}(L)-\rho_{2i}^{-}(-L),\
\dot\rho_{0i}^{+}(L)+\dot\rho_{2i}^{-}(-L)

are

\dot\rho_{0i}^{+}(L)+\dot\rho_{2i}^{-}(-L)\\ \hline

\dfrac{771\,842\,620\,626\,619\,969\,949}
      {503\,815\,127\,560\,830\,891\,000\,000}&
-\dfrac{3\,115\,919\,752\,567\,675\,093\,343}
       {87\,183\,394\,089\,942\,566\,696\,342\,400}\\[2mm]

\dfrac{58\,737\,022\,153}{84\,836\,088\,000\,000}&
-\dfrac{28\,255\,331}{1\,885\,246\,400\,000}\\[2mm]

\dfrac{2\,784\,433}{2\,533\,781\,250}&
\dfrac{288\,320\,604\,187\,753\,560\,314\,875\,013}
      {74\,825\,186\,008\,306\,825\,515\,178\,755\,000\,000}

and hence are all lexicographically positive.  More directly, for every

Thus \(I_i=\varnothing\) or \(L\ne\inf I_i\).  In particular, none of
the three nominal equalities is a genuine lower crossing in the sense
of accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`.

## Proof

Write the vertices of \(A_2\) as
\[
q_0=\left(-\frac1{5000},\frac1{5000}\right),\quad
q_1=\left(\frac1{5000},-\frac1{5000}\right),\quad
q_2=\left(\frac16,\frac{38}{45}\right).
\]
In the notation of accepted facts `0c0621b05f1db4ff` and
`007cd9d294748c86`, the counterclockwise vertices of the terminal
hexagon are
\[
F_0,\ F_1,\ a_\star,\ F_3,\ F_4,\ F_5,
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0&=\left(\frac{1\,350\,241}{4\,455\,000},
             \frac{149\,609}{4\,455\,000}\right),&
F_1&=\left(\frac{603\,919}{1\,155\,000},
            -\frac{215\,069}{1\,155\,000}\right),\\
a_\star&=\left(
\frac{199}{300},
\frac{142\,257\,909\,327\,055\,449\,563}
     {1\,259\,537\,818\,902\,077\,227\,500}\right),&
F_3&=\left(\frac{199}{300},\frac{757}{1800}\right),\\
F_4&=\left(\frac{637}{1125},\frac{199}{300}\right),&
F_5&=\left(\frac{1\,590\,341}{3\,720\,000},
            \frac{199}{300}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The three \(A_0^\star\)-cuts at \(f_i=L\), equivalently \(y_i=p\),
and the three \(A_2\)-cuts at \(f_i=-L\), equivalently \(y_i=0\), have
the following exact types.  The last two columns list the two boundary
edges carrying the chord after moving to the required side.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
i&A_0^\star\text{ at }y_i=p&A_0^\star\text{ for }y_i<p&
A_2\text{ at }y_i=0&A_2\text{ for }y_i>0\\ \hline
1&[a_\star,F_3]\text{ support edge}&
[F_1,a_\star],[F_3,F_4]&
\text{strict interior regular cut}&[q_0,q_1],[q_0,q_2]\\
2&[F_4,F_5]\text{ support edge}&
[F_3,F_4],[F_5,F_0]&
\text{strict interior regular cut}&[q_0,q_1],[q_1,q_2]\\
3&[F_0,F_1]\text{ support edge}&
[F_5,F_0],[F_1,a_\star]&
\text{strict interior regular cut}&[q_0,q_2],[q_1,q_2]
\end{array}
\]
Indeed, the \(A_2\) vertex values are respectively
\[
\left\{-\frac1{5000},\frac1{5000},\frac16\right\},\qquad
\left\{-\frac1{5000},\frac1{5000},\frac{38}{45}\right\},\qquad
\left\{-\frac1{90},1,1\right\};
\]
none equals \(0\).  Thus no \(A_2\)-cut is a projected-vertex
breakpoint.  The displayed \(A_0^\star\) faces are genuine edges and
all other corresponding vertex values are strictly smaller than \(p\).
Hence no support-vertex convention occurs either.  At each support
edge the required interior trace is the normalized edge length, not
the exterior value \(0\).  At a support vertex the convention would
instead give trace \(0\) and the slope of the first interior chord
piece, while at an interior projected-vertex breakpoint it would use
the indicated incoming affine piece.  The exact coordinates show that
neither of those latter cases occurs here.

For \(R=A_0^\star,A_2\), let \(\ell_{Ri}(r)\) be the \(y_2\)-coordinate
span of \(R\cap\{y_1=r\}\) for \(i=1\), and the \(y_1\)-coordinate span
of \(R\cap\{y_i=r\}\) for \(i=2,3\).  For \(i=1,2\),
\(\|\nabla f_i\|=200\) and the Euclidean chord length is the indicated
coordinate span.  For \(i=3\), \(\|\nabla f_3\|=200\sqrt2\), while a
chord on \(y_1+y_2=1-r\) has Euclidean length \(\sqrt2\) times its
\(y_1\)-span.  Therefore in all three directions
\[
\rho_{Ri}(s)=\frac{\ell_{Ri}((199/3-s)/200)}{200},
\qquad
\frac{d\rho_{Ri}}{ds}=-\frac{\ell_{Ri}'}{40\,000}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the required gradient normalization.

Affine interpolation on exactly the edge pairs in the preceding table
gives the following direct chord certificate.  The derivatives are
with respect to \(r=y_i\), on the side indicated by the superscript.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}

\ell_{2i}(0)&\ell_{2i}'(0^+)\\ \hline

For example, for \(i=1\) and \(r<p\), the upper endpoint is
\[
F_3+\frac{p-r}{p-(F_4)_1}(F_4-F_3)
\]

\[
a_\star+\frac{p-r}{p-(F_1)_1}(F_1-a_\star).

Subtracting their \(y_2\)-coordinates gives the first \(A_0^\star\)
row of (2).  The other five entries follow identically from the listed
edge pairs, so (2) is directly checkable from the displayed vertices.

Substitution of (2) into (1), using the right trace at \(L\) for
\(A_0^\star\) and the left trace at \(-L\) for \(A_2\), gives the
complete density-and-slope table
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}

\]
Subtracting the density columns and adding the slope columns gives the
three pairs in the statement.  Their first entries have positive
numerators and positive denominators, so every pair is
lexicographically positive, not negative.

It remains to connect this local calculation to the definition of a
lower crossing.  The next \(A_2\) projected break after \(y_i=0\) is
\(1/5000\) for \(i=1,2\), and is \(1\) for \(i=3\).  For
\(A_0^\star\), the gaps from \(p\) to the next projected vertex are
\[
\frac{437}{4500},\qquad
\frac{437}{1800},\qquad
\frac{553\,706\,930\,168\,400\,677\,213}
     {1\,259\,537\,818\,902\,077\,227\,500},
\]
respectively, each larger than \(1/5000\).  Since changing \(f_i\) by
\(\eta\) changes \(y_i\) by \(\eta/200\), no chord piece changes for
\(0<\eta<1/25\).  The exact quadratic identity of accepted fact
`eddda25c734d34bc`, based on the polygonal regularity of accepted fact
`624b129b68e0fdee`, together with the already established
\(\mu_i(L)=H\), is therefore
\[
\mu_i(L+\eta)-H
=D_i\eta+\frac12E_i\eta^2
=\eta\left(D_i+\frac12E_i\eta\right),
\tag{4}
\]
where \((D_i,E_i)\) is the \(i\)-th pair in the statement.

For \(i=1,2\), \(E_i<0\), and direct reduction gives
\[
D_1+\frac{E_1}{50}
=\frac{166\,877\,835\,588\,688\,720\,582\,394\,717}
       {108\,979\,242\,612\,428\,208\,370\,428\,000\,000}>0,
\]
\[
D_2+\frac{E_2}{50}
=\frac{587\,115\,923\,551}{848\,360\,880\,000\,000}>0.
\]
Thus \(D_i+E_i\eta/2>0\) throughout \(0<\eta<1/25\).  For \(i=3\),
both \(D_3\) and \(E_3\) are positive, so the same conclusion is
immediate.  Equation (4) proves
\[
\mu_i(L+\eta)>H\qquad(0<\eta<1/25)
\]
in all three directions.

By definition, if \(I_i\ne\varnothing\) and \(L=\inf I_i\), then
there must be points of \(I_i\) arbitrarily close to \(L\) from the
right: every point of \(I_i\) is at least \(L\), and the defining
property of the infimum supplies one in every interval
\((L,L+\varepsilon)\).  The preceding strict inequality gives
\((L,L+1/25)\cap I_i=\varnothing\), a contradiction.  If \(I_i\) is
empty, the lower-crossing hypothesis already fails.  Hence none of the
three retained equalities is a genuine lower crossing.

All calculations above are rational.  Exact rational polygon clipping
and shoelace areas independently reproduce (4), but the displayed
vertices, active edge pairs, chord interpolation, and positive margins
are the direct certificate.
