## Statement

Use the common-plane and exact-cell convention of accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`.  Let \(N=\{j,k,\ell\}\), put
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i,\qquad q_i=f_i+L_i,\qquad
\delta=-1-(L_j+L_k+L_\ell)>0,
\]
and let
\[
\Delta
=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_k)|
=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_\ell)|
=|\det(\nabla f_k,\nabla f_\ell)|>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\) be two-dimensional convex polygons with
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume that their positive-area exact cells are precisely
\[
P_j,P_0,P_\ell,P_{k\ell},\qquad
Q_j,Q_{jk},Q_N,Q_{k\ell},\qquad
R_j,R_{jk},R_N,R_{k\ell},
\tag{3}
\]
and that all twelve displayed cells have positive area.  Write
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &P_j&P_0&P_\ell&P_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &a&b&c&d
\end{array},
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &Q_j&Q_{jk}&Q_N&Q_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &e&g_0&g&s
\end{array},
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &R_j&R_{jk}&R_N&R_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &x&y&z&w .
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

Assume the crossings, tails, finite-interior conditions, and \(W=0\)
from accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c`.  Assume also \(M\ge m\),
\(0<t<M\), and that all three crossings are genuine lower crossings in
the sense of accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc`.  Define the one-sided
coordinate traces
\[
\lambda_{0i}=\Delta\rho_{0i}^+,\qquad
\lambda_{2i}=\Delta\rho_{2i}^- .
\tag{5}
\]

Then the full sections \(A_2\cap\{q_k=0\}\) and
\(A_2\cap\{q_\ell=0\}\) belong to \(\overline{Q_{jk}}\).  Under the
affine map
\[
\Psi(v)=(-q_k(v),q_\ell(v)),
\]
put
\[
D_{k\ell}=\Psi(\overline{Q_{jk}})
\subseteq\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Its two coordinate-axis sections have lengths
\[
\mathcal H^1(D_{k\ell}\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb R))
=\lambda_{2k},
\]
\[
\mathcal H^1(D_{k\ell}\cap(\mathbb R\times\{0\}))
=\lambda_{2\ell}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently
\[
\Delta g_0=|D_{k\ell}|
\ge\frac12\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}.
\tag{8}
\]

The exact crossing and weight identities are
\[
c=g_0+y,\qquad
a+e+x=d+s+w,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
2b=5(g_0+y+d+s+w)+7(g+z).
\tag{10}
\]
The genuine lower crossings give the non-strict comparisons
\[
\lambda_{0i}\le\lambda_{2i}
\qquad(i=j,k,\ell),
\tag{11}
\]
including the equal-trace breakpoint case.  Therefore
\[
\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}
\le\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}
\le2\Delta g_0
=2\Delta(c-y)
<2\Delta c.
\tag{12}
\]

The \(k\ell\)-row separator supplied by compatibility in accepted fact
`b431dbefa8a4aec7` is automatically nondegenerate.  Put
\[
q_*=(\sqrt2-1)^2=3-2\sqrt2.
\tag{13}
\]
The strict bulging conclusion and triangle containment from that fact
imply the optimized product bound
\[
\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}
<2\Delta c\,q_*.
\tag{14}
\]

Define
\[
\eta_k=\sqrt{d(d+b)}-d,
\qquad
\eta_\ell
=\sqrt{(c+d)(b+c+d)}-(c+d).
\tag{15}
\]
The sharp chord inequalities in accepted fact
`6a9cc7c11e208bba` give
\[
\lambda_{0k}\ge\frac{2\Delta}{\delta}\eta_k,
\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}\ge\frac{2\Delta}{\delta}\eta_\ell.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every full central common-order configuration satisfies both
\[
2\Delta\eta_k\eta_\ell\le g_0\delta^2
=(c-y)\delta^2,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
2\Delta\eta_k\eta_\ell<cq_*\delta^2.
\tag{18}
\]
These are necessary conditions only.

The boundary conventions are as follows.

1. At a projected polygonal breakpoint, all traces in (5)-(8) are the
stated one-sided traces.  Because both active cutting lines meet
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\) under (3), chord continuity identifies those
traces with the full boundary chords.  No constant-chord assumption is
made.

2. Equality \(\lambda_{0i}=\lambda_{2i}\) is allowed in (11)-(18).
The separate strict negative slope sum required by
`eddda25c734d34bc` remains necessary, but no product estimate above
replaces a non-strict trace comparison by a strict one.

3. Under the full positive support (3), the four traces
\(\lambda_{0k},\lambda_{0\ell},\lambda_{2k},\lambda_{2\ell}\) are
positive, as are \(c,g_0,y\).  Hence the last inequality in (12) and
the bulging bound (14) are strict.

4. In a boundary degeneration for which the two retained axis sections
in (7) remain defined but one has length zero, the convex-hull proof of
(8) remains valid and its product lower bound is zero.  If \(g_0=0\),
it forces at least one retained axis length to vanish.  If \(y=0\),
the last comparison in (12) becomes non-strict.  If \(c=0\), (9) gives
\(g_0=y=0\).  A nondegenerate separator and the strict bulging
conclusion are asserted only when the positive-row hypotheses used
above hold.

## Proof

Since
\[
q_j+q_k+q_\ell=-(\delta+2),
\]
use coordinates
\[
X=q_j,\qquad Y=q_k,\qquad
q_\ell=-(\delta+2)-X-Y.
\tag{19}
\]
The four supported strict \(Q\)-chambers are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
Q_j&X<0,\ Y>0,\ X+Y<-(\delta+2),\\
Q_{jk}&X<0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y<-(\delta+2),\\
Q_N&X<0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y>-(\delta+2),\\
Q_{k\ell}&X>0,\ Y<0,\ X+Y>-(\delta+2).
\end{array}
\tag{20}
\]
Every positive-area exact cell contains a point of
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\) in its strict chamber.

First consider a point of \(A_2\cap\{q_k=0\}\).  If it had
\(q_\ell<0\), move it slightly toward an interior point of \(Q_j\).
When \(X\le0\), the resulting interior points lie in the unsupported
strict chamber \(Q_{j\ell}\); when \(X>0\), they lie in the unsupported
strict chamber \(Q_\ell\).  The case \(X=0\) enters \(Q_{j\ell}\).
Hence the whole section satisfies
\[
q_k=0,\qquad q_\ell\ge0,\qquad q_j<0.
\tag{21}
\]

On \(q_\ell=0\), equation (19) gives
\[
X+Y=-(\delta+2).
\]
If a section point had \(X>0\), moving it slightly toward an interior
point of \(Q_{jk}\) would enter the unsupported strict chamber \(Q_k\).
If it had \(Y>0\), moving it slightly toward an interior point of
\(Q_N\) would enter the unsupported strict chamber \(Q_{j\ell}\).
Therefore the whole section satisfies
\[
q_\ell=0,\qquad q_j\le0,\qquad q_k\le0.
\tag{22}
\]

Positive area in \(Q_j,Q_{jk}\) puts interior points of \(A_2\) on
opposite sides of \(q_k=0\).  Positive area in \(Q_{jk},Q_N\) does the
same for \(q_\ell=0\).  Both lines meet \(\operatorname{int}A_2\).
The relative interior of either chord is contained in
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\), and (21)-(22) show that every small ball
there meets the strict \(Q_{jk}\)-side.  At the possible intersection
of two cutting lines, the ball meets the strict \(Q_{jk}\)-wedge.
Taking closures proves that both full chords, including their endpoints,
belong to \(\overline{Q_{jk}}\).

The Jacobian magnitude of \(\Psi\) is \(\Delta\), so
\[
|D_{k\ell}|=\Delta g_0.
\tag{23}
\]
Along \(q_k=0\), the absolute derivative of \(q_\ell\) with respect to
Euclidean arclength is
\[
\frac{\Delta}{\|\nabla f_k\|};
\]
along \(q_\ell=0\), the absolute derivative of \(-q_k\) is
\[
\frac{\Delta}{\|\nabla f_\ell\|}.
\]
Since both levels meet the interior, polygonal chord continuity makes
these full chord lengths equal to the one-sided traces in (5).  This
proves (7), including breakpoint levels.

Write the two axis intervals as
\[
D_{k\ell}\cap\{U=0\}
=\{0\}\times[v_0,v_1],
\qquad
D_{k\ell}\cap\{V=0\}
=[u_0,u_1]\times\{0\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Their endpoints are nonnegative and
\[
v_1-v_0=\lambda_{2k},\qquad
u_1-u_0=\lambda_{2\ell}.
\tag{25}
\]
Their convex hull lies in \(D_{k\ell}\) and has area
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12(u_1v_1-u_0v_0)
=\frac12\bigl(&u_0(v_1-v_0)+v_0(u_1-u_0)\\
&+(u_1-u_0)(v_1-v_0)\bigr)\\
\ge\frac12\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (23) and (26) prove (8).  The calculation remains literal if
one interval is a singleton.

The \(k\)- and \(\ell\)-crossings in the support (3) are
\[
d+(g_0+g+s)+(y+z+w)=t,
\]
\[
(c+d)+(g+s)+(z+w)=t.
\]
Their difference gives the first identity in (9).  The \(j\)- and
\(k\)-crossing difference gives the second identity.  Expanding \(W=0\)
in the twelve cells and substituting (9) gives (10).

Accepted fact `eddda25c734d34bc` gives (11), including equality with
its strict slope-sum alternative.  Multiplying the two relevant
nonnegative comparisons and using (8)-(9) proves (12).

It remains to prove the separator and optimized bound.  Accepted fact
`b431dbefa8a4aec7` supplies nonnegative coefficients \(A,D\), not both
zero, such that
\[
AX+DY\le AU+DV
\tag{27}
\]
for every
\[
(X,Y)\in
B_{k\ell}
=\{(p_k,-p_\ell):\overline{P_\ell}\}
\]
and
\[
(U,V)\in D_{k\ell}.
\]
Positive area of \(P_\ell\) supplies a point of \(B_{k\ell}\) with
both coordinates positive.  Each axis interval in (24) has positive
length and therefore contains a point with positive nonzero coordinate.
If \(A=0\), use the positive second coordinate of the \(B_{k\ell}\)
point and a point of \(D_{k\ell}\) on \(V=0\) to contradict (27).  If
\(D=0\), use the positive first coordinate of the \(B_{k\ell}\) point
and a point of \(D_{k\ell}\) on \(U=0\).  Thus \(A,D>0\).

Let
\[
C=|B_{k\ell}|=\Delta c,\qquad
\alpha=\frac{\chi}{A},\qquad
\beta=\frac{\chi}{D},\qquad
p=\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell},
\tag{28}
\]
where \(\chi>0\) is the separator support value from
`b431dbefa8a4aec7`.  Its triangle containment and strict bulging
conclusion are
\[
C\le\frac{\alpha\beta}{2},
\qquad
C>\frac12\bigl(
\alpha\lambda_{0k}+\beta\lambda_{0\ell}+p
\bigr).
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(\alpha\beta\ge2C\), arithmetic-geometric mean gives
\[
\alpha\lambda_{0k}+\beta\lambda_{0\ell}
\ge2\sqrt{2Cp}.
\]
Therefore
\[
p+2\sqrt{2Cp}<2C.
\tag{30}
\]
With \(r=\sqrt{p/(2C)}\), this is
\[
r^2+2r<1,
\]
so \(r<\sqrt2-1\) and
\[
p<2C(3-2\sqrt2).
\]
This is (14).

Finally, accepted fact `6a9cc7c11e208bba` gives (16) for the \(k\)-
and \(\ell\)-directions of the \(A_0\) support in (3).  Multiplying
(16) and comparing first with (8),(11), then with (14), proves
(17)-(18).  These deductions establish necessary inequalities only and
complete the proof.
