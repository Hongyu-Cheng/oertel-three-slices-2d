## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, let
\[
h_K(v):=\max_{x\in K}\langle v,x\rangle,
\]
and let nonzero \(v_1,v_2,v_3\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfy
\[
v_1+v_2+v_3=0,\qquad \det(v_1,v_2)\ne0.
\]
Put
\[
P:=\bigcap_{i=1}^3\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\langle v_i,x\rangle\le h_K(v_i)\}.
\]
Then \(P\) is a nondegenerate triangle containing \(K\), and
\[
|P|=\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^3h_K(v_i)\right)^2}
{2|\det(v_1,v_2)|}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^3h_K(v_i)\right)^2
\ge 2|\det(v_1,v_2)|\,|K|.
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(K=P\), equivalently, if and only if \(K\) is the nondegenerate triangle whose three sides are the support lines
\(\langle v_i,x\rangle=h_K(v_i)\), \(i=1,2,3\).

## Proof

Write \(b_i=h_K(v_i)\), \(s=b_1+b_2+b_3\), and
\(D=\det(v_1,v_2)\).

First, the support sum and the construction have the asserted behavior under every translation. For \(a\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
h_{K+a}(v_i)
=\max_{x\in K}\langle v_i,x+a\rangle
=h_K(v_i)+\langle v_i,a\rangle.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3h_{K+a}(v_i)
=s+\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^3v_i,a\right\rangle=s.
\]
Moreover, \(x\) belongs to the intersection of the support halfplanes of
\(K+a\) if and only if
\[
\langle v_i,x-a\rangle\le h_K(v_i)\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
so that intersection is exactly \(a+P\). Thus neither the support sum nor the relevant areas depend on a choice of origin.

The number \(s\) is strictly positive. Indeed, choose \(x_0\in\operatorname{int}K\) and \(\rho>0\) such that
\(x_0+\rho B_2\subseteq K\), where \(B_2\) is the closed Euclidean unit ball. Since every \(v_i\) is nonzero,
\[
b_i\ge
\left\langle v_i,x_0+\rho\frac{v_i}{\|v_i\|}\right\rangle
=\langle v_i,x_0\rangle+\rho\|v_i\|.
\]
Summing and using \(v_1+v_2+v_3=0\) gives
\[
s\ge \rho\sum_{i=1}^3\|v_i\|>0.
\]

Now use the invertible linear coordinate map
\[
L:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,\qquad
Lx=(u,w):=(\langle v_1,x\rangle,\langle v_2,x\rangle).
\]
Its matrix has rows \(v_1^\mathsf T,v_2^\mathsf T\), hence its determinant is exactly \(D\), and its absolute area Jacobian is \(|D|\). Since
\(v_3=-v_1-v_2\), the third support inequality has the sign
\[
\langle v_3,x\rangle=-u-w\le b_3,
\]
or equivalently \(u+w\ge-b_3\). It follows that
\[
L(P)=\{(u,w):u\le b_1,\ w\le b_2,\ u+w\ge-b_3\}.
\]
Apply the affine change of variables
\[
r=b_1-u,\qquad t=b_2-w.
\]
Its linear part is \(-I_2\), so its determinant is \(1\) and its absolute area Jacobian is \(1\). The three displayed inequalities become, with no reversal beyond the explicitly recorded signs,
\[
r\ge0,\qquad t\ge0,\qquad r+t\le b_1+b_2+b_3=s.
\]
Thus \(L(P)\) is affinely congruent to the right triangle
\[
\Delta_s=\{(r,t):r\ge0,\ t\ge0,\ r+t\le s\}.
\]
Because \(s>0\), this is a nondegenerate compact triangle, of area
\[
|\Delta_s|=\frac{s^2}{2}.
\]
Consequently \(P\) is a nondegenerate bounded triangle and, using
\(|L(P)|=|D|\,|P|\),
\[
|P|=\frac{|L(P)|}{|D|}
=\frac{s^2}{2|D|}
=\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^3h_K(v_i)\right)^2}
{2|\det(v_1,v_2)|}.
\]

For every \(x\in K\), the definition of the support function gives
\(\langle v_i,x\rangle\le h_K(v_i)\) for all \(i\), hence \(K\subseteq P\).
Therefore \(|K|\le|P|\), and multiplying this inequality by
\(2|D|>0\) proves
\[
s^2\ge2|D|\,|K|.
\]

It remains to identify equality without any regularity assumption on the boundary of \(K\). If \(K=P\), equality is immediate. Conversely, suppose
\(|K|=|P|\). If \(K\ne P\), choose \(z\in P\setminus K\). Since \(K\) is closed, some open ball \(B(z,\varepsilon)\) is disjoint from \(K\). Choose
\(y\in\operatorname{int}P\). For sufficiently small \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), the point
\[
z_\lambda=(1-\lambda)z+\lambda y
\]
lies in \(\operatorname{int}P\cap B(z,\varepsilon)\). A sufficiently small open ball about \(z_\lambda\) is then contained in
\(P\cap B(z,\varepsilon)\), so \(P\setminus K\) has positive area. This contradicts \(|K|=|P|\). Hence equality forces \(K=P\). Since the three defining inequalities of \(P\) are nonredundant sides of the nondegenerate triangle computed above, this is equivalent to the stated support-line characterization.
