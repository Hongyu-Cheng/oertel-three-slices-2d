## Statement

Assume the locked three-slice problem, put \(t=2T/9\), and, for
\(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), define
\[
d_i(x)=\inf\{|A_i\cap P|:P\text{ is a closed planar halfplane containing }x\}
\]
and
\[
D_i(u)=\{x\in A_i:d_i(x)\geq u\}.
\]
Define the optimized affine-transversal value
\[
\Gamma=
\max_{\substack{x_0\in A_0\\x_2\in A_2}}
\left[
d_1\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
+\min\{d_0(x_0),d_2(x_2)\}
\right].
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Gamma=
\max\left\{
u+v:
u,v\geq0,\quad
\left(\frac{D_0(u)+D_2(u)}2\right)\cap D_1(v)\neq\varnothing
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then some \(z\in A_1\) satisfies \(h_S((1,z))\geq\Gamma\). In
particular, (1)-(2) are the exact optimization of this affine-transversal
method, and \(\Gamma\geq t\) is a sufficient depth-region condition.

Let
\[
\delta_i=\max_{x\in A_i}d_i(x)\qquad(i=0,2)
\]
and
\[
\Phi(p,q)=\frac{(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2}{4}+\min\{p,q\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The exact best value obtained by varying the two endpoint anchors but using
only the square-root middle-depth conclusion of accepted fact
`665c08dbed9743ca` is
\[
\max_{\substack{x_0\in A_0\\x_2\in A_2}}
\Phi(d_0(x_0),d_2(x_2))
=\Phi(\delta_0,\delta_2).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently \(\Gamma\geq\Phi(\delta_0,\delta_2)\). The area-only
specialization used in accepted fact `fbd25afea87ffbac` is exactly
\[
\Phi\!\left(\frac{4a_0}{9},\frac{4a_2}{9}\right)
=
\frac{(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2+4m}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]
For a triangle of area \(a\), the maximum planar depth is exactly \(4a/9\).
Thus moving endpoint anchors cannot improve (5) for triangular outer slices
if the middle depth is replaced only by square-root concavity.

Nevertheless, (2) can be strictly stronger than (5), even in the residual
domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` and below the endpoint-ratio
cutoff of accepted fact `291c24bb45bd4946`. Let
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
A_0=A_1=K,\qquad A_2=\frac12K,
\tag{6}
\]
and let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
Then the locked conclusion holds at the middle centroid
\[
g_1=\left(\frac23,\frac13\right)
\]
by the affine-transversal certificate (2), using
\[
x_0=\left(\frac9{10},\frac9{20}\right),
\qquad
x_2=\left(\frac{13}{30},\frac{13}{60}\right),
\qquad
\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}=g_1.
\tag{8}
\]
More precisely,
\[
h_S((1,g_1))\geq\frac{113}{225}>\frac12=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]

## Proof

First note that a minimizing halfplane in the definition of \(d_i(x)\) may
be translated until its boundary passes through \(x\). Hence
\[
d_i(x)=
\min_{q\in\mathbb S^1}
\left|A_i\cap\{y:q\cdot y\leq q\cdot x\}\right|.
\tag{10}
\]
The cap area on the right is jointly continuous in \((q,x)\): under
convergence of \((q,x)\), its indicators converge away from a line, and a
line has planar area zero. Compactness of \(\mathbb S^1\) makes \(d_i\)
continuous. Therefore all maxima below are attained.

For any \(x_0\in A_0\) and \(x_2\in A_2\), put
\(z=(x_0+x_2)/2\). The three-slice affine-transversal conclusion of accepted
fact `665c08dbed9743ca` gives
\[
h_S((1,z))
\geq d_1(z)+\min\{d_0(x_0),d_2(x_2)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Maximizing (11) proves the assertion following (1).

For a fixed pair \(x_0,x_2\), set
\[
u=\min\{d_0(x_0),d_2(x_2)\},
\qquad v=d_1((x_0+x_2)/2).
\]
Then \((x_0+x_2)/2\) belongs to the intersection in (2), so the right side
of (2) is at least (1). Conversely, a point in the intersection in (2) has
the form \((x_0+x_2)/2\), with \(d_0(x_0),d_2(x_2)\geq u\) and middle
depth at least \(v\); its value in (1) is at least \(u+v\). This proves
(2).

Square-root slice-depth concavity in accepted fact
`665c08dbed9743ca` also gives
\[
d_1\!\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
\geq
\frac{(\sqrt{d_0(x_0)}+\sqrt{d_2(x_2)})^2}{4}.
\tag{12}
\]
The function \(\Phi\) is nondecreasing in each argument. Since the endpoint
anchors are independent and points attaining \(\delta_0,\delta_2\) exist,
(4) follows. Equations (11)-(12) then give
\(\Gamma\geq\Phi(\delta_0,\delta_2)\). Direct substitution gives (5),
which is precisely the outer-centroid affine-transfer value in accepted fact
`fbd25afea87ffbac`.

We next prove the exact triangle-depth formula used both for sharpness and
for the example. Let \(K'\) be a triangle of area \(a\), and let \(x\) have
barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\). Then
\[
d_{K'}(x)=
4a\min_{0\leq i<j\leq2}\lambda_i\lambda_j.
\tag{13}
\]
It suffices by (10) to consider a line through \(x\). Suppose first that the
chosen side contains exactly vertex \(i\). If the line meets the two edges
from \(i\) at fractions \(r,s\) from \(i\), then, for some
\(\theta\in[0,1]\),
\[
\lambda_j=\theta r,\qquad
\lambda_k=(1-\theta)s.
\]
The cap has area fraction
\[
rs=\frac{\lambda_j\lambda_k}{\theta(1-\theta)}
\geq4\lambda_j\lambda_k
\geq4\min_{p<q}\lambda_p\lambda_q.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose instead that the chosen side contains exactly two vertices and
excludes \(i\). The opposite one-vertex cap has the same parametrization, now
with
\[
\lambda_j\leq\theta\leq1-\lambda_k.
\]
Since \(\theta(1-\theta)\) is concave, the chosen two-vertex side has area
fraction at least
\[
\frac{\lambda_i}
{\lambda_i+\max\{\lambda_j,\lambda_k\}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Write the three coordinates in increasing order as
\(\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma\). If the excluded coordinate in (15) is
\(\alpha\), the lower bound is
\(\alpha/(1-\beta)\geq4\alpha\beta\), because
\(4\beta(1-\beta)\leq1\). If it is \(\beta\), the bound is
\(\beta/(1-\alpha)\geq4\alpha\beta\). If it is \(\gamma\), the bound is at
least \(1/2\), while
\(\alpha\beta\leq1/9\), so it is again at least \(4\alpha\beta\).
Degenerate incidences follow by continuity. This proves the lower bound in
(13).

For the reverse inequality, choose the vertex having barycentric coordinate
\(\gamma\), and cut its two incident edges at fractions
\(2\alpha\) and \(2\beta\). These fractions are at most one, their joining
line passes through \(x\), and the resulting cap has area fraction
\(4\alpha\beta\). Thus (13) holds. Its maximum is \(4a/9\), attained at
\(\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\lambda_2=1/3\). This proves the triangular
sharpness assertion following (5).

It remains to verify the exact residual example. The areas in (6) are
\[
a_0=a_1=1,\qquad a_2=\frac14,\qquad T=\frac94.
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac34K\subseteq K=A_1.
\]
Consequently the section of the convex hull (7) at height \(1\) is exactly
\(A_1\), its endpoint sections are \(A_0,A_2\), and its \(x\)-projection is
\([0,2]\). Thus (7) satisfies the locked compatibility and has exactly the
three required integer slices.

Normalize as in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. Here
\[
r=\frac14,\qquad s=1.
\]
Because \(r\leq9/25\), the strict residual inequalities are
\[
1-r<s<1+r,
\]
and indeed \(3/4<1<5/4\). Also \(r=1/4<3/8\), so the instance lies below
the necessary endpoint-ratio cutoff of accepted fact
`291c24bb45bd4946`. The sufficient inequality in accepted fact
`fbd25afea87ffbac` would read
\[
2\leq2\sqrt{\frac14}+3\left(\frac14\right)-1=\frac34,
\]
which is false. Thus that accepted area criterion does not certify this
instance.

Relative to \(A_0=K\), the point \(x_0\) in (8) has barycentric coordinates
\[
\left(\frac1{10},\frac9{20},\frac9{20}\right).
\]
Relative to \(A_2=\frac12K\), the point \(x_2\) has barycentric coordinates
\[
\left(\frac2{15},\frac{13}{30},\frac{13}{30}\right).
\]
Equation (13) therefore gives
\[
d_0(x_0)=\frac9{50},
\qquad
d_2(x_2)=\frac{13}{225}.
\tag{17}
\]
The midpoint calculation in (8) is exact, and \(g_1\) is the centroid of
\(A_1\), so (13) also gives
\[
d_1(g_1)=\frac49.
\tag{18}
\]
Applying (11) to these two non-centroid anchors yields
\[
h_S((1,g_1))
\geq
\frac49+\min\left\{\frac9{50},\frac{13}{225}\right\}
=\frac{113}{225}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\frac{113}{225}-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{113}{225}-\frac12
=\frac1{450}>0,
\]
which proves (9). This also exhibits the nonempty depth-region intersection
in (2) with \(u=13/225\) and \(v=4/9\). Hence pure square-root replacement
collapses exactly to the accepted area value on triangular endpoints, but
varying anchors together with an independently deep middle point can
certify a genuine residual instance beyond that value.
