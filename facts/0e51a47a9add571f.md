## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), write \(0\) for \(\varnothing\), and abbreviate subsets by concatenating their elements, for example \(12=\{1,2\}\). Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on \(\mathbb R^2\) with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\), and let \(A_0,A_2,K\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
(A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K.
\]
For \(J\subseteq N\), define
\[
P_J=A_0\cap\{f_j\le L_j\ (j\in J),\ f_j>L_j\ (j\notin J)\},
\]
\[
Q_J=A_2\cap\{f_j\le-L_j\ (j\in J),\ f_j>-L_j\ (j\notin J)\},
\]
\[
R_J=K\cap\{f_j\le0\ (j\in J),\ f_j>0\ (j\notin J)\}.
\]

The only cells forced empty independently of the positions of the three polytopes are
\[
P_{123}=\varnothing,\qquad Q_0=\varnothing,\qquad R_0=\varnothing.
\]
Every other corresponding ambient sign chamber in \(\mathbb R^2\) has nonempty interior, although its intersection with a particular polytope may be empty.

For \(J\ne123\) and \(H\ne0\), put
\[
\mathcal G(J,H)=\{G\subseteq N:G\ne0,\ J\cap H\subseteq G\subseteq J\cup H\}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{P_J+Q_H}{2}\subseteq\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal G(J,H)}R_G.
\]
If \(G\notin\mathcal G(J,H)\), no midpoint of a point of \(P_J\) and a point of \(Q_H\) can belong to \(R_G\).

In the following complete table, an entry lists the members of \(\mathcal G(J,H)\), while “--” denotes a pair forced empty by \(P_{123}=\varnothing\) or \(Q_0=\varnothing\).

| \(J\backslash H\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(2\) | \(3\) | \(12\) | \(13\) | \(23\) | \(123\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | -- | \(\{1\}\) | \(\{2\}\) | \(\{3\}\) | \(\{1,2,12\}\) | \(\{1,3,13\}\) | \(\{2,3,23\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) |
| \(1\) | -- | \(\{1\}\) | \(\{1,2,12\}\) | \(\{1,3,13\}\) | \(\{1,12\}\) | \(\{1,13\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{1,12,13,123\}\) |
| \(2\) | -- | \(\{1,2,12\}\) | \(\{2\}\) | \(\{2,3,23\}\) | \(\{2,12\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{2,23\}\) | \(\{2,12,23,123\}\) |
| \(3\) | -- | \(\{1,3,13\}\) | \(\{2,3,23\}\) | \(\{3\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{3,13\}\) | \(\{3,23\}\) | \(\{3,13,23,123\}\) |
| \(12\) | -- | \(\{1,12\}\) | \(\{2,12\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{12\}\) | \(\{1,12,13,123\}\) | \(\{2,12,23,123\}\) | \(\{12,123\}\) |
| \(13\) | -- | \(\{1,13\}\) | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{3,13\}\) | \(\{1,12,13,123\}\) | \(\{13\}\) | \(\{3,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{13,123\}\) |
| \(23\) | -- | \(\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{2,23\}\) | \(\{3,23\}\) | \(\{2,12,23,123\}\) | \(\{3,13,23,123\}\) | \(\{23\}\) | \(\{23,123\}\) |
| \(123\) | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |

The target union has the equivalent form
\[
\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal G(J,H)}R_G
=
K\cap\{f_i\le0\ (i\in J\cap H),\ f_i>0\ (i\notin J\cup H)\}.
\]
It is therefore convex even when it contains several exact cells.

Among non-forced endpoint pairs, \(\mathcal G(J,H)\) is a singleton exactly for
\[
(J,H)=(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(12,12),(13,13),(23,23).
\]
Consequently, the complete list of universally forced single-cell Minkowski inclusions is
\[
\frac{P_0+Q_1}{2}\subseteq R_1,\qquad
\frac{P_0+Q_2}{2}\subseteq R_2,\qquad
\frac{P_0+Q_3}{2}\subseteq R_3,
\]
\[
\frac{P_1+Q_1}{2}\subseteq R_1,\qquad
\frac{P_2+Q_2}{2}\subseteq R_2,\qquad
\frac{P_3+Q_3}{2}\subseteq R_3,
\]
\[
\frac{P_{12}+Q_{12}}{2}\subseteq R_{12},\qquad
\frac{P_{13}+Q_{13}}{2}\subseteq R_{13},\qquad
\frac{P_{23}+Q_{23}}{2}\subseteq R_{23}.
\]
Every other non-forced pair has at least two sharply realizable exact middle types, so no additional single exact cell is universally forced.

## Proof

Write
\[
s=L_1+L_2+L_3,\qquad \delta=-1-s>0.
\]
The affine map
\[
F:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\{(r_1,r_2,r_3):r_1+r_2+r_3=-1\},
\qquad
F(x)=(f_1(x),f_2(x),f_3(x)),
\]
is an affine isomorphism. Indeed, its image lies in the displayed plane, and two of the pairwise nonparallel linear parts are linearly independent, so its linear part has rank two.

For the \(P\)-arrangement, put \(p_i=f_i-L_i\). Then
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3=\delta>0.
\]
Thus \(P_{123}\) is empty, since there all three \(p_i\le0\). Conversely, if \(J\ne123\), the vector defined by
\[
p_i=-1\quad(i\in J),\qquad
p_i=\frac{\delta+|J|}{3-|J|}\quad(i\notin J)
\]
has sum \(\delta\) and lies strictly in the sign chamber indexed by \(J\). Since \(F\) is onto the plane of coordinate sum \(-1\), that ambient chamber has nonempty interior.

For the \(Q\)-arrangement, put \(q_i=f_i+L_i\). Then
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=s-1=-(\delta+2)<0.
\]
Hence \(Q_0\) is empty. If \(H\ne0\), the choice
\[
q_i=1\quad(i\notin H),\qquad
q_i=-\frac{\delta+2+3-|H|}{|H|}\quad(i\in H)
\]
has the required sum and lies strictly in the chamber indexed by \(H\).

For the middle arrangement, the coordinate sum is \(-1\), so \(R_0\) is empty. If \(G\ne0\), choosing
\[
f_i=1\quad(i\notin G),\qquad
f_i=-\frac{1+3-|G|}{|G|}\quad(i\in G)
\]
gives a point strictly in the corresponding ambient chamber. This proves all forced-empty and ambient-realizability assertions.

Now take \(x\in P_J\), \(y\in Q_H\), and \(z=(x+y)/2\). Compatibility gives \(z\in K\). Affinity gives, for every \(i\),
\[
2f_i(z)=\bigl(f_i(x)-L_i\bigr)+\bigl(f_i(y)+L_i\bigr).
\]
If \(i\in J\cap H\), both terms on the right are nonpositive, so \(f_i(z)\le0\). If \(i\notin J\cup H\), both terms are strictly positive, so \(f_i(z)>0\). Therefore, for
\[
G(z)=\{i:f_i(z)\le0\},
\]
one has
\[
J\cap H\subseteq G(z)\subseteq J\cup H.
\]
Also \(G(z)\ne0\), since otherwise all three \(f_i(z)\) would be positive, contradicting their sum \(-1\). Hence \(G(z)\in\mathcal G(J,H)\), proving every inclusion and every impossible incidence in the table.

Conversely, let \(J\ne123\), \(H\ne0\), and
\[
0\ne G,\qquad J\cap H\subseteq G\subseteq J\cup H.
\]
We show that this incidence alternative is realizable with strict inequalities. Set
\[
A=J\cap H,\qquad U=J\setminus H,\qquad V=H\setminus J,\qquad
O=N\setminus(J\cup H).
\]
We seek vectors \(p,q,c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
\sum_i p_i=\delta,\qquad
\sum_i q_i=-(\delta+2),\qquad
c=p+q,
\]
with negative-coordinate sets \(J,H,G\), respectively. First choose nonzero \(c_i\) with
\[
\sum_i c_i=-2,\qquad c_i<0\iff i\in G.
\]
For fixed \(c\), the required signs of \(p\) and \(q=c-p\) are equivalent to choosing each \(p_i\) in the following open interval:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{index class}&\text{allowed values of }p_i\\ \hline
A&(c_i,0)\\
O&(0,c_i)\\
U\cap G&(-\infty,c_i)\\
U\setminus G&(-\infty,0)\\
V\cap G&(0,\infty)\\
V\setminus G&(c_i,\infty).
\end{array}
\]
The possible values of \(\sum_i p_i\) therefore form an open interval \((\ell(c),u(c))\), where
\[
\ell(c)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&U\ne0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in A}c_i+\sum_{i\in V\setminus G}c_i,&U=0,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
u(c)=
\begin{cases}
+\infty,&V\ne0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in O}c_i+\sum_{i\in U\cap G}c_i,&V=0.
\end{cases}
\]

The vector \(c\) can always be chosen so that
\[
\ell(c)<\delta<u(c).
\]
If both \(U\) and \(V\) are nonempty, both endpoints are infinite, so any \(c\) with the prescribed signs and sum works. If \(U\ne0\) and \(V=0\), then \(H=A\ne0\) and \(O=N\setminus J\ne0\). Keep the negative coordinates in \(U\cap G\) bounded, make the positive sum over \(O\) arbitrarily large, and compensate in one negative coordinate of \(A\). This preserves \(\sum_i c_i=-2\) and makes \(u(c)>\delta\). If \(U=0\) and \(V\ne0\), choose the positive coordinates in \(V\setminus G\) with total less than \(\delta\), and compensate in one negative coordinate of the nonempty set \(G\). Then \(\ell(c)<\delta\). Finally, if \(U=V=0\), then \(J=H=G\) is nonempty and proper. Choose the positive coordinates in \(O\) with sum greater than \(\delta\), and choose the negative coordinates in \(J\) to make the total \(-2\). Then again \(\ell(c)<\delta<u(c)\).

Choose \(p_i\) in the displayed intervals with \(\sum_i p_i=\delta\), and set \(q=c-p\). Then \(p,q,c\) have exactly the required strict signs and
\[
\sum_iq_i=-2-\delta=s-1.
\]
The vectors
\[
(L_1+p_1,L_2+p_2,L_3+p_3)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(-L_1+q_1,-L_2+q_2,-L_3+q_3)
\]
both have coordinate sum \(-1\). Surjectivity of \(F\) therefore gives points \(x,y\in\mathbb R^2\) with these respective \(F\)-images. They lie strictly in the ambient \(P_J\)- and \(Q_H\)-chambers, and \(z=(x+y)/2\) lies strictly in the ambient \(R_G\)-chamber because \(2F(z)=c\).

To realize the alternative with admissible polytopes, take a sufficiently small two-dimensional square \(B\) about the origin and set
\[
A_0=x+B,\qquad A_2=y+B,\qquad K=z+B.
\]
Strictness permits \(B\) to be chosen so that these three polytopes lie in the respective chambers. Moreover,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=z+\frac{B+B}{2}=z+B=K.
\]
Thus every entry in the table is sharp, and no additional target type can be excluded using the stated affine and offset hypotheses.

The union formula follows because its right-hand side imposes exactly the signs fixed for every \(G\in\mathcal G(J,H)\), while the remaining coordinates are unrestricted; the globally impossible type \(G=0\) adds no point. It is an intersection of \(K\) with open or closed halfspaces and is therefore convex.

Finally, if \(J\cap H\ne0\), then
\[
|\mathcal G(J,H)|=2^{|J\mathbin{\triangle}H|},
\]
so it is one exactly when \(J=H\). The valid diagonal choices are \(1,2,3,12,13,23\). If \(J\cap H=0\), then
\[
|\mathcal G(J,H)|=2^{|J\cup H|}-1,
\]
so it is one exactly when \(J\cup H\) is a singleton. Since \(H\ne0\) and the two sets are disjoint, this gives \((J,H)=(0,1),(0,2),(0,3)\). This proves that the nine displayed inclusions are exhaustive.

Each cutting set \(f_i=L_i\), \(f_i=-L_i\), or \(f_i=0\) is a line because \(f_i\) is nonconstant. Its intersection with a two-dimensional polytope has planar area zero. Hence replacing a weak inequality by a strict one on a cutting line does not change any cell area. The stated \(\le\)/\(>\) convention nevertheless gives an exact disjoint partition and assigns a midpoint with \(f_i=0\) to the cell whose index contains \(i\).
