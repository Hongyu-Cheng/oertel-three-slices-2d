## Statement

Let
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\qquad a_j=|A_j|,\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\]
where \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) are convex bodies of positive area and \(A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\) is a nondegenerate triangle. Put
\[
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\},\qquad M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
Let \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) be a normalized strict three-halfspace certificate supplied by accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, so
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\leq0\}
\]
and the three open middle-section halfplanes cover \(\mathbb R^2\). Assume that, after relabeling the vertices, there are \(b_i\in[0,1]\) such that, for the barycentric coordinates \(\beta_i\) of \(A_1\),
\[
\{w:f_i(w)\leq0\}=\{w:\beta_i(w)\geq b_i\}=:L_i.
\]
Then
\[
\max_{1\leq i\leq3}\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
\geq \frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac m3.
\]
Consequently, if \(M-a_1\leq m/2\), then
\[
\max_{1\leq i\leq3}\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Thus no global counterexample can have such a strict certificate.

## Proof

For each \(i\), define the middle-cap area and the two endpoint areas by
\[
p_i=|A_1\cap L_i|,\qquad
x_i=|\{u\in A_0:f_i(u)-s_i\leq0\}|,\qquad
y_i=|\{v\in A_2:f_i(v)+s_i\leq0\}|.
\]
The sections of \(H_i\) at heights \(0,1,2\) are exactly the three sets occurring in these definitions. Since the three slices are disjoint,
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)=p_i+x_i+y_i.
\]
Hence
\[
e_i:=x_i+y_i\geq0
\]
is the actual endpoint contribution of the same halfspace whose middle contribution is \(p_i\); it is therefore a valid bonus paired with \(p_i\), not an independently chosen quantity. Accepted fact `0b5d84877f51b7c3`, applied to the normalized certificate from `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3 e_i=\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i+y_i)\geq m.
\]

The open middle sections are \(\{\beta_i>b_i\}\) and cover \(\mathbb R^2\), so their closures \(L_1,L_2,L_3\) also cover \(\mathbb R^2\). Accepted fact `79125fb2149eb8c2`, applied once to the same indexed pairs \((p_i,e_i)\), therefore yields
\[
\max_i\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
=\max_i(p_i+e_i)
\geq\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac13\sum_i e_i
\geq\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac m3.
\]
This proves the displayed mass bound without any individual lower bound on an \(e_i\).

In fact, the first inequality is strict for a strict certificate. Equality in the edge-frame inequality of `79125fb2149eb8c2` would require \(b_1=b_2=b_3=1/3\). But the point with barycentric coordinates \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) then lies in none of the open middle sections \(\{\beta_i>b_i\}\), contradicting their cover. Consequently,
\[
\max_i\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)
>\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac13\sum_i e_i
\geq\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac m3.
\]
The strict middle cover therefore excludes the sole edge-frame equality pattern, so equality in the displayed mass bound cannot occur here.

Finally, \(T=a_1+m+M\), and direct subtraction gives
\[
\frac{4a_1}{9}+\frac m3-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{m-2(M-a_1)}9.
\]
Thus \(M-a_1\leq m/2\), together with the preceding strict mass inequality, gives
\[
\max_i\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
The algebraic comparison is an equality exactly on the boundary \(M-a_1=m/2\), but the edge-frame inequality remains strict there because the middle interiors cover the plane. Hence there is no equality in the full mass chain. This contradicts the strict inequalities \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H_i)<2T/9\) required of the certificate in `d2d117bcc58a1ba0`, and the stated area regime is closed, including its boundary.
