## Statement

Assume the actual dominant all-double common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell
convention of accepted facts 29dfdfc94477e532 and f5bb496aa51a2cf9.
Thus \(A_0,A_2,K\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
the positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
and
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\qquad
x_1>\frac{5m}{9}.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,
\]
\[
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123},
\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
The three crossings hold, so
\[
c_i=h-u_i-m+x_i.
\tag{1}
\]
The all-double \(W=0\) identity gives
\[
2M=4m+k+3(U+q+E_R),
\tag{2}
\]
and hence
\[
h=\frac{2(M-m-U-q-E_R)}3.
\tag{3}
\]

Use the dominant bodies of 29dfdfc94477e532:
\[
P=\overline{P_\varnothing},\qquad p=|P|=p_\varnothing,
\]
\[
C=C_1^\sharp,\qquad d=|C|,
\qquad
S_1=\frac{P+C}{2},
\]
\[
\eta_1>0,\qquad
\mathcal V=\mathcal V(P,C),
\qquad
|S_1|=\frac14(p+d+2\mathcal V),
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\frac{4x_1}{9}\leq d\leq\frac{5x_1}{9},
\qquad
\mathcal V\geq\sqrt{pd}.
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
D_0=M-p.
\]
Then
\[
D_0\leq U.
\tag{6}
\]

Assume the exterior alternative of 29dfdfc94477e532.  Uniformly cover
both versions as follows.  Choose distinct
\[
j,\ell\in\{2,3\}
\]
so that
\[
S_1\subseteq
\{f_\ell\geq0,\ f_j\leq-1-\eta_1/2\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \((j,\ell)=(3,2)\) is the displayed
\((\theta_2,\theta_3)=(0,1)\) case, while \((j,\ell)=(2,3)\) is its
\(2/3\)-symmetric version.

Let
\[
r=r_{123}>0,\qquad
d_1=\min_{S_1}f_1,
\]
choose \(z_1\in S_1\) with \(f_1(z_1)=d_1\), and put
\[
a=f_\ell(z_1).
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
d_1\geq\frac{\eta_1}{2}>0,\qquad a\geq0.
\tag{9}
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal T_1=\{f_1,f_2,f_3\leq0\}\subseteq K,
\qquad |\mathcal T_1|=r,
\]
\[
E_1=\mathcal T_1\cap\{f_1=0\},
\qquad
G_1=\operatorname{conv}(E_1\cup\{z_1\}).
\tag{10}
\]
Then the following exact charges hold:
\[
\boxed{\quad
r_j\geq
|S_1|+\frac{rd_1a}{1+a},
\qquad
r_{23}\geq\frac{rd_1}{1+a}.
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\quad
c_j\geq |S_1|+rd_1,
\qquad
E_R\geq2r+\frac{rd_1}{1+a}.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]

The \(j\)-th crossing gives the exact mixed-area endpoint
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal V
\leq
\frac{5p}{6}
+\frac{4(D_0-U)}3
-\frac{10m}{3}
-\frac{4(q+E_R)}3
+2x_j-2u_j
-\frac d2-2rd_1.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, by (6),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\sqrt{pd}\leq\mathcal V\leq\mathscr C_j,
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
\mathscr C_j=
\frac{5p}{6}
-\frac{10m}{3}
-\frac{4(q+E_R)}3
+2x_j-2u_j
-\frac d2-2rd_1.
\tag{15}
\]
The second inequality in (12) may be substituted into (15), giving the
still stronger necessary bound
\[
\mathscr C_j
\leq
\frac{5p}{6}
-\frac{10m}{3}
-\frac{4}{3}\left(
q+2r+\frac{rd_1}{1+a}
\right)
+2x_j-2u_j

\tag{16}

There is also a lossless full-support ledger.  Put

C_j=\overline{Q_{N\setminus\{j\}}},

\boxed{\quad

## Proof

Accepted fact 29dfdfc94477e532 gives (4)--(7), including the exact
mixed-area identity and Minkowski lower endpoint.  Accepted fact
f5bb496aa51a2cf9 gives (9)--(10), the containment \(G_1\subseteq K\),
\[
|G_1|=rd_1,
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
|G_1\cap S_1|=0.
\tag{22}
\]

We first audit the exact cells in the exterior case.  By (7) and the
strict inequality \(f_1\geq\eta_1/2>0\) on \(S_1\), every point of
\(S_1\), except for a null subset of the line \(f_\ell=0\), has signs
\[
f_1>0,\qquad f_\ell>0,\qquad f_j<0.
\]
Thus all of the area of \(S_1\) lies in \(R_j\).

The affine map
\[
z\longmapsto(f_1(z),f_\ell(z))
\tag{23}
\]
is an affine isomorphism.  Indeed, the linear parts of \(f_1\) and
\(f_\ell\) are nonparallel.  In these coordinates the central triangle
\(\mathcal T_1\) has vertices
\[
(-1,0),\qquad(0,-1),\qquad(0,0),
\]
after ordering the last two vertices so that the second coordinate is
\(f_\ell\).  Its edge \(E_1\) is the segment joining
\[
(0,-1)\quad\hbox{to}\quad(0,0),
\]
whereas the bridge apex is
\[
(d_1,a).
\]
The coordinate area of \(\mathcal T_1\) is \(1/2\), while its actual
area is \(r\).  Hence coordinate areas are multiplied by \(2r\).

The line \(f_\ell=0\) joins \((0,0)\) to the point
\[
\left(\frac{d_1}{1+a},0\right)
\]
on the segment from \((0,-1)\) to \((d_1,a)\).  Therefore the part of
\(G_1\) with \(f_\ell>0\) has coordinate area
\[
\frac{d_1a}{2(1+a)},
\]
while the part with \(f_\ell\leq0\) has coordinate area
\[
\frac{d_1}{2(1+a)}.
\]
After multiplication by \(2r\), these areas are respectively
\[
\frac{rd_1a}{1+a},
\qquad
\frac{rd_1}{1+a}.
\tag{24}
\]
The bridge has \(f_1>0\) off its null base and \(f_j\leq0\) everywhere,
because both its base and its apex have \(f_j\leq0\).  Thus the two
parts in (24) lie respectively in \(R_j\) and \(R_{23}\), up to cutting
lines.  Combining this with (22) and the full \(R_j\)-charge of \(S_1\)
proves (11).  Both cells in (11) are counted in \(c_j\), and
\(r_{23}\) is counted once in \(E_R\), while \(r\) is counted twice.
This proves (12).

By the all-double support, the \(j\)-th \(Q\)-marginal is
\[
v_j=m-x_j.
\]
The crossing is therefore exactly (1).  The first inequality in (12)
and (4) give
\[
\frac14(p+d+2\mathcal V)+rd_1
\leq
h-u_j-m+x_j.
\]
Solving for \(\mathcal V\) yields
\[
\mathcal V
\leq
2h-2u_j-2m+2x_j-\frac{p+d}{2}-2rd_1.
\tag{25}
\]
Substitute (3), write \(M=p+D_0\), and collect terms.  This gives
exactly (13).  Equation (6) gives the upper endpoint in (14), while
the lower endpoint is (5).  Substitution of (12) gives (16).

We next prove the full-support ledger.  Every point of \(P\) satisfies
\[
f_j\geq L_j,
\]
and every point of \(C_j\), which is the closure of the \(Q\)-cell
omitting \(j\), satisfies
\[
f_j\geq-L_j.
\]
Consequently midpoint compatibility gives
\[
B_j\subseteq K\cap\{f_j\geq0\}.
\tag{26}
\]
On the other hand, (7) gives \(S_1\subseteq\{f_j<0\}\), and the bridge
construction above gives \(G_1\subseteq\{f_j\leq0\}\).  Hence \(B_j\)
meets \(S_1\cup G_1\) only on the line \(f_j=0\), which has area zero.
Equation (22) proves the remaining pairwise area-disjointness.  All three
sets lie in \(K\), so their areas add.  The mixed-area formula applied
to \(B_j=(P+C_j)/2\), together with (4) and (21), proves (18).
Minkowski's first inequality
\[
\mathcal V(P,C_j)\geq\sqrt{px_j}
\]
and (5) then prove (19).

It remains to derive the universal ratio consequence.  From (15), using
\[
q>0,\quad E_R>0,\quad u_j\geq0,\quad rd_1>0,
\]
\[
x_j<m-x_1,
\qquad
d\geq\frac{4x_1}{9},
\qquad
x_1>\frac{5m}{9},
\]
we obtain strictly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr C_j
&<
\frac{5p}{6}
-\frac{10m}{3}
+2(m-x_1)
-\frac{2x_1}{9}\\
&<
\frac{5p}{6}-\frac{208m}{81}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
At the same time, (5) gives strictly
\[
\sqrt{pd}>
\sqrt{\frac{20pm}{81}}.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (14), (27), and (28) shows
\[
\sqrt{\frac{20pm}{81}}
<
\frac{5p}{6}-\frac{208m}{81}.
\tag{29}
\]
In particular, the right side is positive.  Put \(t=p/m\) and
\(y=\sqrt t\).  Multiplying (29) by \(162/m\) gives
\[
135y^2-36\sqrt5\,y-416>0.
\]
Its positive root is
\[
\frac{36\sqrt5+\sqrt{231120}}{270}
=
\frac{6\sqrt5+2\sqrt{1605}}{45}.
\]
Thus \(y\) is strictly larger than this root, and squaring proves (20).
The argument is symmetric in \(2,3\), so it covers both exterior
alternatives.

All weak/strict discrepancies above lie on one of the affine cutting
lines.  Since every \(f_i\) is nonconstant and all bodies are
two-dimensional, those lines have planar area zero.  Hence every charge,
disjointness assertion, and mixed-area identity respects the exact-cell
convention.
