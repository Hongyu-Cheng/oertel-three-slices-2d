## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad g=(1/3,1/3),
\qquad \delta_0=10^{-8}.
\]
Suppose \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a compact two-dimensional convex body
such that
\[
|K\mathbin{\triangle}\Delta|\leq\delta_0|K|.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\varphi_3\) be proper affine functions.  Suppose
their three vertex-value vectors have Euclidean norm \(1\) and, after
relabeling, lie within \(1/1000\) of the three permutations of
\[
\frac{(2,-1,-1)}{\sqrt6}.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
d_i=-\varphi_i(g)
\]
and
\[
L_i(u)=
\frac{\mathcal H^1(K\cap
\{\varphi_i=\varphi_i(g)+u\})}
{\|\nabla\varphi_i\|}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then, with \(L_*=2\sqrt6/9\),
\[
\left|\frac{L_i(u)}{L_*}-1\right|
<\frac1{100}
\tag{4}
\]
for every \(i\) and every
\[
|u|\leq\max_j|d_j|.
\]

## Proof

Write the vertices of \(\Delta\) as \(v_1,v_2,v_3\), and use barycentric
coordinates \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\).  An affine function whose
vertex-value vector is \(z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)\) satisfies
\[
\varphi(\lambda)=z\cdot\lambda,
\qquad
\varphi(g)=m:=\frac{z_1+z_2+z_3}{3}.
\tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
\eta=\frac1{1000},\qquad a=\frac1{100},
\qquad c=\frac1{\sqrt6}.
\tag{6}
\]

First convert (1) to an absolute area estimate.  If
\[
E=|K\mathbin{\triangle}\Delta|,
\]
then \(|\Delta|=1/2\) and
\[
E\leq\delta_0|K|
\leq\delta_0\left(\frac12+E\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
E\leq\frac{\delta_0}{2(1-\delta_0)}<\delta_0.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix one row and relabel the vertices only within this paragraph so that
its target vector is
\[
w=(2c,-c,-c).
\]
Write \(z=w+e\), where \(\|e\|_2\leq\eta\).  Since the coordinates of
\(w\) sum to zero, (5) and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
|m|\leq\frac{\eta}{\sqrt3}.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
D:=\max_j|d_j|
=\max_j|\varphi_j(g)|
\leq\frac{\eta}{\sqrt3}<\frac a2.
\tag{9}
\]

We next verify that every level used below lies strictly inside the
projection of \(K\).  Put
\[
r_k=z_k-m.
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
\frac2{\sqrt6}>\frac45,\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt6}>\frac25,\qquad
\frac{\eta}{\sqrt3}<\eta
\]
give
\[
r_+>\frac{79}{100},\qquad
r_-<-\frac{39}{100},\qquad
|r_k|<\frac{101}{100}.
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(+\) denotes the vertex carrying the target value \(2c\), and
either minus sign denotes a vertex carrying the target value \(-c\).
On the corner triangle
\(\{\lambda_+\geq3/4\}\), (10) gives
\[
\varphi-\varphi(g)
\geq
\frac34\frac{79}{100}
-\frac14\frac{101}{100}
=\frac{34}{100}>a.
\tag{11}
\]
On either corner triangle
\(\{\lambda_-\geq3/4\}\), it gives
\[
\varphi-\varphi(g)
\leq
-\frac34\frac{39}{100}
+\frac14\frac{101}{100}
=-\frac4{100}<-a.
\tag{12}
\]
Each corner triangle has area
\[
\frac1{16}|\Delta|=\frac1{32}>E.
\]
If the maximum of \(\varphi-\varphi(g)\) on \(K\) were at most \(a\),
the triangle in (11) would lie in \(\Delta\setminus K\), contradicting
(7).  Equation (12) gives the analogous contradiction if the minimum
on \(K\) were at least \(-a\).  Hence the projection interval of \(K\)
under \(\varphi-\varphi(g)\) contains \([-a,a]\) in its interior.  In
particular, all sections at levels in this interval are nonempty,
positive-length nonsupport sections.

For \(t\in[-a,a]\), define the section densities
\[
h(t)=
\frac{\mathcal H^1(K\cap
\{\varphi=\varphi(g)+t\})}
{\|\nabla\varphi\|},
\qquad
\ell(t)=
\frac{\mathcal H^1(\Delta\cap
\{\varphi=\varphi(g)+t\})}
{\|\nabla\varphi\|}.
\tag{13}
\]
By the one-dimensional Brunn--Minkowski inequality, the chord length of
a planar convex body is concave as a function of the parallel-line
parameter on the interior of its projection interval.  Thus \(h\) is
concave on \([-a,a]\).

The signs in (10) remain one positive and two negative after subtracting
any \(t\in[-a,a]\).  Put
\[
p=z_+-z_{-1},\qquad q=z_+-z_{-2}.
\tag{14}
\]
The two edge intercepts give
\[
\left|\Delta\cap
\{\varphi\geq\varphi(g)+t\}\right|
=\frac12\frac{(r_+-t)^2}{pq}.
\tag{15}
\]
Differentiating (15), or applying coarea directly, gives
\[
\ell(t)=\frac{r_+-t}{pq}.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, \(\ell\) is affine on \([-a,a]\), so
\[
f:=h-\ell
\]
is concave there.

Coarea on the same parallel lines gives the exact \(L^1\) control
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}|h(t)-\ell(t)|\,dt
&\leq
\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{\mathcal H^1((K\mathbin{\triangle}\Delta)\cap
\{\varphi=\varphi(g)+t\})}
{\|\nabla\varphi\|}\,dt\\
&=E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

We use the following elementary one-dimensional consequence of
concavity.  If \(f\) is concave on \([-a,a]\) and
\[
\int_{-a}^a|f|\leq E,
\]
then
\[
|f(t)|\leq\frac{3E}{a}
\qquad\left(|t|\leq\frac a2\right).
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, Jensen's inequality on \([-a,0]\) and \([0,a]\) gives
\[
f(-a/2),f(a/2)\geq-\frac Ea.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(f(t)=-M<0\) with \(|t|\leq a/2\), apply Jensen on the largest
symmetric interval about \(t\) contained in \([-a,a]\).  Its radius is
at least \(a/2\), so
\[
E\geq aM.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(f(t)=M\geq0\), concavity puts the graph above the two chords joining
\((-a/2,f(-a/2))\) to \((t,M)\) and \((t,M)\) to
\((a/2,f(a/2))\).  Integrating those chords and using (19) gives
\[
E\geq\int_{-a/2}^{a/2}f
\geq\frac{aM}{2}-\frac E2,
\]
so \(M\leq3E/a\).  This proves (18).

Combining (7), (9), (17), and (18), we obtain throughout the full
registered level window
\[
|h(u)-\ell(u)|
\leq\frac{3E}{a}
<300\delta_0=3\cdot10^{-6}.
\tag{21}
\]

It remains to compare the exact triangle density (16) with one common
number.  Set
\[
L_*=\frac{2c}{(3c)^2}=\frac{2\sqrt6}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]
For every \(|u|\leq D\), (8)--(9) give
\[
\frac{|r_+-u-2c|}{2c}
\leq
\frac{\eta+2\eta/\sqrt3}{2/\sqrt6}
<3\eta.
\tag{23}
\]
Also, since the norm of the functional
\(e\mapsto e_+-e_-\) is \(\sqrt2\),
\[
\frac{|p-3c|}{3c},
\frac{|q-3c|}{3c}
\leq\frac{\sqrt2\eta}{3/\sqrt6}
=\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}\eta
<\frac65\eta=\frac3{2500}.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (16), (22)--(24) imply
\[
\frac{997}{1000}
\left(\frac{2500}{2503}\right)^2
\leq\frac{\ell(u)}{L_*}
\leq
\frac{1003}{1000}
\left(\frac{2500}{2497}\right)^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Exact integer cross multiplication gives
\[
\frac{997}{1000}
\left(\frac{2500}{2503}\right)^2
>\frac{497}{500},
\qquad
\frac{1003}{1000}
\left(\frac{2500}{2497}\right)^2
<\frac{503}{500}.
\tag{26}
\]
For example, the two positive cross-product margins are respectively
\[
1\,915\,527\,000
\quad\text{and}\quad
1\,834\,527\,000.
\]
Thus
\[
\left|\frac{\ell(u)}{L_*}-1\right|<\frac3{500}.
\tag{27}
\]

Finally, \(L_*>1/2\).  Equation (21) therefore gives
\[
\frac{|h(u)-\ell(u)|}{L_*}
<6\cdot10^{-6}<\frac1{1000}.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27) and (28),
\[
\left|\frac{h(u)}{L_*}-1\right|
<\frac3{500}+\frac1{1000}
=\frac7{1000}<\frac1{100}.
\]
The argument applies to each of the three relabeled rows.  By (9), it
holds for every \(|u|\leq\max_j|d_j|\), proving (4).
