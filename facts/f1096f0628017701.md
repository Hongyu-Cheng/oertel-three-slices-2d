## Statement

Let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional compact convex body of area
\[
m=|Q|>0,
\]
let \(g\) be its centroid, and let \(\phi:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be a nonconstant affine function.  Assume that both open sides of the cut meet the interior of \(Q\), so
\[
\phi_-:=\min_Q\phi<0<\phi_+:=\max_Q\phi.
\]
Put
\[
w_\phi=\phi_+-\phi_->0,
\qquad
s=\frac{-\phi_-}{w_\phi}\in(0,1),
\tag{1}
\]
\[
d=\left|Q\cap\{\phi\leq0\}\right|,
\qquad
\rho=\frac dm.
\tag{2}
\]
The cutting line has planar area zero, so the same \(\rho\) is obtained with \(\phi<0\).

After normalizing the support interval of \(\phi\) to \([0,1]\), the cut is at \(s\).  Every such convex body satisfies the exact feasibility bounds
\[
\boxed{\quad
s^2\leq\rho\leq s(2-s).
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
Assume henceforth that
\[
0<\rho\leq\frac49.
\]
Then (3) forces \(0<s\leq2/3\).  In this stability regime, put
\[
A=s(2-s),\qquad B=\frac{A}{2},
\tag{4}
\]
and define
\[
\Theta_s(\rho)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{s^2}{\rho},
&s^2\leq\rho\leq B,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle \frac{4(A-\rho)}{(2-s)^2},
&B\leq\rho\leq A.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Then the sharp fixed-side-fraction centroid bound is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{\phi(g)}{w_\phi}
\geq
\frac{1+\Theta_s(\rho)}3-s.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, if
\[
0<d\leq\frac{4m}{9},
\tag{7}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{\phi(g)}{w_\phi}
\geq
\frac{4-9\rho}{12}
=
\frac{4m-9d}{12m}
\geq0.
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
The last quantity is strictly positive when \(d<4m/9\).
The last inequality is the sharp support-width-normalized quantitative stability refinement of the planar \(4/9\) Grünbaum baseline recorded in accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7`.

For fixed \(s\) and \(\rho\), equality in (6) has the following complete one-dimensional classification.

1. If \(s^2\leq\rho\leq B\), the normalized parallel-section length is the single tent with peak parameter
\[
t=\frac{s^2}{\rho}.
\tag{9}
\]
2. If \(B\leq\rho\leq A\), it is the unique area-normalized mixture of the endpoint tent \(t=0\) and the tent
\[
t_*=\frac{2s}{2-s}
\tag{10}
\]
whose left mass is \(B\).

For \(0<\rho<4/9\), equality in the universal bound (8) holds exactly when
\[
s=\frac{3\rho}{2},
\qquad
t=\frac{9\rho}{4},
\tag{11}
\]
and the normalized section-length profile is the single tent with peak \(t\).  Geometrically, after an affine shear parallel to the cutting line, equality bodies are
\[
\operatorname{conv}
\{(0,0),(1,0),(t,-a),(t,2-a)\},
\qquad
0\leq a\leq2,
\tag{12}
\]
with cut \(x=s\).  For \(0<a<2\) this is a double-cone quadrilateral; \(a=0\) or \(a=2\) gives a triangle.  In the continuous endpoint cases, \(\rho=0\) gives \(s=t=0\) and an endpoint triangle, while \(\rho=4/9\) gives \(s=2/3,t=1\), the triangle equality case for the planar Grünbaum bound.

Finally, apply (8) to the actual dominant cut in accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1`:
\[
Q=A_2,\qquad
\phi=f_1+L_1,\qquad
d=m-x_1.
\]
That accepted fact gives \(x_1>5m/9\), and hence
\[
4m-9d=9x_1-5m>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{f_1(g_2)+L_1}
{\max_{A_2}(f_1+L_1)-\min_{A_2}(f_1+L_1)}
\geq
\frac{9x_1-5m}{12m}.
\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
The denominator and the side fraction (1) are invariant under positive rescaling of the defining affine cut and are explicit support quantities in the global \(Q\)-triangle or barycentric normal form.

## Proof

Apply an invertible affine coordinate change taking the \(\phi\)-coordinate on \(Q\) to
\[
x=\frac{\phi-\phi_-}{w_\phi}\in[0,1].
\]
The cut \(\phi=0\) becomes \(x=s\).  A further constant rescaling in the transverse coordinate does not change area fractions or the normalized centroid coordinate.  Let \(\ell(x)\) be the length of the section of the transformed body on the line with first coordinate \(x\), divided by its total integral, so
\[
\ell(x)\geq0,\qquad
\int_0^1\ell(x)\,dx=1.
\tag{14}
\]
Every section is an interval.  If \(0\leq x<y\leq1\), convexity gives
\[
(1-\lambda)Q_x+\lambda Q_y
\subseteq Q_{(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y},
\]
where \(Q_x\) denotes the transverse section.  Length is additive under Minkowski addition of intervals, so
\[
\ell((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)
\geq
(1-\lambda)\ell(x)+\lambda\ell(y).
\]
Thus \(\ell\) is concave.

Let
\[
\bar x=\int_0^1x\ell(x)\,dx.
\]
Fubini's theorem and the definition of the centroid give
\[
\frac{\phi(g)-\phi_-}{w_\phi}=\bar x,
\qquad
\rho=\int_0^s\ell(x)\,dx.
\tag{15}
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{\phi(g)}{w_\phi}=\bar x-s.
\tag{16}
\]

We now solve the one-dimensional concave-density problem exactly.  For \(0<t<1\), define the area-one tent
\[
T_t(x)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{2x}{t},&0\leq x\leq t,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle \frac{2(1-x)}{1-t},&t\leq x\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]
At the endpoints, put
\[
T_0(x)=2(1-x),\qquad T_1(x)=2x.
\tag{18}
\]
Every nonnegative concave function satisfying (14) is a probability mixture of these tents.  Here is a direct proof.  In the sense of distributions, let
\[
\nu=-\ell''\geq0
\]
and put
\[
G_t(x)
=
\begin{cases}
x(1-t),&x\leq t,\\
t(1-x),&x\geq t.
\end{cases}
\]
The Green-kernel identity is
\[
\ell(x)
=
\ell(0)(1-x)+\ell(1)x
+\int_{(0,1)}G_t(x)\,\nu(dt).
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, both sides have the same endpoint values and the same distributional second derivative.  Moreover,
\[
\int_0^1G_t(x)\,dx=\frac{t(1-t)}2.
\]
Thus (19), after normalizing each summand by its area, writes
\[
\ell(x)=\int_{[0,1]}T_t(x)\,\pi(dt)
\tag{20}
\]
for a probability measure \(\pi\).  The endpoint weights are \(\ell(0)/2\) and \(\ell(1)/2\), and the interior weight is \(t(1-t)\nu(dt)/2\).  Their total is one by (14).

For a single tent, direct integration gives
\[
\int_0^1xT_t(x)\,dx=\frac{1+t}{3}
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
F_s(t):=\int_0^sT_t(x)\,dx
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
1-\frac{(1-s)^2}{1-t},&0\leq t\leq s,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle
\frac{s^2}{t},&s\leq t\leq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{22}
\]
The function \(F_s\) is continuous and strictly decreasing, with
\[
F_s(0)=s(2-s)=A,\qquad
F_s(1)=s^2.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (20)-(23) prove (3) and give
\[
\rho=\int F_s(t)\,\pi(dt),
\qquad
\bar x=\frac{1+\int t\,\pi(dt)}3.
\tag{24}
\]

It remains to minimize the mean of \(t\) subject to the first identity in (24).  In the present regime \(s\leq2/3\).  The exact lower convex envelope of the curve
\[
\bigl(F_s(t),t\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
\]
is (5).  For completeness, invert the two branches of (22):
\[
t
=
1-\frac{(1-s)^2}{1-F}
\quad(s\leq F\leq A),
\tag{25}
\]
\[
t=\frac{s^2}{F}
\quad(s^2\leq F\leq s).
\tag{26}
\]
The function in (25) is strictly concave in \(F\), while the function in (26) is strictly convex.  The line from
\[
(F,t)=(A,0)
\]
tangent to (26) touches it where
\[
F=B=\frac A2,
\qquad
t=t_*=\frac{2s}{2-s}.
\tag{27}
\]
Its equation is
\[
t=\frac{4(A-F)}{(2-s)^2}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (25)-(28), or direct subtraction after clearing positive denominators, show that the lower convex envelope is exactly the two branches in (5).

Since \(\Theta_s\) is convex and
\[
t\geq\Theta_s(F_s(t))
\qquad(0\leq t\leq1),
\]
Jensen's inequality and (24) give
\[
\int t\,\pi(dt)
\geq
\Theta_s\left(\int F_s(t)\,\pi(dt)\right)
=
\Theta_s(\rho).
\tag{29}
\]
Substituting (29) into (24), and then using (16), proves (6).

The equality cases in (6) also follow from this lower-envelope calculation.  On \(s^2\leq\rho<B\), strict convexity of (26) forces \(\pi\) to be concentrated at
\[
t=\frac{s^2}{\rho}.
\]
On \(B<\rho\leq A\), equality is supported on the two endpoints of the exposed segment (28), namely \(t=0\) and \(t=t_*\), with the unique weights that give mean left mass \(\rho\).  At \(\rho=B\), the single tent \(t=t_*\) is forced.  This proves (9)-(10) and the stated fixed-\(s\) classification.

We next optimize over the cut fraction \(s\).  Suppose \(0<\rho<4/9\).  Feasibility (3) gives
\[
0<s<\frac23.
\tag{30}
\]
On the first branch of (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\frac{1+\Theta_s(\rho)}3-s\right)
-\frac{4-9\rho}{12}\\
&\qquad
=
\frac{(2s-3\rho)^2}{12\rho}
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Equality holds exactly at
\[
s=\frac{3\rho}{2}.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(0<\rho<4/9\), this value is feasible and belongs to the first branch because
\[
s^2\leq\rho,
\qquad
s(2-s)\geq2\rho.
\tag{33}
\]

On the second branch of (5), direct simplification gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\frac{1+\Theta_s(\rho)}3-s\right)
-\frac{4-9\rho}{12}\\
&\quad=
\frac{(3s-2)
\left(3\rho s-4s^2-10\rho+8s\right)}
{12(2-s)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Here \(3s-2<0\).  This branch has
\[
\rho\geq\frac{s(2-s)}2.
\]
Since \(3s-10<0\),
\[
3\rho s-4s^2-10\rho+8s
\leq
-\frac12(3s-2)(s-2)s<0.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus the right side of (34) is nonnegative.  It is strictly positive in the interior range (30).  Equations (31)-(35) prove (8), with equality exactly as in (11).

When equality holds in (8), (31) and the equality case of (29) make the normalized section profile the single tent \(T_t\), where
\[
t=\frac{s^2}{\rho}
=
\frac{9\rho}{4}.
\]
For \(0<t<1\), a convex body with this tent section length has point sections at \(x=0,1\) and one section of length \(2\) at \(x=t\).  After a transverse affine shear taking the two endpoint sections to \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\), convexity makes the lower and upper section endpoints affine on each side of \(t\).  The body is therefore exactly (12), with \(0\leq a\leq2\).  Conversely every body in (12) has section profile \(T_t\), so it attains equality.  The triangle and endpoint cases follow by setting \(a\in\{0,2\}\) and by taking \(\rho\) to \(0\) or \(4/9\).  This proves sharpness and the complete equality description.

Finally, accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` gives for its dominant cut
\[
d=m-x_1<\frac{4m}{9},
\qquad
\phi(g_2)=f_1(g_2)+L_1>0.
\]
Substituting \(d=m-x_1\) into (8) gives exactly (13).  The inequality is unchanged by replacing \(\phi\) with a positive scalar multiple, so its support-width normalization is intrinsic to the canonical cut.
