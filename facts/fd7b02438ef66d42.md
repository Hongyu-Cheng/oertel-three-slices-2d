## Statement

Assume the canonical root setting of accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`.  Thus \(A_0,K,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad
M=|A_0|>|A_2|=m,
\qquad
k=|K|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and the three nonconstant affine functions \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) have pairwise nonparallel linear parts and satisfy
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
The three open middle halfplanes \(\{f_r<0\}\) cover \(K\), and no two of them cover \(K\).

For \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\), put
\[
c_r=|K\cap\{f_r\leq0\}|,
\]
\[
F_{0r}(a)=|A_0\cap\{f_r\leq a\}|,
\qquad
F_{2r}(a)=|A_2\cap\{f_r\leq a\}|,
\]
\[
\mu_r(a)=c_r+F_{0r}(a)+F_{2r}(-a),
\qquad
I_r=\{a\in\mathbb R:\mu_r(a)<t\}.
\]
Fix distinct \(i,j,\ell\) with \(\{i,j,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}\), and suppose
\[
c_j+m<t,
\qquad
I_i\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
I_\ell\ne\varnothing.
\]
Choose arbitrary \(a_i\in I_i\) and \(a_\ell\in I_\ell\), and write
\[
u_r=F_{0r}(a_r),
\qquad
v_r=F_{2r}(-a_r)
\qquad(r=i,\ell).
\]
Define
\[
\Delta_j=2t-k+c_j.
\]
Normalize \(M=1\), write \(r=m\), assume
\[
0<\Delta_j<r,
\qquad
q=r-\Delta_j,
\]
and define the Farkas triangle
\[
\mathcal F=\{w\in\mathbb R^2:f_1(w)\leq0,\ f_2(w)\leq0,\ f_3(w)\leq0\},
\qquad
g=|\mathcal F|.
\]
Then \(g>0\), \(\mathcal F\subseteq K\), and
\[
u_i+u_\ell+v_i+v_\ell<\Delta_j-2g.
\tag{1}
\]
For the simultaneous complements
\[
E_0=A_0\cap\{f_i\geq a_i,\ f_\ell\geq a_\ell\},
\qquad
E_2=A_2\cap\{f_i\geq-a_i,\ f_\ell\geq-a_\ell\},
\]
one has
\[
\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}
\subseteq
K\cap\{f_i\geq0,\ f_\ell\geq0,\ f_j\leq-1\},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\left|\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\right|
>
\frac14\left(1+\sqrt{q+2g}\right)^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
c_j>
g+\frac14\left(1+\sqrt{q+2g}\right)^2,
\tag{4}
\]
and therefore
\[
0<g<
c_j-\frac14(1+\sqrt q)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
The conclusion is a strict quantitative restriction on Regime B.  It does not by itself exclude the concurrent-fan limit \(g\downarrow0\), and it does not assert an actual counterexample.

## Proof

Accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194` supplies the canonical normalization, the pairwise nonparallel linear parts, and irredundancy of the three open middle halfplanes on \(K\).  We first prove the geometric consequence of irredundancy that is absent from the area-only Regime B calculation.

Because \(f_i\) and \(f_\ell\) have nonparallel linear parts, the affine map
\[
w\longmapsto(f_i(w),f_\ell(w))
\]
is invertible.  Since \(f_j=-1-f_i-f_\ell\), it maps \(\mathcal F\) onto
\[
\{(x,y):x\leq0,\ y\leq0,\ x+y\geq-1\}.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal F\) is a nondegenerate compact triangle and \(g>0\).  Denote its vertices by \(p_1,p_2,p_3\), indexed so that
\[
f_r(p_r)=-1,
\qquad
f_s(p_r)=0\quad(s\ne r).
\tag{6}
\]

For every \(r\), irredundancy says that the other two open halfplanes do not cover \(K\).  Hence there is \(z_r\in K\) such that
\[
f_s(z_r)\geq0\quad(s\ne r).
\tag{7}
\]
The functions
\[
\lambda_s(w)=-f_s(w)
\]
are the barycentric coordinates of \(w\) with respect to \(p_1,p_2,p_3\): they sum to \(1\), and (6) gives
\[
w=\sum_{s=1}^3\lambda_s(w)p_s.
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(Z\) be the matrix whose \(r\)-th column is
\[
(\lambda_1(z_r),\lambda_2(z_r),\lambda_3(z_r))^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Equation (7) gives
\[
Z_{sr}\leq0\quad(s\ne r),
\]
while the column sum is \(1\), so
\[
Z_{rr}=1+\sum_{s\ne r}|Z_{sr}|.
\tag{9}
\]
The transpose \(Z^{\mathsf T}\) is therefore strictly row diagonally dominant and is invertible.  It also has a nonnegative inverse.  Indeed, if
\[
Z^{\mathsf T}x=y\geq0
\]
and \(x_r=\min_s x_s<0\), then the nonpositive off-diagonal entries in row \(r\), (9), and the fact that the \(r\)-th column of \(Z\) sums to \(1\) give
\[
y_r
=Z_{rr}x_r+\sum_{s\ne r}Z_{sr}x_s
\leq
\left(\sum_sZ_{sr}\right)x_r
=x_r<0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \((Z^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\geq0\), equivalently \(Z^{-1}\geq0\).  Since
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Z=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},
\]
one also has
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Z^{-1}=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Consequently every standard basis vector is a convex combination of the columns of \(Z\).  By (8), every \(p_r\) is a convex combination of \(z_1,z_2,z_3\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal F=\operatorname{conv}\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}
\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}\{z_1,z_2,z_3\}
\subseteq K.
\tag{10}
\]

For \(w\in K\), let
\[
N(w)=|\{s:f_s(w)\leq0\}|.
\]
The identity \(\sum_s f_s=-1\) gives \(N(w)\geq1\) on all of \(K\), while \(N(w)=3\) on \(\mathcal F\).  All three cutting lines have planar area zero.  Therefore (10) gives
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3
=\int_KN(w)\,dw
\geq k+2g.
\tag{11}
\]

Put
\[
R=u_i+u_\ell+v_i+v_\ell.
\]
The two strict low-mass inequalities are
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i<t,
\qquad
u_\ell+v_\ell+c_\ell<t.
\]
Adding them and using (11) gives
\[
R+k+2g-c_j<2t.
\]
Since \(\Delta_j=2t-k+c_j\), this is exactly (1).  In particular,
\[
0<2g<\Delta_j<r.
\tag{12}
\]

The union bound and nullity of the cutting lines give
\[
|E_0|\geq1-u_i-u_\ell,
\qquad
|E_2|\geq r-v_i-v_\ell.
\tag{13}
\]
Both right sides are positive by (1) and (12).  For \(x_0\in E_0\) and \(x_2\in E_2\), compatibility puts
\[
z=\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}
\]
in \(K\), and affinity gives
\[
f_i(z)\geq0,
\qquad
f_\ell(z)\geq0,
\qquad
f_j(z)=-1-f_i(z)-f_\ell(z)\leq-1.
\]
This proves (2).

Set
\[
A=u_i+u_\ell,
\qquad
B=v_i+v_\ell,
\]
so \(A+B=R\).  Planar Brunn--Minkowski, as used in accepted fact `c0b5fee36666b707`, and (13) give
\[
\left|\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\right|
\geq
\frac14\left(\sqrt{1-A}+\sqrt{r-B}\right)^2.
\tag{14}
\]
For fixed \(R=A+B<r\), the concavity argument recorded in accepted fact `c0b5fee36666b707` gives
\[
\sqrt{1-A}+\sqrt{r-B}
\geq
1+\sqrt{r-R}.
\tag{15}
\]
For completeness, the minimum over \(0\leq A\leq R\) is at an endpoint.  The \(A=0\) endpoint is the smaller one because, for \(0<R<r<1\),
\[
\sqrt r-\sqrt{r-R}
>
1-\sqrt{1-R}.
\]
By (1),
\[
r-R>r-\Delta_j+2g=q+2g.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (14)-(16) proves (3).

The triangle \(\mathcal F\) and the midpoint body in (2) both lie in
\[
K\cap\{f_j\leq0\}.
\]
Their intersection is contained in
\[
\{f_i=0,\ f_\ell=0\},
\]
which is a single point because the two linear parts are nonparallel.  Thus their intersection has planar area zero.  It follows from (3) that
\[
c_j
\geq
g+\left|\frac{E_0+E_2}{2}\right|
>
g+\frac14\left(1+\sqrt{q+2g}\right)^2,
\]
which is (4).  Finally \(q>0\) and \(g>0\), so (4) implies
\[
c_j
>
g+\frac14(1+\sqrt q)^2.
\]
Rearrangement proves (5).
