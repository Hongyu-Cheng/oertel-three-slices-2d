## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and work in
\[
\Pi=\{z\in\mathbb R^3:z_1+z_2+z_3=1\},
\]
with planar area normalized so that
\[
\Delta=\{z\in\Pi:z_i\geq0\ (i\in N)\}
\]
has area \(1/2\). Fix
\[
d>\sqrt3,
\qquad
b_1+b_2+b_3=1-d,
\]
and put
\[
A_2=\Delta,
\qquad
A_0=b+d\Delta.
\]
Thus
\[
m:=|A_2|=\frac12,
\qquad
M:=|A_0|=\frac{d^2}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Fix \(0\leq\varepsilon<1\), and define
\[
\ell_1(z)=z_1+\varepsilon z_2,
\qquad
\ell_2(z)=z_2,
\qquad
\ell_3(z)=z_3-\varepsilon z_2.
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=1.
\]
Let
\[
f_i(z)=\alpha(\ell_i(z)-\rho_i),
\qquad
\alpha>0,
\qquad
\sum_i\rho_i=1+\frac1\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
In the coordinates \((z_1,z_2)\), the three gradients are
\[
(1,\varepsilon),\qquad
(0,1),\qquad
(-1,-1-\varepsilon),
\tag{4}
\]
and every pair has determinant of absolute value \(1\). In particular, the three linear parts are pairwise nonparallel. The normalized determinant between the first two directions is
\[
\frac{
|\det((1,\varepsilon),(0,1))|
}{
\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}
}
=
\frac1{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}}.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(L_1+L_2+L_3<-1\). For \(J,H,G\subseteq N\), use the exact convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` and define
\[
P_J
=
A_0\cap
\{f_j\leq L_j\ (j\in J),\
f_j>L_j\ (j\notin J)\},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
Q_H
=
A_2\cap
\{f_j\leq-L_j\ (j\in H),\
f_j>-L_j\ (j\notin H)\},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
R_G
=
K\cap
\{f_j\leq0\ (j\in G),\
f_j>0\ (j\notin G)\},
\tag{8}
\]
where \(K\subseteq\Pi\) is a two-dimensional compact convex polytope satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{9}
\]
Write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,
\qquad
q_H=|Q_H|,
\qquad
r_G=|R_G|,
\qquad
k:=|K|.
\tag{10}
\]
The exact cells partition their source polytopes up to their assigned null cutting lines, so
\[
M=\sum_{J\subseteq N}p_J,
\qquad
m=\sum_{H\subseteq N}q_H,
\qquad
k=\sum_{G\subseteq N}r_G.
\tag{11}
\]
Define
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,
\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,
\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
U=\sum_i u_i,
\qquad
E_R=r_{\{1,2\}}+r_{\{1,3\}}+r_{\{2,3\}}+2r_N,
\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
W
=
\sum_{J\subseteq N}(3|J|-2)p_J
+
\sum_{H\subseteq N}(3|H|-2)q_H
+
\sum_{G\subseteq N}(3|G|-2)r_G.
\tag{14}
\]

Assume the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{N,\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\},
\tag{15}
\]
and put
\[
q=q_N>0,
\qquad
x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Assume the unique dominant index is \(1\):
\[
x_1>\frac{5m}{9},
\qquad
x_1>x_2,\ x_1>x_3.
\tag{17}
\]
Assume
\[
r_{\{1\}}r_{\{2\}}r_{\{3\}}r_N>0,
\tag{18}
\]
the three crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h
\qquad(i\in N),
\tag{19}
\]
\[
W=0,
\tag{20}
\]
the canonical tails
\[
c_i+m\geq h,
\qquad
c_i+M\geq h
\qquad(i\in N),
\tag{21}
\]
and all three strict no-half inequalities
\[
M<m+k,
\qquad
m<M+k,
\qquad
k<M+m.
\tag{22}
\]
Only \(M<m+k\) in (22) is used below.

Define
\[
\tau_i=\rho_i+\frac{L_i}{\alpha},
\qquad
\lambda_i=\rho_i-\frac{L_i}{\alpha}.
\tag{23}
\]
Then
\[
\rho_i=\frac{\tau_i+\lambda_i}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
Put
\[
\beta_i=\ell_i(b).
\]
Assume the six one-cut endpoint caps remain in the corresponding vertex-cap regime:
\[
\beta_1+d\varepsilon<\tau_1<\beta_1+d,
\qquad
\beta_2<\tau_2<\beta_2+d,
\qquad
\beta_3<\tau_3<\beta_3+d,
\tag{25}
\]
\[
\varepsilon<\lambda_1<1,
\qquad
0<\lambda_2<1,
\qquad
0<\lambda_3<1.
\tag{26}
\]
Define their affine cap scales
\[
\pi_i=\beta_i+d-\tau_i,
\qquad
\sigma_i=1-\lambda_i.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus
\[
0<\pi_i<d,\qquad
0<\sigma_i<1.
\tag{28}
\]

If
\[
0\leq\varepsilon\leq
\frac{2(d^2-3d+3)}{5d^2-6d+9},
\tag{29}
\]
then no configuration satisfying (1)-(28) exists.

## Proof

The vertex values of the three functions in (2) are
\[
\ell_1(e_1),\ell_1(e_2),\ell_1(e_3)
=(1,\varepsilon,0),
\]
\[
\ell_2(e_1),\ell_2(e_2),\ell_2(e_3)
=(0,1,0),
\]
\[
\ell_3(e_1),\ell_3(e_2),\ell_3(e_3)
=(0,-\varepsilon,1).
\tag{30}
\]
The vertex-cap assumptions (25)-(26) therefore give exact triangular cap areas. Put
\[
w_1=\frac1{1-\varepsilon},
\qquad
w_2=1,
\qquad
w_3=\frac1{1+\varepsilon}.
\tag{31}
\]
For example, the two edge fractions of
\[
\Delta\cap\{\ell_1>1-\sigma_1\}
\]
at \(e_1\) are \(\sigma_1\) and
\(\sigma_1/(1-\varepsilon)\), while the two edge fractions of the third cap at \(e_3\) are \(\sigma_3\) and
\(\sigma_3/(1+\varepsilon)\). The same calculation on the homothetic triangle \(A_0\) gives
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{f_i>L_i\}\right|
=
\frac{w_i\pi_i^2}{2},
\qquad
x_i
=
\frac{w_i\sigma_i^2}{2}.
\tag{32}
\]
Under (15), the one-cut \(Q\)-cap has area \(x_i\), because all other exact \(Q\)-cells omitting \(i\) have area zero.

The cutting lines have area zero, so
\[
u_i
=
M-\frac{w_i\pi_i^2}{2}.
\tag{33}
\]
The all-double support gives
\[
q
=
m-x_1-x_2-x_3
=
\frac{
1-\sum_iw_i\sigma_i^2
}{2}.
\tag{34}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
\mathcal Q_\varepsilon
:=
\sum_iw_i(\pi_i^2+\sigma_i^2),
\tag{35}
\]
one has
\[
U+q
=
\frac{3d^2+1-\mathcal Q_\varepsilon}{2}.
\tag{36}
\]

We next retain the exact linear cut-level sum. Since each corresponding vertex in (30) has value \(1\),
\[
\max_{A_0}\ell_i=\beta_i+d,
\qquad
\max_{A_2}\ell_i=1.
\]
Thus (27) gives
\[
\sum_i\tau_i
=
\sum_i(\beta_i+d-\pi_i)
=
1+2d-\sum_i\pi_i,
\tag{37}
\]
because
\[
\sum_i\beta_i
=
\sum_i\ell_i(b)
=
\sum_i b_i
=1-d.
\]
Likewise,
\[
\sum_i\lambda_i=3-\sum_i\sigma_i.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (24), (37), and (38), together with
\[
\sum_i\rho_i=1+\frac1\alpha>1,
\]
give
\[
\sum_i\pi_i+\sum_i\sigma_i<2d+2.
\tag{39}
\]

The summed crossing identity is derived directly. Under (15),
\[
\sum_i v_i=2m+q.
\]
Since \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), \(R_\varnothing=\varnothing\), and hence
\[
\sum_i c_i=k+E_R.
\]
Summing (19) and using (13) gives
\[
2M=4m+k+3(U+q+E_R).
\tag{40}
\]
Write
\[
e^2=2k.
\]
Substitute (1) and (36) into (40). Since (18) gives
\[
E_R\geq2r_N>0,
\]
one obtains
\[
3\mathcal Q_\varepsilon
>7d^2+7+e^2.
\tag{41}
\]
The first inequality in (22) gives
\[
e^2>d^2-1,
\]
so
\[
3\mathcal Q_\varepsilon>8d^2+6.
\tag{42}
\]

It remains to control the rotated cap-area error. Equations (28) and (31) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_\varepsilon
&<
d\sum_i\pi_i+\sum_i\sigma_i\\
&\qquad
+
\left(\frac1{1-\varepsilon}-1\right)
(d\pi_1+\sigma_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
The \(i=3\) weight is below \(1\), so it contributes no positive error in (43). By (28),
\[
d\pi_1+\sigma_1<d^2+1,
\]
while (39) and \(d>1\) give
\[
d\sum_i\pi_i+\sum_i\sigma_i
<2d^2+2d.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal Q_\varepsilon
<
2d^2+2d
+
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}(d^2+1).
\tag{44}
\]
If (29) holds, then
\[
\frac{3\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}(d^2+1)
\leq
2(d^2-3d+3).
\tag{45}
\]
Indeed, solving (45) for \(\varepsilon\) gives exactly (29), since
\[
3(d^2+1)+2(d^2-3d+3)
=5d^2-6d+9.
\]
Multiplying (44) by \(3\) and using (45) gives the strict upper bound
\[
3\mathcal Q_\varepsilon
<
6d^2+6d+2(d^2-3d+3)
=8d^2+6.
\tag{46}
\]
This contradicts the strict lower bound (42). The endpoint
\(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_\ast(d)\) is covered because (44) remains strict when (45) is an equality.

Finally,
\[
d^2-3d+3
=
\left(d-\frac32\right)^2+\frac34>0,
\]
and
\[
(5d^2-6d+9)-2(d^2-3d+3)
=3d^2+3>0.
\]
Thus \(\varepsilon_\ast(d)\in(0,1)\), and the stated interval is nonempty. This proves the statement. The proof makes no claim for cap cuts outside the vertex-cap regime (25)-(26) or for a general three-direction perturbation.
