## Statement

Assume the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses,
exact-cell convention, and relabeled positive support
\[
\mathcal S_Q=\{123,1,2,12\}
\]
of accepted facts `0e51a47a9add571f` and `91cb5bfc71da49ac`.
Write
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
a=q_1>0,\qquad b=q_2>0,\qquad
\gamma=q_{12}>0,
\]
\[

\]
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
Assume the three crossings, both canonical tails, \(W=0\),
\(M\geq m\), and the actual-counterexample no-half inequalities
\[
M<m+k,\qquad m<M+k,\qquad k<M+m.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\qquad
r_3>\frac{k+m}{9}+q+\gamma.
\qquad}
\tag{2}
\]

Define the affine sign coordinates
\[
s=f_1-f_2,\qquad t=f_1+f_2=-1-f_3,
\]
and let
\[
\mathfrak a
=\left|\det D(s,t)\right|^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus physical planar area is \(\mathfrak a\,ds\,dt\).
Suppose \(K\) contains two points \(z_-,z_+\) such that
\[
t(z_-)=t(z_+)=-\tau<0,\qquad
s(z_+)-s(z_-)=\ell>0.
\tag{4}
\]
Then every residual realization satisfies
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\mathfrak a\,\ell\tau<2(M+m).
\qquad}
\tag{5}
\]
Both sides of (5) scale identically under a common affine change of the
planar variable, so (5) is affine invariant.

For the explicit strip endpoints below, put
\[
\varepsilon=\frac1n,\qquad D=5n,\qquad T=-D-2,
\]
\[
\alpha=4n+\frac1n,\qquad
\delta=\frac{(D+2)\varepsilon}{\alpha},
\tag{6}
\]
and define
\[
m_n=\frac{(9n+2+1/n)^2}{2(4n^2+1)},\qquad
\beta=\varepsilon^2,\qquad
A_n=\frac{m_n}{\varepsilon+\beta}.
\]
Use
\[
f_1=x,\qquad f_2=y,\qquad f_3=-1-x-y,
\]
\[
L_1=L_2=0,\qquad L_3=-D-1.
\]
With
\[
s=x-y,\qquad t=x+y,
\]
let
\[
A_{0,n}
=\{(s,t):-A_n\leq s\leq A_n,\
D-\varepsilon\leq t\leq D+\beta\},
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
A_{2,n}
=\operatorname{conv}\{
(T-\alpha,T-\varepsilon),
(-T+\alpha,T-\varepsilon),
(0,T+\delta)
\}.
\tag{8}
\]
These are actual convex endpoint bodies with
\[
M=m_n,\qquad
\mathcal S_Q=\{123,1,2,12\},\qquad
q_1=q_2=1.
\tag{9}
\]
Their second-slice area is
\[
m=m_n\leq18.
\tag{10}
\]
Every convex \(K\) satisfying
\[
\frac{A_{0,n}+A_{2,n}}2\subseteq K
\]
and the residual hypotheses above would contain points as in (4) with
\[
\tau=1+\frac1n,\qquad
\ell=10n+2+\frac1n,\qquad
\mathfrak a=\frac12.
\tag{11}
\]
The forced spill of `d3afd91c459bcce8` remains positive and satisfies
\[
0<
\left|\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2)\cap R_{12}\right|
\leq\frac1n+\frac1{2n^2}.
\]
Its normalized sign width satisfies
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{A_{0,n}\cup A_{2,n}}(f_1-f_2)
\geq2A_n>\frac{54n}{5}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Equations (5), (10), and (11) are incompatible for every \(n\geq15\).
Thus this bounded-area perturbation already has \(M=m\) and proper
positive endpoint marginals, yet it cannot be extended merely by
enlarging \(K\).

This does not prove a global sign-width bound from scalar data alone.
For every integer \(N\geq4\), there is an exact scalar calibration with
\[

\tag{13}

The calibration is given explicitly in the proof.  It is not claimed
to be a common-plane convex realization.

## Proof

For the support in the statement,
\[
v_1=m-b,\qquad v_2=m-a,\qquad v_3=q,
\]
and therefore
\[
v_1+v_2=m+q+\gamma.
\tag{14}
\]
The first two crossings give
\[
c_1+c_2
=2h-(u_1+u_2+v_1+v_2)
\leq2h-(m+q+\gamma).
\tag{15}
\]
Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` gives the first inequality in (1) for
an actual counterexample.  Hence
\[
M+m+k<2(m+k),
\]
and
\[
2h=\frac{4(M+m+k)}9<\frac{8(m+k)}9.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (15)-(16),
\[
c_1+c_2
<
\frac{8k-m}{9}-q-\gamma.
\tag{17}
\]

The exact middle-cell multiplicities give
\[
c_1+c_2
=r_1+r_2+2r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r_{123},
\]
so
\[
r_3=k-c_1-c_2+r_{12}+r_{123}.
\tag{18}
\]
Substitution of (17) into (18), followed by
\(r_{12},r_{123}\geq0\), proves (2).  If
\(p_\varnothing>0\), accepted fact `d3afd91c459bcce8` strengthens the
last nonnegative term to \(r_{12}>0\), but no quantitative value is
needed here.

We next prove the geometric separation bound.  Equation (2) gives
\(r_3>0\), so \(R_3\) contains a point \(z_0\) away from its null
cutting boundaries.  In the \((s,t)\)-coordinates,
\[
R_3\subseteq\{t>0,\ -t<s<t\}.
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, \(t(z_0)>0\).  Convexity of \(K\) puts the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{z_-,z_+,z_0\}
\]
inside \(K\).  Its base in the \((s,t)\)-plane has length \(\ell\),
and its height is
\[
t(z_0)+\tau>\tau.
\]
Therefore
\[
k>
\mathfrak a\,\frac{\ell\tau}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
The last inequality in (1) gives \(k<M+m\).  Combining it with (20)
proves (5).

If a common invertible affine map \(G\) is applied to the bodies and
the affine functions are transported as \(f_i\circ G^{-1}\), then the
values of \(s,t,\ell,\tau\) do not change.  Both physical area and
\(\mathfrak a\) are multiplied by \(|\det G|\).  Thus (5) is affine
invariant.

We now apply the bound to the strip endpoints.  The sign and area audit
is direct.  Since
\[
dx\,dy=\frac12\,ds\,dt,
\]
equation (7) has area
\[
M=A_n(\varepsilon+\beta)=m_n.
\tag{21}
\]
It has positive proper \(P\)-marginals in all three directions:
\(A_n>D+\beta\) gives positive left and right end caps, while
\(\beta>0\) gives a positive upper \(P_3\)-cap; the central part below
\(t=D\) gives \(p_\varnothing>0\).  Indeed,
\[
m_n>\frac{81}{10},\qquad
\frac{n^2}{n+1}\geq\frac{2n}{3}\quad(n\geq2),
\]
so
\[
A_n=\frac{m_nn^2}{n+1}>\frac{27n}{5}>D+\beta
\qquad(n\geq2).
\]
The identity
\[
\alpha\delta=(D+2)\varepsilon
\]
puts the two nonhorizontal sides of (8) through
\[
(T,T),\qquad(-T,T).
\]
It follows that (8) has exact positive support
\(\{123,1,2,12\}\).  Its two singleton cells are triangles of area
\[
\frac{(\alpha-\varepsilon)\varepsilon}{4}=1,
\]
proving (9).  The total triangle area is
\[
m_n
=\frac{\varepsilon(D+2+\alpha)^2}{2\alpha}
=\frac{(9n+2+1/n)^2}{2(4n^2+1)}.
\]
For \(n\geq1\),
\[
9n+2+\frac1n\leq12n,\qquad
2(4n^2+1)\geq8n^2,
\]
which proves (10).

Compatibility forces \(K\) to contain the midpoints
\[
z_-=
\frac{(-n,D-\varepsilon)+(T-\alpha,T-\varepsilon)}2,
\]
\[
z_+=
\frac{(n,D-\varepsilon)+(-T+\alpha,T-\varepsilon)}2.
\tag{22}
\]
For \(n\geq2\), the points
\((\pm n,D-\varepsilon)\) lie in the exact
\(P_\varnothing\)-cell of (7), because \(A_n>n\).
Their common \(t\)-coordinate is
\[
\frac{D-\varepsilon+T-\varepsilon}{2}
=-1-\frac1n,
\]
and their \(s\)-separation is
\[
n+D+2+\alpha
=10n+2+\frac1n.
\]
This proves (11).  Equation (5) would then give
\[
\left(10n+2+\frac1n\right)
\left(1+\frac1n\right)
<4(M+m_n)
=8m_n
\leq144.
\tag{23}
\]
The left side is at least \(10n\), so (23) is impossible for every
\(n\geq15\).  This proves the claimed nonextendability of the
finite-interior, \(M=m\) endpoint perturbation under arbitrary
enlargement of \(K\).

For completeness, every point of \(P_\varnothing\) has
\[
D-\varepsilon\leq t<D,
\]
while every point of \(Q_1\cup Q_2\) has
\[
T-\varepsilon\leq t\leq T.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2)\) lies in
\[
-1-\varepsilon\leq t\leq-1.
\]
Since
\[
R_{12}\subseteq\{t<-1,\ t\leq s\leq-t\},
\]
the area Jacobian \(1/2\) gives
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2)\cap R_{12}\right|
\leq
\frac12\int_{-1-\varepsilon}^{-1}(-2t)\,dt
=\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}.
\]
It is strictly positive by `d3afd91c459bcce8`, because
\(p_\varnothing,q_1,q_2>0\).

It remains to separate this geometric obstruction from scalar
feasibility.  Fix \(N\geq4\), and assign the \(P\)-cells by
\[
p_\varnothing=N-\frac3{20},\qquad
p_1=p_2=p_3=\frac1{20},
\tag{24}
\]
with all other \(P\)-cells zero.  Assign the four \(Q\)-cells as in
(12).  Then
\[
(v_1,v_2,v_3)=\left(\frac35,\frac35,\frac1{10}\right).
\]
Put
\[
h=\frac{4N+2}{9},
\]
\[
A=\frac{80N-77}{180},\qquad
C=\frac{80N+13}{180},
\]
\[
E=\frac{20N-47}{60},\qquad
\rho=\frac1N,
\]
\[
e=\frac{E-\rho}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Define the nonzero middle cells by
\[
r_{12}=\rho,\qquad
r_{13}=r_{23}=e,
\]
\[
r_1=r_2=A-\rho-e,\qquad
r_3=C-2e,
\qquad r_{123}=0.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(N\geq4\),
\[
e=\frac{20N^2-47N-60}{120N}>0,
\]
\[
r_1=r_2
=\frac{100N^2-13N-180}{360N}>0,
\]
\[
r_3=\frac{10N+77}{90}+\frac1N>0.
\]
The total middle area is \(k=N\), and its marginals are
\[
c_1=c_2=A,\qquad c_3=C.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac1{20}+\frac35+A=h,
\qquad
\frac1{20}+\frac1{10}+C=h,
\]
so all three crossings hold.  The left tails hold because
\[
\frac1{20}+\frac35<1,\qquad
\frac1{20}+\frac1{10}<1,
\]
and the right tails hold because \(M=N\geq1\).

Here
\[
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}=E,
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1+u_2+u_3+\gamma+2q+E_R
&=\frac3{20}+\frac1{10}+\frac15+E\\
&=\frac{N-1}{3}
=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(W=0\) by accepted facts `0819231d0af83197` and
`91cb5bfc71da49ac`.  Since
\[
M=k=N,\qquad m=1,
\]
all three no-half inequalities are strict, and \(M\geq m\).
Moreover
\[
k-r_{123}=N,\qquad
\min_i(k-c_i)=N-C=\frac{100N-13}{180},
\]
so
\[
\frac95\min_i(k-c_i)
=N-\frac{13}{100}<N=k-r_{123}.
\]
Thus the full middle Scott inequality in accepted fact
`e51585de76810353` holds.  Its projected consequence is
\[
5r_{123}
+9\min_i(u_i+v_i)
=9\cdot\frac3{20}
=\frac{27}{20}<4,
\]
while the endpoint Scott inequality is
\[
1-q=\frac9{10}>
\frac95\min\{a,b\}
=\frac{18}{25}.
\]
Finally \(r_{12}=1/N\to0\).

This is an exact scalar calibration only.  It proves that the width
gate (5) is the first genuinely geometric obstruction: scalar
crossings, tails, \(W=0\), no-half inequalities, and strict spill do
not exclude a mass-growing escape.
