## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact convex bodies with
nonempty interiors and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_i,f_\ell,f_j\) be nonconstant affine functions satisfying
\[
f_i+f_\ell+f_j=-1,
\tag{2}
\]
and fix \(a_i,a_\ell\in\mathbb R\). Define the closed endpoint cells
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
P=A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i,\ f_\ell\ge a_\ell\},&
X_i=A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i,\ f_\ell\ge a_\ell\},\\
X_\ell=A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i,\ f_\ell\le a_\ell\},&
Z=A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i,\ f_\ell\le a_\ell\},
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
Q=A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i,\ f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\},&
B_i=A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a_i,\ f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\},\\
B_\ell=A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i,\ f_\ell\le-a_\ell\},&
R=A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a_i,\ f_\ell\le-a_\ell\}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
p=|P|,\quad x_i=|X_i|,\quad x_\ell=|X_\ell|,
\quad q=|Q|,\quad b_i=|B_i|,\quad b_\ell=|B_\ell|,\\
r=|A_2|,\qquad s=|K|,\qquad
c_h=|K\cap\{f_h\le0\}|\quad(h=i,\ell,j).
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Assume
\[
p>0,\qquad q>0.
\tag{5a}
\]
Empty cells have empty midpoint bodies. Put
\[
D_Q=\frac{P+Q}{2},\qquad
D_i=\frac{P+B_i}{2},\qquad
D_\ell=\frac{P+B_\ell}{2},\qquad
D_R=\frac{P+R}{2},
\tag{6}
\]
and introduce the four remaining high-sign cell-pair bodies
\[
F_{iQ}=\frac{X_i+Q}{2},\qquad
F_{ii}=\frac{X_i+B_i}{2},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
F_{\ell Q}=\frac{X_\ell+Q}{2},\qquad
F_{\ell\ell}=\frac{X_\ell+B_\ell}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
E=|D_i\cap\{f_j>0\}|+
|D_\ell\cap\{f_j>0\}|,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\mathcal R=
\bigl(D_R\cap\{f_j>0\}\bigr)\setminus(D_i\cup D_\ell),
\tag{10}
\]
and partition \(\mathcal R\), up to null cutting lines, into
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_i&=\mathcal R\cap\{f_i\le0,\ f_\ell>0\},\\
\mathcal R_\ell&=\mathcal R\cap\{f_i>0,\ f_\ell\le0\},\\
\mathcal R_{i\ell}&=\mathcal R\cap\{f_i\le0,\ f_\ell\le0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Define the overlap charges
\[
\mathcal T_i=\mathcal R_i\cap(F_{iQ}\cup F_{ii}),
\qquad
\mathcal T_\ell=
\mathcal R_\ell\cap(F_{\ell Q}\cup F_{\ell\ell}),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\Theta_i=|\mathcal T_i|,
\qquad
\Theta_\ell=|\mathcal T_\ell|,
\qquad
E^\sharp=E+\Theta_i+\Theta_\ell.
\tag{13}
\]
Define the uncovered residual sets
\[
\mathcal L_i=
\mathcal R_i\setminus(F_{iQ}\cup F_{ii}),
\qquad
\mathcal L_\ell=
\mathcal R_\ell\setminus(F_{\ell Q}\cup F_{\ell\ell}),
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\Lambda_i=|\mathcal L_i|,
\qquad
\Lambda_\ell=|\mathcal L_\ell|,
\qquad
\Lambda_{i\ell}=|\mathcal R_{i\ell}|,
\qquad
\Lambda=\Lambda_i+\Lambda_\ell+\Lambda_{i\ell}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
E^\sharp+\Lambda
=
\left|\frac{P+A_2}{2}\cap\{f_j>0\}\right|
\ge
\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt r)^2-c_j.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Let
\[
G_\ell=
\frac{
\bigl(A_0\cap\{f_\ell\ge a_\ell\}\bigr)
+
\bigl(A_2\cap\{f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\}\bigr)
}{2},
\tag{17}
\]
\[
G_i=
\frac{
\bigl(A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i\}\bigr)
+
\bigl(A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i\}\bigr)
}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]
The structural identities are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
G_\ell
&=D_Q\cup D_i\cup F_{iQ}\cup F_{ii},\\
G_i
&=D_Q\cup D_\ell\cup F_{\ell Q}\cup F_{\ell\ell},\\
\mathcal R_i\cap G_\ell&=\mathcal T_i,\\
\mathcal R_\ell\cap G_i&=\mathcal T_\ell.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{19}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_i
&\le
s-c_\ell
-\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_i}+\sqrt{q+b_i}
\right)^2,\\
\Lambda_\ell
&\le
s-c_i
-\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_\ell}+\sqrt{q+b_\ell}
\right)^2,\\
\Lambda_{i\ell}
&\le c_i+c_\ell-s+c_j,
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda\le
s+c_j
-\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_i}+\sqrt{q+b_i}
\right)^2
-\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_\ell}+\sqrt{q+b_\ell}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{21}
\]

At the exact calibration
\[
\begin{gathered}
p=\frac{97}{100},\quad
x_i=x_\ell=\frac3{200},\quad
q=\frac9{200},\quad
b_i=b_\ell=\frac1{160},\\
r=\frac1{16},\quad
s=\frac{47}{50},\quad
c_i=c_\ell=\frac{209}{500},\quad
c_j=\frac{29}{80},
\end{gathered}
\tag{22}
\]
put
\[
G_*=
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{197}{200}}+\sqrt{\frac{41}{800}}
\right)^2
=\frac{829+4\sqrt{8077}}{3200}.
\tag{23}
\]
The four new cell-pair bodies also have the compulsory individual
bounds
\[
\begin{aligned}
|F_{iQ}|,\ |F_{\ell Q}|
&\ge\frac{6+3\sqrt3}{400},\\
|F_{ii}|,\ |F_{\ell\ell}|
&\ge\frac{17+4\sqrt{15}}{3200}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23a}
\]
They are not added as if disjoint; their overlaps with
\(D_Q,D_i,D_\ell\) are retained exactly in (19).
Equations (16), (20), and (21) give
\[
E^\sharp+\Lambda
\ge\frac{20\sqrt{97}-167}{1600},
\tag{24}
\]
\[
\Lambda_i,\Lambda_\ell
\le\frac{261}{500}-G_*,
\qquad
\Lambda_{i\ell}\le\frac{517}{2000},
\tag{25}
\]
\[
\Lambda\le
\frac{1255-4\sqrt{8077}}{1600}.
\tag{26}
\]
The upper bound in (26) is larger than the overflow threshold, so the
calibrated cap capacities alone do not close Alternative 1. However,
any geometric estimate
\[
\Lambda<
\frac{20\sqrt{97}-167}{1600}
\tag{27}
\]
forces \(E^\sharp>0\) with no unresolved overlap term.

## Proof

All cutting lines have planar area zero. The closed cells in (4) cover
\(A_2\), even when one of them has zero area. Hence
\[
U:=\frac{P+A_2}{2}
=D_Q\cup D_i\cup D_\ell\cup D_R.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(z\in D_Q\), then
\[
f_i(z)\ge0,\qquad f_\ell(z)\ge0,\qquad f_j(z)\le-1,
\]
so \(D_Q\cap\{f_j>0\}=\varnothing\). If
\(z\in D_i\cap\{f_j>0\}\), then \(f_\ell(z)\ge0\), and (2) gives
\[
f_i(z)<0.
\]
Similarly,
\[
D_\ell\cap\{f_j>0\}
\subseteq\{f_i\ge0,\ f_\ell<0\}.
\]
Thus the two components counted by \(E\) are disjoint.

Removing those two components from
\(D_R\cap\{f_j>0\}\) gives \(\mathcal R\). The three sectors in (11)
are disjoint and cover \(\mathcal R\), because \(f_i,f_\ell\) cannot
both be positive when \(f_j>0\). Within the first two sectors,
\(\mathcal T_i,\mathcal L_i\) and
\(\mathcal T_\ell,\mathcal L_\ell\) are disjoint partitions.
Therefore
\[
U\cap\{f_j>0\}
\]
is the disjoint union of the two sets counted by \(E\),
\(\mathcal T_i,\mathcal T_\ell,\mathcal L_i,\mathcal L_\ell\), and
\(\mathcal R_{i\ell}\). This proves the equality in (16).

Compatibility gives \(U\subseteq K\), and hence
\[
|U\cap\{f_j\le0\}|\le c_j.
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
|U|\ge\frac14(\sqrt p+\sqrt r)^2.
\]
Subtracting proves the inequality in (16). This re-derives the
global-overflow step of accepted fact `7d5ee83bd565a34f` without using
its support rectangle.

The high-\(\ell\) endpoint cells are, up to null cutting lines,
\[
A_0\cap\{f_\ell\ge a_\ell\}=P\cup X_i,
\qquad
A_2\cap\{f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\}=Q\cup B_i.
\]
Minkowski addition distributes over these finite unions, proving the
first identity in (19). The high-\(i\) identity follows symmetrically.

Every point of \(\mathcal R_i\) is outside \(D_Q,D_i,D_\ell\) by
definition and by \(D_Q\cap\{f_j>0\}=\varnothing\). Intersecting the
first union in (19) with \(\mathcal R_i\) therefore leaves only
\(F_{iQ}\cup F_{ii}\), proving
\[
\mathcal R_i\cap G_\ell=\mathcal T_i.
\]
The argument for \(\ell\) is identical. This proves all of (19).
It also proves that \(\mathcal T_i\) can only be covered by the two
cell-pair bodies in (7), and \(\mathcal T_\ell\) only by those in (8).

Every body in (7) has \(f_\ell\ge0\). Moreover \(F_{ii}\) has
\(f_i\le0\); any point of \(F_{iQ}\cap\{f_j>0\}\) also has \(f_i<0\)
by (2). Thus \(\mathcal T_i\) is a genuine exclusive \(i\)-cap charge.
Symmetrically, \(\mathcal T_\ell\) is a genuine exclusive
\(\ell\)-cap charge. Since both lie in \(\mathcal R\), they are
disjoint from the two sets counted by \(E\), and they lie in disjoint
sign sectors from each other. Hence \(E^\sharp\) is a sum of areas of
pairwise disjoint side-cap subsets; this is the asserted cancellation
of \(\Theta\).

The two endpoint bodies in (17) have areas
\[
p+x_i,\qquad q+b_i,
\]
and their midpoint body lies in \(K\cap\{f_\ell\ge0\}\). Therefore
\[
|G_\ell|
\ge\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_i}+\sqrt{q+b_i}
\right)^2.
\tag{29}
\]
By (19),
\[
\mathcal L_i=\mathcal R_i\setminus G_\ell.
\]
The disjoint sets \(G_\ell,\mathcal L_i\) lie in
\(K\cap\{f_\ell\ge0\}\), whose area is \(s-c_\ell\). This proves the
first inequality in (20). The second follows symmetrically from
\[
|G_i|
\ge\frac14\left(
\sqrt{p+x_\ell}+\sqrt{q+b_\ell}
\right)^2.
\tag{30}
\]
The assumptions \(p,q>0\) make both endpoint bodies in each of
(29)-(30) nonempty with positive area. These bounds remain valid if
any one of
\(X_i,X_\ell,B_i,B_\ell\) has zero area. No Brunn--Minkowski bound is
applied to an individual body in (7)-(8), so a zero-area but nonempty
segment or point remains in the exact finite unions without requiring
an area formula.

Every point of \(K\cap\{f_j>0\}\) lies in at least one of the two caps
\(\{f_i\le0\}\), \(\{f_\ell\le0\}\). Inclusion-exclusion therefore
gives
\[
\left|K\cap\{f_i\le0,\ f_\ell\le0\}\right|
\le c_i+c_\ell-s+c_j.
\]
This proves the third inequality in (20). Adding the three inequalities
gives (21).

Substituting (22) gives
\[
p+x_i=p+x_\ell=\frac{197}{200},
\qquad
q+b_i=q+b_\ell=\frac{41}{800},
\]
which yields (23). The remaining substitutions give (24)-(26).
Since all four cells used in (7)-(8) have positive area in (22),
Brunn--Minkowski applied separately gives
\[
|F_{iQ}|,|F_{\ell Q}|
\ge\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac3{200}}+\sqrt{\frac9{200}}
\right)^2
=\frac{6+3\sqrt3}{400},
\]
\[
|F_{ii}|,|F_{\ell\ell}|
\ge\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac3{200}}+\sqrt{\frac1{160}}
\right)^2
=\frac{17+4\sqrt{15}}{3200}.
\]
These prove (23a). No sum of these lower bounds is used, because the
four bodies may overlap the other components of \(G_i,G_\ell\).

Finally, this allocation survives the determinant-one stress that
makes a support rectangle unbounded. Under a common determinant-one
linear map \(S\), all midpoint bodies in (6)-(8) become their images
under \(S\), so their areas and all pulled-back overlaps are unchanged.
If the affine forms are held fixed instead, the sign pieces move, but
the set identities (19), the disjoint allocation in (16), and the cap
capacity proof of (20) remain valid for the transformed configuration.
No normal-depth or width product appears. Empty
\(X_i,X_\ell,B_i,B_\ell,R\) cells simply remove their corresponding
bodies from the finite unions; \(P,Q\) are never empty by (5a).
