## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes and let
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
b=|B|,
\]
and
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+b)}9.
\]
Suppose
\[
\frac{13}{20}\le r<\rho,\qquad
0<2-r-s\le\frac3{200}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then no collection of at most three proper closed halfspaces of mass strictly below \(t\) can cover \(\{1\}\times B\) by their interiors.

Consequently, for
\[
S_B=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
there exists \(z\in B\) such that
\[
h_{S_B}((1,z))\ge\frac{2(M+m+b)}9.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, this proves the conclusion on the part of the residual domain from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` where additionally
\[
\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}<s<2-r.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

Relabel the outer slices so that
\[
|A_0|=M,\qquad |A_2|=m=rM.
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=2-r-s.
\]
By (1),
\[
0<\Delta\le\frac3{200},\qquad
s=2-r-\Delta,
\]
and
\[
\frac tM
=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
=\frac23-\frac{2\Delta}{9}
=:\tau.
\tag{4}
\]

Assume that proper closed halfspaces \(H_1,\ldots,H_N\), where \(N\le3\), have masses below \(t\) and their interiors cover \(\{1\}\times B\). Use the notation of accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`. For each \(j\), define
\[
\beta_j=\frac{b_{j1}}M,\qquad
\rho_{j0}=\frac{b_{j0}}M,\qquad
\rho_{j2}=\frac{b_{j2}}m,
\]
and
\[
p_j=\frac{d_j}{Mm}.
\]
For nonconstant planar normals, accepted fact `541afa94853c6bab` gives the exact tent formula
\[
p_j=\iint K_j(\sigma,\theta)\,
\nu_{j0}(d\sigma)\nu_{j2}(d\theta).
\]
It also gives
\[
q_j
:=\rho_{j0}+\rho_{j2}-p_j
=\frac{b_{j0}b_{j2}+E_j}{Mm}
\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(q_j\) is the exact affine-slack defect, including the product term, rather than a cap-area relaxation.

Define
\[
e_j=(1-r)\rho_{j0}+rq_j.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mu(H_j)}M
&=\beta_j+\rho_{j0}+r\rho_{j2}\\
&=\beta_j+rp_j+e_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The middle interiors cover \(B\), so
\[
\sum_{j=1}^N\beta_j\ge s.
\tag{8}
\]
The product sets \(D_j\) cover \(A_0\times A_2\) by accepted fact `a5309a9087686166`, hence
\[
\sum_{j=1}^Np_j\ge1.
\tag{9}
\]
Summing (7), using the strict mass bounds, \(N\le3\), (8), and (9), gives
\[
s+r+\sum_{j=1}^Ne_j
\le
\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{\mu(H_j)}M
<N\tau
\le3\tau.
\]
Since \(3\tau=2-2\Delta/3\) and \(s+r=2-\Delta\),
\[
\sum_{j=1}^Ne_j<\frac{\Delta}{3}.
\tag{10}
\]

By (9), some index \(k\) satisfies
\[
p_k\ge\frac13.
\tag{11}
\]
Its planar normal is nonzero. Indeed, a halfspace with zero planar normal has \(p_k\in\{0,1\}\); if \(p_k=1\), its middle interior contains all of \(B\), so its mass is at least \(b\). Here
\[
r<\rho<\frac{17}{25},
\]
because \(\sqrt{26}>5\), and therefore
\[
s=2-r-\Delta
>
2-\frac{17}{25}-\frac3{200}
=\frac{261}{200}
>\tau.
\]
Thus \(b>t\), excluding \(p_k=1\).

Write
\[
X=X_k(Z_0),\qquad Y=Y_k(Z_2),
\]
for independent uniform \(Z_i\in A_i\), and abbreviate
\[
\rho_0=\rho_{k0},\quad
\rho_2=\rho_{k2},\quad
p=p_k,\quad q=q_k,\quad
e=e_k,\quad \beta=\beta_k.
\]
The projection distributions are continuous.

Since \(\mu(H_k)/M<\tau<2/3\), one has
\[
\rho_0<\frac23<\frac78.
\]
Choose \(h\ge0\) such that
\[
\Pr(X<h)=\frac78,\qquad
\Pr(X\ge h)=\frac18.
\tag{12}
\]
Set
\[
\eta=\Pr(Y<-h).
\]
On the event
\[
\{X\ge h,\,-h\le Y\le0\},
\]
one has \(Y\le0\) and \(X+Y\ge0\). The pointwise integrand defining
\[
q=\Pr(X\le0)+\Pr(Y\le0)-\Pr(X+Y<0)
\]
is therefore at least one on this event. Independence and (12) give
\[
q\ge\frac18(\rho_2-\eta).
\]
Since \(\rho_2=p+q-\rho_0\),
\[
\eta\ge p-\rho_0-7q.
\tag{13}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
e=(1-r)\rho_0+rq.
\]
Because \(r<17/25<7/8\),
\[
\rho_0+7q
\le\frac7r e.
\]
Thus (13) yields
\[
\eta\ge p-\frac7r e.
\tag{14}
\]

If \(\eta=0\), then (14) and (11) imply
\[
e\ge\frac{rp}{7}\ge\frac r{21}
\ge\frac{13}{420}
>\frac1{200}.
\tag{15}
\]
Suppose henceforth that \(\eta>0\). Define the two convex caps
\[
A'_0=\{z_0\in A_0:X_k(z_0)<h\},
\qquad
A'_2=\{z_2\in A_2:Y_k(z_2)<-h\}.
\]
They have areas
\[
|A'_0|=\frac78M,\qquad
|A'_2|=\eta rM.
\]
For every \(z_0\in A'_0,z_2\in A'_2\),
\[
X_k(z_0)+Y_k(z_2)<0.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{A'_0+A'_2}{2}
\subseteq
B\cap\{z:\ell_k(1,z)<0\}.
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski gives
\[
\beta
\ge
\frac14
\left(
\sqrt{\frac78}+\sqrt{r\eta}
\right)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

On the other hand, (7), (11), and \(e\ge0\) give
\[
\beta
<
\tau-rp-e
\le
\tau-\frac r3
<
\frac{2-r}{3}.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (16) and (17),
\[
r\eta
<
\left(
2\sqrt{\frac{2-r}{3}}-\sqrt{\frac78}
\right)^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (11), (14), and (18) imply
\[
e>
E_0(r)
:=
\frac17
\left[
\frac r3-
\left(
2\sqrt{\frac{2-r}{3}}-\sqrt{\frac78}
\right)^2
\right].
\tag{19}
\]

For \(r<1\),
\[
E_0'(r)
=
\frac1{21}
\left(
5-
\frac{2\sqrt{7/8}}{\sqrt{(2-r)/3}}
\right)
>0.
\]
Therefore, using \(r\ge13/20\),
\[
E_0(r)\ge E_0\left(\frac{13}{20}\right)
=
\frac{36\sqrt{70}-295}{840}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\left(\frac{374}{45}\right)^2
=\frac{139876}{2025}
<70,
\]
so
\[
\frac{36\sqrt{70}-295}{840}
>\frac1{200}.
\]
Together with (15), this proves in all cases that
\[
e_k>\frac1{200}.
\tag{20}
\]

Now (10), (20), and \(\Delta\le3/200\) give
\[
\frac1{200}
<e_k
\le\sum_{j=1}^Ne_j
<\frac{\Delta}{3}
\le\frac1{200},
\]
a contradiction. Thus the asserted strict low-mass cover does not exist.

If every point of \(\{1\}\times B\) had depth below \(t\), choose at each point a halfspace of mass below \(t\), enlarge it slightly so that the point lies in its interior, and take a finite subcover by compactness. Applying planar Helly to the complements of the middle interior sections reduces this cover to at most three halfspaces, contradicting what was just proved. This establishes (2).

Finally, accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives, for \(r>1/2\), the residual upper boundary \(U(r)=2-r\) and lower boundary
\[
L(r)=\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\]
Hence (1) and (3) describe a nonempty part of the omitted upper branch. For example,
\[
r=\frac{13}{20},\qquad s=\frac{67}{50}
\]
has \(\Delta=1/100\), \(r<\rho\), and
\[
L(r)
=\frac{19}{40}+\sqrt{\frac{13}{20}}
<
\frac{67}{50}
<
\frac{27}{20}
=2-r.
\]
