## Statement

Let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(D\), and interpret all homotheties as centered at one fixed vertex \(V_0\) of \(\Delta\). Let
\[
a\ge1,\qquad 0<c\le\frac1{3\sqrt2},
\]
and set
\[
A_0=V_0+a(\Delta-V_0),\qquad A_1=\Delta,\qquad
A_2=V_0+c(\Delta-V_0),
\]
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
If \(g\) is the centroid of \(\Delta\), then
\[
h_S((1,g))\ge D\left(\frac49+c^2\right).
\]
Thus the conclusion holds in particular under the additional compatibility hypothesis \(a+c\le2\) appearing in the registered subgoal.

If also \(a^2<1+c^2\), then, with
\[
T=(a^2+1+c^2)D,
\]
one has
\[
h_S((1,g))>\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
In particular, accepted fact `8be4b2011a05cfd2` supplies this strict conclusion for every residual pair in the stated small-\(c\), \(a\ge1\) region.

## Proof

Use accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` with scale factors
\[
(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(a,1,c)
\]
and barycentric coordinates
\[
(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)=\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13\right)
\]
for \(g\). For a normalized projected direction, order the three projected vertex values as
\[
\gamma,\qquad \gamma+\rho,\qquad \gamma+1,
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1.
\]
Their average is
\[
m=\gamma+\frac{1+\rho}{3}.
\]

Write
\[
\phi_\rho(u):=\Phi_{\gamma,\rho}(\gamma+u).
\]
For \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
\phi_\rho(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2/\rho,&0\le u\le\rho,\\
1-(1-u)^2/(1-\rho),&\rho\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
For the tied lower endpoint \(\rho=0\), the nonconstant branch is
\[
\phi_0(u)=1-(1-u)^2,\qquad 0\le u\le1,
\]
and for the tied upper endpoint \(\rho=1\), it is
\[
\phi_1(u)=u^2,\qquad 0\le u\le1.
\]
Thus all tied directions are included.

The middle-slice contribution is
\[
p(\rho):=\phi_\rho\left(\frac{1+\rho}{3}\right)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{5-5\rho-\rho^2}{9(1-\rho)},
&0\le\rho\le\frac12,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{(1+\rho)^2}{9\rho},
&\frac12\le\rho\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \((1+\rho)/3\) lies above the middle knot when \(\rho\le1/2\) and below it when \(\rho\ge1/2\). On the first interval,
\[
p(\rho)-\frac12
=
\frac{(1-2\rho)(1+\rho)}{18(1-\rho)}
\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(c^2\le1/18\), (2) gives
\[
p(\rho)\ge\frac12\ge\frac49+c^2
\qquad
\left(0\le\rho\le\frac12\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Hence only \(1/2\le\rho\le1\) remains. On this interval, (1) becomes
\[
p(\rho)=\frac49+d(\rho),
\qquad
d(\rho):=\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{9\rho}.
\tag{4}
\]

We next record a direction-independent lower bound for the two outer caps. Define
\[
\psi(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le0,\\
u^2,&0\le u\le1,\\
1,&u\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\phi_\rho(u)\ge\psi(u)
\tag{5}
\]
for every \(0\le\rho\le1\). On the lower quadratic branch this follows from \(u^2/\rho\ge u^2\). On the upper branch,
\[
\phi_\rho(u)-u^2
=
\frac{(1-u)((2-\rho)u-\rho)}{1-\rho}\ge0,
\]
because \(u\ge\rho\); the endpoint splines satisfy the same inequality directly.

Suppose \(K\ge0\), \(a\ge c\), and
\[
au+cv=K.
\]
Put \(x=au\) and \(y=cv\). From (5),
\[
a^2\phi_\rho(u)+c^2\phi_\rho(v)
\ge
a^2\psi(x/a)+c^2\psi(y/c).
\]
If \(x,y\ge0\) and neither term is saturated, the right-hand side is
\[
x^2+y^2\ge\frac{(x+y)^2}{2}=\frac{K^2}{2}.
\]
If \(x\ge a\), it is at least \(a^2\ge c^2\), and if \(y\ge c\), it is at least \(c^2\). If \(x<0\), then \(y>K\); either \(y\ge c\), or the second term is \(y^2\ge K^2\). The case \(y<0\) is symmetric, using \(a\ge c\). Consequently,
\[
a^2\phi_\rho(u)+c^2\phi_\rho(v)
\ge
\min\left\{c^2,\frac{K^2}{2}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

For a halfspace parameter \(\sigma\), put
\[
u_0=\frac{m+\sigma}{a}-\gamma,\qquad
u_2=\frac{m-\sigma}{c}-\gamma.
\]
Its normalized nonhorizontal mass is
\[
p(\rho)+a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2),
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
au_0+cu_2=2m-(a+c)\gamma=:K.
\tag{8}
\]
We now treat the six projected-vertex chambers exactly.

If \(V_0\) is the minimum projected vertex, the permutations are
\[
(0,1,2),\qquad(0,2,1).
\]
Here \(\gamma=0\), so
\[
K=\frac{2(1+\rho)}3\ge1\ge\sqrt2\,c.
\tag{9}
\]
By (6), the outer contribution is at least \(c^2\).

If \(V_0\) is the middle projected vertex, the permutations are
\[
(1,0,2),\qquad(2,0,1).
\]
Here \(\gamma=-\rho\), so
\[
K=\frac23+\rho\left(a+c-\frac43\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Because \(a\ge1\), \(c<1/3\), and \(\rho\le1\),
\[
K\ge\frac23+\rho\left(c-\frac13\right)
\ge\frac13+c
\ge\frac13
\ge\sqrt2\,c.
\tag{11}
\]
Again (6) gives outer contribution at least \(c^2\).

If \(V_0\) is the maximum projected vertex, the permutations are
\[
(1,2,0),\qquad(2,1,0).
\]
Here \(\gamma=-1\), and therefore
\[
K=a+c-\frac43+\frac{2\rho}{3}
\ge c+\frac{2\rho-1}{3}.
\tag{12}
\]
Set
\[
t=2\rho-1\in[0,1],
\qquad
K_0=c+\frac t3.
\]
Since \(K\ge K_0\ge0\), (6) gives an outer contribution at least
\[
\min\left\{c^2,\frac{K_0^2}{2}\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
We claim
\[
d(\rho)+\frac{K_0^2}{2}\ge c^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Using
\[
\rho=\frac{1+t}{2},
\qquad
d(\rho)=\frac{(1-t)^2}{18(1+t)},
\]
the left side of (14) minus \(c^2\), multiplied by \(18\), is
\[
-9c^2+6ct+t^2+\frac{(1-t)^2}{1+t}.
\tag{15}
\]
For fixed \(t\), (15) is concave in \(c\), so its minimum on
\[
0\le c\le\frac1{3\sqrt2}
\]
occurs at an endpoint. At \(c=0\), it is nonnegative. At
\(c=1/(3\sqrt2)\), it equals
\[
-\frac12+\sqrt2\,t+t^2+\frac{(1-t)^2}{1+t}.
\]
Since \(1+t\le2\), this is at least
\[
-\frac12+\sqrt2\,t+t^2+\frac{(1-t)^2}{2}
=
(\sqrt2-1)t+\frac32t^2
\ge0.
\]
This proves (14). If \(K_0^2/2\ge c^2\), (13) itself is at least \(c^2\); otherwise (14) shows that its deficit is covered by \(d(\rho)\). Combining (4), (7), (13), and (14) therefore gives
\[
p(\rho)+a^2\phi_\rho(u_0)+c^2\phi_\rho(u_2)
\ge\frac49+c^2
\]
also in the two maximum-vertex chambers.

The paired formulas agree on tied chamber boundaries: at \(\rho=0\), the minimum-vertex and middle-vertex formulas both give \(K=2/3\), and at \(\rho=1\), the middle-vertex and maximum-vertex formulas both give \(K=a+c-2/3\). Thus no tied direction is omitted.

Finally, the two horizontal halfspaces in accepted fact `cb46be6a5648e0bd` have normalized masses
\[
a^2+1,\qquad 1+c^2,
\]
both at least \(4/9+c^2\). Hence every halfspace through \((1,g)\) has mass at least
\[
D\left(\frac49+c^2\right),
\]
which proves the depth bound.

If \(a^2<1+c^2\), then
\[
\left(\frac49+c^2\right)
-\frac{2(a^2+1+c^2)}9
=
\frac{2+7c^2-2a^2}{9}
>
\frac{5c^2}{9}>0.
\]
Accepted fact `8be4b2011a05cfd2` states that every residual pair satisfies \(a^2<1+c^2\), giving the asserted strict residual conclusion.
