## Statement

Write
\[
\mathcal L=\{(x,y):x\le-|y|\},\qquad
\mathcal R=\{(x,y):x\ge|y|\},\qquad
\mathcal U=\{(x,y):y\ge|x|\},
\]

\[

\]
The complete normalized support-contact hypothesis list used here is the
following.

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(N1)}
&A_0,A_2,K\text{ are compact two-dimensional convex polygons},\\
&A_0\subset\mathcal L,\quad A_2\subset\mathcal R,\quad

\mathrm{(N2)}
&M:=|A_0|>k:=|K|>m:=|A_2|>0,\\

\mathrm{(N3)}

\mathrm{(N4)}
&|K\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|=|K\cap\{x-y\ge0\}|=\pi.\\[1mm]

&|(G\setminus B)\cap\{x-y\ge0\}|<\pi-m/4.\\[1mm]

Let

\[

\qquad

\tag{20}

\tag{21}

masses simultaneously if it admits two finite positive base measures
\(\lambda_Q,\lambda_N\) for which the induced lifts are unit-dominated
and satisfy (21) and (22), respectively.  The same obstruction below
also excludes a single two-labelled lift whose two labelled components

\tag{25}

Finally, this is not a counterexample to the registered lower functional.

The actual forced-tail midpoint body \((Q^+-P)/2\) has positive area

## Proof

We first check (N1)--(N9) in their displayed order.  Every point of
\(A_0\) has \(x\le-20\) and \(|y|\le19\), while the vertical
section of \(A_2\) at \(0\le x\le400\) is
\[
\left[-\frac{x}{400},\frac{x}{400}\right].
\]
Thus \(A_0\subset\mathcal L\) and \(A_2\subset\mathcal R\).
The cyclic vertices of \(B\) are
\[
\left(-\frac{2\,885}{19},-\frac{19}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{915}{19},-10\right),(190,-10),(190,10),
\left(\frac{915}{19},10\right),
\left(-\frac{2\,885}{19},\frac{19}{2}\right).
\]
They all lie in \(K\), proving (N1).

Direct area calculation gives
\[
M=38\left(\frac{5\,770}{19}-20\right)=10\,780,
\qquad
m=400,\qquad k=386\cdot20=7\,720.
\]
Hence
\[
t=\frac{2(10\,780+400+7\,720)}9=4\,200,
\qquad
\pi=t-m=3\,800,
\]
and
\[
2M=21\,560=7m+9\pi-2k.
\]
This proves every assertion in (N2).

The left side of \(A_0\) is \(x=-\delta\), so
\(\min_{A_0}x=-\delta\).  On \(A_2\), both \(x+y\) and
\(x-y\) are nonnegative and vanish only at the origin.  This proves
(N3), including the two contact points.

For \(-10\le y\le10\), the \(x+y\ge0\) cap of \(K\) has
horizontal length \(190+y\), while the \(x-y\ge0\) cap has length
\(190-y\).  Therefore
\[
\int_{-10}^{10}(190+y)\,dy
=\int_{-10}^{10}(190-y)\,dy
=3\,800=\pi,
\]
proving (N4).

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1\).  Since both \(x+y\) and \(x-y\) are at
most \(-1\) on \(A_0\),
\[
A_0\cap\{x+y\ge-\varepsilon\}
=A_0\cap\{x-y\ge-\varepsilon\}=\varnothing.
\]
Each of the two nonnegative affine functions \(x+y,x-y\) has a
positive-area sublevel strip \(0\le x\pm y<\varepsilon\) in the
two-dimensional triangle \(A_2\).  Hence
\[
|A_2\cap\{x+y\ge\varepsilon\}|<m,\qquad
|A_2\cap\{x-y\ge\varepsilon\}|<m.
\]
Since \(t=\pi+m\), both strict inequalities in (N5) follow.

The absolute section is
\[
C=197\cdot20=3\,940,\qquad C-\pi=140.
\]
The tail cut is
\[
x\ge\delta+2=\frac{5\,808}{19}.
\]
Integrating the vertical section length \(x/200\) of \(A_2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta:=|A_2^+|
&=400\left(1-\left(\frac{5\,808}{7\,600}\right)^2\right)\\
&=\frac{1\,501\,696}{9\,025}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The line \(x=\delta+2\) has area zero, so \(v_1=m-\theta\).
Consequently
\[
C+m-t=140>0,
\]
\[
t-C-v_1=\theta-140
=\frac{238\,196}{9\,025}>0.
\]
This proves (N6), as well as the positive-area assertion in (N7).
For \(a_0\in A_0\) and \(a_2\in A_2^+\),
\[
x\left(\frac{a_0+a_2}{2}\right)
\ge\frac{-\delta+(\delta+2)}2=1,
\]
which completes (N7).

Here
\[
Y=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,1),(1,10),(10,10)\}.
\]
Direct convex-hull enumeration gives the cyclic vertices
\[
\begin{aligned}
G=\operatorname{conv}\biggl\{&
\left(-\frac{2\,885}{19},-\frac{19}{2}\right),
\left(\frac{915}{19},-10\right),(190,-10),(190,10),\\
&(1,10),
\left(-\frac{2\,885}{19},\frac{19}{2}\right)
\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the only region added to \(B\) is the triangle with horizontal
base from \(x=1\) to \(x=915/19\) and height \(1/2\), and
\[
|G\setminus B|
=\frac12\left(\frac{915}{19}-1\right)\frac12
=\frac{224}{19}.
\]
Each of the two diagonal excesses in (N8) is at most this total excess,
and
\[
\frac{224}{19}<3\,700=\pi-\frac m4.
\]
This proves (N8).  Finally, the minimum of either transformed coordinate
on \(K\) is \(-196-10=-206\), so
\[
\alpha=\beta=206.
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
R_0=7C+2\pi-2k=19\,740>0,
\qquad
R_0-4\alpha\beta=-150\,004<0,
\]
which proves (N9).  The full list (1) is now checked.

We next derive the argmin correspondence.  Direct transformation gives
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(39,1),
\left(\frac{6\,131}{19},\frac{5\,409}{19}\right),
\left(\frac{5\,409}{19},\frac{6\,131}{19}\right),
(1,39)
\right\},
\]
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(399,401),(401,399)\}.
\tag{28}
\]
Both nonzero vertices of \(Q\) come from points with
\(x=400>\delta+2\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup Q^+)=Q,
\]
which proves (8)--(10).  Since \(D\) has absolute determinant \(2\),
(27) gives
\[
|Q^+|=2\theta
=\frac{3\,003\,392}{9\,025}.
\tag{29}
\]

The lower endpoint of a horizontal section of \(Q/2\) lies on the
edge from \((0,0)\) to \((399/2,401/2)\).  Thus
\[
\gamma_-(z)=\frac{399}{401}z,
\qquad 0\le z\le\frac{401}{2}.
\tag{30}
\]
The right endpoint of a horizontal section of \(P\) is
\[
p_+(\eta)=
\begin{cases}
\eta+38,
&1\le\eta\le\dfrac{5\,409}{19},\\[2mm]
\dfrac{11\,540}{19}-\eta,
&\dfrac{5\,409}{19}\le\eta\le\dfrac{6\,131}{19}.
\end{cases}
\]
Since \(E=-P/2\),
\[
e_-(y)=-\frac12p_+(-2y)
=
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{5\,770}{19}-y,
&-\dfrac{6\,131}{38}\le y\le-\dfrac{5\,409}{38},\\[2mm]
y-19,
&-\dfrac{5\,409}{38}\le y\le-\dfrac12.
\end{cases}
\tag{31}
\]

For every \(v\) in the height projection of \(W\), the Minkowski-sum
definition and (10) give the exact lower-endpoint formula
\[
a_W(v)
=\min_{\substack{z+y=v\\
I_{\mathcal C}(z),I_E(y)\ne\varnothing}}
\bigl(\gamma_-(z)+e_-(y)\bigr).
\tag{32}
\]
The minimum exists by compactness.  As a function of \(z\), the
objective in (32) has slope
\[
1+\frac{399}{401}>0
\]
when \(v-z\) is in the first branch of (31), and slope
\[
\frac{399}{401}-1<0
\]
when \(v-z\) is in the second branch.  Hence the unique minimizing
height is
\[
z_*(v)=
\begin{cases}
0,
&-\dfrac{6\,131}{38}\le v\le-\dfrac{5\,409}{38},\\[2mm]
v+\dfrac{5\,409}{38},
&-\dfrac{5\,409}{38}\le v\le\dfrac{1\,105}{19},\\[2mm]
\dfrac{401}{2},
&\dfrac{1\,105}{19}\le v\le200.
\end{cases}
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (11), (32), and (33) prove (12).  They also show that the
height pair in every fiber \(\Omega_v\) is unique, although (13)--(15)
still allow arbitrary probability distributions over the full product
of the two horizontal sections at that pair.

We now prove the body-marginal obstruction.  Let
\[
h_{\mathcal C}(u,z)=z,\qquad h_E(x,y)=y.
\]
For a unit-dominated lift, define its height-pair projection
\[
\Pi=(h_{\mathcal C}\circ\rho_{\mathcal C},
h_E\circ\rho_E)_\#
\Lambda_{\lambda,\kappa}.
\tag{34}
\]
By (13), (15), and (16), \(\Pi\) is supported on \(\Gamma\), and
\[
\|\Pi\|=\|\Lambda_{\lambda,\kappa}\|
=\lambda(\mathbb R),
\tag{35}
\]
because every \(\kappa_v\) is a probability measure.

Define the section lengths
\[
w_{\mathcal C}(z)=|I_{\mathcal C}(z)|,\qquad
w_E(y)=|I_E(y)|.
\]
Cavalieri's theorem and the definitions (18)--(20) give
\[
(h_{\mathcal C})_\#\mu_{\mathcal C}
=w_{\mathcal C}(z)\,dz
+\sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
w_{\mathcal C}(\zeta)\delta_\zeta
=:\nu_{\mathcal C},
\]
\[
(h_E)_\#\mu_E
=w_E(y)\,dy
+\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal Y}
w_E(\eta)\delta_\eta
=:\nu_E.
\tag{36}
\]
Pushing the two inequalities in (20) forward by the height maps yields
the formal height-marginal domination
\[
(\operatorname{pr}_z)_\#\Pi\le\nu_{\mathcal C},
\qquad
(\operatorname{pr}_y)_\#\Pi\le\nu_E.
\tag{37}
\]

The two endpoint sections of \(\mathcal C\) which support the vertical
argmin segments in (12) are points.  Therefore
\[
w_{\mathcal C}(0)
=w_{\mathcal C}\left(\frac{401}{2}\right)=0.
\tag{38}
\]
At the horizontal argmin segment, the relevant \(P\)-section is
\[
\left[\frac{4\,687}{19},\frac{6\,131}{19}\right]
\times\left\{\frac{5\,409}{19}\right\}.
\]
The homothety by \(1/2\) gives
\[
w_E\left(-\frac{5\,409}{38}\right)
=\frac12\left(\frac{6\,131-4\,687}{19}\right)=38.
\tag{39}
\]

Partition \(\Gamma\) into the first vertical segment without its upper
endpoint, the full horizontal segment, and the third vertical segment
without its lower endpoint.  By (37)--(38), the first and third pieces
have zero \(\Pi\)-mass, because their \(z\)-projections are the
singletons \(0\) and \(401/2\), respectively.  All remaining mass is on
the horizontal piece \(y=-5\,409/38\).  Equations (36)--(39) give
\[
\|\Pi\|
\le\nu_E\left(\left\{-\frac{5\,409}{38}\right\}\right)
=38.
\]
Together with (35), this proves (23).  Notice that this argument starts
from the body measures (19), so it already includes arbitrary smearing
over every full horizontal section product in (13).

The preservation contradictions are now formal.  In original
coordinates, the quadrant \(u\le0,v\le0\) is
\(x\le-|y|\).  Hence
\[
H=2\pi=7\,600,
\]
\[
N=2\int_{-10}^{10}(196-|y|)\,dy=7\,640.
\]
Thus an \((N-H)\)-preserving lift has, by (22) and (35),
\[
\|\Pi\|=\lambda(\mathbb R)=40>38,
\]
contradicting (23).  A \(Q^+\)-preserving lift has, by (21), (29),
and (35),
\[
\|\Pi\|=\lambda(\mathbb R)
=\frac{3\,003\,392}{9\,025}>40>38,
\]
again contradicting (23).  Therefore neither preserving lift exists,
and a fortiori no single kernel admits both.  For a two-labelled lift,
either labelled component alone gives the same contradiction.

It remains to verify the positive functional calibration and the
nonzero tail halves.  A horizontal section of \(K'\) has, for
\(v\le0\) in the relevant range,
\[
r(v)=v+20.
\]
Thus
\[
J=[-9,0].
\tag{40}
\]
The cyclic vertices of \(W=D(B)\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(-\frac{6\,131}{38},-\frac{5\,409}{38}\right),
\left(\frac{725}{19},\frac{1\,105}{19}\right),(180,200),
(200,180),\\
&\left(\frac{1\,105}{19},\frac{725}{19}\right),
\left(-\frac{5\,409}{38},-\frac{6\,131}{38}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
On \(J\), (30)--(33) give
\[
a_W(v)=\frac{399}{401}v-\frac{150\,170}{7\,619},
\qquad a_W(v)<2-v.
\]
Direct integration yields
\[
\int_J
\left(5(2-v)-a_W(v)-4r(v)\right)\,dv
=-\frac{364\,284}{7\,619}.
\tag{42}
\]
For \(v>0\), the cut \(2-v\) lies between the endpoints of
\(I_W(v)\) precisely for
\[
0<v<\frac{5\,169}{475}.
\]
Indeed, on the range containing this interval, (41) gives
\[
b_W(v)=\frac{401}{399}v+\frac{150\,170}{7\,581}>2-v,
\]
whereas
\[
2-v-a_W(v)
=\frac{165\,408-15\,200v}{7\,619}
\]
is positive exactly before \(v=5\,169/475\) and is thereafter
nonpositive.  Hence
\[
5\int_{v>0}
\left[\min\{b_W(v),2-v\}-a_W(v)\right]_+\,dv
=\frac{427\,496\,976}{723\,805}.
\tag{43}
\]
Adding (42) and (43) gives (26).

Finally, \(R=(Q^+-P)/2\) is two-dimensional.  Its height projection
has endpoints
\[
\frac12\left(
\frac{399}{400}\frac{5\,808}{19}-\frac{6\,131}{19}
\right)
=-\frac{4\,219}{475}<0,
\qquad
\frac{401-1}{2}=200>0.
\]
Thus \(R\) has positive area in each open halfplane \(v<0\) and
\(v>0\).  The marginal obstruction is present away from \(T=0\) and
away from both zero-half strata.  Since (26) is positive, the packet
does not refute the registered functional or the main theorem.
