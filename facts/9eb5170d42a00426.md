## Statement

Use the objects of accepted fact `48df266dc82dd70d`, explicitly
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2\},\qquad
v_0=(0,0),\quad v_1=(1,0),\quad v_2=(0,1),
\qquad g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
and let \(Q\) have cyclic vertices
\[
q_0=\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
q_1=\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
q_2=\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
q_3=\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
For every real \(\alpha>0\), put
\[
M_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\ \alpha&1+\alpha^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B_\alpha=g+M_\alpha(\Delta-g).
\]
Then
\[
\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}\subseteq B_\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0<\alpha\leq\frac{63}{1060}.
\]
For every \(0<\alpha\leq63/1060\), set
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=B_\alpha,\qquad A_2=Q,
\qquad
S_\alpha=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i).
\]
One has
\[
|Q|=\frac{27}{16000},\qquad
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{16027}{16000},
\qquad t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000},
\]
and
\[
h_{S_\alpha}((1,g))\geq t+\frac{21}{16000}>t.
\]
Consequently no collection of closed halfspaces of \(S_\alpha\)-mass strictly below \(t\), even a collection of arbitrary cardinality, can cover \(\{1\}\times B_\alpha\) by its interiors. In particular, the middle-slice requirement in accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` fails for every compatible member of the family.

## Proof

Since \(\det M_\alpha=1\), the inverse matrix is
\[
M_\alpha^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}1+\alpha^2&-\alpha\\-\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(z=(x,y)\), define its barycentric coordinates relative to \(B_\alpha\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
X(z)&=(1+\alpha^2)x-\alpha y+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^2}{3},\\
Y(z)&=-\alpha x+y+\frac\alpha3,\\
Z(z)&=1-X(z)-Y(z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \((X(z),Y(z))=g+M_\alpha^{-1}(z-g)\), so
\[
B_\alpha=\{z:X(z)\geq0,\ Y(z)\geq0,\ Z(z)\geq0\}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first determine compatibility. Each function in (1) is affine, hence for \(d\in\Delta\) and \(q\in Q\),
\[
X\left(\frac{d+q}{2}\right)=\frac{X(d)+X(q)}2,
\tag{3}
\]
and likewise for \(Y,Z\).

Suppose \(0<\alpha\leq63/1060\). On \(\Delta\), the minimum of \(X\) is attained at \(v_2\), where
\[
X(v_2)=-\frac{\alpha(2+\alpha)}3.
\]
On \(Q\), the minimum is attained at \(q_0\). To check this, subtract \(X(q_0)\) from the other three vertex values:
\[
\frac{1+\alpha^2}{2}-\frac\alpha{400},\qquad
\frac{9(1+\alpha^2)}{20}-\frac{3\alpha}{400},\qquad
\frac{1+\alpha^2}{20}-\frac\alpha{400},
\]
which are positive in the stated range. Thus (3) gives
\[
\min_{z\in(\Delta+Q)/2}X(z)
=\frac{3-23\alpha-37\alpha^2}{120}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
The numerator in (4) is decreasing for \(\alpha>0\), and at \(63/1060\) it equals \(1688007/1123600>0\).

The minimum of \(Y\) on \(\Delta\) is \(Y(v_1)=-2\alpha/3\). Its four values on \(Q\) are
\[
\frac1{20}+\frac{17\alpha}{60},\qquad
\frac{21}{400}-\frac{13\alpha}{60},\qquad
\frac{23}{400}-\frac\alpha6,\qquad
\frac{21}{400}+\frac{7\alpha}{30}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(0<\alpha\leq1/200\), the first entry in (5) is the minimum; for \(\alpha\geq1/200\), the second is the minimum. Indeed, in the first range the other three entries minus the first are
\[
\frac1{400}-\frac\alpha2,\qquad
\frac3{400}-\frac{9\alpha}{20},\qquad
\frac1{400}-\frac\alpha{20},
\]
and in the second range the first, third, and fourth entries minus the second are
\[
\frac\alpha2-\frac1{400},\qquad
\frac1{200}+\frac\alpha{20},\qquad
\frac{9\alpha}{20}.
\]
All are nonnegative in their respective ranges. Hence
\[
\min_{z\in(\Delta+Q)/2}Y(z)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac1{40}-\frac{23\alpha}{120}>0,
&0<\alpha\leq1/200,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle \frac{21}{800}-\frac{53\alpha}{120}\geq0,
&1/200\leq\alpha\leq63/1060.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality is an equality exactly at \(\alpha=63/1060\).

At the vertices of \(\Delta\), the values of \(Z\) are
\[
\frac{3-2\alpha+\alpha^2}{3},\qquad
\frac{\alpha(1-2\alpha)}3,\qquad
\frac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}3,
\tag{7}
\]
all positive because \(\alpha<3/50<1/2\). Every point \((x,y)\in Q\) has \(1-x-y\geq159/400\). Since \(0<\alpha<1\), equation (1) gives
\[
Z(x,y)
\geq1-x-y-\frac{2\alpha-\alpha^2}{3}
>\frac{159}{400}-\frac1{25}
=\frac{143}{400}>0,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(\alpha\leq63/1060<3/50\) was used. Equations (3), (7), and (8) make \(Z\) positive on \((\Delta+Q)/2\). Together with (2), (4), and (6), this proves compatibility throughout \((0,63/1060]\).

Conversely, the point \((v_1+q_1)/2\) belongs to \((\Delta+Q)/2\), and exact substitution in (1) gives
\[
Y\left(\frac{v_1+q_1}{2}\right)
=\frac{63-1060\alpha}{2400}.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(\alpha>63/1060\), this is negative, so (2) excludes that point from \(B_\alpha\). This proves the exact compatibility set.

We next prove uniform middle protection. Shoelace evaluation gives \(|Q|=27/16000\), and \(\det M_\alpha=1\) gives \(|B_\alpha|=|\Delta|=1/2\), proving the displayed values of \(T\) and \(t\).

We need two exact planar estimates. First, put
\[
C=g+\frac{17}{20}(\Delta-g).
\]
The barycentric coordinates of every point of \(C\) relative to \(\Delta\) are at least \(1/20\). The displayed vertices of \(Q\) have \(x\geq1/20\), \(y\geq1/20\), and \(1-x-y\geq159/400>1/20\), so
\[
Q\subseteq C.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(\ell\) be any nonzero linear functional centered at \(g\), so \(\ell(g)=0\), and write \(-A\) for its minimum on \(\Delta\). Order and relabel the vertex values as
\[
-A,\qquad A-D,\qquad D,
\]
where \(A,D>0\). Their sum is zero, and the ordering is equivalent to
\[
\frac12\leq\rho:=\frac DA\leq2.
\tag{11}
\]
We claim
\[
\left|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}\right|
+\left|\Delta\cap\left\{\ell\geq\frac{17A}{20}\right\}\right|
\geq\frac14.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(1/2\leq\rho\leq1\), the first cap alone has area
\[
\frac12\left(1-\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}\right)\geq\frac14.
\]
For \(1\leq\rho\leq2\), similar triangles give the sum in (12) as
\[
\frac{\rho^2+(\rho-17/20)^2}
{2(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}.
\]
Its difference from \(1/4\) is
\[
\frac{400\rho^2-880\rho+489}
{800(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}
=\frac{400(\rho-11/10)^2+5}
{800(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}>0.
\]
This proves (12). The same cap formulas also show that every halfplane through the centroid of a triangle of area \(1/2\) has area at least \(2/9\): in the second range,
\[
\frac{\rho^2}{2(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}-\frac29
=\frac{(\rho-2)^2}{18(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}\geq0,
\]
while the first range gives the stronger lower bound \(1/4\). This applies to \(B_\alpha\) because it is a determinant-one affine image of \(\Delta\) fixing the centroid.

Second, define
\[
\delta_\alpha=|\Delta\setminus B_\alpha|.
\]
On \(\Delta\), the coordinate \(Z\) in (1) is positive by (7). The negative region of \(X\) is the corner triangle at \(v_2\), and the negative region of \(Y\) is the corner triangle at \(v_1\). They are disjoint because \(X+Y\) is positive at all three vertices of \(\Delta\). More explicitly, the three vertex values of \(X+Y\) are
\[
\frac{\alpha(2-\alpha)}3,\qquad
1-\frac\alpha3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{3},\qquad
1-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^2}{3},
\]
which are positive in the compatible range. The two corner-triangle area ratios therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_\alpha
&=\frac{2\alpha}{9(1+\alpha)}
+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)^2}{18(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)}\\
&=\frac{\alpha(\alpha^3+9\alpha^2+12\alpha+8)}
{18(1+\alpha)(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
For completeness, the first term in (13) is
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac{2\alpha}{3(1+\alpha)},
\]
obtained from the two zero crossings of \(Y\) on the edges out of \(v_1\). The second is
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac{\alpha+2}{3}\cdot
\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}{3(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)},
\]
obtained analogously from \(X\) at \(v_2\).

The derivative of (13) is
\[
\delta_\alpha'
=\frac{\alpha^6+4\alpha^5+12\alpha^4+24\alpha^3
+35\alpha^2+24\alpha+8}
{18(1+\alpha)^2(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)^2}>0.
\]
Since \(63/1060<3/50\), every compatible \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
\delta_\alpha
<\delta_{3/50}
=\frac{1094077}{42278100}
<\frac{13}{500},
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
\frac{13}{500}-\frac{1094077}{42278100}
=\frac{6442}{52847625}>0.
\]

Now let \(H\) be a closed halfspace containing \((1,g)\). If necessary, translate its boundary parallel toward \((1,g)\); the resulting halfspace is contained in \(H\), has boundary through \((1,g)\), and can only have smaller mass. It is therefore enough to consider boundary-through-point halfspaces. The whole-space case, if included in the convention, has mass \(T\). If the planar normal vanishes, the two proper possibilities have masses \(1\) and \(1/2+27/16000\), both larger than \(t\). Otherwise write
\[
H=\{(h,z):\ell(z)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\},
\qquad \ell(g)=0,
\tag{15}
\]
with \(\ell\neq0\). Its sections at heights \(0,1,2\) are respectively
\[
\{\ell\geq\beta\},\qquad
\{\ell\geq0\},\qquad
\{\ell\geq-\beta\}.
\tag{16}
\]

If the last section contains all of \(Q\), the centroid-cap bound gives
\[
\mathcal H_2(S_\alpha\cap H)
\geq\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}
=t+\frac{21}{16000}.
\tag{17}
\]
Otherwise choose \(q\in Q\) with \(\ell(q)+\beta<0\). By (10),
\[
\ell(q)\geq\min_C\ell
=\frac{17}{20}\min_\Delta\ell
=-\frac{17A}{20},
\]
so \(\beta<17A/20\). Hence the height-zero section in (16) contains \(\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq17A/20\}\). Moreover,
\[
|B_\alpha\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|
\geq|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|-\delta_\alpha.
\]
Dropping the nonnegative height-two contribution and using (12) and (14),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_2(S_\alpha\cap H)
&\geq
\left|\Delta\cap\left\{\ell\geq\frac{17A}{20}\right\}\right|
+|\Delta\cap\{\ell\geq0\}|-\delta_\alpha\\
&>\frac14-\frac{13}{500}
=\frac{28}{125}
=t+\frac{101}{72000}
>t+\frac{21}{16000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (17) and (18), followed by the infimum over all \(H\), prove
\[
h_{S_\alpha}((1,g))\geq t+\frac{21}{16000}>t.
\]
Finally, \(g\) is in the interior of the nondegenerate triangle \(B_\alpha\). Any interior cover of the middle slice contains \((1,g)\) in the interior of one of its halfspaces, but every such closed halfspace has mass strictly above \(t\). Thus no strict-low interior cover of the middle slice exists.
