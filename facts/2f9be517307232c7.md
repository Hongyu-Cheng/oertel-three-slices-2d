## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty planar convex bodies
with finite positive planar Lebesgue area. Write \(|\cdot|\) for planar
Lebesgue area, set
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\qquad
a_i=|A_i|,
\qquad
T=a_0+a_1+a_2,
\]
and define the slice measure
\[
\mu(B)=\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|\{z\in A_i:(i,z)\in B\}\right|
\]
for measurable \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^3\). For \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu(S\cap H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^3
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]
If \(a_k/T\ge 1/2\) for some \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), then, for the planar centroid
\[
g_k=\frac1{a_k}\int_{A_k}z\,dz,
\]
the point \(y_k=(k,g_k)\in S\) satisfies
\[
h_S(y_k)\ge \frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

We use the planar Grünbaum inequality: if \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a
convex body with centroid \(g\), then every closed halfplane \(Q\) whose
boundary contains \(g\) satisfies
\[
|A\cap Q|\ge \left(\frac23\right)^2|A|=\frac49|A|.
\]
The same bound holds for every closed halfplane containing \(g\): translate
its boundary parallel toward \(g\) until the boundary passes through \(g\).
The resulting halfplane is contained in the original one.

This is Theorem 2 of Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of
convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960),
no. 4, 1257–1261,
https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. The theorem proves that
every halfspace containing the centroid of an \(n\)-dimensional convex body
has volume at least \((n/(n+1))^n\) times the body's volume; for \(n=2\),
this is \(4/9\).

Fix any closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) containing \(y_k\). After
orienting its defining inequality, write
\[
H=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2:\alpha x+u\cdot z\ge c\},
\]
where \((\alpha,u)\ne0\).

If \(u=0\), then \(y_k\in H\) gives \(\alpha k\ge c\), so the entire slice
plane \(\{k\}\times\mathbb R^2\) lies in \(H\). Hence
\[
\mu(S\cap H)\ge a_k\ge\frac49a_k.
\]

If \(u\ne0\), the section of \(H\) by the \(k\)-th slice plane is the planar
halfspace
\[
Q=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:u\cdot z\ge c-\alpha k\}.
\]
Since \(y_k\in H\), we have \(g_k\in Q\). Moreover,
\[
Q_0=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:u\cdot z\ge u\cdot g_k\}\subseteq Q,
\]
and the boundary of \(Q_0\) contains \(g_k\). Grünbaum's inequality therefore
gives
\[
|A_k\cap Q|
\ge |A_k\cap Q_0|
\ge\frac49a_k.
\]
Thus every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(y_k\), whether its boundary is
tilted or parallel to the slice planes and whether \(y_k\) lies on its
boundary or in its interior, satisfies
\[
\mu(S\cap H)\ge\frac49a_k.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) yields
\[
h_S(y_k)\ge\frac49a_k.
\]
Since \(a_k/T\ge1/2\),
\[
h_S(y_k)\ge\frac49a_k
\ge\frac49\cdot\frac T2
=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
