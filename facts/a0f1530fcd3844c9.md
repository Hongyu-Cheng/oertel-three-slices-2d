## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal C(z;\alpha)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\ge\alpha\}\right|.
\]
There exists a constant
\[
r_*\in\left(0,\frac15\right)
\tag{1}
\]
with the following property.

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\det U\ne0,\qquad \det V\ne0,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\ge0
\qquad(k=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|,\qquad
r=\frac AB,\qquad S=\frac1B,
\tag{4}
\]
and assume
\[
A\le B,\qquad
0<r<r_*,\qquad
1-r<S<1+\frac r2.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(R\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), \(\tau\in\mathbb R^3\), and
\(\delta>0\) satisfy
\[
\det R\ne0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3R_{ij}=\delta\quad(j=1,2,3),\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\tau_i>\delta.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
M_i
=\mathcal C(R_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((RU)_{i*};-\tau_i)
+B\mathcal C((RV)_{i*};\tau_i)
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\ \max_{1\le i\le3}M_i\ge t.\ }
\tag{9}
\]

In particular, put
\[
P=3I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathcal O=
\left\{\frac1{\sqrt{18}}\Pi P:
\Pi\text{ is a permutation matrix}\right\},
\tag{10}
\]
and take the explicit constant \(\eta_*=1\). Under (2)--(8), the
implication
\[
\operatorname{dist}_F
\left(\frac R{\lVert R\rVert_F},\mathcal O\right)<\eta_*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_iM_i\ge t
\tag{11}
\]
holds whenever \(0<r<r_*\). In fact the distance hypothesis in (11)
is unnecessary.

## Proof

Suppose no \(r_*\) in (1) exists. For every positive integer \(n\), choose
data satisfying (2)--(8), with
\[
0<r_n<\min\left\{\frac1n,\frac15\right\},
\tag{12}
\]
such that
\[
M_{i,n}<t_n\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(r_n\to0\). These are exact real systems; no definability,
Puiseux expansion, or relation among the rates of the remaining
variables is imposed.

First record where such a sequence would have to lie in cover-matrix
space. Conditions (2)--(8) are the sequence hypotheses of accepted fact
`0caa344e37fc948a`. Since (13) gives
\[
\limsup_n\max_i(M_{i,n}-t_n)\le0,
\]
that fact yields
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
Accepted fact `91c3b1cc058a6768` now applies and gives
\[
\operatorname{dist}_F
\left(
\frac{R_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F},
\mathcal O
\right)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{15}
\]
together with convergence of all three middle caps to \(4/9\) and
vanishing of all three large-end caps. Thus every hypothetical arbitrary-rate
countersequence really does enter the balanced scale-zero boundary; no
non-Puiseux escape can stay outside it.

We now use the stronger geometric pinch contradiction. Put
\[
L_n=\frac{R_n}{\delta_n},\qquad
\sigma_n=\frac{\tau_n}{\delta_n}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_i(L_n)_{ij}=1,\qquad
\det L_n\ne0,\qquad
\sum_i\sigma_{i,n}>1,
\]
and positive homogeneity of \(\mathcal C\) shows that (13) is unchanged.
Because \(r_n<1/5\), condition (5) lies inside the exact residual strip.
Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` therefore reconstructs from
\((U_n,V_n,L_n,\sigma_n)\) a genuine compatible triangular
counterexample with slice areas
\[
A_n:1:B_n.
\tag{16}
\]
This uses the exact compatibility equivalence (3), not an area-only
relaxation.

Divide every planar area and every halfspace mass by \(B_n\), and reflect
the height coordinate so that the endpoint of area \(B_n\) is at height
zero. The normalized slice areas are
\[
|A_{0,n}|=1,\qquad
|A_{2,n}|=r_n,\qquad
|A_{1,n}|=S_n.
\tag{17}
\]
Counterexample status is unchanged by this common positive area scaling
and by height reflection. Moreover, accepted fact
`f74f24523b5f53b2`, or directly (5), gives
\[
1-r_n<S_n<1+\frac{r_n}{2}<1+r_n.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus (17)--(18) are exactly the pinch normalization for the accepted
moving-frame facts.

Choose an arbitrary normalized minimal three-halfspace certificate for
each counterexample in (17). Let \(p_{i,n}\) be its three middle-cap
areas, let \(e_{i,n}\) be its actual paired endpoint contributions, and
put
\[
D_n=\frac{4S_n}{3}-\sum_{i=1}^3p_{i,n}.
\tag{19}
\]
Accepted fact `33f64ca8c4f58f43` treats the exact paired thresholds,
including a top cap at a vertex or support edge and both possible
point-or-segment limits of the small endpoint. It gives, for all
sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
D_n>\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{20}
\]
On the other hand, accepted fact `8261fac79ed77a4f` is the
arbitrary-rate moving-frame stability estimate for the same certificate.
It gives
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{D_n}{r_n}\le0,
\tag{21}
\]
and in particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
D_n\le\frac{2r_n}{3}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (20) and (22) contradict one another.

Hence a sequence (12)--(13) cannot exist. If (9) failed for arbitrarily
small positive \(r\), choosing one failure with \(r<1/n\) would produce
exactly such a sequence. Therefore some \(r_*\in(0,1/5)\) satisfies
(9). The localized implication (11) follows immediately, with
\(\eta_*=1\), because (9) has no distance hypothesis. The accepted
moving-frame arguments invoked above explicitly include all threshold
transition strata and make no assumption on relative rates, completing
the claimed uniform upgrade.
