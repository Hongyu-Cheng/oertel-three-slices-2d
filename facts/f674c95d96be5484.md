## Statement

Work in the polytopal \(A_2\)-only setting of accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e`: three pairwise interior-disjoint outer caps \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) have equal area, the central remainder has twice that area, and \(\mathcal T_Q\) is the global triangle cut out by the three halfplanes complementary to the cap halfplanes. Suppose \(A_2\) is a quadrilateral with type-2 cap incidence. After relabeling, \(C_1\) strictly contains adjacent vertices \(v_1,v_2\), while \(C_2,C_3\) strictly contain \(v_3,v_4\), respectively. Assume there is an affine reflection \(\sigma\) such that
\[
\sigma(v_1)=v_2,\qquad \sigma(v_3)=v_4,
\qquad \sigma(C_1)=C_1,\qquad \sigma(C_2)=C_3.
\]
If \(A_2\) is a parallelogram, accepted fact `1de8e08b08202b2b` excludes it. Otherwise,
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|<\frac{17}{2}|A_2|.
\]
After the physical area-one normalization of accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e`, this contradicts its required bound
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}
\]
or, equivalently, its normal-form condition \(D\le4/169\). Hence no reflection-symmetric type-2 quadrilateral occurs in a full strict-table realization.

More explicitly, an invertible affine normalization sends the reflection and vertices to
\[
\sigma(x,y)=(-x,y),
\]
\[
v_1=(-t,1),\quad v_2=(t,1),\quad
v_3=(1,0),\quad v_4=(-1,0),
\qquad t>0.
\]
Relative to \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_3,v_4\}\),
\[
v_2=v_1+t v_3-t v_4,
\]
so the barycentric parameters \(v_2=(r,w,-h)\) satisfy the exact relations
\[
r=1,\qquad w=h=t.
\]
If the double-cap fractions from \(v_1\) toward \(v_4\) and from \(v_2\) toward \(v_3\) are \(a,\kappa\), the \(v_3\)-cap fractions toward \(v_2,v_4\) are \(b,d\), and the \(v_4\)-cap fractions toward \(v_3,v_1\) are \(c,e\), then symmetry gives
\[
\kappa=a,\qquad c=d,\qquad e=b.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e` gives three equal-area outer caps, pairwise disjoint up to their boundaries, and a central remainder of twice the common cap area. Their total is therefore five times one cap area, so
\[
|C_i|=\frac15|A_2|. \tag{1}
\]
The same accepted fact identifies \(\mathcal T_Q\) as the global triangle formed by the three complementary halfplanes and, after physical normalization to \(|A_2|=1\), requires
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|\ge\frac{169}{10}. \tag{2}
\]
If \(A_2\) is a parallelogram, accepted fact `1de8e08b08202b2b` applies to its type-2 cap incidence and already excludes it. For the remainder of the proof, assume \(A_2\) is not a parallelogram.

Apply the affine normalization in the statement. The quadrilateral has area
\[
|A_2|=1+t
\]
in these coordinates, so each normalized cap has area
\[
p=\frac{1+t}{5}. \tag{3}
\]
The reflection gives the displayed relations \(r=1\), \(w=h=t\), \(\kappa=a\), \(c=d\), and \(e=b\). The \(C_1\)-line is horizontal at height \(1-a\). Its cap is a trapezoid of height \(a\), top width \(2t\), and bottom width \(2(t+a(1-t))\). The cap at \(v_3\) is a corner triangle whose fractions are \(b,d\). Thus (1) is exactly
\[
a\bigl(2t+a(1-t)\bigr)=p,
\qquad
bd=p. \tag{4}
\]
Pairwise interior-disjointness along either leg and along the bottom edge gives
\[
a+b\le1,\qquad 2d\le1. \tag{5}
\]
These are precisely all three edge-order inequalities after imposing the reflection relations.

From \(bd=p\) and \(d\le1/2\), one has \(b\ge2p\). Hence
\[
a\le1-2p=\frac{3-2t}{5}. \tag{6}
\]
In particular \(t<3/2\). For fixed \(t\), put
\[
F_t(x)=x\bigl(2t+x(1-t)\bigr).
\]
If \(1<t<3/2\), then \(F_t\) is strictly increasing on
\([0,(3-2t)/5]\), while
\[
F_t\left(\frac{3-2t}{5}\right)-p
=-\frac{4(t-1)(t+1)^2}{25}<0.
\]
Equations (4) and (6) would give
\[
p=F_t(a)
\le F_t\left(\frac{3-2t}{5}\right)<p,
\]
a contradiction. Therefore \(t\le1\). The normalized quadrilateral is a parallelogram exactly when \(t=1\), which has already been excluded by accepted fact `1de8e08b08202b2b`. Hence
\[
0<t<1. \tag{7}
\]

The function \(F_t\) is increasing for \(x>0\). Direct evaluation gives
\[
F_t\left(\frac15\right)-p
=\frac{4(t-1)}{25}<0
\]
and
\[
F_t\left(\frac1{\sqrt5}\right)-p
=2t\left(\frac1{\sqrt5}-\frac15\right)>0.
\]
Using (4),
\[
\frac15<a<\frac1{\sqrt5}. \tag{8}
\]
Set
\[
H=10a-5a^2-1.
\]
Rearranging the first equation in (4) gives
\[
tH=1-5a^2.
\]
The right side is positive by (8), so \(H>0\), and
\[
t=\frac{1-5a^2}{H},
\qquad
p=\frac{2a(1-a)}{H}. \tag{9}
\]

Put \(z=2d\). Equations (4)--(5) imply
\[
\frac{4a}{H}
=\frac{2p}{1-a}
\le z\le1. \tag{10}
\]
The \(C_2\)-line passes through
\[
\bigl(1+b(t-1),b\bigr),\qquad (1-z,0).
\]
It therefore has equation
\[
x=1-z+my,
\qquad
m=t-1+\frac{2d}{b}
=t-1+\frac{z^2}{2p}. \tag{11}
\]
Its cap is on the right, so its complementary halfplane is
\[
x\le1-z+my.
\]
Reflection gives the other singleton cap's complementary halfplane as
\[
x\ge-1+z-my. \tag{12}
\]

We next bound \(m\) exactly. Substituting (9) into (11) gives
\[
m=\frac{2(1-5a)}{H}
+\frac{z^2H}{4a(1-a)}.
\]
This increases with \(z\). Equation (10) therefore yields
\[
m\ge
\frac{2(5a^2-4a+1)}{H(1-a)}. \tag{13}
\]
Here
\[
5a^2-4a+1
=5\left(a-\frac25\right)^2+\frac15
\ge\frac15.
\]
Moreover, \(H\) is increasing on the interval (8), and
\[
H<2\sqrt5-2<\frac52,
\qquad
1-a<\frac45.
\]
Thus \(H(1-a)<2\), and (13) gives
\[
m>\frac15. \tag{14}
\]
Conversely, \(z\le1\), (3), and (11) give
\[
m\le t-1+\frac1{2p}
=t-1+\frac5{2(1+t)}
<\frac32, \tag{15}
\]
where the last comparison follows from
\[
\frac32-\left(t-1+\frac5{2(1+t)}\right)
=\frac{t(3-2t)}{2(1+t)}>0
\]
under (7).

The \(C_1\)-complement is \(y\le1-a\). By (14), the two lines in (11)--(12) meet at
\[
\left(0,-\frac{1-z}{m}\right).
\]
At \(y=1-a\), their other two intersections are
\[
\bigl(\pm(1-z+m(1-a)),\,1-a\bigr).
\]
Hence their three complementary halfplanes form a triangle of area
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|
=\frac{(1-z+m(1-a))^2}{m}. \tag{16}
\]
Dividing by the normalized quadrilateral area \(1+t\) and expanding gives
\[
\frac{|\mathcal T_Q|}{|A_2|}
=\frac{1}{1+t}
\left(
\frac{(1-z)^2}{m}
+2(1-z)(1-a)
+m(1-a)^2
\right).
\]
Since \(0<z\le1\), \(0<a<1\), and (14)--(15) hold,
\[
\frac{|\mathcal T_Q|}{|A_2|}
<5+2+\frac32
=\frac{17}{2}. \tag{17}
\]

Under the physical area-one normalization of accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e`, equation (17) gives
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|<\frac{17}{2}<\frac{169}{10}.
\]
This contradicts (2). Equivalently, the identity
\[
|\mathcal T_Q|=\frac{2}{5D}
\]
in the accepted normal form shows that (17) forces \(D>4/85\), whereas that fact requires \(D\le4/169\). This proves the claim.
