## Statement

Let
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\rho,\sigma,\tau>0,\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1,
\]
and assume
\[
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}\le \rho\le1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta},
\quad
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\le \sigma\le1+\frac{\beta}{\gamma},
\quad
\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\le \tau\le1+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma},
\]
\[
\sigma+\tau\ge1,\qquad
\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho\ge1,\qquad
\rho+\frac1\tau\ge1.
\]
Define
\[
A_1=2\beta\gamma+\beta^2(1-\rho)+\gamma^2(1-\sigma),
\]
\[
A_2=2\gamma\alpha+\gamma^2(1-\tau)
+\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\rho\right),
\]
\[
A_3=2\alpha\beta+\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)
+\beta^2\left(1-\frac1\tau\right),
\]
and
\[
b=\gamma^2(\sigma+\tau-1)
+\alpha^2\left(\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho-1\right)
+\beta^2\left(\rho+\frac1\tau-1\right).
\]
Suppose \((\sqrt{A_1},\sqrt{A_2},\sqrt{A_3})\) satisfies the triangle inequalities. Then every \(0\le d_i\le A_i\) satisfies
\[
b\ge
\sqrt{d_1d_2}+\sqrt{d_2d_3}+\sqrt{d_3d_1}
-\frac12(d_1+d_2+d_3).
\]
Equality holds exactly when
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,\qquad
d_1=d_2=d_3=\frac29.
\]

## Proof

The endpoint bounds give \(A_i\ge0\). For example,
\[
A_1=(\beta+\gamma)^2-\beta^2\rho-\gamma^2\sigma\ge0,
\]
and the other two cases follow similarly, using the lower bounds on \(\rho,\sigma,\tau\) after taking reciprocals. Direct expansion gives
\[
A_1+A_2+A_3+b=1. \tag{1}
\]

Put
\[
Q(y_1,y_2,y_3)
=y_1y_2+y_2y_3+y_3y_1-\frac12(y_1^2+y_2^2+y_3^2).
\]
We first record the elementary box maximization used below. If \(x_i\ge0\) satisfy the triangle inequalities, then
\[
Q(y_1,y_2,y_3)\le Q(x_1,x_2,x_3)
\qquad(0\le y_i\le x_i). \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, at a box maximizer, maximizing in one coordinate with the other two fixed gives
\[
y_i=\min\{x_i,y_j+y_k\}. \tag{3}
\]
If all \(x_i>0\) and some \(y_i<x_i\), then \(y_i=y_j+y_k\). Unless the maximizer is the zero vector, applying (3) to the other coordinates shows \(y_j=x_j\) and \(y_k=x_k\), whence
\[
y_i=x_j+x_k\ge x_i,
\]
a contradiction. The zero vector is not maximal because \(Q(x)>0\) for a positive triangle. Thus the maximizer is \(x\). If, say, \(x_1=0\), the triangle inequalities force \(x_2=x_3\), and
\[
Q(0,y_2,y_3)=-\frac12(y_2-y_3)^2\le0=Q(x).
\]
The remaining zero cases are immediate. This proves (2).

Set
\[
x_i=\sqrt{A_i}.
\]
By the balanced hypothesis there are \(p,q,r\ge0\) such that
\[
x_1=q+r,\qquad x_2=r+p,\qquad x_3=p+q.
\]
Write
\[
h=p+q+r=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{2}.
\]
A direct expansion gives
\[
Q(x_1,x_2,x_3)=2(pq+qr+rp). \tag{4}
\]
Using (1),
\[
b-Q(x_1,x_2,x_3)
=1-\frac12(x_1+x_2+x_3)^2
=1-2h^2. \tag{5}
\]
It therefore remains to prove \(h^2\le1/2\).

Introduce the six positive endpoint quantities
\[
a_{12}=\beta\sqrt\rho,\qquad a_{21}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt\rho},
\]
\[
a_{13}=\gamma\sqrt\sigma,\qquad a_{31}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt\sigma},
\]
\[
a_{23}=\gamma\sqrt\tau,\qquad a_{32}=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt\tau}.
\]
They satisfy
\[
a_{ij}a_{ji}=w_iw_j,\qquad
(w_1,w_2,w_3)=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma), \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
A_1=(1-w_1)^2-a_{12}^2-a_{13}^2,
\]
\[
A_2=(1-w_2)^2-a_{21}^2-a_{23}^2,
\]
\[
A_3=(1-w_3)^2-a_{31}^2-a_{32}^2. \tag{7}
\]
The vectors
\[
v_1=(a_{12},a_{13},0),\quad
v_2=(-a_{21},0,a_{23}),\quad
v_3=(0,-a_{31},-a_{32})
\]
therefore have Gram matrix
\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
(1-w_1)^2-A_1&-w_1w_2&-w_1w_3\\
-w_1w_2&(1-w_2)^2-A_2&-w_2w_3\\
-w_1w_3&-w_2w_3&(1-w_3)^2-A_3
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0. \tag{8}
\]

If \(h=0\), the desired bound is immediate. Otherwise set
\[
u_1=\frac ph,\qquad u_2=\frac qh,\qquad u_3=\frac rh.
\]
Thus \(u_i\ge0\), \(\sum u_i=1\), and
\[
A_i=h^2(1-u_i)^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
G=G_0(w)-h^2D(u), \tag{9}
\]
where \(G_0(w)\) has diagonal entries \((1-w_i)^2\), off-diagonal entries \(-w_iw_j\), and
\[
D(u)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl((1-u_1)^2,(1-u_2)^2,(1-u_3)^2\bigr).
\]

Apply (9) to the test vector
\[
c_i=\frac1{1-w_i}.
\]
Put
\[
P=w_1w_2+w_2w_3+w_3w_1,\qquad
R=w_1w_2w_3.
\]
Since
\[
\prod_i(1-w_i)=P-R,
\]
one obtains
\[
c^{T}G_0(w)c
=3-2\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_iw_j}{(1-w_i)(1-w_j)}
=\frac{P+3R}{P-R}. \tag{10}
\]
Cauchy gives
\[
c^{T}D(u)c
=\sum_i\frac{(1-u_i)^2}{(1-w_i)^2}
\ge
\frac{\left(\sum_i(1-u_i)\right)^2}
{\sum_i(1-w_i)^2}
=\frac{2}{1-P}. \tag{11}
\]
Because \(G\succeq0\), (9)-(11) imply
\[
h^2
\le
\frac{(1-P)(P+3R)}{2(P-R)}. \tag{12}
\]

It remains to verify
\[
(1-P)(P+3R)\le P-R, \tag{13}
\]
or equivalently
\[
P^2+3PR-4R\ge0. \tag{14}
\]
Here is a self-contained proof. For arbitrary positive \(w_1,w_2,w_3\), put
\[
s=w_1+w_2+w_3.
\]
The homogeneous form of (14) is
\[
F=sP^2+3PR-4Rs^2\ge0. \tag{15}
\]
For fixed \(s,P\), the coefficient \(3P-4s^2\) of \(R\) is strictly negative because \(P\le s^2/3\). Hence \(F\) decreases as \(R\) increases. Under fixed \(s,P\), the maximum of \(R=w_1w_2w_3\) occurs when two variables are equal. This follows directly from Lagrange multipliers: at an interior maximum, subtracting the three multiplier equations forces two variables to coincide unless all three already coincide. The maximum cannot occur at \(R=0\), since a positive feasible triple is available.

Writing the maximizing triple as \((x,x,y)\), direct expansion yields
\[
F=2x^3(x-y)^2\ge0.
\]
Since every other feasible triple has no larger \(R\), (15) follows. Equality for a positive triple forces \(R\) to be maximal and \(x=y\), hence
\[
w_1=w_2=w_3.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (12)-(14) gives \(h^2\le1/2\). Equations (2), (4), and (5) now prove the stated inequality.

EQUALITY
Suppose equality holds. Then equality must hold throughout
\[
h^2
\le
\frac{c^TG_0(w)c}{c^TD(u)c}
\le
\frac{(1-P)(P+3R)}{2(P-R)}
\le\frac12.
\]
Equality in (14) and positivity give
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13.
\]
Equality in (11) then forces
\[
u_1=u_2=u_3=\frac13.
\]
Thus
\[
h=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
p=q=r=\frac1{3\sqrt2},\qquad
A_1=A_2=A_3=\frac29. \tag{17}
\]

By (7), the sum of the squares of the six endpoint quantities is then
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}^2
=3\left(\frac49-\frac29\right)=\frac23.
\]
On the other hand, (6) and AM-GM give
\[
\sum_{i<j}(a_{ij}^2+a_{ji}^2)
\ge2\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}a_{ji}
=\frac23.
\]
Therefore equality holds in every paired AM-GM inequality, so
\[
a_{ij}=a_{ji}=\frac13
\qquad(i\ne j).
\]
The definitions of the \(a_{ij}\) now give
\[
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1.
\]
All three caps in (17) are positive, so the box maximizer in (2) is unique. Equality therefore also forces
\[
d_i=A_i=\frac29.
\]

Conversely,
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1
\]
gives \(A_i=2/9\) and \(b=1/3\). Taking \(d_i=2/9\) gives equality.
