## Statement

There is no function
\[
F:(0,\infty)^3\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
\]
such that every common-plane configuration satisfying the hypotheses of
accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, with
\[
p_\varnothing q_1q_2>0,
\]
obeys
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2)\cap R_{12}\right|
\geq F(p_\varnothing,q_1,q_2),
\qquad
U_i=\frac{P_\varnothing+\overline{Q_i}}2.
\tag{1}
\]

More precisely, for every integer \(n\geq1\) there are nonempty
two-dimensional compact convex polytopes
\[
A_{0,n},A_{2,n},K_n\subset\mathbb R^2
\]
and affine functions and offsets satisfying all sign-plane hypotheses
of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` such that
\[
\frac{A_{0,n}+A_{2,n}}2=K_n,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
p_\varnothing=q_1=q_2=1,
\tag{3}
\]
and the exact positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\mathcal S_Q=\{123,1,2,12\}.
\tag{4}
\]
If
\[
S_n=
\left|\operatorname{conv}(U_{1,n}\cup U_{2,n})\cap R_{12}\right|,
\]
then
\[
0<S_n\leq\frac1n+\frac1{2n^2},
\tag{5}
\]
so \(S_n\to0\).

Under the fixed normalization \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\), define
\[
\mathcal W_n
=\operatorname{diam}_{A_{0,n}\cup A_{2,n}}(f_1-f_2).
\tag{6}
\]
This quantity is invariant under a common invertible affine change of
the planar variable, with the affine functions transported by
composition. The family satisfies
\[
\mathcal W_n=18n+4+\frac2n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

Finally, let
\[
B:(0,\infty)^3\times(0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
\]
be any function for which the proposed universal inequality
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(U_1\cup U_2)\cap R_{12}\right|
\geq B(p_\varnothing,q_1,q_2,\mathcal W)
\tag{8}
\]
holds for every such configuration, where
\[
\mathcal W=\operatorname{diam}_{A_0\cup A_2}(f_1-f_2).
\]
Then this family forces
\[
0\leq B(1,1,1,\mathcal W_n)
\leq S_n
\leq\frac1n+\frac1{2n^2},
\tag{9}
\]
and hence
\[
B(1,1,1,\mathcal W_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus a lower bound that explicitly depends on this width cannot remain
uniformly positive along this family. Statements (8)-(10) do not
exclude lower bounds involving other geometric or cell parameters.

## Proof

Use coordinates \(x,y\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), and put
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad
f_2(x,y)=y,\qquad
f_3(x,y)=-1-x-y.
\tag{11}
\]
Their linear parts are pairwise nonparallel and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Introduce
\[
s=x-y,\qquad t=x+y.
\]
Then
\[
x=\frac{t+s}{2},\qquad y=\frac{t-s}{2},
\qquad dx\,dy=\frac12\,ds\,dt.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix \(n\geq1\), and define
\[
\varepsilon=\frac1n,\qquad
D=5n,\qquad
T=-D-2,
\]
\[
\alpha=4n+\frac1n,\qquad
\delta=\frac{(D+2)\varepsilon}{\alpha}
=\frac{5n+2}{4n^2+1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Take
\[
L_1=L_2=0,\qquad L_3=-D-1.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3=-D-1<-1,
\]
so the offset hypothesis of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` holds.

Define the first endpoint body by
\[
A_{0,n}
=\left\{(s,t):-n\leq s\leq n,\quad
D-\varepsilon\leq t\leq D\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
This is a compact two-dimensional parallelogram. Apart from its null
top edge, every point satisfies
\[
f_1>L_1,\qquad f_2>L_2,\qquad f_3>L_3.
\]
Indeed, \(t>|s|\), while
\[
f_3-L_3=D-t\in[0,\varepsilon].
\]
Using (12), the exact cell area is
\[
p_\varnothing
=\frac12(2n)\varepsilon=1.
\tag{16}
\]

Put
\[
B_0=T-\varepsilon
\]
and define the second endpoint body as the triangle
\[
A_{2,n}
=\operatorname{conv}\{v_-,v_+,v_0\},
\tag{17}
\]
where, in the \((s,t)\)-coordinates,
\[
v_-=(T-\alpha,B_0),\qquad
v_+=(-T+\alpha,B_0),\qquad
v_0=(0,T+\delta).
\tag{18}
\]
The three endpoint sign coordinates are
\[
f_1+L_1=\frac{t+s}{2},\qquad
f_2+L_2=\frac{t-s}{2},\qquad
f_3+L_3=T-t.
\tag{19}
\]
The identity
\[
\alpha\delta=(-T)\varepsilon
\tag{20}
\]
shows that the edge \([v_-,v_0]\) passes through \((T,T)\), while
\([v_+,v_0]\) passes through \((-T,T)\).

For \(t<T\), the supported sign chambers are
\[
Q_1:\ s<t,\qquad
Q_{12}:\ t\leq s\leq-t,\qquad
Q_2:\ s>-t.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(t>T\), every cross-section of the triangle lies strictly between
\(s=t\) and \(s=-t\), and hence belongs to \(Q_{123}\). Cutting-line
pieces have area zero under the exact convention of accepted fact
`0e51a47a9add571f`. Therefore the only positive-area cells are exactly
\[
Q_1,Q_2,Q_{12},Q_{123}.
\tag{22}
\]
This is the single-double support classified in accepted fact
`91cb5bfc71da49ac`.

The cell \(Q_1\) is the triangle with vertices
\[
v_-,\qquad (B_0,B_0),\qquad (T,T).
\]
Its area in the \((s,t)\)-plane is
\((\alpha-\varepsilon)\varepsilon/2\); the Jacobian in (12) supplies
another factor \(1/2\). Hence
\[
q_1
=\frac{(\alpha-\varepsilon)\varepsilon}{4}
=\frac{(4n)(1/n)}4=1.
\tag{23}
\]
Symmetry gives
\[
q_2=1.
\tag{24}
\]
The cells \(Q_{12}\) and \(Q_{123}\) have positive area because
\(\varepsilon,\delta>0\), proving the exact support assertion (4).

Set
\[
K_n=\frac{A_{0,n}+A_{2,n}}2.
\tag{25}
\]
This is a compact two-dimensional convex polygon and proves (2).
Define
\[
U_{1,n}
=\frac{P_\varnothing+\overline{Q_1}}2,\qquad
U_{2,n}
=\frac{P_\varnothing+\overline{Q_2}}2.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (16), (23), and (24) give (3). Accepted fact
`d3afd91c459bcce8` applies to the actual convex bodies in
(15), (17), and (25), and proves
\[
S_n>0.
\tag{27}
\]

It remains to prove the vanishing upper bound. On
\(P_\varnothing\),
\[
D-\varepsilon\leq t\leq D,
\]
whereas on \(Q_1\cup Q_2\),
\[
T-\varepsilon\leq t\leq T.
\]
Since \(D+T=-2\), every point of
\(U_{1,n}\cup U_{2,n}\), and therefore of its convex hull, lies in
\[
-1-\varepsilon\leq t\leq-1.
\tag{28}
\]
The middle exact chamber \(R_{12}\) satisfies
\[
t<-1,\qquad t\leq s\leq-t.
\tag{29}
\]
Using the Jacobian in (12),
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_n
&\leq
\frac12\int_{-1-\varepsilon}^{-1}
\bigl((-t)-t\bigr)\,dt\\
&=\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}
=\frac1n+\frac1{2n^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with (27), this proves (5). If a positive function \(F\)
satisfied (1), then (3) and (5) would give
\[
0<F(1,1,1)\leq S_n\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is impossible. This proves that no lower bound depending only on
\((p_\varnothing,q_1,q_2)\) can be universally positive.

Finally, \(f_1-f_2=s\). The extreme \(s\)-coordinates of \(A_{2,n}\)
are
\[
T-\alpha,\qquad -T+\alpha,
\]
and these contain the interval \([-n,n]\) from \(A_{0,n}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal W_n
=2(-T+\alpha)
=2(D+2+\alpha)
=18n+4+\frac2n,
\]
which proves (7). If a common invertible affine map \(G\) is applied to
the bodies and the functions are transported as \(f_i\circ G^{-1}\),
then their values, and hence the diameter in (6), are unchanged. This
proves the asserted affine invariance and the width divergence.

If \(B\) satisfies (8), applying it to the \(n\)-th member of the actual
family and using (3), (5), and (7) gives (9). The squeeze theorem gives
(10), so such a width-dependent lower bound cannot remain uniformly
positive along this family. This conclusion is only about bounds of
the quantified form (8) and makes no necessity or uniqueness claim
about possible correction parameters.
