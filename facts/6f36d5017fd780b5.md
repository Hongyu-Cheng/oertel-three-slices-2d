## Statement

Assume the locked three-slice problem and the exact residual area strip of
accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Normalize \(M=1\), and write
\[
r=m/M,\qquad s=a_1/M.
\]
Thus
\[
0<r<\rho,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\leq 9/25,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2},&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\leq1/2,\\[2mm]
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]
The sufficient centroid criterion in accepted fact `c02e26ea799dadd7` is
equivalent on (1) to
\[
G(r,s):=7r-11-18s+12\sqrt{5s}\geq0.
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
S_c(r)=
\left(\frac{2\sqrt5+\sqrt{14r-2}}6\right)^2,
\qquad
r_0=\frac{163-12\sqrt{51}}{125}.
\tag{3}
\]
The portion of (1) not certified by that criterion is exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
0<r\leq1/5,\qquad L(r)<s<U(r),\\[2mm]
1/5<r<r_0,\qquad S_c(r)<s<U(r).
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently every counterexample to the locked problem must have normalized
area parameters in (4).

## Proof

The raw residual inequalities in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` include
\[
1-r<s<1+r.
\tag{5}
\]
With
\[
\delta=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9,
\]
equation (5) gives
\[
0<\delta<\frac{4r}{9}<r.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, every square root in the normalized criterion of accepted
fact `c02e26ea799dadd7` is real and has positive radicand.

We also need a uniform sign before isolating and squaring a radical.  If
\(0<r\leq9/25\), then
\[
s>L(r)=1-r\geq\frac{16}{25}>\frac59.
\]
If \(r>9/25\), the function
\[
D(r)=\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}
\]
is strictly increasing and \(D(9/25)=16/25\), so again
\[
s>L(r)>\frac59.
\tag{7}
\]

After the normalization \(M=1\), the criterion in accepted fact
`c02e26ea799dadd7` is
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{r-\delta}\right)^2\geq\frac{20s}{9}.
\tag{8}
\]
By (6), the left base in (8) is positive, and the right side is positive.
Moreover,
\[
r-\delta=\frac{7r+2s-2}{9}>0.
\]
It is therefore reversible to take the nonnegative square root of both sides
of (8).  This gives
\[
3+\sqrt{7r+2s-2}\geq2\sqrt{5s}.
\]
By (7), \(2\sqrt{5s}-3>0\).  Hence it is also reversible to isolate the
remaining radical and square:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{7r+2s-2}\geq2\sqrt{5s}-3
&\iff
7r+2s-2\geq\left(2\sqrt{5s}-3\right)^2\\
&\iff
7r-11-18s+12\sqrt{5s}\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (2), with the signs checked before both squaring operations.

For fixed \(r\), equation (7) also gives
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial s}
=-18+\frac{6\sqrt5}{\sqrt s}<0.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus \(G(r,\cdot)\) is strictly decreasing throughout every vertical section
of the residual strip.

We next audit the complete lower boundary.  On \(0<r\leq9/25\),
\[
G(r,1-r)=12\sqrt{5(1-r)}-(29-25r).
\]
Here \(29-25r>0\), and rationalization gives
\[
G(r,1-r)
=
\frac{720(1-r)-(29-25r)^2}
     {12\sqrt{5(1-r)}+29-25r}
=
\frac{(5r-1)(121-125r)}
     {12\sqrt{5(1-r)}+29-25r}.
\tag{10}
\]
The denominator and \(121-125r\) are positive on this branch.  Hence (10)
is negative for \(0<r<1/5\), zero at \(r=1/5\), and positive for
\(1/5<r\leq9/25\).

On the second lower branch, put \(x=\sqrt r\).  For
\(9/25<r<\rho\), one has
\[
\frac35<x<\sqrt\rho=\frac{\sqrt{26}-1}{5}<\frac56.
\tag{11}
\]
For the last inequality, \(5x^2+2x-5\) is strictly increasing for \(x>0\),
its positive zero is \(\sqrt\rho\), and its value at \(5/6\) is \(5/36>0\).
Direct substitution and rationalization give
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(r,D(r))
&=
12\sqrt{\frac52(3x^2+2x-1)}
 -(20x^2+18x+2)\\
&=
\frac{4Q(x)}
{12\sqrt{\frac52(3x^2+2x-1)}+20x^2+18x+2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
where
\[
Q(x)=-100x^4-180x^3+169x^2+162x-91.
\]
The denominator in (12) is positive.  On \(3/5\leq x\leq5/6\),
\[
Q''(x)=-1200x^2-1080x+338\leq-742<0,
\]
while
\[
Q(3/5)=\frac{76}{5}>0,
\qquad
Q(5/6)=\frac{1453}{162}>0.
\]
Concavity places \(Q\) above the positive chord joining these endpoint
values, so \(Q(x)>0\) on the whole interval.  Equations (11)-(12) show that
\(G(r,L(r))>0\) throughout the second lower branch.  Combining the two
branches,
\[
G(r,L(r))
\begin{cases}
<0,&0<r<1/5,\\
=0,&r=1/5,\\
>0,&1/5<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(r=1/5\) is the only lower-boundary crossing in the residual range.

Now audit both upper-boundary pieces.  For \(0<r\leq1/2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(r,1+r)
&=12\sqrt{5(1+r)}-(29+11r)\\
&=
\frac{-121r^2+82r-121}
     {12\sqrt{5(1+r)}+29+11r}<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, the denominator is positive, and
\(121r^2-82r+121>0\) for every real \(r\), since its discriminant is
\(-51840\).

For \(1/2<r<\rho\), one has \(\rho<1\), so \(47-25r>0\).  Rationalization
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(r,2-r)
&=12\sqrt{5(2-r)}-(47-25r)\\
&=
\frac{-625r^2+1630r-769}
     {12\sqrt{5(2-r)}+47-25r}\\
&=
\frac{625(r-r_0)(r_1-r)}
     {12\sqrt{5(2-r)}+47-25r},
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
r_1=\frac{163+12\sqrt{51}}{125}>1.
\]
The relevant root is inside the second upper branch.  First,
\[
r_0-\frac12=\frac{201-24\sqrt{51}}{250}>0,
\]
because both quantities compared are positive and
\[
201^2=40401>29376=24^2\cdot51.
\]
Second,
\[
\rho-r_0
=\frac{12\sqrt{51}-10\sqrt{26}-28}{125}>0.
\]
To check this last sign without an unsafe square, both
\(12\sqrt{51}\) and \(28+10\sqrt{26}\) are positive, and
\[
(12\sqrt{51})^2-(28+10\sqrt{26})^2
=40(99-14\sqrt{26})>0,
\]
where \(99>14\sqrt{26}\) follows by squaring the two positive quantities:
\(99^2=9801>5096=14^2\cdot26\).  Thus
\[
\frac12<r_0<\rho<1<r_1.
\tag{16}
\]
Every denominator in (15) is positive, so (14)-(16) yield the complete
upper-boundary sign table
\[
G(r,U(r))
\begin{cases}
<0,&0<r<r_0,\\
=0,&r=r_0,\\
>0,&r_0<r<\rho.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]
There is no crossing on the first upper piece, and \(r_0\) is the only
crossing on the second piece in the residual range.

For \(r>1/5\), solve the unique interior threshold explicitly.  Put
\(y=\sqrt s\).  By (7), \(y>\sqrt5/3\), and
\[
G(r,s)=0
\iff
18y^2-12\sqrt5\,y+11-7r=0.
\]
Since \(r>1/5\), the two roots are real and equal to
\[
y_\pm=\frac{2\sqrt5\pm\sqrt{14r-2}}6.
\]
The smaller root satisfies \(y_-<\sqrt5/3\), while
\(y_+>\sqrt5/3\).  Hence the only root in the range (7) is
\[
s=y_+^2=S_c(r).
\tag{18}
\]
By strict monotonicity (9), for \(s>5/9\),
\[
G(r,s)\geq0\iff s\leq S_c(r),
\qquad
G(r,s)<0\iff s>S_c(r).
\tag{19}
\]
The two transition values are exact:
\[
S_c(1/5)=\frac45=L(1/5),
\tag{20}
\]
and, by (17)-(18),
\[
S_c(r_0)=U(r_0)=2-r_0.
\tag{21}
\]

It remains only to combine the strict endpoints.  If \(0<r\leq1/5\), then
\(s>L(r)\), so (9) and (13) give
\[
G(r,s)<G(r,L(r))\leq0.
\]
At \(r=1/5\), equality occurs only at the excluded lower boundary, which is
why \(r=1/5\) remains in the first line of (4).  If
\(1/5<r<r_0\), equations (13), (17), and (9) put the unique zero strictly
between the two residual boundaries:
\[
L(r)<S_c(r)<U(r).
\]
Since equality in (2) already satisfies the nonstrict centroid criterion,
the uncertified part has the strict lower condition \(s>S_c(r)\).
At \(r=r_0\), the equality in (17) occurs only at the excluded upper
boundary; every residual \(s<U(r_0)\) has
\[
G(r_0,s)>G(r_0,U(r_0))=0.
\]
For \(r_0<r<\rho\), equations (9) and (17) similarly give
\[
G(r,s)>G(r,U(r))>0
\]
for every residual \(s<U(r)\).  Thus \(r=r_0\) and every larger residual
ratio are certified.  These observations prove exactly (4), including all
strict and nonstrict endpoint conventions.
