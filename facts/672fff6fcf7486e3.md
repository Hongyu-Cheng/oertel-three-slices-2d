## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, and set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad
S_B=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
b=|B|,\qquad
T=M+m+b.
\]
If
\[
|A_0|<\frac T2,\qquad b<\frac T2,\qquad |A_2|<\frac T2,
\]
then, for the centroid \(g_B\) of \(B\),
\[
h_{S_B}((1,g_B))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]
In particular, this holds throughout the strict balanced residual area regime.

## Proof

Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` reduces the depth infimum to halfspaces whose boundary contains \((1,g_B)\), and separates the slice-parallel halfspaces. Their masses are
\[
|A_0|+b=T-|A_2|>\frac T2
\quad\text{and}\quad
b+|A_2|=T-|A_0|>\frac T2,
\]
so both exceed \(2T/9\).

Consider a tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\). In the normal form of `400272f6de6c67bb`, its three planar sections are
\[
A_0\cap\{\langle e,w-g_B\rangle\le\lambda\},\qquad
B\cap\{\langle e,w-g_B\rangle\le0\},\qquad
A_2\cap\{\langle e,w-g_B\rangle\le-\lambda\}
\]
for some \(e\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Relabel the two outer bodies only in the notation so that their areas are \(M\) and \(m\), and denote the corresponding lower-cap areas by \(x\) and \(y\). Put
\[
h=\left|B\cap\{\langle e,w-g_B\rangle\le0\}\right|.
\]

Grünbaum's centroid inequality in dimension two, applied to both sides of the line through \(g_B\), gives
\[
\frac{4b}{9}\le h\le\frac{5b}{9}.
\tag{1}
\]
The full-body Brunn-Minkowski consequence contained in accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` gives
\[
\sqrt b\ge\frac{\sqrt M+\sqrt m}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

If
\[
2b\ge M+m,
\]
then (1) immediately yields
\[
h\ge\frac{4b}{9}\ge\frac{2(M+m+b)}9=\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

It remains to treat
\[
2b<M+m.
\tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM.
\]
Strict balance of the larger outer slice gives \(M<m+b\). Together with (4),
\[
s>1-r,\qquad s<\frac{1+r}{2},
\tag{5}
\]
and hence
\[
\frac13<r\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
By (2),
\[
s\ge\frac{(1+\sqrt r)^2}{4}>\frac9{16}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let
\[
P=M-x,\qquad Q=m-y
\]
be the areas of the opposite closed upper caps. Boundary lines have planar area zero, so these are exact complements. If \(P=0\) or \(Q=0\), one lower outer cap contains its whole body and therefore has area at least \(m\). By (1) and (5), the halfspace mass is then strictly larger than the target because
\[
h+m-\frac{2T}{9}
\ge
\frac{4b}{9}+m-\frac{2T}{9}
=
\frac{M}{9}(2s+7r-2)
>
\frac{5Mr}{9}>0.
\tag{8}
\]

Assume now that \(P,Q>0\). The two upper caps are nonempty. Their threshold values average to \(\langle e,g_B\rangle\), so the upper-cap inclusion and Brunn-Minkowski inequality in accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` apply and give
\[
\sqrt P+\sqrt Q\le2\sqrt{b-h}.
\tag{9}
\]
Put
\[
U=\sqrt P,\qquad V=\sqrt Q,\qquad R=2\sqrt{b-h}.
\]
From (1) and (7),
\[
b-h\ge\frac{4b}{9}>\frac M4,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad R>\sqrt M.
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(U\le\sqrt M\), \(V\le\sqrt m\le\sqrt M\), and \(U+V\le R\).

These constraints imply
\[
P+Q=U^2+V^2
\le M+(R-\sqrt M)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, if \(U+V\le\sqrt M\), then \(U^2+V^2\le M\). If \(U+V\ge\sqrt M\), then
\[
M+(U+V-\sqrt M)^2-(U^2+V^2)
=
2(\sqrt M-U)(\sqrt M-V)\ge0,
\]
and \(U+V\le R\), together with \(R>\sqrt M\), proves (11).

Consequently the halfspace mass satisfies
\[
h+x+y
\ge
h+m-\left(2\sqrt{b-h}-\sqrt M\right)^2
=:\Phi(h).
\tag{12}
\]
For \(h\) in the interval (1),
\[
\Phi'(h)
=
5-2\sqrt{\frac{M}{b-h}}
>1
\tag{13}
\]
by (10). Thus \(\Phi\) is increasing, and (1) yields
\[
h+x+y
\ge
\Phi\left(\frac{4b}{9}\right)
=
m-M-\frac{16b}{9}
+4\sqrt{\frac{5Mb}{9}}.
\tag{14}
\]
After subtracting \(2T/9\), the right-hand side of (14) gives
\[
h+x+y-\frac{2T}{9}
\ge
\frac{M}{9}G(r,s),
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
G(r,s)=7r-11-18s+12\sqrt{5s}.
\]
By (7), \(s>5/9\), and hence
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial s}
=
-18+6\sqrt{\frac5s}<0.
\tag{16}
\]
Using (5),
\[
G(r,s)>
G\left(r,\frac{1+r}{2}\right)
=
12\sqrt{\frac{5(1+r)}2}-20-2r.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(1/3<r\le1\),
\[
90(1+r)-(10+r)^2
=
-r^2+70r-10>0,
\tag{18}
\]
because the final polynomial is increasing on \([1/3,1]\) and equals \(119/9\) at \(r=1/3\). Since both sides being compared are positive, (18) implies
\[
12\sqrt{\frac{5(1+r)}2}>20+2r.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (15), (17), and (19) prove that every tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\) has mass strictly greater than \(2T/9\) in case (4).

Together with (3), (8), and the slice-parallel calculation, accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` now gives
\[
h_{S_B}((1,g_B))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. Theorem 2 states that if \(K\subset\mathbb R^n\) is a convex body with centroid \(g_K\), then every closed halfspace bounded by a hyperplane through \(g_K\) contains at least \((n/(n+1))^n|K|\). Applied with \(n=2\) and \(K=B\), it gives the two inequalities in (1).
