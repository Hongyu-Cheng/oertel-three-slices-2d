## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal C(z;\alpha)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\ge\alpha\}\right|.
\]
Assume the exact \(N=3\) barycentric system of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` in the surviving orientation of accepted fact
`09393c6edf1fc4e2`.  Thus \(U,V,L\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and
\(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top L=\mathbf1^\top,
\]
\[
\det U\ne0,\qquad \det V\ne0,\qquad \det L\ne0,
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\ge0\quad(k=1,2,3),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i>1,
\]
and fix
\[
A=|\det U|=\frac14,\qquad
B=|\det V|=1,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9=\frac12.
\]
For any \(\delta>0\), put
\[
R=\delta L,\qquad \tau=\delta\sigma.
\]
As in the lossless scale-free parametrization of accepted fact
`f74f24523b5f53b2`, the columns of \(R\) sum to \(\delta\),
\(\sum_i\tau_i>\delta\), and the three row masses are
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(R_{i*};0)
+\frac14\mathcal C((RU)_{i*};-\tau_i)
+\mathcal C((RV)_{i*};\tau_i)
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
P=3I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&-1\\
-1&2&-1\\
-1&-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Suppose there are \(c>0\) and permutation matrices
\(\Pi,\Gamma\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) such that
\[
\max_{1\le i,j\le3}
\left|c^{-1}(\Pi R\Gamma)_{ij}-P_{ij}\right|
\le\frac1{50}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\;
\max_{1\le i\le3}(M_i-t)
\ge\frac{523}{69312}>0.
\;}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, no strict rational or real witness to the system of
accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` occurs anywhere in the full
neighborhood (2).  The conclusion is independent of the shapes of
\(U,V\), their determinant signs, and the three endpoint allocations.

## Proof

Positive scaling of a row and permutation of its three entries do not
change \(\mathcal C(z;0)\).  Row permutation only permutes the three
middle caps.  We may therefore use
\[
\widehat R=c^{-1}\Pi R\Gamma
\]
when estimating their sum.

Fix a row \(i\), and let \(j,k\) be the other two column indices.
Condition (2) gives
\[
\frac{99}{50}\le \widehat R_{ii}\le\frac{101}{50},
\qquad
-\frac{51}{50}\le\widehat R_{ij},\widehat R_{ik}
\le-\frac{49}{50}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus this row lies in the one-positive-vertex branch of the complete
cap formula in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.  Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C(\widehat R_{i*};0)
&=
\frac{\widehat R_{ii}^{\,2}}
{(\widehat R_{ii}-\widehat R_{ij})
 (\widehat R_{ii}-\widehat R_{ik})}\\
&\ge
\frac{(99/50)^2}{(152/50)^2}
=\left(\frac{99}{152}\right)^2
=\frac{9801}{23104}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal C(R_{i*};0)
\ge\frac{29403}{23104}.
\tag{6}
\]

The three small-end caps in (1) cover \(\Delta\).  Indeed, if some
\(\lambda\in\Delta\) were outside all of them, then
\[
(RU)_{i*}\cdot\lambda<-\tau_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
The column-sum identities give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(RU)_{i*}
=\delta\mathbf1^\top,
\]
so summing the three strict inequalities would give
\[
\delta
=\delta\mathbf1^\top\lambda
<-\sum_{i=1}^3\tau_i
<-\delta,
\]
which is impossible.  Hence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3
\mathcal C((RU)_{i*};-\tau_i)\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]
This pointwise argument includes constant or repeated endpoint
projections and uses the independent values of all three \(\tau_i\).

The large-end terms in (1) are nonnegative.  Combining (5)--(7) and
using that the maximum is at least the average gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_iM_i
&\ge
\frac13\left(
3\frac{9801}{23104}+\frac14
\right)\\
&=
\frac{9801}{23104}+\frac1{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(t=1/2\),
\[
\max_i(M_i-t)
\ge
\frac{9801}{23104}-\frac5{12}
=\frac{523}{69312}>0,
\]
which proves (3).

The proof used no restriction on the endpoint shapes beyond the exact
system hypotheses, no relation among their three allocations, and only
the absolute determinant value \(A=1/4\).  Thus it applies verbatim to
every nonhomothetic stochastic parametrization and every determinant-sign
orbit allowed by accepted fact `f74f24523b5f53b2`.
