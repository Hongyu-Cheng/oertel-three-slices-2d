## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes such that
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\qquad
L_s=(1-s)B+sA_1
\quad(0\le s\le1).
\]
Put
\[
S_s=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times L_s)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\qquad
a(s)=|L_s|,
\qquad
q(s)=\frac{2(|A_0|+a(s)+|A_2|)}9.
\]
For a planar convex body \(K\), let
\[
d_K(z)=\inf\{|K\cap Q|:Q\text{ is a closed planar halfplane containing }z\}.
\]
Define
\[
D=\max\left\{
\max_{z\in A_0}d_{A_0}(z),
\max_{z\in A_2}d_{A_2}(z)
\right\}
\]
and
\[
M(s)=\max_{z\in L_s}h_{S_s}((1,z)).
\]
Then \(M\) and
\[
\Psi(s)=\max_{y\in S_s}h_{S_s}(y)-q(s)
\]
are continuous on \([0,1]\), and
\[
\Psi(s)=\max\{D,M(s)\}-q(s).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(s_0<1\) satisfy \(\Psi(s_0)=0\), and write
\[
\alpha=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{a(s_0+h)-a(s_0)}h.
\]
This finite limit exists because the area of a planar Minkowski interpolation of two polytopes is a quadratic polynomial in the interpolation parameter. Exactly one of the following active-set cases applies.

1. If \(M(s_0)<q(s_0)\), then \(D=q(s_0)\) and
\[
\lim_{h\downarrow0}
\frac{\Psi(s_0+h)-\Psi(s_0)}h
=-\frac{2\alpha}{9}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(\alpha>0\), the proposed zero barrier fails at \(s_0\).

2. If \(M(s_0)=q(s_0)>D\), then
\[
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}
\frac{\Psi(s_0+h)-\Psi(s_0)}h
=
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}\frac{M(s_0+h)-M(s_0)}h
-\frac{2\alpha}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

3. If \(M(s_0)=D=q(s_0)\), then
\[
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}
\frac{\Psi(s_0+h)-\Psi(s_0)}h
=
\liminf_{h\downarrow0}
\frac{\max\{0,M(s_0+h)-M(s_0)\}}h
-\frac{2\alpha}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, at any first zero with \(\alpha>0\), the zero-barrier property requires \(M(s_0)=q(s_0)\) and the global middle optimum, allowing its maximizing point and active halfspaces to switch, must satisfy the growth condition encoded by (3) or (4). Continuity and the accepted minimal endpoint alone do not supply that condition.

## Proof

For a two-dimensional convex body \(K\), a direction \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), and \(c\in\mathbb R\), write
\[
G_K^u(c)=|K\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le c\}|.
\]
We first prove the joint continuity needed below. If
\[
s_n\to s,\qquad u_n\to u,\qquad c_n\to c,
\]
then \(L_{s_n}\to L_s\) in Hausdorff distance. All these bodies lie in one fixed compact ball. Away from the boundary of \(L_s\) and the line
\(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle=c\}\), membership in
\[
L_{s_n}\cap\{w:\langle u_n,w\rangle\le c_n\}
\]
converges pointwise to membership in
\[
L_s\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le c\}.
\]
The exceptional set has planar area zero. Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[
G_{L_{s_n}}^{u_n}(c_n)\longrightarrow G_{L_s}^u(c).
\tag{5}
\]
The same argument applies to the fixed bodies \(A_0,A_2\).

Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` gives, for \(z\in L_s\),
\[
h_{S_s}((1,z))
=
\min_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_{L_s}^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+
\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left(
G_{A_0}^u(\langle u,z\rangle+\lambda)
+
G_{A_2}^u(\langle u,z\rangle-\lambda)
\right)
\right].
\tag{6}
\]
The two minima in (6) are genuine minima. To obtain a fixed compact parameter set for the inner one, choose \(R>0\) such that \(A_0,A_2\), and every \(L_s\) lie in the open ball of radius \(R\) about the origin. Then
\[
|\langle u,z\rangle|<R
\]
for every relevant \(u,z\). If \(\lambda\ge3R\), the two outer cap terms in (6) are respectively \(|A_0|\) and \(0\). If \(\lambda\le-3R\), they are respectively \(0\) and \(|A_2|\). The same tail values already occur at \(\lambda=3R\) and \(\lambda=-3R\). Hence the inner minimum in (6) is unchanged when \(\lambda\) is restricted to \([-3R,3R]\).

Equation (5) now shows that the bracketed function before minimization is jointly continuous in \(s,z,u,\lambda\). Taking a minimum first over the fixed compact interval \([-3R,3R]\) and then over the fixed compact circle \(\mathbb S^1\) preserves continuity. Thus
\[
(s,z)\longmapsto h_{S_s}((1,z))
\tag{7}
\]
is continuous on the compact graph \(\{(s,z):0\le s\le1,\ z\in L_s\}\).

The set-valued map \(s\mapsto L_s\) is Hausdorff-continuous. Indeed, every point represented as
\((1-s)b+sa\), with \(b\in B\) and \(a\in A_1\), is carried to
\((1-t)b+ta\) at parameter \(t\), and the displacement is uniformly bounded by a constant times \(|s-t|\). Compactness and (7) then give continuity of
\[
M(s)=\max_{z\in L_s}h_{S_s}((1,z)).
\tag{8}
\]
Explicitly, maximizers along any convergent parameter sequence have convergent subsequences giving the upper inequality, while a fixed representation of a maximizer at the limit parameter gives approximating feasible points and the lower inequality.

The same cap argument shows that \(d_{A_i}\) is continuous on \(A_i\), so its maximum is attained. Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` also gives
\[
h_{S_s}((0,z))=d_{A_0}(z),
\qquad
h_{S_s}((2,z))=d_{A_2}(z).
\tag{9}
\]
These two outer depth profiles do not depend on \(s\). Taking the maximum over all three supported slices in (8) and (9) proves (1). The area \(a(s)\) is continuous, so (1) also proves continuity of \(\Psi\).

For completeness, the asserted existence of \(\alpha\) is elementary for polytopes. Insert zero-length edges so that the boundaries of \(B\) and \(A_1\) use one common cyclic list of edge directions. The corresponding edge vectors of \((1-s)B+sA_1\) are affine functions of \(s\). After fixing one vertex, all remaining vertices are affine functions of \(s\), and the shoelace formula shows that \(a(s)\) is quadratic. Hence its one-sided derivative exists at every \(s_0<1\).

Now assume \(\Psi(s_0)=0\). Equation (1) gives
\[
D\le q(s_0),\qquad M(s_0)\le q(s_0),
\qquad \max\{D,M(s_0)\}=q(s_0).
\tag{10}
\]
If \(M(s_0)<q(s_0)\), (10) forces \(D=q(s_0)\). By continuity of \(M\), one has \(M(s_0+h)<D\) for all sufficiently small positive \(h\). Thus (1) gives
\[
\Psi(s_0+h)-\Psi(s_0)
=-\frac29\bigl(a(s_0+h)-a(s_0)\bigr),
\]
which proves (2).

If \(M(s_0)=q(s_0)>D\), continuity again preserves the strict inequality \(M(s_0+h)>D\) for all sufficiently small positive \(h\). Substitution in (1), followed by subtraction of the convergent area difference quotient, gives (3).

Finally, if \(M(s_0)=D=q(s_0)\), equation (1) gives exactly
\[
\Psi(s_0+h)-\Psi(s_0)
=
\max\{0,M(s_0+h)-M(s_0)\}
-\frac29\bigl(a(s_0+h)-a(s_0)\bigr).
\]
Taking the lower limit proves (4). The three cases exhaust (10).

Accepted theorem `96f5513b8ab88a8f` supplies \(\Psi(0)\ge0\) because \(L_0=B=(A_0+A_2)/2\). Equations (2) through (4) show precisely what is still required at a first zero reached from that endpoint. In particular, a fixed-witness derivative cannot replace the global quantity \(M\), which already maximizes over every middle point and allows its active halfspaces to switch.
