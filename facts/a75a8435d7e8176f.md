## Statement

Assume all hypotheses of the exact barycentric system in accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`, and let \(N=2\). Put
\[
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9.
\]
For arbitrary \(L\in\mathbb R^{2\times3}\) with column sums \(1\) and
arbitrary independent \(\sigma _1,\sigma _2\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
M_i=
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]
Then
\[
\max\{M_1,M_2\}>t.
\]
Consequently, the two simultaneous strict inequalities \(M_1<t\) and
\(M_2<t\) are infeasible.

## Proof

We first record a complement-cover identity which includes all constant,
repeated-value, and boundary cases. For \(z\in\mathbb R^3\) and
\(\tau,\eta\in\mathbb R\), if
\[
\tau+\eta\le1,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
\mathcal C(z;\tau)+\mathcal C(\mathbf1-z;\eta)\ge1.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, for every \(\lambda\in\Delta\),
\[
z\cdot\lambda+(\mathbf1-z)\cdot\lambda=1.
\]
If \(\lambda\) belonged to neither closed cap in (2), then
\[
z\cdot\lambda<\tau,\qquad
(\mathbf1-z)\cdot\lambda<\eta,
\]
and adding would give \(1<\tau+\eta\), contrary to (1). Thus the two
closed caps cover \(\Delta\), and their normalized areas have sum at
least \(1\).

Let
\[
\ell=L_{1*},\qquad q=\sigma_1+\sigma_2.
\]
The column-sum identity for \(L\) gives
\[
L_{2*}=\mathbf1^\top-\ell.
\tag{3}
\]
Since \(U\) and \(V\) also have column sums \(1\),
\[
(LU)_{2*}=\mathbf1^\top-\ell U,\qquad
(LV)_{2*}=\mathbf1^\top-\ell V.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply (2) first with \(z=\ell\) and \(\tau=\eta=0\). This gives the
unconditional middle-slice bound
\[
\mathcal C(L_{1*};0)+\mathcal C(L_{2*};0)\ge1.
\tag{5}
\]

At height zero, the two thresholds are \(-\sigma_1\) and
\(-\sigma_2\). Hence, whenever \(q\ge-1\), their sum is at most \(1\),
and (2), (4) give
\[
\mathcal C((LU)_{1*};-\sigma_1)
+\mathcal C((LU)_{2*};-\sigma_2)\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]
At height two, the thresholds are \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\).
Whenever \(q\le1\), (2), (4) give
\[
\mathcal C((LV)_{1*};\sigma_1)
+\mathcal C((LV)_{2*};\sigma_2)\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]
Every real \(q\) satisfies at least one of \(q\ge-1\) and \(q\le1\).
Combining (5) with the applicable endpoint inequality therefore yields
\[
M_1+M_2\ge1+\min\{A,B\}.
\tag{8}
\]
More explicitly, (6) gives \(M_1+M_2\ge1+A\), while (7) gives
\(M_1+M_2\ge1+B\). If \(-1\le q\le1\), both hold simultaneously and
the stronger bound \(M_1+M_2\ge1+A+B\) follows. This is where the two
independent endpoint slopes enter; no equality or coupling between
\(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\) has been imposed.

It remains to compare (8) with the exact constant \(2/9\). Write
\[
m=\min\{A,B\},\qquad M=\max\{A,B\}.
\]
The residual-strip variables in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` are
\[
R=\frac mM,\qquad S=\frac1M.
\]
The strict lower strip bound implies
\[
S>1-R.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(0<R\le9/25\), this is exactly \(S>L_0(R)=1-R\). For
\(R>9/25\),
\[
L_0(R)-(1-R)
=\frac{5R+2\sqrt R-3}{2}>0,
\]
because the numerator is \(0\) at \(R=9/25\) and is strictly
increasing. Thus (9) holds throughout the residual strip. Multiplying
(9) by \(M>0\) gives the strict area-triangle inequality
\[
M<1+m.
\tag{10}
\]

Using (8) and (10),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{M_1+M_2}{2}-\frac{2(1+A+B)}9
&\ge
\frac{1+m}{2}-\frac{2(1+m+M)}9\\
&=\frac{5(1+m)-4M}{18}\\
&>\frac{1+m}{18}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore the larger of \(M_1,M_2\) is strictly greater than
\(2(1+A+B)/9\), proving the statement.

STRATA AND STRICTNESS AUDIT
The argument uses the cap definition itself rather than any denominator
from the piecewise-quadratic formulas. Consequently it applies
simultaneously to every ordering of the three entries of each of
\(L_{i*}\), \((LU)_{i*}\), and \((LV)_{i*}\), to every two-value
coalescence, and to every constant row. A constant cap may be empty or
full, and two complementary caps may overlap in positive area, but the
pointwise cover (2) remains exact.

The boundary values \(q=-1\) and \(q=1\) are also included. At equality
in (1), a point outside both closed caps would imply the impossible
strict inequality \(1<1\), so no measure-zero passage is being used.
Finally, the residual strip is strict, hence (10) and (11) are strict.
If both registered row inequalities held, their sum would be strictly
less than \(4(1+A+B)/9\), contradicting (8) and (11).
