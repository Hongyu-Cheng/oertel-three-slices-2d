## Statement

Let
\[
 f_1(x,y)=x,\qquad f_2(x,y)=y,\qquad
 f_3(x,y)=-1-x-y,
\qquad L_1=L_2=L_3=-100.
\]
Let
\[
 A_0=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
 \left(-\frac{999}{10},-\frac{981}{10}\right),
 \left(197,-\frac{981}{10}\right),
 \left(\frac{49}{2},-50\right)
 \right\}.
\]
Let \(A_2\) be the parallelogram with center and full edge generators
\[
 c=\left(-\frac{99}{2},\frac{991}{10}\right),\qquad
 a=\left(302,\frac{19}{10}\right),\qquad
 b=\left(-\frac{1}{10},\frac{1}{20}\right),
\]
that is,
\[
 A_2=c+\left[-\frac a2,\frac a2\right]
       +\left[-\frac b2,\frac b2\right],
\qquad
 B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Suppose that \(K\supseteq B\) is a compact two-dimensional convex body and,
with
\[
 A=|K\setminus B|,
\]
satisfies the three crossing equalities
\[
 \left|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq L_i\}\right|
 {}\mathbin{+}\left|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-L_i\}\right|
 {}\mathbin{+}\left|K\cap\{f_i\leq0\}\right|
 =
 \frac{2(|A_0|+|A_2|+|K|)}9
 \quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
 A\notin
 \left[
 \frac{23930259}{6650},\,3600
 \right].
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, although \(A=3600\) lies strictly inside the scalar interval
of accepted fact `6f6869df3d151764`, no convex rational polygon \(K\) can
realize its three prescribed cap increments.

## Proof

Exact Minkowski addition and shoelace calculation give the cyclic vertices
of \(B\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(-\frac{6009}{40},\frac3{80}\right),
\left(-\frac{6007}{40},\frac1{80}\right),
\left(-\frac{69}{40},\frac1{80}\right),
\left(\frac{5971}{40},\frac{77}{80}\right),\\
&\left(\frac{5969}{40},\frac{79}{80}\right),
\left(\frac{2519}{40},\frac{2003}{80}\right),
\left(-\frac{3521}{40},\frac{1927}{80}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The endpoint and core areas are
\[
 M=|A_0|=\frac{1428089}{200},\qquad
 m=|A_2|=\frac{1529}{100},\qquad
 k_0=|B|=\frac{2202453}{400}.
\tag{4}
\]
All three \(A_0\)-caps in (1) are empty.  The three \(A_2\)-caps and the
three core caps are, respectively,
\[
 (v_1,v_2,v_3)=
 \left(
 \frac{918929}{60400},
 \frac{56573}{3800},
 \frac{3083993}{202600}
 \right),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
 (b_1,b_2,b_3)=
 \left(
 \frac{698967161}{241600},
 0,
 \frac{14291658397}{4862400}
 \right).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
 h_0=\frac{2(M+m+k_0)}9=\frac{1688249}{600}
\]
and the three residuals in accepted fact `6f6869df3d151764` are
\[
 (F_1,F_2,F_3)=
 \left(
 \frac{68523839}{724800},
 -\frac{7976753}{2850},
 \frac{684104333}{4862400}
 \right).
\tag{7}
\]
That accepted fact gives the scalar interval
\[
 \frac{23930259}{6650}
 \leq A\leq
 \frac{17881582275919}{2325037600}.
\tag{8}
\]
It also rewrites the second crossing equation as
\[
 e_2:=
 |(K\setminus B)\cap\{y\leq0\}|
 =
 \frac{2A}{9}-F_2
 =
 \frac{2A}{9}+\frac{7976753}{2850}.
\tag{9}
\]

The polygon \(B\) lies in \(\{y\geq1/80\}\), and its bottom face is
\[
 E_0=
 \left[
 \left(-\frac{6007}{40},\frac1{80}\right),
 \left(-\frac{69}{40},\frac1{80}\right)
 \right].
\]
Write
\[
 W=|E_0|=\frac{2969}{20}.
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(B\) has no area in \(\{y\leq0\}\), equation (9) is simply
\[
 e_2=|K\cap\{y\leq0\}|.
\tag{11}
\]
The complementary extension area above the line \(y=0\) is
\[
 s:=A-e_2
 =\frac{7A}{9}-\frac{7976753}{2850}.
\tag{12}
\]
For calibration, accepted fact `cf1a8ff16a0da82a`, applied to
\(H=\{y\geq0\}\), gives
\[
 s\geq\Gamma(B,H)=\frac{2969}{3200}.
\tag{13}
\]
The value in (13) is the triangle area obtained from the bottom face
\(E_0\) and any minimizing point on \(y=0\).

We now retain the whole section on \(y=0\), rather than only one point.
Let
\[
 I=K\cap\{y=0\}
\]
and let \(w\geq0\) be its length.  Convexity gives
\[
 \operatorname{conv}(E_0\cup I)
 \subseteq K\cap\left\{0\leq y\leq\frac1{80}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The convex hull of two horizontal segments of lengths \(W,w\), separated
by height \(1/80\), has area
\[
 \frac{W+w}{160};
\tag{15}
\]
this formula is independent of their horizontal displacement.  The body
\(B\) has no planar area below \(y=1/80\), so (14)-(15) and (12) imply
\[
 s\geq\frac{W+w}{160},
\qquad
 w\leq160s-W.
\tag{16}
\]
This is the exact multi-point strengthening of (13).

Assume now that \(A\leq3600\).  Equation (12) gives
\[
 s\leq
 \frac{3247}{2850}.
\]
Consequently (16) gives
\[
 0\leq w\leq
 160\frac{3247}{2850}-\frac{2969}{20}
 =\frac{7715}{228}
 =:w_\star,
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
 W-w_\star=\frac{65329}{570}>0.
\tag{18}
\]

For a real height \(r\) in the vertical projection of \(K\), let
\[
 \ell(r)=\left|K\cap\{y=r\}\right|
\]
be the one-dimensional length of its horizontal section.  Convexity of
\(K\) implies concavity of \(\ell\): for any two heights, the corresponding
section intervals have their weighted Minkowski sum inside the section at
the weighted height, and interval lengths add under Minkowski sum.
In particular,
\[
 \ell(0)=w,\qquad
 \ell(1/80)\geq W.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(t>0\) such that the section at height \(-t\) is nonempty, combine that
section with the section at \(1/80\) in the proportions
\[
 \frac{1/80}{t+1/80},
\qquad
 \frac{t}{t+1/80},
\]
so that the resulting height is \(0\).  Taking interval lengths gives
\[
 w\geq
 \frac{1/80}{t+1/80}\ell(-t)
 +
 \frac{t}{t+1/80}\ell(1/80).
\]
Using (19) and rearranging,
\[
 \ell(-t)\leq w-80t(W-w).
\tag{20}
\]
The right side of (20) is nonnegative only for
\[
 0\leq t\leq\frac{w}{80(W-w)}.
\]
Integrating (20), using (11), yields the exact lower-cap bound
\[
 e_2
 \leq
 \int_0^{w/[80(W-w)]}
 \left(w-80t(W-w)\right)\,dt
 =
 \frac{w^2}{160(W-w)}.
\tag{21}
\]
The right side of (21) is increasing for \(0\leq w<W\).  Equations
(17)-(18) therefore give
\[
 e_2\leq
 \frac{w_\star^2}{160(W-w_\star)}
 =
 \frac{59521225}{953280768}.
\tag{22}
\]

On the other hand, the left endpoint in (8) is generated by the
\(-F_2\) term.  Substitution in (9) gives
\[
 e_2\geq
 e_2\left(\frac{23930259}{6650}\right)
 =
 \frac{23930259}{6650}.
\tag{23}
\]
The exact gap between (23) and the upper bound (22) is
\[
 \frac{23930259}{6650}
 -
 \frac{59521225}{953280768}
 =
 \frac{600312101626649}{166824134400}
 >0.
\tag{24}
\]
This contradiction proves (2).

All polygon areas, the residuals in (7), the interval in (8), and every
rational identity in (17), (22), and (24) were independently checked over
\(\mathbb Q\) with SageMath \(10.8\).  The proof itself uses only convexity,
one-dimensional interval addition, and the two accepted predecessor facts.
