## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}
\subset
\mathcal A:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^3:
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1\},
\]
and normalize planar area so that \(|\Delta|=1\). For
\(z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\mathcal C(z_1,z_2,z_3;\tau)
:=
\left|
\left\{\lambda\in\Delta:
\sum_{j=1}^3z_j\lambda_j\ge\tau
\right\}
\right|.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(a\le b\le c\) are the sorted entries of \(z\), then the complete formula is as follows.

If \(a<b<c\), then
\[
\mathcal C(a,b,c;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\le a,\\[1mm]
1-\dfrac{(\tau-a)^2}{(b-a)(c-a)},&a<\tau\le b,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{(c-\tau)^2}{(c-a)(c-b)},&b<\tau<c,\\[3mm]
0,&\tau\ge c.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
If \(a=b<c\), then
\[
\mathcal C(a,a,c;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\le a,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(c-\tau)^2}{(c-a)^2},&a<\tau<c,\\[3mm]
0,&\tau\ge c.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
If \(a<b=c\), then
\[
\mathcal C(a,c,c;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\le a,\\[1mm]
1-\dfrac{(\tau-a)^2}{(c-a)^2},&a<\tau<c,\\[3mm]
0,&\tau\ge c.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
If \(a=b=c\), then
\[
\mathcal C(a,a,a;\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau\le a,\\
0,&\tau>a.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\det U\ne0,\qquad\det V\ne0,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\ge0
\qquad(k=1,2,3).
\tag{7}
\]
Put
\[
A=|\det U|,\qquad B=|\det V|,
\qquad
R=\frac{\min\{A,B\}}{\max\{A,B\}},
\qquad
S=\frac1{\max\{A,B\}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Assume that \((R,S)\) lies in the exact strict residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, namely
\[
0<R<\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\qquad
L_0(R)<S<U_0(R),
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
L_0(R)=
\begin{cases}
1-R,&0<R\le9/25,\\[1mm]
(3R+2\sqrt R-1)/2,&9/25<R,
\end{cases}
\quad
U_0(R)=
\begin{cases}
1+R,&0<R\le1/2,\\[1mm]
2-R,&1/2<R.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
Define
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{u^1,u^2,u^3\},
\qquad
A_1=\Delta,
\qquad
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{v^1,v^2,v^3\},
\tag{11}
\]
where \(u^j,v^j\) are the columns of \(U,V\).

Then the compatible residual triangular triple (11) is a counterexample to the locked \(2T/9\) conclusion if and only if there exist
\[
N\in\{2,3\},\qquad
L\in\mathbb R^{N\times3},\qquad
\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_N\in\mathbb R,
\tag{12}
\]
such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^NL_{ij}=1
\qquad(j=1,2,3)
\tag{13}
\]
and, for every \(i=1,\ldots,N\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\,\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\,\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
<
\frac{2(1+A+B)}9.}
\tag{14}
\]
Here a counterexample means the compact polytope reconstructed in the proof has exactly the three prescribed integer slices and has no point in any slice of halfspace depth at least \(2T/9\). Formula (14) retains an independent exact endpoint allocation in each row. No sign assumption is made on \(\det U\) or \(\det V\).

## Proof

We first prove the determinant and compatibility assertions.

Let
\[
\mathcal A_0=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\mathbf1^\top x=0\}
\]
be the translation space of \(\mathcal A\). Equation (6) says that \(U\) and \(V\) map \(\mathcal A\) to itself and \(\mathcal A_0\) to itself. The restriction of \(U\) to \(\mathcal A\) sends \(e_j\) to \(u^j\), so it sends \(\Delta\) to \(A_0\). On the one-dimensional quotient
\(\mathbb R^3/\mathcal A_0\), the induced map is the identity because
\[
\mathbf1^\top Ux=\mathbf1^\top x.
\]
In a basis consisting of two vectors in \(\mathcal A_0\) and one vector whose coordinate sum is \(1\), the matrix of \(U\) is block upper triangular and its quotient diagonal entry is \(1\). Hence
\[
\det U=\det(U|_{\mathcal A_0}).
\]
The absolute determinant of the two-dimensional restriction is its planar area multiplier. Since \(|A_1|=1\),
\[
|A_0|=|\det U|=A.
\tag{15}
\]
The same proof gives
\[
|A_2|=|\det V|=B.
\tag{16}
\]
This also proves that negative determinants merely reverse a chosen vertex ordering. They do not change the triangle, its area, or any cap fraction. Nonzero determinants are exactly the nondegeneracy condition for the two endpoint triangles.

For the \(k\)-th barycentric coordinate,
\[
\min_{x_0\in A_0,\ x_2\in A_2}
\lambda_k\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)
=
\frac12\left(\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Every point in the midpoint sum has coordinate sum \(1\). Therefore it lies in \(\Delta\) if and only if all three coordinates are nonnegative. Equation (17) proves the exact equivalence
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\ge0
\ \ (k=1,2,3).
\tag{18}
\]
Thus (7) is neither a vertexwise relaxation nor an area-only condition.

We next derive the cap formulas. Permuting the vertices leaves (1) unchanged, so suppose the vertex values are already sorted.

If \(a<b<c\) and \(a<\tau\le b\), the strict sublevel set
\[
\left\{\lambda\in\Delta:
a\lambda_1+b\lambda_2+c\lambda_3<\tau\right\}
\]
is the triangle at the \(a\)-vertex. Its two edge-intercept ratios are
\[
\frac{\tau-a}{b-a},
\qquad
\frac{\tau-a}{c-a}.
\]
Its area fraction is their product. Taking the complementary upper cap gives the second branch of (2). If \(b<\tau<c\), the upper cap itself is the triangle at the \(c\)-vertex. Its edge-intercept ratios are
\[
\frac{c-\tau}{c-a},
\qquad
\frac{c-\tau}{c-b},
\]
which gives the third branch. The support endpoint branches are immediate. At \(\tau=b\), the two quadratic expressions agree.

If \(a=b<c\), every nontrivial upper cap is the similar triangle at the unique \(c\)-vertex, giving (3). If \(a<b=c\), the omitted lower cap is the similar triangle at the unique \(a\)-vertex, giving (4). If all three values coincide, the affine functional is constant on the whole triangle, so the closed upper cap is the whole triangle at equality and is empty only above that value. This gives (5).

For every nonconstant functional, a level set is a line and has planar area zero. Hence replacing \(>\) by \(\ge\), or \(<\) by \(\le\), does not change any area in (2)-(4), including a repeated value corresponding to an entire support edge. Formula (5) separately records the only case where open and closed caps differ by positive area. This completes every projection stratum.

We now prove the implication from a residual triangular counterexample to the matrix system.

Apply one invertible affine map to the planar coordinate in all three slices, sending the middle triangle to \(\Delta\). Rescale planar area by the common positive absolute determinant so that \(|A_1|=1\). All three slice areas and every halfspace mass are multiplied by the same number. Compatibility, the depth ratio \(2T/9\), and counterexample status are therefore unchanged. Choosing any orderings of the endpoint vertices produces matrices \(U,V\) satisfying (6). Equations (15)-(18) give (7)-(8), and accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives exactly (9)-(10).

Put
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9.
\tag{19}
\]
For a nonzero planar direction \(q\), write
\[
F_{j,q}(r)
=
\left|A_j\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le r\}\right|
\qquad(j=0,1,2)
\tag{20}
\]
and define the exact middle-slice profile
\[
\Phi_q(r)
=
F_{1,q}(r)
+\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left(
F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)
+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)
\right).
\tag{21}
\]
The allocation \(\lambda\) in (21) is the actual slope of a three-dimensional halfspace through height \(1\). It is not fixed across directions.

Each cap function in (20) is continuous because every nonzero directional level set is a line of planar area zero. For \(r\) in a bounded interval, the infimum in (21) may be restricted to a bounded interval of \(\lambda\): outside it one endpoint cap is full and the other is empty. Joint continuity then proves that \(\Phi_q\) is continuous. It is nondecreasing in \(r\). For sufficiently negative \(r\) it is \(0\), and for sufficiently positive \(r\) it is
\[
1+\min\{A,B\}.
\]
The strict area-triangle inequalities contained in the residual strip give
\[
\max\{A,B\}<1+\min\{A,B\},
\]
so
\[
1+A+B<2\bigl(1+\min\{A,B\}\bigr).
\]
Consequently
\[
t<\frac49\bigl(1+\min\{A,B\}\bigr)
<1+\min\{A,B\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho_q:=\inf\{r:\Phi_q(r)\ge t\}
\tag{22}
\]
is finite and satisfies
\[
\Phi_q(\rho_q)=t,\qquad
\{r:\Phi_q(r)\ge t\}=[\rho_q,\infty).
\tag{23}
\]

The profile halfplanes already cut out a subset of \(A_1\); no extra copy of \(A_1\) is needed in the Helly step. Indeed, put
\[
m_j(q)=\min_{x\in A_j}q\cdot x.
\]
Compatibility gives
\[
m_0(q)+m_2(q)\ge2m_1(q).
\tag{24}
\]
At \(r=m_1(q)\), take \(\lambda=m_0(q)-m_1(q)\). The endpoint thresholds in (21) are then
\[
m_0(q),\qquad
2m_1(q)-m_0(q)\le m_2(q).
\]
Both endpoint caps and the middle support cap have area zero. Hence
\[
\Phi_q(m_1(q))=0<t,
\qquad
\rho_q>m_1(q).
\tag{25}
\]
Therefore
\[
\bigcap_{q\ne0}\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q\}
\subseteq
\bigcap_{q\ne0}\{z:q\cdot z\ge m_1(q)\}
=A_1.
\tag{26}
\]

For a point \(z\in A_1\), every nonhorizontal closed halfspace containing \((1,z)\) may be translated parallel toward that point until its boundary passes through it. After choosing the planar orientation, its three thresholds are
\[
q\cdot z+\lambda,\qquad q\cdot z,\qquad q\cdot z-\lambda.
\]
Thus its least possible mass in direction \(q\) is exactly
\(\Phi_q(q\cdot z)\). The two horizontal halfspaces containing height \(1\) have masses \(A+1\) and \(1+B\), both greater than \(T/2>t\) in the strict residual area-triangle regime. Consequently the middle depth region at level \(t\) is
\[
\bigcap_{q\ne0}\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q\}.
\tag{27}
\]
Because the normalized triple is a counterexample, (27) is empty.

We justify the finite Helly step, rather than invoking an invalid infinite-family version. Choose three fixed directions whose positive hull is the whole plane. The intersection of their three profile halfplanes is either empty, in which case it is already a finite certificate, or is a bounded closed polygon \(P\), because its recession cone is \(\{0\}\). In the latter case,
\[
P\cap
\bigcap_{q\ne0}\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q\}
=\varnothing.
\]
Compactness of \(P\) gives finitely many further profile halfplanes whose intersection with \(P\) is empty. Expanding \(P\) back into its three defining halfplanes produces a finite empty family consisting only of profile halfplanes. Planar Helly applied to that finite family supplies a minimal empty subfamily of at most three profile halfplanes. One halfplane is never empty, so its size is
\[
N\in\{2,3\}.
\tag{28}
\]
Write that subfamily as
\[
q_i\cdot z\ge\rho_i
\qquad(i=1,\ldots,N).
\]
The elementary Farkas alternative for an empty minimal family of affine halfplanes gives positive numbers \(\alpha_i\) such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\alpha_iq_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^N\alpha_i\rho_i>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Minimality makes every \(\alpha_i\) positive, including the two-row case of opposite normals.

Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that
\[
c_i=\rho_i-\varepsilon,
\qquad
W:=\sum_{i=1}^N\alpha_ic_i>0.
\tag{30}
\]
By (23),
\[
\Phi_{q_i}(c_i)<t.
\tag{31}
\]
The continuous endpoint objective in (21) attains its infimum: on each of its two tails it is exactly one of the full endpoint areas, so the minimization reduces to a compact interval. Choose an attaining allocation \(\lambda_i\).

Define
\[
\ell_i(x)
=
\frac{\alpha_i}{W}(c_i-q_i\cdot x).
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (29)-(30) give the affine identity
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\ell_i(x)=1
\qquad(x\in\mathcal A).
\tag{33}
\]
Let
\[
L_{ij}=\ell_i(e_j),
\qquad
\sigma_i=\frac{\alpha_i\lambda_i}{W}.
\tag{34}
\]
Equation (33) is exactly (13). Moreover,
\[
q_i\cdot x\le c_i+\lambda_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell_i(x)\ge-\sigma_i,
\]
\[
q_i\cdot x\le c_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell_i(x)\ge0,
\]
\[
q_i\cdot x\le c_i-\lambda_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\ell_i(x)\ge\sigma_i.
\tag{35}
\]
On \(A_0\), write a point as \(U\mu\) with \(\mu\in\Delta\). Then
\[
\ell_i(U\mu)=(LU)_{i*}\cdot\mu.
\]
The analogous identity holds with \(V\) on \(A_2\). Equations (1) and (35) therefore identify the three cap areas in row \(i\) with the three terms on the left side of (14). Equation (31) proves their strict sum is below \(t\). This constructs \(N,L,\sigma_i\) and proves the forward implication with the full independently attained infimal convolution.

The perturbation in (30) is also the exact open-versus-closed step. The closed profile halfplanes at \(\rho_i\) are empty in common. Moving each threshold strictly below \(\rho_i\) preserves infeasibility because (29)-(30) give
\[
\sum_i\alpha_iq_i\cdot z=0
<
W=\sum_i\alpha_ic_i.
\]
Their open complementary caps \(q_i\cdot z<c_i\), equivalently
\(\ell_i(z)>0\), cover the whole plane. Each corresponding closed three-dimensional halfspace has strictly low mass by (31). Nonconstant cap boundaries have area zero, so this strict cover does not alter any row mass.

We prove the converse and the reconstruction.

Assume (6)-(14). Define \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) by (11) and put
\[
C=
\operatorname{conv}\left(
(\{0\}\times A_0)
\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\right).
\tag{36}
\]
This is a compact polytope. Its height-zero and height-two sections are \(A_0\) and \(A_2\). A point in its height-one section has the form
\[
\tau x_0+(1-2\tau)x_1+\tau x_2
=(1-2\tau)x_1
+2\tau\frac{x_0+x_2}{2},
\qquad
0\le\tau\le\frac12.
\tag{37}
\]
Compatibility (18) and convexity of \(A_1\) put (37) in \(A_1\). Conversely the whole copy of \(A_1\) occurs at height \(1\). Since \(C\) lies between heights \(0\) and \(2\),
\[
C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)
=
(\{0\}\times A_0)
\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\tag{38}
\]
Thus (36) reconstructs the locked three-slice polytope exactly.

For each row of \(L\), let
\[
\ell_i(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^3L_{ij}\lambda_j
\]
and define the closed three-dimensional halfspace
\[
H_i=
\{(h,\lambda):
\ell_i(\lambda)-\sigma_i(h-1)\ge0\}.
\tag{39}
\]
Its three integer-slice sections are precisely
\[
\ell_i\ge-\sigma_i,\qquad
\ell_i\ge0,\qquad
\ell_i\ge\sigma_i.
\tag{40}
\]
The determinant area ratios and the cap formulas show that its mass is exactly the left side of (14). Hence every \(H_i\) has mass strictly below \(t\).

Equation (13) gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\ell_i(\lambda)
=
\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_j
=1
\qquad(\lambda\in\mathcal A).
\tag{41}
\]
Therefore, at every middle point, some row satisfies
\(\ell_i(\lambda)>0\). That point belongs to the corresponding closed halfspace \(H_i\), whose mass is below \(t\). No middle point has depth at least \(t\).

Constant affine rows cause no hidden boundary problem. If
\(\ell_i\equiv c\), formulas (3)-(5) give the exact full or empty middle and endpoint sections in (40). Equation (41), not a measure-zero argument, still guarantees a strictly positive row at every middle point. If \(c<0\) and \(\sigma_i=0\), (39) is empty and the row can simply be discarded; it covers no middle point. If \(c\ge0\) and \(\sigma_i=0\), (5) records the closed equality correctly. If \(\sigma_i\ne0\), (39) is an ordinary horizontal halfspace and (5) again gives its exact slice masses. Thus reconstruction remains literal on every constant and repeated projection stratum.

It remains only to exclude witnesses in the endpoint slices. We use an elementary triangle bound. Let \(x\) have barycentric coordinates
\[
0\le\beta\le\gamma\le\delta,\qquad
\beta+\gamma+\delta=1
\]
in a triangle of area \(a\).

First suppose \(\beta=\gamma=0\). Then \(x\) is the vertex carrying coordinate \(\delta=1\). Choose a supporting line through that vertex whose normal lies in the interior of its normal cone. The closed halfplane on the exterior side containing the vertex intersects the triangle only in that vertex, so its planar cap has area \(0\).

Now suppose \((\beta,\gamma)\ne(0,0)\). At the vertex carrying coordinate \(\delta\), take points at fractions \(2\beta\) and \(2\gamma\) along its two incident edges. They are distinct in this case, and their midpoint is \(x\). The line through them bounds a triangular cap containing \(x\) on its boundary and having area
\[
4\beta\gamma a\le\frac{4a}{9}.
\tag{42}
\]
This remains literal when exactly one of \(\beta,\gamma\) is zero: one edge point is the \(\delta\)-vertex, the line is a supporting edge line, and the closed cap has area \(0\). The last inequality in (42) follows from
\(\beta+\gamma\le2/3\). Thus in every barycentric case there is a closed planar halfplane containing \(x\) whose cap area is at most \(4a/9\). A three-dimensional halfspace may realize this cap on the endpoint slice while excluding the other two compact slices by choosing a sufficiently large coefficient in the height direction. Hence every endpoint point has mixed depth at most \(4a/9\).

The residual strip in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` includes the strict area-triangle inequalities, so each of \(A,B,1\) is strictly less than
\[
\frac{1+A+B}{2}.
\]
Equation (42), together with the zero-area vertex case just proved, gives, on either endpoint slice,
\[
h_S(y)\le\frac{4a}{9}
<
\frac{2(1+A+B)}9=t.
\tag{43}
\]
Together with the middle low-halfspace cover, this proves that the reconstructed triple is a genuine counterexample to the locked conclusion.

We have proved both implications. All determinant signs, two-row and three-row Helly certificates, independently optimized endpoint allocations, strict open covers, closed cap masses, repeated projection values, constant rows, endpoint exclusion including all vertex and edge degeneracies, and polytope reconstruction are included. Finally, on every fixed ordering and multiplicity stratum of the entries of \(L_{i*}\), \((LU)_{i*}\), and \((LV)_{i*}\), formulas (2)-(5) make (14) a finite piecewise-quadratic rational system. Proving that this strict system is infeasible is therefore exactly the remaining finite lemma, which is not asserted here.
