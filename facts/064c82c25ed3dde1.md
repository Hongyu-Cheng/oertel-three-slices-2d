## Statement

Use the notation and constraints of accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e`.
Suppose exactly one cyclic endpoint pair vanishes.  Then
\[
D>\frac4{169}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus no exactly-one-pair stratum satisfies the necessary scale condition
\(D\leq4/169\).

## Proof

By relabeling the barycentric vertices, suppose
\[
x_{12}=x_{21}=0,
\tag{2}
\]
while \(x_{13},x_{31},x_{23},x_{32}\) are positive.  Put
\[
\ell_i=L_i,\qquad
b=x_{13}=1-\ell_1,\qquad
d=x_{23}=1-\ell_2,\qquad
e=x_{32},\qquad f=x_{31}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
e+f=1-\ell_3,
\qquad
0<\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,b,d,e,f<1.
\tag{4}
\]
Formula (4) of the accepted normal form gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D
&=\ell_3+\ell_2e+\ell_1f
  +\rho_{23}de+\rho_{31}fb\\
&\geq \ell_3+\ell_2e+\ell_1f.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Write the cap vertices in barycentric coordinates as
\[
u_1=(-h_1,A,B),\qquad
u_2=(C,-h_2,E),\qquad
u_3=(F,G,-h_3),
\tag{6}
\]
where all six letters are positive,
\[
A+B=1+h_1,\qquad
C+E=1+h_2,\qquad
F+G=1+h_3,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
h_i=\frac{D}{2\ell_i}.
\tag{8}
\]
The relevant marked points, in \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\)-coordinates, are
\[
p_{13}=(0,\ell_1),\quad
p_{12}=p_{21}=e_3=(0,0),\quad
p_{23}=(\ell_2,0),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
p_{32}=(1-e,e),\qquad
p_{31}=(f,1-f).
\tag{10}
\]

Five instances of convexity constraint (10) in the accepted normal form
give
\[
AC\geq h_1h_2,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
fB\geq b(f+h_1),\qquad
bF\geq f(b+h_3),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
eE\geq d(e+h_2),\qquad
dG\geq e(d+h_3).
\tag{13}
\]
For completeness, (11) is the determinant of the directed edge
\(u_1e_3\) against \(u_2\).  The two determinants in (12) use respectively
the edge \(p_{13}u_1\) against \(p_{31}\), and the edge \(u_3p_{31}\)
against \(p_{13}\).  The two in (13) use respectively the edge
\(u_2p_{23}\) against \(p_{32}\), and the edge \(p_{32}u_3\) against
\(p_{23}\).

We first show that one of \(\ell_1,\ell_2\) is not small.  The four points
\(e_3,u_1,u_2,u_3\) lie in the reduced body \(K\), whose coordinate area
is \(5D/4\).  Their convex hull has twice its area equal to
\[
T=CG+h_2F+FA+h_1G.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore \(T\leq5D/2\).  By (11) and AM--GM,
\[
\begin{aligned}
T
&=Fh_2+Gh_1+FA+GC\\
&\geq
\left(\sqrt{Fh_2}+\sqrt{Gh_1}\right)^2\\
&\geq
(F+G)\min(h_1,h_2)
=(1+h_3)\min(h_1,h_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Equation (5) gives \(\ell_3<D\), hence \(h_3>1/2\).  Combining
(14)--(15) with (8) yields
\[
\max(\ell_1,\ell_2)>\frac3{10}.
\tag{16}
\]
Interchanging labels \(1\) and \(2\) if necessary, assume from now on
\[
L:=\ell_1\geq\frac3{10}.
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that
\[
0<D\leq\delta:=\frac4{169}.
\tag{18}
\]
Introduce the positive dimensionless variables
\[
\ell_2=Da,\qquad
\ell_3=Dc,\qquad
f=Dk.
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
e=1-D(c+k),\qquad
d=1-Da,
\tag{20}
\]
and (5) becomes
\[
c+a\bigl(1-D(c+k)\bigr)+Lk\leq1.
\tag{21}
\]
In particular \(c+Lk<1\).  Since \(L\geq3/10\),
\[
0<c<1,\qquad
0<k<\frac{10}{3},\qquad
c+k<\frac{10}{3}.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus
\[
1-\delta(c+k)>\frac{467}{507}>\frac9{10}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (20)--(23) and (21) also give
\[
0<a<\frac{507}{467}<\frac{11}{10}.
\tag{24}
\]
Replacing \(D\) by its upper bound in (21) gives the necessary inequality
\[
c+a\bigl(1-\delta(c+k)\bigr)+Lk\leq1.
\tag{25}
\]

Put
\[
t=a-c-k.
\tag{26}
\]
The two inequalities involving \(F,G\) in (12)--(13), together with
\(F+G=1+h_3\), imply
\[
f+\frac{fh_3}{b}
+e+\frac{eh_3}{d}
\leq1+h_3.
\]
Using (19)--(20) and \(h_3=1/(2c)\), this is exactly
\[
\frac{k}{1-L}+\frac{t}{1-Da}\leq2c^2.
\tag{27}
\]

The other three determinant inequalities give a product constraint.
Indeed, (12)--(13) and (7) imply
\[
A\leq L+h_1\left(1-\frac{b}{f}\right),
\qquad
C\leq Da+h_2\left(1-\frac{d}{e}\right).
\tag{28}
\]
Define
\[
P_D=2L^2+D-\frac{1-L}{k},
\qquad
Q_D=2a^2+\frac{t}{1-D(c+k)}.
\tag{29}
\]
Dividing (28) by \(h_1,h_2\), respectively, gives
\[
\frac{A}{h_1}\leq\frac{P_D}{D},
\qquad
\frac{C}{h_2}\leq DQ_D.
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(A,C>0\), both right sides in (30) are positive.  Combining
(11) and (30) yields
\[
P_DQ_D\geq1.
\tag{31}
\]

It remains to separate the sign of \(t\).  First suppose \(t\geq0\).
Equations (18), (23), and (27), and equations (29)--(31), imply
\[
\frac{k}{1-L}+t\leq2c^2,
\tag{32}
\]
and, on putting
\[
P=2L^2+\delta-\frac{1-L}{k},
\qquad
Q_+=2a^2+\frac{t}{1-\delta(c+k)},
\tag{33}
\]
\[
P>0,\qquad Q_+>0,\qquad PQ_+\geq1.
\tag{34}
\]
Let
\[
w=\frac{1-L}{k}>0.
\tag{35}
\]
Since \(a=c+k+t\), equations (17), (23), and (25) give
\[
1>
c+\frac9{10}(c+k)+\frac3{10}k
>\frac{19}{10}c.
\tag{36}
\]
Hence \(c<10/19\).  Equation (32) now gives
\[
w\geq\frac1{2c^2}>
\frac{361}{200}>
\frac74+\delta.
\tag{37}
\]
Consequently
\[
P=2L^2+\delta-w<\frac14.
\tag{38}
\]
On the other hand, (23)--(24) and \(0\leq t<a\) give
\[
Q_+
<2\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^2
  +\frac{11/10}{9/10}
<4.
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (38)--(39) contradict (34).

Finally suppose \(t<0\).  Since \(D\leq\delta\), equation (27) implies
\[
\frac{k}{1-L}+\frac{t}{1-\delta a}\leq2c^2.
\tag{40}
\]
Put
\[
P=2L^2+\delta-\frac{1-L}{k},
\qquad
Q=2a^2+t.
\tag{41}
\]
Because \(t<0\), equations (29)--(31) imply
\[
P>0,\qquad Q>0,\qquad PQ\geq1.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus (17), (24)--(26), (40)--(42) are exactly hypotheses (1)--(6)
of accepted fact `298f2b7fba57e5ae`.  That fact says this system has
no solution, giving the final contradiction.

Both signs of \(t\) are impossible under (18), proving (1).  The argument
is invariant under the initial relabeling, so it excludes all three
exactly-one-pair strata.
