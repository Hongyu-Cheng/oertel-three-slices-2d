## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and areas \(a_0,a_1,a_2\). For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), set
\[
m_i(q)=\min_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,\qquad
M_i(q)=\max_{x\in A_i}q\cdot x,
\]
\[
F_{i,q}(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{x:q\cdot x\le c\}\right|.
\]
Let \(Q_{i,q}:[0,a_i]\to\mathbb R\) be the endpoint-normalized cap quantile
\[
Q_{i,q}(0)=m_i(q),\qquad
Q_{i,q}(u)=\inf\{c:F_{i,q}(c)\ge u\}\quad(0<u\le a_i),
\]
so \(Q_{i,q}(a_i)=M_i(q)\). Put \(\mu=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\), and, as in accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878`, define
\[
I_q(s)=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\bigl(F_{0,q}(c)+F_{2,q}(s-c)\bigr),
\qquad
R_q(b)=\inf\{s:I_q(s)\ge b\}\quad(0<b\le\mu).
\]
Retain the actual zero-demand and over-capacity conventions
\[
D_{1,q}(a)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&a=0,\\
Q_{1,q}(a),&0<a\le a_1,\\
+\infty,&a>a_1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
E_q(b)=
\begin{cases}
-\infty,&b=0,\\
R_q(b),&0<b\le\mu,\\
+\infty,&b>\mu.
\end{cases}
\]
Then:

1. The map \((q,u)\mapsto Q_{i,q}(u)\) is continuous on \(\mathbb S^1\times[0,a_i]\) for each \(i\).

2. The finite endpoint extension
\[
\overline E_q(b)=
\begin{cases}
m_0(q)+m_2(q),&b=0,\\
E_q(b),&0<b\le\mu
\end{cases}
\]
is jointly continuous on \(\mathbb S^1\times[0,\mu]\) and is strictly increasing in \(b\). In particular, \(E_q\) is strictly increasing on positive demands and
\[
\lim_{b\downarrow0}E_q(b)=m_0(q)+m_2(q),
\]
but this limit is not the actual zero-demand value \(E_q(0)=-\infty\).

3. Fix \(0<t<a_1+\mu\), and define
\[
\rho_q(t)=\min_{0\le a\le t}
\max\left\{D_{1,q}(a),\frac12E_q(t-a)\right\}.
\]
This minimum has a unique optimizer \(a_*(q)\), and both maps
\[
q\longmapsto a_*(q),\qquad q\longmapsto\rho_q(t)
\]
are continuous on \(\mathbb S^1\).

More explicitly, let
\[
L=\max\{0,t-\mu\},\qquad U=\min\{t,a_1\},
\]
\[
A_q(a)=Q_{1,q}(a),\qquad
B_q(a)=\frac12\overline E_q(t-a),\qquad
\delta_q(a)=A_q(a)-B_q(a)
\quad(L\le a\le U).
\]
Then \(L<U\), \(\delta_q\) is continuous and strictly increasing, and
\[
a_*(q)=
\begin{cases}
L,&\delta_q(L)\ge0,\\
U,&\delta_q(U)\le0,\\
\text{the unique \(a\in(L,U)\) with \(\delta_q(a)=0\)},&
\delta_q(L)<0<\delta_q(U).
\end{cases}
\]
The first two cases cannot occur simultaneously. The attained value, with the actual \(-\infty\) conventions rather than the finite extensions, is
\[
\rho_q(t)=
\begin{cases}
B_q(L),&\delta_q(L)\ge0\ \text{and }L=0,\\
A_q(L),&\delta_q(L)\ge0\ \text{and }L>0,\\
A_q(U),&\delta_q(U)\le0\ \text{and }U=t,\\
B_q(U),&\delta_q(U)\le0\ \text{and }U<t,\\
A_q(a_*(q))=B_q(a_*(q)),&
\delta_q(L)<0<\delta_q(U).
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the cases \(a=0\), \(t-a=0\), \(a=a_1\), and \(t-a=\mu\), including coincidences such as \(t=a_1\) or \(t=\mu\), are all covered.

## Proof

Accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` proves, for each fixed \(q\), that \(F_{i,q}\) is continuous and strictly increasing from \(0\) to \(a_i\) on \([m_i(q),M_i(q)]\), that \(Q_{i,q}\) is its continuous strictly increasing endpoint-normalized inverse, and that
\[
R_q(b)=\max_{0\le u\le b}
\bigl(Q_{0,q}(u)+Q_{2,q}(b-u)\bigr)
\quad(0<b\le\mu).
\tag{1}
\]
We first make the dependence on \(q\) explicit.

Choose \(K_i<\infty\) with \(\|x\|\le K_i\) for all \(x\in A_i\). Then
\[
|m_i(q)-m_i(q')|\le K_i\|q-q'\|,
\qquad
|M_i(q)-M_i(q')|\le K_i\|q-q'\|,
\tag{2}
\]
so both support endpoints are continuous in \(q\).

The joint cap-continuity observation in accepted fact `9e512193da02119e` applies here without the polytope or compatibility hypotheses. For completeness, if \((q_n,c_n)\to(q,c)\), then
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{q_n\cdot x\le c_n\}}
\longrightarrow
\mathbf 1_{\{q\cdot x\le c\}}
\]
for every \(x\) outside the line \(\{x:q\cdot x=c\}\). That line has planar area zero. Dominated convergence on \(A_i\) gives
\[
F_{i,q_n}(c_n)\longrightarrow F_{i,q}(c).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \((q,c)\mapsto F_{i,q}(c)\) is jointly continuous.

Now let \((q_n,u_n)\to(q,u)\) in
\(\mathbb S^1\times[0,a_i]\), and put
\(c_n=Q_{i,q_n}(u_n)\). Equations (2) and
\[
m_i(q_n)\le c_n\le M_i(q_n)
\]
make \((c_n)\) bounded. Along any convergent subsequence, say
\(c_n\to c\), endpoint normalization and strict invertibility give
\[
F_{i,q_n}(c_n)=u_n.
\]
By (3), \(F_{i,q}(c)=u\). If \(0<u<a_i\), strict increase gives
\(c=Q_{i,q}(u)\). If \(u=0\), (2) also gives
\(c\ge m_i(q)\); since \(F_{i,q}(c)=0\), strict increase on the support interval forces \(c=m_i(q)=Q_{i,q}(0)\). If \(u=a_i\), similarly \(c\le M_i(q)\) and \(F_{i,q}(c)=a_i\) force \(c=M_i(q)=Q_{i,q}(a_i)\). Every convergent subsequence has the same limit, proving joint continuity of \(Q_{i,q}\), including both quantile endpoints.

Extend (1) to \(b=0\). After writing \(u=bv\), it becomes
\[
\overline E_q(b)
=
\max_{0\le v\le1}
\left(
Q_{0,q}(bv)+Q_{2,q}(b(1-v))
\right)
\quad(0\le b\le\mu).
\tag{4}
\]
At \(b=0\), the right side is
\(m_0(q)+m_2(q)\), exactly the declared finite extension. The maximand in (4) is jointly continuous in \((q,b,v)\) on a compact set. Taking convergent maximizing subsequences proves that \((q,b)\mapsto\overline E_q(b)\) is jointly continuous.

It is also strictly increasing in \(b\). Indeed, let
\(0\le b'<b\le\mu\), and choose \(u'\in[0,b']\) attaining (1), with the evident interpretation \(u'=0\) when \(b'=0\). The same allocation \(u'\) is feasible at demand \(b\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline E_q(b)
&\ge Q_{0,q}(u')+Q_{2,q}(b-u')\\
&>Q_{0,q}(u')+Q_{2,q}(b'-u')
=\overline E_q(b').
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The strict inequality uses the strict increase of \(Q_{2,q}\); both of its arguments lie in \([0,a_2]\) because \(b\le\mu\le a_2\). This proves all claims about \(\overline E_q\) and the positive-demand part of \(E_q\). Notice that (4) does not redefine the optimization convention: throughout the original minimization, \(E_q(0)\) remains \(-\infty\).

Fix \(t\) as in the statement. The objective is \(+\infty\) whenever \(a>a_1\) or \(t-a>\mu\), so every optimizer lies in
\[
J=[L,U]=[\max\{0,t-\mu\},\min\{t,a_1\}].
\tag{6}
\]
This interval is nondegenerate. Indeed, \(L<t\) because \(\mu>0\), and \(L<a_1\) because \(a_1>0\) and \(t-\mu<a_1\); hence \(L<\min\{t,a_1\}=U\).

On \(J\), \(A_q\) is jointly continuous and strictly increasing in \(a\), while \(B_q\) is jointly continuous and strictly decreasing in \(a\), by (4) and (5). Hence \(\delta_q=A_q-B_q\) is continuous and strictly increasing. The continuous proxy
\[
\overline H_q(a)=\max\{A_q(a),B_q(a)\}
\tag{7}
\]
therefore has exactly one minimizer. If \(\delta_q(L)\ge0\), then for every \(a>L\),
\[
\overline H_q(a)\ge A_q(a)>A_q(L)=\overline H_q(L).
\]
If \(\delta_q(U)\le0\), then for every \(a<U\),
\[
\overline H_q(a)\ge B_q(a)>B_q(U)=\overline H_q(U).
\]
Otherwise \(\delta_q(L)<0<\delta_q(U)\), so there is a unique \(c\in(L,U)\) with \(A_q(c)=B_q(c)\); for \(a<c\), \(B_q(a)>B_q(c)\), and for \(a>c\), \(A_q(a)>A_q(c)\). This proves the displayed classification for the proxy minimizer.

We next check that the original objective
\[
H_q(a)=
\max\left\{D_{1,q}(a),\frac12E_q(t-a)\right\}
\tag{8}
\]
has the same unique minimizer, even though it is not the proxy at a zero-demand endpoint. On \(L<a<U\), both demands are positive and \(H_q=\overline H_q\). At \(L\), the two functions differ only when \(L=0\), in which case
\[
H_q(L)=B_q(L)
\tag{9}
\]
because \(D_{1,q}(0)=-\infty\). At \(U\), they differ only when \(U=t\), in which case
\[
H_q(U)=A_q(U)
\tag{10}
\]
because \(E_q(0)=-\infty\).

If \(\delta_q(L)\ge0\), every \(a\in(L,U)\) has
\[
H_q(a)\ge A_q(a)>A_q(L)\ge H_q(L),
\]
where the last inequality is equality when \(L>0\) and follows from
\(A_q(L)\ge B_q(L)=H_q(L)\) when \(L=0\). If \(U=t\), (10) also gives
\(H_q(U)=A_q(U)>A_q(L)\ge H_q(L)\); if \(U<t\), the same conclusion follows from \(H_q(U)=\overline H_q(U)>\overline H_q(L)\ge H_q(L)\). Thus \(L\) is the unique optimizer.

If \(\delta_q(U)\le0\), every \(a\in(L,U)\) has
\[
H_q(a)\ge B_q(a)>B_q(U)\ge H_q(U),
\]
where the last inequality is equality when \(U<t\) and follows from
\(B_q(U)\ge A_q(U)=H_q(U)\) when \(U=t\). If \(L=0\), (9) gives
\(H_q(L)=B_q(L)>B_q(U)\ge H_q(U)\); the case \(L>0\) follows in the same way from the proxy. Thus \(U\) is the unique optimizer.

Finally, in the crossing case, for \(a<c\) the positive outer-demand branch gives \(H_q(a)\ge B_q(a)>B_q(c)\), including \(a=L=0\) by (9); for \(a>c\), the middle-demand branch gives \(H_q(a)\ge A_q(a)>A_q(c)\), including \(a=U=t\) by (10). Hence \(c\) is the unique optimizer. This proves both uniqueness and the formula for the attained value. It also explicitly covers the other two feasible endpoints: if \(L>0\), then \(L=t-\mu\) and the outer demand is the active capacity \(\mu\); if \(U<t\), then \(U=a_1\) and the middle demand is the active capacity \(a_1\). Equalities \(t=\mu\) and \(t=a_1\) fall under (9) and (10), respectively, with the other demand possibly capacity-active.

It remains to prove continuity. The proxy (7) is jointly continuous on the fixed compact set \(\mathbb S^1\times J\), and its unique minimizer is the same \(a_*(q)\) just proved for the original objective. If \(q_n\to q\), compactness gives a convergent subsequence \(a_*(q_{n_k})\to\bar a\). The minimizing inequalities for (7) pass to the limit and show that \(\bar a\) minimizes \(\overline H_q\). Uniqueness gives \(\bar a=a_*(q)\). Every convergent subsequence has this limit, so
\[
a_*(q_n)\longrightarrow a_*(q).
\tag{11}
\]

For the value, take any sequence \(q_n\to q\) and pass to a subsequence lying in one of the three allocation regimes. In the left-endpoint regime, the displayed value formula is the continuous function \(B_{q_n}(L)\) when \(L=0\) and \(A_{q_n}(L)\) when \(L>0\). Its limit is the corresponding value at \(q\); if the limiting endpoint satisfies \(\delta_q(L)=0\), then \(A_q(L)=B_q(L)\), so this is also the value from the adjacent crossing regime. The right-endpoint regime is identical, using \(A_{q_n}(U)\) when \(U=t\) and \(B_{q_n}(U)\) when \(U<t\).

In the crossing regime,
\[
\rho_{q_n}(t)
=A_{q_n}(a_*(q_n))
=B_{q_n}(a_*(q_n)).
\]
By (11) and joint continuity, this tends to the common branch value at \(a_*(q)\). If the limiting optimizer is \(L\) or \(U\), continuity of \(\delta\) forces equality of the two branches at that endpoint, so the limit again equals the actual endpoint value in (9) or (10). Every subsequence therefore has the same limit \(\rho_q(t)\), proving continuity of \(q\mapsto\rho_q(t)\).
