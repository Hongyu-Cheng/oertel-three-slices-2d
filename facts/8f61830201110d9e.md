## Statement

Use the exact objects of accepted fact 9eb5170d42a00426. Explicitly, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{d_0,d_1,d_2\},\qquad
d_0=(0,0),\quad d_1=(1,0),\quad d_2=(0,1),\qquad
g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
and let \(Q\) have cyclic vertices
\[
q_0=\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
q_1=\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
q_2=\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
q_3=\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
For \(\alpha>0\), put
\[
M_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\ \alpha&1+\alpha^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B_\alpha=g+M_\alpha(\Delta-g).
\]
For a translation \(v=(a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\), define
\[
s_1=(1+\alpha^2)a-\alpha b,\qquad s_2=-\alpha a+b,
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_X(\alpha)&=\frac{3-23\alpha-37\alpha^2}{120},\\
m_{Y,0}(\alpha)&=\frac{3-23\alpha}{120},\\
m_{Y,1}(\alpha)&=\frac{63-1060\alpha}{2400},\\
m_Z(\alpha)&=\frac{477+323\alpha-1060\alpha^2}{2400}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then the full compatibility set is exactly the semialgebraic set
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C
=\{(\alpha,a,b):\;&\alpha>0,\quad
s_1\leq m_X(\alpha),\quad
s_2\leq m_{Y,0}(\alpha),\\
&s_2\leq m_{Y,1}(\alpha),\quad
s_1+s_2\geq-m_Z(\alpha)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}\subseteq B_\alpha+v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\alpha,a,b)\in\mathcal C.
\tag{2}
\]
Its exact projection onto the shear parameter is
\[
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_*:=\frac{\sqrt{639201}-399}{1200}.
\tag{3}
\]
More explicitly, set
\[
m_Y(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
m_{Y,0}(\alpha),&0<\alpha\leq1/200,\\
m_{Y,1}(\alpha),&\alpha\geq1/200.
\end{cases}
\]
For each \(0<\alpha\leq\alpha_*\), the compatible translation set is the image under \(M_\alpha\) of the possibly degenerate triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(m_X,m_Y),\ (m_X,-m_Z-m_X),\ (-m_Z-m_Y,m_Y)
\right\},
\tag{4}
\]
where all four \(m\)'s in (4) are evaluated at \(\alpha\). At \(\alpha=\alpha_*\), this triangle is a singleton.

For every \((\alpha,a,b)\in\mathcal C\), set
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=B_\alpha+v,\qquad A_2=Q,
\qquad
S_{\alpha,v}=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\), put
\[
\mu_{\alpha,v}(H)=\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|\{z\in A_i:(i,z)\in H\}\right|,
\]
and put
\[
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{16027}{16000},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000},
\]
\[
h_{S_{\alpha,v}}(y)=\inf\{\mu_{\alpha,v}(H):
H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_{S_{\alpha,v}}((1,g+v))\geq\frac{49}{220}
=\frac{16027}{72000}+\frac{103}{792000}
>\frac{16027}{72000}=t.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, for every compatible pair, no collection of closed halfspaces of mass strictly below \(t\), even a collection of arbitrary cardinality, covers the middle slice by its interiors.

## Proof

The inverse shear is
\[
M_\alpha^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\alpha^2&-\alpha\\-\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus the barycentric coordinates of \(z=(x,y)\) relative to \(B_\alpha\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
X(z)&=(1+\alpha^2)x-\alpha y+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^2}{3},\\
Y(z)&=-\alpha x+y+\frac\alpha3,\\
Z(z)&=1-X(z)-Y(z),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
and \(B_\alpha=\{X\geq0,Y\geq0,Z\geq0\}\). Translation gives
\[
X(z-v)=X(z)-s_1,\qquad
Y(z-v)=Y(z)-s_2,\qquad
Z(z-v)=Z(z)+s_1+s_2.
\tag{7}
\]

We first compute all three minima on \((\Delta+Q)/2\). At the vertices of \(\Delta\), direct substitution in (6) gives
\[
\min_\Delta X=-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}3,\qquad
\min_\Delta Y=-\frac{2\alpha}3,\qquad
\min_\Delta Z=\frac{\alpha(1-2\alpha)}3.
\tag{8}
\]
These minima occur at \(d_2,d_1,d_1\), respectively, for every \(\alpha>0\). For \(X\), the two other vertex values exceed the displayed minimum by \(\alpha\) and \(1+\alpha+\alpha^2\); for \(Y\), they exceed it by \(\alpha\) and \(1+\alpha\); and for \(Z\), they exceed it by \(\alpha^2-\alpha+1\) and \(\alpha^2\). On \(Q\), the minimum of \(X\) is always attained at \(q_0\), and the minimum of \(Z\) is always attained at \(q_1\). Indeed, the three values of \(X(q_j)-X(q_0)\), \(j=1,2,3\), are
\[
\frac{200\alpha^2-\alpha+200}{400},\qquad
\frac{3(60\alpha^2-\alpha+60)}{400},\qquad
\frac{20\alpha^2-\alpha+20}{400},
\]
which are positive, while
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(q_0)-Z(q_1)&=\frac{200\alpha^2-201\alpha+201}{400},\\
Z(q_2)-Z(q_1)&=\frac{10\alpha^2-9\alpha+9}{200},\\
Z(q_3)-Z(q_1)&=\frac{9(\alpha^2-\alpha+1)}{20},
\end{aligned}
\]
which are also positive. Hence
\[
\min_QX=\frac{3+17\alpha-17\alpha^2}{60},\qquad
\min_QZ=\frac{477-77\alpha-260\alpha^2}{1200}.
\tag{9}
\]
The only competition for the minimum of \(Y\) is between \(q_0\) and \(q_1\):
\[
Y(q_0)=\frac{3+17\alpha}{60},\qquad
Y(q_1)=\frac{63-260\alpha}{1200},
\]
and \(Y(q_1)-Y(q_0)=(1-200\alpha)/400\). For \(0<\alpha\leq1/200\), the differences \(Y(q_2)-Y(q_0)\) and \(Y(q_3)-Y(q_0)\) are
\[
\frac{3(1-60\alpha)}{400},\qquad
\frac{1-20\alpha}{400},
\]
and are positive. For \(\alpha\geq1/200\), the differences \(Y(q_2)-Y(q_1)\) and \(Y(q_3)-Y(q_1)\) are
\[
\frac1{200}+\frac\alpha{20},\qquad \frac{9\alpha}{20},
\]
and are positive. Therefore
\[
\min_QY=
\begin{cases}
(3+17\alpha)/60,&0<\alpha\leq1/200,\\
(63-260\alpha)/1200,&\alpha\geq1/200.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]

The minimum of an affine functional on \((\Delta+Q)/2\) is the average of its separate minima on \(\Delta\) and \(Q\). Equations (8) through (10) show that
\[
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}X=m_X,\qquad
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}Z=m_Z,\qquad
\min_{(\Delta+Q)/2}Y=\min\{m_{Y,0},m_{Y,1}\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Now (7) proves (1) and (2), including necessity: each of the displayed inequalities is exactly one translated facet inequality minimized over the whole Minkowski average.

For completeness, the section of (1) at fixed \(\alpha\), in \((s_1,s_2)\)-coordinates, is
\[
s_1\leq m_X,\qquad s_2\leq m_Y,\qquad s_1+s_2\geq-m_Z.
\tag{12}
\]
It is nonempty exactly when \(m_X+m_Y+m_Z\geq0\). For \(0<\alpha\leq1/200\), this sum is
\[
\frac{199-199\alpha-600\alpha^2}{800}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(\alpha\geq1/200\), it is
\[
\frac{200-399\alpha-600\alpha^2}{800}.
\tag{14}
\]
The numerator in (14) is strictly decreasing for positive \(\alpha\), and its unique positive root is \(\alpha_*\) in (3). This proves the projection (3). Intersecting the three boundary lines in (12) gives the three points in (4), and \((s_1,s_2)^\mathsf T=M_\alpha^{-1}v\), so multiplication by \(M_\alpha\) proves the full cross-section formula.

We next prove uniform middle protection. Compatibility has the following useful equivalent form. Define
\[
\mathbf r_\alpha=
\left(\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}3,\frac{2\alpha}3,
\frac{\alpha(2\alpha-1)}3\right),
\qquad
\kappa_\alpha=1-\alpha-\alpha^2.
\tag{15}
\]
For any affine barycentric coordinate \(F\in\{X,Y,Z\}\), any \(d\in\Delta\), and any \(q\in Q\),
\[
2F\left(\frac{d+q}{2}-v\right)=F(d)+F(q-2v).
\tag{16}
\]
Together with (8), this shows
\[
\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}\subseteq B_\alpha+v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q-2v\subseteq R_\alpha,
\tag{17}
\]
where
\[
R_\alpha=\{z:X(z)\geq r_{\alpha,0},\ Y(z)\geq r_{\alpha,1},\ Z(z)\geq r_{\alpha,2}\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The polynomial defining \(\alpha_*\) satisfies
\[
600\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2
+399\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)-200=\frac{481}{160}>0.
\]
Consequently every compatible shear satisfies
\[
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_*<\frac{27}{80}<1,
\tag{19}
\]
so \(\kappa_\alpha>0\). Since the entries in (15) sum to \(\alpha+\alpha^2\), (18) can be written in barycentric coordinates as
\[
R_\alpha=\{\mathbf r_\alpha+\kappa_\alpha\boldsymbol\mu:
\mu_i\geq0,\ \mu_0+\mu_1+\mu_2=1\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Accepted fact 9eb5170d42a00426 gives \(|Q|=27/16000\). Also \(\det M_\alpha=1\), so
\[
|A_0|=|A_1|=\frac12,\qquad
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{16027}{16000},\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000}.
\tag{21}
\]

Let \(H\) be a closed halfspace containing \((1,g+v)\). Unless \(H\) is the whole space, moving its boundary parallel toward that point produces a contained halfspace whose boundary passes through the point. It therefore suffices to bound proper boundary-through-point halfspaces. If the planar normal is nonzero, write
\[
H=\{(h,z):L(z-g-v)+\beta(h-1)\geq0\},
\tag{22}
\]
where \(L\) is a nonzero linear functional on \(\mathbb R^2\). The height-one contribution is
\[
|B_\alpha\cap\{z:L(z-g)\geq0\}|.
\tag{23}
\]

Let \(b_i=g+M_\alpha(d_i-g)\) be the vertices of \(B_\alpha\). The three values \(L(b_i-g)\) sum to zero. After positive rescaling, order them as
\[
-1,\qquad 1-\rho,\qquad \rho,
\qquad \frac12\leq\rho\leq2.
\tag{24}
\]
Similar triangles give the exact area in (23): for \(1/2\leq\rho\leq1\), it is
\[
\frac12\left(1-\frac1{(2-\rho)(1+\rho)}\right)\geq\frac14,
\tag{25}
\]
and for \(1\leq\rho\leq2\), it is
\[
f(\rho)=\frac{\rho^2}{2(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}.
\tag{26}
\]
Here \(f\) is decreasing and
\[
f(\rho)-\frac29=
\frac{(\rho-2)^2}{18(1+\rho)(2\rho-1)}\geq0.
\tag{27}
\]
In particular, if \(\rho\leq7/4\), the middle slice alone contributes at least
\[
f(7/4)=\frac{49}{220}.
\tag{28}
\]

If the height-two section of (22) contains all of \(Q\), equations (21) and (27) give total mass at least
\[
\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}>\frac{49}{220},
\qquad
\frac29+\frac{27}{16000}-\frac{49}{220}
=\frac{1873}{1584000}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
It remains only to consider \(7/4<\rho\leq2\) when the height-two section does not contain \(Q\).

Choose \(q\in Q\) outside that closed section. Then
\[
L(q-g-v)+\beta<0.
\]
At height zero, (22) cuts \(\Delta\) by
\[
L(z-g)\geq L(v)+\beta,
\]
and the strict preceding inequality gives
\[
L(v)+\beta<2L(v)-L(q-g)=-L(q-2v-g).
\tag{30}
\]
By (17), \(q-2v\in R_\alpha\). If
\[
A_R=-\min_{z\in R_\alpha}L(z-g),
\tag{31}
\]
then (30) shows that the height-zero contribution contains
\[
\Delta\cap\{z:L(z-g)\geq A_R\}.
\tag{32}
\]

Index the normalized values in (24) by barycentric coordinate and write
\[
\lambda_h=\rho,\qquad \lambda_m=-1,\qquad
\lambda_o=1-\rho,
\]
where \((h,m,o)\) is a permutation of \((0,1,2)\). Since
\(L(z-g)=\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot(X(z),Y(z),Z(z))\), equation (20) gives
\[
A_R=\kappa_\alpha-\mathbf r_\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\lambda.
\tag{33}
\]
Put \(\eta_j=L(d_j-g)-A_R\). Substitution of (6), (15), and (33) gives the complete table
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(h,m,o)&\eta_0&\eta_1&\eta_2\\ \hline
(0,1,2)&(2-\rho)\alpha^2+(2\rho-1)\alpha-\rho
&(\rho+1)(\alpha^2+\alpha)+\rho-1
&(2-\rho)\alpha^2-2\\[2pt]
(0,2,1)&3\alpha-2
&(\rho+1)(\alpha^2+\alpha)+\rho-1
&(2-\rho)\alpha-\rho\\[2pt]
(1,0,2)&(2-\rho)\alpha^2+(2\rho-1)\alpha-\rho
&-2
&(2-\rho)\alpha^2+(\rho+1)\alpha+\rho-1\\[2pt]
(1,2,0)&3\alpha-2
&(2-\rho)(\alpha^2+\alpha)-\rho
&(\rho+1)\alpha+\rho-1\\[2pt]
(2,0,1)&(\rho+1)\alpha^2+(1-2\rho)\alpha+\rho-1
&-2
&(\rho+1)\alpha^2+(2-\rho)\alpha-\rho\\[2pt]
(2,1,0)&(\rho+1)\alpha^2+(1-2\rho)\alpha+\rho-1
&(2-\rho)(\alpha^2+\alpha)-\rho
&(\rho+1)\alpha^2-2
\end{array}
\tag{34}
\]
For the first two rows, \(\eta_1>\rho-1>3/4\), and for the next two rows, \(\eta_2>\rho-1>3/4\). For the last two rows, \(\eta_0\) is
\[
P(\alpha,\rho)=(\rho+1)\alpha^2+(1-2\rho)\alpha+\rho-1.
\]
Since \(\partial P/\partial\rho=(1-\alpha)^2>0\), equation (19) gives
\[
P(\alpha,\rho)>
\frac{11\alpha^2-10\alpha+3}{4}
>\frac{5619}{25600}>\frac15.
\tag{35}
\]
Here the middle quadratic is decreasing for \(0<\alpha<27/80\), and \(5619/25600\) is its value at \(27/80\).

Every other entry in (34) is negative. This follows directly from
\(0\leq2-\rho<1/4\), \(0<\alpha<27/80\), and \(7/4<\rho\leq2\). Indeed, all nonconstant negative entries in (34) are among
\[
\begin{gathered}
(2-\rho)\alpha^2+(2\rho-1)\alpha-\rho,\quad
3\alpha-2,\quad (2-\rho)\alpha-\rho,\quad
(2-\rho)\alpha^2-2,\\
(2-\rho)(\alpha^2+\alpha)-\rho,\quad
(\rho+1)\alpha^2+(2-\rho)\alpha-\rho,\quad
(\rho+1)\alpha^2-2,
\end{gathered}
\]
and their respective upper bounds are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac14\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2
+3\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)-\frac74
=-\frac{18151}{25600},
&&3\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)-2=-\frac{79}{80},\\
&\frac14\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)-\frac74
=-\frac{533}{320},
&&\frac14\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2-2
=-\frac{50471}{25600},\\
&\frac14\left(\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2+\frac{27}{80}\right)-\frac74
=-\frac{41911}{25600},
&&3\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2
+\frac14\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)-\frac74
=-\frac{8473}{6400},\\
&3\left(\frac{27}{80}\right)^2-2
=-\frac{10613}{6400}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The constant entry \(-2\) is negative as well. Thus (32) is a one-vertex corner triangle, and its positive vertex excess is greater than \(1/5\).

It remains to bound the two edge drops from that vertex. Let
\(F=(X,Y,Z)\). Directly from (6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(d_1)-F(d_0)&=(1+\alpha^2,-\alpha,-1+\alpha-\alpha^2),\\
F(d_2)-F(d_0)&=(-\alpha,1,\alpha-1),\\
F(d_1)-F(d_2)&=(1+\alpha+\alpha^2,-1-\alpha,-\alpha^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
Each vector sums to zero, and the sums of its positive coordinates are, respectively,
\(1+\alpha^2\), \(1\), and \(1+\alpha+\alpha^2\). The normalized values \(\lambda_i\) lie in \([-1,2]\), so every pairwise difference of \(L\) on the vertices of \(\Delta\) is less than
\[
3(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)
<3\left(1+\frac{27}{80}+\frac{729}{6400}\right)
=\frac{27867}{6400}<\frac92.
\tag{38}
\]
If \(d_k\) is the unique positive vertex in (34) and \(d_i,d_j\) are the other two vertices, similar triangles, (35), and (38) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\Delta\cap\{z:L(z-g)\geq A_R\}\right|
&=\frac12\frac{\eta_k^2}
{(L(d_k-g)-L(d_i-g))(L(d_k-g)-L(d_j-g))}\\
&>\frac12\frac{(1/5)^2}{(9/2)^2}
=\frac2{2025}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
Combining (27), (32), and (39), the height-zero and height-one contributions in the remaining case have total mass greater than
\[
\frac29+\frac2{2025}>\frac{49}{220},
\qquad
\frac29+\frac2{2025}-\frac{49}{220}=\frac{43}{89100}>0.
\tag{40}
\]

If the planar normal of a proper boundary-through-point halfspace is zero, it contains either the height-zero and height-one slices, of mass \(1\), or the height-one and height-two slices, of mass \(1/2+27/16000\). Both exceed \(49/220\); the whole space has mass \(T\). Equations (28), (29), and (40) therefore show that every closed halfspace containing \((1,g+v)\) has mass at least \(49/220\). Taking the infimum proves (5), since
\[
\frac{49}{220}-\frac{16027}{72000}=\frac{103}{792000}>0.
\]
Finally, \(g+v\) lies in the interior of \(B_\alpha+v\). Any interior cover of the middle slice must place this point in one of its halfspaces, whose mass is then at least its depth and hence strictly larger than \(t\). This proves the uniform middle protection claim.
