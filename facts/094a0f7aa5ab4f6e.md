## Statement

Assume the complete common-plane hypotheses of accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1`, and use the \(A_2\) coordinates and exact-cell conventions of accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b`:
\[
 S=102,\qquad U=-q_k,\qquad V=q_\ell,\qquad -q_j=S-U+V.
\]
Thus the only positive-area exact \(A_2\)-cells are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
Q_j&U<0&V>0&-q_j>0\\
Q_{jk}&U>0&V>0&-q_j>0\\
Q_N&U>0&V<0&-q_j>0\\
Q_{k\ell}&U>0&V<0&-q_j<0 .
\end{array}
\]
At the rational point of accepted fact `cc1a8f11f870255e`, coordinate area equals physical area because \(\Delta=1\), and
\[
 |Q_j|=\frac15,\qquad |Q_{jk}|=\frac1{10}.
\]
The three full closed sections are
\[
 A_2\cap\{U=0\}
 =\{0\}\times\left[\frac3{10},\frac12\right],
\]
\[
 A_2\cap\{V=0\}
 =\left[\frac3{10},\frac{11}{20}\right]\times\{0\},
\]
\[
 A_2\cap\{-q_j=0\}
 =\{(102-t,-t):50\le t\le251/5\}.
\]
No compact two-dimensional convex polygon \(A_2\) has these exact cells, areas, and full sections.  More precisely, every such convex polygon would satisfy
\[
 |Q_j|\le\frac{121}{850}<\frac15.
\]
Consequently the displayed rational point has no full realization by convex polygons \(A_0,A_2,K\) with \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\), irrespective of the remaining \(P\)- and \(R\)-cell data.

## Proof

Accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1` requires \(A_2\) to be a compact two-dimensional convex polygon with exactly the positive-area cells displayed above.  Accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b` gives the stated \(U,V\) conventions and full-section parametrizations, and accepted fact `cc1a8f11f870255e` gives all of the stated rational values.  Suppose for contradiction that this \(A_2\) exists.

Let \(I\) be the projection of \(A_2\) onto the \(U\)-axis.  For \(x\in I\), define
\[
 h(x)=\max\{V:(x,V)\in A_2\},\qquad
 \ell(x)=\min\{V:(x,V)\in A_2\}.
\]
Convexity of \(A_2\) makes \(h\) concave and \(\ell\) convex: convex combinations of points on the upper boundary give the concavity inequality for \(h\), and convex combinations of points on the lower boundary give the convexity inequality for \(\ell\).  Since \(A_2\) is a polygon, both functions are continuous and piecewise affine on the interior of \(I\).  The positive areas of \(Q_j\) and \(Q_{jk}\) put points of \(A_2\) on both sides of \(U=0\), so \(0\) lies in the interior of \(I\) and all four one-sided slopes used below are finite.

Put
\[
 r=u=\frac3{10},\qquad
 q=\frac12,\qquad
 L=\frac{11}{20}.
\]
The full \(U=0\) chord gives
\[
 \ell(0)=r,\qquad h(0)=q.
\]
We next identify the other two boundary values and exclude all extra positive-\(V\) area beyond \(U=L\).

The point
\[
 C=(52,-50)
\]
belongs to the full \(-q_j=0\) chord, by taking \(t=50\).  If \(A_2\) contained a point \((x,y)\) with \(x>L\) and \(y>0\), the segment from \((x,y)\) to \(C\) would meet \(V=0\) at a point whose \(U\)-coordinate is a strict convex combination of \(x\) and \(52\), hence is greater than \(L\).  This contradicts the assertion that the full \(V=0\) section is exactly \([u,L]\times\{0\}\).  Therefore
\[
 A_2\cap\{U>L,V>0\}=\varnothing.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, because \((L,0)\in A_2\), one has
\[
 h(L)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, a point \((L,y)\) with \(y>0\), joined to \(C\), would already produce the same forbidden \(V=0\) point with \(U>L\).

Similarly, if \(0\le x<u\) and \((x,y)\in A_2\) had \(y<0\), its segment to \((0,r)\in A_2\) would meet \(V=0\) at a \(U\)-coordinate strictly between \(0\) and \(u\), again contradicting the full horizontal section.  The same argument at \(x=u\) rules out a point below \((u,0)\).  Hence
\[
 \ell(x)\ge0\quad(0\le x\le u),\qquad
 \ell(u)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
The segments from \((0,q)\) to \((L,0)\), and from \((0,r)\) to \((u,0)\), show that \(h(x)>0\) for \(0\le x<L\) and \(\ell(x)>0\) for \(0\le x<u\).  For \(u\le x\le L\), the full horizontal chord supplies \((x,0)\in A_2\).  Moreover, if \(0\le x\le L\) and \(V>0\), then
\[
 -q_j=102-x+V>0.
\]
Together with (1)-(3), this proves the exact coordinate-area identity
\[
 |Q_{jk}|
 =\int_0^L h(x)\,dx-\int_0^u\ell(x)\,dx.
\tag{4}
\]
There is no omitted positive lobe, and the cutting-line boundary strata have area zero.

Define the two nonnegative excess areas
\[
 E_h=\int_0^L h(x)\,dx-\frac{qL}{2},
\qquad
 E_\ell=\frac{ru}{2}-\int_0^u\ell(x)\,dx.
\tag{5}
\]
They are nonnegative because a concave \(h\) lies above the chord from \((0,q)\) to \((L,0)\), while a convex \(\ell\) lies below the chord from \((0,r)\) to \((u,0)\).  From (4), (5), and \(|Q_{jk}|=1/10\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_h+E_\ell
 &=\frac1{10}
   -\left(\frac{qL}{2}-\frac{ru}{2}\right)\\
 &=\frac1{10}
   -\left(\frac{11}{80}-\frac9{200}\right)
 =\frac3{400}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Let
\[
 a=h'_+(0),\qquad b=\ell'_+(0).
\]
The right derivative of a concave function is a supergradient at \(0\), so
\[
 h(x)\le q+ax\qquad(0\le x\le L).
\]
Integrating this inequality and using (5) gives
\[
 a+\frac{q}{L}
 \ge\frac{2E_h}{L^2},
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
 a+\frac{10}{11}\ge\frac{800}{121}E_h.
\tag{7}
\]
The right derivative of a convex function is a subgradient at \(0\), so
\[
 \ell(x)\ge r+bx\qquad(0\le x\le u).
\]
After integration,
\[
 -\frac{r}{u}-b
 \ge\frac{2E_\ell}{u^2},
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
 -1-b\ge\frac{200}{9}E_\ell.
\tag{8}
\]
Since \(200/9>800/121\), equations (6)-(8) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a-b
 &=
 \left(a+\frac{10}{11}\right)
 +\frac1{11}
 +(-1-b)\\
 &\ge
 \frac1{11}
 +\frac{800}{121}(E_h+E_\ell)\\
 &=\frac{11}{121}+\frac6{121}
 =\frac{17}{121}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

For the negative-\(U\) cap, set
\[
 w(x)=h(x)-\ell(x).
\]
The function \(w\) is nonnegative and concave because \(h\) and \(-\ell\) are concave.  Let
\[
 d=w'_-(0)=h'_-(0)-\ell'_-(0).
\]
Monotonicity of one-sided slopes for concave and convex functions gives
\[
 h'_-(0)\ge h'_+(0)=a,\qquad
 \ell'_-(0)\le\ell'_+(0)=b.
\]
Consequently
\[
 d\ge a-b\ge\frac{17}{121}.
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(w(0)=q-r=1/5\).

There is no point of \(A_2\) with \(U<0,V<0\).  A point strictly inside such an unsupported sign chamber, even if initially on \(\partial A_2\), can be moved slightly toward an interior point of \(A_2\); the resulting point lies in \(\operatorname{int}A_2\) and retains all strict signs, producing a positive-area unsupported exact cell.  A possible point on \(-q_j=0\) is separately excluded by the displayed full \(-q_j=0\) chord, whose \(U\)-coordinates lie in \([259/5,52]\).  Thus every negative-\(U\) vertical section lies in \(V\ge0\).  Since then \(-q_j=102-U+V>0\), the whole negative-\(U\) portion is \(Q_j\), up to cutting-line boundaries.

Let \(x_-\) be the left endpoint of the \(U\)-projection of \(A_2\).  Concavity of \(w\) says that its left derivative \(d\) at \(0\) is a supergradient for points to the left:
\[
 w(x)\le w(0)+dx=\frac15+dx
\qquad(x_-\le x\le0).
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(w(x)\ge0\), (11) also forces \(x\ge-1/(5d)\) throughout the negative-\(U\) projection.  This remains valid if the leftmost boundary is a vertical segment; no vanishing-width endpoint was assumed.  Therefore, using \(\Delta=1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |Q_j|
 &=\int_{x_-}^0w(x)\,dx\\
 &\le
 \int_{-1/(5d)}^0\left(\frac15+dx\right)\,dx
 =\frac{1}{50d}\\
 &\le\frac{121}{850},
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
where the last inequality is (10).  But the rational survivor requires
\[
 |Q_j|=\frac15=\frac{170}{850},
\]
and \(121<170\).  Hence \(121/850<1/5\), contradicting (12).

Thus the prescribed \(A_2\) convex polygon does not exist.  Every complete common-plane realization in accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1` contains such an \(A_2\), so no choices of \(A_0\) or \(K\), and no remaining cell-area audit, can realize the rational point of accepted fact `cc1a8f11f870255e`.
