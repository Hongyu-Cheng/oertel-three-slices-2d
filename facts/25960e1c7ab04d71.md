## Statement

Use the normalized coordinates and cap conventions of accepted fact `4ec05d1da924da91`, the residual area domain of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, and the notation of the affine special case in accepted fact `b0a6a33598b0969e`.

Let
\[
\delta,w,r>0,\qquad D=\delta+2,
\]
and let
\[
P=\{(x,y):-\delta\le x\le0,\ 0\le y\le h_P(x)\},
\]
\[
Q=\{(x,y):D\le x\le D+w,\ -h_Q(x)\le y\le0\},
\]
\[
R=\{(x,y):0\le x\le r,\ -a(x)\le y\le c(x)\},
\]
where \(h_P,h_Q,a,c\) are finite-valued nonnegative concave functions on their respective compact intervals. No differentiability or endpoint continuity is assumed. Assume \(P,Q,R\) have positive area and
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R.
\]
Set
\[
M=|P|=\int_{-\delta}^{0}h_P(x)\,dx,\qquad
m=|Q|=\int_D^{D+w}h_Q(x)\,dx,
\]
\[
A=\int_0^r a(x)\,dx,\qquad
C=\int_0^r c(x)\,dx,\qquad
k=|R|=A+C,
\]
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,\qquad M\ge m.
\]
With
\[
\mu=\frac mM,\qquad \sigma=\frac kM,\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
assume
\[
0<\mu<\rho,\qquad L(\mu)<\sigma<U(\mu),
\]
where
\[
L(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1-\mu,&0<\mu\le9/25,\\
(3\mu+2\sqrt\mu-1)/2,&9/25<\mu<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1+\mu,&0<\mu\le1/2,\\
2-\mu,&1/2<\mu<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(E\in\{P,Q,R\}\), define
\[
\alpha_E=|E\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
\beta_E=|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|.
\]
Assume
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t,
\]
\[
\alpha_R+m\ge t,\qquad \beta_R+m\ge t.
\]
Define
\[
b=|Q\cap\{x\ge D\}|,\qquad
d=|R\cap\{x\ge1\}|,
\]
and assume
\[
b>0,\qquad k-d+m\ge t.
\]
Then the hypotheses are inconsistent. In particular,
\[
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `4ec05d1da924da91` supplies the normalized cap conventions, accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` supplies the strict residual strip, and accepted fact `b0a6a33598b0969e` proves the affine special case. We prove all section identities anew because the present height functions need not be affine or smooth.

A finite nonnegative concave function on a compact interval is bounded and Borel measurable. Indeed, it is continuous on the interval's interior, while its endpoint values affect measurability only at two points. If \(z\) is the midpoint of the interval, concavity and nonnegativity show that every value on either half of the interval is at most \(2f(z)\). Thus all four height functions are Lebesgue integrable, the displayed bodies are Borel sets of finite area, and Tonelli's theorem applies to their indicators.

The vertical section lengths immediately give
\[
|P|=\int_{-\delta}^{0}h_P(x)\,dx,\qquad
|Q|=\int_D^{D+w}h_Q(x)\,dx,
\]
and
\[
|R|=\int_0^r(a(x)+c(x))\,dx=A+C.
\]
For the cut \(y\le0\), a vertical section of \(P\) meets the cut only at \(y=0\), a set of one-dimensional measure zero; a section of \(Q\) lies wholly in the cut; and a section of \(R\) contributes length \(a(x)\). Hence Tonelli gives
\[
\alpha_P=0,\qquad \alpha_Q=m,\qquad \alpha_R=A.
\tag{1}
\]

Write \([u]_+=\max\{u,0\}\), and define
\[
B=\int_{-\delta}^{0}[h_P(x)+x]_+\,dx,
\]
\[
\Gamma=\int_D^{D+w}[h_Q(x)-x]_+\,dx,
\]
\[
G=\int_0^r[a(x)-x]_+\,dx.
\tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(x\in[-\delta,0]\), the vertical section of
\(P\cap\{x+y\ge0\}\) is the interval from \(-x\) to \(h_P(x)\) when
\(h_P(x)\ge-x\), and is empty otherwise. Its length is therefore
\([h_P(x)+x]_+\).

For fixed \(x\in[D,D+w]\), the section of \(Q\) has length \(h_Q(x)\), while the part failing \(x+y\ge0\) has length \([h_Q(x)-x]_+\). Thus the retained length is
\[
h_Q(x)-[h_Q(x)-x]_+=\min\{h_Q(x),x\}.
\]
For fixed \(x\in[0,r]\), the section of \(R\) has length \(a(x)+c(x)\), while the part failing \(x+y\ge0\) has length \([a(x)-x]_+\). Consequently, Tonelli gives the exact identities
\[
\beta_P=B,\qquad
\beta_Q=m-\Gamma,\qquad
\beta_R=A+C-G.
\tag{3}
\]
At a threshold equality such as \(h_Q(x)=x\) or \(a(x)=x\), the cutting line meets that vertical section at a single point, whose one-dimensional measure is zero. Hence (1) and (3) remain exact even if the set of threshold abscissae has positive measure. Zero-height endpoint sections also have zero area and require no separate convention. Pointwise,
\[
0\le\Gamma\le m,\qquad 0\le G\le A.
\tag{4}
\]

The first crossing equality and (1) give
\[
t=m+A.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining this with \(t=2(M+m+A+C)/9\) yields
\[
2(M+C)=7(m+A).
\tag{6}
\]
The second crossing equality, (3), and (5) give
\[
B+(m-\Gamma)+(A+C-G)=m+A,
\]
hence
\[
B+C=\Gamma+G.
\tag{7}
\]
The tail inequality \(\beta_R+m\ge t\), together with (3) and (5), gives
\[
A+C-G+m\ge m+A,
\]
so
\[
C\ge G.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (7) and (8) imply
\[
\Gamma\ge B.
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose first that \(\Gamma>0\). Since the integrand defining \(\Gamma\) is finite, nonnegative, and measurable, the set
\[
\{x\in[D,D+w]:h_Q(x)>x\}
\]
has positive measure. The two endpoints have measure zero, so choose
\[
\xi\in(D,D+w)\quad\text{with}\quad h_Q(\xi)>\xi.
\tag{10}
\]
The points \((-\delta,0)\) and \((0,0)\) belong to \(P\), regardless of whether either endpoint height vanishes, and
\[
(\xi,-h_Q(\xi))\in Q.
\]
Put
\[
x_1=\frac{\xi-\delta}{2},\qquad
x_2=\frac{\xi}{2},\qquad
H=\frac{h_Q(\xi)}2.
\]
The two corresponding midpoints belong to \(R\):
\[
(x_1,-H),\ (x_2,-H)\in R.
\]
Therefore
\[
0\le x_1<x_2\le r,\qquad
a(x_1)\ge H,\qquad a(x_2)\ge H,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
x_2-x_1=\frac\delta2,\qquad
H>\frac{\xi}{2},\qquad \xi\ge D.
\tag{12}
\]

For every \(x=(1-\lambda)x_1+\lambda x_2\) with \(0\le\lambda\le1\), concavity gives
\[
a(x)\ge(1-\lambda)a(x_1)+\lambda a(x_2)\ge H.
\]
This chord inequality uses neither differentiability nor endpoint continuity. It is valid even if \(x_1\) or \(x_2\) is an endpoint of the domain. Hence
\[
A\ge\int_{x_1}^{x_2}a(x)\,dx
\ge H(x_2-x_1)
>\frac{\xi\delta}{4}
\ge\frac{D\delta}{4}
>\frac{\delta^2}{5},
\tag{13}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(D=\delta+2\) and \(\delta>0\).

Equations (6) and (7) give
\[
M-B=\frac72(m+A)-\Gamma-G.
\tag{14}
\]
Using (4) and (13),
\[
M-B\ge\frac52(m+A)
>\frac52A
>\frac{\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]
On the other hand, the pointwise identity
\[
h_P(x)-[h_P(x)+x]_+=\min\{h_P(x),-x\},
\qquad -\delta\le x\le0,
\]
gives
\[
M-B
=\int_{-\delta}^{0}\min\{h_P(x),-x\}\,dx
\le\int_{-\delta}^{0}(-x)\,dx
=\frac{\delta^2}{2},
\tag{16}
\]
contradicting (15).

It remains to consider \(\Gamma=0\). By (9), \(B=0\). Equations (7) and (8) then give
\[
C=G\le A.
\tag{17}
\]
Because \(R\) has positive area, \(k=A+C>0\); thus (17) implies \(A>0\). From (6) and \(C\le A\),
\[
7(m+A)=2(M+C)\le2(M+A),
\]
so
\[
5A+7m\le2M.
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(A>0\),
\[
\mu=\frac mM<\frac27<\frac9{25}.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict residual lower bound from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` is therefore
\[
\frac{k}{M}>1-\mu,
\]
equivalently
\[
k>M-m.
\tag{20}
\]
But (17) gives \(k=A+C\le2A\), so
\[
2A>M-m.
\tag{21}
\]
Multiplying (21) by \(5/2\) and combining it with (18) yields
\[
\frac52(M-m)<5A\le2M-7m.
\]
After multiplication by \(2\), this becomes
\[
5M-5m<4M-14m,
\]
or
\[
M+9m<0,
\]
contrary to \(M,m>0\).

Both cases are impossible. Therefore no \(P,Q,R\) satisfy the stated hypotheses, and the requested inequality
\[
b+d\le m+k-t
\]
follows.
