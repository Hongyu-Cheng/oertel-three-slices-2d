## Proof

For one row, write the full \(B\)-axis intervals as
\[
[b_x,b_x+L_x]\times\{0\},\qquad
\{0\}\times[b_y,b_y+L_y],
\]
and the full \(D\)-axis intervals as
\[
[d_x,d_x+M_x]\times\{0\},\qquad
\{0\}\times[d_y,d_y+M_y].
\]
The missing-middle compatibility argument used in accepted facts
`2b42506f5de09be1` and `52ca8432ea5d39f0` separates \(B-D\) from
the open positive quadrant.  Positivity of the supported \(B\)-cell
or union gives a point of \(B\) with both coordinates positive.
The two positive full \(D\)-section lengths give nonzero points on
both axes.  Therefore both separator coefficients are positive.

Using the far \(B\)-axis endpoint and the lower \(D\)-axis endpoint
on each coordinate separately gives
\[
d_x\ge b_x+L_x,\qquad d_y\ge b_y+L_y.
\tag{32}
\]
The hull of the two \(D\)-axis intervals has coordinate area
\[
\frac12\left(d_xM_y+d_yM_x+M_xM_y\right).
\tag{33}
\]
It lies in \(D\).  Substituting (32) into (33) gives
\[
2|D|_{\rm coord}
\ge(b_x+L_x)M_y+(b_y+L_y)M_x+M_xM_y.
\tag{34}
\]

For the \(j\ell\)-row, (2) gives
\[
(b_x+L_x,b_y+L_y,M_x,M_y)
=(\beta+H,\alpha+J,H_2,K_2),
\]
and \(|D|_{\rm coord}=\Delta g\).  Equation (34) is (4a).
For the \(k\ell\)-row the corresponding tuple is
\[
(\tau+H,\gamma+K,H_2,J_2),
\]
and the area is \(\Delta g_0\), giving (4b).

For the \(jk\)-row, the retained full sections belong to the convex
unions in (1).  Their tuple is
\[
(\delta+\gamma+K,\delta-\alpha,J_2,K_2),
\]
and the union \(Q_{jk}\cup Q_N\) has physical area \(g_0+g\).
Thus (34) is (4c).  Strict coordinate domination in this row would
put the midpoint in the absent \(R_\ell\)-cell, so the same
compatibility separation applies to the unions.  The other rows give
the absent \(R_k\)-cell, or the absent \(R_\ell/R_{j\ell}\)-cells,
exactly as in the accepted \(k\ell\)-row argument.

These are the three unordered pairs of the three cutting lines, so
the cyclic list is exhaustive.  The derivation is polynomial after
the separator and lower endpoints have been removed.  It remains
valid on all strata in (8), because no offset was divided out.

Accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b` already proves that its conditions,
partitioned by (7), are a finite semialgebraic system and that
(8) lists its literal zero-offset equivalences.  Intersecting each
piece with the polynomial halfspaces (4) proves (5) and the claimed
finite exhaustive partition.  The exact calculations (17)-(31)
prove that the displayed rational point lies in the relative
interior.  This establishes nonemptiness of the augmented necessary
system, but none of the inequalities is asserted to be sufficient
for common-plane polygon realization.
