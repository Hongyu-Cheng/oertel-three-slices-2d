## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty
interior, let \(T=|K|>0\), and let
\(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) be nonzero pairwise nonparallel linear forms
satisfying
\[
\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0.
\]
Put
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
If
\[
c_1,c_2\leq\frac T2,
\]
then
\[
c_3T\geq(T-c_1)(T-c_2).
\]

## Proof

Let \(O\) be the common zero of the three forms, and write
\[
p_i(K)=\frac{|K\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|}{|K|},\qquad
\Delta(K)=p_3(K)-(1-p_1(K))(1-p_2(K)).
\]
Suppose for contradiction that
\[
p_1(K),p_2(K)\leq\frac12,\qquad \Delta(K)<0.                    \tag{1}
\]
Accepted fact `44d54af9bf8d0bd5` gives
\[
O\in\operatorname{int}K.                                      \tag{2}
\]

We first make both cap constraints strict. Since \(\ell_1,\ell_2\)
are nonparallel, the map
\[
x\longmapsto(\ell_1(x),\ell_2(x))
\]
is invertible. Choose \(v\) such that
\[
\ell_1(v)>0,\qquad\ell_2(v)>0,                                 \tag{3}
\]
and put \(K_t=K+t v\). Translation preserves total area, and
\[
|K_t\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|
=|K\cap\{\ell_i\leq-t\ell_i(v)\}|.                             \tag{4}
\]
By (2), some ball \(B(O,r)\) is contained in \(K\). Hence, for
\(i=1,2\) and every sufficiently small \(t>0\), the slab
\[
K\cap\{-t\ell_i(v)<\ell_i\leq0\}
\]
has positive area. Equations (3)-(4) therefore give
\[
p_i(K_t)<p_i(K)\qquad(i=1,2).                                  \tag{5}
\]

This treats all active-constraint cases. If exactly one of
\(p_1(K),p_2(K)\) equals \(1/2\), (5) makes that constraint strict
and keeps the other strict. If both equal \(1/2\), (5) makes both
strict. If both were strict initially, they remain strict as well.

For completeness, all three cap ratios are continuous in \(t\). For
any nonzero linear form \(\ell\), the distribution function
\[
s\longmapsto |K\cap\{\ell\leq s\}|
\]
is continuous: if \(s_n\to s\), the corresponding indicators converge
pointwise away from the line \(\{\ell=s\}\), which has planar area zero,
and all are dominated by \(\mathbf1_K\). Applying this to (4), also for
\(i=3\), proves continuity of \(p_i(K_t)\) and hence of
\(\Delta(K_t)\). Choose \(t>0\) so small that, in addition to (5),
\[
p_1(K_t),p_2(K_t)<\frac12,\qquad
\Delta(K_t)<0.                                                   \tag{6}
\]
We may also require \(t|v|<r\); then \(-tv\in\operatorname{int}K\),
so \(O\in\operatorname{int}K_t\).

Set \(K'=K_t\). We next approximate \(K'\) by polygons. For each
\(n\), choose a finite \(1/n\)-net \(E_n\subset K'\), including three
fixed noncollinear interior points, and put
\[
P_n=\operatorname{conv}E_n.
\]
Then \(P_n\subset K'\), every \(P_n\) is two-dimensional, and
\[
d_H(P_n,K')\leq\frac1n.                                         \tag{7}
\]

We justify simultaneously the convergence of total area and all three
cut areas. If \(x\in\operatorname{int}K'\), choose
\(\rho>0\) with \(x+\rho B\subset K'\). For all sufficiently large
\(n\), (7) implies \(x\in P_n\). Indeed, otherwise strict separation
would give a unit vector \(u\) with
\[
u\mathbin{\cdot}x>h_{P_n}(u),
\]
whereas Hausdorff convergence of convex bodies gives
\[
h_{P_n}(u)\geq h_{K'}(u)-d_H(P_n,K')
\geq u\mathbin{\cdot}x+\rho-d_H(P_n,K')
>u\mathbin{\cdot}x,
\]
a contradiction. Since \(P_n\subset K'\), it follows that
\[
\mathbf1_{P_n}(x)\longrightarrow\mathbf1_{K'}(x)
\]
for every \(x\notin\partial K'\). The boundary of a planar convex body
has planar area zero, and all sets lie in one fixed bounded set.
Dominated convergence therefore yields
\[
|P_n|\longrightarrow|K'|                                      \tag{8}
\]
and, after multiplying the indicators by the fixed halfplane
indicator,
\[
|P_n\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|
\longrightarrow
|K'\cap\{\ell_i\leq0\}|
\qquad(i=1,2,3).                                                 \tag{9}
\]
Since the limiting total area is positive, (8)-(9) imply
\[
p_i(P_n)\longrightarrow p_i(K')\qquad(i=1,2,3),                \tag{10}
\]
and consequently
\[
\Delta(P_n)\longrightarrow\Delta(K').                          \tag{11}
\]

The inequalities in (6) are strict. Hence (10)-(11) give, for every
sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
p_1(P_n),p_2(P_n)<\frac12,\qquad \Delta(P_n)<0.                 \tag{12}
\]
Finally apply a positive homothety about \(O\) to make \(P_n\) have
area one. Each halfplane \(\{\ell_i\leq0\}\) is invariant under this
homothety, so all three ratios and the defect in (12) are unchanged.
The resulting area-one convex polygon contradicts accepted fact
`b0c2275528e42b65`.

Thus a strict convex-body counterexample cannot exist, and the asserted
inequality follows.
