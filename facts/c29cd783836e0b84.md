## Statement

Use only the scalar datum of accepted fact `8d1cb8030731422b` and the
endpoint-coordinate and full-chord convention of accepted fact
`fa0e57b225178b3b`.  Thus
\[
\Delta=1,\qquad \delta=100,
\]
\[
J=H=K=\frac1{1000},\qquad
\alpha=99,\qquad
\beta=\frac{99{,}999}{1000},\qquad
\gamma=\tau=0,
\]
\[
(a,b,c,d_{\rm area})
=\left(
\frac1{100{,}000{,}000},
\frac{612{,}806{,}201}{40{,}000{,}000},
\frac3{62{,}500},
\frac1{100{,}000{,}000}
\right),
\tag{1}
\]
and the three prescribed full chords are
\[
X=0,\quad
Y\in\left[99,\frac{99{,}001}{1000}\right],
\tag{2}
\]
\[
Y=0,\quad
X\in\left[\frac{99{,}999}{1000},100\right],
\tag{3}
\]
\[
X+Y=100,\quad
(X,Y)=(100+s,-s),\quad
0\le s\le\frac1{1000}.
\tag{4}
\]
There is no compact convex polygon having (2)-(4) as its full
sections and having all four prescribed positive cells.  In
particular, there is no strictly convex rational polygon realizing
the datum.

## Proof

1. Endpoint incidences and cyclic order.

The accepted exact-cell convention gives the four interiors
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
P_j&X<0&Y>0&X+Y<100\\
P_0&X>0&Y>0&X+Y<100\\
P_\ell&X>0&Y<0&X+Y<100\\
P_{k\ell}&X>0&Y<0&X+Y>100 .
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
J_-=(0,99).
\]
The four boundary points needed below are
\[
J_+=\left(0,\frac{99{,}001}{1000}\right),\qquad
O=(100,0),
\tag{6}
\]
\[
K_+=\left(\frac{100{,}001}{1000},-\frac1{1000}\right),
\qquad
L=\left(\frac{99{,}999}{1000},0\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(J_+\) is the \(P_j/P_0\) endpoint of the \(X=0\)
chord, \(O\) is the common \(P_0/P_\ell/P_{k\ell}\) endpoint
forced by \(\gamma=\tau=0\), \(K_+\) is the other
\(P_\ell/P_{k\ell}\) endpoint of the \(p_k=0\) chord, and \(L\)
is the other \(P_0/P_\ell\) endpoint of the \(Y=0\) chord.
For a direct order check, the four axis-chord endpoints
\[
J_+,\ O,\ L,\ J_-
\]
have consecutive turn determinants
\[
-\frac{99{,}001}{1{,}000{,}000},\qquad
-\frac{99}{1000},\qquad
-\frac{99{,}999}{1{,}000{,}000},\qquad
-\frac1{10}.
\]
They are therefore in strict clockwise convex position in the
displayed order.  The positive \(P_{k\ell}\) boundary arc leaves
\(O\) and ends at \(K_+\), and the positive \(P_\ell\) boundary arc
then ends at \(L\).  Hence the required four points occur in the
cyclic boundary order
\[
J_+,\ O,\ K_+,\ L.
\tag{8}
\]
The outer boundary of \(P_{k\ell}\) is the boundary arc from \(O\)
to \(K_+\); its other boundary piece is the full chord
\([O,K_+]\).

We use the following elementary chord-chain fact.  If
\(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\) occur in that cyclic order on the boundary of a
convex polygon, then the boundary arc from \(z_2\) to \(z_3\) lies
on the \(z_3\)-side of the line \(z_1z_2\) and on the \(z_2\)-side
of the line \(z_3z_4\).  Indeed, a line through two boundary points
separates the two open boundary chains between them into its two
closed halfplanes.  If the two points lie on one boundary edge, the
whole polygon is already in one of those halfplanes.  This proves the
same closed containment in every case.  Convexity then gives the
containment for the region bounded by the arc and its chord.

2. The two exact halfplane bounds.

Introduce determinant-one coordinates
\[
t=-Y,\qquad q=X+Y-100,
\qquad
(X,Y)=(100+t+q,-t).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus coordinate area is unchanged.  Since \(\Delta=1\), it is also
the physical-area coordinate convention of `fa0e57b225178b3b`.
The four points (6)-(7) become
\[
J_+=\left(-\frac{99{,}001}{1000},
          -\frac{999}{1000}\right),\quad
O=(0,0),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
K_+=\left(\frac1{1000},0\right),\quad
L=\left(0,-\frac1{1000}\right).
\tag{11}
\]
In these coordinates \(P_{k\ell}\) has \(q\ge0\).

The line \(J_+O\) is
\[
q=\frac{999}{99{,}001}t.
\tag{12}
\]
The point \(K_+\) lies on its lower side.  By (8) and the
chord-chain fact, the complete outer \(P_{k\ell}\) arc, and hence
the complete cell, satisfies
\[
q\le\frac{999}{99{,}001}t.
\tag{13}
\]
The line \(K_+L\) is
\[
q=t-\frac1{1000}.
\tag{14}
\]
The point \(O\) lies on its upper side.  The same chord-chain fact
therefore gives
\[
q\ge t-\frac1{1000}
\tag{15}
\]
throughout \(P_{k\ell}\).

These side choices can also be checked directly by determinants.
For (12), the determinant at \(K_+\) is
\(-999/1{,}000{,}000<0\).  For (14), the determinant at \(O\)
is \(-1/1{,}000{,}000<0\).  Thus no orientation choice is hidden
in (13)-(15).

3. The area contradiction.

Put
\[
\rho=\frac{999}{99{,}001},\qquad h=\frac1{1000}.
\]
Equations (13), (15), and \(q\ge0\) put \(P_{k\ell}\) in
\[
\mathcal T
=\{(t,q):q\ge0,\ q\le\rho t,\ q\ge t-h\}.
\tag{16}
\]
This is the triangle with vertices
\[
(0,0),\qquad (h,0),\qquad
\left(
\frac{99{,}001}{98{,}002{,}000},
\frac{999}{98{,}002{,}000}
\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, the last vertex is obtained from
\(\rho t=t-h\), since
\[
1-\rho=\frac{98{,}002}{99{,}001}.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\mathcal T|
=\frac12\cdot\frac1{1000}
 \cdot\frac{999}{98{,}002{,}000}
=\frac{999}{196{,}004{,}000{,}000}.
\tag{18}
\]
But the prescribed \(P_{k\ell}\) area exceeds this upper bound:
\[
\frac1{100{,}000{,}000}
-\frac{999}{196{,}004{,}000{,}000}
=\frac{12{,}013}{2{,}450{,}050{,}000{,}000}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
This contradicts (1).  The remaining prescribed cell areas cannot
repair the failed \(P_{k\ell}\) incidence, so the full \(A_0\)
completion is impossible.
