## Statement

Assume the exact triangular \(N=3\) system of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` in the surviving orientation, so
\[
A=|\det U|\le B=|\det V|,\qquad
q:=\sum_{i=1}^3\sigma_i>1,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\delta>0\), put
\[
R=\delta L,\qquad \tau=\delta\sigma,
\tag{2}
\]
and suppose that, after the permitted row permutation and common
vertex relabeling,
\[
R=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&-a&-b\\
-b&p&-a\\
-a&-b&p
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad p,a,b>0,\qquad p>a+b.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
c=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}.
\tag{4}
\]
For row \(i\), let \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) be the absolute areas of its
closed caps in the endpoint triangles of areas \(A\) and \(B\),
respectively. Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3x_i\ge A.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, in every putative strict system
\[
M_i=c+x_i+y_i<t\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{6}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{\quad
\sqrt{A-x_i}+\sqrt{B-y_i}
\le 2\sqrt{1-c}
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]

The following scalar statement is sharp independently of the geometric
origin of the variables. For \(A,B>0\), \(A\le B\), and
\(4/9<c<1\), define
\[
H=4(1-c).
\tag{8}
\]
The minimum of
\[
E=\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i+y_i)
\tag{9}
\]
subject to
\[
0\le x_i\le A,\qquad 0\le y_i\le B,\qquad
\sum_i x_i\ge A,
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{A-x_i}+\sqrt{B-y_i}\le\sqrt H
\qquad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{11}
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
E_*=
\begin{cases}
3(A+B-H),
& B\ge H,\\[1mm]
A,
& B<H\ \hbox{and}\ 2A\le3Q,\\[1mm]
3(A-Q),
& B<H\ \hbox{and}\ 2A\ge3Q,
\end{cases}}
\qquad
Q=(\sqrt H-\sqrt B)^2.
\tag{12}
\]
At \(B=H\), the first and limiting third formulas agree. At
\(B<H\) and \(2A=3Q\), the second and third formulas agree.

Let
\[
3t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}3.
\tag{13}
\]
Define the following chamberwise gap numerators:
\[
\Psi_1=45c+7A+7B-38,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\Psi_2=9c+A-2B-2,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_3
&=9c+7A-2B-2-9Q\\
&=45c+7A-11B-38
  +36\sqrt{B(1-c)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Then, in the respective three chambers of (12),
\[
3\left(3c+E_*-3t\right)
=\Psi_1,\quad\Psi_2,\quad\Psi_3.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently the exact scalar target-covered region is
\[
\begin{cases}
B\ge H,\quad \Psi_1\ge0,\\
\text{or }B<H,\quad 2A\le3Q,\quad \Psi_2\ge0,\\
\text{or }B<H,\quad 2A\ge3Q,\quad \Psi_3\ge0.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
\]
The exact scalar residual is obtained by replacing the applicable
\(\Psi_j\ge0\) in (18) by \(\Psi_j<0\).

For the actual exact triangular parameter projection, intersect these
sets with
\[
\mathcal D_{\rm tri}=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
0<A\le B,\quad r=A/B,\\
0<r<(27-2\sqrt{26})/25,\\
L_0(r)<1/B<U_0(r),\\
1+A<2B,\quad 4/9<c<1
\end{array}
\right\},
\tag{19}
\]
where \(L_0,U_0\) are those in accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`. If the current cyclic search is restricted further
to \(0<r<3/8\), intersect (19) with that inequality as well.
On \(\mathcal D_{\rm tri}\), the strict area-triangle inequality
\(B<1+A\) makes the entire first chamber \(B\ge H\)
target-covered. Hence the exact target residual in the triangular
application is only
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_{\rm scalar}
=\mathcal D_{\rm tri}\cap
\bigl(&\{B<H,\ 2A\le3Q,\ \Psi_2<0\}\\
&{}\cup
\{B<H,\ 2A\ge3Q,\ \Psi_3<0\}\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{20}
\]
The common boundary \(2A=3Q\) causes no ambiguity because
\(\Psi_2=\Psi_3\) there.

## Proof

The column sums of (3) are all
\[
\delta=p-a-b>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Every row is a permutation of \((p,-a,-b)\). The exact cap formula in
accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` therefore gives (4), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
9p^2-4(p+a)(p+b)
&=(a-b)^2+6(p-a-b)(a+b)\\
&\qquad+5(p-a-b)^2>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(a,b>0\) and \(p-a-b>0\), so every term needed for the
one-positive-vertex cap chamber has the asserted sign. Also
\((p+a)(p+b)>p^2\). Thus
\[
\frac49<c<1.
\tag{23}
\]

We first prove (5). Write the endpoint triangles in the normalized
barycentric model as
\[
A_0=U\Delta,\qquad A_2=V\Delta,
\qquad |A_0|=A,\quad |A_2|=B.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb R^3\), write
\[
r_i(z)=R_{i*}z.
\tag{25}
\]
The small-end cap in row \(i\) is
\[
X_i=A_0\cap\{r_i\ge-\tau_i\},
\qquad |X_i|=x_i.
\tag{26}
\]
If \(z\in A_0\) lay outside all three closed caps, then
\[
r_i(z)<-\tau_i\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{27}
\]
Since \(\mathbf1^\top R=\delta\mathbf1^\top\) and every point of
\(A_0\) has coordinate sum \(1\), summing (27) would give
\[
\delta=\sum_i r_i(z)<-\sum_i\tau_i
=-\delta q<-\delta,
\tag{28}
\]
which is impossible. Hence the three \(X_i\) cover \(A_0\), and
subadditivity of area gives (5). This is the same exact pointwise cover
used in accepted fact `b8a065ca6eeaad3f`; it includes constant endpoint
rows and all threshold equalities.

We next prove (7), including the boundary conventions. The large-end
cap is
\[
Y_i=A_2\cap\{r_i\ge\tau_i\},
\qquad |Y_i|=y_i.
\tag{29}
\]
The residual strip implies the strict area-triangle inequality
\[
B<1+A.
\tag{30}
\]
Using (23), (30), and \(A\le B\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c+A-t
&>\frac49+A-\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\\
&=\frac{2+7A-2B}{9}
>\frac{5A}{9}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Thus (6) forces
\[
x_i<A,\qquad y_i<B.
\tag{32}
\]
Indeed, \(x_i=A\) would give \(M_i\ge c+A>t\), and
\(y_i=B\) would give \(M_i\ge c+B\ge c+A>t\).

Define the closed lower complementary caps
\[
D_{0i}=A_0\cap\{r_i\le-\tau_i\},\qquad
D_{2i}=A_2\cap\{r_i\le\tau_i\},
\tag{33}
\]
and the closed middle complement
\[
D_{1i}=\Delta\cap\{r_i\le0\}.
\tag{34}
\]
If an endpoint functional is nonconstant, its threshold line has
planar area zero, so (32) gives
\[
|D_{0i}|=A-x_i>0,\qquad
|D_{2i}|=B-y_i>0.
\tag{35}
\]
If an endpoint functional is constant, its closed upper cap is either
empty or full. The full case is excluded by (32), so the upper cap is
empty and the corresponding lower complement is the whole endpoint
triangle. Thus (35) remains exact in every constant-row case.

The middle functional has vertex values \(p,-a,-b\), with
\(p,a,b>0\), so it is nonconstant even when \(a=b\). Its zero line has
area zero, and hence
\[
|D_{1i}|=1-c.
\tag{36}
\]
For any \(z_0\in D_{0i}\) and \(z_2\in D_{2i}\), exact compatibility
from accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives
\[
\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\in
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq\Delta,
\tag{37}
\]
while
\[
r_i\left(\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)
\le\frac{-\tau_i+\tau_i}{2}=0.
\tag{38}
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{D_{0i}+D_{2i}}2\subseteq D_{1i}.
\tag{39}
\]
The sets in (35) are positive-area compact convex sets. Planar
Brunn--Minkowski and (36)--(39) give
\[
\sqrt{1-c}
\ge\frac{\sqrt{A-x_i}+\sqrt{B-y_i}}2,
\tag{40}
\]
which is (7).

This also settles degenerate conventions. A threshold through a support
vertex or support edge is included in both closed caps, but that boundary
has zero planar area whenever the functional is nonconstant. Classical
Brunn--Minkowski also permits a nonempty zero-area compact set by
approximation, although (32) gives positive area in the strict system.
If a lower complement is literally empty, the associated upper cap is
full, and (31) already excludes the row. No assertion about the
Minkowski sum with the empty set is used.

It remains to solve the scalar problem. Put
\[
u_i=A-x_i,\qquad v_i=B-y_i.
\tag{41}
\]
Then
\[
0\le u_i\le A,\qquad 0\le v_i\le B,\qquad
\sum_i u_i\le2A,
\tag{42}
\]
and (11) is
\[
\sqrt{u_i}+\sqrt{v_i}\le\sqrt H.
\tag{43}
\]
Minimizing \(E\) is equivalent to maximizing
\[
F=\sum_i(u_i+v_i),
\qquad
E=3(A+B)-F.
\tag{44}
\]

Case 1: \(B\ge H\).

Equation (43) gives
\[
u_i+v_i\le
(\sqrt{u_i}+\sqrt{v_i})^2\le H.
\tag{45}
\]
Thus \(F\le3H\). Equality is attained by
\[
u_i=0,\qquad v_i=H\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{46}
\]
which respects \(v_i\le B\) and \(\sum_i u_i\le2A\). Hence
\[
E_*=3(A+B-H).
\tag{47}
\]

Case 2 and Case 3: \(B<H\).

Now \(A\le B<H\), so every \(u\in[0,A]\) satisfies \(u<H\). For fixed
\(u\), the largest allowed \(v\) is
\[
v_{\max}(u)=
\min\{B,(\sqrt H-\sqrt u)^2\}.
\tag{48}
\]
With \(Q=(\sqrt H-\sqrt B)^2\), this gives
\[
\phi(u):=u+v_{\max}(u)
=
\begin{cases}
B+u,&0\le u\le Q,\\
H+2u-2\sqrt{Hu},&Q\le u\le A.
\end{cases}
\tag{49}
\]
The second interval can be nonempty only when \(Q\le A\le B\). On
that interval,
\[
\begin{aligned}
B+Q-\phi(u)
&=2(\sqrt B-\sqrt u)
   (\sqrt B+\sqrt u-\sqrt H)\\
&\ge0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{50}
\]
because \(u\le B\) and \(u\ge Q\). On the first interval,
\(\phi(u)=B+u\le B+Q\). Therefore
\[
\phi(u)\le B+Q\qquad(0\le u\le A).
\tag{51}
\]

If \(2A\le3Q\), then (42), (48), and \(v_i\le B\) give
\[
F\le3B+\sum_i u_i\le3B+2A.
\tag{52}
\]
This is attained symmetrically by
\[
u_i=\frac{2A}{3}\le Q,\qquad v_i=B
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{53}
\]
Consequently
\[
E_*=3(A+B)-(3B+2A)=A.
\tag{54}
\]

If \(2A\ge3Q\), summing (51) gives
\[
F\le3(B+Q).
\tag{55}
\]
Equality is attained by
\[
u_i=Q,\qquad v_i=B
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{56}
\]
because \(3Q\le2A\). Thus
\[
E_*=3(A+B)-3(B+Q)=3(A-Q).
\tag{57}
\]
Equations (47), (54), and (57) prove (12). They also prove sharpness,
not merely an infimum. In the original variables, symmetric minimizers
are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{chamber}&x_i&y_i\\ \hline
B\ge H&A&B-H\\
B<H,\ 2A\le3Q&A/3&0\\
B<H,\ 2A\ge3Q&A-Q&0.
\end{array}
\tag{58}
\]
The allocation constraint is an equality in the second chamber and
follows from \(2A\ge3Q\) in the third.

Substitution of (12) into
\[
3c+E_*-\frac{2(1+A+B)}3
\tag{59}
\]
gives (14)--(17). Equality in (17) is already enough to exclude three
strict rows, because their sum would be strictly less than \(3t\).
Conversely, when the applicable \(\Psi_j<0\), the symmetric allocation
in (58) makes every scalar row equal to
\[
c+\frac{E_*}{3}<t.
\tag{60}
\]
Thus (18) and its strict complement are the exact target-covered and
residual regions for the stated scalar information.

Finally, on \(\mathcal D_{\rm tri}\), (30) and (23) imply
\[
c+A>t.
\tag{61}
\]
If \(B\ge H\), then (47) gives \(E_*\ge3A\), so
\[
3c+E_*\ge3(c+A)>3t.
\tag{62}
\]
This removes the first chamber from the triangular residual and proves
(20).
