## Statement

Let
\[
E=\{(s_1,s_2,s_3)\in\mathbb R^3:s_1+s_2+s_3=1\},
\]
equipped with any fixed positive constant multiple of planar Lebesgue measure, denoted by \(|\cdot|\). For every compact two-dimensional convex body \(L\subset E\),
\[
\max_{1\le j\le3}|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|\ge \frac49|L|. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, there is no compact two-dimensional convex body \(L\subset E\) satisfying
\[
|L|=\frac89,
\qquad
|L\cap\{s_j>0\}|<\frac13\quad(j=1,2,3). \tag{2}
\]
In particular, (2) is impossible even before imposing rational vertices or containment of the triangle whose vertex matrix is
\[
Z=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&-1/18\\
-4&4&0\\
5&-3&19/18
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## Proof

Let \(g=(g_1,g_2,g_3)\in E\) be the centroid of \(L\) with respect to the chosen area normalization. Multiplying planar Lebesgue measure by a positive constant changes neither \(g\) nor any area ratio. Because \(g\in E\),
\[
g_1+g_2+g_3=1.
\]
Choose \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) such that \(g_k\ge1/3\). Consider the closed halfplane of \(E\)
\[
Q=\{s\in E:s_k\ge g_k\}.
\]
Its boundary contains the centroid \(g\). The planar Grünbaum centroid inequality therefore gives
\[
|L\cap Q|\ge\left(\frac23\right)^2|L|=\frac49|L|. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(g_k\ge1/3>0\), every point of \(Q\) has positive \(k\)-th coordinate. Hence
\[
Q\subseteq\{s\in E:s_k>0\}.
\]
Together with (3), this proves
\[
|L\cap\{s_k>0\}|\ge |L\cap Q|\ge\frac49|L|,
\]
which is (1). This argument uses convexity through the centroid halfplane inequality and applies without any bound on the number of vertices.

If \(|L|=8/9\), (1) yields an index \(k\) for which
\[
|L\cap\{s_k>0\}|\ge\frac49\cdot\frac89
=\frac{32}{81}
=\frac13+\frac5{81}
>\frac13.
\]
This contradicts the three strict inequalities in (2). Therefore the fixed extension problem is globally infeasible for arbitrary compact two-dimensional convex \(L\), and a fortiori for rational convex polygons containing the specified triangle.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), no. 4, 1257–1261, Theorem 2, https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. The theorem proves that the centroid of an \(n\)-dimensional convex body belongs to every-halfspace depth level \((n/(n+1))^n\). Applied with \(n=2\) after identifying the affine plane \(E\) with \(\mathbb R^2\), it gives (3); multiplying Lebesgue measure by the problem's fixed normalization does not change the ratio.
