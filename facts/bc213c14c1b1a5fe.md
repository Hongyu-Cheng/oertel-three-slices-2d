## Statement

Use the notation and endpoint contribution sets of accepted fact `84d4961270542ec6`.  Put
\[
o=u-H-\tau,\qquad d=w_0-A,
\]
and
\[
O=(o,0),\qquad P=(2-d,d),\qquad
Q=(\psi-H-\tau,w_0+K_2).
\]
The following conditions are jointly necessary and sufficient for

1. \(O,P\in T_N\);
2. \(Q\) to lie in the closed halfspace \(x+y\geq2\);
3. \(x+y\) not to be identically \(2\) on \(OQ\);
4. the resulting unique level crossing \(R\in OQ\cap\{x+y=2\}\) to lie on the closed hypotenuse of \(T_N\) and satisfy \(R_y\geq d\):

\[
0\leq o\leq2,\qquad 0\leq d\leq2,\qquad
\beta\geq0,\qquad \beta+2-o>0,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
d(\beta+2-o)
\leq(2-o)(w_0+K_2)
\leq2(\beta+2-o).
\tag{2}
\]
On this branch,
\[
R=(2-r,r),\qquad
r=\frac{(2-o)(w_0+K_2)}{\beta+2-o},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{O,P,R\}\subseteq F_N.
\tag{4}
\]
In the displayed order \(O,P,R\), its determinant is nonnegative and its exact coordinate area is
\[
\mathscr A_{\triangle}
=\frac{(2-o)\bigl((2-o)(w_0+K_2)
-d(\beta+2-o)\bigr)}
       {2(\beta+2-o)}.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(\Delta>0\), the corresponding forced physical \(R_N\)-area is
\[
\frac{\mathscr A_{\triangle}}{4\Delta}
=\frac{(2-o)\bigl((2-o)(w_0+K_2)
-d(\beta+2-o)\bigr)}
       {8\Delta(\beta+2-o)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus, for a prescribed budget \(z\geq0\), these three endpoint sums alone overflow that budget whenever
\[
(2-o)\bigl((2-o)(w_0+K_2)
-d(\beta+2-o)\bigr)
>8\Delta z(\beta+2-o).
\tag{7}
\]
On the positive-denominator branch, equality in (7) only saturates the budget and is not an overflow conclusion.

All boundary cases in (1)-(2) are included.  More explicitly:

- If \(\beta=0\), then \(\beta+2-o>0\) forces \(o<2\), \(R=Q\), condition (2) is \(d\leq w_0+K_2\leq2\), and (5) is \((2-o)(w_0+K_2-d)/2\).
- If \(o=2\), then the positive-denominator condition forces \(\beta>0\), condition (2) forces \(d=0\), and \(R=O=P=(2,0)\).
- Equality on the left of (2) is exactly \(r=d\), hence \(R=P\), and it is exactly the zero-area case of (5).  This includes \(r=d=0\), \(o=2\), and the case \(w_0+K_2=0\) when \(o<2\).
- Equality on the right of (2) is exactly \(r=2\), hence \(R=(0,2)\), and (5) becomes \((2-o)(2-d)/2\).  If \(d=2\), both sides of (2) are equalities, \(R=P=(0,2)\), and the area is zero.

Under \(0\leq o\leq2\) and \(\beta\geq0\), the excluded denominator equality
\[
\beta+2-o=0
\tag{8}
\]
is equivalent to \(o=2\) and \(\beta=0\).  Then the whole segment \(OQ\) lies in \(x+y=2\).  The quotient formulas (3), (5), and (6), and the unique-\(R\) assertions made before a degenerate \(R\) is chosen, are undefined on this branch.  Subject also to \(0\leq d\leq2\), a point \(R\in OQ\cap T_N\) with \(R_y\geq d\) exists exactly when
\[
d=0\quad\text{or}\quad w_0+K_2\geq d.
\tag{9}
\]
In that case one may take \(R=P\), after which (4) is well-defined and holds with coordinate and physical triangle area zero.  If (9) fails, there is no such \(R\).  The polynomial inequality (7) remains algebraically defined on (8): both sides are zero, so it specializes to the false inequality \(0>0\).  It is not obtained there from the positive-denominator overflow equivalence.

## Proof

The three points really are fixed endpoint sums from accepted fact `84d4961270542ec6`.  Indeed,
\[
O=(-(H+\tau),0)+(u,0),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
(-\chi,-A)+(S-w_0,w_0)
&=(-(\delta-A)+\delta+2-w_0,w_0-A)\\
&=(2-d,d)=P,
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
Q=(-(H+\tau),0)+(\psi,w_0+K_2).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently \(O,P,Q\in D_{\mathrm{ep}}\).

Since \(T_N=\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq2\}\),
\[
O\in T_N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad0\leq o\leq2
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
P\in T_N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad0\leq d\leq2.
\tag{14}
\]
Also, using \(\psi=S-w_0-K_2\), \(S=\delta+2\), and
\(\beta+H=\delta-\tau\),
\[
Q_x+Q_y
=S-H-\tau
=\beta+2.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(Q_x+Q_y\geq2\) is exactly \(\beta\geq0\).

Along \(OQ\), the affine function \(x+y\) runs from \(o\) to
\(\beta+2\).  Under (13), (15), and \(\beta\geq0\), its total increase
from the level \(2\) at the two ends is measured by
\[
(\beta+2)-o=\beta+2-o=(2-o)+\beta\geq0.
\tag{16}
\]
It is not identically \(2\) precisely when (16) is positive.  In that
case the unique level parameter is
\[
\lambda=\frac{2-o}{\beta+2-o}\in[0,1],
\tag{17}
\]
so \(R=O+\lambda(Q-O)\) lies on \(OQ\).  Its \(y\)-coordinate is exactly
the number \(r\) in (3), and its \(x\)-coordinate is \(2-r\).  Therefore
\[
R\text{ lies on the closed hypotenuse and }R_y\geq d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\leq r\leq2.
\tag{18}
\]
Because the denominator in (3) is positive, multiplying (18) by it
gives exactly (2).  Equations (13)-(18) prove both directions of the
claimed exact branch characterization.  Notice that no separate sign
assumption on \(w_0+K_2\) is needed: if \(o<2\), it follows from the
left side of (2), while if \(o=2\), the crossing is already \(O\).

Convexity of \(D_{\mathrm{ep}}\) and (10), (12) give
\[
R\in[O,Q]\subseteq D_{\mathrm{ep}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Together with \(P\in D_{\mathrm{ep}}\), this implies
\(\operatorname{conv}\{O,P,R\}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{ep}}\).  Conditions
(1)-(2) put all three vertices in the convex set \(T_N\), hence their
convex hull lies in \(T_N\) as well.  Intersecting the two containments
and using \(F_N=D_{\mathrm{ep}}\cap T_N\) proves (4).

Using (3), the oriented determinant in the stated vertex order is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(P-O,R-O)
&=\det\bigl((2-d-o,d),(2-r-o,r)\bigr)\\
&=(2-o)(r-d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Both factors in (20) are nonnegative by (1) and (18), so the oriented
area is the geometric area.  Dividing (20) by \(2\) and substituting
(3) gives exactly (5).  Equation (20) also proves that the area is zero
exactly when \(r=d\): if \(o=2\), (2) already forces \(d=r=0\).  This
verifies every zero and equality assertion following (7).

Accepted fact `84d4961270542ec6` gives the physical Jacobian factor:
coordinate area in \(F_N\) is \(4\Delta\) times physical area in
\(\overline{R_N}\).  For \(\Delta>0\), dividing (5) by \(4\Delta\)
proves (6).  Since the denominator in (6) is positive, the strict
physical inequality \(\mathscr A_{\triangle}/(4\Delta)>z\) is
equivalent, without reversing its direction, to (7).  If equality
holds, the forced triangle has area exactly \(z\), so this area
argument supplies no contradiction.  If \(z=0\), overflow is
equivalent to strictness on the left side of (2).

It remains only to audit (8).  Nonnegativity of \(2-o\) and \(\beta\)
shows that (8) is equivalent to \(o=2,\beta=0\).  Writing
\(t=w_0+K_2\) only in this audit, (15) gives
\[
O=(2,0),\qquad Q=(2-t,t),
\tag{21}
\]
and a point of \(OQ\) has the form \((2-st,st)\), \(0\leq s\leq1\).
If \(d=0\), then \(P=O\), so \(R=P\) works for every \(t\).  If \(d>0\),
a point with \(R_y\geq d\) and \(R\in T_N\) exists exactly when
\(t\geq d\); then \(s=d/t\) gives \(R=P=(2-d,d)\).  This proves (9),
the failure assertion when (9) is false, and the zero-area containment
on every valid denominator-zero branch.  Finally, substituting
\(2-o=0\) and \(\beta+2-o=0\) directly into the two polynomial sides of
(7) gives \(0>0\).  Hence (7) is defined but false on this branch, as
claimed; no division by the zero denominator is involved in that
substitution.
