## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and assume the common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  Thus
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,
\]
\(A_0,A_2,K\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes,
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
and \(P_J,Q_H,R_G\) are the exact sign cells from that fact.  Write
\[
p_J=|P_J|,\qquad q_H=|Q_H|,\qquad r_G=|R_G|,
\]
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad \kappa=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,
\]
and
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9.
\]
Assume the three crossings
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=t\qquad(i\in N),
\tag{1}
\]
the canonical tails
\[
c_i+m\ge t,\qquad c_i+M>t\qquad(i\in N),
\tag{2}
\]
and finite interior
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<m,\qquad 0<c_i<\kappa
\qquad(i\in N).
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
W=
\sum_J(3|J|-2)p_J+
\sum_H(3|H|-2)q_H+
\sum_G(3|G|-2)r_G,
\tag{4}
\]
and assume \(W=0\).  Fix \(j\in N\), write
\[
N\setminus\{j\}=\{k,\ell\},
\]
and assume the opposite pair
\[
q_{\{j\}}>0,\qquad q_{\{k,\ell\}}>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Then:

1. Necessarily
\[
p_\varnothing>0.
\tag{6}
\]

2. The positive \(Q\)-support contains at least one of the four paths
\[
\{\{j\},\{j,k\},\{k\},\{k,\ell\}\},
\tag{7a}
\]
\[
\{\{j\},\{j,\ell\},\{\ell\},\{k,\ell\}\},
\tag{7b}
\]
\[
\{\{j\},\{j,k\},N,\{k,\ell\}\},
\tag{7c}
\]
\[
\{\{j\},\{j,\ell\},N,\{k,\ell\}\}.
\tag{7d}
\]
Up to interchanging \(k,\ell\), these are exactly two minimal types: an outer path and a path through \(N\).  Each of the four sets in (7) is realizable as the exact positive-area support of a two-dimensional convex polytope for the same affine sign arrangement.  This last assertion concerns the \(Q\)-body alone and does not assert a full realization of (1)--(5).

3. Put
\[
E=\overline{Q_{\{j\}}},\qquad
F=\overline{Q_{\{k,\ell\}}},\qquad
H=\operatorname{conv}(E\cup F),
\]
\[
Z=\frac{P_\varnothing+H}{2},\qquad
s_j=|Z\cap\{-1<f_j<0\}|.
\]
Accepted facts `c472ceb412ca0594` and `9922bcace1cdc113` give \(s_j>0\).  Every realization satisfying (1)--(5) obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
2p_\varnothing\ge{}&
q_{\{j\}}+4q_{\{k,\ell\}}\\
&+\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_\varnothing}+
\sqrt{q_{\{j\}}}\right)^2
+\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_\varnothing}+
\sqrt{q_{\{k,\ell\}}}\right)^2
+4s_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the strict slab is included in the positive \(R\)-cell budget, and (8) is a necessary inequality rather than a contradiction.

4. The following exact nonnegative scalar table satisfies the numerical conditions in (1)--(5), has the connected outer-path support in (7a), and has sufficient separate target-cell capacities for the endpoint and slab lower bounds used in (8).  Take
\[
(j,k,\ell)=(3,1,2)
\]
and set every unlisted cell mass to zero.  Put
\[
p_\varnothing=\frac{851}{1000},\qquad
p_1=\frac{49}{1000},\qquad
p_2=p_3=\frac1{20},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
q_3=\frac{49}{1000},\qquad
q_{12}=\frac1{20},\qquad
q_{13}=q_1=\frac1{2000},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
r_1=\frac4{15}-\frac1{2000},\qquad
r_2=\frac4{15}+\frac1{1000},\qquad
r_3=\frac{11}{30}-\frac1{1000},
\tag{11}
\]
\[
r_{12}=\frac1{10}-\frac1{1000},\qquad
r_{13}=\frac3{2000}.
\tag{12}
\]
It has exact scalar \(Q\)-support
\[
\{3,13,1,12\},
\tag{13}
\]
and one may set
\[
s_3=\frac1{1000}<r_{13}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is only a scalar calibration.  It does not assert that the individual cell masses in (9)--(12) are realized by planar convex bodies, that they arise from one common affine arrangement, or that they admit a compatible triple \(A_0,A_2,K\).  It proves only the mutual consistency of the explicitly verified scalar conditions and separate capacity inequalities.  Consequently, a proof excluding the remaining branch must use some additional convex-geometric or common-arrangement restriction not represented by those checks.  A simultaneous transverse-placement inequality is one possible such restriction.

## Proof

Accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` gives
\[
P_N=Q_\varnothing=R_\varnothing=\varnothing.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently the only negative coefficient in (4) belongs to
\(P_\varnothing\): every nonempty-index cell has coefficient at least
\(1\).  If \(p_\varnothing=0\), then (5) gives
\[
W\ge q_{\{j\}}+4q_{\{k,\ell\}}>0,
\]
contrary to \(W=0\).  This proves (6).

We next prove the support classification.  In the affine coordinates
\[
q_i=f_i+L_i,
\]
the three cutting lines \(q_i=0\) are pairwise nonparallel and
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-\sigma
\]
for some \(\sigma>0\).  Their seven chambers have adjacency graph consisting of the outer cycle
\[
\{3\},\{1,3\},\{1\},\{1,2\},\{2\},\{2,3\}
\tag{16}
\]
and the central chamber \(N\), adjacent exactly to the three doubleton chambers.

The positive-area support of a convex two-dimensional \(A_2\) is connected in this graph.  Indeed, positive area in a cell supplies a strict point of
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\) in that chamber.  The segment between two such points lies in
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\).  If it crosses one cutting line at a time, the successive chambers form an adjacency path.  If it meets two cutting lines simultaneously, a small ball about that interior point lies in \(A_2\) and meets all four local chambers, again supplying an adjacency path.  Three lines cannot meet because their coordinate sum is \(-\sigma\ne0\).

In the graph (16), the graph distance from \(\{j\}\) to
\(\{k,\ell\}\) is three.  Its four shortest paths are exactly
(7a)--(7d).  If \(N\) is absent from a connected support containing the two endpoints, the support lies in the outer six-cycle and must contain one of the two three-edge arcs between the antipodal vertices.  If \(N\) is present, then the supported edge \(N-\{k,\ell\}\), together with a supported neighbor of \(\{j\}\), gives either
\[
\{j\}-\{j,k\}-N-\{k,\ell\}
\]
or
\[
\{j\}-\{j,\ell\}-N-\{k,\ell\}.
\]
Thus every connected support containing the opposite pair contains one of (7a)--(7d).  This refines the chamber-filling conclusion of accepted fact `78d1d57e3961c4e4`.

For completeness, both minimal types are realizable at the \(Q\)-body level.  Normalize to
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-1
\]
and take \((j,k,\ell)=(3,1,2)\).  The segment joining
\[
x=\left(\frac1{10},\frac9{10},-2\right)
\quad\hbox{to}\quad
y=\left(-\frac{29}{10},-\frac1{10},2\right)
\]
crosses \(q_1=0,q_3=0,q_2=0\) at
\[
\frac1{30},\qquad\frac12,\qquad\frac9{10},
\]
so its successive strict chambers are
\[
\{3\},\{1,3\},\{1\},\{1,2\}.
\]
The segment joining the same \(x\) to
\[
y'=\left(-1,-\frac1{10},\frac1{10}\right)
\]
crosses \(q_1=0,q_2=0,q_3=0\) at
\[
\frac1{11},\qquad\frac9{10},\qquad\frac{20}{21},
\]
so its successive strict chambers are
\[
\{3\},\{1,3\},N,\{1,2\}.
\]
For either segment, its Minkowski sum with a sufficiently small centrally symmetric two-dimensional polygon meets exactly the four displayed chambers, each in positive area.  Interchanging the first two coordinates gives the other two paths.  This proves part 2.

Now assume \(p_\varnothing>0\) and define
\[
U=\frac{P_\varnothing+E}{2},\qquad
V=\frac{P_\varnothing+F}{2},\qquad
D=Z\cap\{-1<f_j<0\}.
\]
Accepted facts `c472ceb412ca0594` and `9922bcace1cdc113`, together with the exact incidence table in `0e51a47a9add571f`, give
\[
U\subseteq R_{\{j\}},
\tag{17}
\]
\[
V\subseteq R_{\{k\}}\sqcup R_{\{\ell\}}\sqcup R_{\{k,\ell\}},
\tag{18}
\]
\[
D\subseteq R_{\{j,k\}}\sqcup R_{\{j,\ell\}}\sqcup R_N,
\qquad |D|=s_j>0.
\tag{19}
\]
They are pairwise disjoint because they lie respectively in
\[
f_j<-1,\qquad f_j>0,\qquad -1<f_j<0.
\]
The weighted \(R\)-contribution is therefore at least
\[
|U|+|V|+4s_j.
\tag{20}
\]
The two endpoint Brunn--Minkowski bounds in accepted fact
`9922bcace1cdc113` give
\[
|U|\ge
\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_\varnothing}+
\sqrt{q_{\{j\}}}\right)^2,
\tag{21}
\]
\[
|V|\ge
\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_\varnothing}+
\sqrt{q_{\{k,\ell\}}}\right)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
The two opposite \(Q\)-cells contribute
\[
q_{\{j\}}+4q_{\{k,\ell\}}
\tag{23}
\]
to \(W\).  All uncounted nonempty-index cell contributions are nonnegative.  Since (15) and \(W=0\) say that the total positive contribution is exactly
\(2p_\varnothing\), adding (20)--(23) proves (8).

It remains to verify the scalar calibration.  From (9)--(12),
\[
M=1,\qquad m=\frac1{10},\qquad \kappa=1,
\tag{24}
\]
and
\[
(u_1,u_2,u_3)
=
\left(\frac{49}{1000},\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),
\tag{25}
\]
\[
(v_1,v_2,v_3)
=
\left(\frac{51}{1000},\frac1{20},\frac{99}{2000}\right),
\tag{26}
\]
\[
(c_1,c_2,c_3)
=
\left(\frac{11}{30},\frac{11}{30},
\frac{11}{30}+\frac1{2000}\right).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus all nine quantities in (25)--(27) are strictly between zero and their corresponding ambient totals.  Moreover,
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=\frac7{15}
=\frac29\left(1+\frac1{10}+1\right)
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{28}
\]
so all crossings hold.  The left tails are equalities for \(i=1,2\) and strict for \(i=3\):
\[
c_1+m=c_2+m=\frac7{15},\qquad
c_3+m=\frac7{15}+\frac1{2000}.
\tag{29}
\]
All right tails are strict because \(c_i+M>7/15\).

The three weighted contributions are
\[
W_P=-\frac{1553}{1000}=-\frac{3106}{2000},
\tag{30}
\]
\[
W_Q=q_1+q_3+4q_{12}+4q_{13}
=\frac{503}{2000},
\tag{31}
\]
\[
W_R=r_1+r_2+r_3+4r_{12}+4r_{13}
=\frac{2603}{2000}.
\tag{32}
\]
Hence \(W=0\) exactly.  The support (13) is the outer path in (7a), so it satisfies the connected-support requirement and the conclusion of
`78d1d57e3961c4e4`.

Finally set
\[
A=
\frac14\left(\sqrt{\frac{851}{1000}}+
\sqrt{\frac{49}{1000}}\right)^2,
\qquad
B=
\frac14\left(\sqrt{\frac{851}{1000}}+
\sqrt{\frac1{20}}\right)^2.
\]
Since \(\sqrt{851}<30\) and \(\sqrt{1702}<42\),
\[
A<\frac{33}{100},\qquad
B<\frac{1321}{4000}.
\tag{33}
\]
The separate target capacities satisfy
\[
r_3=\frac{1097}{3000}>A,
\tag{34}
\]
\[
r_1+r_2+r_{12}
=\frac{3797}{6000}>B,
\tag{35}
\]
\[
r_{13}=\frac3{2000}>s_3=\frac1{1000},
\tag{36}
\]
and
\[
\kappa=1>A+B+s_3.
\tag{37}
\]
The necessary budget (8) also has slack:
\[
q_3+4q_{12}+A+B+4s_3
<
\frac{3653}{4000}
<
2p_\varnothing.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (24)--(38) prove only the claimed scalar consistency and separate capacity checks.  They do not supply a geometric realization of the table.  This completes the proof.
