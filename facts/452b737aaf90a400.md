## Statement

In the setting of the locked problem, put \(m=\min(a_0,a_2)\) and \(M=\max(a_0,a_2)\). Let \(X_0,X_2\) be independent and uniformly distributed on \(A_0,A_2\), let \(\widehat\mu\) be the probability law of \((X_0+X_2)/2\), and define
\[
\nu(B)=|A_1\cap B|+m\widehat\mu(B)
\]
for Borel \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^2\). For \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), define
\[
G_i^u(c)=\left|A_i\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq c\}\right|,
\]
and define the unnormalized closed-halfplane depth
\[
d_\nu(z)=\inf\{\nu(Q):Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ is a closed halfplane and }z\in Q\}.
\]
Then, for every \(z\in A_1\),
\[
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}\left[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+\inf_{\substack{c_0,c_2\in\mathbb R\\c_0+c_2=2\langle u,z\rangle}}
\bigl(G_0^u(c_0)+G_2^u(c_2)\bigr)
\right]\geq d_\nu(z).
\]
Moreover, some \(z\in A_1\) satisfies \(d_\nu(z)\geq(a_1+m)/3\). Consequently, if \(2M\leq a_1+m\), then some \(z\in A_1\) satisfies
\[
h_S((1,z))\geq\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
The uniform law on \(A_i\) has density \(\mathbf 1_{A_i}/a_i\), so \(\widehat\mu\) is a probability measure. The displayed inclusion shows that \(\widehat\mu\), and hence \(\nu\), is supported on \(A_1\). Thus
\[
\nu(\mathbb R^2)=a_1+m.
\]
Also, \(\nu\) gives every affine line measure zero. Indeed, the Lebesgue part does so, and, conditional on \(X_2\), the event \((X_0+X_2)/2\in L\) requires \(X_0\in 2L-X_2\), an affine line of \(X_0\)-probability zero. Integrating over \(X_2\) proves \(\widehat\mu(L)=0\).

Fix \(z\in A_1\), \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), and put \(t=\langle u,z\rangle\). For arbitrary \(c_0+c_2=2t\), set \(U_i=\langle u,X_i\rangle\). Uniform normalization gives
\[
G_i^u(c_i)=a_i\mathbb P(U_i\leq c_i)\qquad(i=0,2).
\]
The closed-inequality event inclusion
\[
\{U_0+U_2\leq c_0+c_2\}
\subseteq
\{U_0\leq c_0\}\cup\{U_2\leq c_2\}
\]
is exact: if both inequalities on the right fail, then \(U_0+U_2>c_0+c_2\). Since \(m\leq a_0,a_2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_0^u(c_0)+G_2^u(c_2)
&\geq m\bigl(\mathbb P(U_0\leq c_0)+\mathbb P(U_2\leq c_2)\bigr)\\
&\geq m\mathbb P(U_0+U_2\leq2t)\\
&=m\widehat\mu\bigl(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq t\}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding \(G_1^u(t)\) makes the right-hand side
\[
\nu\bigl(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle\}\bigr),
\]
the mass of a closed halfplane containing \(z\), and therefore at least \(d_\nu(z)\). Taking first the infimum over \(c_0,c_2\) and then over \(u\) proves the claimed hybrid lower bound.

It remains to produce a centerpoint inside \(A_1\). Write \(N=a_1+m\) and \(K=A_1\). Consider all closed halfplanes \(H\) satisfying \(\nu(H)>2N/3\). The intersections \(K\cap H\) have the finite-intersection property. First, any at most three of them intersect: otherwise \(K\) would be covered by at most three complements \(H^c\), each of measure less than \(N/3\), contradicting \(\nu(K)=N\).

For completeness, the needed planar finite Helly step follows directly from linear algebra. If a finite family of compact convex sets, every at most three of which intersect, had empty intersection, choose a minimal empty subfamily \(C_1,\ldots,C_r\), where \(r\geq4\), and points \(p_i\in\bigcap_{j\neq i}C_j\). The four vectors \((p_i,1)\in\mathbb R^3\), \(1\leq i\leq4\), are linearly dependent. Separating the positive and negative coefficients and normalizing gives a point belonging to the convex hull of each of two disjoint subsets of \(\{p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4\}\). For every \(C_j\), at least one of those two subsets omits \(p_j\), while if \(j>4\), both do. Convexity then puts that point in every \(C_j\), a contradiction. Hence every finite collection of the sets \(K\cap H\) intersects. Since they are closed subsets of compact \(K\), their total intersection is nonempty. Choose \(z\) in it; in particular, \(z\in A_1\).

Every closed halfplane \(Q\) containing this \(z\) has \(\nu(Q)\geq N/3\). Otherwise, after orienting \(Q\), the boundary-through-\(z\) halfplane
\[
Q_z=\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle\}\subseteq Q
\]
also has measure less than \(N/3\). Because every affine line is \(\nu\)-null, the function \(r\mapsto\nu(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq r\})\) is continuous. Hence some \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfies
\[
\nu\bigl(\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\leq\langle u,z\rangle+\varepsilon\}\bigr)<\frac N3.
\]
The opposite closed halfplane
\[
H=\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\geq\langle u,z\rangle+\varepsilon\}
\]
then has measure greater than \(2N/3\), again because its boundary line is null, but it does not contain \(z\). This contradicts the choice of \(z\) in every such \(H\). Thus \(d_\nu(z)\geq N/3\).

Accepted fact `9e512193da02119e` gives the exact middle-slice formula: \(h_S((1,z))\) is the minimum of the displayed tilted expression and the two slice-parallel masses
\[
a_0+a_1,\qquad a_1+a_2.
\]
The halfspaces are closed, so the slice-parallel halfspaces \(x\leq1\) and \(x\geq1\) include all of \(A_1\); these are therefore the exact horizontal terms, not limits of tilted halfspaces. Since \(\{a_0,a_2\}=\{m,M\}\), the two terms are \(a_1+m=N\) and \(a_1+M\geq N\), while the tilted term at the centerpoint is at least \(N/3\).

Finally, \(T=a_1+m+M=N+M\), and the assumed regime \(2M\leq N\) gives
\[
\frac N3-\frac{2T}{9}
=\frac{N-2M}{9}\geq0.
\]
Thus the tilted term is at least \(2T/9\), and both exact horizontal terms are at least \(N\geq N/3\geq2T/9\). Their minimum is \(h_S((1,z))\), proving the result.
