## Statement

Let \(\gamma>2\), and let \(L_1,L_2,L_3\) be arbitrary real offsets satisfying
\[
L_1+L_2+L_3=1-\gamma.
\]
There exist nonconstant affine functions \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
and an area-one two-dimensional triangle \(A_2\) whose exact cells in the convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` satisfy
\[
q_N=\frac25,\qquad q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15,
\]
with every other \(q_H=0\). Hence the \(A_2\)-source portion of the strict rational calibration in accepted fact `c5aad5c9408fcf74` is feasible for every admissible offset triple.

Writing
\[
T_\gamma=\{x:f_i(x)+L_i\le0\ (i=1,2,3)\},
\qquad
T_1=\{x:f_i(x)\le0\ (i=1,2,3)\},
\]
the construction has
\[
|T_\gamma|=\left(2-\frac3{\sqrt5}\right)^2,
\qquad
|T_1|=\frac1{\gamma^2}
\left(2-\frac3{\sqrt5}\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, for \(\gamma=101/50\),
\[
|T_1|>\frac1{10}.
\tag{2}
\]
No \(A_0\), \(K\), or full common-plane realization is asserted.

## Proof

Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be any triangle of area one, and let
\[
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3
\]
be its barycentric coordinate functions. Thus
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1,
\qquad
Q=\{x:\alpha_i(x)\ge0\ (i=1,2,3)\}.
\]
Put
\[
c=1-\frac1{\sqrt5},
\qquad
d=3c-1=2-\frac3{\sqrt5}.
\]
Both \(c>1/2\) and \(d>0\). Define
\[
\lambda=\frac{\gamma}{d},
\qquad
\ell_i(x)=\lambda\bigl(\alpha_i(x)-c\bigr),
\qquad
f_i(x)=\ell_i(x)-L_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\),
\[
\sum_i\ell_i
=\lambda(1-3c)
=-\lambda d
=-\gamma.
\]
The offset identity therefore gives
\[
\sum_i f_i
=-\gamma-\sum_iL_i
=-\gamma-(1-\gamma)
=-1.
\]
The linear parts of the barycentric coordinates of a nondegenerate triangle are pairwise nonparallel, so the same is true of the \(f_i\). Finally,
\[
f_i+L_i=\ell_i,
\]
so the exact \(Q\)-cell signs are simply the signs of \(\alpha_i-c\).

Set \(A_2=Q\). For each \(i\), the region
\[
C_i=Q\cap\{\ell_i>0\}
=Q\cap\{\alpha_i>c\}
\]
is the corner triangle at the \(i\)-th vertex. It is homothetic to \(Q\) with linear ratio
\[
1-c=\frac1{\sqrt5},
\]
and therefore
\[
|C_i|=\frac15.
\tag{4}
\]
The three cap interiors are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if both
\(\alpha_i>c\) and \(\alpha_j>c\), then
\[
\alpha_i+\alpha_j>2c>1,
\]
contradicting \(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=1\) and the nonnegativity of the remaining barycentric coordinate on \(Q\).

A point of \(Q\) outside
\[
Q\cap T_\gamma
=Q\cap\{\alpha_i\le c\ (i=1,2,3)\}
\]
therefore lies in exactly one of the three caps. Cutting lines have area zero, so (4) gives
\[
|Q\cap T_\gamma|
=1-\sum_i|C_i|
=1-\frac35
=\frac25.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, no point of \(Q\) has two positive \(\ell_i\)-coordinates. Hence the only nonempty open exact cells are \(Q_N\) and the three doubletons, with
\[
|Q_N|=\frac25,\qquad
|Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}|=|C_i|=\frac15.
\]
This proves the exact source table, including all zero cells and the weak/strict boundary convention.

The same barycentric description gives the global central triangle exactly. A point lies in \(T_\gamma\) if and only if
\[
\alpha_i\le c\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
For such a point put
\[
\eta_i=\frac{c-\alpha_i}{d}.
\]
Then \(\eta_i\ge0\) and
\[
\sum_i\eta_i=\frac{3c-1}{d}=1.
\]
Conversely every barycentric vector \((\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3)\) with nonnegative entries and sum one yields
\[
\alpha_i=c-d\eta_i
\]
and hence a point of \(T_\gamma\). This affine map has linear ratio \(d\) on the two-dimensional barycentric plane. Since \(|Q|=1\),
\[
|T_\gamma|=d^2
=\left(2-\frac3{\sqrt5}\right)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The central source cell in (5) is the intersection \(Q\cap T_\gamma\); unlike the equal-quarter case in accepted fact `9e30bd5f26892d6a`, it is a proper subset of the global triangle.

For completeness, the global middle triangle is related to \(T_\gamma\) by the affine scaling used in accepted fact `9e30bd5f26892d6a`. Under the affine isomorphism
\[
x\longmapsto(f_1(x),f_2(x),f_3(x)),
\]
\(T_1\) maps to the standard negative simplex of coordinate sum \(-1\). If \(x\in T_\gamma\), then
\[
w_i=f_i(x)+L_i\le0,
\qquad
\sum_iw_i=-\gamma.
\]
Thus the image of \(T_\gamma\) is a translate of the dilation by factor \(\gamma\) of the image of \(T_1\). Consequently
\[
|T_\gamma|=\gamma^2|T_1|.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)-(7) prove (1).

Now take the admissible value
\[
\gamma=\frac{101}{50}>2.
\]
First,
\[
d^2=\frac{29}{5}-\frac{12}{\sqrt5}>\frac{41}{100}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, (8) is equivalent to
\[
\frac{539}{100}>\frac{12}{\sqrt5},
\]
and both sides are positive, while
\[
5\cdot539^2=1452605>1440000=144\cdot10000.
\]
Also
\[
\gamma^2=\frac{10201}{2500}<\frac{41}{10}.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (7)-(9) gives
\[
|T_1|
=\frac{d^2}{\gamma^2}
>
\frac{41/100}{41/10}
=\frac1{10},
\]
which proves (2). Thus even the ambient middle central triangle has enough area to contain a middle central cell of area \(r_N=1/10\) at this admissible offset scale.

Finally, the cap data are strictly inside both inequalities of accepted fact `8234d1112ec2f031`:
\[
\left(\frac25\right)^2\cdot1
=\frac4{25}
>
\frac4{125}
=4\left(\frac15\right)^3,
\]
and
\[
3\sqrt{\frac15}
=\frac3{\sqrt5}
<
\frac32.
\]
Therefore its equality characterization supplies no triangular-medial rigidity here, exactly as the construction demonstrates.
