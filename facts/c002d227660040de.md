## Statement

Let \(s,a,b,\delta>0\), and let \(P\) be a strictly convex rational polygon
with clockwise vertices
\[
p_0=(-a,\delta+a),\qquad p_1=(\delta+b,-b),\qquad
p_2,\ldots,p_{N-1},
\]
where \(N\geq5\).  Assume every \(p_j\), \(j\geq2\), has positive
coordinates and lies strictly below \(x+y=\delta\).  Thus \(p_0\) is the
only vertex with \(x<0\), \(p_1\) is the only vertex with \(y<0\), and
\(p_0p_1\) is the support edge for \(x+y\).

There is no such polygon satisfying the complete four-condition system
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\leq0\}|=\frac{s}{5},\qquad
\delta<a+b.
\tag{1}
\]

## Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that all four conditions in (1) hold.  Apply
the affine map
\[
\Phi(x,y)=(\xi,h)=(x+a,\delta-x-y).
\tag{2}
\]
Its linear part has determinant \(-1\), so it preserves area and reverses
orientation.  Put
\[
\widetilde P=\Phi(P),\qquad L=\delta+a+b.
\tag{3}
\]
The first two vertices become
\[
q_0=(0,0),\qquad q_1=(L,0).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(j=2,\ldots,N-1\), write \(q_j=(\xi_j,h_j)\).  Since both
coordinates of \(p_j\) are positive and \(x(p_j)+y(p_j)<\delta\),
\[
\xi_j>a,\qquad h_j>0,\qquad
\xi_j+h_j=L-b-y(p_j)<L-b.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
0<\xi_j<L.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(q_0\) and \(q_1\) are respectively the unique leftmost and rightmost
vertices of \(\widetilde P\), \(q_0q_1\) is its bottom edge, and all other
vertices lie strictly above that edge.  Convexity therefore gives a
nonnegative concave piecewise-affine function \(f:[0,L]\to\mathbb R\)
such that
\[
\widetilde P\cap\{\xi=\tau\}
=\{(\tau,h):0\leq h\leq f(\tau)\},
\qquad
f(0)=f(L)=0.
\tag{7}
\]

Let the two vertices adjacent to the endpoints along the upper boundary
be
\[
q_{N-1}=(E,v),\qquad q_2=(C,u).
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (5)-(6) give
\[
E>a,\qquad v>0,\qquad C<L,\qquad u>0.
\tag{9}
\]
The two endpoint edges of the upper boundary yield
\[
f(\tau)=\alpha\tau\quad(0\leq\tau\leq E),
\qquad
f(\tau)=\beta(L-\tau)\quad(C\leq\tau\leq L),
\tag{10}
\]
where
\[
\alpha=\frac{v}{E}>0,\qquad
\beta=\frac{u}{L-C}>0.
\tag{11}
\]

The original line \(x=0\) becomes \(\xi=a\).  Since \(E>a\), the portion
of \(\widetilde P\) with \(\xi\leq a\) lies below the first endpoint edge
in (10) and is a triangle.  The second condition in (1) is therefore
\[
\frac{\alpha a^2}{2}=\frac{s}{8},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{s}{4a^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

The inverse of (2) gives
\[
y=L-b-\xi-h.
\tag{13}
\]
Hence \(y=0\) becomes \(h=L-b-\xi\).  On the right endpoint edge, set
\(r=L-\xi\).  Its upper boundary is \(h=\beta r\), whereas \(y=0\) is
\(h=r-b\).  At \(q_2\), positivity of the original \(y\)-coordinate gives
\[
C+u<L-b,
\tag{14}
\]
so
\[
(1-\beta)(L-C)=L-C-u>b.
\]
Thus \(0<\beta<1\), and
\[
0<\frac{b}{1-\beta}<L-C.
\]
The two lines therefore meet in the relative interior of \(q_1q_2\).
Their intersection is
\[
r=\frac{b}{1-\beta},
\qquad
h=\frac{b\beta}{1-\beta}.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently the portion with \(y\leq0\) is the triangle whose horizontal
base on \(q_0q_1\) has length \(b\) and whose height is the second
quantity in (15).  The third condition in (1) is therefore
\[
\frac{b^2\beta}{2(1-\beta)}=\frac{s}{5},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{2s}{5b^2+2s}.
\tag{16}
\]

Concavity bounds every intermediate vertex, regardless of their number.
For \(E<\tau\leq L\), concavity between \(0\) and \(\tau\), evaluated at
\(E\), gives
\[
f(E)\geq\frac{E}{\tau}f(\tau),
\]
and hence \(f(\tau)\leq\alpha\tau\).  Equality already holds on
\([0,E]\).  Applying the same argument to the concave function
\(r\mapsto f(L-r)\) gives the right-endpoint bound.  Therefore
\[
f(\tau)\leq
\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}
\qquad(0\leq\tau\leq L).
\tag{17}
\]
The two lines in (17) meet at
\(\tau=\beta L/(\alpha+\beta)\).  Since \(\Phi\) preserves area,
integration of this tent, followed by (12) and (16), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P|
&=|\widetilde P|
=\int_0^L f(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\leq
\int_0^L\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{\alpha\beta L^2}{2(\alpha+\beta)}
=\frac{L^2}{2(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1})}\\
&=\frac{sL^2}{8a^2+5b^2+2s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Using the first condition \(|P|=16s\) in (18) gives
\[
L^2\geq16(8a^2+5b^2+2s)
=128a^2+80b^2+32s.
\tag{19}
\]
This lower bound is strictly larger than \(4(a+b)^2\), because
\[
\begin{aligned}
&128a^2+80b^2+32s-4(a+b)^2\\
&\qquad=120a^2+72b^2+4(a-b)^2+32s>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(L,a,b>0\), equations (19)-(20) imply
\[
L>2(a+b).
\]
By \(L=\delta+a+b\), this is
\[
\delta>a+b.
\tag{21}
\]
Equation (21) contradicts the fourth condition \(\delta<a+b\) in (1).
Therefore no polygon satisfying the complete system (1) exists.  The
proof uses only convexity and the declared support and sign conditions,
so it applies uniformly to every finite \(N\geq5\).
