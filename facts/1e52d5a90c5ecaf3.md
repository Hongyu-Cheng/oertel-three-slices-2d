## Statement

Assume the full central common-order hypotheses and notation of accepted
facts `03d5b07df78ee595` and `2b42506f5de09be1`.  Thus the positive cell
areas are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &P_j&P_0&P_\ell&P_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &a&b&c&d
\end{array},
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &Q_j&Q_{jk}&Q_N&Q_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &e&g_0&g&s
\end{array},
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &R_j&R_{jk}&R_N&R_{k\ell}\\ \hline
 &x&y&z&w ,
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
all twelve entries are positive, and
\[
\delta>0,\qquad\Delta>0.
\]
The exact crossing and weight identities are
\[
c=g_0+y,\qquad
a+e+x=d+s+w,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
2b=5(g_0+y+d+s+w)+7(g+z).
\tag{3}
\]

Put
\[
\eta_j=\sqrt{a(a+b)}-a,\qquad
\eta_k=\sqrt{d(d+b)}-d,
\]
\[
\eta_\ell
=\sqrt{(c+d)(b+c+d)}-(c+d),
\tag{4}
\]
\[
L_i=\frac{2\Delta}{\delta}\eta_i
\qquad(i=j,k,\ell),
\qquad
q_*=3-2\sqrt2.
\tag{5}
\]
Consider the complete trace relaxation supplied by the two accepted
facts:
\[
\lambda_{0i}\ge L_i,\qquad
\lambda_{2i}\ge\lambda_{0i},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\lambda_{2i}>\lambda_{0i}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\lambda_{0i}>L_i
\qquad(i=j,k,\ell),
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}\le2\Delta g_0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}<2\Delta cq_*.
\tag{9}
\]
There exist six nonnegative trace values satisfying (6)-(9) if and
only if
\[
L_kL_\ell<2\Delta g_0,
\qquad
L_kL_\ell<2\Delta cq_*.
\tag{10}
\]
Equivalently, after eliminating all six traces,
\[
2\Delta\eta_k\eta_\ell<g_0\delta^2,
\qquad
2\Delta\eta_k\eta_\ell<cq_*\delta^2,
\tag{11}
\]
or, using the first identity in (2),
\[
2\Delta\eta_k\eta_\ell
<
\delta^2\min\{c-y,q_*c\}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is the strongest pure cell-area condition obtained from
(6)-(9), at the fixed canonical scale \((\delta,\Delta)\): it is both
necessary and sufficient for that trace relaxation.
The \(j\)-trace pair imposes no further cell-area restriction because
no accepted upper bound in (8)-(9) contains it.

The strict area condition (12) does not contradict the positive scalar
identities and tails.  An exact calibration, different from the
discarded equality-trace data, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
P&a&b&c&d\\ \hline
 &1&59/2&3&1
\end{array},
\quad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
Q&e&g_0&g&s\\ \hline
 &4&1&1&2
\end{array},
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
R&x&y&z&w\\ \hline
 &1&2&1&3 .
\end{array}
\tag{13}
\]
Take
\[
\delta=100,\qquad\Delta=1,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\lambda_{0j}=\lambda_{0k}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}=\frac15,
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\lambda_{2j}=\lambda_{2k}=\frac15,
\qquad
\lambda_{2\ell}=\frac14.
\tag{16}
\]
All three trace gaps in (7) are strict, all three sharp lower bounds in
(6) have strict slack, and (8)-(9) have strict slack.  This is an exact
calibration of the accepted scalar and trace inequalities only.  It
does not assert that the three \(A_0\) profiles, the three \(A_2\)
profiles, the retained axis intervals, or a compatibility separator
arise simultaneously from common convex polygons.

## Proof

Accepted fact `03d5b07df78ee595` gives, in every direction,
\[
\lambda_{0i}\ge L_i
\]
and the strict disjunction (7).  Accepted fact
`2b42506f5de09be1` gives the non-strict trace comparisons, the retained
chord product (8), and the optimized bulging product (9).

For fixed \(i\), conditions (6)-(7) are equivalent to
\[
\lambda_{0i}\ge L_i,\qquad
\lambda_{2i}\ge\lambda_{0i},\qquad
\lambda_{2i}>L_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, if \(\lambda_{2i}>\lambda_{0i}\), then
\(\lambda_{2i}>L_i\).  Otherwise
\(\lambda_{2i}=\lambda_{0i}\), and (7) forces
\(\lambda_{0i}>L_i\).  Conversely, if (17) holds, then either
\(\lambda_{2i}>\lambda_{0i}\), or equality of the two traces forces
\(\lambda_{0i}=\lambda_{2i}>L_i\).

Applying (17) to \(k,\ell\) in (8) gives
\[
L_kL_\ell
<
\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}
\le2\Delta g_0.
\tag{18}
\]
Applying the lower bounds for the \(A_0\)-traces in (9) gives
\[
L_kL_\ell
\le\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}
<2\Delta cq_*.
\tag{19}
\]
This proves necessity of (10).

Conversely, suppose (10) holds.  Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently
small that
\[
(L_k+\varepsilon)(L_\ell+\varepsilon)<2\Delta g_0.
\]
Set
\[
\lambda_{0k}=L_k,\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}=L_\ell,
\]
\[
\lambda_{2k}=L_k+\varepsilon,\qquad
\lambda_{2\ell}=L_\ell+\varepsilon.
\]
Then (6)-(8) hold, while the second inequality in (10) gives (9).
For the remaining direction, set
\[
\lambda_{0j}=L_j,\qquad
\lambda_{2j}=L_j+\varepsilon.
\]
Every disjunction in (7) holds by its strict trace-gap alternative.
Thus (10) is also sufficient.  Substitution of (5) proves (11), and
\(g_0=c-y\) from (2) proves (12).

We next verify the exact calibration.  From (13),
\[
M=\frac{69}{2},\qquad m=8,\qquad\kappa=7,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9=11.
\tag{20}
\]
The three cap triples are
\[
(u_j,v_j,C_j)=(1,6,4),\qquad
(u_k,v_k,C_k)=(1,4,6),
\]
\[
(u_\ell,v_\ell,C_\ell)=(4,3,4).
\tag{21}
\]
Thus all three crossings equal \(t\); every cap is positive and proper.
The left-tail values are
\[
C_j+m=12,\qquad C_k+m=14,\qquad C_\ell+m=12,
\]
and every right tail is strict because \(M>t\).  Also \(M\ge m\) and
\(0<t<M\).

The weighted contributions are
\[
W_P=-51,\qquad W_Q=23,\qquad W_R=28,
\]
so \(W=0\).  Directly,
\[
c=g_0+y=3,\qquad
a+e+x=d+s+w=6,
\]
\[
2b=59
=5(g_0+y+d+s+w)+7(g+z).
\]
Hence (2)-(3) hold exactly.

For (13),
\[
\eta_j=\eta_k=\sqrt{\frac{61}{2}}-1,
\qquad
\eta_\ell=\sqrt{134}-4.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (14)-(15) give
\[
\lambda_{0j}\delta=\lambda_{0k}\delta=10
>2\left(\sqrt{\frac{61}{2}}-1\right),
\]
because \(\sqrt{61/2}<6\), and
\[
\lambda_{0\ell}\delta=20
>2(\sqrt{134}-4),
\]
because \(\sqrt{134}<14\).  Thus every sharp lower bound in (6) is
strict.  Equations (15)-(16) give a strict trace gap in all three
directions.  Moreover,
\[
\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}
=\frac1{20}<2=2\Delta g_0,
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}
=\frac1{50}
<6(3-2\sqrt2)
=2\Delta cq_*.
\tag{24}
\]
For example, (24) follows from
\(3-2\sqrt2>1/6\).  This verifies every inequality and strict
disjunction in (6)-(9), and hence (12).

The unoptimized separator-area inequalities also have slack at the
level of scalar row data.  Taking formal separator intercepts
\(\alpha=\beta=3\) gives
\[
\Delta c=3<\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}=\frac92,
\]
\[
\frac12\left(
\alpha\lambda_{0k}+\beta\lambda_{0\ell}
+\lambda_{0k}\lambda_{0\ell}
\right)
=\frac{23}{50}<3.
\]
If the two retained axis intervals start at those intercepts, their
convex-hull area using the \(A_2\)-traces is
\[
\frac12\left(
3\lambda_{2k}+3\lambda_{2\ell}
+\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}
\right)
=\frac7{10}<g_0=1.
\]
These are formal row checks, not a separator arising from actual
polygons.

Each direction separately passes the known marginal profile checks.
The accepted sharp-chord construction applies to the three
\((u_i,b,\lambda_{0i})\) triples because the inequalities above are
strict.  For \(A_2\), a nonnegative concave triangular profile with
trace \(\lambda_{2i}\), left area \(\Delta v_i\), and right area
\(\Delta(m-v_i)\) is obtained by taking support lengths
\[
\frac{2\Delta v_i}{\lambda_{2i}},
\qquad
\frac{2\Delta(m-v_i)}{\lambda_{2i}}
\]
on the two sides of zero.  Its left slope is positive and its right
slope is negative, so concavity holds.  Because every trace gap is
strict, the genuine-crossing alternative imposes no equality-case slope
condition.

What remains unproved is joint realization: the three marginal chord
profiles must be sections of one \(A_0\) and one \(A_2\) under the
linearly dependent coordinates
\[
p_j+p_k+p_\ell=\delta,\qquad
q_j+q_k+q_\ell=-(\delta+2),
\]
while the same \(A_2\) must place the two retained \(Q_{jk}\) axis
intervals and the same pair \(A_0,A_2\) must generate a genuine
nondegenerate compatibility separator.  None of (6)-(12) couples the
three profile slopes, breakpoints, or axis offsets inside a common
polygon.  This is the first genuinely joint profile condition still
missing, and the scalar calibration above makes no claim that it is
satisfied.
