## Statement

Let \(T\) be a nondegenerate triangle in
\[
H_s:=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=s\},
\qquad s>0,
\]
let \(g\) be its area centroid, and define
\[
w_i:=\frac{|T\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|}{|T|}
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Then:

1. For every \(i\),
\[
g_i<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\ w_i<\frac59.\ }
\tag{1}
\]
The constant \(5/9\) is sharp as a supremum.

2. For distinct \(i,j,k\), put
\[
R_{ij}:=T\cap\{x_i>0,\ x_j>0,\ x_k<0\},
\]
and put
\[
R_{123}:=T\cap\{x_1>0,\ x_2>0,\ x_3>0\}.
\]
If
\[
|R_{12}|>0,\qquad |R_{13}|>0,\qquad |R_{23}|>0,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\ |R_{123}|>0.\ }
\tag{2}
\]

In the singleton-singleton orbit of accepted facts `64760c76cadff481` and `a5bbbf5d61041b23`,
\[
I_0=\{1\}
\]
implies
\[
u_2,u_3<\frac59.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the proposed missing-\(X_3\) endpoint cap vector
\[
(u_1,u_2,u_3)
=
\left(\frac{697}{900},\frac{247}{900},\frac{697}{900}\right)
\tag{4}
\]
is impossible, because \(697/900>5/9\).  The accompanying proposed sign-cell allocation is independently impossible: it assigns the positive areas
\[
|R_{12}|=\frac{11}{2250},\qquad
|R_{13}|=\frac{652991}{9000000},\qquad
|R_{23}|=\frac{44009}{9000000},
\]
but assigns \(|R_{123}|=0\), contrary to (2).

## Proof

Fix a coordinate \(i\).  Its restriction to \(T\) is a nonconstant affine functional: otherwise the two-dimensional triangle \(T\) would lie in the intersection of the distinct planes \(H_s\) and \(x_i=\mathrm{constant}\), which is a line.  Let
\[
a\leq b\leq c
\]
be its three vertex values.  The centroid value is
\[
g_i=\frac{a+b+c}{3}.
\tag{5}
\]
Assume \(g_i<0\), so
\[
a+b+c<0.
\tag{6}
\]
The complete triangle-cap formulas of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` give three exhaustive sign chambers at threshold \(0\).

If \(c\leq0\), the nonnegative cap is contained in a support point or support edge and has area zero.  Hence \(w_i=0<5/9\).

Suppose \(b\leq0<c\).  Put
\[
x=-a\geq y=-b\geq0.
\]
Equation (6) gives \(c<x+y\), and the unique-positive-vertex cap formula is
\[
w_i=\frac{c^2}{(c+x)(c+y)}.
\tag{7}
\]
The right side of (7) is strictly increasing in \(c>0\).  Therefore
\[
w_i
<
\frac{(x+y)^2}{(2x+y)(x+2y)}
\leq\frac12<\frac59,
\tag{8}
\]
where the middle inequality is equivalent to \(0\leq xy\).  This includes \(b=0\) and the repeated lower value \(a=b\).

It remains to consider \(a<0\leq b\leq c\).  Put \(x=-a>0\) and \(r=b+c\).  Equation (6) gives \(0\leq r<x\).  The two-positive-vertex cap formula is
\[
w_i
=
1-\frac{x^2}{(x+b)(x+c)}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(bc\leq r^2/4<x^2/4\),
\[
(x+b)(x+c)
=x^2+xr+bc
<\frac94x^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) give \(w_i<5/9\).  This includes \(b=0\) and the repeated upper value \(b=c\).  The three cases prove (1), including all zero and repeated-value boundaries.

For sharpness, take vertex values
\[
(-2-\varepsilon,1,1)
\qquad(\varepsilon>0).
\]
For example, these are the first-coordinate values of the nondegenerate triangle in \(H_1\) with vertices
\[
(-2-\varepsilon,3+\varepsilon,0),\qquad
(1,-1,1),\qquad
(1,0,0).
\]
Their mean is negative, while their cap fraction is
\[
1-\frac{(2+\varepsilon)^2}{(3+\varepsilon)^2}
\longrightarrow\frac59
\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]

We next prove (2).  Let
\[
\Delta_s:=H_s\cap\{x_1\geq0,\ x_2\geq0,\ x_3\geq0\}.
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(|R_{123}|=0\).  Then the relative interiors of the two convex bodies \(T\) and \(\Delta_s\) are disjoint.  Indeed, any common relative-interior point would have a relative neighborhood contained in both bodies and would give \(|R_{123}|>0\).

Two planar convex bodies with disjoint relative interiors admit a common separating line.  Thus, after reversing its normal if necessary, there is a nonconstant vector \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)\) such that
\[
q\cdot x\geq sM\quad(x\in T),
\qquad
q\cdot z\leq sM\quad(z\in\Delta_s),
\tag{11}
\]
where
\[
M=\max\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}.
\]
Choose \(k\) with \(q_k=M\), and let \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).  By hypothesis, \(R_{ij}\) has positive area, so it contains a point \(x\) with
\[
x_i>0,\qquad x_j>0,\qquad x_k<0.
\]
Using \(x_1+x_2+x_3=s\),
\[
q\cdot x-sM
=
(q_i-M)x_i+(q_j-M)x_j<0.
\tag{12}
\]
The inequality is strict because \(q\) is nonconstant, both \(x_i,x_j\) are positive, and at least one of \(q_i,q_j\) is strictly below \(M\).  Equation (12) contradicts the first inequality in (11).  Hence the relative interiors intersect, and their intersection is an open planar set of positive area.  This proves (2).

Finally, accepted fact `a5bbbf5d61041b23` gives
\[
(g_0)_2<0,\qquad(g_0)_3<0
\]
in the orbit \(I_0=\{1\}\).  Applying (1) to \(T=P_0\) proves (3).  Since
\[
\frac{697}{900}-\frac59
=\frac{197}{900}>0,
\]
the cap vector (4) cannot arise from \(P_0\).  The three displayed pair-cell areas are all strictly positive, so (2) separately excludes their zero triple-cell assignment.  Thus the proposed scalar/support survivor fails triangle realizability before any compatibility or matrix reconstruction question arises.
