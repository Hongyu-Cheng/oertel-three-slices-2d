## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=[x_0,x_0+w_0]\times[y_0,y_0+h_0],
\qquad
A_2=[x_2,x_2+w_2]\times[y_2,y_2+h_2],
\tag{1}
\]
where all four side lengths are positive rational numbers, and set
\[
K=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Write
\[
M=w_0h_0,\qquad m=w_2h_2,\qquad
k=|K|=\frac{(w_0+w_2)(h_0+h_2)}4,
\tag{3}
\]
relabeling so that \(M\geq m\), and put
\[
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\tag{4}
\]
Assume the exact residual area domain of accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e`.

In the shared coordinates
\[
g_i=x,\qquad g_\ell=y,\qquad g_q=1-x-y,
\tag{5}
\]
put \(f_j=-g_j\), and define
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\leq0\}|,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\mu_j(a)
=c_j+|A_0\cap\{f_j\leq a\}|
       +|A_2\cap\{f_j\leq-a\}|,
\qquad
I_j=\{a:\mu_j(a)<t\}.
\tag{7}
\]
If
\[
c_q+m<t,
\tag{8}
\]
then
\[
I_i=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad I_\ell=\varnothing.
\tag{9}
\]

More precisely, define
\[
P=w_0+w_2,\qquad H=h_0+h_2,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
R_x=x_0+w_0+x_2+w_2=2\max_K x,
\qquad
R_y=y_0+h_0+y_2+h_2=2\max_K y.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(L>0\), write
\[
[z]_L=\min\{L,\max\{0,z\}\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then the two middle caps are exactly
\[
c_i=\frac H4[R_x]_P,\qquad
c_\ell=\frac P4[R_y]_H.
\tag{13}
\]
The two exact optimized endpoint sums are
\[
\inf_a\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-a\}|
\right)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&R_x\leq0,\\[2pt]
\gamma_xR_x,&0\leq R_x\leq m/\gamma_x,\\[2pt]
m,&R_x\geq m/\gamma_x,
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
\gamma_x=\min\{h_0,h_2\},
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
\inf_a\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_\ell\leq a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_\ell\leq-a\}|
\right)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&R_y\leq0,\\[2pt]
\gamma_yR_y,&0\leq R_y\leq m/\gamma_y,\\[2pt]
m,&R_y\geq m/\gamma_y,
\end{cases}
\tag{16}
\]
where
\[
\gamma_y=\min\{w_0,w_2\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The formulas agree at every displayed equality endpoint.  In particular,
because \(I_j\) uses a strict inequality, equality of either exact minimum
\(\inf_a\mu_j(a)\) with \(t\) makes \(I_j\) empty.

## Proof

First compute the midpoint rectangle from (1):
\[
K=
\left[
\frac{x_0+x_2}{2},
\frac{x_0+x_2+w_0+w_2}{2}
\right]
\times
\left[
\frac{y_0+y_2}{2},
\frac{y_0+y_2+h_0+h_2}{2}
\right].
\tag{18}
\]
Its side lengths are \(P/2\) and \(H/2\), proving (3).

We first audit the two true shifted profiles.  Formula (12) records all
empty, partial, full, and equality cases without any generic-position
assumption.  Since \(f_i=-x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_i(a)
&:=|A_0\cap\{f_i\leq a\}|
  +|A_2\cap\{f_i\leq-a\}|\\
&=
h_0[a+x_0+w_0]_{w_0}
+h_2[x_2+w_2-a]_{w_2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The first clipped term changes from empty to partial to full at
\[
a=-x_0-w_0,\qquad a=-x_0,
\tag{20}
\]
and the second changes from full to partial to empty at
\[
a=x_2,\qquad a=x_2+w_2.
\tag{21}
\]
No ordering of these four breakpoints has been assumed.

Put
\[
u=a+x_0+w_0.
\]
The other clipped argument in (19) is
\[
x_2+w_2-a=R_x-u,
\]
so
\[
\Phi_i(a)=h_0[u]_{w_0}+h_2[R_x-u]_{w_2}.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(R_x\leq0\), any \(u\in[R_x,0]\) makes both endpoint caps empty.
Thus the minimum is \(0\), including the equality cell \(R_x=0\).

Suppose \(R_x>0\).  For a given \(u\), put
\[
\lambda_0=[u]_{w_0},\qquad
\lambda_2=[R_x-u]_{w_2}.
\]
If either cap is full, then its contribution is \(M\) or \(m\).
Since \(M\geq m\), the full-cap contribution alone is at least \(m\).
If neither cap is full and their total contribution is less than \(m\),
then inspection of the four clipping cells gives
\[
\lambda_0+\lambda_2\geq R_x.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, equality holds in the partial-partial cell; if one cap is empty,
the other unclipped length is at least \(R_x\).  Therefore
\[
\Phi_i(a)
=h_0\lambda_0+h_2\lambda_2
\geq\gamma_xR_x
\tag{24}
\]
whenever \(\Phi_i(a)<m\).  Hence
\[
\Phi_i(a)\geq\min\{m,\gamma_xR_x\}.
\tag{25}
\]

This lower bound is attained in every cell.  If
\(\gamma_x=h_2\) and \(0<R_x\leq m/h_2=w_2\), take \(u=0\):
the \(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap is partial, with value
\(h_2R_x\).  If \(\gamma_x=h_0\), then
\[
\frac m{h_0}\leq\frac M{h_0}=w_0,
\tag{26}
\]
and for \(0<R_x\leq m/h_0\), taking \(u=R_x\) makes the
\(A_0\)-cap partial and the \(A_2\)-cap empty, with value \(h_0R_x\).
For \(R_x\geq m/\gamma_x\), take \(u\) sufficiently negative.  The
\(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap is full, so the value is \(m\).
At \(R_x=m/\gamma_x\), the partial construction and the full-small-cap
construction both have value \(m\).  This proves every branch and equality
in (14).

The same computation in the \(y\)-coordinate gives the raw formula
\[
\Phi_\ell(a)
=w_0[a+y_0+h_0]_{h_0}
+w_2[y_2+h_2-a]_{h_2}.
\tag{27}
\]
Its four cap breakpoints are
\[
-y_0-h_0,\quad -y_0,\quad y_2,\quad y_2+h_2.
\tag{28}
\]
With \(v=a+y_0+h_0\), the other clipped argument is \(R_y-v\).
Replacing
\[
(w_0,w_2,h_0,h_2,R_x)
\quad\text{by}\quad
(h_0,h_2,w_0,w_2,R_y)
\]
in the preceding argument proves (16), including every empty, partial,
full, plateau, and equality cell.

The cap formulas (13) follow directly from (18).  For example, the width
of \(K\cap\{x\geq0\}\) is
\[
\frac12[R_x]_P,
\]
and the height of \(K\) is \(H/2\).  This proves the first formula in
(13); the second is symmetric.  Equations (13), (14), and (16) give the
exact minima of both \(\mu_i\) and \(\mu_\ell\).  All minima above are
attained at finite shifts.  Thus
\[
I_i\neq\varnothing\iff c_i+\inf_a\Phi_i(a)<t,
\qquad
I_\ell\neq\varnothing\iff c_\ell+\inf_a\Phi_\ell(a)<t.
\tag{29}
\]
In particular,
\[
I_i\neq\varnothing\Longrightarrow c_i<t,
\qquad
I_\ell\neq\varnothing\Longrightarrow c_\ell<t.
\tag{30}
\]

We next prove the rectangle corner-cut inequality used for the joint
step.  For \(\theta>0\), define
\[
F_\theta(z)
=\left|
\{(\xi,\eta)\in[0,1]^2:\xi+\theta\eta\leq z\}
\right|.
\tag{31}
\]
Direct integration gives the complete exact profile
\[
F_\theta(z)=
\begin{cases}
0,
&z\leq0,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{z^2}{2\theta},
&0\leq z\leq\min\{1,\theta\},\\[8pt]
\dfrac{\min\{1,\theta\}z-\min\{1,\theta\}^2/2}{\theta},
&\min\{1,\theta\}\leq z\leq\max\{1,\theta\},\\[10pt]
1-\dfrac{(1+\theta-z)^2}{2\theta},
&\max\{1,\theta\}\leq z\leq1+\theta,\\[10pt]
1,
&z\geq1+\theta.
\end{cases}
\tag{32}
\]
The consecutive formulas agree at
\[
z=0,\quad z=\min\{1,\theta\},\quad
z=\max\{1,\theta\},\quad z=1+\theta.
\]
Thus (32) includes the empty, triangular, trapezoidal,
complement-triangular, full, and every equality cell.

For \(u,v\in[0,1]\), (32) also gives the exact slope optimization
\[
\inf_{\theta>0}F_\theta(u+\theta v)
=\min\{u,v,2uv\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Here are the breakpoint checks.  In the lower triangular cell,
\[
F_\theta(u+\theta v)
=\frac{u^2}{2\theta}+uv+\frac{\theta v^2}{2}.
\tag{34}
\]
Its only interior minimum is at \(\theta=u/v\), with value \(2uv\);
if that point is not in the cell, the minimum is at a cell boundary.
In the two middle cells, the expressions are respectively
\[
u+\theta\left(v-\frac12\right),
\qquad
v+\frac{u-1/2}{\theta},
\tag{35}
\]
so they are constant or monotone and again minimize at a boundary.
In the upper triangular cell, the expression is
\[
1-(1-u)(1-v)
-\frac{(1-u)^2}{2\theta}
-\frac{\theta(1-v)^2}{2},
\tag{36}
\]
which is concave in \(\theta\), so it also minimizes at a boundary.
The two unbounded endpoints give
\[
\lim_{\theta\downarrow0}F_\theta(u+\theta v)=u,
\qquad
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}F_\theta(u+\theta v)=v.
\tag{37}
\]

The lower-triangle boundary values not already in (37) are
\[
\frac{u}{2(1-v)},\qquad
\frac{v}{2(1-u)}.
\tag{38}
\]
They are at least \(2uv\), because
\[
4v(1-v)\leq1,\qquad 4u(1-u)\leq1.
\]
The corresponding upper-triangle boundary values, which occur when
\(u+v\geq1\), are
\[
1-\frac{1-v}{2u},\qquad
1-\frac{1-u}{2v}.
\tag{39}
\]
Each is at least \(\min\{u,v\}\).  For example, if \(u\leq v\), then
\(1-v\leq\min\{u,1-u\}\leq2u(1-u)\), proving that the first value in
(39) is at least \(u\); if \(v\leq u\), then \(u\geq1/2\), and the same
inequality is immediate.  The second value is symmetric.  Equations
(34)-(39) prove (33), including \(u=0,1\) or \(v=0,1\) by continuity.

Apply (33) to an arbitrary axis-aligned rectangle \(K\) of area \(k\).
Let
\[
c_x=|K\cap\{x\geq0\}|,\qquad
c_y=|K\cap\{y\geq0\}|.
\]
If both coordinate axes meet the corresponding side intervals of \(K\),
normalize \(K\) to \([0,1]^2\) and put
\[
u=\frac{k-c_x}{k},\qquad
v=\frac{k-c_y}{k}.
\]
The line \(x+y=0\) becomes
\[
\xi+\theta\eta=u+\theta v
\]
for the positive aspect parameter \(\theta\).  Thus (33) gives
\[
|K\cap\{x+y\leq0\}|
\geq
\min\left\{
k-c_x,\ k-c_y,\
\frac{2(k-c_x)(k-c_y)}k
\right\}.
\tag{40}
\]
If \(K\) is wholly to the right of an axis, one of the first two
quantities is \(0\), so (40) is immediate.  If \(K\) is wholly to the
left of the \(y\)-axis, then
\[
K\cap\{y\leq0\}\subseteq K\cap\{x+y\leq0\},
\]
and the right side of (40) reduces to at most \(k-c_y\).  The other
outside-axis cases are symmetric.  Hence (40) holds in all empty, full,
touching, and interior cells.

For completeness, the actual diagonal cap \(c_q\) is governed by the same
exact formula (32), with its argument increased by the positive normalized
offset corresponding to the constant \(1\) in \(x+y\leq1\).  Equivalently,
the direct inclusion
\[
\{x+y\leq0\}\subseteq\{x+y\leq1\}
\]
gives
\[
c_q=|K\cap\{x+y\leq1\}|
\geq |K\cap\{x+y\leq0\}|.
\tag{41}
\]

It remains to prove the scalar inequality supplied by the residual area
strip.  Normalize by \(M\) and write, as in accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e`,
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac kM,\qquad
\tau=\frac tM=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\tag{42}
\]
We claim
\[
\frac{2(s-\tau)^2}{s}>\tau-r.
\tag{43}
\]
Define the difference
\[
G_r(s)
=\frac{2(s-\tau)^2}{s}-(\tau-r)
=\frac{80s^2+(7r-74)s+8(1+r)^2}{81s}.
\tag{44}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\frac{\partial G_r}{\partial s}
=\frac{8\bigl(10s^2-(1+r)^2\bigr)}{81s^2}.
\tag{45}
\]

On the first residual branch \(0<r\leq9/25\), one has \(s>1-r\).
Since \(r<1/2\) and \(\sqrt{10}>3\),
\[
s>1-r>\frac{1+r}{3}>\frac{1+r}{\sqrt{10}}.
\]
Thus (45) is positive throughout this branch, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_r(s)
&>G_r(1-r)\\
&=\frac{81r^2-63r+14}{81(1-r)}\\
&=\frac{81(r-7/18)^2+7/4}{81(1-r)}
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]

On the second residual branch \(9/25<r<\rho\), put \(x=\sqrt r>3/5\).
The lower endpoint is
\[
D(r)=\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
D(r)-\frac{1+r}{3}
=\frac{7r+6\sqrt r-5}{6}>0,
\]
because the numerator is increasing and is positive at \(r=9/25\).
Hence \(s>D(r)>(1+r)/\sqrt{10}\), so (45) is again positive.  Direct
substitution gives
\[
G_r(D(r))
=\frac{
397x^4+494x^3-277x^2-308x+130
}{
81(x+1)(3x-1)
}.
\tag{47}
\]
With \(z=x-3/5>0\), the numerator in (47) is
\[
397z^4+\frac{7234}{5}z^3+\frac{36743}{25}z^2
+\frac{29516}{125}z+\frac{2272}{625}>0.
\tag{48}
\]
The denominator in (47) is positive as well.  Therefore
\[
G_r(s)>G_r(D(r))>0.
\tag{49}
\]
Equations (46) and (49) prove (43) on the entire exact residual domain.
Returning to unnormalized areas, (43) is exactly
\[
\frac{2(k-t)^2}{k}>t-m.
\tag{50}
\]

We now prove (9).  Suppose for contradiction that
\[
I_i\neq\varnothing,\qquad I_\ell\neq\varnothing.
\tag{51}
\]
By (30),
\[
c_i<t,\qquad c_\ell<t.
\tag{52}
\]
The strict \(q\)-tail (8) gives
\[
t-m>c_q\geq0.
\tag{53}
\]
The residual lower inequality \(s>1-r\) is
\[
k+m>M.
\tag{54}
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
(k-t)-(t-m)
&=k+m-2t\\
&=\frac{5(k+m)-4M}{9}\\
&>\frac M9>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{55}
\]
In particular, \(k-t>t-m>0\).  Equations (52), (55), and (50) give
\[
k-c_i>k-t>t-m,
\tag{56}
\]
\[
k-c_\ell>k-t>t-m,
\tag{57}
\]
and
\[
\frac{2(k-c_i)(k-c_\ell)}k
>
\frac{2(k-t)^2}{k}
>t-m.
\tag{58}
\]
Combining (40), (41), and (56)-(58) yields
\[
c_q>t-m.
\tag{59}
\]
This contradicts (8), which says \(c_q<t-m\).  Therefore (51) is
impossible, proving
\[
I_i=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad I_\ell=\varnothing.
\]

Every strict or equality endpoint has been retained: equality in a true
profile minimum with \(t\) makes the corresponding \(I_j\) empty; the raw
cap equalities are included by clipping in (19) and (27); the diagonal cap
formulas agree at all four breakpoints in (32); and the final contradiction
uses only the registered strict residual and \(q\)-tail inequalities.
