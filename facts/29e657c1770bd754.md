## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K_0\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact
two-dimensional convex polytopes, let \(f_3\) be a nonconstant affine
function, and assume
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K_0.
\]
Write
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k_0=|K_0|,
\]
\[
u_3=|A_0\cap\{f_3\leq L_3\}|,\qquad
v_3=|A_2\cap\{f_3\leq-L_3\}|,
\]
\[
c_{3,0}=|K_0\cap\{f_3\leq0\}|,
\qquad
h_0=\frac{2(M+m+k_0)}9.
\]
Suppose
\[
\alpha:=h_0-u_3-v_3-c_{3,0}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
If a compact convex polygon \(K\supseteq K_0\), of area \(k\), satisfies
the third crossing
\[
u_3+v_3+|K\cap\{f_3\leq0\}|
=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\tag{2}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\quad k-k_0\geq\frac97\alpha.\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, if additionally
\[
\frac97\alpha\geq M+m-k_0,
\tag{4}
\]
there is no such \(K\) satisfying the strict no-half inequality
\[
k<M+m.
\tag{5}
\]

The following exact rational common-plane endpoint family satisfies the
strict version of (4).  Put
\[
d=\frac1{1000000},
\qquad
\psi_1(x,y)=x,\quad
\psi_2(x,y)=y,\quad
\psi_3(x,y)=-x-y,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
f_i=\frac{\psi_i}{3d}-\frac13,
\qquad
L_i=-\frac1{9d}-\frac13
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{7}
\]
In the plane \(r_1+r_2+r_3=1\), let
\[
X=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(1,0,0),(0,1,0),
(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12),(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)
\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
and define
\[
A_0=\{\psi:r_i=\psi_i+\tfrac13,\ r\in X\}.
\tag{9}
\]
In the plane \(\tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3=1\), let
\[
\mathcal Y=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(-\frac{99}{20000},\frac{20101}{20000},-\frac1{10000}\right),
\left(\frac{20101}{20000},-\frac{99}{20000},-\frac1{10000}\right),
\left(\frac{99}{200},\frac{99}{200},\frac1{100}\right)
\right\},
\tag{10}
\]
put
\[
s=1+6d=\frac{500003}{500000},
\qquad
t_i=s\tau_i-2d,
\qquad
\psi_i=\frac13-t_i,
\tag{11}
\]
and define \(A_2\) as the image of \(\mathcal Y\) under (11).  Set
\[
K_0=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then the exact positive \(P\)-support is \(\{\varnothing\}\), the exact
positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\{123,1,2,12\},
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
M=\frac38,
\qquad
m=\frac{2\,550\,280\,603\,091\,809}
{500000000000000000},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
k_0=
\frac{447601825903091809}
{2000000000000000000}.
\tag{15}
\]
For direction \(3\),
\[
u_3=0,
\qquad
v_3=q_{123}
=\frac{250003000009}{50000000000000},
\tag{16}
\]
\[
c_{3,0}
=\frac{750006000009}{200000000000000},
\tag{17}
\]
\[
h_0
=\frac{241560589663091809}
{1800000000000000000}.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence
\[
\alpha
=\frac{225810427662686809}
{1800000000000000000},
\tag{19}
\]
and the two competing enlargement bounds are
\[
\frac97\alpha
=\frac{225810427662686809}
{1400000000000000000},
\tag{20}
\]
\[
M+m-k_0
=\frac{312599296509275427}
{2000000000000000000}.
\tag{21}
\]
Their exact gap is
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac97\alpha-(M+m-k_0)
=\frac{69909201061940101}
{14000000000000000000}>0.
\quad}
\tag{22}
\]
Therefore no convex polygon \(K\supseteq K_0\) realizes the third
crossing and \(k<M+m\) for this endpoint family.

## Proof

We first prove the general enlargement bound.  Put
\[
\Delta k=k-k_0\geq0,
\qquad
c_3=|K\cap\{f_3\leq0\}|.
\]
Because \(K_0\subseteq K\),
\[
c_3-c_{3,0}
=|(K\setminus K_0)\cap\{f_3\leq0\}|
\leq|K\setminus K_0|
=\Delta k.
\tag{23}
\]
Cutting-line pieces have planar area zero because \(f_3\) is
nonconstant, so (23) is unaffected by the weak exact-cell convention.

The right side of the crossing rises linearly with the area of \(K\):
\[
\frac{2(M+m+k)}9=h_0+\frac29\Delta k.
\]
Subtracting the defining identity
\[
u_3+v_3+c_{3,0}=h_0-\alpha
\]
from (2) gives
\[
c_3-c_{3,0}
=\alpha+\frac29\Delta k.
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (23) and (24),
\[
\alpha+\frac29\Delta k\leq\Delta k,
\]
which is exactly (3).  If (5) also holds, then
\[
\Delta k<M+m-k_0.
\]
This is incompatible with (3) under (4), proving the general statement.

Equivalently, use the seven exact middle-sector increments
\[
x_G=|R_G(K)|-|R_G(K_0)|\geq0
\qquad
(G\in\{1,2,3,12,13,23,123\}).
\tag{25}
\]
They satisfy
\[
\Delta k=\sum_Gx_G.
\]
The third crossing is exactly the linear target
\[
x_3+x_{13}+x_{23}+x_{123}
=\alpha+\frac29\sum_Gx_G.
\tag{26}
\]
The left side of (26) is at most \(\sum_Gx_G\), recovering (3).
Thus no unrecorded convexity or support-function assumption enters the
obstruction; it already holds for every measurable enlargement.

It remains to audit the exact endpoint data.  Equations (6)-(7) give
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3=-\frac1{3d}-1<-1,
\]
and pairwise nonparallel gradients.  Moreover,
\[
f_i\leq L_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\psi_i\leq-\frac13
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_i\leq0.
\]
The interior of (8) has all \(r_i>0\), so \(A_0\) has only
\(P_\varnothing\) with positive area.  The shoelace formula on (8) gives
\[
M=p_\varnothing=\frac38,
\qquad u_3=0,
\]
proving the first part of (14) and (16).

Since \(\sum_i\tau_i=1\), equation (11) gives
\[
\sum_it_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_i\psi_i=0.
\]
The exact \(Q\)-condition is
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_i\leq-L_i
&\Longleftrightarrow
\psi_i\leq\frac13+2d\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
t_i\geq-2d\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\tau_i\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The first two vertices of (10) have strict sign types \(Q_2,Q_1\);
the third has strict type \(Q_{123}\).  Below \(\tau_3=0\), the segment
between the first two vertices has a positive subsegment with
\(\tau_1,\tau_2>0\), giving \(Q_{12}\).  Above that line both
\(\tau_1,\tau_2\) are positive.  This proves the exact support (13).

Clipping (10) by \(\tau_i=0\) gives, before the common area factor
\(s^2\),
\[
|\mathcal Y_{123}|=\frac1{200}.
\]
Therefore
\[
q_{123}
=s^2\frac1{200}
=\frac{250003000009}{50000000000000},
\]
which proves the second part of (16).  The total triangle area is
\[
|\mathcal Y|=\frac{10201}{2000000}.
\]
Multiplication by \(s^2\) gives the value of \(m\) in (14).

For completeness, the midpoint body can be audited without a hidden
optimization.  In the \((r_1-t_1,r_2-t_2)\)-coordinates, write
\[
Z=X-\{t(\tau):\tau\in\mathcal Y\}.
\]
Then \(K_0=Z/2\), and exact Minkowski vertex addition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z=\operatorname{conv}\{&
\left(-\frac{10050540303}{10000000000},
       \frac{5049520297}{10000000000}\right),\\
&\left(\frac{5049520297}{10000000000},
       -\frac{10050540303}{10000000000}\right),\\
&\left(\frac{10049520297}{10000000000},
       -\frac{10050540303}{10000000000}\right),\\
&\left(\frac{50499903}{100000000},
       -\frac{49500097}{100000000}\right),\\
&\left(-\frac{49500097}{100000000},
       \frac{50499903}{100000000}\right),\\
&\left(-\frac{10050540303}{10000000000},
       \frac{10049520297}{10000000000}\right)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The shoelace formula gives
\[
|Z|
=\frac{447601825903091809}
{500000000000000000}.
\]
Dividing by four proves (15).

For a midpoint \(\psi=z/2\),
\[
f_i(\psi)\leq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_i\leq2d.
\tag{29}
\]
Clipping (28) by the three lines in (29) gives the unscaled \(Z\)-areas
\[
|Z_3|=\frac{249703088209}{5\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000},
\]
\[
|Z_{13}|=|Z_{23}|
=\frac{74750896822691}{10000000000000000},
\qquad
|Z_{123}|=\frac9{500000000000}.
\tag{30}
\]
The actual \(K_0=Z/2\) areas are one quarter of (30).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{3,0}
&=\frac14\left(
|Z_3|+|Z_{13}|+|Z_{23}|+|Z_{123}|
\right)\\
&=\frac{750006000009}{200000000000000},
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (17).  Substitution of (14)-(17) into the definition of
\(h_0\) gives (18), and then (19) follows by exact subtraction.
Finally, (20)-(22) are direct rational simplifications.  Their strict
positive gap proves that this complete endpoint family has no admissible
middle convex enlargement.
