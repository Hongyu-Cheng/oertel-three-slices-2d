## Statement

The universal implication being refuted is the following.  Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes such that
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{H1}
\]
Put
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\tag{H2}
\]
and assume the exact strict residual area conditions supplied by accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Let
\[
g_i=x,\qquad g_\ell=y,\qquad g_q=1-x-y,
\tag{H3}
\]
and \(f_j=-g_j\), so the three nonconstant affine functions have pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_i+f_\ell+f_q=-1.
\tag{H4}
\]
Define
\[
c_j=|K\cap\{f_j\le0\}|=|K\cap\{g_j\ge0\}|
\quad(j\in\{i,\ell,q\}),
\tag{H5}
\]
\[
p_j=\min_{a\in\mathbb R}\left(
|A_0\cap\{f_j\le a\}|+
|A_2\cap\{f_j\le-a\}|
\right)
\quad(j\in\{i,\ell\}),
\tag{H6}
\]
\[
E=c_i+c_\ell+c_q-k,\qquad
D_0=3t-k-m,
\tag{H7}
\]
and
\[
d_r=\max_{A_r}(x+y)-\min_{A_r}x-\min_{A_r}y
\quad(r\in\{0,2\}).
\tag{H8}
\]
The implication claims
\[
\frac{(d_0+d_2)^2}{8}\ge M+m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E+p_i+p_\ell\ge D_0.
\tag{P}
\]

For \(h>0\), define the rational parallelogram
\[
P(h)=\left\{(x,y):
-14\le\frac{x-y}{2}\le14,\quad
2-h\le x+y\le2+h
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
A_0=K=P\left(\frac1{56}\right),
\qquad
A_2=P\left(\frac1{504}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Then all hypotheses above hold, as do
\[
c_q+m<t,\qquad D_0>m,\qquad E>0,
\tag{3}
\]
and the antecedent of \((P)\), but
\[
E+p_i+p_\ell=\frac{37}{126}
<\frac8{27}=D_0.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \((P)\) is false.

## Proof

Use the rational linear coordinates
\[
u=\frac{x-y}{2},\qquad v=x+y.
\tag{5}
\]
Their inverse is
\[
x=u+\frac v2,\qquad y=-u+\frac v2,
\tag{6}
\]
and the absolute determinant of the inverse map is \(1\).  Therefore \(P(h)\) is the image of the rectangle
\[
[-14,14]\times[2-h,2+h]
\tag{7}
\]
under an area-preserving rational linear map.  In particular, it is a nonempty two-dimensional rational parallelogram and
\[
|P(h)|=28(2h)=56h.
\tag{8}
\]
It follows from (2) and (8) that
\[
M=|A_0|=|K|=k=1,\qquad
m=|A_2|=\frac19.
\tag{9}
\]

Minkowski addition of the rectangles in (7) gives the exact identity
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=P\left(\frac12\left(\frac1{56}+\frac1{504}\right)\right)
=P\left(\frac5{504}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(5/504<1/56=9/504\), equation (10) is contained in \(K=P(1/56)\).  This proves (H1).

In the normalization of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`,
\[
r=\frac mM=\frac19,\qquad s=\frac kM=1.
\tag{11}
\]
The complete raw residual conditions hold term by term:
\[
0<\frac19\le1,\qquad
\frac{(1+\sqrt{1/9})^2}{4}=\frac49\le1,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
1-\frac19=\frac89<1<\frac{10}{9}=1+\frac19,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
1<2-\frac19=\frac{17}{9},
\qquad
1>\frac{3/9+2/3-1}{2}=0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus this is strictly inside the accepted residual strip; the only weak residual inequality in (12) also has strict slack.

Equations (9) give
\[
t=\frac{2(1+1/9+1)}9=\frac{38}{81},
\qquad
D_0=3t-k-m=\frac8{27}>\frac19=m.
\tag{15}
\]
Every point of \(K=P(1/56)\) has
\[
x+y=v\ge2-\frac1{56}=\frac{111}{56}>1.
\tag{16}
\]
Hence its \(q\)-cap is empty:
\[
c_q=|K\cap\{x+y\le1\}|=0.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently the strict \(q\)-tail holds:
\[
c_q+m=\frac19<\frac{38}{81}=t.
\tag{18}
\]

We next compute the other two middle caps exactly.  At a fixed \(v\in[2-1/56,2+1/56]\), condition \(x\ge0\) is \(u\ge-v/2\).  Since \(v/2<14\), its \(u\)-section in \(K\) has length \(14+v/2\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i
&=\int_{2-1/56}^{2+1/56}
\left(14+\frac v2\right)\,dv\\
&=\frac12+\frac1{28}
=\frac{15}{28}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Reflection across \(x=y\) preserves \(K\) and exchanges the \(i\)- and \(\ell\)-caps, so
\[
c_\ell=\frac{15}{28}.
\tag{20}
\]
Using (17), (19), and (20),
\[
E=c_i+c_\ell+c_q-k
=\frac{15}{28}+\frac{15}{28}-1
=\frac1{14}>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Equivalently, because \(v>0\), the \(x\)- and \(y\)-caps cover \(K\), and their overlap has, at fixed \(v\), the \(u\)-interval \([-v/2,v/2]\) of length \(v\).  Its area is
\[
\int_{2-1/56}^{2+1/56}v\,dv=\frac1{14},
\]
which independently checks (21).

It remains to compute the two true endpoint profile minima.  For \(h\in\{1/56,1/504\}\), write
\[
F_h(z)=|P(h)\cap\{x\ge z\}|.
\tag{22}
\]
The inclusion \(P(1/504)\subset P(1/56)\) gives
\[
F_{1/56}(z)\ge F_{1/504}(z)
\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb R.
\tag{23}
\]
The smaller parallelogram \(P(1/504)\) is centrally symmetric about \((1,1)\).  Up to null cutting lines, this symmetry maps its set \(\{x<a\}\) onto its set \(\{x>2-a\}\).  Hence
\[
\frac19-F_{1/504}(a)=F_{1/504}(2-a).
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(2-a>-a\), monotonicity of \(F_{1/504}\), followed by (23), gives
\[
F_{1/56}(-a)\ge F_{1/504}(-a)
\ge F_{1/504}(2-a).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(f_i=-x\), equations (24)-(25) imply, for every real \(a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_0\cap\{f_i\le a\}|
+|A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a\}|
&=F_{1/56}(-a)+F_{1/504}(a)\\
&\ge F_{1/504}(2-a)+F_{1/504}(a)\\
&=\frac19.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Equality is attained.  Indeed, let
\[
a_*=-\max_{A_0}x
=-\left(15+\frac1{112}\right).
\tag{27}
\]
Then the \(A_0\)-cap in (26) is only its right exposed edge and has area zero.  Moreover
\[
a_*<
-13-\frac1{1008}
=\min_{A_2}x,
\tag{28}
\]
so the \(A_2\)-cap in (26) is all of \(A_2\) and has area \(1/9\).  Thus
\[
p_i=\frac19.
\tag{29}
\]
Reflection across \(x=y\) proves by the same calculation that
\[
p_\ell=\frac19.
\tag{30}
\]

Finally, for \(A_r=P(h_r)\), where
\[
h_0=\frac1{56},\qquad h_2=\frac1{504},
\]
one has
\[
\max_{A_r}(x+y)=2+h_r,
\tag{31}
\]
\[
\min_{A_r}x=\min_{A_r}y=-13-\frac{h_r}{2}.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore
\[
d_r=(2+h_r)-2\left(-13-\frac{h_r}{2}\right)
=28+2h_r,
\tag{33}
\]
so
\[
d_0=28+\frac1{28},\qquad
d_2=28+\frac1{252},
\qquad
d_0+d_2=\frac{7061}{126}>56.
\tag{34}
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{(d_0+d_2)^2}{8}>392>\frac{10}{9}=M+m,
\tag{35}
\]
which verifies the antecedent of \((P)\).

Combining (21), (29), and (30) gives
\[
E+p_i+p_\ell
=\frac1{14}+\frac19+\frac19
=\frac{37}{126}.
\tag{36}
\]
By (15),
\[
D_0-\left(E+p_i+p_\ell\right)
=\frac8{27}-\frac{37}{126}
=\frac1{378}>0.
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (35) and (37) are the required exact violation of \((P)\).  The construction refutes only the proposed overlap-payment implication.  Indeed, (19), (20), (29), and (30) give \(c_i+p_i=c_\ell+p_\ell>t\), so it does not refute the original tail-exclusion conclusion.
