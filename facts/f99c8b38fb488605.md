## Statement

Assume the normalized Regime A Alternative 1 homothetic setting of accepted
fact `f37aced9de558c8d`.  Put
\[
\sigma=x+y,\qquad \eta=x-y,
\]
and suppose
\[
A_0=\{(x,y):0\le \sigma\le 1/w,\ |\eta-c\sigma|\le w\},
\qquad
A_2=hA_0,
\]
where \(w>0\), \(c\in\mathbb R\), and
\[
0<h<h_0:=\frac{2\sqrt{71}-9}{29}.
\]
Let \(K\) be the actual middle convex body, of area \(s\), and assume
\[
1-h^2<s<1+h^2,\qquad
\lambda A_0\subseteq K,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{1+h}{2}.
\]
Define
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{x\le0\}|,\qquad
c_\ell=|K\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
c_j=|K\cap\{\sigma\ge-1\}|,
\]
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{x\le0\}|,\qquad
u_\ell=|A_0\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+h^2+s)}9.
\]
Then, for every \(w>0\) and \(c\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\boxed{
\max\left\{
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i,\
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell,\
c_j+h^2
\right\}\ge t.}
\tag{1}
\]
The conclusion includes the cap-transition and support equalities, zero
middle-cap cells, nontrivial support faces, \(h=1/5\), and every target
equality.

## Proof

The Jacobian of \((x,y)\mapsto(\sigma,\eta)\) has absolute value \(2\).
Introduce
\[
\tau=w\sigma,\qquad
\zeta=\frac{\eta-c\sigma}{w}.
\]
Then \(A_0\) becomes the rectangle
\[
0\le\tau\le1,\qquad -1\le\zeta\le1,
\]
and physical area is \(\frac12\,d\tau\,d\zeta\).  Define, for
\(k\in\mathbb R\),
\[
q(k)=
\begin{cases}
1+\dfrac1{4k},&k\le-1,\\[2mm]
\dfrac12-\dfrac{k}{4},&-1\le k\le1,\\[2mm]
\dfrac1{4k},&k\ge1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The formulas agree at \(k=\pm1\).  Since \(x\le0\) is
\[
\zeta\le-\frac{1+c}{w^2}\tau,
\]
direct integration of the vertical length in the rectangle gives
\[
u_i=q\left(\frac{1+c}{w^2}\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Similarly, \(y\le0\) is
\[
\zeta\ge\frac{1-c}{w^2}\tau.
\]
Reflecting \(\zeta\) gives the second exact formula
\[
u_\ell=q\left(\frac{1-c}{w^2}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus (3)-(4), rather than shear invariance of the cuts, are the actual
side-cap areas for every \(w,c\).

Suppose for contradiction that all three entries in (1) are strictly below
\(t\):
\[
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i<t,\qquad
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell<t,\qquad
c_j+h^2<t.
\tag{5}
\]
We first prove the shape-independent middle estimate
\[
c_i+c_\ell\ge\frac{8s}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
This is the threshold-independent part of accepted fact
`f22ed0c4b6e9ec74`; here is the argument, with the exact predecessors used
made explicit.

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(K\).  The planar centroid halfplane inequality
recorded in accepted fact `6e68b44550cac3c8` gives area at least \(4s/9\)
to every closed halfplane containing \(g\).  Since
\[
\frac{4s}{9}-(t-h^2)
=\frac{2s+7h^2-2}{9}
>\frac{5h^2}{9}>0,
\tag{7}
\]
the last inequality in (5) forces \(f_j(g)>0\), where
\(f_j=-1-x-y\).  Since \(x+f_j+y=-1\), at least one of \(x(g)\) and
\(y(g)\) is nonpositive.  Interchange \(i,\ell\) if necessary and assume
\(x(g)\le0\).  Then
\[
c_i\ge\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{8}
\]

Put
\[
F(z)=2\sqrt{sz}-2z.
\]
The positive root \(h_0\) of \(29h^2+18h-7\) is less than \(1/3\), so
\(h^2<1/9\).  In particular \(t<s\).  Direct substitution gives
\[
4st-(3t-h^2)^2
=
\frac{
4(s-1+h^2)(s+1+2h^2)+h^2(8-9h^2)
}{9}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(3t-h^2>0\), equation (9) says
\[
F(t)>t-h^2>c_j.
\tag{10}
\]
The function \(F\) is strictly decreasing on \([4s/9,s]\).
If \(y(g)\le0\), the centroid inequality gives
\[
c_\ell\ge\frac{4s}{9}
=F\left(\frac{4s}{9}\right)\ge F(c_i).
\]
If \(y(g)>0\), apply accepted fact `0688f679297c8baa` to
\((f_1,f_2,f_3)=(y,f_j,x)\).  It gives
\[
\max\{c_\ell,c_j\}\ge F(c_i).
\]
Equations (5), (8), and (10) give
\[
F(c_i)>F(t)>c_j,
\]
so again \(c_\ell\ge F(c_i)\).  Finally,
\(z+F(z)=2\sqrt{sz}-z\) is increasing for \(0<z<s\).  Hence
\[
c_i+c_\ell
\ge c_i+F(c_i)
\ge\frac{4s}{9}+F\left(\frac{4s}{9}\right)
=\frac{8s}{9},
\]
which proves (6).

Add the first two inequalities in (5) and use (6).  With
\[
\Delta=1+h^2-s,
\]
where \(0<\Delta<2h^2\), this gives
\[
u_i+u_\ell
<
\frac{4\Delta}{9(1+h^2)}
<
\frac{8h^2}{9(1+h^2)}
<
\frac4{45}
<
\frac14.
\tag{11}
\]
From (2), \(q(k)<1/4\) holds exactly when \(k>1\).  Since each cap in
(11) is nonnegative, equations (3)-(4) therefore imply
\[
\frac{1+c}{w^2}>1,\qquad
\frac{1-c}{w^2}>1.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular
\[
-1<c<1,
\tag{13}
\]
and both caps are in their actual, generally unequal triangular branches:
\[
u_i=\frac{w^2}{4(1+c)},\qquad
u_\ell=\frac{w^2}{4(1-c)}.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently
\[
u_i+u_\ell=\frac{w^2}{2(1-c^2)}
\tag{15}
\]
and (11) sharpens to
\[
w^2
<
\frac{8(1-c^2)\Delta}{9(1+h^2)}
<
\frac{16h^2(1-c^2)}{9(1+h^2)}.
\tag{16}
\]

We now derive the actual support strip from the sheared parallelogram.  Put
\[
d=\lambda^2,\qquad D=\lambda A_0,\qquad
L=\frac{\lambda}{w},\qquad a=\lambda w.
\]
In the \((\sigma,\eta)\)-plane,
\[
D=\{0\le\sigma\le L,\ |\eta-c\sigma|\le a\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The coordinate area of \(K\) is \(2s\).  For any
\(p=(\sigma,\eta)\in K\), convexity joins \(p\) to each full long edge
\[
E_\pm=\{(r,cr\pm a):0\le r\le L\}
\]
of \(D\).  The two resulting triangles have coordinate areas
\[
\frac L2\,|\eta-c\sigma-a|,
\qquad
\frac L2\,|\eta-c\sigma+a|.
\]
Each is at most \(2s\).  Since the maximum of the two absolute values is
\(|\eta-c\sigma|+a\), every point of \(K\) satisfies
\[
|\eta-c\sigma|
\le \frac{4s}{L}-a
=w\left(\frac{4s}{\lambda}-\lambda\right)
=:M.
\tag{18}
\]
This is the sheared containment estimate; replacing it by
\(|\eta|\le M\) would be false when \(c\ne0\).

Set
\[
A=1+c,\qquad B=1-c.
\]
Both are positive by (13), and below \(r_+=\max\{r,0\}\).  In the root
exterior \(\{\sigma<-1\}\), a
point outside the \(i\)-cap has \(\eta>-\sigma\).  By (18), it lies in
\[
\sigma<-1,\qquad -\sigma<\eta\le c\sigma+M.
\]
The exact physical area of this ambient region is
\[
\theta_i=\frac{(M-A)_+^2}{4A}.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, it is empty for \(M\le A\); for \(M>A\), its coordinate width is
\(A\sigma+M\) on \(-M/A<\sigma<-1\), whose coordinate area is
\((M-A)^2/(2A)\), and physical area is half of that.  Likewise, a root
exterior point outside the \(\ell\)-cap has \(\eta<\sigma\).  The lower
support inequality in (18) gives the second exact ambient area
\[
\theta_\ell=\frac{(M-B)_+^2}{4B}.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus the two actual root-exterior leakages are bounded separately by
\(\theta_i,\theta_\ell\); they are not shear-invariant.

The root exterior has area \(s-c_j\).  Equations (19)-(20) therefore give
\[
c_i\ge s-c_j-\theta_i,\qquad
c_\ell\ge s-c_j-\theta_\ell.
\tag{21}
\]
Combining (21) with the corresponding inequalities in (5) yields
\[
\theta_i>
s-2t+h^2+(1+h^2)u_i,
\qquad
\theta_\ell>
s-2t+h^2+(1+h^2)u_\ell.
\tag{22}
\]
But
\[
s-2t+h^2=\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}>\frac19.
\tag{23}
\]
It follows from (19), (20), and (22) that
\[
M>A+\frac23\sqrt A,\qquad
M>B+\frac23\sqrt B.
\tag{24}
\]
The function \(z\mapsto z+\frac23\sqrt z\) is increasing for \(z>0\),
and \(\max\{A,B\}=1+|c|\ge1\).  Hence (24) implies
\[
\boxed{M>\frac53.}
\tag{25}
\]
Notice that (24), not merely (25), is the exact sheared root-exterior
conclusion.

We finish with two exact \(h\)-ranges.  Containment gives \(s\ge d\), so
\(4s-d>0\).  First suppose \(0<h\le1/5\).  Equations (16), (18), and
\(s<1+h^2\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
M^2
&=w^2\frac{(4s-d)^2}{d}\\
&<
\frac{16h^2(1-c^2)}{9(1+h^2)}
\frac{(4(1+h^2)-d)^2}{d}\\
&\le
U(h):=
\frac{
4h^2(15h^2-2h+15)^2
}{
9(1+h)^2(1+h^2)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Exact differentiation gives
\[
U'(h)=
\frac{
8h(15h^2-2h+15)
(15h^5+30h^4+13h^3+43h^2-4h+15)
}{
9(1+h)^3(1+h^2)^2
}>0
\tag{27}
\]
on \(0<h\le1/5\).  Therefore
\[
M^2<U(h)\le U(1/5)=\frac{2888}{1053}<\frac{25}{9},
\tag{28}
\]
where the final strict comparison is
\(25\cdot1053-9\cdot2888=333>0\).  This contradicts (25).

It remains to consider \(1/5<h<h_0\).  Since
\[
M=w\frac{4s-d}{\lambda}
=a\,\frac{4s-d}{d},
\]
equation (25) gives
\[
a>\frac{5d}{3(4s-d)}.
\tag{29}
\]
The last inequality in (5) has \(c_j<s\), because
\[
s-(t-h^2)=\frac{7s+7h^2-2}{9}>\frac59.
\tag{30}
\]
Hence \(K\) contains a point \(p\) with \(\sigma(p)=-b<-1\).
The vertical edge of the actual sheared parallelogram (17) at
\(\sigma=0\) is still
\[
\{0\}\times[-a,a].
\]
The triangle joining \(p\) to this full edge lies in \(K\).  Its portion
in \(-1\le\sigma\le0\) has physical area
\[
a\left(1-\frac1{2b}\right)>\frac a2.
\]
It is disjoint in area from \(D\), and both sets lie in
\(\{\sigma\ge-1\}\).  Thus (29) gives
\[
c_j>d+\frac a2
>
d+\frac{5d}{6(4s-d)}
>
d+\frac{5d}{6(4(1+h^2)-d)}.
\tag{31}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
t-h^2<\frac{4-5h^2}{9}.
\tag{32}
\]
Exact simplification gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&d+\frac{5d}{6(4(1+h^2)-d)}
-\frac{4-5h^2}{9}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{
435h^4+212h^3+324h^2+344h-75
}{
36(15h^2-2h+15)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
The denominator is positive.  The numerator is strictly increasing for
\(h>0\), and at \(h=1/5\) it equals \(1144/125>0\).  Equations
(31)-(33) therefore give \(c_j>t-h^2\), contradicting (5).

Both \(h\)-ranges are impossible, so the three strict inequalities (5)
cannot hold simultaneously.  This proves the nonstrict maximum inequality
(1) for every real shear \(c\).

BOUNDARY AUDIT
The exact cap function (2) includes \(k=-1,0,1\), hence all side-support
transitions and \(c=\pm1\).  A strict target failure itself forces the open
branch (12), so no boundary shear is discarded.  The positive parts in
(19)-(20) include the root-support equalities \(M=1+c\) and \(M=1-c\);
(22) shows directly that neither can occur in a strict failure.  The
long-edge triangles used for (18) and the vertical-edge triangle used for
(31) remain valid when a support line contains a nontrivial face.  No cap
area, cell area, chord length, or contact angle is divided by, so zero
middle-cap cells are included.  All cutting lines have planar area zero.
The case \(h=1/5\) is included in (26)-(28), and excluding only simultaneous
strict failure proves (1) with every target-equality boundary retained.
