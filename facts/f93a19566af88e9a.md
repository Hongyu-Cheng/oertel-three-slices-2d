## Statement

Let the six sectors around the origin be bounded in cyclic order by
\[
r_0=(1,0),\quad r_1=(0,1),\quad r_2=(-1,1),\quad
r_3=(-1,0),\quad r_4=(0,-1),\quad r_5=(1,-1),
\]
and denote the sectors between consecutive rays by
\[
P,W_x,N_x,O,N_y,W_y.
\]
The convex rational polygon
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,6),\,(-4,-22),\,(3,-1)\}
\]
contains the origin in its interior and has sector data
\[
(P,W_x,N_x,O,N_y,W_y)
=\left(2,8,8,64,\frac{25}{2},\frac72\right).
\]
In particular,
\[
e=N_x-W_y=N_y-W_x=\frac92>0.
\]
There is no triangle containing the origin in its interior with one vertex in each of the open sectors \(P,N_x,N_y\) and with
\[
(W_x,N_x,O,N_y,W_y)
=\left(8,8,64,\frac{25}{2},\frac72\right).
\]
Consequently the claimed replacement, even without the requirement that \(P\) not decrease, is impossible.

## Proof

We first derive a necessary inequality for every triangle of the proposed alternating-sector type.  Let \(T\) contain the origin in its interior and have one vertex in each of \(P,N_x,N_y\).  Its boundary meets \(r_i\) at \(a_ir_i\), where \(a_i>0\), and put \(u_i=1/a_i\).  The sectors \(W_x,O,W_y\) contain no vertex of \(T\), so their boundary arcs are single sides and, writing
\[
w=W_x,\qquad o=O,\qquad v=W_y,
\]
one has
\[
w=\frac{a_1a_2}{2},\qquad
o=\frac{a_3a_4}{2},\qquad
v=\frac{a_5a_0}{2}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
u_1u_2=\frac1{2w},\qquad
u_3u_4=\frac1{2o},\qquad
u_5u_0=\frac1{2v}.
\]

Consider the vertex in \(N_x\), and write \(n=N_x\).  The two incident sides, across \(W_x\) and \(O\), have equations
\[
L_1(x,y)=(u_1-u_2)x+u_1y=1,
\qquad
L_2(x,y)=-u_3x-u_4y=1.
\]
Parametrize directions in \(N_x\) by
\[
d_\lambda=(1-\lambda)r_2+\lambda r_3=(-1,1-\lambda),
\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1.
\]
Since the vertex is strictly inside \(N_x\), the two edge-slope and ray-order inequalities are
\[
u_2>u_3-u_4,\qquad u_3>u_2-u_1.
\]
They say exactly that \(L_1(d_\lambda)=u_2-u_1\lambda\) and
\(L_2(d_\lambda)=u_3-u_4+u_4\lambda\) cross once at a parameter
\(\lambda_0\in(0,1)\).  No third side is active in this angular sector.  If
\[
D=u_1u_3+u_2u_4-u_1u_4,
\]
their common value is \(D/(u_1+u_4)\).  Since
\(\det(d_\lambda,\partial_\lambda d_\lambda)=1\), direct radial integration gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2n
&=\int_0^{\lambda_0}\frac{d\lambda}{(u_2-u_1\lambda)^2}\\
&\quad+\int_{\lambda_0}^1\frac{d\lambda}
  {(u_3-u_4+u_4\lambda)^2}\\
&=\left(\frac1{u_1}+\frac1{u_4}\right)\frac{u_1+u_4}{D}
  -\frac1{u_1u_2}-\frac1{u_3u_4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore, with \(S=n+w+o\),
\[
S=\frac{(u_1+u_4)^2}{2u_1u_4D}.
\]

Set
\[
r=\frac{u_1}{u_4},\qquad q=u_4^2,\qquad
k=\frac1{2w},\qquad \ell=\frac1{2o}.
\]
Using \(u_2=k/u_1\) and \(u_3=\ell/u_4\), the preceding identity becomes
\[
q=\ell+\frac{k}{r^2}-\frac{(r+1)^2}{2Sr^2}.
\]
The two strict edge-slope inequalities above become, respectively,
\[
q>\ell-\frac{k}{r},
\qquad
\frac{k}{r}-\ell<rq.
\]
Substitution of the displayed formula for \(q\) gives the exact interval
\[
\frac{o}{n+w}<r<\frac{n+o}{w}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{dq}{dr}
=\frac{r+1-S/w}{Sr^3}<0
\]
throughout this interval.  Evaluating \(q\) at its excluded left endpoint yields
\[
u_4^2=q<
\frac{n(n+w)}{2wo^2}.
\]
Traversing the same sector in the reverse direction interchanges the two adjacent chord sectors and gives the companion bound
\[
u_1^2<\frac{n(n+o)}{2ow^2}.
\]
Thus every cyclic corner of an alternating-sector triangle satisfies both strict bounds, and all edge-slope inequalities used to obtain them have been recorded explicitly.

Apply the first bound at the \(N_x\) vertex:
\[
u_4^2<\frac{N_x(N_x+W_x)}{2W_xO^2}.
\]
Apply the reverse-direction companion bound at the \(N_y\) vertex, where the preceding and following chord sectors are \(O\) and \(W_y\):
\[
u_3^2<\frac{N_y(N_y+W_y)}{2W_yO^2}.
\]
But \(u_3u_4=1/(2O)\).  Multiplication therefore forces the necessary inequality
\[
W_xW_yO^2
<
N_xN_y(N_x+W_x)(N_y+W_y).
\]
Allowing a vertex on a fan ray only replaces the strict sign by a weak one.

It remains to verify the claimed rational polygon.  In the cyclic order
\[
U=(-4,6),\qquad V=(-4,-22),\qquad W=(3,-1),
\]
one has
\[
\det(U,V)=112,\qquad \det(V,W)=70,\qquad \det(W,U)=14.
\]
Hence
\[
0=\frac5{14}U+\frac1{14}V+\frac47W
\]
is a strict convex combination, so \(K\) is two-dimensional, convex, and has the fan point in its interior.  Its vertices lie respectively in \(W_x,O,W_y\).

The boundary intersections with the six rays are
\[
2r_0,\quad 2r_1,\quad 4r_2,\quad 4r_3,\quad
10r_4,\quad \frac52r_5.
\]
The sector areas follow directly from the shoelace formula.  Listing the nonzero boundary chain in each sector, one obtains
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{sector}&\text{boundary chain}&\text{area}\\ \hline
P&(2,0),(0,2)&2\\
W_x&(0,2),(-4,6),(-4,4)&8\\
N_x&(-4,4),(-4,0)&8\\
O&(-4,0),(-4,-22),(0,-10)&64\\
N_y&(0,-10),(5/2,-5/2)&25/2\\
W_y&(5/2,-5/2),(3,-1),(2,0)&7/2.
\end{array}
\]
This proves the sector table and the balance relation with \(e=9/2\).

For these five fixed values, the \(N\)-side of the necessary inequality is
\[
N_xN_y(N_x+W_x)(N_y+W_y)
=8\cdot\frac{25}{2}\cdot(8+8)
\cdot\left(\frac{25}{2}+\frac72\right)
=25600,
\]
whereas its \(W\)-side is
\[
W_xW_yO^2
=8\cdot\frac72\cdot64^2
=114688.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{25600}{114688}=\frac{25}{112}<1,
\]
the reverse of the necessary inequality.  Hence no triangle with vertices in \(P,N_x,N_y\) realizes the five fixed data.  The proposed one-parity finite replacement lemma is false.  The counterexample is itself a triangle in the complementary cyclic parity class \(W_x,O,W_y\), so that class cannot be omitted from any corrected triangle localization.  No broader localization claim follows from this counterexample.
