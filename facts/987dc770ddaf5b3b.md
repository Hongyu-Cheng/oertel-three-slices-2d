## Statement

Assume the full transformed triangular system of accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2`, `13c0df55864b79eb`, `64760c76cadff481`, and `a61bda3f7065e3e1` in the branch
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\leq\frac12,
\qquad
I_0=\{3\},
\qquad
I_1=\{1,2\},
\qquad
0<u_3<1,
\qquad
\frac AB\geq\frac3{10}.
\]
Thus the normalized areas of \(P_0,P_1,P_2\) are \(A,1,B\),
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,
\qquad
A+B>1,
\]
and all three literal row masses satisfy
\[
A u_i+c_i+Bv_i<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Use the common row and column labeling from `13c0df55864b79eb`, so that the first two rows of the middle vertex matrix \(L\) are
\[
(p,-a,-b),
\qquad
(-c,r,-d),
\]
where \(p,r,a,b,c,d>0\), \(p\geq a+b\), and \(r\geq c+d\).  If \(g_2\) is the centroid of \(P_2\), put
\[
s=\frac{2-(g_2)_3}{4}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\max\{p,r\}<22464s.}
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, with the normalized middle areas and either open or closed nonconstant caps,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|P_1\cap\{x_1>s\}\right|
 +\left|P_1\cap\{x_2>s\}\right|\\
&\qquad<
\left(\frac{22463}{22464}\right)^2(c_1+c_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Hence no sequence of such full strict systems can have
\(\max\{p,r\}/s\to\infty\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` supplies the compatible transformed triangles and the representation
\[
P_1=L\Delta,
\qquad
P_2=LV\Delta-\sigma,
\qquad
B=|\det V|>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` supplies the exact cap formulas and the equivalent endpoint compatibility system.  Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` supplies the displayed sign pattern and the inequalities \(p\geq a+b\), \(r\geq c+d\).  Finally, accepted fact `a61bda3f7065e3e1` gives
\[
(g_2)_1<0,
\qquad
(g_2)_2<0,
\qquad
(g_2)_3<0.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(s>1/2\).

Interchanging the first two coordinate indices and their two isolated positive columns if necessary, assume
\[
p=\max\{p,r\},
\qquad
0<r\leq p.
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
\alpha=p-a-b\geq0,
\qquad
\beta=r-c-d\geq0.
\tag{6}
\]
The exact unique-positive cap formulas are
\[
c_1=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)},
\qquad
c_2=\frac{r^2}{(r+c)(r+d)}.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(c_i<t\leq1/2\),
\[
ab>p\alpha,
\qquad
cd>r\beta.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12-c_1
&=\frac{ab-p\alpha}{2(p+a)(p+b)}
<\frac{b}{2p},\\
\frac12-c_2
&=\frac{cd-r\beta}{2(r+c)(r+d)}
<\frac{d}{2r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Define
\[
\varepsilon=\frac bp+\frac dr.
\tag{10}
\]
The first two strict literal rows and (9) give
\[
A(u_1+u_2)+B(v_1+v_2)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]

We next obtain an exact lower bound on \(\varepsilon\) from endpoint compatibility.  Let
\[
Q=P_1\cap\{x_1<0,\ x_2<0\}.
\]
Write a point of \(P_1=L\Delta\) as \(L\lambda\).  Using (6), the first two coordinates are
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=p(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)
   +b(\lambda_2-\lambda_3)+\alpha\lambda_2,\\
x_2&=-r(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)
   +d(\lambda_1-\lambda_3)+\beta\lambda_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(x_1<0\) and \(x_2<0\) imply
\[
-\frac dr<\lambda_1-\lambda_2<\frac bp.
\tag{13}
\]
Under normalized area on \(\Delta\), the variable
\(z=\lambda_1-\lambda_2\) has density \(1-|z|\) on \([-1,1]\): for \(z\geq0\), for example, one writes \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2+z\), and the interval
\(0\leq\lambda_2\leq(1-z)/2\) has normalized area density \(1-z\).  The negative side is symmetric.  Since \(b/p<1\) and \(d/r<1\), (13) yields
\[
|Q|\leq\varepsilon.
\tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
E_0=P_0\cap\{x_1<0,\ x_2<0\}.
\]
The union bound for the two closed endpoint caps gives
\[
|E_0|\geq A(1-u_1-u_2).
\tag{15}
\]
By (4) and compatibility, the translated half-scale set
\[
\frac{E_0+g_2}{2}
\]
is contained in \(Q\) and has area \(|E_0|/4\).  Hence (11), (14), and (15) imply
\[
\varepsilon
\geq |Q|
\geq\frac{|E_0|}{4}
\geq\frac{A(1-u_1-u_2)}4
>\frac A4-\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(A/B\geq3/10\) and \(A+B>1\),
\[
A>\frac3{13}.
\tag{17}
\]
Solving (16) and using (17) gives the uniform strict estimate
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon>\frac2{39}.}
\tag{18}
\]

The third row of \(L\), forced by the column sums, is
\[
(1-p+c,\ 1+a-r,\ 1+b+d).
\tag{19}
\]
Let \(W\) be the range of its three entries.  The third entry exceeds the first, and (6) gives
\[
W\geq p-r+b+2d+\beta.
\tag{20}
\]
Set \(\rho=r/p\), \(x=b/p\), and \(y=d/r\).  Dividing (20) by \(p\) and dropping \(\beta/p\) gives
\[
\frac Wp\geq1-\rho+x+2\rho y.
\tag{21}
\]
This is strictly larger than or equal to \(x+y\).  Indeed, if \(\rho\geq1/2\), the difference is
\(1-\rho+(2\rho-1)y\geq0\).  If \(\rho<1/2\), then \(y<1\), because \(c+d\leq r\) and \(c>0\), and the same difference is strictly greater than
\(1-\rho-(1-2\rho)=\rho\).  Equations (10), (18), and (21) therefore yield
\[
W>\frac{2p}{39},
\qquad
p<\frac{39}{2}W.
\tag{22}
\]

We now transfer this middle-row width to the third coordinate of \(P_2\) without excluding any compatibility boundary or determinant sign.  For a three-vector \(z\), write
\(\operatorname{rng}(z)=\max_jz_j-\min_jz_j\).  Exact compatibility in `64760c76cadff481` says that every midpoint of a column of \(U\) and a column of \(V\) lies in \(\Delta\).  Hence, for each row \(k\),
\[
\max_jU_{kj}+\max_jV_{kj}\leq2,
\qquad
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\geq0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\operatorname{rng}(V_{k*})\leq2.
\tag{23}
\]

For completeness, define
\[
D_V=
\begin{pmatrix}
V_{11}-V_{13}&V_{12}-V_{13}\\
V_{21}-V_{23}&V_{22}-V_{23}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
\]
The column-sum identities for \(V\) give
\(\det D_V=\det V\), while (23) bounds every entry of \(D_V\) in absolute value by \(2\).  If \(z\in\mathbb R^3\), put
\[
\xi=(z_1-z_3,z_2-z_3)
\]
and
\[
\omega=((zV)_1-(zV)_3,(zV)_2-(zV)_3).
\]
Then \(\omega=\xi D_V\).  Since every component of \(\omega\) has absolute value at most \(\operatorname{rng}(zV)\), inversion of (24) gives
\[
\|\xi\|_\infty\leq\frac{4}{B}\operatorname{rng}(zV).
\]
It follows that
\[
\operatorname{rng}(z)
\leq2\|\xi\|_\infty
\leq\frac8B\operatorname{rng}(zV).
\tag{25}
\]
Apply (25) to the third row of \(L\), and put
\[
W_2=\operatorname{rng}((LV)_{3*}).
\]
Translations do not affect range, so \(W_2\) is also the range of the third-coordinate vertex values on \(P_2\).  Equations (22) and (25) give
\[
W\leq\frac8B W_2.
\tag{26}
\]

It remains to bound \(W_2\) by the centroid displacement \(s\).  First, the three closed middle coordinate caps cover \(P_1\), because every point of \(P_1\) has coordinate sum one.  Hence
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\geq1.
\tag{27}
\]
Since \(c_1,c_2<t\), if
\[
E=A+B-1,
\]
then \(0<E\leq1/4\) and
\[
c_3>1-2t=\frac{1-4E}{9}.
\tag{28}
\]
The centroid cap inequality in `13c0df55864b79eb` gives \(u_3\geq4/9\).  The third strict literal row, (28), and the definition of \(t\) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
9B\left(\frac49-v_3\right)
&>2E+9c_3\\
&>1-2E\\
&\geq\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Thus
\[
\frac49-v_3>\frac1{18B}.
\tag{30}
\]

We record the exact one-dimensional estimate converting (30) into a width bound.  For any nonconstant triple of range \(R\), the complete formulas in `64760c76cadff481` show that
\(\tau\mapsto\mathcal C(z;\tau)\) is \(2/R\)-Lipschitz: on either quadratic branch its derivative has absolute value at most \(2/R\), and the repeated-value formulas obey the same bound.  Also, if the three entries have mean zero, their cap at threshold zero has fraction at least \(4/9\).  In the one-positive case this is the exact inequality
\[
\frac{(u+v)^2}{(2u+v)(u+2v)}\geq\frac49,
\]
whose difference after cross multiplication is \((u-v)^2\geq0\); the other sign cases give at least \(1/2\) or the full cap.

Let \(y_1,y_2,y_3\) be the third-coordinate vertex values on \(P_2\), with mean
\[
m=(g_2)_3=2-4s<0.
\]
The centered triple \(y-m\mathbf1\) has mean zero and range \(W_2>0\), so the preceding estimate gives
\[
\frac49-v_3
\leq
\mathcal C(y-m\mathbf1;0)
-\mathcal C(y-m\mathbf1;-m)
\leq\frac{2(-m)}{W_2}
<\frac{8s}{W_2}.
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (30) and (31) yields
\[
W_2<144Bs.
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (22), (26), and (32) now give
\[
p<\frac{39}{2}W
\leq\frac{156}{B}W_2
<22464s.
\]
This proves (1), including every nonsingular endpoint matrix and every exact compatibility boundary.

Finally, the exact unique-positive formulas in `64760c76cadff481` give
\[
\left|P_1\cap\{x_1>s\}\right|
=c_1\left(1-\frac{s}{p}\right)_+^2,
\qquad
\left|P_1\cap\{x_2>s\}\right|
=c_2\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)_+^2.
\tag{33}
\]
Since both \(p/s\) and \(r/s\) are strictly below \(22464\), (33) implies (2).  The exact lost fraction relative to the zero-level capacity is
\[
1-\left(\frac{22463}{22464}\right)^2
=\frac{44927}{22464^2}>0.
\]
This is the required uniform shifted-cap decrement and rules out support-ratio divergence.
