## Statement

Write \(\mathcal L^k\) for \(k\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  For a
Borel set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
E_z=\{r\in\mathbb R^d:(z,r)\in E\},
\qquad
\mu(E)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathcal L^d(E_z).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be compact and convex,
let \(q_0=(b,v_0)\in C\), let \(0<\alpha<1\), and put
\[
D=(1-\alpha)C+\alpha q_0.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose that Borel maps
\[
\rho:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
a:\mathbb R^n\to C
\]
satisfy
\[
\pi_{\mathbb R^n}a(q)
=b+\frac{\rho(q)-q}{\alpha},
\qquad
\mathcal L^n(\rho^{-1}(z))\leq1
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
Then every affine \(L:\mathbb R^{n+d}\to\mathbb R\) satisfies
\[
\mu(C\cap\{L\geq0\})
\geq
\mathcal L^{n+d}\left\{
(q,r)\in D:
L(q,r)\geq
\alpha\bigl(L(q_0)-L(a(q))\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the reflected three-slice specialization, let
\(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact
two-dimensional convex bodies satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)
\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\right),
\qquad
K_v=\{r:(v,r)\in K\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
K_v=
\begin{cases}
(1-v)A_0+vA_1,&0\leq v\leq1,\\
(2-v)A_1+(v-1)A_2,&1\leq v\leq2.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
For an affine \(L:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\), define
\[
H_1=\{r\in\mathbb R^2:L(1,r)\geq0\},
\qquad
m_i=\max_{r\in A_i}L(i,r),
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
M_L(v)=\max_{r\in K_v}L(v,r)
=
\begin{cases}
(1-v)m_0+vm_1,&0\leq v\leq1,\\
(2-v)m_1+(v-1)m_2,&1\leq v\leq2.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
The exact reflected paired-support inequality is
\[
\boxed{
|A_1\cap H_1|
\geq
\frac18\int_0^2
\left|
\left\{r\in K_v:
L(v,r)+M_L(2-v)\geq0
\right\}
\right|\,dv.}
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

Fix \(L\), put \(H=\{L\geq0\}\), and let
\[
E=\left\{(q,r)\in D:
L(q,r)\geq\alpha\bigl(L(q_0)-L(a(q))\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The function \(q\mapsto L(q_0)-L(a(q))\) is Borel.  Hence \(E\) is
Borel.  Its sections \(E_q\) are Borel, and
\[
e(q):=\mathcal L^d(E_q)
\tag{12}
\]
is measurable by Tonelli applied to \(\mathbf 1_E\).  For
\(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
c_z=\mathcal L^d((C\cap H)_z).
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(C\) is compact, only finitely many integer fibers of \(C\)
are nonempty, every \(c_z\) is finite, and
\(\mu(C\cap H)=\sum_zc_z<\infty\).

Take \(w=(q,r)\in E\).  Since \(w\in D\), there is \(y\in C\) such that
\[
w=(1-\alpha)y+\alpha q_0.
\]
Define
\[
\widetilde w=w+\alpha(a(q)-q_0)
=(1-\alpha)y+\alpha a(q).
\tag{14}
\]
Convexity gives \(\widetilde w\in C\), while (3) gives
\[
\pi_{\mathbb R^n}\widetilde w
=q+\alpha\bigl(\pi_{\mathbb R^n}a(q)-b\bigr)
=\rho(q).
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(L\) is affine,
\[
L(\widetilde w)
=L(w)+\alpha\bigl(L(a(q))-L(q_0)\bigr)
\geq0.
\tag{16}
\]
For fixed \(q\), the continuous coordinate in (14) is translated by
the fixed vector \(\alpha(\pi_{\mathbb R^d}a(q)-v_0)\).  Thus this map
preserves \(\mathcal L^d\) and sends \(E_q\) into
\((C\cap H)_{\rho(q)}\).  Consequently
\[
e(q)\leq c_{\rho(q)}.
\tag{17}
\]
This also covers an empty target fiber, in which case \(e(q)=c_{\rho(q)}=0\).

The Borel sets \(\rho^{-1}(z)\) partition \(\mathbb R^n\).  Tonelli,
(3), and (17) now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L^{n+d}(E)
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e(q)\,dq\\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
  \int_{\rho^{-1}(z)}e(q)\,dq\\
&\leq\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
  c_z\,\mathcal L^n(\rho^{-1}(z))
\leq\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}c_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
This is (4).  All inequalities are weak: equality in (11) gives
\(L(\widetilde w)=0\), which belongs to the boundary of the closed
halfspace \(H\).

We next prove the exact section formula (7).  A point of \(K_v\) has
the form
\[
r=\lambda_0r_0+\lambda_1r_1+\lambda_2r_2,
\quad
\lambda_i\geq0,\quad
\sum_i\lambda_i=1,\quad
v=\lambda_1+2\lambda_2,
\tag{19}
\]
with \(r_i\in A_i\).  If \(0\leq v\leq1\), then
\(\lambda_0-\lambda_2=1-v\geq0\), and (5) gives
\[
r=(\lambda_0-\lambda_2)r_0+\lambda_1r_1
+2\lambda_2\frac{r_0+r_2}{2}
\in(1-v)A_0+vA_1.
\]
The reverse inclusion follows directly from the convex hull.  If
\(1\leq v\leq2\), then \(\lambda_2-\lambda_0=v-1\geq0\), and similarly
\[
r=\lambda_1r_1+2\lambda_0\frac{r_0+r_2}{2}
+(\lambda_2-\lambda_0)r_2
\in(2-v)A_1+(v-1)A_2.
\]
Again the reverse inclusion is immediate.  This proves (7), including
\(K_1=A_1\).

Compactness gives points \(u_i\in A_i\) with \(L(i,u_i)=m_i\).  Define
\[
p_v=
\begin{cases}
\bigl(v,(1-v)u_0+vu_1\bigr),&0\leq v\leq1,\\
\bigl(v,(2-v)u_1+(v-1)u_2\bigr),&1\leq v\leq2.
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]
Formula (7) and the definition of \(m_i\) give the corresponding
piecewise upper bound in (9) for every point of \(K_v\).  They also show
\(p_v\in\{v\}\times K_v\), and affinity shows that \(p_v\) attains that
bound.  This proves (9); in particular \(M_L\) is continuous and every
set under the integral in (10) is a Borel section.

Choose any \(v_0\in A_1\), set \(q_0=(1,v_0)\), and take
\(\alpha=1/2\).  Define
\[
\rho(q)=
\begin{cases}
1,&q=3/2,\\
\lfloor q+1/2\rfloor,&q\ne3/2,
\end{cases}
\qquad
s(q)=1+2(\rho(q)-q),
\qquad
a(q)=p_{s(q)}.
\tag{21}
\]
For the unmodified rounding cells \(s(q)\in(0,2]\), and at \(q=3/2\)
one has \(s(q)=0\).  Thus \(a(q)\in K\) for every \(q\), and \(a\) is
Borel.  Moreover
\[
\pi_{\mathbb R}a(q)=1+\frac{\rho(q)-q}{1/2}.
\tag{22}
\]
Every preimage \(\rho^{-1}(z)\) has length one: the usual half-open
interval \([z-1/2,z+1/2)\) is unchanged except that \(3/2\) is moved
from the \(z=2\) fiber to the \(z=1\) fiber.  In particular,
\[
\rho^{-1}(1)=[1/2,3/2].
\tag{23}
\]

Now \(D=(K+q_0)/2\) has base projection \([1/2,3/2]\).  Therefore every
source section in (11) is sent by (14) to the single target fiber
\((K\cap H)_1=A_1\cap H_1\).  The fiber estimate (17), before summing
over target integers, gives
\[
\mathcal L^3(E)
=\int_{1/2}^{3/2}e(q)\,dq
\leq |A_1\cap H_1|.
\tag{24}
\]

Finally parameterize \(D\) by
\[
(v,r)\longmapsto
\left(\frac{1+v}{2},\frac{v_0+r}{2}\right),
\qquad (v,r)\in K.
\tag{25}
\]
This homothety has three-dimensional Jacobian \(2^{-3}=1/8\).  For
\(q=(1+v)/2\), equations (21) and (23) give
\[
a(q)=p_{2-v},
\qquad
L(a(q))=M_L(2-v).
\tag{26}
\]
Also, by affinity,
\[
L\left(\frac{q_0+(v,r)}2\right)
=\frac{L(q_0)+L(v,r)}2.
\]
Hence the source condition (11) is exactly
\[
L(v,r)+M_L(2-v)\geq0.
\tag{27}
\]
The change of variables (25) and Fubini therefore give
\[
\mathcal L^3(E)
=\frac18\int_0^2
\left|
\left\{r\in K_v:
L(v,r)+M_L(2-v)\geq0
\right\}
\right|\,dv.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (24) and (28) proves (10).  The convention in (21) treats
both base endpoints exactly, and the weak inequality in (27) includes
all closed-halfspace boundary points.
