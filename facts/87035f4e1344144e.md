## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes which are compatible, meaning that they are the three integer-height sections of a compact polytope in \(\mathbb R^3\). By accepted fact \(8ad0785e5b4ae9ef\), compatibility is equivalent to
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Write \(a_i=|A_i|\), \(T=a_0+a_1+a_2\), and
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i).
\]
If
\[
2\max\{a_0,a_2\}\le a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\},
\]
then there exists \(z\in A_1\) such that
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge \frac{2T}{9}.
\]
Moreover, every compatible triple with \(a_0=a_2\) satisfies the displayed area condition. In particular, the conclusion holds whenever \(A_0=A_2\).

## Proof

Put \(t=2T/9\). Suppose for contradiction that every point of \(\{1\}\times A_1\) has depth strictly below \(t\).

The finite-cover construction in accepted fact \(8f5f00ddd4f22d2f\) is slice-local: its construction for a fixed slice uses only the strict low-depth assumption on that slice. Indeed, for each \(y\in\{1\}\times A_1\), choose a proper closed halfspace of mass below \(t\) containing \(y\). Enlarging it by a positive amount tending to zero puts \(y\) in its interior while its mass decreases to the original mass by continuity from above, so a sufficiently small enlargement still has mass below \(t\). Compactness gives a finite interior cover of the middle slice, and the planar Helly argument in \(8f5f00ddd4f22d2f\) reduces it to at most three members. Consequently, there are \(q\in\{1,2,3\}\) and proper closed halfspaces \(H_1,\ldots,H_q\) such that
\[
\mu(H_r)<t\quad(1\le r\le q),
\qquad
\{1\}\times A_1\subseteq\bigcup_{r=1}^q\operatorname{int}H_r.
\]

Write
\[
H_r=\{p\in\mathbb R^3:\ell_r(p)\le0\},
\]
where \(\ell_r\) is a nonconstant affine function, and define
\[
B_{ri}=\{z\in A_i:\ell_r(i,z)\le0\},
\qquad
b_{ri}=|B_{ri}|.
\]
Then
\[
\mu(H_r)=b_{r0}+b_{r1}+b_{r2}.
\]
The interior cover implies
\[
a_1\le\sum_{r=1}^q b_{r1}.
\]
This uses only that every strict middle section
\[
\{z\in A_1:\ell_r(1,z)<0\}
\]
is contained in \(B_{r1}\). No equality between open and closed section areas is required. Thus the argument also covers the degenerate case in which the boundary of \(H_r\) contains the entire middle plane.

Now fix \((z_0,z_2)\in A_0\times A_2\). Compatibility gives
\[
\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\in A_1.
\]
Hence the interior cover supplies an index \(r\) for which
\[
\ell_r\left(1,\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)<0.
\]
Because \(\ell_r\) is affine,
\[
\ell_r\left(1,\frac{z_0+z_2}{2}\right)
=
\frac{\ell_r(0,z_0)+\ell_r(2,z_2)}{2}.
\]
At least one of \(\ell_r(0,z_0)\) and \(\ell_r(2,z_2)\) is therefore strictly negative. In particular, \(z_0\in B_{r0}\) or \(z_2\in B_{r2}\). Thus, pointwise on \(A_0\times A_2\),
\[
1\le
\sum_{r=1}^q
\left(\mathbf 1_{B_{r0}}(z_0)+\mathbf 1_{B_{r2}}(z_2)\right).
\]
Integrating with respect to product Lebesgue measure and using Fubini gives
\[
a_0a_2
\le
\sum_{r=1}^q\left(a_2b_{r0}+a_0b_{r2}\right).
\]

Let
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_2\},
\qquad
m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
Since both areas are positive, the preceding inequality yields
\[
Mm\le M\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2}),
\]
and hence
\[
m\le\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2}).
\]
On the other hand, the strict mass bounds, the fact that \(q\le3\), and the middle-slice cover give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=1}^q(b_{r0}+b_{r2})
&=\sum_{r=1}^q\mu(H_r)-\sum_{r=1}^q b_{r1}\\
&<qt-\sum_{r=1}^q b_{r1}\\
&\le3t-a_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The strict inequality is preserved even when \(q<3\). Combining the last two displays,
\[
m<3t-a_1
=\frac{2(M+m+a_1)}{3}-a_1
=\frac{2M+2m-a_1}{3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
a_1+m<2M,
\]
contradicting the assumed inequality \(2M\le a_1+m\). Hence some point of the middle slice has depth at least \(2T/9\).

Finally, if \(a_0=a_2=a\), the planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality and compatibility give
\[
\sqrt{a_1}
\ge
\sqrt{\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right|}
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_2}}{2}
=\sqrt a.
\]
Thus \(a_1\ge a\), so
\[
2\max\{a_0,a_2\}=2a\le a_1+a
=a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
When \(A_0=A_2\), the same conclusion follows directly from
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=A_0\subseteq A_1.
\]
