## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},\qquad |\Delta|=1,
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal C(z;\tau)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:z\cdot\lambda\ge\tau\}\right|.
\]
For every \(n\), let \(U_n,V_n,R_n\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\),
\(\tau_n\in\mathbb R^3\), and \(\delta_n>0\) satisfy
\[
\det U_n\ne0,\qquad \det V_n\ne0,\qquad \det R_n\ne0,
\]
\[
A_n:=|\det U_n|\le B_n:=|\det V_n|,\qquad
r_n:=\frac{A_n}{B_n},\qquad S_n:=\frac1{B_n},
\]
\[
0<r_n<\frac38,\qquad
1-r_n<S_n<1+\frac{r_n}{2},
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3(R_n)_{ij}=\delta_n
\qquad(j=1,2,3).
\]
Put
\[
t_n=\frac{2(1+A_n+B_n)}9
\]
and
\[
M_{i,n}
=\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)
+A_n\mathcal C((R_nU_n)_{i*};-\tau_{i,n})
+B_n\mathcal C((R_nV_n)_{i*};\tau_{i,n}).
\]
Assume \(r_n\to0\), and define
\[
\varepsilon_n=\frac{\delta_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F},
\qquad
\gamma(x)=\min\left\{\frac1{18},\frac{5x^2}{18}\right\}.
\]
Then, for every \(n\),
\[
\max_{1\le i\le3}\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)
\ge\frac49+\gamma(\varepsilon_n)
\]
and consequently
\[
\max_{1\le i\le3}(M_{i,n}-t_n)
\ge\frac49+\gamma(\varepsilon_n)-t_n.
\]
In particular, if
\[
\underline\varepsilon:=\liminf_n\varepsilon_n>0,
\]
then
\[
\liminf_n\max_i(M_{i,n}-t_n)
\ge\gamma(\underline\varepsilon)>0.
\]
More generally, the asymptotic near-strict condition
\[
\limsup_n\max_i(M_{i,n}-t_n)\le0
\]
forces \(\varepsilon_n\to0\). Therefore accepted fact
`91c3b1cc058a6768` applies: every cluster point of
\(R_n/\lVert R_n\rVert_F\) is
\[
\frac1{\sqrt{18}}\,\Pi(3I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top)
\]
for a permutation matrix \(\Pi\), every middle cap tends to \(4/9\),
and every large-end cap tends to zero.

## Proof

We first prove a quantitative form of the centroid-cap inequality. Let
\(z=(a,b,c)\ne0\), sort its entries so that \(a\le b\le c\), and suppose
\[
w:=a+b+c>0.
\]
We claim
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)
\ge
\frac49+
\min\left\{\frac1{18},
\frac{5w^2}{18\lVert z\rVert_2^2}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The complete cap formulas, including repeated entries and support contact,
are those of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.

If \(a\ge0\), the cap is all of \(\Delta\), so (1) is immediate. Suppose
\(a<0\le b\le c\), and put \(u=-a>0\). Since \(w>0\), one has
\(b+c>u\). The two-nonnegative-vertex cap formula gives
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)
=1-\frac{u^2}{(u+b)(u+c)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
(u+b)(u+c)-2u^2
=u(b+c-u)+bc>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C(z;0)>1/2=4/9+1/18\), which proves (1) in this case.

It remains to consider \(a\le b<0<c\). Write
\[
u=-a\ge v=-b>0,\qquad c=u+v+w.
\]
The one-positive-vertex formula gives
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)=\frac{c^2}{(c+u)(c+v)}.
\]
An exact expansion yields
\[
9c^2-4(c+u)(c+v)
=(u-v)^2+6(u+v)w+5w^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Hence
\[
\mathcal C(z;0)-\frac49
\ge\frac{5w^2}{9(c+u)(c+v)}.
\tag{3}
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
c+u\le\sqrt2\,(c^2+u^2)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt2\,\lVert z\rVert_2,
\]
and likewise \(c+v\le\sqrt2\,\lVert z\rVert_2\). Therefore
\[
(c+u)(c+v)\le2\lVert z\rVert_2^2.
\]
Substitution in (3) proves the second alternative in (1), and taking
the minimum with \(1/18\) proves (1) uniformly. This also covers
arbitrarily close approaches to \(b=0\); the case \(b=0\) itself was
already included in the preceding case.

Fix \(n\), and write
\[
w_{i,n}=\sum_{j=1}^3(R_n)_{ij}.
\]
The column-sum identities imply
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3w_{i,n}=3\delta_n.
\]
Thus some row \(i=i(n)\) satisfies \(w_{i,n}\ge\delta_n>0\). Its
Euclidean norm is at most \(\lVert R_n\rVert_F\), so
\[
\frac{w_{i,n}}{\lVert(R_n)_{i*}\rVert_2}
\ge
\frac{\delta_n}{\lVert R_n\rVert_F}
=\varepsilon_n.
\]
Apply (1) to this nonzero row. Since \(\gamma\) is nondecreasing,
\[
\mathcal C((R_n)_{i*};0)
\ge\frac49+\gamma(\varepsilon_n).
\]
This proves the first asserted bound. The two endpoint terms defining
\(M_{i,n}\) are nonnegative, so the second bound follows immediately.

Finally, \(r_n\to0\) and
\[
1-r_n<S_n<1+\frac{r_n}{2}
\]
give \(S_n\to1\), hence \(B_n=1/S_n\to1\) and
\(A_n=r_nB_n\to0\). Therefore \(t_n\to4/9\). Taking lower limits in
the pointwise bound proves the assertion when
\(\underline\varepsilon>0\). If instead
\[
\limsup_n\max_i(M_{i,n}-t_n)\le0,
\]
the same pointwise bound and \(t_n\to4/9\) force
\(\gamma(\varepsilon_n)\to0\). By the definition of \(\gamma\), this
is equivalent to \(\varepsilon_n\to0\). All hypotheses of accepted
fact `91c3b1cc058a6768` are now satisfied, and its conclusions give
the stated balanced-cover classification and vanishing of the three
large-end caps.
