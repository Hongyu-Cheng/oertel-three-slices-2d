## Statement

Use the opposite notation of accepted fact `18e8f69f5a782c16`, the \(A_0\) full-chord coordinates of accepted fact `989c9b585d2b42cc`, the \(A_2\) full-chord coordinates of accepted fact `fa0e57b225178b3b`, and the physical Jacobian \(\Delta\) of accepted fact `2b42506f5de09be1`.  Thus
\[
\widehat A=(0,A),\qquad E=(\beta,0),\qquad C=(B,0),
\]
\[
A=\alpha+J,\qquad B=\beta+H,\qquad
\tau+H=\delta-\beta,\qquad S=\delta+2,
\]
and the lower endpoint of the \(V=0\) chord of \(A_2\) is
\[
q^*:(U,V)=(u,0).
\]
Assume
\[
A<B,\qquad u\ge\tau+H,\qquad B\le S-u.
\]
Then every pair of compact convex endpoint polygons realizing these full chords satisfies
\[
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap R_{jk}\right|
\ge
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{AH^2}{8\Delta(B-A)},&A\le\beta,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\frac{AH}{8\Delta},&\beta\le A<B.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
The two values agree at \(A=\beta\).  At the legal stratum \(A=B\), the second value in (1), \(AH/(8\Delta)\), remains valid.  Consequently the endpoint-only elimination test is
\[
\frac{AH}{8\Delta}
\min\left\{1,\frac{H}{B-A}\right\}>y
\tag{2}
\]
when \(A<B\), with \(AH/(8\Delta)>y\) when \(A=B\).  The fixed-datum triangle of accepted fact `5ef0bf9afb2503ba` is the first case of (1).

Without \(A\le\beta\), the registered stronger bound is false even with strict \(A<B\), equality \(u=\tau+H\), strict \(B<S-u\), all three exact full chords on each endpoint polygon, and all literal endpoint incidences.  An exact rational counterexample is given in the proof.

## Proof

First prove (1).  Suppose \(A<\beta\).  On the segment \([\widehat A,C]\), the coordinate sum \(X+Y\) increases affinely from \(A\) to \(B\).  Hence there is a unique point \(D\) with \(D_X+D_Y=\beta\), namely
\[
D=\frac{H}{B-A}\widehat A
+\frac{\beta-A}{B-A}C.
\]
Its height is
\[
D_Y=\frac{AH}{B-A}.
\]
Convexity gives
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{E,C,D\}\subseteq A_0,
\]
and
\[
|T|_{\rm coord}
=\frac12H D_Y
=\frac{AH^2}{2(B-A)}.
\tag{3}
\]
The same construction applies at \(A=\beta\), with \(D=\widehat A\).

If \(\beta<A<B\), instead take
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{\widehat A,E,C\}\subseteq A_0.
\]
Now
\[
|T|_{\rm coord}=\frac{AH}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
This triangle also applies at \(A=B\).  In either construction every point \((X,Y)\in\operatorname{int}T\) satisfies
\[
Y>0,\qquad X+Y>\beta,\qquad X<B.
\tag{5}
\]
For the first construction, the first two vertices of the edge \(ED\) have \(X+Y=\beta\), and all other interior points lie strictly above that line.  For the second construction, \(E\) is the only vertex with sum \(\beta\) when \(A>\beta\), while the edge \(\widehat A E\) has sum \(\beta\) only in the tie \(A=\beta\).  In both cases \(C\) is the only point of \(T\) with \(X=B\).

The endpoint \(q^*\) belongs to \(A_2\).  For the midpoint of \(q^*\) and any point of \(\operatorname{int}T\), the coordinate definitions give
\[
2r_j=X+u-S,\qquad
2r_k=\delta-X-Y-u,\qquad
2r_\ell=Y.
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(B\le S-u\), \(u\ge\tau+H=\delta-\beta\), and (5) holds,
\[
2r_j=X+u-S<B+u-S\le0,\qquad
2r_k=\delta-X-Y-u<\delta-\beta-u\le0,\qquad
2r_\ell>0.
\]
More directly, the first two quantities in (6) are strictly negative because \(X<B\) and \(X+Y>\beta\).  Thus
\[
\frac{\operatorname{int}T+q^*}{2}
\subseteq
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap R_{jk}.
\tag{7}
\]
Coordinate area is \(\Delta\) times physical area, and the physical homothety in (7) has area factor \(1/4\).  Dividing (3) and (4) by \(4\Delta\) proves (1).  This also checks all equality strata literally.  At \(u=\tau+H\), the strict inequality \(X+Y>\beta\) makes \(r_k<0\).  At \(B=S-u\), the strict inequality \(X<B\) makes \(r_j<0\).  At \(A=B\), the triangle in (4) is nondegenerate and gives \(AH/(8\Delta)\); the registered expression is undefined because its denominator vanishes.

It remains to refute the registered coefficient when \(\beta<A<B\).  Work in a common physical affine plane with
\[
f_j(x,y)=x,\qquad f_\ell(x,y)=y,\qquad
f_k(x,y)=-1-x-y,
\]
and offsets
\[
L_j=L_\ell=0,\qquad L_k=-2.
\]
Then \(\delta=1\), \(\Delta=1\), and \(S=3\).  On \(A_0\), \((X,Y)=(p_j,p_\ell)=(x,y)\).  On \(A_2\),
\[
U=-q_k=3+x+y,\qquad V=q_\ell=y,
\qquad q_j=U-V-3.
\tag{8}
\]

Take
\[
\alpha=\frac{989}{1000},\quad
J=\frac{10999}{10^6},\quad
A=\frac{999999}{10^6},
\]
\[
\beta=\frac12,\quad H=\frac12,\quad B=1,
\qquad
\gamma=\tau=0,\quad K=\frac1{100}.
\tag{9}
\]
In cyclic order, let \(A_0\) be the convex hull of
\[
\left(-\frac1{100000},\frac{1988999}{2000000}\right),
\left(0,\frac{999999}{10^6}\right),
(1,0),
\left(\frac{1001}{1000},-\frac{1999999}{2000000000}\right),
\left(\frac{101}{100},-\frac1{100}\right),
\left(\frac12,0\right),
\left(0,\frac{989}{1000}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The consecutive cross products are
\[
-\frac{550949999}{10^{11}},\
-\frac1{2000000000},\
-\frac1{200000000000},\
-\frac{900000051}{200000000000},\
-\frac{49939}{100000},\
-\frac{136993}{50000000},\
-\frac{10999}{10^{11}}.
\tag{11}
\]
They are all negative, so (10) is strictly convex in the displayed order.  Direct intersection with the three cutting lines gives exactly
\[
X=0,\quad
Y\in\left[\frac{989}{1000},\frac{999999}{10^6}\right],
\]
\[
Y=0,\quad X\in\left[\frac12,1\right],
\]
\[
X+Y=1,\quad
(X,Y)=(1+t,-t),\quad
t\in\left[0,\frac1{100}\right].
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, the fourth vertex in (10) has \(X+Y=1+1/2000000000\), while every other vertex not on the displayed diagonal chord has \(X+Y<1\).  The first vertex has \(X<0\); hence all four required \(A_0\) sign cells have positive area.

For \(A_2\), take
\[
u=\frac12,\quad H_2=\frac{11}{100},\qquad
v=3,\quad J_2=\frac1{10},\qquad
w_0=2,\quad K_2=\frac1{10}.
\tag{13}
\]
In cyclic \((U,V)\)-order, let \(A_2\) be the affine image under (8) of the convex hull of
\[
\left(-\frac1{100000},\frac{61}{20}\right),
\left(0,\frac{31}{10}\right),
\left(\frac{61}{100},0\right),
(1,-2),
\left(\frac{95001}{100000},-\frac{205001}{100000}\right),
\left(\frac9{10},-\frac{21}{10}\right),
\left(\frac12,0\right),
(0,3).
\tag{14}
\]
Its consecutive cross products are
\[
-\frac{30531}{10^6},\
-\frac{11}{1000},\
-\frac{1194839}{10^7},\
-\frac1{500000},\
-\frac{125017}{10^6},\
-\frac3{20},\
-\frac{2497}{100000},\
-\frac1{10^6}.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus (14) is strictly convex, and its full sections are exactly
\[
U=0,\quad V\in\left[3,\frac{31}{10}\right],
\]
\[
V=0,\quad U\in\left[\frac12,\frac{61}{100}\right],
\]
\[
U-V=3,\quad
(U,V)=(3-t,-t),\quad
t\in\left[2,\frac{21}{10}\right].
\tag{16}
\]
The fifth vertex in (14) has \(U-V=150001/50000>3\), and the first vertex has \(U<0\), so all four required \(A_2\) sign cells have positive area.  Also
\[
\theta=S-u-H_2=\frac{239}{100}>0,\qquad
\psi=S-w_0-K_2=\frac9{10}>0.
\]
Hence all literal incidences hold.  The branch assumptions are
\[
A<B,\qquad
u=\frac12=\tau+H,\qquad
B=1<S-u=\frac52.
\tag{17}
\]

Finally, bound the whole midpoint body, not merely its \(R_{jk}\)-part.  The \(p_j\)- and \(p_\ell\)-widths of (10) are
\[
\frac{101001}{100000},\qquad
\frac{1009999}{10^6}.
\]
By (8), the \(q_j\)- and \(q_\ell\)-widths of (14) are
\[
\frac{305001}{50000},\qquad \frac{26}{5}.
\]
Therefore the image of \((A_0+A_2)/2\) in the \((r_j,r_\ell)\)-plane lies in a rectangle whose side lengths are at most
\[
\frac{711003}{200000}<4,\qquad
\frac{6209999}{2000000}<4.
\]
Since \(\Delta=1\),
\[
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap R_{jk}\right|
\le
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right|
<16.
\tag{18}
\]
But the registered right side equals
\[
\frac{AH^2}{8\Delta(B-A)}
=\frac{999999}{32}>16.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (17)--(19) are an exact counterexample to the proposed formula under its stated hypotheses.  Formula (1) is the corrected endpoint-uniform conclusion.
