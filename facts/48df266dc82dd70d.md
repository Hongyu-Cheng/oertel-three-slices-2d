## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad g=\left(\frac13,\frac13\right),
\]
and let \(Q\) be the rational quadrilateral with cyclic vertices
\[
q_0=\left(\frac1{20},\frac1{20}\right),\quad
q_1=\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{21}{400}\right),\quad
q_2=\left(\frac12,\frac{23}{400}\right),\quad
q_3=\left(\frac1{10},\frac{21}{400}\right).
\]
For every rational number
\[
0<\alpha\le \frac1{100000},
\]
set
\[
M_\alpha=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\alpha\\
\alpha&1+\alpha^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_\alpha=g+M_\alpha(\Delta-g),
\]
and use the canonical slice notation
\[
A_0=\Delta,\qquad A_1=B_\alpha,\qquad A_2=Q.
\]
Equivalently, the cyclic vertices of \(A_1\) are
\[
b_0=\left(-\frac\alpha3,-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^2}{3}\right),
\quad
b_1=\left(1-\frac\alpha3,\frac{2\alpha-\alpha^2}{3}\right),
\quad
b_2=\left(\frac{2\alpha}{3},1-\frac\alpha3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{3}\right).
\]
These are compatible rational two-dimensional polytopes. The two large slices are distinct nonhomothetic triangles, all three bodies are non-centrally-symmetric, and no edge of any one body is parallel to an edge of another body. Thus this geometry has neither a centrally symmetric slice nor a shared edge direction.

Let
\[
S_\alpha=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\qquad
T=|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|=\frac{16027}{16000},
\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{16027}{72000}.
\]
Then
\[
h_{S_\alpha}((1,g))
\ge t+\frac1{11700}-|\Delta\setminus B_\alpha|
>t+\frac{383}{5{,}850{,}000}>t.
\]
Consequently no collection of halfspaces of \(S_\alpha\)-mass strictly below \(t\), even an arbitrarily large collection, can cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\) by its interiors. In particular this entire transverse nonhomothetic escape family cannot supply the three strict-low halfspaces required on the middle slice.

## Proof

First audit the rational geometry. One has
\[
\det M_\alpha=1(1+\alpha^2)-\alpha^2=1,
\]
so \(B_\alpha\) is a nondegenerate rational triangle of area \(1/2\), and its centroid is \(g\). The area and strict convexity computation in accepted fact a58dc34569c34d62, specialized to \(\varepsilon=1/20\), gives
\[
|Q|=\frac{27}{2}\left(\frac1{20}\right)^3
=\frac{27}{16000}.
\]
This proves the displayed values of \(T\) and \(t\).

The edge slopes of \(A_0\) are \(0,\infty,-1\). The three edge directions of \(A_1\) are the images under \(M_\alpha\) of the three edge directions of \(\Delta\), and their slopes are
\[
\alpha,
\qquad
-\frac{1-\alpha+\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}<-1,
\qquad
\frac{1+\alpha^2}{\alpha}>100000.
\]
The four edge slopes of \(Q\) are
\[
\frac1{200},\qquad -\frac1{10},\qquad
\frac1{80},\qquad \frac1{20}.
\]
The stated range of \(\alpha\) makes these three lists pairwise disjoint. Thus the bodies are pairwise edge-transverse, and in particular \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) are not homothetic. Neither triangle is centrally symmetric. Moreover
\[
q_0+q_2=\left(\frac{11}{20},\frac{43}{400}\right)
\ne
\left(\frac{13}{20},\frac{21}{200}\right)=q_1+q_3,
\]
so \(Q\) is not centrally symmetric either.

We next prove compatibility exactly. Put
\[
\lambda=\frac1{(1+\alpha)^2},
\qquad
C_\lambda=g+\lambda(\Delta-g).
\]
Since \(\alpha<1/100\),
\[
\lambda>\left(\frac{100}{101}\right)^2
=\frac{10000}{10201}>\frac{37}{40}.
\tag{1}
\]
We claim that \(C_\lambda\subseteq B_\alpha\). Indeed,
\[
M_\alpha^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\alpha^2&-\alpha\\
-\alpha&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the vertices \(v_0=(0,0),v_1=(1,0),v_2=(0,1)\) of \(\Delta\), let
\[
r_j=g+\lambda M_\alpha^{-1}(v_j-g).
\]
Writing planar barycentric coordinates relative to \(\Delta\) as
\((x,y,1-x-y)\), direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{bar}(r_0)
&=\frac13\bigl(
1-\lambda(1-\alpha+\alpha^2),
1-\lambda(1-\alpha),
1+\lambda(2-2\alpha+\alpha^2)
\bigr),\\
\operatorname{bar}(r_1)
&=\frac13\bigl(
1+\lambda(2+\alpha+2\alpha^2),
1-\lambda(1+2\alpha),
1-\lambda(1-\alpha+2\alpha^2)
\bigr),\\
\operatorname{bar}(r_2)
&=\frac13\bigl(
0,
1+\lambda(2+\alpha),
2-\lambda(2+\alpha)
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Every entry in (2) is nonnegative. For the only non-immediate entries, this follows after multiplying by \((1+\alpha)^2\) from
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1+\alpha)^2-(1-\alpha+\alpha^2)&=3\alpha,\\
(1+\alpha)^2-(1-\alpha)&=3\alpha+\alpha^2,\\
(1+\alpha)^2-(1+2\alpha)&=\alpha^2,\\
(1+\alpha)^2-(1-\alpha+2\alpha^2)&=3\alpha-\alpha^2>0,\\
2(1+\alpha)^2-(2+\alpha)&=3\alpha+2\alpha^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(r_j\in\Delta\) for all three vertices. By convexity,
\[
g+\lambda M_\alpha^{-1}(\Delta-g)\subseteq\Delta,
\]
which is equivalent to the claim \(C_\lambda\subseteq B_\alpha\).

The three barycentric coordinates \(x,y,1-x-y\) on \(Q\) have respective lower bounds
\[
\frac1{20},\qquad \frac1{20},\qquad \frac{159}{400}.
\]
Therefore every point of \((\Delta+Q)/2\) has all three barycentric coordinates at least \(1/40\). On the other hand,
\[
C_\lambda
=\left\{z\in\Delta:
x(z),y(z),1-x(z)-y(z)\ge\frac{1-\lambda}{3}\right\},
\]
and (1) gives \((1-\lambda)/3<1/40\). Hence
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac{\Delta+Q}{2}
\subseteq C_\lambda\subseteq B_\alpha=A_1.
\tag{3}
\]
By accepted fact 8ad0785e5b4ae9ef, (3) proves that these are exactly the three integer-height sections of a compact polytope.

It remains to prove the all-direction obstruction. Since
\(C_\lambda\subseteq B_\alpha\) and \(C_\lambda\subseteq\Delta\),
\[
\delta_\alpha:=|\Delta\setminus B_\alpha|
\le |\Delta\setminus C_\lambda|
=\frac{1-\lambda^2}{2}
=\frac{1-(1+\alpha)^{-4}}2.
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(\alpha>0\), exact expansion gives
\[
4\alpha-\bigl(1-(1+\alpha)^{-4}\bigr)
=\frac{\alpha^2(10+20\alpha+15\alpha^2+4\alpha^3)}{(1+\alpha)^4}>0.
\]
Combining this with (4) and the upper bound on \(\alpha\) yields
\[
\delta_\alpha<2\alpha\le\frac1{50000}.
\tag{5}
\]

For comparison, let \(\widetilde S\) be the three-slice set with
\[
\widetilde A_0=\Delta,
\qquad \widetilde A_1=\Delta,
\qquad \widetilde A_2=Q.
\]
Accepted fact a58dc34569c34d62 applies with
\(p=27/16000\). Since
\[
\frac{7p}{9}=\frac{21}{16000}>\frac1{11700},
\]
it gives
\[
h_{\widetilde S}((1,g))\ge t+\frac1{11700}.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) be any closed halfspace containing
\((1,g)\), and write
\[
E_H=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:(1,z)\in H\}
\]
for its height-\(1\) section. The height-\(0\) and height-\(2\) slices of
\(S_\alpha\) and \(\widetilde S\) agree, while
\[
|B_\alpha\cap E_H|-|\Delta\cap E_H|
\ge-|\Delta\setminus B_\alpha|
=-\delta_\alpha.
\]
Thus (6), applied to this same arbitrary halfspace \(H\), gives
\[
\mathcal H_2(S_\alpha\cap H)
\ge t+\frac1{11700}-\delta_\alpha.
\]
Taking the infimum over all such \(H\), then using (5), proves
\[
h_{S_\alpha}((1,g))
\ge t+\frac1{11700}-\delta_\alpha
>t+\left(\frac1{11700}-\frac1{50000}\right)
=t+\frac{383}{5{,}850{,}000}>t.
\]
Finally, \(g\in\operatorname{int}B_\alpha\). If interiors of strict-low halfspaces covered the middle slice, one of those halfspaces would contain \((1,g)\), contradicting the displayed all-direction lower bound. This proves the asserted obstruction for every member of the declared rational family.
