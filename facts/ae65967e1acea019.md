## Proof

The sign conventions are
\[
 f_i\le L_i\iff r_i\le0,\qquad
 f_i\le-L_i\iff\tau_i\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]
Intersecting (3) with \(r_i\ge0\) gives, in cyclic order, exactly the
five vertices in (6).  Shoelace gives (7).  Clipping (3) by the three
coordinate halfplanes gives (8), and their sum is (9).

For (4), the edge \([y_1,z_1]\) contains \(e_1\), while
\([y_2,z_2]\) contains \(e_2\).  The base \([y_1,y_2]\) has
\(\tau_3=-\omega\), and the inner edge \([z_2,z_1]\) lies strictly in
\(\{\tau_i>0:i=1,2,3\}\).  Hence the only positive sign cells are
\(1,2,12,123\).  Direct determinants give, before the area factor
\(s^2\),
\[
 |Q_1|_\tau=\frac{\rho\omega}{2},\qquad
 |Q_2|_\tau=\frac{\chi\omega}{2},\qquad
 |Q_{12}|_\tau=\omega+\frac{\omega^2}{2},
\tag{22}
\]
\[
 |Q_{123}|_\tau=
 \frac{\omega}{2}\left(
 \frac1{10}+\frac18
 -\frac1{80}(\rho+\chi+\omega)\right).
\tag{23}
\]
Multiplication by \(s^2\) gives (10)-(11).  Equation (12) follows by
cross multiplication; the second strict inequality in its first
part follows from
\(\sqrt{21}<19/4\).

We next verify that all three displayed levels really are actual
lower crossings, not merely stationary candidates.  For
\(\varepsilon\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
 F_i(\varepsilon)=
 |A_0\cap\{r_i\le\varepsilon\}|
 +s^2|A_2\cap\{\tau_i\ge\varepsilon/s\}|
 -u_i-v_i.
\tag{24}
\]
This is the endpoint mass at level \(L_i+40\varepsilon\), minus its
value at \(L_i\).  At every regular value,
\[
 F_i'(\varepsilon)
 =\ell_i^{A_0}(\varepsilon)
 -s\,\ell_i^{A_2}(\varepsilon/s),
\tag{25}
\]
where the lengths are measured in the complementary affine
coordinate.

For \(-\alpha\le\varepsilon\le0\), the three \(A_0\)-section lengths
are bounded respectively by
\[
 \frac{11}{10^6},\qquad
 \frac1{25000},\qquad
 1+3\alpha=\frac{1000003}{10^6}.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(-\alpha/s\le\zeta\le0\), the first two \(A_2\)-section lengths
are
\[
 \ell_1^{A_2}(\zeta)
 =\omega\left(1+\frac{\zeta}{\chi}\right),\qquad
 \ell_2^{A_2}(\zeta)
 =\omega\left(1+\frac{\zeta}{\rho}\right),
\tag{27}
\]
and the third is at least \(1\).  Therefore the three lower bounds
for \(s\ell_i^{A_2}\) are
\[
 \frac{41999}{400000000},\qquad
 \frac{10499}{100000000},\qquad
 \frac{21}{20}.
\tag{28}
\]
Each number in (28) is strictly larger than the corresponding number
in (26).  Thus \(F_i'<0\) throughout \([-\alpha,0]\), apart from the
finitely many harmless breakpoints.  Since \(A_0\subseteq
\{r_i\ge-\alpha\}\), for \(\varepsilon\le-\alpha\) its \(i\)-cap is
empty while the \(A_2\)-cap only increases as \(\varepsilon\)
decreases.  Hence
\[
 F_i(\varepsilon)\ge0\quad(\varepsilon\le0).
\]
The strict inequalities in (26)-(28) persist on a right
neighborhood of \(0\), so \(F_i(\varepsilon)<0\) for all sufficiently
small \(\varepsilon>0\).  Consequently
\[
 0=\inf\{\varepsilon:F_i(\varepsilon)<0\},
\]
which is exactly the actual-lower-crossing condition in every row.

The endpoint tail restrictions are also strict.  From (8) and (10),
\[
\begin{aligned}

 \frac{2204830640819}{999928000000000000}>0,\\

 \frac{2200553252469}{3991959460000000000}>0,\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(M>m\), the other three endpoint tail slacks are positive as
well.  All endpoint caps are positive and proper by (8) and (10).
