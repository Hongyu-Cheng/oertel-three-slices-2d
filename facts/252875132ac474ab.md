## Statement

Use the threshold, optimizer, contact-set, weighted-sum, and larger-slice contact conventions of accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`, and the residual strip of accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`.  Let
\[
\Delta=\{(\xi,\eta):\xi\ge0,\ \eta\ge0,\ \xi+\eta\le1\},
\qquad
\iota(\xi,\eta)=\left(\frac53\xi,\frac54\eta\right),
\]
\[
P=\iota(\Delta),\qquad D=|P|=\frac{25}{24},
\qquad
A_0=A_1=P,
\qquad
A_2(y)=\iota\left((0,y)+\frac12\Delta\right).
\]
Take the unit positive-spanning directions and weights
\[
q_1=(1,0),\qquad q_+=\frac{(-99,20)}{101},\qquad
q_-=\frac{(-99,-20)}{101},
\qquad
(\alpha_1,\alpha_+,\alpha_-)=(198,101,101),
\]
the same frame as in accepted fact `b256f5eaf768bb98`, but at homothetic scale \(1/2\) rather than \(2/5\).

The family is compatible exactly for
\[
0\le y\le\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
Every compatible member is in the full strict residual strip.  Its areas and threshold are
\[
a_0=a_1=D,\qquad a_2=\frac D4,\qquad
T=\frac94D,\qquad t=\frac D2.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
x=25y,\qquad \tau=\frac12,\qquad
R_+=\frac{165}{2}+x,\qquad R_-=\frac{165}{2}-x,
\]
\[
S_+=\sqrt{47025-\frac{R_+^2}{2}},\qquad
S_-=\sqrt{34650-\frac{R_-^2}{2}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(p_\ell=D\pi_\ell\) and \(b_\ell=D\beta_\ell\), there is a single optimizer chamber on the whole closed interval (1).  All three actual optimizers are strict interior crossings and
\[
\pi_1=\frac{29-6\sqrt6}{50},\qquad
\beta_1=\frac{3\sqrt6-2}{25},\qquad
\rho_{q_1}(t)=\frac{4-\sqrt6}{6},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\pi_+=\frac{(R_++S_+)^2}{282150},\qquad
\beta_+=\tau-\pi_+,\qquad
\rho_{q_+}(t)=\frac{-495+R_++S_+}{303},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\pi_-=\frac{(R_-+S_-)^2}{207900},\qquad
\beta_-=\tau-\pi_-,\qquad
\rho_{q_-}(t)=\frac{-495+R_-+S_-}{303}.
\tag{6}
\]
For every \(0\le y\le1/2\),
\[
\frac14<\pi_\ell<\frac12,
\qquad
0<\beta_\ell<\frac14
\qquad(\ell\in\{1,+,-\}),
\tag{7}
\]
and the complete true endpoint contact sets are
\[
\mathcal U_{q_1}(b_1)=\{0\},\qquad
\mathcal U_{q_+}(b_+)=\left\{\frac{b_+}{2}\right\},\qquad
\mathcal U_{q_-}(b_-)=\left\{\frac{b_-}{2}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(F(x)=S_+(x)+S_-(x)\).  The exact weighted threshold and the true larger-slice contact maximum are
\[
W(y)=-143-33\sqrt6+\frac13F(25y),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
H(y):=
\max_{\substack{u_1\in\mathcal U_{q_1}(b_1)\\
u_+\in\mathcal U_{q_+}(b_+)\\
u_-\in\mathcal U_{q_-}(b_-)}}
\left(198Q_{0,q_1}(u_1)+101Q_{0,q_+}(u_+)
+101Q_{0,q_-}(u_-)\right)
=-\frac{715}{2}+\frac13F(25y).
\tag{10}
\]
Both have the same unique maximizer
\[
y_*=\frac{33}{10}
\frac{\sqrt{209}-\sqrt{154}}{\sqrt{209}+\sqrt{154}}
\in\left(0,\frac12\right).
\tag{11}
\]
At this point
\[
F(25y_*)=
\sqrt{\frac{136125}{2}+4950\sqrt{266}},
\tag{12}
\]
and hence
\[
\max_{0\le y\le1/2}W(y)
=-143-33\sqrt6
+\frac13\sqrt{\frac{136125}{2}+4950\sqrt{266}}<0,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\max_{0\le y\le1/2}H(y)
=-\frac{715}{2}
+\frac13\sqrt{\frac{136125}{2}+4950\sqrt{266}}<0.
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, there is no compatible \(y\) in this scale-\(1/2\) family with \(W(y)>0\), so this family cannot violate the implication \((CB)\) of accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.

## Proof

The midpoint contribution at height one forced by the outer slices is
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2(y)}2
=\iota\left((0,y/2)+\frac34\Delta\right).
\tag{15}
\]
It is contained in \(A_1=\iota(\Delta)\) exactly when
\(y/2\ge0\) and \(y/2+3/4\le1\), which is (1).  Necessity follows because every convex set containing the two outer slices contains (15) in its height-one slice.  Conversely, under these inequalities the convex hull of the three displayed slices has height-one slice
\(\operatorname{conv}(A_1\cup(A_0+A_2)/2)=A_1\), and its only integer heights are \(0,1,2\), proving compatibility.

Equation (2) follows from homothetic area scaling.  Thus
\[
M=D,\qquad m=\frac D4,\qquad r=\frac14,\qquad s=1.
\]
Since \(r<9/25\), accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` gives residual bounds
\[
1-r=\frac34<s=1<\frac54=1+r,
\]
and its upper bound on \(r\) is larger than \(1/2\).  Hence every compatible translation is strictly residual.  The feasible normalized middle allocation is therefore
\[
\lambda:=\frac{t-m}{D}=\frac14
\le \pi:=\frac pD\le\frac tD=\frac12=\tau,
\qquad \beta:=\frac bD=\tau-\pi.
\tag{16}
\]

Define the exact normalization alias
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q}(v):=Q_{i,q}(Dv),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_q(\beta):=D^{-1}\mathcal U_q(D\beta).
\tag{17}
\]
The vertex calculation for \(P\), equivalently the base-triangle quantiles proved in accepted fact `b256f5eaf768bb98`, gives
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_1}(v)=\frac53(1-\sqrt{1-v}),
\tag{18}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_+}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-165+\sqrt{31350v}}{101},&0\le v\le33/38,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{25-\sqrt{4750(1-v)}}{101},&33/38\le v\le1,
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{i,q_-}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-165+\sqrt{23100v}}{101},&0\le v\le28/33,\\[3pt]
-\dfrac{\sqrt{4125(1-v)}}{101},&28/33\le v\le1,
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
\]
for \(i=0,1\).  Since \(A_2(y)\) is a translate of \(P/2\),
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q}(v)=q\cdot\iota(0,y)
+\frac12\widehat Q_{0,q}(4v).
\tag{21}
\]
In particular,
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_1}(v)=\frac56(1-\sqrt{1-4v})
\qquad(0\le v\le1/4),
\tag{22}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_+}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x-165/2+\sqrt{31350v}}{101},&0\le v\le33/152,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{x+25/2-\tfrac12\sqrt{4750(1-4v)}}{101},&33/152\le v\le1/4,
\end{cases}
\tag{23}
\]
\[
\widehat Q_{2,q_-}(v)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{-x-165/2+\sqrt{23100v}}{101},&0\le v\le7/33,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{-x-\tfrac12\sqrt{4125(1-4v)}}{101},&7/33\le v\le1/4.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]

For \(0\le\beta\le1/4\), let
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_q(\beta)
=\max_{0\le u\le\beta}
\bigl(\widehat Q_{0,q}(u)+\widehat Q_{2,q}(\beta-u)\bigr).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(q_1\), the summand is strictly convex in \(u\), so its maximum is at an endpoint.  For \(\beta>0\), the endpoint assigning all demand to the scale-\(1/2\) triangle and the endpoint assigning all demand to \(P\) are respectively
\[
\frac{10\beta/3}{1+\sqrt{1-4\beta}}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\frac{5\beta/3}{1+\sqrt{1-\beta}}.
\]
The first is strictly larger, so
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_1}(\beta)
=\frac56(1-\sqrt{1-4\beta}),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_1}(\beta)=\{0\}.
\tag{26}
\]

For \(q_+\) and \(q_-\), the \(A_0\) cap is always on its lower branch.  If the \(A_2\) cap is also on its lower branch, the split-dependent part is
\(\sqrt{\kappa u}+\sqrt{\kappa(\beta-u)}\), where
\(\kappa=31350\) or \(23100\), and it has the unique maximum \(u=\beta/2\).  This split is on the lower branch because
\[
\frac\beta2\le\frac18<\min\left\{\frac{33}{152},\frac7{33}\right\}.
\]
On an upper small-triangle branch the split-dependent part has derivative
\[
\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{2\sqrt u}
-\frac d{2\sqrt{C-d\beta+du}},
\tag{27}
\]
with
\[
(\kappa,d,C)=(31350,4750,2375/2)
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
(23100,4125,4125/4).
\]
For interior \(u>0\), (27) is positive exactly when
\[
\kappa(C-d\beta)+d(\kappa-d)u>0.
\]
This holds because \(C-d\beta=d(1/4-\beta)\ge0\) and \(\kappa>d\); the boundary case follows by the one-sided limit.  Hence every upper piece increases toward the lower branch, and the unique global contact remains \(u=\beta/2\).  Therefore, for \(\beta>0\),
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_+}(\beta)
=\frac{x-495/2+\sqrt{62700\beta}}{101},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_+}(\beta)=\{\beta/2\},
\tag{28}
\]
\[
\widehat{\overline E}_{q_-}(\beta)
=\frac{-x-495/2+\sqrt{46200\beta}}{101},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{q_-}(\beta)=\{\beta/2\}.
\tag{29}
\]
At \(\beta=0\), all finite contact sets are \(\{0\}\); these finite extensions are used only for endpoint comparison.  The actual convention remains \(E_q(0)=-\infty\), exactly as required by accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.

For finite endpoint comparison on (16), set
\[
\delta_q(\pi)=\widehat Q_{1,q}(\pi)
-\frac12\widehat{\overline E}_q(\tau-\pi).
\tag{30}
\]
Each \(\delta_q\) is strictly increasing.  For \(q_1\),
\[
\delta_{q_1}(\lambda)=\frac{5(3-2\sqrt3)}{12}<0,
\qquad
\delta_{q_1}(\tau)=\frac53\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)>0.
\tag{31}
\]
For the other rows, put \(\kappa_+=31350\) and \(\kappa_-=23100\).  Equations (19), (28), and (29) give
\[
\delta_{q_\pm}(\pi)
=\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa_\pm\pi}
-\sqrt{2\kappa_\pm(\tau-\pi)}-R_\pm}{202},
\tag{32}
\]
so
\[
\delta_{q_\pm}(\lambda)
=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_\pm}-\sqrt{\kappa_\pm/2}-R_\pm}{202}<0,
\qquad
\delta_{q_\pm}(\tau)
=\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa_\pm/2}-R_\pm}{202}>0.
\tag{33}
\]
These signs are uniform for \(0\le x\le25/2\): one has
\[
165/2\le R_+\le95,
\qquad
70\le R_-\le165/2,
\]
while
\[
\sqrt{31350}-\sqrt{15675}<178-125=53,
\qquad
\sqrt{23100}-\sqrt{11550}<152-107=45,
\]
and \(2\sqrt{15675}>250\), \(2\sqrt{11550}>214\).

Thus every row has one strict crossing in \((\lambda,\tau)\), including at both endpoints of the compatible translation interval.  Since before the crossing the decreasing convolution term is larger and after it the increasing middle quantile is larger, this crossing is the unique actual threshold optimizer.  There are no further optimizer chambers, and (7) follows.

For \(q_1\), writing \(z=\sqrt{1-\pi_1}\), the crossing equation becomes
\[
\sqrt{3-4z^2}=4z-3,
\qquad
10z^2-12z+3=0.
\]
The unique feasible root is \(z=(6+\sqrt6)/10\), which gives (4).  For either remaining row, write \(Z=\sqrt{\kappa_\pm\pi_\pm}\).  The crossing equation is
\[
2Z-\sqrt{2\kappa_\pm(\tau-\pi_\pm)}=R_\pm.
\tag{34}
\]
Using \(\pi_\pm+(\tau-\pi_\pm)=\tau\) gives
\[
3Z^2-2R_\pm Z+\frac{R_\pm^2}{2}-\kappa_\pm\tau=0.
\]
On the compatible interval, \(R_+^2<\kappa_+\) and \(R_-^2<\kappa_-\), so
\[
S_\pm^2-R_\pm^2=\frac32(\kappa_\pm-R_\pm^2)>0.
\]
Thus the minus quadratic root is negative and the only positive feasible root is \(Z=(R_\pm+S_\pm)/3\), proving (5) and (6).  Since every \(\beta_\ell\) is positive, (26), (28), and (29), together with (17), prove the actual contact identities (8).

The directions are unit, noncollinear, and satisfy
\[
198q_1+101q_++101q_-=0.
\]
Using (4)--(6) and \(R_++R_-=165\) yields (9).  The singleton contacts and the lower branches (19)--(20) give
\[
H(y)=-330
+\sqrt{\frac{31350\beta_+}{2}}
+\sqrt{\frac{23100\beta_-}{2}}.
\tag{35}
\]
Equation (34) and \(Z=(R_\pm+S_\pm)/3\) imply
\[
\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_\pm\beta_\pm}{2}}
=\frac{2S_\pm-R_\pm}{6}.
\]
Substitution in (35) proves (10).

It remains to maximize
\[
F(x)=\sqrt{47025-\frac{(165/2+x)^2}{2}}
+\sqrt{34650-\frac{(165/2-x)^2}{2}}
\qquad\left(0\le x\le\frac{25}{2}\right).
\tag{36}
\]
Both summands have strictly negative second derivative on this interval, so \(F\) is strictly concave.  Its critical equation is
\[
\frac{R_-}{S_-}=\frac{R_+}{S_+},
\]
which, since all quantities are positive, is equivalent to
\[
47025R_-^2=34650R_+^2.
\]
The unique solution is
\[
x_*=\frac{165}{2}
\frac{\sqrt{47025}-\sqrt{34650}}
{\sqrt{47025}+\sqrt{34650}}
=\frac{165}{2}
\frac{\sqrt{209}-\sqrt{154}}
{\sqrt{209}+\sqrt{154}}.
\tag{37}
\]
It lies in \((0,25/2)\): positivity is clear, and
\(196\cdot47025<361\cdot34650\) gives \(x_*<25/2\).  Strict concavity makes it the unique global maximizer, and division by \(25\) gives (11).

Let \(A=47025\), \(B=34650\), and \(U=\sqrt A+\sqrt B\).  At (37),
\[
R_+=\frac{165\sqrt A}{U},\qquad
R_-=\frac{165\sqrt B}{U}.
\]
Consequently
\[
F(x_*)=\sqrt{U^2-\frac{165^2}{2}}.
\]
Since \(\sqrt{AB}=2475\sqrt{266}\), this is exactly (12).  Finally,
\[
\sqrt A<217,\qquad \sqrt B<187,
\]
so \(F(x_*)<U<404\).  Equations (9) and (10) then give
\[
\max W<-143-33\sqrt6+\frac{404}{3}<0,
\qquad
\max H<-\frac{715}{2}+\frac{404}{3}<0.
\]
This proves (13), (14), and the claimed complete scale-\(1/2\) no-go result.
