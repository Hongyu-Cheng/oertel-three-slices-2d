## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\]
Let \(f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine and let \(L\in\mathbb R\).
Define
\[
I_0=\int_{A_0}(L-f(x))_+\,dx,\qquad
I_1=\int_{A_1}(-f(z))_+\,dz,\qquad
I_2=\int_{A_2}(-L-f(y))_+\,dy.
\]
If both closed caps
\[
A_0\cap\{f\le L\}\quad\text{and}\quad
A_2\cap\{f\le-L\}
\]
are nonempty, then
\[
I_1^{1/3}\ge
\frac{I_0^{1/3}+I_2^{1/3}}{2}.
\]
This includes constant affine functions and cases in which either endpoint
cap only touches its cutting level and the corresponding moment is zero.

## Proof

Set
\[
g_0(u)=L-f(u),\qquad g_1(u)=-f(u),\qquad
g_2(u)=-L-f(u),
\]
and, in \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\), define
\[
K_i=\{(u,t):u\in A_i,\ 0\le t\le g_i(u)\},
\qquad i=0,1,2.
\]
These definitions use closed inequalities. Thus a base point with
\(g_i(u)=0\) contributes the point \((u,0)\), while a base point with
\(g_i(u)<0\) contributes no point.

Each \(K_i\) is closed because \(A_i\) is closed and \(g_i\) is continuous.
It is bounded because \(A_i\) is bounded, each \(g_i\) is bounded on \(A_i\),
and every point of \(K_i\) has \(0\le t\le\max_{A_i}g_i\). Hence every
\(K_i\) is compact, with the empty set allowed at this stage. Moreover,
\(A_i\times\mathbb R\), \(\{t\ge0\}\), and
\(\{(u,t):t\le g_i(u)\}\) are convex, so their intersection \(K_i\) is
convex.

The first cap hypothesis supplies \(x\in A_0\) with \(g_0(x)\ge0\), and
then \((x,0)\in K_0\). Similarly, the second cap hypothesis supplies
\((y,0)\in K_2\). Thus \(K_0\) and \(K_2\) are nonempty. This is the only
place where nonemptiness of the two endpoint caps is required.

For \((x,t)\in K_0\) and \((y,s)\in K_2\), put
\[
z=\frac{x+y}{2},\qquad r=\frac{t+s}{2}.
\]
The assumed Minkowski containment gives \(z\in A_1\), and \(r\ge0\).
Affinity of \(f\) and the defining upper bounds for \(t,s\) give
\[
r
\le\frac{L-f(x)-L-f(y)}2
=-\frac{f(x)+f(y)}2
=-f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)
=g_1(z).
\]
Therefore \((z,r)\in K_1\), proving the exact closed-set containment
\[
\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\subseteq K_1.
\]
In particular, \(K_1\) is also nonempty.

The sets \(K_i\) are compact and hence Borel. For \(u\in A_i\), the vertical
fiber of \(K_i\) is empty if \(g_i(u)<0\), is the singleton \(\{0\}\) if
\(g_i(u)=0\), and is \([0,g_i(u)]\) if \(g_i(u)>0\). Its one-dimensional
Lebesgue measure is therefore \((g_i(u))_+\). Tonelli's theorem yields the
exact identities
\[
\lambda_3(K_i)
=\int_{A_i}(g_i(u))_+\,du
=I_i,\qquad i=0,1,2.
\]
Thus equality points on any cutting line are included in the lifted set and
contribute zero volume, exactly as required by the positive-part integrals.

We next record why the three-dimensional Brunn-Minkowski step remains valid
when one or both nonempty endpoint lifts have zero volume. For any nonempty
compact convex sets \(X,Y\subset\mathbb R^3\), the midpoint form is
\[
\lambda_3\left(\frac{X+Y}{2}\right)^{1/3}
\ge
\frac{\lambda_3(X)^{1/3}+\lambda_3(Y)^{1/3}}2.
\]
If Brunn-Minkowski is stated only for convex bodies with positive volume,
let \(B\) be the closed unit ball. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the sets
\(X+\varepsilon B\) and \(Y+\varepsilon B\) are convex bodies with positive
volume, so that version gives
\[
\lambda_3\left(\frac{X+Y}{2}+\varepsilon B\right)^{1/3}
\ge
\frac{\lambda_3(X+\varepsilon B)^{1/3}
+\lambda_3(Y+\varepsilon B)^{1/3}}2.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), these compact outer parallel sets decrease
to \(X\), \(Y\), and \((X+Y)/2\), respectively. Continuity from above of
Lebesgue measure therefore proves the displayed midpoint inequality without
any positive-volume assumption.

Apply this inequality to the nonempty compact convex sets \(K_0,K_2\).
Volume monotonicity, midpoint containment, and the volume identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_1^{1/3}
&=\lambda_3(K_1)^{1/3}\\
&\ge
\lambda_3\left(\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}\right)^{1/3}\\
&\ge
\frac{\lambda_3(K_0)^{1/3}+\lambda_3(K_2)^{1/3}}2\\
&=\frac{I_0^{1/3}+I_2^{1/3}}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

If an endpoint cap merely touches its cutting level, its lift still contains
the corresponding zero-height points; if its moment is zero, the volume
identity assigns that nonempty lift volume zero, and the regularized
Brunn-Minkowski argument above applies unchanged. If \(f\) is constant, all
three \(g_i\) are constant, and every compactness, containment, fiber, and
volume argument above remains literal; no nonzero slope or cutting-line
normal was used. Hence all stated degenerate and boundary cases are covered.
The argument does not apply if an endpoint cap is empty, because the
corresponding lift is then empty.
