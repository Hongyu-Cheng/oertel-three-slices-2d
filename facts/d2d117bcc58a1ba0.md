## Statement

For \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), and \(s\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
F_{i,q}(s)=
\left|A_i\cap\{w:q\cdot w\le s\}\right|
\]
and
\[
\Phi_q(r)=F_{1,q}(r)+
\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\left(F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(\Phi_q\) is continuous and nondecreasing, with
\[
\lim_{r\to-\infty}\Phi_q(r)=0,\qquad
\lim_{r\to+\infty}\Phi_q(r)=a_1+\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every
\[
0<t<\min\{a_0+a_1,a_1+a_2\},
\tag{3}
\]
there is a finite number \(\rho_q(t)\) such that
\[
\Phi_q(\rho_q(t))=t,\qquad
\{r:\Phi_q(r)\ge t\}=[\rho_q(t),\infty).
\tag{4}
\]
Writing
\[
K_q(t)=\{z:q\cdot z\ge\rho_q(t)\},
\tag{5}
\]
one has the exact identity
\[
D_t=A_1\cap\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}K_q(t).
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, \(D_t\) is closed and convex.

If \(D_t=\varnothing\), there are \(m\in\{1,2,3\}\) and directions
\(q_1,\ldots,q_m\) such that
\[
A_1\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^m K_{q_i}(t)=\varnothing.
\tag{7}
\]
The exact Helly alternatives are:

1. \(m=1\), where \(A_1\cap K_{q_1}(t)=\varnothing\);
2. \(m=2\), where either the two profile halfplanes are already disjoint or
   their intersection is merely disjoint from \(A_1\);
3. \(m=3\), where
   \(K_{q_1}(t)\cap K_{q_2}(t)\cap K_{q_3}(t)=\varnothing\).

For every one of these alternatives, the thresholds can be moved strictly
below their profile levels while preserving the cover of \(A_1\).  The
resulting strict-low profile values are realized up to strict inequality by
finite slopes and hence give \(m\) proper closed halfspaces of mass below
\(t\) whose middle-slice interiors cover \(A_1\).

Under a global counterexample at \(t=2T/9\), the minimal alternative has
\(m=3\).  Its directions admit positive weights, and after a strict inward
shift of the three profile thresholds there are numbers
\(\alpha_i>0,r_i,\lambda_i\) such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_iq_i=0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i r_i=1,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):
\alpha_i(q_i\cdot w-r_i)+
\alpha_i\lambda_i(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad
\mu(H_i)<t,
\tag{9}
\]
and the three middle-slice interiors cover the whole middle plane.  Thus,
with
\[
f_i(w)=\alpha_i(q_i\cdot w-r_i),\qquad
s_i=\alpha_i\lambda_i,
\]
the same halfspaces have the accepted normal form
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+s_i(h-1)\le0\},
\qquad
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

Extend every cap function to the compact ordered line
\([-\infty,+\infty]\) by
\[
F_{i,q}(-\infty)=0,\qquad F_{i,q}(+\infty)=a_i.
\]
For finite \(s\), continuity follows from dominated convergence: the only
possible pointwise discontinuity of the moving halfplane indicator lies on
the line \(q\cdot w=s\), which has planar area zero.  Continuity at the two
ends follows from compactness of \(A_i\).

For fixed \(r\), the infimum in (1) equals the minimum over
\(\lambda\in[-\infty,+\infty]\), where the endpoint values of its two-cap
part are respectively
\[
a_0\quad\text{and}\quad a_2.
\tag{11}
\]
The function
\[
(r,\lambda)\longmapsto
F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)
\]
is jointly continuous on
\(\mathbb R\times[-\infty,+\infty]\).  At an infinite endpoint this follows
uniformly for \(r\) in a bounded interval because the \(A_i\) are compact.
The minimum is therefore continuous in \(r\).  Explicitly, upper
semicontinuity follows by keeping a minimizer at the limiting \(r\);
for lower semicontinuity, take minimizers along a convergent sequence of
\(r\)'s, extract a convergent slope subsequence in the compactified line, and
pass to the limit.  Adding \(F_{1,q}(r)\) proves continuity of \(\Phi_q\).

If \(r'<r\), then for every fixed finite or infinite \(\lambda\), each of
\[
F_{0,q}(r+\lambda),\quad F_{1,q}(r),\quad
F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)
\]
is at least its value with \(r'\) in place of \(r\).  Taking the two minima
proves
\[
\Phi_q(r')\le\Phi_q(r).
\tag{12}
\]
This is the required profile monotonicity.

Taking \(\lambda=0\) proves the first limit in (2).  For the second, (11)
gives
\[
\Phi_q(r)\le F_{1,q}(r)+\min\{a_0,a_2\}.
\]
On the other hand, for every \(\lambda\), at least one of
\(r+\lambda,r-\lambda\) is at least \(r\).  Hence
\[
F_{0,q}(r+\lambda)+F_{2,q}(r-\lambda)
\ge
\min\{F_{0,q}(r),F_{2,q}(r)\}.
\]
Letting \(r\to+\infty\) proves the other inequality in the second limit.

Under (3), continuity, monotonicity, and (2) show that
\[
\rho_q(t)=\inf\{r:\Phi_q(r)\ge t\}
\]
is finite and satisfies (4).  In particular the profile level itself is
attained as a value of the one-variable function \(\Phi_q\).  This does not
assert that the slope infimum in (1) is attained at a finite \(\lambda\).
Its compactified minimizer can be \(+\infty\) or \(-\infty\).

For \(z\in A_1\), accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` gives
\[
h_S((1,z))
=
\min\left\{
\inf_{q\in\mathbb S^1}\Phi_q(q\cdot z),
\ a_0+a_1,\ a_1+a_2
\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(t\) is strictly below the two horizontal terms, (13) is at least
\(t\) exactly when
\(\Phi_q(q\cdot z)\ge t\) for every \(q\).  Equations (4)-(5) now give (6).
Every set in the intersection is closed and convex, proving the same for
\(D_t\).  If \(t\le0\), the omitted trivial case is \(D_t=A_1\).

Suppose \(D_t=\varnothing\).  The open halfplanes
\[
U_q(t)=\{z:q\cdot z<\rho_q(t)\}
\tag{14}
\]
cover the compact set \(A_1\), so finitely many of them cover it.  Apply
planar Helly to \(A_1\) and the corresponding finite family of closed
halfplanes \(K_q(t)\).  An empty subfamily has at most three sets.  If it
contains \(A_1\), it contains at most two profile halfplanes.  If it does not
contain \(A_1\), at most three profile halfplanes already have empty
intersection.  Since \(A_1\) and every \(K_q(t)\) are nonempty, this gives
exactly the three alternatives listed above.  Notice in particular that a
three-direction Helly certificate cannot also contain \(A_1\): the three
profile halfplanes themselves must have empty intersection.

The open formulation is equivalent to strict low-mass halfspaces, including
the one- and two-direction cases.  For directions selected in (7), set
\[
\eta=
\min_{z\in A_1}
\max_{1\le i\le m}
\bigl(\rho_{q_i}(t)-q_i\cdot z\bigr).
\]
The expression inside the minimum is continuous and strictly positive on
compact \(A_1\), so \(\eta>0\).  Put
\[
r_i=\rho_{q_i}(t)-\frac{\eta}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then the open halfplanes \(q_i\cdot z<r_i\) still cover \(A_1\), and (4)
gives
\[
\Phi_{q_i}(r_i)<t.
\tag{16}
\]
Although the infimum in (1) need not be attained, the strict inequality
(16) guarantees a finite \(\lambda_i\) such that
\[
F_{0,q_i}(r_i+\lambda_i)+F_{1,q_i}(r_i)
+F_{2,q_i}(r_i-\lambda_i)<t.
\tag{17}
\]
Define
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):
q_i\cdot w-r_i+\lambda_i(h-1)\le0\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Its sections at heights \(0,1,2\) have the three thresholds occurring in
(17), so (17) is exactly \(\mu(H_i)<t\).  Its middle-slice interior is
\(q_i\cdot w<r_i\).  Thus (18) gives one, two, or three strict-low-mass
halfspaces in the corresponding Helly alternative.

Conversely, any halfspace of the form (18) with mass below \(t\) satisfies
\[
\Phi_{q_i}(r_i)\le\mu(H_i)<t.
\tag{19}
\]
Its middle open section is therefore contained in the profile-low halfplane
(14).  Hence a finite strict-low-mass middle cover is precisely a finite
strict realization of a directional-profile Helly certificate.  Equations
(15)-(19) also explain why the canonical closed threshold is used to prove
closedness, while a strictly lower open threshold is used to obtain an
actual low-mass halfspace.

Now assume a global counterexample at \(t=2T/9\).  Accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` gives \(a_i<T/2\) for every \(i\).  Therefore
\[
a_0+a_1=T-a_2>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9},
\qquad
a_1+a_2=T-a_0>\frac T2>\frac{2T}{9},
\]
so the preceding profile description applies, and the global counterexample
gives \(D_t=\varnothing\).  Accepted fact `8f5f00ddd4f22d2f` gives the same
finite low-mass middle cover from the halfspace side.  Accepted fact
`fdfc3e7459f0e194` proves, in particular, that no strict-low-mass cover of
the middle slice by one or two proper halfspaces exists.  The construction
(15)-(18) therefore rules out the one- and two-direction Helly alternatives.
The minimal certificate has exactly three directions.

For such a minimal triple, every pair of the canonical halfplanes
\[
K_i=\{w:q_i\cdot w\ge\rho_i\},
\qquad \rho_i=\rho_{q_i}(t),
\]
intersects, while \(K_1\cap K_2\cap K_3=\varnothing\).  Let \(Q\) be the
\(3\times2\) matrix with rows \(q_i^{\mathsf T}\).  The elementary affine
Farkas alternative gives numbers \(\gamma_i\ge0\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\gamma_iq_i=0,\qquad
c:=\sum_{i=1}^3\gamma_i\rho_i>0.
\tag{20}
\]
For completeness, apply strict separation to the point
\((\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3)\) and the closed polyhedral cone
\[
\{Qw-u:w\in\mathbb R^2,\ u\in\mathbb R_+^3\}.
\]
The separator annihilates \(Qw\), is nonnegative because the cone contains
every \(-u\), and is strictly positive on the displayed point, which is
exactly (20).

Every \(\gamma_i\) is positive.  A one-element support cannot annihilate a
unit vector.  If the support had two elements, a point in the corresponding
pairwise intersection would give
\[
0=\sum_i\gamma_iq_i\cdot w
\ge\sum_i\gamma_i\rho_i=c>0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus the three directions positively span the plane.
They are not collinear: otherwise all three constraints would be closed
halfline constraints on one scalar coordinate, and pairwise feasibility of
three closed halflines implies their common feasibility, contrary to the
empty triple intersection.

Choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<
\frac{c}{\gamma_1+\gamma_2+\gamma_3},
\qquad
r_i=\rho_i-\varepsilon,\qquad
c'=\sum_{i=1}^3\gamma_i r_i>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Then \(\Phi_{q_i}(r_i)<t\).  Moreover the three closed halfplanes
\[
P_i=\{w:q_i\cdot w\ge r_i\}
\]
still have empty intersection, since a common point would imply
\[
0=\sum_i\gamma_iq_i\cdot w
\ge\sum_i\gamma_i r_i=c'>0.
\]
Choose finite \(\lambda_i\) satisfying (17), and set
\[
\alpha_i=\frac{\gamma_i}{c'},\qquad
f_i(w)=\alpha_i(q_i\cdot w-r_i),\qquad
s_i=\alpha_i\lambda_i.
\]
Positive rescaling does not change the three halfspaces (18).  Equations
(20)-(21) give
\[
\sum_i\alpha_iq_i=0,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i r_i=1,
\]
and hence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3 f_i(w)=-1
\qquad(w\in\mathbb R^2).
\]
This proves (8)-(10) directly from the global Helly certificate.

Finally, this construction is exactly equivalent to the normalized legacy
triple in accepted fact `fdfc3e7459f0e194`.  Write one of its affine
functions as
\[
f_i(w)=v_i\cdot w+b_i,
\qquad
\beta_i=\lVert v_i\rVert>0,\quad
q_i=\frac{v_i}{\beta_i},\quad
r_i=-\frac{b_i}{\beta_i},\quad
\lambda_i=\frac{s_i}{\beta_i}.
\]
Its halfspace is exactly (18), and its mass is exactly
\[
\mu(H_i)=
F_{0,q_i}(r_i+\lambda_i)+F_{1,q_i}(r_i)
+F_{2,q_i}(r_i-\lambda_i).
\tag{22}
\]
Thus each accepted strict inequality \(\mu(H_i)<t\) is the explicit finite
slope witness
\[
\Phi_{q_i}(r_i)\le\mu(H_i)<t.
\tag{23}
\]
The accepted relation \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\) is exactly
\[
\sum_i\beta_iq_i=0,\qquad
\sum_i\beta_i r_i=1,
\]
so the positive spanning weights are \(\beta_i\).  Its accepted complementary
halfplanes
\[
\{f_i\ge0\}=\{q_i\cdot w\ge r_i\}
\]
have empty intersection, and the three middle open sections cover the whole
plane.  Conversely, (20)-(22) construct a triple of precisely this accepted
form from a minimal profile Helly certificate.  The offsets \(r_i\) satisfy
\(r_i<\rho_{q_i}(t)\); this strict offset, rather than an unsupported claim
of finite-slope attainment at \(\rho_{q_i}(t)\), is what relates the three
low profile values to the three low-mass halfspaces.
