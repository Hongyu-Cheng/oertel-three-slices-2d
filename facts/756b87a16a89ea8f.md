## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies with nonempty interiors, and let

\[
A_1=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad
a_i=|A_i|,\qquad
T=a_0+a_1+a_2.
\]

Let \(g_0,g_2\) be the outer centroids and set

\[
z=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},\qquad
K_i=A_i-g_i\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
K_M=A_1-z=\frac{K_0+K_2}{2}.
\]

Then \(z\in A_1\).

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\), choose a unit vector \(v\perp q\), and define

\[
I_i(t)=\{s\in\mathbb R:tq+sv\in K_i\},\qquad
f_i(t)=|I_i(t)|
\]

for \(i=0,2\). Define \(I_M,f_M\) analogously for \(K_M\). Write the projection supports as

\[
[\ell_i,r_i]=\{\langle q,x\rangle:x\in K_i\},
\qquad
[\ell_M,r_M]
=
\left[
\frac{\ell_0+\ell_2}{2},
\frac{r_0+r_2}{2}
\right].
\]

The functions \(f_0,f_2,f_M\) are nonnegative and concave on their respective support intervals. Their zero extensions are used only in integrals. Moreover,

\[
a_i=\int_{\mathbb R}f_i(t)\,dt,\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}t f_i(t)\,dt=0
\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
a_1=\int_{\mathbb R}f_M(t)\,dt.
\]

For

\[
J_s=[\ell_0,r_0]\cap[2s-r_2,\,2s-\ell_2],
\]

the middle section is exactly

\[
I_M(s)
=
\bigcup_{t\in J_s}
\frac{I_0(t)+I_2(2s-t)}2.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
f_M(s)\ge
\frac12\sup_{t\in J_s}
\bigl(f_0(t)+f_2(2s-t)\bigr)
\tag{2}
\]

whenever \(J_s\neq\varnothing\). Equality is not asserted.

Define

\[
F_i(u)=\int_{-\infty}^{u}f_i(t)\,dt
\quad(i=0,2),\qquad
F_M(u)=\int_{-\infty}^{u}f_M(t)\,dt.
\]

For every tilted halfspace through \((1,z)\), accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` gives parameters \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Put

\[
d=\langle q,g_0-z\rangle,\qquad
\tau=\lambda-d.
\]

Its exact mass is

\[
F_0(\tau)+F_M(0)+F_2(-\tau).
\tag{3}
\]

The map \(\lambda\mapsto\tau\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb R\). Since each centered outer body is two-dimensional,

\[
\ell_i<0<r_i.
\]

Let

\[
J_q=[\ell_0,-\ell_2].
\]

Then

\[
\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\bigl(F_0(\tau)+F_2(-\tau)\bigr)
=
\min_{\tau\in J_q}
\bigl(F_0(\tau)+F_2(-\tau)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the exact directional tilted-halfspace infimum is

\[
D_q=
F_M(0)
+
\min_{\tau\in J_q}
\bigl(F_0(\tau)+F_2(-\tau)\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]

For every \(u\ge\ell_0\) and \(w\ge\ell_2\), the closed lower caps are nonempty and satisfy

\[
\frac{
(K_0\cap\{\langle q,x\rangle\le u\})
+
(K_2\cap\{\langle q,x\rangle\le w\})
}{2}
\subseteq
K_M\cap
\left\{\langle q,x\rangle\le\frac{u+w}{2}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Planar Brunn-Minkowski gives

\[
\sqrt{F_M((u+w)/2)}
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{F_0(u)}+\sqrt{F_2(w)}}2.
\tag{7}
\]

This remains valid if either cap is a zero-area support face or is saturated and equal to the whole body. If \(u<\ell_0\) or \(w<\ell_2\), the corresponding cap is empty and the nonzero term from the other cap cannot be retained.

Likewise, for \(u\le r_0\) and \(w\le r_2\),

\[
\sqrt{a_1-F_M((u+w)/2)}
\ge
\frac{
\sqrt{a_0-F_0(u)}+\sqrt{a_2-F_2(w)}
}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Taking \(w=-u\) in (7), for every \(u\in J_q\),

\[
F_M(0)\ge
\frac14
\left(\sqrt{F_0(u)}+\sqrt{F_2(-u)}\right)^2.
\tag{9}
\]

The exact analytic target for the canonical point is

\[
F_M(0)
+
\min_{u\in[\ell_0,-\ell_2]}
\left(F_0(u)+F_2(-u)\right)
\ge
\frac{2}{9}(a_0+a_1+a_2)
\tag{10}
\]

for every \(q\in\mathbb S^1\). Equivalently,

\[
\int_{\ell_M}^{0}f_M(s)\,ds
+
\min_{u\in[\ell_0,-\ell_2]}
\left[
\int_{\ell_0}^{u}f_0(s)\,ds
+
\int_{\ell_2}^{-u}f_2(s)\,ds
\right]
\ge
\frac29
\left(
\int f_0+\int f_M+\int f_2
\right).
\tag{11}
\]

Under \(a_i<T/2\), proving (10) for every \(q\) implies

\[
h_S((1,z))\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

A stronger outer-tail sufficient target obtained from (9) is

\[
\min_{u\in J_q}
\bigl(F_0(u)+F_2(-u)\bigr)
+
\frac14\sup_{u\in J_q}
\left(\sqrt{F_0(u)}+\sqrt{F_2(-u)}\right)^2
\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{12}
\]

No assertion that (12) always holds is made.

## Proof

The centroid of a two-dimensional convex body lies in its interior. Hence \(g_i\in A_i\), and the exact Minkowski identity gives \(z\in A_1\). Translation gives \(K_M=(K_0+K_2)/2\).

Every \(I_i(t)\) is a compact interval when \(t\in[\ell_i,r_i]\) and is empty otherwise. Convexity implies

\[
(1-\theta)I_i(s)+\theta I_i(t)
\subseteq
I_i((1-\theta)s+\theta t),
\]

so taking interval lengths proves concavity of each section density on its support. Fubini gives the area identities and, because the outer centroids are zero, the two first-moment identities.

A point \(sq+yv\) belongs to \(K_M\) exactly when it is the average of points \(tq+y_0v\in K_0\) and \((2s-t)q+y_2v\in K_2\). This proves (1). The Minkowski average of two nonempty compact intervals has length \((f_0(t)+f_2(2s-t))/2\), including singleton endpoint sections, which proves (2).

For a tilted halfspace through \((1,z)\), the slice-\(0\) inequality becomes

\[
\langle q,w-g_0\rangle\le\lambda-d=\tau,
\]

while the slice-\(2\) inequality becomes

\[
\langle q,w-g_2\rangle\le d-\lambda=-\tau.
\]

The middle threshold is zero. Fubini proves (3), including thresholds at or outside the projection supports.

To prove (4), if \(\tau<\ell_0\), then

\[
F_0(\tau)=F_0(\ell_0)=0,\qquad
F_2(-\tau)\ge F_2(-\ell_0).
\]

Replacing \(\tau\) by \(\ell_0\) does not increase the outer mass. If \(\tau>-\ell_2\), then

\[
F_2(-\tau)=F_2(\ell_2)=0,\qquad
F_0(\tau)\ge F_0(-\ell_2).
\]

Replacing \(\tau\) by \(-\ell_2\) does not increase it. The cumulative functions are continuous, so the infimum is attained on \(J_q\). This clipping permits Brunn-Minkowski without assuming that the original halfspace has two nonempty endpoint caps.

In (6), every average point lies in \(K_M\), and its \(q\)-coordinate is at most \((u+w)/2\). When both caps are nonempty, Brunn-Minkowski applies even if one has zero planar area. This follows from the standard compact-set form or by thickening the compact caps by \(\varepsilon\)-balls and passing to the limit. Scaling by \(1/2\) gives (7). There is no upper restriction on \(u,w\), so saturated lower caps are included.

For upper caps, intersections with the boundary line have planar area zero. Their areas are exactly \(a_i-F_i(u)\). The same inclusion and Brunn-Minkowski argument proves (8), including saturated upper caps and zero-area support faces. If a cap is empty, its Minkowski sum with the other cap is empty, so the contribution from the other cap cannot be inferred. This is precisely the failure exhibited by the recorded cap vector \((0,0,1/4)\).

Equation (9) follows from (7). Equations (3) and (4) prove (5), so (10) is exactly the remaining directional inequality. Under balance, the slice-parallel masses are \(T-a_2>T/2\) and \(T-a_0>T/2\). Accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` then proves the depth implication. Taking the supremum in (9) and adding the minimum outer mass proves that (12) is sufficient.

It remains to show why the actual middle density cannot be discarded. For rational \(m\), let

\[
K_m=
\left\{
(x,y):
-1\le x\le1,\
-\frac12\le y-mx\le\frac12
\right\}.
\]

This is a centrally symmetric rational parallelogram of area \(2\) and centroid \(0\). In direction \(q=(1,0)\), every \(K_m\) has section density

\[
f_m(t)=1\qquad(-1\le t\le1).
\]

At fixed average coordinate \(s\), write the two outer coordinates as \(s+\delta\) and \(s-\delta\), where \(|\delta|\le1-|s|\). Their relative shear contributes an interval of length \(|m_0-m_2|(1-|s|)\), in addition to the common unit vertical interval. Hence

\[
f_{(K_{m_0}+K_{m_2})/2}(s)
=
1+|m_0-m_2|(1-|s|)
\qquad(-1\le s\le1).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus \(m_0=m_2=0\) gives middle density \(1\) and area \(2\), whereas \(m_0=0,m_2=1\) gives middle density \(2-|s|\) and area \(3\). The two outer projection densities are identical in both cases. Therefore neither \(f_M\) nor \(a_1\) is determined by \(f_0,f_2\), and replacing the actual middle density by a common density or by the pointwise lower bound in (2) is not exact.
