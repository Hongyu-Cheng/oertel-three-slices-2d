## Statement

Assume the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses and
exact-cell convention of accepted facts `0e51a47a9add571f` and
`91cb5bfc71da49ac`.  Relabel the positive \(Q\)-support as
\[
\mathcal S_Q=\{123,1,2,12\},
\]
and write
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad
a=q_1>0,\qquad b=q_2>0,\qquad
\gamma=q_{12}>0,
\]
\[
m=q+a+b+\gamma.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the endpoint marginals are
\[
v_1=m-b,\qquad v_2=m-a,\qquad v_3=q.
\tag{2}
\]
Let
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and assume the three crossings and canonical tails,
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=h,
\qquad c_i+m\geq h,\qquad c_i+M\geq h.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
0\leq u_1\leq b,\qquad
0\leq u_2\leq a,\qquad
0\leq u_3\leq m-q.
\tag{4}
\]

Put
\[
r=r_{123},\qquad
E_R=r_{12}+r_{13}+r_{23}+2r,
\]
\[
U=u_1+u_2+u_3,\qquad
\mathcal L=U+\gamma+2q+E_R.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(W=0\), then
\[
\boxed{\quad 2M=m+k+3\mathcal L.\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
If the table comes from an actual counterexample, accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7` gives
\[
M<m+k,\qquad m<M+k,\qquad k<M+m.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(p_\varnothing>0\), accepted fact `d3afd91c459bcce8`
also gives
\[
\boxed{\quad r_{12}>0,\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
but supplies no numerical lower bound for \(r_{12}\).

For the three middle positive halves
\[
K_i=K\cap\{f_i\geq0\},
\]
Scott's planar union theorem applies and gives the exact necessary
inequality
\[
\boxed{\quad
k-r\geq\frac95
\min_{1\leq i\leq3}(k-c_i).
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
Using (2)-(3), this is equivalently
\[
\boxed{\quad
2(M+m+k)
\geq
4k+5r
+9\min\{u_1+m-b,\ u_2+m-a,\ u_3+q\}.
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (6), (7), and (10) gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
5r
+9\min\{u_1+m-b,\ u_2+m-a,\ u_3+q\}
<4m.
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\min\{q,m-a,m-b\}<\frac{4m}{9}.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]
The excluded ratio region is nonempty.  For example,
\[
(q,a,b,\gamma)
=m\left(\frac9{20},\frac14,\frac14,\frac1{20}\right)
\tag{13}
\]
has all four coordinates positive and makes the minimum in (12) equal
to \(9m/20>4m/9\).

For the three endpoint positive halves
\[
Q_i^+=A_2\cap\{f_i\geq-L_i\},
\]
Scott's theorem also applies, now with exact union area \(m-q\), and
gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
m-q=a+b+\gamma
\geq\frac95\min\{a,b\}.
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
This is redundant for the single-double support, because
\[
a+b+\gamma>2\min\{a,b\}>\frac95\min\{a,b\}.
\tag{15}
\]

The preceding inequalities do not close the complementary ratio region.
The following exact scalar table is a witness.  Every unlisted cell has

\[

q_{123}=\frac1{10},\qquad
q_1=q_2=\frac25,\qquad q_{12}=\frac1{10},
\tag{16}
\]
\[
p_\varnothing=\frac{291}{200},\qquad

\tag{17}
\]
\[

It has

\[

## Proof

We first audit Scott's applicability to the middle halves.  Each \(K_i\)
is the intersection of the compact convex polygon \(K\) with a closed
halfplane, hence is compact and convex.  Finite interior gives
\[
M-u_i>0,\qquad m-v_i>0.
\]
The closures of
\[
A_0\cap\{f_i>L_i\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
A_2\cap\{f_i>-L_i\}
\]
are two-dimensional convex bodies.  Compatibility and affineness put
their midpoint body in \(K_i\), because both displayed quantities are
nonnegative on the closures; they are strict on the positive-area
interiors.  Thus every \(K_i\) has positive area and is a
two-dimensional convex body.

The exact cells with \(i\in G\) have total area \(c_i\).  The cutting
line has area zero, so
\[
|K_i|=k-c_i>0.
\]
Their common intersection is empty: a point in all three would satisfy
\[
f_1\geq0,\qquad f_2\geq0,\qquad f_3\geq0,
\]
contrary to \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\).

Up to the three null cutting lines, a point is outside
\(\bigcup_iK_i\) exactly when all three \(f_i\) are negative.  This is
exactly the interior part of \(R_{123}\); changing strict to weak
inequalities on the cutting lines changes no area.  Therefore
\[
\left|\bigcup_iK_i\right|=k-r.
\tag{20}
\]
Scott's theorem, in the exact form recorded and applied in accepted fact
`c98440f17e611ea2`, now proves (9).

By the crossings,
\[
k-c_i=k-h+u_i+v_i.
\]
Let
\[
z=\min\{u_1+m-b,\ u_2+m-a,\ u_3+q\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Multiplying (9) by \(5\) and rearranging gives
\[
9h\geq4k+5r+9z.
\]
Since \(9h=2(M+m+k)\), this is (10).

Accepted fact `0819231d0af83197` gives, for the support in (1),
\[
U+\gamma+2q+E_R=\frac{2M-m-k}{3}.
\]
This proves (6).  Substitution in (10) gives
\[
k\leq3m+3\mathcal L-5r-9z.
\tag{22}
\]
On the other hand, the first no-half inequality in (7), together with
(6), gives
\[
3\mathcal L<m+k,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
k>3\mathcal L-m.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (22)-(23) cancels \(k\) and \(\mathcal L\), proving (11).
The other two no-half inequalities are retained as
\[
k+\mathcal L>\frac m3,
\qquad
k<3m+3\mathcal L.
\tag{24}
\]
If the canonical relabeling has \(M\geq m\), equation (6) also gives
\[
k+3\mathcal L\geq m.
\tag{25}
\]

The left tails in (3), together with the crossings, give
\[
u_i+v_i\leq m.
\]
Using (2) proves (4).  Also \(u_i\geq0\), so the minimum in (21) is at
least
\[
\min\{m-b,m-a,q\}.
\]
Dropping \(5r\geq0\) from (11) proves (12), and direct substitution
verifies (13).

We next audit the endpoint halves.  Each \(Q_i^+\) is a compact convex
intersection.  Under the exact support (1), its area is
\[
|Q_1^+|=b,\qquad
|Q_2^+|=a,\qquad
|Q_3^+|=a+b+\gamma=m-q.
\tag{26}
\]
All three areas are positive, so these sets are two-dimensional convex
bodies.  Their common intersection is empty, because a common point
would satisfy
\[
f_i+L_i\geq0\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
whereas
\[
\sum_i(f_i+L_i)=-1+\sum_iL_i< -2.
\]
Up to null cutting lines, a point lies outside their union exactly when
all three \(f_i+L_i\) are negative, namely in \(Q_{123}\).  Hence
\[
\left|\bigcup_iQ_i^+\right|=m-q.
\tag{27}
\]
Scott's theorem applied to (26)-(27) proves (14), and positivity of
\(\gamma\) proves (15).

Accepted fact `d3afd91c459bcce8` applies because
\(p_\varnothing,q_1,q_2>0\).  Its convex-hull spill has positive area
inside \(R_{12}\), proving (8).  The accepted statement is qualitative:
it contains no lower bound in terms of the endpoint cell areas,
relative widths, or an affine normalization.  Therefore it cannot
replace \(r_{12}>0\) by a positive term of known size in (6), (11), or
(22).

It remains to verify the scalar survivor.  Equations (16)-(18) give
\[
U=\frac3{20},\qquad
E_R=\frac3{100},\qquad
\mathcal L
=\frac3{20}+\frac1{10}+\frac15+\frac3{100}
=\frac{12}{25}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{2M-m-k}{3}
=\frac{12}{25}
=\mathcal L,
\]
which proves \(W=0\) by accepted facts `91cb5bfc71da49ac` and
`0819231d0af83197`.  Directly, the \(P,Q,R\) contributions to \(W\)
are respectively
\[
-\frac{69}{25},\qquad
\frac{19}{10},\qquad
\frac{43}{50},
\]
and sum to zero.

The total area is
\[
M+m+k=\frac{27}{8},
\]
so \(h=3/4\).  Equation (19) verifies all three crossings.  Moreover,
\[
c_i+m>h,\qquad c_i+M>h
\]
for every \(i\), so both canonical tails hold strictly.

Every slice is strictly smaller than half the total:
\[
\frac{321}{200}<\frac{27}{16},\qquad
1<\frac{27}{16},\qquad
\frac{77}{100}<\frac{27}{16}.
\]
Thus all inequalities in (7) hold, and \(M\geq m\).

For the middle Scott inequality,
\[
k-r=\frac{19}{25},
\qquad
\min_i(k-c_i)=\frac{17}{100},
\]
so
\[
\frac{19}{25}>
\frac95\frac{17}{100}
=\frac{153}{500}.
\]
For the endpoint Scott inequality,
\[
m-q=\frac9{10},
\qquad
\min_i|Q_i^+|=\frac25,
\]
so
\[
\frac9{10}>
\frac95\frac25
=\frac{18}{25}.
\]
Finally,
\[
5r+9\min_i(u_i+v_i)
=\frac1{20}+\frac{27}{20}
=\frac75<4m,
\]
and \(r_{12}=1/100>0\).  This verifies every derived scalar
constraint.

The table does not specify affine functions or convex bodies.  What it
lacks is precisely a quantitative joint consequence of
\[
\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_1}{2}\subseteq R_1,\qquad
\frac{P_\varnothing+Q_2}{2}\subseteq R_2,
\]
and the positive \(R_{12}\)-spill between those two midpoint bodies.
An affine-normalized lower bound for that spill, or a joint union
inequality charging it together with the two midpoint-body areas, is
the next geometric input.  Scott's two separate applications do not
supply such a coupling.

## External sources

P. R. Scott, "On the union of convex bodies with no interior point in common", Mathematika 37 (1990), 245-250, https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300012961.  The theorem used states that three two-dimensional convex bodies whose interiors have empty common intersection satisfy
\[
\left|K_1\cup K_2\cup K_3\right|
\geq\frac95\min_i|K_i|.
\]
Equations (20) and (27), together with the positive-area and empty-common-intersection checks above, verify every hypothesis in both applications.
