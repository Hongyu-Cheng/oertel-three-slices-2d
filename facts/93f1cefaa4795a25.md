## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine functions on \(\mathbb R^2\) with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional convex polygon. For every nonempty \(J\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), define, as in accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`,
\[
R_J
=
K\cap
\{f_j\le 0\ (j\in J),\ f_j>0\ (j\notin J)\},
\qquad
r_J=|R_J|.
\]
Then
\[
r_{12}>0,\qquad r_{13}>0,\qquad r_{23}>0
\]
imply
\[
r_{123}>0.
\]
Consequently, no common-plane polygons \(A_0,A_2,K\) can realize all cell masses of accepted fact `a6e9e8f9abdefa92`, irrespective of the midpoint inclusion and crossing equalities.

## Proof

Consider the affine map
\[
F:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow
\Pi:=\{z\in\mathbb R^3:z_1+z_2+z_3=-1\},
\qquad
F(x)=(f_1(x),f_2(x),f_3(x)).
\]
Its linear part has rank two because two nonparallel linear parts among the \(f_i\) are linearly independent. Thus \(F\) is an affine isomorphism onto \(\Pi\). In particular, it preserves collinearity and preserves positivity or vanishing of two-dimensional area up to a fixed positive factor. Put \(\widehat K=F(K)\).

In \(\Pi\), the strict interiors of the three doubleton chambers are
\[
U_{23}=\{z_1>0,\ z_2<0,\ z_3<0\},
\]
\[
U_{13}=\{z_1<0,\ z_2>0,\ z_3<0\},
\]
\[
U_{12}=\{z_1<0,\ z_2<0,\ z_3>0\}.
\]
For example, \(F(R_{23})\setminus U_{23}\) is contained in the two lines
\[
\Pi\cap\{z_2=0\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Pi\cap\{z_3=0\},
\]
and hence has two-dimensional area zero. The same observation applies to the other two doubleton chambers. Therefore the three hypotheses \(r_{23},r_{13},r_{12}>0\) imply that
\[
|\widehat K\cap U_{23}|>0,\qquad
|\widehat K\cap U_{13}|>0,\qquad
|\widehat K\cap U_{12}|>0.
\]

Choose
\[
v_1\in\widehat K\cap U_{23},
\qquad
v_2\in\widehat K\cap U_{13}.
\]
These points are distinct. The line through them has two-dimensional area zero, so the positive-area set \(\widehat K\cap U_{12}\) contains a point \(v_3\) outside that line. Thus \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) are affinely independent. Write
\[
v_1=(a,-b,-c),\qquad
v_2=(-d,e,-f),\qquad
v_3=(-g,-h,i),
\]
where all nine displayed letters are positive. Since each point belongs to \(\Pi\),
\[
a+1=b+c,\qquad
e+1=d+f,\qquad
i+1=g+h.
\]

Define
\[
\lambda_1=dg+dh+fg,
\]
\[
\lambda_2=bg+bh+ch,
\]
\[
\lambda_3=bf+cd+cf.
\]
All three numbers are strictly positive. Let \(V\) be the matrix whose columns are \(v_1,v_2,v_3\). The preceding identities give
\[
V+I=
\begin{pmatrix}
b+c&-d&-g\\
-b&d+f&-h\\
-c&-f&g+h
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct expansion yields
\[
(b+c)\lambda_1=d\lambda_2+g\lambda_3,
\]
\[
(d+f)\lambda_2=b\lambda_1+h\lambda_3,
\]
\[
(g+h)\lambda_3=c\lambda_1+f\lambda_2.
\]
Hence
\[
(V+I)
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda_1\\
\lambda_2\\
\lambda_3
\end{pmatrix}
=0.
\]
Writing \(\Lambda=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3\), it follows that
\[
z:=
\frac{\lambda_1v_1+\lambda_2v_2+\lambda_3v_3}{\Lambda}
=
\left(
-\frac{\lambda_1}{\Lambda},
-\frac{\lambda_2}{\Lambda},
-\frac{\lambda_3}{\Lambda}
\right).
\]
Thus every coordinate of \(z\) is strictly negative. Since all barycentric coefficients are strictly positive and \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) are affinely independent, \(z\) lies in the relative interior of the nondegenerate triangle
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}.
\]
Convexity gives \(\Delta\subseteq\widehat K\). Moreover, \(z\) lies in the open central chamber
\[
U_{123}=\{z_1<0,\ z_2<0,\ z_3<0\}.
\]
Therefore a sufficiently small open disk in \(\Pi\) centered at \(z\) lies in both \(\Delta\) and \(U_{123}\). Hence
\[
|\widehat K\cap U_{123}|>0,
\]
and consequently \(r_{123}>0\).

This also covers all boundary and degeneracy possibilities. Chamber boundaries are finite unions of lines and cannot account for a positive-area doubleton intersection. The third point was chosen off the line through the first two, so the triangle is nondegenerate. The constructed barycentric coefficients and central coordinates are all strict, so neither the convex combination nor its central-chamber intersection lies only on a boundary.

Finally, accepted fact `a6e9e8f9abdefa92` prescribes
\[
r_{12}=r_{13}=r_{23}=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
r_{123}=0.
\]
The universal implication just proved would force \(r_{123}>0\). Thus its \(R\)-cell data already have no two-dimensional convex common-plane realization. Choices of \(A_0,A_2\), the inclusion \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\), and the crossing equalities cannot remove this contradiction.
