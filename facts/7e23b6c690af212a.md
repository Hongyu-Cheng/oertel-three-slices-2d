## Statement

Write
\[
[u,v]=u_1v_2-u_2v_1,\qquad Ju=(-u_2,u_1).
\]
Use the singleton coordinates \(S=f_2,T=f_3\) and physical-area
factor
\[
\gamma=\frac1{|\det(\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3)|}
\]
from accepted fact `82b3ba85b724a42f`.  Let
\[
B=(b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}),\qquad b_j=(S_j,T_j),
\]
be a counterclockwise boundary list with every zero-length edge
deleted.  Put
\[
d_j=b_{j+1}-b_j,\qquad
g_j(X)=\lambda_j(X)=-[d_j,X-b_j].
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(g_j\leq0\) on \(B\).  Set
\[
g_{\sf S}(S,T)=-S,\qquad g_{\sf T}(S,T)=-T,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal J_{\rm ch}
=\{j:\min(S_j,S_{j+1})<0
\text{ or }\min(T_j,T_{j+1})<0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For
\[
I_Z=\{{\sf S},{\sf T}\}\cup\mathcal J_{\rm ch},
\qquad
I_W=\{{\sf S},{\sf T}\}\cup\{0,\ldots,n-1\},
\tag{4}
\]
define
\[
Z=\bigcap_{i\in I_Z}\{g_i\leq0\},\qquad
W=\bigcap_{i\in I_W}\{g_i\leq0\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(W=B\cap\{S,T\geq0\}\), \(W\subseteq Z\), and, whenever \(Z\)
is bounded, the zero-charge capacity is
\[
\boxed{\mathscr C(B)=|Z\setminus W|=|Z|-|W|.}
\tag{6}
\]
Here and below area is physical planar area.  Boundaries on
\(S=0\) or \(T=0\) have zero area, so (6) is unchanged if the open
singleton chamber is used.

The following is an exact finite fixed-code formula for either
\(P=Z\) or \(P=W\).  Write every form in coordinate coefficients,
\[
g_i(X)=n_i\mathbin{\cdot}X+c_i.
\tag{7}
\]
For the axes,
\[
(n_{\sf S},c_{\sf S})=((-1,0),0),\qquad
(n_{\sf T},c_{\sf T})=((0,-1),0),
\tag{8}
\]
and for a polygon edge,
\[
n_j=((d_j)_2,-(d_j)_1),\qquad c_j=[d_j,b_j].
\tag{9}
\]
For two nonparallel support lines put
\[
\delta_{ik}=[n_i,n_k],
\qquad
x(i,k)=
\frac1{\delta_{ik}}
\left(
(n_i)_2c_k-c_i(n_k)_2,\,
c_i(n_k)_1-(n_i)_1c_k
\right).
\tag{10}
\]

A two-dimensional bounded code for \(P\) consists of distinct
support indices \(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}\), \(m\geq3\), and vertices
\[
p_\nu=x(a_{\nu-1},a_\nu)
\qquad(\nu\ {\rm mod}\ m),
\tag{11}
\]
subject to the finite predicates
\[
\delta_{a_{\nu-1},a_\nu}\neq0,
\qquad
\|p_{\nu+1}-p_\nu\|^2>0,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
g_{a_\nu}(p_\mu)<0
\quad
\text{if }\mu\notin\{\nu,\nu+1\},
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
h_{\nu i}:=-g_i(p_\nu)\geq0
\quad(i\in I_P).
\tag{14}
\]
The incidences
\[
g_{a_{\nu-1}}(p_\nu)
=g_{a_\nu}(p_\nu)=0
\tag{15}
\]
are identities.  Conditions (12)--(14) certify the exposed-edge
cycle but do not choose one of its two orientations.  Define
\[
\Omega_P=\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}[p_\nu,p_{\nu+1}].
\tag{16}
\]
If a supplied cycle has \(\Omega_P<0\), replace it canonically by
\[
p'_\nu=p_{-\nu},\qquad a'_\nu=a_{-\nu-1}.
\tag{17}
\]
This preserves (11)--(15) and changes \(\Omega_P\) to
\(-\Omega_P\).  A valid two-dimensional code requires
\[
\boxed{\Omega_P>0.}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus its cycle is counterclockwise.  A zero of \(\Omega_P\) is a
dimension or orientation recoding event.  The certified polygon and
its area are
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{p_0,\ldots,p_{m-1}\},
\qquad
|P|=\frac{\gamma}{2}\Omega_P.
\tag{19}
\]
All quantities in (10)--(19) are rational functions of the support
coefficients.  Signs may be tested polynomially by multiplying by
even powers of the nonzero denominators in (10).

Let the polygon vertices move with velocities
\(\beta_j=\dot b_j\), and put
\(\dot d_j=\beta_{j+1}-\beta_j\).  The axes have
\(\dot n=\dot c=0\), while
\[
\dot n_j=((\dot d_j)_2,-(\dot d_j)_1),
\qquad
\dot c_j=[\dot d_j,b_j]+[d_j,\beta_j].
\tag{20}
\]
For \(p_\nu=x(i,k)\), its exact velocity is the unique solution
\[
\begin{pmatrix}n_i^{\mathsf T}\\ n_k^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}
\dot p_\nu
=-
\begin{pmatrix}
\dot n_i\mathbin{\cdot}p_\nu+\dot c_i\\
\dot n_k\mathbin{\cdot}p_\nu+\dot c_k
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equivalently, for an active moving polygon edge,
\[
0=Dg_j(p_\nu)
=-[\dot d_j,p_\nu-b_j]-[d_j,\dot p_\nu-\beta_j],
\tag{22}
\]
and an active axis gives respectively
\(\dot S(p_\nu)=0\) or \(\dot T(p_\nu)=0\).
Thus wall vertices require no slope division.  Differentiating the
counterclockwise shoelace formula in (19) gives
\[
D|P|[\beta]
=\frac{\gamma}{2}
\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}
[p_{\nu-1}-p_{\nu+1},\dot p_\nu].
\tag{23}
\]
For fixed bounded counterclockwise codes of \(Z\) and \(W\),
\[
\boxed{
D\mathscr C(B)[\beta]
=\frac{\gamma}{2}
\sum_\nu[z_{\nu-1}-z_{\nu+1},\dot z_\nu]
-\frac{\gamma}{2}
\sum_\mu[w_{\mu-1}-w_{\mu+1},\dot w_\mu].
}
\tag{24}
\]
This is the capacity row required by accepted fact
`42e17c270e11ad3e`.

Nonempty bounded lower-dimensional codes are as follows.  A carrier
certificate is a finite set \(A\subseteq I_P\) and positive rational
functions \(\mu_i\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{i\in A}\mu_i g_i\equiv0.
\tag{25}
\]
Every feasible point then satisfies \(g_i=0\) for all \(i\in A\).
The rank of \(\{n_i:i\in A\}\) is the codimension of the carrier.

For rank one, solve one nonzero carrier equation rationally as
\[
X=X_0+tq.
\tag{26}
\]
Every remaining inequality restricts to
\[
\alpha_i t+\kappa_i\leq0.
\tag{27}
\]
Choose a lower-bound index \(\ell\) with \(\alpha_\ell<0\) and an
upper-bound index \(u\) with \(\alpha_u>0\), and put
\[
t_-=-\frac{\kappa_\ell}{\alpha_\ell},
\qquad
t_+=-\frac{\kappa_u}{\alpha_u}.
\tag{28}
\]
The exact bounded interval predicates are
\[
t_-<t_+,\qquad
h^-_i:=-(\alpha_i t_-+\kappa_i)\geq0,\qquad
h^+_i:=-(\alpha_i t_++\kappa_i)\geq0
\quad(i\in I_P).
\tag{29}
\]
Then
\[
P=[X_0+t_-q,X_0+t_+q].
\tag{30}
\]
At a tight nonstructural endpoint inequality, retaining this code
requires \(Dh^-_i[\beta]\geq0\) or \(Dh^+_i[\beta]\geq0\).

For carrier rank two, two independent carrier equations give one
rational point \(X_0\).  Define every point slack
\[
h_i^0:=-g_i(X_0)\geq0\qquad(i\in I_P).
\tag{31}
\]
Then \(P=\{X_0\}\).  If \(h_i^0=0\) is a declared persistent
incidence, its differential is an equality.  For every tight
nonstructural inequality \(h_i^0=0\), the intrinsic feasibility row is
\[
\boxed{
Dh_i^0[\beta]
=-Dg_i(X_0)[\beta]
=-\bigl(\dot n_i\mathbin{\cdot}X_0
+n_i\mathbin{\cdot}\dot X_0+\dot c_i\bigr)\geq0.
}
\tag{32}
\]
Here \(\dot X_0\) is obtained by differentiating the two independent
structural carrier equations.  The interval and singleton cases have
\[
|P|=0,\qquad D|P|=0
\tag{33}
\]
on their persistent carrier strata.  A zero interval length or a
carrier-rank change is a recoding event.

There is also an exact empty-intersection code.  It consists of a
fixed subset \(F\subseteq I_P\) and positive rational functions
\(\theta_i\), obtained from one fixed nonsingular pivot choice, such
that
\[
\sum_{i\in F}\theta_i=1,\qquad
\sum_{i\in F}\theta_i n_i=0,\qquad
\sigma_F:=\sum_{i\in F}\theta_i c_i>0.
\tag{34}
\]
The first two identities are structural.  The strict validity
predicates are the pivot denominator being nonzero,
\(\theta_i>0\) for every \(i\in F\), and \(\sigma_F>0\).
On this code,
\[
P=\varnothing,\qquad |P|=0,\qquad D|P|=0.
\tag{35}
\]
The first zero of the pivot denominator, a coefficient
\(\theta_i\), or the strict Farkas margin \(\sigma_F\) is an immediate
empty-code recoding event.  The intersection may still be empty
under a different certificate, but that requires a new code.

Boundedness of a nonempty half-plane intersection has an exact finite
certificate.  Put
\[
\operatorname{rec}(P)
=\{r:n_i\mathbin{\cdot}r\leq0\text{ for every }i\in I_P\}.
\tag{36}
\]
Because every \(n_i\neq0\),
\[
\operatorname{rec}(P)\neq\{0\}
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{for some }i\in I_P,\ \varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},
\quad
n_k\mathbin{\cdot}(\varepsilon Jn_i)\leq0
\text{ for every }k\in I_P.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus a bounded fixed code chooses, for each
\((i,\varepsilon)\), one witness
\(\kappa(i,\varepsilon)\in I_P\) and requires the strict rational
margin
\[
\rho_{i,\varepsilon}
:=
n_{\kappa(i,\varepsilon)}
\mathbin{\cdot}(\varepsilon Jn_i)>0.
\tag{38}
\]
The finite family (38) exists exactly when
\(\operatorname{rec}(P)=\{0\}\).  The first zero of a chosen
\(\rho_{i,\varepsilon}\) is an immediate boundedness recoding event.
If another witness remains positive, one recodes; if no complete
witness family exists, the intersection is unbounded and the
bounded-capacity release stops.

The complete validity and event classification for bounded codes is
the following.

1. The input polygon predicates are
\[
\|d_j\|^2>0,\qquad
-g_j(b_k)>0\quad(k\notin\{j,j+1\})
\tag{39}
\]
after reduction to genuine geometric facets, together with the signs
of \(S_j,T_j,S_{j+1},T_{j+1}\) which determine (3).  The adjacent
incidences \(g_j(b_j)=g_j(b_{j+1})=0\) are structural.  If a
collinear subdivision is intentionally retained, its additional zero
support incidences are structural instead of strict; otherwise the
first zero in (39) is a polygon boundary event.

2. For a two-dimensional bounded code the rational predicates are
the denominators and noncollision predicates in (12), the strict
skeleton predicates (13), the weak facet-vertex slacks (14),
\(\Omega_P>0\) in (18), and the boundedness certificate (38).  A raw
cycle with \(\Omega_P<0\) is reversed once by (17).  For rank one they
are the carrier identities and fixed rank minor in (25)--(26), the
bound-slope signs in (28), the endpoint separation and weak slacks in
(29), and (38).  For rank two they are the carrier identities, a
fixed nonzero rank-two minor, all point slacks (31), and (38).  For
the empty code they are exactly the identities and strict predicates
in (34); no recession code is needed.

3. A zero is a persistent structural equality precisely when the
code declares that geometric incidence for the whole stratum.  This
class contains (15), marked wall incidences, fixed shared or parallel
normal rays, coincident positive rescalings of one support, the
carrier identities (25), the two identities in (34), and any retained
triple, endpoint, or singleton incidence.  Its differential is an
equality row.

4. Apart from structural incidences, a zero of a weak
facet-vertex slack in (14), an endpoint slack in (29), or a singleton
slack in (31) is an active intrinsic feasibility constraint.  Its row
is respectively
\[
Dh_{\nu i}[\beta]\geq0,\qquad
Dh_i^\pm[\beta]\geq0,\qquad
Dh_i^0[\beta]\geq0,
\tag{40}
\]
with the last row given explicitly by (32).  These are exactly the
capacity-code feasibility rows besides \(D\mathscr C\).

5. Every other zero is an immediate boundary event: a formerly
unmarked polygon vertex reaches a wall and can change
\(\mathcal J_{\rm ch}\); an input support slack in (39) vanishes; a
polygon edge reaches zero length; a selected intersection denominator
vanishes; two selected vertices collide; a strict skeleton slack in
(13) vanishes; \(\Omega_P\) vanishes; a carrier multiplier or fixed
nonzero rank minor vanishes; a one-dimensional bound slope vanishes;
an interval collapses; an empty-code pivot, coefficient, or strict
Farkas margin in (34) vanishes; or a chosen boundedness margin in
(38) vanishes.  At such a point one recodes before using another
differential formula.  If boundedness itself is lost, the release
stops rather than assigning a finite capacity row.

Parallel and redundant lines cause no missing bounded case.  Adjacent
selected supports cannot be parallel because of (12).  Parallel
supports which describe the same oriented line are merged, and
parallel supports at different offsets are ordered by the vertex,
endpoint, or point slacks.  Oppositely oriented parallel supports can
be nonadjacent selected facets and are harmless.  Parallelism
involving only a redundant inequality is irrelevant unless its weak
slack becomes zero, when (40) is its intrinsic row.  A repeated listed
polygon vertex gives \(d_j=0\) and \(g_j\equiv0\); deleting it changes
neither (5) nor any formula.  Consecutive collinear subdivisions give
positive multiples of one support and are merged.

The bounded classification is exhaustive.  A finite half-plane
intersection is either empty, certified by (34), or nonempty.  A
nonempty bounded intersection has affine dimension two, one, or zero.
These are exactly the codes (11)--(19), (25)--(30), and (25),
(31), respectively.  Conditions (37)--(38) certify that no nonzero
recession direction has been omitted.  No exhaustiveness claim is
made for unbounded intersections.

Unbounded \(Z\) is a separate regime.  If \(Z\) is nonempty,
two-dimensional, and unbounded, then
\[
\mathscr C(B)=+\infty
\tag{41}
\]
and no finite \(C^1\) capacity row exists.  An unbounded
lower-dimensional \(Z\) can have zero planar area, but this lemma
deliberately assigns it no fixed capacity formula or derivative:
failure of (38) is a stopping event before applying accepted fact
`42e17c270e11ad3e`.  This is the entire assertion made here about
unbounded \(Z\).

The construction is invariant under cyclic relabeling, the canonical
orientation reversal (17), insertion or deletion of repeated boundary
points, subdivision or merging of a collinear side, and positive
\(C^1\) rescaling of any support equation.  Before (3)--(5), an
arbitrary description of \(B\) is reduced to its geometric boundary
facets: zero edges and nonfacet auxiliary inequalities are deleted,
while collinear subdivisions are merged.  The genuine facet
constraints in \(I_Z\) and \(I_W\) are then all retained in the weak
slack lists, even when one is redundant for the particular \(Z\) or
\(W\).  Any two fixed bounded codes for the same moving set give the
same area.  Their differentials agree on the tangent space to their
common structural equalities.  At an active zero, positive rescaling
only multiplies a row in (40) by a positive scalar, so the intrinsic
feasible tangent cone is unchanged.

## Proof

Accepted fact `82b3ba85b724a42f` proves that, for a point in the open
singleton chamber, both transverse fan charges vanish exactly on the
region \(Z\) in (5).  It also proves \(g_j\leq0\) on \(B\).
The standard half-plane description of a counterclockwise convex
polygon therefore gives
\[
B=\bigcap_{j=0}^{n-1}\{g_j\leq0\},
\]
after harmless deletion of zero-length edges.  Intersecting with the
two axis halfspaces proves
\[
W=B\cap\{S,T\geq0\}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal J_{\rm ch}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), one has
\(W\subseteq Z\).  Hence the zero-charge part outside \(B\) is
\(Z\setminus W\).  The two coordinate walls are null for planar area,
so the open-chamber characterization from the accepted fact gives
(6), including every wall boundary.  Under these hypotheses the
origin lies in \(W\), so the empty codes below are not realized; they
are included to make the bounded half-plane classification complete.

We prove the two-dimensional formula.  Expanding (1) gives
\[
g_j(S,T)
=(d_j)_2S-(d_j)_1T+[d_j,b_j],
\]
which is (9).  Cramer's rule applied to
\[
n_i\mathbin{\cdot}X=-c_i,\qquad
n_k\mathbin{\cdot}X=-c_k
\]
gives (10).  Therefore every selected vertex in (11) is rational and
the incidences (15) hold identically.

For each \(\nu\), the line \(g_{a_\nu}=0\) contains
\(p_\nu,p_{\nu+1}\).  By (13), all other selected vertices lie
strictly in its inward halfspace.  Thus
\([p_\nu,p_{\nu+1}]\) is an exposed edge of the convex hull of all
the \(p_\mu\).  The noncollision condition makes these \(m\) exposed
edges distinct.  A convex hull of \(m\) points has at most \(m\)
edges, so these are all its edges and the displayed list is its cyclic
boundary, with orientation not yet chosen.  Since the hull is
two-dimensional, \(\Omega_P\neq0\).  Reversal (17) preserves every
support incidence, feasibility slack, and exposed segment, while
replacing \(\Omega_P\) by \(-\Omega_P\).  Condition (18) chooses
exactly the counterclockwise order.  It follows that
\[
\bigcap_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\{g_{a_\nu}\leq0\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{p_0,\ldots,p_{m-1}\}.
\tag{42}
\]
Every remaining inequality contains every \(p_\nu\) by (14), hence,
by affinity, contains their convex hull.  Intersecting those
remaining halfspaces does not change (42), proving (19).
Counterclockwise shoelace in the \(S,T\)-plane, followed by
multiplication by \(\gamma\), proves its area formula.

Differentiate the two active line equations at a selected vertex.
Their coefficient matrix has determinant \(\delta_{ik}\neq0\), so
the velocity exists uniquely and is (21).  Direct differentiation of
(1) gives (22).  Differentiating shoelace and reindexing its second
sum gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D|P|[\beta]
&=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_\nu
\bigl([\dot p_\nu,p_{\nu+1}]
      +[p_\nu,\dot p_{\nu+1}]\bigr)\\
&=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_\nu
[p_{\nu-1}-p_{\nu+1},\dot p_\nu],
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (23) and (24).  Condition (18) ensures that this is the
differential of positive area.  Equations (10), (20), and (21) prove
rationality.

For the carrier construction, (25) and \(g_i\leq0\) imply
\(g_i=0\) for every \(i\in A\).  A rank-one carrier is a line, and
substitution of (26) gives (27).  The signs in (28) make \(t_-\) the
selected lower endpoint and \(t_+\) the selected upper endpoint.
The weak slacks (29) say that every inequality contains both
endpoints, hence the entire interval, proving (30).  Differentiating
a tight weak endpoint slack gives its row in (40).

A rank-two carrier has a unique common point \(X_0\), and (31) is
equivalent to feasibility of that point.  There are no other points
because every feasible point satisfies all carrier equalities.  If a
nonstructural slack is tight, a one-sided motion stays feasible only
if its derivative is nonnegative.  Differentiating
\(-g_i(X_0)\) gives exactly (32), including the velocity of the moving
carrier point.  Thus the rank-two point code has all intrinsic rows,
despite its area and area differential being zero.

Conversely, for a nonempty lower-dimensional polyhedron, the
inequalities active on its relative interior positively span the
orthogonal complement of its affine hull.  A finite positive
dependence among their affine forms supplies (25).  For a bounded set,
rank one then has both finite endpoints and rank two is the single
point above.  Hence the carrier codes omit no nonempty bounded
lower-dimensional case.

For the empty code, summing \(g_i(X)\leq0\) with the positive weights
in (34) would give
\[
0<\sigma_F
=\sum_{i\in F}\theta_i g_i(X)\leq0,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence \(P\) is empty.  Conversely, apply the
finite-dimensional Farkas separation argument proved in accepted fact
`42e17c270e11ad3e` to
\(n_i\mathbin{\cdot}X\leq-c_i\).  It gives nonnegative weights, not
all zero, with
\[
\sum_i\theta_i n_i=0,\qquad
\sum_i\theta_i c_i>0
\]
whenever the system is infeasible.  Delete zero weights and normalize
the rest to sum to one.  At a rational realization the certificate
can be chosen rational.  Fixing a nonsingular pivot and any free
coordinates makes the weights rational functions locally; strict
positivity and strict margin persist until a listed event.  This
proves (34)--(35).

We prove the boundedness criterion.  If a displayed direction
\(\varepsilon Jn_i\) in (37) satisfies all homogeneous inequalities,
it is a nonzero recession direction.  Conversely, a nontrivial
planar polyhedral cone is a line, halfplane, or cone with an extreme
ray.  It has a nonzero boundary ray on
\(n_i\mathbin{\cdot}r=0\) for some input normal, and that ray is a
positive multiple of \(Jn_i\) or \(-Jn_i\).  This proves (37).
Negating (37) gives exactly the finite witness family (38).

We can now check completeness without making an unbounded
classification claim.  As long as the input predicates (39) and wall
signs do not change, (1)--(4) give the same reduced polygon and
half-plane families.  In dimension two, nonvanishing selected
determinants keep every vertex finite; noncollision, strict exposure,
and orientation keep the same counterclockwise exposed-edge cycle;
and the weak slacks keep every other halfspace redundant on its convex
hull.  Conversely, failure of that formula is detected by
(12)--(14) or (18).  In rank one, the carrier rank, two bound slopes,
endpoint separation, and endpoint slacks are exactly the bounded
interval construction.  In rank two, the carrier rank and point
slacks are exactly the singleton construction.  The strict Farkas
certificate is exact for the empty case.  Finally, (38) certifies
boundedness and its first lost strict witness causes recoding.  This
proves exhaustiveness for bounded fixed codes only.

The three-way zero classification follows.  Declared incidences,
carrier identities, and Farkas identities are structural equalities.
A tight weak vertex, endpoint, or singleton slack is an intrinsic
feasibility row, including every row in (32).  Every zero of a
denominator, strict exposure or orientation sign, dimension minor,
endpoint separation, strict Farkas predicate, or boundedness witness
changes the fixed code and is an immediate event.  This supplies the
finite formula and exhaustive bounded-code predicate hypothesis which
accepted fact `42e17c270e11ad3e` left as an input.

Finally, cyclic relabeling permutes the halfspaces and shoelace
summands and preserves \(\Omega_P\).  Reversal (17) preserves the
geometric polygon and flips the signed shoelace sum, so the canonical
rule selects the same positive orientation.  A zero-length edge has
\(g_j\equiv0\).  Subdividing a collinear side replaces its support by
positive multiples of the same form.  Such a side is charging after
subdivision if and only if at least one subedge is charging: if an
endpoint of the whole side has a negative wall coordinate, the
adjacent subedge has it, while an affine coordinate cannot become
negative inside a segment whose endpoints are nonnegative.  Thus
subdivision changes neither \(Z\) nor \(W\).

If \(g_i\) is replaced by \(\alpha_i g_i\), with
\(\alpha_i>0\), its halfspace and line intersections are unchanged.
Every strict geometric sign and \(\Omega_P\) are unchanged.  Each
boundedness margin in (38) is multiplied by a positive factor.  At an
active zero,
\[
D(\alpha_i g_i)=\alpha_iDg_i,
\]
so structural and feasibility rows change only by positive scaling.
For an empty certificate, replacing
\(\theta_i\) by a common normalization of
\(\theta_i/\alpha_i\) preserves (34) and its strict margin sign.
Different valid bounded codes for the same geometric set therefore
give the same area, and their differentials agree on their common
structural tangent space.  Genuine facet constraints remain in the
weak slack lists even when currently redundant, so their first
contact is not lost.  This proves all asserted invariances.
