## Statement

Use the normalized cap and tail conventions of accepted fact \(4ec05d1da924da91\). Let
\[
P=[-\delta,0]\times[0,h],\qquad
Q=[\delta+2,\delta+2+w]\times[-q,0],
\qquad
R=[0,r]\times[-a,c],
\]
where \(\delta,h,w,q,r,a,c>0\), and assume
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}\subseteq R.
\]
Put
\[
M=\delta h,\qquad m=wq,\qquad k=r(a+c),\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9,
\]
and assume \(M\ge m\). Assume also the residual-area domain of accepted fact \(15bb2c1680a0254e\): if
\[
\mu=\frac mM,\qquad \sigma=\frac kM,\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
then \(0<\mu<\rho\) and \(L(\mu)<\sigma<U(\mu)\), where
\[
L(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1-\mu,&0<\mu\le 9/25,\\
(3\mu+2\sqrt\mu-1)/2,&9/25<\mu<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
U(\mu)=
\begin{cases}
1+\mu,&0<\mu\le1/2,\\
2-\mu,&1/2<\mu<\rho.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(E\in\{P,Q,R\}\), define
\[
\alpha_E=|E\cap\{y\le0\}|,\qquad
\beta_E=|E\cap\{x+y\ge0\}|.
\]
Assume
\[
\alpha_P+\alpha_Q+\alpha_R=t,\qquad
\beta_P+\beta_Q+\beta_R=t,
\]
\[
\alpha_R+m\ge t,\qquad \beta_R+m\ge t.
\]
Define
\[
b=|Q\cap\{x\ge\delta+2\}|,\qquad
d=|R\cap\{x\ge1\}|,
\]
and assume
\[
b>0,\qquad k-d+m\ge t.
\]
Then
\[
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]
In fact, no positive parameters satisfy the midpoint inclusion, the two crossing equalities, and \(\beta_R+m\ge t\).

## Proof

Set \(D=\delta+2\). Direct Minkowski addition gives
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}
=
\left[1,\frac{D+w}{2}\right]\times
\left[-\frac q2,\frac h2\right].
\]
Thus full midpoint inclusion is equivalent to
\[
r\ge\frac{D+w}{2},\qquad a\ge\frac q2,\qquad c\ge\frac h2.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
r>\frac D2>1.
\tag{2}
\]
This eliminates the threshold orderings \(r<1\) and \(r=1\).

All crossing caps can be integrated exactly. For the \(y\le0\) crossing,
\[
\alpha_P=0,\qquad \alpha_Q=wq=m,\qquad \alpha_R=ra.
\tag{3}
\]
For the \(x+y\ge0\) crossing,
\[
\beta_P=
\begin{cases}
h^2/2,&h\le\delta,\\
\delta h-\delta^2/2,&h\ge\delta,
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\beta_Q=
\begin{cases}
wq,&q\le D,\\
q(D+w)-\dfrac{q^2+D^2}{2},&D\le q\le D+w,\\
wD+\dfrac{w^2}{2},&q\ge D+w,
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\beta_R=
\begin{cases}
rc+ra-\dfrac{a^2}{2},&a\le r,\\
rc+\dfrac{r^2}{2},&a\ge r.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The formulas agree at every shared threshold.

For completeness, the contact tails in every ordering of \(r\) relative to \(1\) are
\[
b=m,\qquad
d=
\begin{cases}
0,&r\le1,\\
(r-1)(a+c),&r\ge1.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
By (2), only the second formula for \(d\) can occur.

The first crossing equality and (3) give
\[
t=m+ra.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining this with the definition of \(t\) yields
\[
m+ra=\frac{2(M+m+ra+rc)}9,
\]
hence
\[
rc=\frac72(m+ra)-M.
\tag{9}
\]

Let
\[
C_Q=m-\beta_Q.
\]
From (5),
\[
C_Q=
\begin{cases}
0,&q\le D,\\
\dfrac{(q-D)^2}{2},&D\le q\le D+w,\\
w\left(q-D-\dfrac w2\right),&q\ge D+w.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
0\le C_Q\le m.
\tag{11}
\]

The second crossing equality and (8) give
\[
\beta_P+(m-C_Q)+\beta_R=m+ra,
\]
or
\[
C_Q=\beta_P+\beta_R-ra.
\tag{12}
\]
The crossing-tail condition \(\beta_R+m\ge t\), together with (8), says
\[
\beta_R-ra\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(\beta_P>0\), equations (12) and (13) imply \(C_Q>0\). Formula (10) therefore forces
\[
q>D.
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (1) and (14) gives
\[
a\ge\frac q2>\frac D2,\qquad
r\ge\frac{D+w}{2}>\frac D2.
\tag{15}
\]

It remains to eliminate the two possible threshold orderings of \(a\) and \(r\).

Suppose first that \(a\le r\). Equations (6), (9), and (12) give
\[
M-\beta_P
=\frac72(m+ra)-\frac{a^2}{2}-C_Q.
\]
Using \(C_Q\le m\) and \(a^2\le ra\),
\[
M-\beta_P
\ge\frac52m+\frac72ra-\frac{a^2}{2}
\ge\frac52m+3ra
>3ra.
\]
By (15),
\[
M-\beta_P>\frac{3D^2}{4}.
\tag{16}
\]

Suppose instead that \(a\ge r\). Equations (6), (9), and (12) give
\[
M-\beta_P
=\frac72m+\frac52ra+\frac{r^2}{2}-C_Q.
\]
Using \(C_Q\le m\) and \(ra\ge r^2\),
\[
M-\beta_P
\ge\frac52m+\frac52ra+\frac{r^2}{2}
\ge\frac52m+3r^2
>3r^2.
\]
Again by (15),
\[
M-\beta_P>\frac{3D^2}{4}.
\tag{17}
\]

On the other hand, (4) gives in every ordering of \(h\) and \(\delta\)
\[
M-\beta_P=
\begin{cases}
\delta h-h^2/2,&h\le\delta,\\
\delta^2/2,&h\ge\delta,
\end{cases}
\le\frac{\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]
But \(D=\delta+2\), so
\[
\frac{3D^2}{4}-\frac{\delta^2}{2}
=\frac{\delta^2+12\delta+12}{4}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Both (16) and (17) contradict (18) and (19). Therefore the stated joint hypotheses have no solution. The desired inequality follows.
