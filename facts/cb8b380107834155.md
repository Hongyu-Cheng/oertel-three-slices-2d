## Statement

Use the exact common-plane \(A_0\) calibration of accepted fact
`331355cde8383f52`.  In its affine coordinates
\[
X=p_j,\qquad Y=p_\ell,\qquad
p_k=\frac32-X-Y,
\]
the determinant magnitude is \(1\), and
\[
|P_\ell|=\frac1{920},\qquad
\lambda_{0k}=\frac1{46},\qquad
\lambda_{0\ell}=\frac1{10}.
\tag{1}
\]

For a proposed two-dimensional convex polygon \(A_2\), put
\[
u=-q_j,\qquad v=-q_\ell,\qquad w=-q_k,
\qquad u+v+w=\tau=\frac72.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(H\subseteq N=\{j,k,\ell\}\), define the exact cells
\[
Q_H=
A_2\cap
\{r_i\ge0\ (i\in H),\ r_i<0\ (i\notin H)\},
\qquad
(r_j,r_k,r_\ell)=(u,w,v).
\tag{3}
\]
Assume that the only positive-area \(Q\)-cells are
\[
Q_j,\qquad Q_{jk},\qquad Q_N,\qquad Q_{k\ell},
\tag{4}
\]
and that all four have positive area.  Write
\[
g_0=|Q_{jk}|,\qquad g=|Q_N|,\qquad s=|Q_{k\ell}|.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(\lambda_{2k},\lambda_{2\ell}\) be the one-sided coordinate chord
traces of \(A_2\) at \(w=0,v=0\), respectively, and assume
\[
\lambda_{2k}>\frac1{46},\qquad
\lambda_{2\ell}>\frac1{10}.
\tag{6}
\]

Suppose the middle support is the central common-order support
\[
R_j,\qquad R_{jk},\qquad R_N,\qquad R_{k\ell},
\tag{7}
\]
with all four areas positive.  Put
\[
y=|R_{jk}|,\qquad z=|R_N|,\qquad
\omega=|R_{k\ell}|,
\tag{8}
\]
and suppose the \(k\)- and \(\ell\)-crossing equations hold with a
common value \(\theta\).  Then no such \(A_2\) and middle budget exist.
Consequently this exact \(A_0\) calibration has no compatible central
common-order completion satisfying (4), (6), (7), all three crossings,
and \(W=0\).

## Proof

The strict sign chambers in (4), in the coordinate order
\((u,w,v)\), are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &u&w&v\\ \hline
Q_j&>0&<0&<0\\
Q_{jk}&>0&>0&<0\\
Q_N&>0&>0&>0\\
Q_{k\ell}&<0&>0&>0 .
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]
Every positive-area cell contains a point of
\(\operatorname{int}A_2\) with its strict sign pattern.

We first determine the whole \(v=0\) chord.  If a point of
\(A_2\cap\{v=0\}\) had \(w<0\), then \(u=\tau-w>0\).  Moving it
slightly toward an interior point of \(Q_N\) gives interior points with
\[
u>0,\qquad w<0,\qquad v>0,
\]
which lie in the unsupported strict chamber \(Q_{j\ell}\).  If instead
the chord point had \(u<0\), then \(w=\tau-u>0\).  Moving it slightly
toward an interior point of \(Q_{jk}\) gives interior points with
\[
u<0,\qquad w>0,\qquad v<0,
\]
which lie in the unsupported strict chamber \(Q_k\).  Either case
would give an unsupported cell positive area.  Hence
\[
A_2\cap\{v=0\}\subseteq\{u\ge0,\ w\ge0,\ v=0\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Now consider the whole \(w=0\) chord.  If one of its points had
\(v>0\), move it slightly toward an interior point of \(Q_j\).  The
perturbed point has \(v>0,w<0\).  According to the sign of \(u\), it
lies in the unsupported strict chamber \(Q_{j\ell}\) or \(Q_\ell\);
if \(u=0\) at the original point, the perturbation has \(u>0\) and
lies in \(Q_{j\ell}\).  Thus \(v\le0\) throughout the chord.  Since
\(u+v=\tau>0\) on \(w=0\), one also has \(u>0\).  Therefore
\[
A_2\cap\{w=0\}\subseteq\{u>0,\ w=0,\ v\le0\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Positive area in \(Q_{jk}\) and \(Q_N\) puts interior points of
\(A_2\) on opposite sides of \(v=0\).  Positive area in \(Q_j\) and
\(Q_{jk}\) does the same for \(w=0\).  Both cutting lines therefore
meet \(\operatorname{int}A_2\).  The relative interior of either
chord lies in \(\operatorname{int}A_2\); by (10)-(11), a small ball at
each such point meets the strict \(Q_{jk}\)-side.  At a possible
intersection of two cutting lines, the ball meets the strict
\(Q_{jk}\)-wedge.  Taking closures shows that both full chords belong
to \(\overline{Q_{jk}}\), including their endpoints.  Since the
active levels meet the interior, polygonal chord continuity also shows
that the one-sided traces equal the actual full chord lengths.  This
handles support edges, cutting-line intersections, and the weak/strict
boundary convention in (3).

Map the closed cell \(\overline{Q_{jk}}\) to the nonnegative quadrant
by
\[
(r,t)=(w,-v).
\tag{12}
\]
The determinant magnitude is \(1\).  Its image \(D\) is compact and
convex, lies in \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\), and has
\[
|D|=g_0.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (10)-(11) and chord continuity give axis intervals
\[
D\cap\{r=0\}
=\{0\}\times[t_0,t_1],
\qquad
t_1-t_0=\lambda_{2k},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
D\cap\{t=0\}
=[r_0,r_1]\times\{0\},
\qquad
r_1-r_0=\lambda_{2\ell},
\tag{15}
\]
where all four endpoints are nonnegative.

Convexity puts the convex hull of the two intervals in (14)-(15)
inside \(D\).  Its area is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12(r_1t_1-r_0t_0)
={}&\frac12\bigl(
r_0(t_1-t_0)+t_0(r_1-r_0)\\
&\hspace{22mm}+(r_1-r_0)(t_1-t_0)
\bigr)\\
\ge{}&\frac12\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Using (1) and the strict trace inequalities (6),
\[
g_0
\ge\frac12\lambda_{2k}\lambda_{2\ell}
>
\frac12\cdot\frac1{46}\cdot\frac1{10}
=\frac1{920}
=|P_\ell|.
\tag{17}
\]

It remains to use the two crossings.  Write
\[
d=|P_{k\ell}|,\qquad c=|P_\ell|=\frac1{920}.
\]
In the supports (4) and (7), the \(k\)-crossing is
\[
d+(g_0+g+s)+(y+z+\omega)=\theta,
\tag{18}
\]
whereas the \(\ell\)-crossing is
\[
(c+d)+(g+s)+(z+\omega)=\theta.
\tag{19}
\]
Subtracting (19) from (18) gives
\[
c=g_0+y.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(y>0\), equation (20) gives \(g_0<c\), contradicting (17).

Thus no \(A_2\) satisfying (2)-(6) can support the two crossing
equations.  The contradiction precedes and rules out every completion
that additionally imposes the third crossing, \(W=0\), midpoint
containment, or a positive support-gap direction.
