## Statement

Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be affine functions such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla f_1,\nabla f_2,\nabla f_3\}
=\mathbb R^2.
\]
For cyclic \((i,j,k)\), let \(O_i\) be the intersection of \(f_j=0\) and
\(f_k=0\), and put
\[
\mathcal T=\operatorname{conv}\{O_1,O_2,O_3\},
\qquad
J=|\det(\nabla f_j,\nabla f_k)|>0.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
polytopes such that, with
\[
B_0=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\]
one has \(B_0\subseteq K\) and \(\mathcal T\subseteq K\).  Define
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(B_0\cup\mathcal T)\subseteq K.
\]
For \(G\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), use the exact middle cells
\[
R_G=K\cap
\{f_\ell\leq0\ (\ell\in G),\ f_\ell>0\ (\ell\notin G)\},
\]
and put
\[
d_\ell=|D\cap\{f_\ell\leq0\}|.
\]
Suppose corner \(i\) is admissible.  In the affine coordinates
\[
s=f_j,\qquad t=f_k,
\]
write its two support contacts as
\[
F_i=(-a_i,p_i a_i),\qquad
E_i=(c_i,-q_i c_i),
\qquad
0<p_i<q_i,\quad a_i,c_i>0.
\]
Both contacts lie in \(B_0\), so each is an actual midpoint of one point of \(A_0\) and one point of \(A_2\).  Put
\[
X_i=\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}{p_i a_i+q_i c_i},
\qquad
Y_i=\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}{a_i+c_i}.
\]
Then every actual common core satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
|D|\geq\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}{2J}.
\quad}
\tag{1}
\]
If \(X_i\geq1\) and \(Y_i\geq1\), it also satisfies the localized cross-wing bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
|D\cap R_{jk}|
\geq\frac{X_iY_i-1}{2J}.
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, consider the rational corner-\(1\) data
\[
J=1,\qquad
p_1=\frac3{58},\qquad q_1=\frac{58}{3},
\qquad a_1=\frac{116}{3},\qquad c_1=2
\tag{3}
\]
and the formal alternating family
\[
\begin{aligned}
\zeta_1&=0,&
\zeta_2&=50+\tau,&
\zeta_3&=50-\tau,\\
\zeta_{12}&=50-\tau,&
\zeta_{13}&=50+\tau,&
\zeta_{23}&=1,&
\zeta_{123}&=\frac12,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\zeta_G=|D\cap R_G|\).  This family keeps fixed
\[
|D|=\frac{403}{2},\qquad
(d_1,d_2,d_3)
=\left(\frac{201}{2},\frac{203}{2},\frac{203}{2}\right),
\tag{5}
\]
and has
\[
\Delta_1
=\zeta_2+\zeta_{13}-\zeta_3-\zeta_{12}
=4\tau.
\tag{6}
\]
Exact-cell nonnegativity alone permits \(-200\leq\Delta_1\leq200\).  Requiring the two separate forced wings in addition gives the exact formal interval
\[
-\frac{10786}{55}\leq\Delta_1\leq\frac{10786}{55}.
\tag{7}
\]
Nevertheless, when the two actual contacts in (3) are imposed simultaneously, the convex-geometric attainable set is
\[
\boxed{\quad \{\Delta_1:\text{an actual }D\text{ satisfies (3)--(5)}\}
=\varnothing.\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently every member of the rational equality-side calibration represented by (3)--(5), including every sufficiently small rational alternating transfer, is excluded by common-plane convexity.

## Proof

Accepted fact `bd8c4d0245cddf92` puts \(F_i,E_i\) in \(B_0\) and \(O_i\) in \(\mathcal T\).  Since \(D\) is convex,
\[
H_i:=\operatorname{conv}\{O_i,F_i,E_i\}\subseteq D.
\tag{9}
\]
The \((s,t)\)-coordinate area of this triangle is
\[
\frac12|\det(F_i,E_i)|
=\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}2.
\]
The coordinate map has absolute Jacobian \(J\).  Dividing by \(J\) proves (1).

The segment \([F_i,E_i]\) meets the negative \(s\)-axis and the negative \(t\)-axis at
\[
(-X_i,0),\qquad(0,-Y_i),
\tag{10}
\]
respectively.  Indeed, setting \(t=0\) on that segment gives
\[
s=-\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}{p_i a_i+q_i c_i},
\]
and setting \(s=0\) gives
\[
t=-\frac{a_ic_i(q_i-p_i)}{a_i+c_i}.
\]
It follows from (9) that
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-X_i,0),(0,-Y_i)\}\subseteq D.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(X_i,Y_i\geq1\), this triangle contains the central triangle
\[
\mathcal T
=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1)\}.
\]
Up to the three null cutting lines, its complement of \(\mathcal T\) is contained in
\[
R_{jk}=\{s\leq0,\ t\leq0,\ s+t<-1\}.
\]
Its physical area is \(X_iY_i/(2J)\), whereas accepted fact `b4a2962b4691ff67` gives
\[
|\mathcal T|=\frac1{2J}.
\]
Subtraction proves (2).

For (3),
\[
p_1a_1=2,\qquad q_1c_1=\frac{116}{3},
\qquad
a_1c_1(q_1-p_1)=\frac{13420}{9}.
\tag{12}
\]
Therefore
\[
X_1=Y_1=\frac{110}{3}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equation (1) would require
\[
|D|\geq\frac{6710}{9}>\frac{403}{2},
\tag{14}
\]
already contradicting (5).  More locally, (2) would require
\[
\zeta_{23}
\geq
\frac12\left(\left(\frac{110}{3}\right)^2-1\right)
=\frac{12091}{18}>1,
\tag{15}
\]
contradicting (4).  Both contradictions are invariant under \(\tau\).

For completeness, the four moving cells in (4) are nonnegative exactly when
\[
-50\leq\tau\leq50,
\]
which proves the first formal interval after multiplication by \(4\).  The left forced wing is
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-1,0),F_1\},
\]
and the right forced wing is
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,-1),E_1\}.
\]
Each has area \(1\).  On either ray from the origin to its support contact, the line \(s+t=-1\) is reached at the fraction
\[
\frac{1}{110/3}=\frac3{110}.
\]
Hence each wing has doubleton area \(3/110\) and singleton area \(107/110\).  The four capacity inequalities are
\[
50+\tau\geq\frac{107}{110},\qquad
50-\tau\geq\frac3{110},
\]
\[
50-\tau\geq\frac{107}{110},\qquad
50+\tau\geq\frac3{110}.
\]
Their intersection is
\[
-\frac{5393}{110}\leq\tau\leq\frac{5393}{110},
\]
which proves (7).  Equations (14)--(15) show that simultaneous convexity of the two actual midpoint contacts removes this entire formal interval, proving (8).
