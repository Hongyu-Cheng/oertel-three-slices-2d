## Statement

Assume Alternative 2 of accepted fact 879a3c45b5f50670. Thus
\[
 |A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=r,\qquad |K|=s,\qquad
 t=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9,
\]
\[
 0<r<\frac13,\qquad s>1-r,
\]
and, for distinct \(i,j,\ell\), there are nonconstant affine functions
\[
 f_i+f_j+f_\ell=-1
\]
with pairwise nonparallel linear parts, middle-cap areas
\[
 c_h=|K\cap\{f_h\le0\}|,
\]
and actual low shifts \(a_i,a_\ell\) such that
\[
 c_h+|A_0\cap\{f_h\le a_h\}|+|A_2\cap\{f_h\le-a_h\}|<t
 \quad(h=i,\ell).
\]
After interchanging \(i,\ell\) if necessary, Alternative 2 gives
\[
 |A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a_i\}|=r,
 \qquad
 |A_2\cap\{f_\ell\le-a_\ell\}|=0,
\]
and the original and propagated root tails give
\[
 c_j+r<t,\qquad c_i+r<t.
\]
Then necessarily
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac14\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{7-2r+2s}{9}}+\sqrt r
 \right)^2
 <
 \frac{4+4s-14r}{9}.}
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 r<r_*:=\frac{4601-12\sqrt{8958}}{14521}
 =0.2386364204\ldots .}
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

We first record the planar centroid inequality in the precise form used below. If \(B\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a convex body of area \(b\), with centroid \(g_B\), then every closed halfplane containing \(g_B\) cuts from \(B\) area at least \(4b/9\). This is the planar centroid halfspace inequality. One standard self-contained reduction is the following. Rotate and translate so that the halfplane is \(\{x\ge0\}\) and \(g_B=0\). If \(\varphi(x)\) is the length of the vertical section of \(B\), convexity makes \(\varphi\) concave on its support, while the centroid identity is
\[
 \int x\varphi(x)\,dx=0.
\]
The one-dimensional concavity inequality
\[
 \int_0^\infty\varphi(x)\,dx
 \ge \frac49\int_{-\infty}^\infty\varphi(x)\,dx
\tag{3}
\]
is the elementary planar Grünbaum calculation: first verify it for a polygonal concave \(\varphi\) by integrating \(1\) and \(x\) on each linearity interval and summing the nonnegative downward jumps of its slopes, then pass to a uniform polygonal approximation. Equality is the affine section profile of a triangle whose centroid lies on the cutting line. This proves the stated centroid inequality; reflecting the coordinate gives the opposite orientation.

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(K\). The no-half lower bound \(s>1-r\) gives
\[
 \frac{4s}{9}-(t-r)
 =\frac{2s+7r-2}{9}
 >\frac{5r}{9}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(f_j(g)\le0\), the planar centroid inequality applied to the \(j\)-th middle cap would give
\[
 c_j\ge\frac{4s}{9}>t-r,
\]
contrary to the original root tail. Hence \(f_j(g)>0\). The same argument with the propagated root tail gives \(f_i(g)>0\). The shared affine relation therefore yields
\[
 f_\ell(g)=-1-f_i(g)-f_j(g)<-1,
\]
so the centroid lies in the interior of the remaining middle cap. A second application of the centroid inequality gives
\[
 c_\ell\ge\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]
This step uses the genuine three-line arrangement. At each of the three pairwise crossings of the zero lines, the remaining affine function equals \(-1\); pairwise nonparallelity rules out every parallel or coincident degeneration.

Put
\[
 u_\ell=|A_0\cap\{f_\ell\le a_\ell\}|.
\]
The second actual shift has zero \(A_2\)-charge in Alternative 2. Its strict low-mass inequality and (5) imply
\[
 u_\ell<t-c_\ell\le t-\frac{4s}{9},
 \qquad
 1-u_\ell>1-t+\frac{4s}{9}
 =\frac{7-2r+2s}{9}.
\tag{6}
\]
The right side is positive. Since affine cutting lines have planar area zero, the closure
\[
 W=\overline{A_0\cap\{f_\ell>a_\ell\}}
   =A_0\cap\{f_\ell\ge a_\ell\}
\]
is a convex body of area \(1-u_\ell\).

Compatibility, retained in accepted fact 879a3c45b5f50670, gives
\[
 D:=\frac{W+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{7}
\]
For every \(x\in W\) and \(z\in A_2\), Alternative 2 gives
\[
 f_\ell(x)\ge a_\ell,
 \qquad
 f_\ell(z)\ge-a_\ell,
\]
so \(f_\ell((x+z)/2)\ge0\). The shared relation then gives
\[
 f_i\left(\frac{x+z}{2}\right)
 +f_j\left(\frac{x+z}{2}\right)
 =-1-f_\ell\left(\frac{x+z}{2}\right)
 \le-1.
\]
Thus every point of \(D\) belongs to the \(i\)-th or \(j\)-th middle cap, and hence
\[
 |D|\le c_i+c_j<2(t-r).
\tag{8}
\]
The first actual shift is essential upstream: its full \(A_2\)-charge gives
\[
 c_i+r\le c_i+|A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i\}|+r<t,
\]
which is the second strict inequality used in (8).

Planar Brunn--Minkowski applied to (7), followed by (6), gives
\[
 |D|
 \ge\frac14\left(\sqrt{1-u_\ell}+\sqrt r\right)^2
 >\frac14\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{7-2r+2s}{9}}+\sqrt r
 \right)^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (8) and (9), and using
\[
 2(t-r)=\frac{4+4s-14r}{9},
\]
proves (1).

It remains to extract the ratio consequence. For fixed \(r\), let
\[
 F_r(s)=\frac{4+4s-14r}{9}
 -\frac14\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{7-2r+2s}{9}}+\sqrt r
 \right)^2.
\]
On the Regime A strip one has \((7-2r+2s)/9>r\), and direct differentiation gives
\[
 F_r'(s)
 =\frac49-
 \frac{1+\sqrt{r/((7-2r+2s)/9)}}{18}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(r\le1/5\), then already \(r<r_*\). Suppose \(1/5<r<1/3\). Accepted fact 879a3c45b5f50670 gives \(s<2-4r\). Since (1) says \(F_r(s)>0\), monotonicity implies
\[
 F_r(2-4r)>0.
\]
After multiplying by \(36\), this is
\[
 \left(\sqrt{11-10r}+3\sqrt r\right)^2
 <24(2-5r),
\]
or equivalently
\[
 6\sqrt{r(11-10r)}<37-119r.
\tag{11}
\]
If the right side is nonpositive there is no solution. Otherwise squaring (11) gives
\[
 14521r^2-9202r+1369>0.
\tag{12}
\]
The two roots of the quadratic are
\[
 \frac{4601\pm12\sqrt{8958}}{14521}.
\]
The larger root exceeds \(1/3\), whereas the smaller root is \(r_*\in(1/5,1/3)\). Hence (12), together with \(r<1/3\), forces \(r<r_*\). This proves (2).
