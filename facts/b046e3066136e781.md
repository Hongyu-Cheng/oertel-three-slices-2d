## Proof

Accepted fact `4ec05d1da924da91` gives the support coordinates, cutting lines, cap conventions, midpoint inclusion, crossing equalities, and the equivalence between the desired contact-tail inequality and
\[
b+d\le m+k-t.
\]
Dividing every area by the positive raw value
\(\Lambda=|P|\) changes none of those relations. It gives normalized outer area \(M=1\), while retaining the exact coordinate thresholds \(D=\delta+2\) and \(1\).

We first verify the endpoint formulas. Let \(z\) be affine on an interval of width \(w>0\), with endpoint values \(r,s\). Direct integration gives
\[
\int[z(x)]_+\,dx=
\begin{cases}
0,&r,s\le0,\\
\dfrac w2(r+s),&r,s\ge0,\\
\dfrac{ws^2}{2(s-r)},&r<0<s,\\
\dfrac{wr^2}{2(r-s)},&s<0<r.
\end{cases}
\tag{29}
\]
The crossing cases are the areas of the corresponding triangles. In
\(\mathrm C_+\), \(\Lambda>0\) and \(s-r>0\); in
\(\mathrm C_-\), \(\Lambda>0\) and \(r-s>0\). Thus (1) is exactly the graph of
\[
I=\frac1\Lambda\int[z(x)]_+\,dx
\]
with every denominator sign-certified. The boundary convention in (1) is exhaustive, including zero endpoint values.

For a vertical interval \([L_E(x),U_E(x)]\), its contribution to \(y\le0\) is
\[
[-L_E(x)]_+-[-U_E(x)]_+,
\]
and its contribution to \(x+y\ge0\) is
\[
[U_E(x)+x]_+-[L_E(x)+x]_+.
\]
Using (29) and the definitions of the four endpoint pairs therefore yields
\[
\alpha_E=I_{E1}-I_{E2},\qquad
\beta_E=I_{E3}-I_{E4}.
\tag{30}
\]
Hence (16) and (17) are exactly the two crossing equalities.

The height function \(h_E=U_E-L_E\) is affine with endpoint values
\(h_{E0},h_{E1}\). Conditions (7) imply \(h_E\ge0\) throughout its support, and the strict last inequality in (7) gives positive area. Its area is
\[
|E|=\frac{w_E}{2}(h_{E0}+h_{E1}),
\]
so (8) and (9) are exactly
\[
|P|=\Lambda,\qquad |Q|=\Lambda m,\qquad |R|=\Lambda k.
\]

If \(\ell<c<\ell+w=u\), affine interpolation gives
\[
h(c)=\frac{(u-c)h_0+(c-\ell)h_1}{w}.
\]
Thus
\[
\int_c^u h(x)\,dx
=\frac{u-c}{2}\bigl(h(c)+h_1\bigr),
\]
and multiplication by the positive denominator \(w\) gives exactly (4). If \(c=\ell\), the tail is the full body and is assigned to (3). If \(c=u\), it is zero and is assigned to (5). Thus (3) through (5) are disjoint, exhaustive, and exact on support boundaries. The omitted \(Q\)-case \(\mathrm Z\) would force \(b=0\), contrary to (20). This also proves exactness at the boundary \(D=u_Q\). The cases \(1=\ell_R\) and \(1=u_R\) are included respectively in \(\mathrm F\) and \(\mathrm Z\).

Conditions (21), (22), and (23), together with (30), are precisely
\[
\alpha_R+m\ge t,\qquad
\beta_R+m\ge t,\qquad
k-d+m\ge t.
\]

Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, after setting \(M=1\), says that the residual strip is
\[
0<m<\rho,\qquad L(m)<k<U(m),
\qquad
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}.
\]
With \(q>0\) and \(m=q^2\),
\[
m<\rho
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
5q^2+2q-5<0.
\]
The lower breakpoint is \(q=3/5\), and the upper breakpoint is
\(2q^2=1\). Assigning equality to (12) and (13), respectively, shows that (11) through (14) are a disjoint exhaustive rational-polynomial form of that strip.

We next verify midpoint inclusion. Conditions (7) make each body compact and convex, and
\[
E=\operatorname{conv}
\{(x_{E0},\lambda_{E0}),
  (x_{E0},\upsilon_{E0}),
  (x_{E1},\lambda_{E1}),
  (x_{E1},\upsilon_{E1})\}.
\tag{31}
\]
Zero endpoint height merely makes two displayed vertices coincide. It causes no loss.

For a chosen vertex pair, its midpoint is
\[
\left(\frac{X_{ij}}2,\frac{Y_{iajc}}2\right).
\]
Condition (24) places its first coordinate in the support of \(R\). Multiplying
\[
L_R(X_{ij}/2)\le Y_{iajc}/2
\le U_R(X_{ij}/2)
\]
by the positive \(2w_R\) gives exactly (25) and (26). Thus all sixteen vertex midpoints lie in \(R\). By (31), convexity, and bilinearity of Minkowski addition,
\[
\frac{P+Q}{2}
=
\operatorname{conv}
\left\{
\frac{p+q}{2}:
p\text{ is a displayed vertex of }P,\
q\text{ is a displayed vertex of }Q
\right\},
\]
so (24) through (26) are equivalent to
\[
(P+Q)/2\subseteq R.
\]
This remains true with degenerate vertical sides.

Define
\[
g_E(x)=[-L_E(x)]_+-[-U_E(x)]_+
+[U_E(x)+x]_+-[L_E(x)+x]_+.
\]
Equation (30) gives
\[
\frac1\Lambda\int g_E
=I_{E1}-I_{E2}+I_{E3}-I_{E4}.
\]
Therefore (27) is exactly the normalized form of
\[
\int g_P+
\int\!\left(g_Q-\mathbf1_{\{x\ge D\}}h_Q\right)+
\int\!\left(g_R-\mathbf1_{\{x\ge1\}}h_R\right).
\]
For \(x\ge D>0\) or \(x\ge1\),
\[
g_E(x)-h_E(x)
=
|[L_E(x),U_E(x)]\cap[-x,0]|\ge0.
\]
Away from a subtracted tail, \(g_E\ge0\). Hence \(\Omega\ge0\) is exact, not an additional restriction. Equations (16) and (17) give
\[
\Omega=2t-b-d,
\]
which is the second equality in (28).

Using (10),
\[
(m+k-t)-(b+d)
=
\Omega-\frac{2-m-k}{3}.
\tag{32}
\]
Consequently,
\[
3\Omega<2-m-k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b+d>m+k-t.
\]
The residual upper bounds imply \(k<2-m\), so the right-hand side of the first inequality is strictly positive.

We now prove both directions.

Suppose first that a remaining mixed-sign geometric violation is given. Set \(\Lambda=|P|\), divide all areas and cap integrals by \(\Lambda\), take \(q=\sqrt m>0\), and record the endpoint data. Conditions (6) through (10) follow from the geometry. Exactly one of (12) through (14) holds. Each of the twelve endpoint pairs belongs to exactly one case in (1), producing a unique label vector \(\sigma\). Mixed-sign means that at least one label is \(\mathrm C_+\) or \(\mathrm C_-\). The condition \(b>0\) places the \(Q\)-threshold in exactly one of \(\mathrm F,\mathrm I\), and the \(R\)-threshold lies in exactly one of \(\mathrm F,\mathrm I,\mathrm Z\).

The involution
\[
\mathscr S(x,y)=(x,-x-y)
\]
preserves \(x\), all areas, both vertical tails, midpoint inclusion, and the violation. It exchanges \(\alpha_E\) with \(\beta_E\). On endpoint data it acts by
\[
\lambda'_{Ei}=-x_{Ei}-\upsilon_{Ei},\qquad
\upsilon'_{Ei}=-x_{Ei}-\lambda_{Ei},
\]
and therefore permutes the four endpoint pairs by
\[
1\leftrightarrow3,\qquad2\leftrightarrow4.
\]
Thus it sends \(\sigma\) to \(\pi\sigma\). If necessary apply \(\mathscr S\) once so that (2) holds. All displayed system constraints are then satisfied, including (28) by (32).

Conversely, suppose
\(\mathcal S_{\eta,\tau_Q,\tau_R,\sigma}\) is feasible. Reconstruct \(L_E,U_E\) by affine interpolation. Conditions (6) through (10) give compact positive-area two-dimensional convex polytopes with normalized areas \(1,m,k\). Conditions (24) through (26) give midpoint inclusion. Equations (1) and (15), whose only divided quantities have certified positive denominators, give the exact cap integrals (30). Equations (16) through (23) give both crossings, \(b>0\), and all three tail conditions. Equations (11) through (14) give the exact strict residual strip. At least one label is a strict crossing label, so the reconstruction is mixed-sign. Finally, (27), (28), and (32) give
\[
b+d>m+k-t.
\]

It remains only to check that no feasible reconstruction lies in the discarded fact-covered class. If it lies in \(\mathcal O\), then its affine nonnegative heights satisfy all hypotheses of accepted fact `25960e1c7ab04d71`, with raw areas
\[
M=\Lambda,\qquad m_{\rm raw}=\Lambda m,\qquad
k_{\rm raw}=\Lambda k,\qquad t_{\rm raw}=\Lambda t.
\]
That accepted fact says these hypotheses are inconsistent. If the reconstruction lies in \(\mathscr S(\mathcal O)\), applying \(\mathscr S\) produces a datum in \(\mathcal O\) while preserving every hypothesis, again contradicting accepted fact `25960e1c7ab04d71`. Hence every feasible system reconstructs a violation in the requested remaining class.

Finally, the count is exhaustive and symmetry-reduced. There are
\(4^{12}-2^{12}\) mixed labelings. The involution fixes
\(4^6-2^6\) of them. Burnside's formula gives \(8388576\) sign orbits, and the lexicographic rule (2) selects exactly one representative from each. Multiplication by the eighteen disjoint residual-tail choices gives the stated \(150994368\) systems.

Every displayed equality or inequality is polynomial with rational coefficients. The only quantities corresponding to denominators are \(\Lambda\), the widths \(w_E\), the strict crossing differences \(s-r\) or \(r-s\), and the interpolation width \(w_R\); each is explicitly constrained to be positive.
