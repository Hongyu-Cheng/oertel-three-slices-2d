## Statement

Fix
\[
r\in\mathbb Q,\qquad \frac13<r\le\frac9{25},
\]
and put
\[
p=\frac{1+r}{2},\qquad q=\frac{2r}{1+r}.
\]
Define
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\]
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(p,0),(0,q)\},
\qquad
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
B=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),
\left(\frac{3+r}{4},0\right),
\left(\frac{1+r}{4},\frac12\right),
\left(0,\frac{1+3r}{2(1+r)}\right)
\right\}.
\]
Its centroid is
\[
g_B=
\left(
\frac{5r^2+14r+13}{48(1+r)},
\frac{13r^2+14r+5}{24(1+r)^2}
\right).
\]
For
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2),
\]
one has
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{10(1+r)}}3+\frac{r-17}{18}
>
\frac{1+r}{6}
=
\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

At the rational endpoint \(r=9/25\),
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),\left(\frac{17}{25},0\right),\left(0,\frac9{17}\right)
\right\},
\]
\[
B=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(0,0),\left(\frac{21}{25},0\right),
\left(\frac{17}{50},\frac12\right),
\left(0,\frac{13}{17}\right)
\right\},
\qquad
g_B=\left(\frac{73}{255},\frac{229}{867}\right),
\]
and
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
\frac{2\sqrt{85}}{15}-\frac{208}{225}
>
\frac{17}{75}
=
\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Since
\[
pq=r,\qquad
p-q=\frac{(1-r)^2}{2(1+r)}>0,
\]
the two outer triangles are nonhomothetic. Because \(p>q\), the convex hull of their nine vertex sums is
\[
A_0+A_2
=
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1+p,0),(p,1),(0,1+q)\},
\]
which gives the displayed vertex list for \(B\).

Shoelace calculations give
\[
M=|A_0|=\frac12,\qquad
m=|A_2|=\frac r2,\qquad
b=|B|=\frac{1+r}{4}.
\]
Consequently
\[
b=\frac{M+m}{2}<M+m,
\qquad
T=M+m+b=\frac{3(1+r)}4.
\]
Moreover,
\[
M<\frac T2
\iff r>\frac13,
\]
while \(m<T/2\) and \(b<T/2\) are immediate. Thus every member lies exactly on the lower boundary of the omitted upper-\(b\) regime and is strictly balanced.

The polygon-centroid formula applied to the displayed vertices of \(B\) gives the stated \(g_B\). The outer centroids satisfy
\[
\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}
=
\left(
\frac{3+r}{12},
\frac{1+3r}{6(1+r)}
\right),
\]
and hence
\[
g_B-\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}
=
\left(
\frac{(1-r)^2}{48(1+r)},
\frac{(1-r)^2}{24(1+r)^2}
\right)\ne(0,0).
\]
Thus this is genuinely a test of the middle body’s own centroid.

For an arbitrary tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\), let \(c\) be its middle-cap area and let \(x,y\) be its \(A_0,A_2\) lower-cap areas. The exact cap relaxation in accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1` applies uniformly to every direction and slope:
\[
\frac{1+r}{9}\le c\le\frac{5(1+r)}{36},
\qquad
0\le x\le\frac12,
\qquad
0\le y\le\frac r2,
\]
together with
\[
(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)^2\le4c
\quad\text{when }x,y>0,
\]
and
\[
\left(\sqrt{\frac12-x}+\sqrt{\frac r2-y}\right)^2
\le1+r-4c
\quad\text{when }x<\frac12,\ y<\frac r2.
\]
That accepted fact proves that the exact infimum allowed by these constraints is
\[
\Phi\left(\frac12,\frac r2,\frac{1+r}{4}\right).
\]

Here
\[
\frac{20b}{9}=\frac{5(1+r)}9>\frac12=M,
\]
and
\[
\frac{20b}{9}
<
\left(\sqrt M+\sqrt m\right)^2
\iff
1+r<18\sqrt r,
\]
which holds for \(r>1/3\). Therefore the middle branch of the accepted formula gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
c+x+y
&\ge
\frac{4b}{9}+m-
\left(\sqrt{\frac{20b}{9}}-\sqrt M\right)^2\\
&=
\frac{\sqrt{10(1+r)}}3+\frac{r-17}{18}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is simultaneous control of all nonhorizontal directions and all slopes, including empty and saturated outer caps. The two slice-parallel halfspaces have exact masses
\[
M+b=\frac{3+r}{4},
\qquad
b+m=\frac{1+3r}{4},
\]
both larger than \(2T/9\).

Finally,
\[
9\left[
\frac{\sqrt{10(1+r)}}3+\frac{r-17}{18}
-\frac{1+r}{6}
\right]
=
3\sqrt{10(1+r)}-(10+r).
\]
Both sides of the desired comparison are positive, and
\[
90(1+r)-(10+r)^2=-10+70r-r^2.
\]
This expression is increasing on the stated interval and at \(r=1/3\) equals \(119/9>0\). Hence the cap bound is strictly larger than
\[
\frac{1+r}{6}=\frac{2T}{9}.
\]

Finally,
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)\bigr)
\]
is a compact polytope whose sections at integer heights \(0,1,2\) are exactly \(A_0,B,A_2\), so the family is compatible with the original three-slice problem.
