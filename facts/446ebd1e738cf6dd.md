## Statement

Let \(S\) arise from any compatible convex-polytopal triple \((A_0,A_1,A_2)\) in the locked problem. For every \(c\in A_1\), define
\[
\Phi(c)=\left|A_1\cap(2c-A_1)\right|
+2\left|A_2\cap(2c-A_0)\right|.
\]
Then
\[
h_S((1,c))\ge \frac{\Phi(c)}2.
\]
Consequently, if \(\Phi(c)\ge 4T/9\), then \((1,c)\in S\) and
\[
h_S((1,c))\ge \frac{2T}{9}.
\]

## Proof

Fix \(c\in A_1\), put \(y=(1,c)\), and let \(R(w)=2c-w\). Define
\[
B_1=A_1\cap R(A_1),\qquad
B_2=A_2\cap R(A_0),\qquad
B_0=R(B_2).
\]
These sets are compact, hence Borel measurable. Moreover,
\[
B_1\subseteq A_1,\qquad
B_2\subseteq A_2,\qquad
B_0\subseteq R(R(A_0))=A_0,
\]
where the last inclusion follows from \(B_2\subseteq R(A_0)\). Also,
\[
R(B_1)=R(A_1)\cap A_1=B_1,\qquad
R(B_0)=B_2,
\]
and planar area is preserved by \(R\), so \(|B_0|=|B_2|\).

Set
\[
B=(\{0\}\times B_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B_1)
  \cup(\{2\}\times B_2)\subseteq S
\]
and let \(\nu(E)=\mathcal H_2(B\cap E)\). This is a finite Borel measure. Central inversion about \(y\),
\[
J(x,w)=2y-(x,w)=(2-x,R(w)),
\]
exchanges the outer components of \(B\) and preserves its middle component. Thus \(J(B)=B\), and the isometry invariance of \(\mathcal H_2\) gives \(\nu(J(E))=\nu(E)\) for every Borel set \(E\). Additivity across the three disjoint slices gives
\[
\nu(\mathbb R^3)
=|B_0|+|B_1|+|B_2|
=|B_1|+2|B_2|
=\Phi(c).
\]

Let \(K\) be any proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(y\). For some nonzero linear functional \(\ell\),
\[
K=\{p:\ell(p-y)\le0\}.
\]
Since \(J(p)-y=-(p-y)\),
\[
J(K)=\{p:\ell(p-y)\ge0\},
\]
so \(K\cup J(K)=\mathbb R^3\) and \(K\cap J(K)=\partial K\). By \(J\)-invariance and finite-measure inclusion-exclusion,
\[
2\nu(K)
=\nu(K)+\nu(J(K))
=\nu(\mathbb R^3)+\nu(\partial K)
=\Phi(c)+\nu(\partial K)
\ge\Phi(c).
\]
This identity makes no zero-boundary-mass assumption. In particular, when \(\partial K=\{x=1\}\), it may contain the entire middle component \(\{1\}\times B_1\).

Because \(B\subseteq S\),
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap K)\ge\nu(K)\ge\frac{\Phi(c)}2.
\]
For an arbitrary proper closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(y\), accepted fact `400272f6de6c67bb` supplies such a boundary-through-\(y\) halfspace \(K\subseteq H\). Hence
\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge\mathcal H_2(S\cap K)
\ge\frac{\Phi(c)}2.
\]
If the whole space is admitted as a degenerate halfspace, it also satisfies the bound: \(|B_1|\le a_1\), while \(|B_2|\le a_0\) and \(|B_2|\le a_2\), so \(\Phi(c)\le T\). Taking the infimum over all closed halfspaces containing \(y\) proves the lemma. The stated consequence follows immediately from \(\Phi(c)\ge4T/9\).
