## Statement

Normalize \(m=1\).  Let
\[
x_1=a,\qquad x_2=b,\qquad x_3=c,\qquad q=q_{123},
\]
where
\[
a,b,c,q>0,\qquad a+b+c+q=1,\qquad a>\frac59.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the exact-cell and null-boundary convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, and retain the strict no-half-slice necessary conditions supplied by accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7`.  For every such ratio tuple there are nonnegative scalar cell masses \(p_J,q_H,r_G\) satisfying all of the following:

1. the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly \(\{123,12,13,23\}\), with \(q_{23}=a,q_{13}=b,q_{12}=c,q_{123}=q\);
2. \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\);
3. the three crossings and both canonical tails hold, \(W=0\), \(M\geq1\), and
\[
M<1+k,\qquad k<M+1,\qquad 1<M+k;
\]
4. all \(19\) conditional Brunn--Minkowski rows of accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3` hold, and they may all be chosen active;
5. the overlap inequality of accepted fact `2bb72698f0248a65` holds.

If the data in (1) are rational, all scalar cell masses can be chosen rational.  Multiplying every mass by an arbitrary positive rational number gives the same statement at arbitrary rational scale \(m>0\).

## Proof

We first give a family covering the whole domain (1).  Put
\[
\delta=1+\frac94(1-a).
\tag{2}
\]
The dominant inequality is exactly what places \(\delta\) strictly between the Scott and no-half thresholds:
\[
\delta-\frac92(1-a)
=1-\frac94(1-a)
=\frac{9a-5}{4}>0,
\qquad
\delta<2.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(0<\zeta<1/2\).  Choose
\[
0<\rho<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\min\{a,b,c\}.
\tag{4}
\]
All these parameters may be rational when (1) is rational.  Let \(M\) be a positive parameter and define
\[
k=M+1-\delta,\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+1+k)}9.
\tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
u_1=\varepsilon a,\qquad
u_2=\varepsilon b,\qquad
u_3=\varepsilon c.
\tag{6}
\]
Define the \(P\)-cell masses by
\[
p_{12}=p_{13}=p_{23}=\rho,\qquad
p_i=u_i-2\rho,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
p_0=M-(u_1+u_2+u_3)+3\rho,
\tag{8}
\]
with every other \(P\)-cell zero.  Equations (4), (7), and (8) make all seven non-forced \(P\)-cells positive for sufficiently large \(M\), their total is \(M\), and their marginals are exactly the values in (6).

Under the all-double support,
\[
v_i=1-x_i,
\qquad (x_1,x_2,x_3)=(a,b,c).
\]
Define
\[
c_i=h-u_i-v_i
=h-1+(1-\varepsilon)x_i,
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
E=c_1+c_2+c_3-k.
\tag{10}
\]
As \(M\) tends to infinity with all ratio parameters fixed,
\[
h=\frac49M+O(1),\qquad
c_i=\frac49M+O(1),\qquad
E=\frac13M+O(1).
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(E>0\) for sufficiently large \(M\).  Define the \(R\)-cell masses by
\[
r_{123}=\zeta E,\qquad
r_{12}=r_{13}=r_{23}=\frac{1-2\zeta}{3}E,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
r_i=c_i-\frac{2-\zeta}{3}E.
\tag{13}
\]
The leading coefficient of every \(r_i\) in \(M\) is
\[
\frac49-\frac{2-\zeta}{9}
=\frac{2+\zeta}{9}>0.
\]
Hence all seven \(R\)-cells in (12)-(13) are positive for sufficiently large \(M\).  Their marginals are \(c_i\), and their total is \(k\): indeed,
\[
r_i+r_{ij}+r_{ik}+r_{123}=c_i,
\]
\[
\sum_i r_i
=k-(1-\zeta)E,
\]
\[
\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}+r_{123}
=(1-\zeta)E.
\tag{14}
\]

The crossings hold by (9).  The left tails are strict because
\[
c_i+1-h
=1-u_i-v_i
=(1-\varepsilon)x_i>0.
\tag{15}
\]
The right tails are also strict for sufficiently large \(M\), since
\[
c_i+M-h=M-u_i-v_i>0.
\tag{16}
\]
All finite-interior marginals are then proper.  In particular,
\[
0<u_i<M,\qquad 0<v_i<1,\qquad 0<c_i<k.
\]

Summing the crossings gives
\[
(u_1+u_2+u_3)+(2+q)+(k+E)
=3h
=\frac{2(M+1+k)}3.
\]
Therefore
\[
(u_1+u_2+u_3)+1+q+E
=\frac{2M-1-k}{3}.
\tag{17}
\]
By the exact all-double identity in accepted fact `91cb5bfc71da49ac`, (17) is \(W=0\).

The three no-half-slice inequalities follow directly from (2), (3), and (5).  Namely,
\[
M<1+k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta<2,
\]
\[
k<M+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta>0,
\]
and \(1<M+k\) for sufficiently large \(M\).  We also have \(M\geq1\).  The independent Scott restriction of accepted fact `aee01fb887f2b3bd` holds strictly:
\[
M+1-k
=\delta
>\frac92(1-a)
=\frac92\min_i(1-x_i).
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to audit all midpoint inequalities.  For nonnegative \(X,Y,Z\),
\[
X+Y\leq2Z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\sqrt X+\sqrt Y)^2
\leq2(X+Y)
\leq4Z.
\tag{19}
\]
For the six row types \(\mathrm F0,\mathrm F1,\ldots,\mathrm F5\) in accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3`, substitute (5)-(13) into
\[
2Z-(X+Y).
\]
Its coefficient of \(M\) is respectively
\[
1,\qquad
\frac19,\qquad
\frac89,\qquad
\frac{2(3-\zeta)}9,\qquad
\frac{2(1+\zeta)}9,\qquad
\frac{2(1-2\zeta)}9.
\tag{20}
\]
Every number in (20) is positive.  There are only \(19\) instances, and every non-leading term is a fixed real number depending on (1), \(\varepsilon,\rho,\zeta\).  Hence a sufficiently large \(M\) makes \(X+Y\leq2Z\) hold simultaneously in every instance.  Equations (7)-(8) and (1) make both sources of all \(19\) rows positive, so none is omitted.  Equation (19) then proves the complete active \(19\)-row system exactly.

For the overlap inequality, put
\[
S_R=r_1+r_2+r_3.
\]
Equations (10), (12)-(14) give
\[
k-r_{123}+S_R=2k-E.
\tag{21}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\frac14\sum_{i=1}^3(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{x_i})^2
\leq
\frac12\left(3p_0+a+b+c\right)
=\frac12\left(3p_0+1-q\right).
\tag{22}
\]
The coefficient of \(M\) in the right side of (21) minus the last expression in (22) is
\[
2-\frac13-\frac32=\frac16>0.
\]
Increasing \(M\) once more if necessary proves the accepted overlap inequality.  All conditions used to choose \(M\) are strict affine inequalities with rational coefficients when the parameters are rational.  Their finitely many roots are rational, so a rational \(M\) may be chosen.  This completes the scalar family for every ratio in (1).

The family also calibrates the possible aggregate bounds.  Letting \(a\downarrow5/9\) through rational values shows that no positive dominant-excess gap is forced.  Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) shows that no positive lower bound on \(u_1\) is forced.  Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\) after choosing \(M\) shows that no positive lower bound on \(r_{123}\) is forced.  The parameter \(M\), and hence \(k=M+1-\delta\), is unbounded.

There is likewise no positive lower bound on \(d_R=\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}\) under the conditional-source convention of accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3`.  Set \(\rho=0\), so \(p_{ij}=0\), \(p_i=u_i>0\), and the \(\mathrm F4,\mathrm F5\) rows are not imposed.  Given any \(0<\tau<E\), put
\[
\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}=\tau,\qquad
r_{12}=r_{13}=r_{23}=\frac{\tau}{3},\qquad
r_{123}=\frac{E-\tau}{2},
\]
\[
r_i=c_i-\frac{2\tau}{3}-\frac{E-\tau}{2}.
\tag{23}
\]
For sufficiently large \(M\), all singleton masses in (23) are positive.  The \(\mathrm F0,\mathrm F1,\mathrm F2,\mathrm F3\) and overlap arguments above remain valid, while every \(\mathrm F4,\mathrm F5\) source has zero \(P\)-area and is correctly omitted.  Letting \(\tau\downarrow0\) gives \(d_R\downarrow0\).

The dominant excess is

The marginals and crossing level are
