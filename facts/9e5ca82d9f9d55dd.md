## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact two-dimensional convex polytopes in the canonical finite-boundary setting. Put
\[
M=|A_0|\ge m=|A_2|>0,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad t=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be the canonical nonconstant affine rows, with pairwise nonparallel linear parts and
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
and put
\[
H_i=\{f_i\le0\},\qquad c_i=|K\cap H_i|.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose rows \(2\) and \(3\) are empty-full lower crossings in the sense of accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`. Thus
\[
u_2=u_3=0,\qquad v_2=v_3=m,\qquad
c_2=c_3=t-m.
\tag{3}
\]
Write
\[
C=c_1,
\]
and assume the canonical contact-tail condition
\[
C+m\ge t.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\qquad
M-(m+k)\ge\frac m2+2(t-C).
\qquad}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, for any contact-side endpoint cap area \(v_1\ge0\),
\[
C+v_1<t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M-(m+k)
\ge
\frac m2+2v_1+2(t-C-v_1)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, under the residual no-half inequality
\[
M<m+k
\tag{7}
\]
from accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, one necessarily has
\[
C+v_1\ge t.
\tag{8}
\]

The geometric ingredient used in (5) is the following trimmed three-cap lemma. Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body, let \(G_1,G_2,G_3\) be proper closed halfplanes satisfying
\[
G_1\cup G_2\cup G_3=\mathbb R^2,
\]
and put
\[
q=|Q|,\qquad d_i=|Q\cap G_i|.
\]
If for some \(p\ge0\),
\[
d_2\le p,\qquad d_3\le p,\qquad d_1\ge p,
\tag{9}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\qquad 4q\le4d_1+5p.\qquad}
\tag{10}
\]
This includes \(p=0\) and every support-contact configuration.

## Proof

We first prove the trimmed three-cap lemma. An invertible affine change of coordinates multiplies every area by the same positive factor, so we may write
\[
G_1=\{x\le1\}.
\]
Put \(d_1=D\).

Assume first that \(p>0\). For \(s\le1\), define
\[
F(s)=|Q\cap\{s\le x\le1\}|.
\tag{11}
\]
The function \(F\) is continuous. Indeed, if \(s_n\to s\), the indicators of the slabs in (11) converge pointwise away from the line \(x=s\), and that line has planar area zero; dominated convergence applies on the compact body \(Q\). This argument is unchanged when \(x=s\) or \(x=1\) contains a positive-length support face.

One has
\[
F(1)=0.
\]
If \(s\) is at or to the left of the minimum \(x\)-support of \(Q\), then
\[
F(s)=D.
\]
Condition (9) gives \(0<p\le D\), so continuity supplies \(s_0\le1\) with
\[
F(s_0)=p.
\tag{12}
\]
Set
\[
Q_p=Q\cap\{x\ge s_0\}.
\]
This is convex. Equation (12), with cutting lines ignored only as planar null sets, gives
\[
|Q_p\cap G_1|=p.
\tag{13}
\]
The removed portion \(Q\setminus Q_p\) lies in \(G_1\), and the part of the \(G_1\)-cap that remains has area \(p\). Therefore
\[
|Q_p|=q-(D-p)=q-D+p.
\tag{14}
\]
Also,
\[
|Q_p\cap G_2|\le d_2\le p,\qquad
|Q_p\cap G_3|\le d_3\le p.
\tag{15}
\]
Since (13) has positive area, \(Q_p\) is two-dimensional. Let \(g_p\) be its centroid. The three closed halfplanes cover the plane, so \(g_p\in G_i\) for some \(i\). Accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` records the planar Grünbaum centroid inequality: every closed halfplane containing the centroid of a planar convex body cuts area at least \(4/9\) of the body's area. If \(g_p\) is in the interior of \(G_i\), translating the boundary of \(G_i\) parallel toward \(g_p\) gives a centroidal halfplane contained in \(G_i\), so the same lower bound applies to \(G_i\) itself. Equations (13) and (15) consequently give
\[
\frac49|Q_p|\le p.
\tag{16}
\]
Substituting (14) into (16) yields
\[
4(q-D+p)\le9p,
\]
which rearranges to (10).

Now let \(p=0\). Put
\[
Q_0=Q\cap\{x\ge1\}.
\]
The set \(Q_0\cap G_1\) lies on the line \(x=1\), so it has area zero. Conditions (9) give
\[
|Q_0\cap G_2|\le d_2=0,\qquad
|Q_0\cap G_3|\le d_3=0.
\]
Since \(G_1,G_2,G_3\) cover the plane, these three null intersections cover \(Q_0\), and hence
\[
|Q_0|=0.
\]
Thus \(Q\setminus G_1\) has area zero and \(q=D\). Equation (10) follows as equality on its \(4q\le4D\) part. This proves the trimmed lemma, including literal emptiness, point contact, positive-length support faces, and the \(p=0\) boundary.

We apply the lemma to \(Q=K\) and \(G_i=H_i\). The affine identity in (2) implies
\[
H_1\cup H_2\cup H_3=\mathbb R^2:
\]
otherwise a point outside all three closed halfplanes would satisfy \(f_i>0\) for all \(i\), contradicting \(f_1+f_2+f_3=-1\). Put
\[
\pi=t-m.
\tag{17}
\]
By (3),
\[
c_2=c_3=\pi.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular \(\pi\ge0\). The weak contact-tail condition (4), not a strict strengthening, gives
\[
C\ge t-m=\pi.
\tag{19}
\]
The trimmed lemma with \(p=\pi\), including \(\pi=0\), now gives
\[
4k\le4C+5\pi.
\tag{20}
\]
No lower-crossing derivative or support-face convention is used beyond the exact empty-full identities in (3).

It remains to audit the scalar ledger. Put
\[
\sigma=m+k-M.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (1) and (17) give
\[
9\pi=9(t-m)=2M-7m+2k.
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently,
\[
5m+9\pi+2\sigma
=5m+(2M-7m+2k)+2(m+k-M)
=4k.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (20) and (23), then using \(\pi=t-m\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
5m+9\pi+2\sigma
&\le4C+5\pi,\\
2\sigma
&\le4C-5m-4\pi\\
&=-m-4(t-C).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(M-(m+k)=-\sigma\), division by \(2\) gives exactly (5).

If \(C+v_1<t\), put
\[
\eta=t-C-v_1>0.
\]
Then \(t-C=v_1+\eta\), and (5) becomes
\[
M-(m+k)
\ge\frac m2+2v_1+2\eta>0,
\]
proving (6). Finally, accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` supplies the strict residual inequality (7), which contradicts (6). Therefore (8) holds throughout the residual double-empty-full support-contact pattern.
