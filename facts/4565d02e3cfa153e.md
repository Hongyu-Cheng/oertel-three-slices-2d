## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex
bodies, with planar areas
\[
M=|A_0|>m=|A_2|>0.
\]
For \(p\in A_0\), define its planar halfspace depth in \(A_0\) by
\[
d_{A_0}(p)=
\inf\left\{
|A_0\cap H|:
H\subset\mathbb R^2\text{ is a closed halfplane and }p\in H
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Suppose that there is \(z\in\mathbb R^2\) such that, for every
\(a\in A_2\),
\[
z-a\in A_0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d_{A_0}(z-a)>m.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix a nonzero linear form
\(\lambda:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), and define
\[
F_\lambda(s)=
\left|A_0\cap\{\lambda\le s\}\right|,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
q(\lambda)=
\inf\left\{
s\in\mathbb R:F_\lambda(s)\ge m
\right\},
\qquad
\beta(\lambda)=\max_{a\in A_2}\lambda(a).
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(F_\lambda\) is continuous, the infimum defining \(q(\lambda)\)
is attained, and
\[
F_\lambda(q(\lambda))=m.
\tag{5}
\]
The maximum defining \(\beta(\lambda)\) is also attained, including
when its maximizers form a positive-length support face.  Moreover,
\[
q(\lambda)+\beta(\lambda)<\lambda(z).
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently, if \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) are nonzero linear
forms satisfying
\[
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=0,
\]
then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3
\bigl(q(\lambda_i)+\beta(\lambda_i)\bigr)<0.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, such a triple cannot satisfy the necessary
projection-quantile inequality
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3
\bigl(q(\lambda_i)+\beta(\lambda_i)\bigr)>2
\tag{8}
\]
from accepted fact `19703e1e768865b1`.

## Proof

Fix a nonzero linear form \(\lambda\).  We first prove all assertions
about its cap-area function.  Let
\[
\alpha=\min_{x\in A_0}\lambda(x),
\qquad
\delta=\max_{x\in A_0}\lambda(x).
\tag{9}
\]
Both extrema are attained because \(A_0\) is compact.  Since \(A_0\)
has nonempty interior and \(\lambda\ne0\), one has
\(\alpha<\delta\).

For any real sequence \(s_n\to s\), the indicators
\[
\mathbf 1_{A_0\cap\{\lambda\le s_n\}}(x)
\]
converge to
\(\mathbf 1_{A_0\cap\{\lambda\le s\}}(x)\) for every
\(x\notin\{\lambda=s\}\).  The exceptional affine line
\(\{\lambda=s\}\) has planar measure zero, while all the indicators are
bounded by the integrable function \(\mathbf 1_{A_0}\).  Dominated
convergence therefore gives
\[
F_\lambda(s_n)\longrightarrow F_\lambda(s).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(F_\lambda\) is continuous.  This argument includes thresholds
whose level line supports \(A_0\), passes through a nonsmooth boundary
point, or contains a positive-length face.

The lower support cap
\[
A_0\cap\{\lambda\le\alpha\}
\]
is contained in the support line \(\{\lambda=\alpha\}\), so it has
planar area zero.  The upper cap at \(\delta\) is all of \(A_0\).
Consequently,
\[
F_\lambda(\alpha)=0<m<M=F_\lambda(\delta).
\tag{11}
\]
The set
\[
E_\lambda=\{s\in\mathbb R:F_\lambda(s)\ge m\}
\]
is nonempty, closed by (10), and bounded below.  Hence its infimum
\(q(\lambda)\) belongs to \(E_\lambda\), so the infimum is attained and
\(F_\lambda(q(\lambda))\ge m\).  Also
\(q(\lambda)>\alpha\) by (11).  If
\(F_\lambda(q(\lambda))>m\), continuity would give some
\(s<q(\lambda)\), still larger than \(\alpha\), with
\(F_\lambda(s)>m\), contradicting the definition of the infimum.
This proves (5).  The same continuity argument at \(\delta\), using
\(F_\lambda(\delta)=M>m\), also gives \(q(\lambda)<\delta\), so both
support thresholds are covered without a hidden endpoint case.

Compactness of \(A_2\) implies that \(\lambda\) attains its maximum
there.  Choose any
\[
a_\lambda\in A_2
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\lambda(a_\lambda)=\beta(\lambda).
\tag{12}
\]
No uniqueness or strict convexity is needed: if the exposed support set
is a segment, any point of that segment satisfies (12).  Put
\[
p_\lambda=z-a_\lambda.
\tag{13}
\]
By (2), \(p_\lambda\in A_0\) and
\(d_{A_0}(p_\lambda)>m\).

For completeness, (2) automatically rules out every boundary-support
case for \(p_\lambda\).  Indeed, if \(p\in\partial A_0\), a supporting
line through \(p\) has \(A_0\) in one of its closed sides.  The opposite
closed halfplane also contains \(p\), while its intersection with
\(A_0\) is contained in that line and hence has planar area zero.
Thus
\[
p\in\partial A_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{A_0}(p)=0.
\tag{14}
\]
Since \(m>0\), (2) places every \(z-a\), and in particular
\(p_\lambda\), in \(\operatorname{int}A_0\).

The closed halfplane
\[
H_\lambda=
\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\lambda(x)\le\lambda(p_\lambda)\}
\]
contains \(p_\lambda\).  From the definition (1) and (2),
\[
F_\lambda(\lambda(p_\lambda))
=|A_0\cap H_\lambda|
\ge d_{A_0}(p_\lambda)>m.
\tag{15}
\]
On the other hand, (5) gives
\(F_\lambda(q(\lambda))=m\).  Since \(F_\lambda\) is nondecreasing,
(15) forces
\[
q(\lambda)<\lambda(p_\lambda).
\tag{16}
\]
Using (12)-(13), this becomes
\[
q(\lambda)
<\lambda(z-a_\lambda)
=\lambda(z)-\beta(\lambda),
\]
which is exactly (6).  Notice that strictness comes from the strict
depth hypothesis in (2); it is unaffected by a support face of \(A_2\)
or a flat face of \(A_0\).

Apply (6) to each of
\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\).  Their zero-sum identity gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3
\bigl(q(\lambda_i)+\beta(\lambda_i)\bigr)
<
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i(z)
=0,
\]
which proves (7).  Inequality (7) is incompatible with (8), completing
the proof.
