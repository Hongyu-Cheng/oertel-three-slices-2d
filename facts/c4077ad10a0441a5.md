## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex body with nonempty interior, let \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), and let \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) be closed halfplanes whose boundary lines pass through \(z\). Suppose their defining oriented normals are nonzero, pairwise nonparallel, and admit a strictly positive linear dependence. Write \(a_1=|K|\), \(c_i=|K\cap Q_i|\), and \(s_3=|K\setminus(Q_1\cup Q_2)|\).

If \(c_i+c_j<a_1\) for every distinct \(i,j\), then \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\), and there is a nondegenerate triangle \(\Delta\) with \(z\in\operatorname{int}\Delta\) such that
\[
|\Delta|=a_1,\qquad
|\Delta\cap Q_i|=c_i\quad(i=1,2,3),\qquad
|\Delta\setminus(Q_1\cup Q_2)|\ge s_3.                 \tag{1}
\]
No chord-centrality assumption is used or claimed to be preserved by the optimization producing \(\Delta\).

In particular, suppose \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\) and label the caps so that \(c_1\ge c_2\ge c_3\). Then
\[
u=\frac54c_1+c_2-a_1\ge0.                            \tag{2}
\]
If \(c_1+c_2\ge a_1\), the inequality
\[
4s_3\le\left(\frac32\sqrt{c_1}+\sqrt{u}\right)^2        \tag{3}
\]
holds. If \(c_1+c_2<a_1\), the triangle in (1) has the same \(a_1,c_1,c_2,c_3,u\) and has singleton area at least \(s_3\). Thus (3) holds for all the stated convex bodies if and only if it holds for all nondegenerate triangles with the stated cuts and cap ordering.

The rank assertion of accepted fact 1b4ea45680c75628 is used with its \(H_i=Q_i\) and its fan center \(O=z\); no normalized cap variables from that fact are reused. The three-cap inequality of accepted fact 851e063a1b9908a3 is used with its total area \(A=a_1\), and its cap variables are specified at the invocation below.

## Proof

Translate \(z\) to the origin and positively rescale the defining forms so that \(Q_i=\{\ell_i\le0\}\) and \(\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3=0\). This changes none of the regions or areas. Use the form coordinates \(u_0=\ell_1\), \(v_0=\ell_2\). These coordinate names are local; in particular, \(u_0\) is not the scalar \(u\) in (2). As in accepted fact 1b4ea45680c75628, the six open fan sectors in cyclic order are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A:\ u_0<0,\ v_0<0,&
B:\ u_0>0,\ v_0<0,\ u_0+v_0<0,&
C:\ u_0>0,\ v_0<0,\ u_0+v_0>0,\\
D:\ u_0>0,\ v_0>0,&
E:\ u_0<0,\ v_0>0,\ u_0+v_0>0,&
F:\ u_0<0,\ v_0>0,\ u_0+v_0<0.
\end{array}                                                       \tag{4}
\]
Here \(A\) is a sector name, not an area variable. Up to the null cutting lines, \(Q_1=A\cup E\cup F\), \(Q_2=A\cup B\cup C\), \(Q_3=C\cup D\cup E\), and the singleton measured by \(s_3\) is \(D\). Opposite sectors differ by three positions. Any three consecutive sectors have total angular width \(\pi\), because their outside bounding rays are opposite rays of one cutting line.

First, the three strict pair inequalities imply that each singleton sector has positive area in \(K\): the singleton belonging to \(Q_i\) has area at least \(a_1-c_j-c_k>0\). Choose points of \(K\) in \(B,D,F\). In the coordinates of (4), write them as
\[
P_B=(\xi_1,-\eta_1),\qquad
P_D=(\xi_2,\eta_2),\qquad
P_F=(-\xi_3,\eta_3),
\]
where \(\eta_1>\xi_1>0\), \(\xi_3>\eta_3>0\), and \(\xi_2,\eta_2>0\). The cyclic determinants
\[
\det(P_B,P_D)=\xi_1\eta_2+\eta_1\xi_2,\quad
\det(P_D,P_F)=\xi_2\eta_3+\eta_2\xi_3,\quad
\det(P_F,P_B)=\xi_3\eta_1-\eta_3\xi_1
\]
are strictly positive. The determinant identity
\[
\det(P_D,P_F)P_B+\det(P_F,P_B)P_D
+\det(P_B,P_D)P_F=0
\]
therefore expresses the origin as a convex combination with strictly positive coefficients of a nondegenerate triangle contained in \(K\). The triangle is nondegenerate since its signed doubled area is the sum of those three positive determinants. Hence \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\). This argument also applies to every other convex body having the same strict pair inequalities.

We next prove the compactness needed for a fixed-cap optimization. Fix a positive total area \(a_1\), the three cutting lines, and \(\varepsilon>0\). The diameters of all convex bodies \(P\) satisfying
\[
|P|=a_1,\qquad
|P\cap Q_i|+|P\cap Q_j|\le a_1-\varepsilon
\quad(i\ne j)                                      \tag{5}
\]
are uniformly bounded. All such bodies contain the origin in their interiors by the preceding argument.

Suppose instead that their diameters \(D_n\) tend to infinity. Take diameter endpoints for \(P_n\), let \(e_n\) be the unit direction from the first to the second, and take a perpendicular unit vector \(f_n\). After passing to a subsequence, \(e_n\to e\). Use coordinates \(p=x e_n+y f_n\) with origin \(0\). The projection interval in \(x\) has length \(D_n\). Its perpendicular section-length function is nonnegative and concave, and its integral is \(a_1\). Joining a point of maximum height to the two endpoints with height zero gives a triangle below its graph. Thus every section length is at most \(2a_1/D_n\).

Every point of \(P_n\) is at distance at most \(2a_1/D_n\) from the diameter line, since the triangle formed by that point and the diameter endpoints lies in \(P_n\). The same bound applies to the distance of the origin from the diameter line. Consequently \(|y|\le4a_1/D_n\) throughout \(P_n\).

If \(\ell_i(e)\ne0\), then, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the signs of \(\ell_i(p)\) and \(x\ell_i(e_n)\) can disagree only when \(|x|\le C_i/D_n\), for a fixed constant \(C_i\). The area of this strip is at most \(4a_1C_i/D_n^2\), by the section-length bound. After a further subsequence, let \(\gamma=\lim_n|P_n\cap\{x\le0\}|\). It follows that the \(i\)-th cap area tends to \(\gamma\) if \(\ell_i(e)>0\) and to \(a_1-\gamma\) if \(\ell_i(e)<0\).

At most one of the forms vanishes on \(e\), since their kernels are pairwise distinct. Their sum vanishes identically, so among their nonzero values on \(e\) there are both signs. Some pair of limiting cap areas therefore sums to \(a_1\), contradicting (5). This proves the asserted diameter bound.

We prove (1) first when \(K\) is a convex polygon. Let \(N\) be its number of genuine vertices. Consider convex polygons \(P\) with at most \(N\) genuine vertices satisfying \(|P|=a_1\) and \(|P\cap Q_i|=c_i\) for all three indices. The class is nonempty. Taking \(\varepsilon=\min_{i\ne j}(a_1-c_i-c_j)>0\) in (5) bounds its diameters. Since every member contains the origin, all vertices lie in one fixed ball. A polygon with at most \(N\) vertices is the convex hull of \(N\) points if repetitions are allowed. Compactness of that ball, together with continuity of convex-body area and fixed-sector or fixed-halfplane intersection area under Hausdorff convergence, makes this class compact. These continuity statements follow from almost-everywhere convergence of the body indicators off the boundary of the positive-area convex limit; the fixed cutting lines also have area zero. The constraints keep the limiting area equal to \(a_1>0\), and the strict pair inequalities put its center in its interior.

The continuous function \(P\mapsto |P\cap D|\) therefore has a maximizer \(P\) in this class. If \(P\) is a triangle, it already proves (1). Suppose it has \(n>3\) genuine vertices.

We give the first-variation argument including vertices on fan rays. List its genuine vertices \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) counterclockwise, set \(d_i=P_{i+1}-P_i\), and write \(x_i(t)=P_i+t d_i\), with cyclic indices and \(0\le t\le1\). Let \(V_i\) be arbitrary vertex velocities. The derivative \(L\) of the vector of the six sector areas is
\[
(LV)_J
=\sum_{i=1}^n\int_0^1
\mathbf 1_J(x_i(t))
\det\bigl((1-t)V_i+tV_{i+1},d_i\bigr)\,dt ,
\qquad J\in\{A,B,C,D,E,F\}.                          \tag{6}
\]
No edge lies on a cutting line, since such an edge's supporting line would contain the interior point \(0\). A fan-ray crossing is consequently transversal whenever it lies in an edge, including an incident edge at a ray vertex. Under a small vertex perturbation, replacing its perturbed crossing by the old crossing changes a swept region by a wedge whose length and width are both of the order of the perturbation. This error is of second order, including crossings entering or leaving an edge endpoint. The signed area swept by the remaining boundary element is exactly the integrand in (6). This proves (6) at ray vertices as well as at ordinary crossings.

It also proves that the sector-area map is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of these genuine vertex coordinates. Indeed, the first derivatives in (6) are integrals of bounded affine functions on edge intervals. Their interval endpoints vary continuously through an endpoint crossing, with newly appearing intervals having lengths tending to zero. Equivalently, their integrands converge for almost every \(t\); thus all first derivatives vary continuously. Small arbitrary vertex motions preserve convexity, the genuine-vertex property, and the center's interiority.

The rank of \(L\) cannot be six. If it were, the inverse-function theorem applied to an invertible six-coordinate minor would realize any sufficiently small change in its six output areas. In particular it would realize, for small \(\eta>0\), the sector-area change
\[
\eta(1,-1,0,1,-1,0)
\quad\hbox{in the order }(A,B,C,D,E,F).              \tag{7}
\]
The sums specifying total area and each of the three caps in (4) are unchanged by (7), whereas the area in \(D\) increases by \(\eta\). The perturbed polygon still has \(n\le N\) genuine vertices and remains convex, so this contradicts maximality.

If no vertex is on a fan ray, all fan-ray intersections are transversal and accepted fact 1b4ea45680c75628 applies to \(L\). Since \(P\) is not a triangle and \(L\) is not onto, that fact gives one open sector \(S\) containing all but one vertices and a unique vertex in the opposite open sector \(-S\).

Here is the complete endpoint argument when at least one fan ray meets a vertex. It does not use an annihilator restriction to the span of area and cap weights. Since \(L\) is not onto, choose a nonzero six-vector \(\lambda\) with \(L^T\lambda=0\). On an edge, let \(\lambda_i(t)\) be its sector weight. Collecting the coefficient of each arbitrary \(V_i\) in (6), and using the nonparallelism of the two incident edge directions at a genuine vertex, gives the two moment equations on every edge:
\[
\int_0^1(1-t)\lambda_i(t)\,dt=0,\qquad
\int_0^1t\lambda_i(t)\,dt=0.                         \tag{8}
\]
For disjoint positive-length intervals \(I<J\) on an edge,
\[
\det\left(
\left(\int_I(1-t)\,dt,\int_I t\,dt\right),
\left(\int_J(1-t)\,dt,\int_J t\,dt\right)
\right)
=|I|\,|J|(\bar t_J-\bar t_I)>0.                     \tag{9}
\]
Thus an edge on which some sector weight is nonzero must contain at least three positive-length pieces with nonzero weights. In particular it has at least two interior fan-ray crossings.

Let \(r\ge1\) be the number of fan rays meeting vertices and \(k_i\) the number meeting the interior of edge \(i\). Then \(\sum_i k_i=6-r\le5\). If all six weights were nonzero, every edge would have \(k_i\ge2\), contradicting \(n\ge3\). Therefore some sector weight is zero. Also, there are at most two edges carrying nonzero weights.

There cannot be only one such edge. Every nonzero-weight sector would then have its entire boundary arc on that edge: an adjacent edge containing any positive angular portion of the same sector would also carry a nonzero weight. By (9) there are at least three such sectors. A connected angular arc containing at least three entire fan sectors contains three consecutive entire sectors and has width at least \(\pi\). But a supporting edge of a convex polygon seen from an interior point has angular width strictly less than \(\pi\). This is a contradiction.

There are therefore exactly two edges carrying nonzero weights. Each meets at least three nonzero-weight sectors. If their sets of such sectors were disjoint, all six sector weights would be nonzero, already excluded. They share a nonzero-weight sector. The boundary arc in any open fan sector is connected, and no intervening edge can lie in a nonzero-weight sector while carrying only zero weights. Thus the two edges must be adjacent at a vertex \(V\) in that open sector. Two adjacent edge arcs can share only that one open sector.

It follows that there are exactly five nonzero-weight sectors and each of the two edges meets exactly three of them. Let \(S\) be the unique zero-weight sector. Every other edge lies in \(\overline S\); hence all vertices other than \(V\) lie in \(\overline S\). To locate \(V\), list the five nonzero sectors in their order around the complement of \(S\). The two adjacent edges cover, respectively, the first three and the last three, sharing the third. This shared sector is opposite \(S\). Thus \(V\in -S\). This proves that every endpoint rank defect relevant here also has all but one vertices in one closed sector and its remaining vertex in the opposite open sector.

It remains to replace such an opposite-sector cluster by a triangle. This replacement uses all six sector areas, rather than only the optimization objective. Let \(P_-\) and \(P_+\) be the two neighbors of the unique vertex \(V\in -S\). The ear triangle \(\Delta_0=\operatorname{conv}\{V,P_-,P_+\}\) has positive area, since \(V\) is genuine. The diagonal \(P_-P_+\) splits the convex polygon into \(\Delta_0\) and the convex hull of its other vertices. The latter hull lies in \(\overline S\). Consequently \(P\) and \(\Delta_0\) agree outside \(\overline S\).

Choose
\[
\rho=\sqrt{\frac{a_1}{|\Delta_0|}}\ge1,\qquad
P'_\pm=V+\rho(P_\pm-V),\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{V,P'_-,P'_+\}.
\]
Since \(P_\pm\in\overline S\) and \(-V\in S\), the identity \(P'_\pm=\rho P_\pm+(\rho-1)(-V)\) places \(P'_\pm\) in \(\overline S\). The part added to \(\Delta_0\) by this dilation is contained in the quadrilateral with vertices \(P_-,P_+,P'_+,P'_-\), which lies in \(\overline S\). Hence \(\Delta\) also agrees with \(P\) outside \(\overline S\). Its area is \(\rho^2|\Delta_0|=a_1\), so its area inside \(S\) agrees as well. Boundaries of the sectors are null. Therefore all six sector areas, and in particular all three caps and the singleton \(D\), are exactly preserved in this replacement. The strict pair inequalities again imply \(0\in\operatorname{int}\Delta\). Since the replaced \(P\) maximized the singleton area, (1) follows for the original polygon \(K\).

Finally let \(K\) be an arbitrary compact convex body with the strict pair inequalities. Approximate it in Hausdorff distance by convex polygons containing the origin in their interiors, then dilate each polygon about the origin to have area exactly \(a_1\). Their cap areas \(c_{i,n}\) tend to \(c_i\), and their areas in \(D\) tend to \(s_3\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), every pair sum is at most \(a_1-\varepsilon\), for one fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). Apply the polygon result to obtain triangles \(\Delta_n\) of area \(a_1\), having cap areas \(c_{i,n}\) and at least the respective polygon's singleton area. The uniform diameter bound from (5) applies to these triangles. Taking a subsequence of their three vertices yields a limiting triangle \(\Delta\). Its area remains \(a_1>0\), so it is nondegenerate. Continuity gives all the area equalities and the singleton inequality in (1); the limiting strict pair inequalities ensure that its center remains interior. This proves (1) for all \(K\).

We now prove (2) and the asserted equivalence. If \(c_1+c_2\ge a_1\), then \(u\ge c_1/4\). Otherwise \(c_1+c_2<a_1\). For \(0\le t\le1\), replace only the third defining form by
\(\ell_{3,t}=-(\ell_1+(1-t)\ell_2)\).
Its cap area is continuous in \(t\). At \(t=0\) it is \(c_3\le c_2\), and at \(t=1\) it is \(a_1-c_1>c_2\). There is therefore \(t_*<1\) at which this cap has area \(c_2\). The three forms \(\ell_1,(1-t_*)\ell_2,\ell_{3,t_*}\) are nonzero, pairwise nonparallel, and sum to zero; their cap areas are \(c_1,c_2,c_2\). Accepted fact 851e063a1b9908a3, with its \(A=a_1\) and \((b_1,b_2,b_3)=(c_1,c_2,c_2)\), gives
\[
5(c_1+2c_2)-6c_2\ge4a_1,
\]
which is exactly \(u\ge0\).

The complement of \(Q_1\cup Q_2\) lies in \(Q_3\) up to the cutting lines, so \(s_3\le c_3\le c_1\). When \(c_1+c_2\ge a_1\), the already established bound \(u\ge c_1/4\) gives
\[
\left(\frac32\sqrt{c_1}+\sqrt u\right)^2
\ge4c_1\ge4s_3.
\]
When \(c_1+c_2<a_1\), all three pair sums are strictly below \(a_1\) by the ordering. The triangle provided by (1) has exactly the same right side of (3) and a singleton area at least that of \(K\). Therefore the triangle case of (3) implies its general convex-body case. The reverse implication is immediate because triangles are among these convex bodies. No triangle case of (3) has been assumed as a proved premise.
