## Statement

Work in centered coordinates \((x,y)\), and let
\[
\mathcal C_2=\{(x,y):y\le x\},\qquad
\mathcal C_3=\{(x,y):y\ge-x\},\qquad
\mathcal C_1=\{(x,y):x\le1\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be two-dimensional convex bodies, and put
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\qquad \tau=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
For \(i=2,3\), define
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\mathcal C_i|,\qquad
v_i=|A_2\cap\mathcal C_i|,\qquad
c_i=|K\cap\mathcal C_i|,
\]
and define \(c_1=|K\cap\mathcal C_1|\).  Fix \(\delta>0\), and define
\[
v_1=\left|A_2\cap\{(x,y):x\le2+\delta\}\right|.
\]
Assume
\[
A_0\subseteq\{(x,y):x\le-|y|\},
\qquad
A_2\subseteq\{(x,y):x\ge|y|\},
\]
and
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(L,A),(L,-A),(B,H),(B,-H)\},
\qquad
L<0<1<B,\qquad 0<A\le H.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume the exact crossing equalities
\[
c_2+u_2+v_2=\tau,\qquad c_3+u_3+v_3=\tau,
\tag{2}
\]
the contact lower bound
\[
c_1+m\ge\tau,
\tag{3}
\]
and the smallest-contact condition
\[
c_1+v_1
\le
\min\{c_2+u_2+v_2,\ c_3+u_3+v_3\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
M>m+k.
\]
In particular, the lower chamber \(M<m+k\) is impossible for the entire class (1).

## Proof

As in accepted fact 003d3ba47b37335b, the two endpoint-cone containments give
\[
u_2=u_3=0,\qquad v_2=v_3=m.
\]
Reflection in the \(x\)-axis exchanges \(\mathcal C_2\) and \(\mathcal C_3\), so \(c_2=c_3\).  Define
\[
p=\frac{c_2}{k}=\frac{c_3}{k},\qquad
q=\frac{c_1}{k},\qquad
\mu=\frac Mk,\qquad \nu=\frac mk.
\]
Equations (2) give \(\tau=pk+m\), and hence
\[
\frac{\tau}{k}=p+\nu,\qquad
\mu=\frac{9p+7\nu-2}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
M<m+k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
9p+5\nu<4.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (2) and (3) give \(q\ge p\), while (2) and (4) give
\[
q+\frac{v_1}{k}\le p+\nu,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\nu\ge q-p.
\tag{7}
\]
It is therefore enough to prove
\[
4p+5q\ge4.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, (7) would then give \(9p+5\nu\ge4p+5q\ge4\), contrary to (6).

We next compute \(p\) and \(q\) exactly in all crossing regimes.  Put
\[
W=B-L,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{-L}{W},\qquad
b=\frac BW=1-\lambda,\qquad
a=\frac AW,\qquad
h=\frac HW,
\]
\[
D=a+h,\qquad
t=\frac{h-a}{a+h},\qquad
r=\frac{1-L}{W}.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
0<\lambda,b<1,\qquad D>0,\qquad 0\le t<1,\qquad
\lambda<r<1,
\]
and
\[
a=\frac{1-t}{2}D,\qquad h=\frac{1+t}{2}D.
\tag{10}
\]
Writing \(u=(x-L)/W\), the normalized half-width of \(K\) at \(u\in[0,1]\) is
\[
a+(h-a)u,
\]
and \(k=W^2D\).  The contact line \(x=1\) corresponds to \(u=r\), so direct integration gives the exact contact-cap ratio
\[
q=\frac{2ar+(h-a)r^2}{D}
=r(1-t+tr).
\tag{11}
\]
It will be useful to set
\[
q_0=\lambda(1-t+t\lambda)
=(1-b)(1-bt).
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(r>\lambda\) and the right side of (11) is strictly increasing in \(r\),
\[
q>q_0.
\tag{13}
\]

The line \(y=x\) meets the left vertical side exactly when \(a\ge\lambda\), equivalently \(A\ge-L\), and it meets the right vertical side exactly when \(h\ge b\), equivalently \(H\ge B\).  Otherwise it enters through the lower sloping side or exits through the upper sloping side, respectively.  At equality it passes through the corresponding vertex.

For completeness, the exact crossing-cap ratio in the four regimes is
\[
p=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{(b+h)^2}{2D(1+h-a)},
&a<\lambda,\ h\ge b,
\\[3mm]
\displaystyle
\frac12+\frac{2b-1}{2D},
&a\ge\lambda,\ h\ge b,
\\[3mm]
\displaystyle
\frac1{2D}
\left(
\frac{(b+h)^2}{1+h-a}
-
\frac{(b-h)^2}{1-h+a}
\right),
&a<\lambda,\ h<b,
\\[4mm]
\displaystyle
1-\frac{(\lambda+a)^2}{2D(1-h+a)},
&a\ge\lambda,\ h<b.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
The adjacent formulas agree when \(a=\lambda\) or \(h=b\).

To verify (14), in normalized coordinates the line has height \(u-\lambda\).  If \(a<\lambda\), its intersection with the lower sloping side is
\[
u_-=\frac{\lambda-a}{1+h-a};
\]
if \(h<b\), its intersection with the upper sloping side is
\[
u_+=\frac{\lambda+a}{1-h+a}.
\]
In the first regime, integrating \(u-\lambda+a+(h-a)u\) from \(u_-\) to \(1\) gives the first formula.  In the second regime the same integrand is integrated from \(0\) to \(1\).  In the third regime one subtracts from the first-regime expression the overcounted upper-right triangle, whose normalized area is
\[
\frac{(b-h)^2}{2(1-h+a)}.
\]
In the fourth regime the complementary upper-left triangle has normalized area
\[
\frac{(\lambda+a)^2}{2(1-h+a)}.
\]
Division by the total normalized area \(D\) gives (14).

We prove the following geometric implication:
\[
p<\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4p+5q_0>4.
\tag{15}
\]
Together with (13), this proves (8) whenever \(p<4/9\).

First suppose \(a<\lambda\) and \(h\ge b\).  For fixed \(b,t\), the first formula in (14) is
\[
P(D)=
\frac{\left(b+\frac{1+t}{2}D\right)^2}
{2D(1+tD)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
P'(D)=
-\frac{(D(1+t)+2b)\bigl(2b-D(1+t-4bt)\bigr)}
{8D^2(1+tD)^2}.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(1+t-4bt\le0\), then \(P\) decreases to
\[
\frac{(1+t)^2}{8t}\ge\frac12,
\]
so \(p<4/9\) is impossible.  Thus \(1+t-4bt>0\), and (17) shows
\[
p\ge P_*:=b(1+t-2bt),
\tag{18}
\]
the value at \(D=2b/(1+t-4bt)\).

Now
\[
4P_*+5q_0-4=1-b-bt-3b^2t.
\tag{19}
\]
Suppose the right side of (19) were nonpositive.  As a function of \(b>0\), it is strictly decreasing; let \(\beta\le b\) be its positive zero.  At that zero,
\[
t=\frac{1-\beta}{\beta(1+3\beta)}
\]
and hence
\[
P_*(\beta)
=\frac{5\beta^2-2\beta+1}{3\beta+1}
=\frac49+\frac{5(3\beta-1)^2}{9(3\beta+1)}
\ge\frac49.
\tag{20}
\]
Moreover, the derivative of \(b(1+t-2bt)\) with respect to \(b\) is \(1+t-4bt\), which is positive throughout \([\beta,b]\).  Thus (18) and (20) would imply \(p\ge4/9\), a contradiction.  Therefore the right side of (19) is positive, and (15) follows in this regime.

Next suppose \(a\ge\lambda\) and \(h\ge b\).  If \(p<4/9\), the second formula in (14) forces \(b<1/2\).  Since
\[
\frac{1-t}{2}D=a\ge\lambda=1-b,
\]
we have \(D\ge2(1-b)/(1-t)\).  For \(b<1/2\), the second formula in (14) is increasing in \(D\), so
\[
p\ge P_0:=
\frac{1+t-2bt}{4(1-b)}.
\tag{21}
\]
The inequality \(P_0<4/9\) implies
\[
0\le b<\frac7{16},
\qquad
0\le t<T:=\frac{7-16b}{9(1-2b)}.
\tag{22}
\]
For fixed \(b\), the expression
\[
G_0(t):=4P_0+5(1-b)(1-bt)-4
\tag{23}
\]
is affine in \(t\).  At \(t=0\),
\[
G_0(0)=\frac{5b^2-6b+2}{1-b}>0,
\tag{24}
\]
because the numerator has negative discriminant.  At \(t=T\), one has \(P_0=4/9\).  To check the other term, put \(u=7/16-b\ge0\).  Direct simplification gives
\[
(1-b)(1-bT)-\frac12
=
\frac{2048u^3+2560u^2-216u+9}{144+2304u}>0.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed,
\[
2560u^2-216u\ge-\frac{729}{160},
\]
so the numerator in (25) is at least \(9-729/160>0\).  Thus \(G_0(T)>0\).  Since (23) is affine, (22), (24), and (25) give \(G_0(t)>0\), and (21) proves (15) in this regime.

We record next that the fourth regime in (14), including its boundary \(h=b\), always has
\[
p\ge\frac12.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, write
\[
d=a-\lambda\ge0,\qquad e=b-h\ge0,\qquad R=1-2\lambda.
\]
The inequality \(h\ge a\) gives \(d+e\le R\), in particular \(R\ge0\).  A direct expansion gives
\[
D(1-h+a)-(\lambda+a)^2
=R(d+e+2\lambda)-e^2.
\]
Since \(e\le R\), the right side is at least \(R(d+2\lambda)\ge0\).  The fourth formula in (14) now gives (26).

It remains to treat \(a<\lambda\) and \(h<b\).  In this regime, with \(b,t\) fixed, the third formula in (14) is
\[
P(D)=
\frac1{2D}
\left[
\frac{\left(b+\frac{1+t}{2}D\right)^2}{1+tD}
-
\frac{\left(b-\frac{1+t}{2}D\right)^2}{1-tD}
\right].
\tag{27}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
P'(D)=
-\frac{Dt(1+t-2bt)^2}
{2(1-tD)^2(1+tD)^2}
\le0.
\tag{28}
\]
The allowed values of \(D\) satisfy
\[
D<\frac{2(1-b)}{1-t},
\qquad
D<\frac{2b}{1+t}.
\tag{29}
\]
If the first upper bound in (29) is no larger than the second, then \(b\ge(1+t)/2\).  Letting \(D\) reach the first bound gives \(a=\lambda\), where (26) applies; by (28), \(p\ge1/2\).  Hence this alternative cannot occur when \(p<4/9\).

We may therefore assume \(b<(1+t)/2\), so the second upper bound in (29) is the smaller one.  Equations (27) and (28) give
\[
p\ge P_*:=
\frac{b(1+t)^2}{1+t+2bt}.
\tag{30}
\]
If \(p<4/9\), then
\[
b<\beta(t):=
\frac{4(1+t)}{9+10t+9t^2}.
\tag{31}
\]
Define
\[
G_*(b,t)=
4\frac{b(1+t)^2}{1+t+2bt}
+5(1-b)(1-bt)-4.
\tag{32}
\]
The numerator of \(\partial G_*/\partial b\), after multiplication by the positive denominator \((1+t+2bt)^2\), is
\[
(1+t)^3(5x^3+5x^2-5x-1),
\qquad
x=\frac{2bt}{1+t}.
\tag{33}
\]
For \(0\le b\le\beta(t)\),
\[
0\le x\le\frac{8t}{9+10t+9t^2}<\frac13,
\tag{34}
\]
because
\[
\frac13-\frac{8t}{9+10t+9t^2}
=
\frac{9t^2-14t+9}{3(9+10t+9t^2)}>0.
\]
On \(0\le x\le1/3\), the polynomial in (33) is negative: its derivative is \(5(3x-1)(x+1)\le0\), and its value at \(x=0\) is \(-1\).  Thus \(G_*\) is strictly decreasing in \(b\) on the interval in (31).  At its right endpoint,
\[
G_*(\beta(t),t)
=
\frac{
5(81t^4+36t^3+230t^2+36t+81)
}{
9(9t^2+10t+9)^2
}>0.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (30)--(35) prove (15) in the remaining regime.

We have proved (15) in every regime in which \(p<4/9\).  By (13),
\[
p<\frac49
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4p+5q>4.
\tag{36}
\]
If instead \(p\ge4/9\), then \(m>0\), so \(\nu>0\) and
\[
9p+5\nu>4.
\tag{37}
\]
If \(p<4/9\), equations (7) and (36) give
\[
9p+5\nu\ge4p+5q>4.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus \(9p+5\nu>4\) in all cases.  By (5),
\[
\mu-(\nu+1)=\frac{9p+5\nu-4}{2}>0,
\]
which is \(M>m+k\), as claimed.
