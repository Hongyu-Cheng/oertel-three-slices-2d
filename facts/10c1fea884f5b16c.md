## Statement

Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body
in coordinates
\[
(x,y)=(\theta_1,\theta_2),\qquad
\theta_3=-12-x-y.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the exact-cell convention in which an index records
\(\theta_i\leq0\).  Suppose \(Q_2\) has area zero and the other six
proper cells have positive area.  Define the two convex tail bodies
\[
B_1=Q\cap\{x+y\leq-12\},\qquad
B_3=Q\cap\{x\geq0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Write their vertical projections as
\[
\operatorname{proj}_y B_i=[-a_i,h_i],
\qquad a_i,h_i>0
\quad(i=1,3),
\tag{3}
\]
and write \(A_J\) for coordinate area of \(Q_J\).  Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{A_{12}}{A_1}
\leq
\frac{a_1(2h_1+a_1)}{h_1^2},
\qquad
\frac{A_{23}}{A_3}
\leq
\frac{a_3(2h_3+a_3)}{h_3^2}.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, for every
\[
0<c\leq\min\{a_1,a_3,12\},
\]
one has the exact bridge inequality
\[
\boxed{\quad
A_{123}\geq12c-\frac{c^2}{2}.
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
Both conclusions include horizontal support faces, zero-length
endpoint sections, and every weak/strict cutting-line boundary.

Apply (4)--(5) to the table and actual-height proposal in accepted
fact `9aea6365aa810898`.  Put
\[
D=16072081,\qquad
\eta=\frac{D}{32000000000}.
\tag{6}
\]
Its coordinate cell areas are
\[
(A_1,A_3,A_{12},A_{13},A_{23},A_{123})
=(550,1,150,99,199,1)\eta.
\tag{7}
\]
There is no convex realization of (7) whose actual tail heights are
\[
h_1=\frac{131}{200},\qquad
h_3=\frac{13}{5000}.
\tag{8}
\]
The proposed values of \(r_1,r_3\) are irrelevant to this
nonrealizability.

The conclusion is stronger when the target midpoint areas in that
accepted fact are imposed.  If actual support parameters satisfy
\[
\rho_1\geq
\frac{\gamma}{4}(50+550\eta+10r_1),
\qquad
\rho_3\geq
\frac{\gamma}{4}(50+\eta+10r_3),
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
550\eta\leq r_1h_1-\frac{h_1^2}{2},
\qquad
\eta\leq r_3h_3-\frac{h_3^2}{2},
\tag{10}
\]
with the exact \(\gamma,\rho_1,\rho_3\) of
`9aea6365aa810898`, then no choice of positive
\(r_1,h_1,r_3,h_3\) can arise from a convex realization of (7).
Consequently the new \(Q\)-table cannot occur in any full realization
of its target \(\rho\)-table.

## Proof

We first prove the general profile inequalities.  For a compact
convex planar body \(B\), let
\[
\ell_B(t)=
\mathcal H_1\bigl(B\cap(\mathbb R\times\{t\})\bigr)
\tag{11}
\]
be its horizontal section length.  On the vertical projection of
\(B\), the function \(\ell_B\) is nonnegative and concave: if
\(s,t\) are two section levels, convexity contains the corresponding
weighted Minkowski sum of the two section intervals, whose length is
the same weighted sum of their lengths.

We use the following one-dimensional consequence.  Let
\(f\geq0\) be concave on \([-a,h]\), where \(a,h>0\), and suppose
\(f(0)>0\).  Concavity between \(-u,0,h\) gives, for
\(0\leq u\leq a\),
\[
f(-u)\leq\frac{h+u}{h}f(0),
\tag{12}
\]
while for \(0\leq t\leq h\),
\[
f(t)\geq\left(1-\frac th\right)f(0).
\tag{13}
\]
Integration yields
\[
\int_{-a}^0f
\leq
f(0)\left(a+\frac{a^2}{2h}\right),
\qquad
\int_0^hf
\geq\frac{hf(0)}2.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\int_{-a}^0f}{\int_0^hf}
\leq
\frac{a(2h+a)}{h^2}.
\tag{15}
\]
No endpoint section is assumed positive in this derivation.

Apply (15) first to \(B_1\).  Above \(y=0\), its area is exactly
\(A_1\).  Below \(y=0\), it is
\(A_{12}+A_{\{2\}}=A_{12}\), because
\(A_{\{2\}}=0\).  Both areas are positive, so its concave section profile
has positive value at \(0\).  This proves the first inequality in
(4).  For \(B_3\), the upper area is \(A_3\), while the lower area is
\(A_{23}+A_{\{2\}}=A_{23}\).  The same argument proves the second
inequality in (4).

Now fix \(0<c\leq\min\{a_1,a_3,12\}\).  For every
\(-c<t<0\), both tail profiles are positive.  Thus the horizontal
section \(Q_t\), which is one interval, contains a point with
\[
x<-12-t
\]
and a point with
\[
x>0.
\]
It consequently contains the whole interval
\([-12-t,0]\).  Its interior belongs to \(Q_{123}\).  Integrating
these forced intervals gives
\[
A_{123}
\geq\int_{-c}^0(12+t)\,dt
=12c-\frac{c^2}{2},
\]
which is (5).  The three cutting lines have planar area zero, so this
argument is unchanged by every allowed weak/strict boundary
assignment.  It also shows that isolated projection endpoints and
horizontal support faces cause no exceptional case.

We apply the result first to the literal parameters proposed in
accepted fact `9aea6365aa810898`.  From (7),
\[
\frac{A_{12}}{A_1}=\frac3{11},
\qquad
\frac{A_{23}}{A_3}=199.
\tag{16}
\]
For fixed \(h>0\), the right side of (4) is strictly increasing in
the corresponding lower reach \(a>0\).
Put \(c=1/100\).  If \(a_1\leq c\), the first right side in (4) is
at most
\[
\frac{c(2h_1+c)}{h_1^2}
=\frac{528}{17161}
<\frac3{11},
\tag{17}
\]
where the exact gap is
\[
\frac3{11}-\frac{528}{17161}
=\frac{45675}{188771}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(a_1>c\).  If \(a_3\leq c\), the second right side in (4) is
at most
\[
\frac{c(2h_3+c)}{h_3^2}
=\frac{3800}{169}
<199,
\tag{19}
\]
with exact gap
\[
199-\frac{3800}{169}
=\frac{29831}{169}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(a_3>c\).  Equation (5) now forces
\[
A_{123}\geq
12c-\frac{c^2}{2}
=\frac{2399}{20000}.
\tag{21}
\]
But (7) requires \(A_{123}=\eta\), and
\[
\frac{2399}{20000}-\eta
=\frac{3822327919}{32000000000}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
This contradiction proves that the four proposed support-height
numbers cannot be actual extrema of one convex \(Q\).

Finally, we remove the proposed numerical choice of heights.  Solving
the two inequalities (9) for the actual support widths gives
\[
r_1\leq
\frac{73695475241}{96000000000}<1,
\tag{23}
\]
\[
r_3\leq
\frac{78539443259}{320000000000}<\frac14.
\tag{24}
\]
The exact gaps from \(1\) and \(1/4\) are respectively
\[
\frac{22304524759}{96000000000}>0,
\qquad
\frac{1460556741}{320000000000}>0.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (10), (23), and (24) imply
\[
h_1>550\eta>\frac1{10},
\qquad
h_3>4\eta,
\tag{26}
\]
where
\[
550\eta-\frac1{10}
=\frac{112792891}{640000000}>0.
\tag{27}
\]

For fixed \(c>0\), the function
\[
h\longmapsto\frac{c(2h+c)}{h^2}
\tag{28}
\]
is strictly decreasing on \(h>0\).  If \(a_1\leq c=1/100\), then
(4) and (26) would give
\[
\frac3{11}
<
\frac{c(2/10+c)}{(1/10)^2}
=\frac{21}{100},
\]
which is impossible because
\[
\frac3{11}-\frac{21}{100}
=\frac{69}{1100}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Hence \(a_1>c\).  If \(a_3\leq c\), then (4), (26), and (28) would
give
\[
199<
\frac{c(8\eta+c)}{(4\eta)^2}
=
\frac{8971532960000\cdot1000}{258311787670561}.
\tag{30}
\]
The inequality in (30) is false, since its exact reverse gap is
\[
199-
\frac{8971532960000\cdot1000}{258311787670561}
=
\frac{42432512786441639}{258311787670561}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Thus \(a_3>c\) as well.  The same bridge contradiction
(21)--(22) follows for every possible pair of actual heights.

All quantities in (16)--(31) are exact rationals.  The two section
arguments use only interval convexity and concavity, not a polygonal
ansatz or a floating-point feasibility decision.
