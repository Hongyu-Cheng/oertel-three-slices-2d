## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\},
\qquad
\bar\lambda=\frac13(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system and incidence orbit of accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2`, `64760c76cadff481`, and `f8e871d10dac1791`, in the labeling fixed there, with \(t\leq1/2\). Thus \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and \(A,B,t\) are fixed, \((U\Delta+V\Delta)/2\subseteq\Delta\), and \(L=(\ell_{ij})\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), \(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfy
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}L=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},
\qquad q:=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma>1,
\qquad \det L\ne0,
\]
and
\[
M_i:=\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where \(\mathcal C\) is the literal closed-cap function of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`. Put
\[
\mu_0=U\bar\lambda,\qquad \mu_2=V\bar\lambda,
\]
\[
g_0=L\mu_0+\sigma,\qquad
g_1=L\bar\lambda,\qquad
g_2=L\mu_2-\sigma.
\]
The fixed incidence orbit has
\[
(g_0)_1,(g_0)_2\geq0,\qquad (g_0)_3<0,
\]
\[
(g_1)_1,(g_1)_2<0,\qquad (g_1)_3>1,
\qquad
(g_2)_1,(g_2)_2,(g_2)_3<0,
\]
and, after the fixed allowed column choice,
\[
L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b),qquad p,a,b>0.
\]
Suppose the zero set
\[
Z(L):=\{(i,j):\ell_{ij}=0\}
\]
is nonempty, with no restriction on its cardinality.

Define the explicit three-dimensional linear perturbation space
\[
\mathscr P
=\left\{
\left(Q(w),\eta(w)\right):w\in\mathbb R^3
\right\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^{3\times3}\times\mathbb R^3,
\]
where
\[
Q(w)=(e_1-e_2)w^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\eta(w)=-Q(w)\mu_0.
\]
There is a vector \(w\in\mathbb R^3\) and a number \(\delta>0\) such that, for every \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\),
\[
L_\varepsilon=L+\varepsilon Q(w),
\qquad
\sigma_\varepsilon=\sigma+\varepsilon\eta(w)
\]
have all of the following properties:

1. every entry of \(L_\varepsilon\) is nonzero and \(\det L_\varepsilon\ne0\);
2. \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}L_\varepsilon=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma_\varepsilon=q>1\), and \(U,V,A,B,t\) are unchanged;
3. the transformed triangles
   \[
   P_{0,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon U\Delta+\sigma_\varepsilon,
   \quad
   P_{1,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon\Delta,
   \quad
   P_{2,\varepsilon}=L_\varepsilon V\Delta-\sigma_\varepsilon
   \]
   satisfy \((P_{0,\varepsilon}+P_{2,\varepsilon})/2\subseteq P_{1,\varepsilon}\);
4. the centroid incidence orbit, including every possible equality among \((g_0)_1,(g_0)_2\), is unchanged;
5. the three perturbed literal row masses
   \[
   M_i(\varepsilon)
   =\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon)_{i*};0)
   +A\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon U)_{i*};-(\sigma_\varepsilon)_i)
   +B\mathcal C((L_\varepsilon V)_{i*};(\sigma_\varepsilon)_i)
   \]
   satisfy \(M_i(\varepsilon)<t\) for \(i=1,2,3\).

Consequently, every boundary counterexample in this incidence orbit is a limit of all-nine-nonzero strict systems lying in the contained-support open-chamber orbit of accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915`. Up to one fixed allowed interchange of rows \(1,2\) and one fixed allowed interchange of columns \(2,3\), that chamber is
\[
J_1=\{2\},\qquad J_2=\{2,3\},\qquad J_3=\{1\}.
\]
No exclusion of this contained-support chamber is asserted.

## Proof

The normal form in accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` gives
\(L_{3*}=(p,-a,-b)\) with \(p,a,b>0\). Hence no zero entry lies in row \(3\), regardless of how many entries vanish in the first two rows.

For \(w\in\mathbb R^3\), the matrix \(Q(w)\) has rows
\[
Q(w)_{1*}=w^{\mathsf T},\qquad
Q(w)_{2*}=-w^{\mathsf T},\qquad
Q(w)_{3*}=0,
\]
so
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Q(w)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(w\) so that \(w_j\ne0\) for every column \(j\) containing a zero entry of \(L\). Such vectors are the complement in \(\mathbb R^3\) of finitely many coordinate hyperplanes. If \(\ell_{1j}=0\), then
\[
(L_\varepsilon)_{1j}=\varepsilon w_j\ne0;
\]
if \(\ell_{2j}=0\), then
\[
(L_\varepsilon)_{2j}=-\varepsilon w_j\ne0.
\]
The third row is fixed and already has no zero entry. Every originally nonzero entry remains nonzero for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\). Thus all nine entries are nonzero simultaneously. This is one perturbation from the single space \(\mathscr P\), not an iteration over the elements of \(Z(L)\). It is the simultaneous version of the one-zero update in accepted fact `bce2c51784540e70`.

Accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` gives \(\det L\ne0\) for the surviving strict \(N=3\) system, including its zero-entry boundary strata. Since determinant is continuous,
\[
\det(L+\varepsilon Q(w))\longrightarrow\det L\ne0,
\]
so \(\det L_\varepsilon\ne0\) after decreasing the same positive upper bound on \(\varepsilon\).

Equation (1) gives
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}L_\varepsilon
=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}L
+\varepsilon\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Q(w)
=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},
\tag{2}
\]
and, because \(\eta(w)=-Q(w)\mu_0\),
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma_\varepsilon
=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\sigma
-\varepsilon\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Q(w)\mu_0
=q.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus column sums and \(q>1\) are preserved exactly.

Compatibility is also exact. The endpoint translations cancel, and the original barycentric compatibility gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{P_{0,\varepsilon}+P_{2,\varepsilon}}2
&=L_\varepsilon\frac{U\Delta+V\Delta}{2}\\
&\subseteq L_\varepsilon\Delta
=P_{1,\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(U,V,A,B,t\) are not perturbed, all endpoint determinants, area ratios, and the threshold remain fixed. Invertibility of \(L_\varepsilon\) keeps all three transformed triangles nondegenerate.

The particular \(\sigma\)-component of \(\mathscr P\) fixes the possibly non-strict small-endpoint incidence exactly:
\[
g_{0,\varepsilon}
=L_\varepsilon\mu_0+\sigma_\varepsilon
=L\mu_0+\sigma
=g_0.
\tag{5}
\]
The other centroids obey
\[
g_{1,\varepsilon}=g_1+\varepsilon Q(w)\bar\lambda,
\qquad
g_{2,\varepsilon}=g_2+\varepsilon Q(w)(\mu_0+\mu_2).
\tag{6}
\]
The third coordinate of each vector in (6) is unchanged because the third row of \(Q(w)\) is zero. The first two coordinates of \(g_1\) and all coordinates of \(g_2\) have strict signs in accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791`; hence their signs persist for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). Equation (5) preserves both signs and possible equalities of \(g_0\). The third row of \(L\) and the third component of \(\sigma\) are fixed as well. Therefore the labeling and every defining centroid incidence remain in the same orbit.

It remains to preserve the strict row masses without assuming distinct endpoint values. We record the needed semicontinuity directly from the literal definition in accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`. If
\((z_n,\tau_n)\to(z,\tau)\), then for every \(\lambda\in\Delta\),
\[
\limsup_n
\mathbf1_{\{z_n\cdot\lambda\geq\tau_n\}}
\leq
\mathbf1_{\{z\cdot\lambda\geq\tau\}}.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the only nontrivial case is \(z\cdot\lambda<\tau\), when the strict gap makes the left side eventually zero. Applying Fatou's lemma to the complementary indicators gives
\[
\limsup_n\mathcal C(z_n;\tau_n)
\leq\mathcal C(z;\tau).
\tag{8}
\]
This proof includes repeated vertex values, a threshold equal to a repeated value, and the constant-at-threshold case.

Every argument and threshold in each perturbed cap is continuous in \(\varepsilon\). Since \(A,B>0\), (8) gives
\[
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}M_i(\varepsilon)
\leq M_i(0)<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus all three strict row inequalities hold for every sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Intersecting the finitely many positive bounds obtained above produces a single \(\delta>0\) for properties 1 through 5.

Choose any sequence \(\varepsilon_n\downarrow0\) below \(\delta\). The signs of all entries of \(L_{\varepsilon_n}\) are constant once \(n\) is large: the original nonzero entries retain their signs, and each activated zero has the fixed sign of the corresponding entry of \(Q(w)\). Each pair \((L_{\varepsilon_n},\sigma_{\varepsilon_n})\) is therefore an all-nine-nonzero strict system in one fixed open sign chamber and in the original incidence orbit. Accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915` places that chamber in the contained-support orbit. Since the support pattern is fixed, one fixed allowed relabeling, rather than an \(n\)-dependent choice, puts the whole tail in the representative
\[
J_1=\{2\},\qquad J_2=\{2,3\},\qquad J_3=\{1\}.
\]
Finally \((L_{\varepsilon_n},\sigma_{\varepsilon_n})\to(L,\sigma)\), proving the asserted boundary-to-open-chamber limit. Accepted fact `89f1aef98e9b8915` explicitly leaves the contained-support chamber unresolved, and the present perturbation supplies no further inequality there. Therefore no exclusion of that chamber follows.
