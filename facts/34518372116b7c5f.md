## Statement

Let \(C\) and \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) satisfy the locked three-slice problem.  Put
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
r=\frac mM.
\]
If no point of
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2)
\]
has halfspace depth at least \(2(|A_0|+|A_1|+|A_2|)/9\), then
\[
r<\frac{23}{40}.
\]

## Proof

Assume for a contradiction that a counterexample has \(r\geq23/40\).
Scale planar area so that \(M=1\), reflect the height coordinate if needed,
and henceforth write
\[
|A_0|=r,\qquad |A_2|=1,\qquad s=|A_1|.
\]
Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
g=\frac{2-r-s}{3},\qquad
q=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9=\frac{2(1-g)}3.
\tag{2}
\]

### 1. Exact parameter range from the accepted centroid theorem

Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` applies to every counterexample and gives,
among its strict residual inequalities,
\[
1-r<s<2-r.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(g>0\), and the area hypotheses of accepted fact
`c02e26ea799dadd7` hold.  In the normalization above, its parameter is
\[
\delta=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9.
\]
Because its displayed weak inequality is sufficient for the locked
conclusion, a counterexample must satisfy the strict reverse inequality
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{r-\delta}\right)^2<\frac{20s}{9}.
\tag{4}
\]

Set
\[
\xi=\sqrt{5r+2-6g}=3\sqrt{r-\delta}.
\]
Using \(s=2-r-3g\), or equivalently
\[
s=\frac{\xi^2+2-7r}{2},
\]
inequality (4) becomes
\[
9\xi^2-6\xi+11-70r>0.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(r\geq23/40\), one has \(\sqrt{70r-10}>1\).  The two roots of the
quadratic in (5) are
\[
\frac{1-\sqrt{70r-10}}3<0,\qquad
\frac{1+\sqrt{70r-10}}3>0.
\]
Since \(\xi\geq0\), (5) is equivalent to
\[
\xi>\frac{1+\sqrt{70r-10}}3.
\]
After squaring positive quantities and substituting the definition of
\(\xi\), this is exactly
\[
0<g<g_c(r),\qquad
g_c(r)=\frac{27-25r-2\sqrt{70r-10}}{54}.
\tag{6}
\]

The function \(g_c\) is strictly decreasing, with
\[
g_c'(r)=-\frac{25+70/\sqrt{70r-10}}{54}.
\tag{7}
\]
Its unique zero in the present range is
\[
r_c=\frac{163-12\sqrt{51}}{125}.
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac35<r_c<\frac58.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, the first comparison reduces to \(22>3\sqrt{51}\), whose squared
form is \(484>459\).  The second reduces to
\(679<96\sqrt{51}\), whose squared margin is
\[
96^2\cdot51-679^2=8975>0.
\]
Thus (6) already rules out \(r\geq r_c\).  It remains to exclude the compact
envelope
\[
\frac{23}{40}\leq r\leq r_c,\qquad
0\leq g\leq g_c(r),
\tag{10}
\]
where allowing equality in \(g\) can only strengthen the estimates below.
On this envelope,
\[
\frac23<-g_c'(r)<\frac7{10}.
\tag{11}
\]
For the upper bound, use
\(\sqrt{70r-10}\geq11/2\) and
\(140/11<64/5\) in (7).  For the lower bound, (9) gives
\(70r-10<135/4<36\), so \(70/\sqrt{70r-10}>35/3>11\).

### 2. Canonical rows and exact product coverage

Accepted fact `d2d117bcc58a1ba0` gives nonconstant affine functions
\(f_1,f_2,f_3\) and real numbers \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\) such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1
\tag{12}
\]
and the three closed halfspaces
\[
H_i=\{(h,w):f_i(w)+\sigma_i(h-1)\leq0\}
\]
have masses strictly below \(q\), while their open middle sections cover the
whole middle plane.  Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
X_i(u)&=f_i(u)-\sigma_i,&
C_{0i}&=\{u\in A_0:X_i(u)\leq0\},&
x_i&=|C_{0i}|,\\
Y_i(v)&=f_i(v)+\sigma_i,&
C_{2i}&=\{v\in A_2:Y_i(v)\leq0\},&
y_i&=|C_{2i}|,\\
B_i&=\{w\in A_1:f_i(w)\leq0\},&&&
b_i&=|B_i|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
x_i+b_i+y_i<q.
\tag{14}
\]
Because (12) makes some \(f_i(w)\) strictly negative at every \(w\),
\[
\sum_i b_i\geq s.
\tag{15}
\]
Writing \(z_i=x_i+y_i\), equations (2), (14), and (15) give
\[
\sum_i z_i<3q-s=r+g.
\tag{16}
\]

For each row define the strict midpoint graph
\[
E_i=\{(u,v)\in A_0\times A_2:X_i(u)+Y_i(v)<0\},
\qquad d_i=|E_i|.
\tag{17}
\]
For every \((u,v)\in A_0\times A_2\), compatibility (1) places
\((u+v)/2\) in \(A_1\).  Equation (12) makes
\(f_i((u+v)/2)<0\) for some \(i\), and affinity gives
\[
X_i(u)+Y_i(v)=2f_i\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)<0.
\]
Thus the three \(E_i\) cover \(A_0\times A_2\), including a strict choice
of row at every pair, and hence
\[
\sum_i d_i\geq r.
\tag{18}
\]
Also \(X_i(u)+Y_i(v)<0\) implies \(X_i(u)<0\) or \(Y_i(v)<0\).  Therefore
\[
E_i\subseteq
(C_{0i}\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times C_{2i}).
\]
Taking product measures and then summing also gives
\[
r\leq\sum_i(x_i+ry_i)\leq\sum_i z_i.
\tag{19}
\]
This rederives both the graph product coverage and its cap-cylinder
consequence.

### 3. Measure-preserving ranks and the row envelope

Fix one row and omit its index.  Since \(X\) and \(Y\) are nonconstant
affine functions on two-dimensional convex bodies, every level set has
planar area zero.  Their distribution functions are therefore continuous.
Define the lower ranks
\[
p(u)=|\{u'\in A_0:X(u')\leq X(u)\}|\in[0,r],
\]
\[
w(v)=|\{v'\in A_2:Y(v')\leq Y(v)\}|\in[0,1].
\tag{20}
\]
The probability integral transform for these continuous distributions says
precisely that \(p\) pushes planar area on \(A_0\) to Lebesgue measure on
\([0,r]\), while \(w\) pushes planar area on \(A_2\) to Lebesgue measure on
\([0,1]\).  Consequently \((p,w)\) pushes product area to Lebesgue measure
on \([0,r]\times[0,1]\).

Suppose \((u,v)\in E\).  The two lower subcaps used in (20) have areas
\(p=p(u)\) and \(w=w(v)\).  Every midpoint of one point from each subcap
lies in \(B\), because the corresponding sum of \(X\)- and \(Y\)-values is
at most \(X(u)+Y(v)<0\).  The planar Brunn-Minkowski inequality, in the
boundary-inclusive form recorded in accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f`,
therefore gives
\[
\sqrt p+\sqrt w\leq2\sqrt b.
\tag{21}
\]
Furthermore \(X(u)+Y(v)<0\) implies
\[
p\leq x\quad\hbox{or}\quad w\leq y.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(E\) is contained in the inverse image, under the
measure-preserving rank map, of
\[
\mathcal R(b;x,y)=
\left\{(p,w)\in[0,r]\times[0,1]:
\sqrt p+\sqrt w\leq2\sqrt b,\
p\leq x\ \hbox{or}\ w\leq y\right\}.
\]
It follows that
\[
d\leq D_r(b;x,y):=|\mathcal R(b;x,y)|.
\tag{23}
\]

For \(c,z\geq0\), put
\[
J(b,c,z)=
\int_0^z
\min\left\{c,(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\right\}\,dp.
\tag{24}
\]
Splitting the union in (22) into its vertical strip and the remaining
horizontal strip gives the exact identity
\[
D_r(b;x,y)
=J(b,1,x)+J(b,y,r)-J(b,y,x).
\tag{25}
\]
The right side is nondecreasing in \(b\).  Since (14) gives
\(b<q-x-y\), setting \(z=x+y\) yields
\[
d\leq D_r(q-z;x,y).
\tag{26}
\]

### 4. Exact fixed-sum split

We prove that for every feasible \(x+y=z\), the right side of (26) is
bounded by the larger of the all-small and balanced profiles.

First note that throughout (10),
\[
r<\frac{16q}{17}.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, (11) shows that
\(g_c(r)+51r/32\) is strictly increasing.  At \(r_c\) it is
\(51r_c/32<255/256<1\) by (9).  Hence
\[
g+\frac{51r}{32}\leq
g_c(r)+\frac{51r}{32}<1,
\]
which is exactly (27) after using (2).

Fix \(z\), put \(b=q-z\), and consider feasible splits
\(0\leq x\leq r\), \(0\leq y\leq1\), \(x+y=z\).  Since
\(z<q<2/3<2r\), a maximizing split can be chosen with \(x\geq y\).
To see this, if \(x<y\leq r\), reflect the curved set
\(\sqrt p+\sqrt w\leq2\sqrt b\) across \(p=w\).  Its part in
\([0,r]^2\) has the same covered area after swapping \(x,y\); in the
remaining strip \([0,r]\times(r,1]\), the swap enlarges the vertical
threshold from \(x\) to \(y\).  If \(y>r\), replace the split by
\((r,z-r)\); its vertical strip covers the entire \(p\)-range and hence
the entire curved set in the capacity rectangle.

Write
\[
x=z-y,\qquad
\max\{0,z-r\}\leq y\leq\frac z2,
\]
and
\[
\mathcal H(y)=D_r(b;z-y,y).
\]
Put
\[
A(p)=\min\{1,(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\},
\qquad
p_y=(2\sqrt b-\sqrt y)_+^2.
\]
The function \(\mathcal H\) is absolutely continuous and, at every
differentiability point, (25) gives
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=
-A(x)+\min\{y,A(x)\}
+\bigl(\min\{r,p_y\}-x\bigr)_+.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(y\geq A(x)\), then \(p_y\leq x\) and \(\mathcal H'(y)=0\).
This is a terminal interval, because
\(\sqrt{x}+\sqrt y\) is nondecreasing as \(y\) moves toward \(z/2\).
If \(y<A(x)\) and \(p_y<r\), then \(p_y>x\), necessarily \(A(x)<1\),
and direct simplification gives
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=
2(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)
\left(2\sqrt b-\sqrt x-\sqrt y\right)\geq0.
\tag{29}
\]

It remains to exclude an interior local maximum in the chamber
\(p_y\geq r\).  There
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=r-z+2y-A(x).
\tag{30}
\]
If \(A(x)=1\), the right side has derivative \(2\), so it cannot cross
from positive to negative.  Otherwise put
\[
c=2\sqrt b,\qquad u=\sqrt x,\qquad W=\sqrt z.
\]
At a zero of (30),
\[
r=c^2-2cu+3u^2-W^2,\qquad
q=\frac{c^2}{4}+W^2.
\tag{31}
\]
A downward crossing, and hence an interior local maximum, requires
\[
\frac{d}{dy}\mathcal H'(y)=3-\frac cu<0,
\qquad\hbox{so}\qquad u<\frac c3.
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(x\geq y\), one has \(W^2=z\leq2u^2\).  Equations (31)-(32) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
17r-16q
&=13c^2-34cu+51u^2-33W^2\\
&\geq13c^2-34cu-15u^2\\
&=c^2\left(1-3\frac uc\right)
\left(13+5\frac uc\right)>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
contrary to (27).  The transition boundaries in (28) are continuous;
the formulas (29) and (30) agree at \(p_y=r\), (29) approaches zero at
\(p_y=x\), and a terminal zero-derivative interval reaches the balanced
endpoint.  Thus no omitted nondifferentiability can create an interior
maximum.  The maximum occurs at the lower split endpoint or at \(y=z/2\).

Define
\[
\Phi(b,z)=J(b,1,z),\qquad
f_q(z)=\Phi(q-z,z).
\tag{34}
\]
If \(z\leq r\), the lower split endpoint equals \(f_q(z)\).  If \(z>r\),
that endpoint is \(D_r(b;r,z-r)\), which is at most the full curved area
\(J(b,1,r)\leq J(b,1,z)=f_q(z)\).  Therefore (26)-(33) prove
\[
d\leq
\max\left\{
f_q(z),
D_r\!\left(q-z;\frac z2,\frac z2\right)
\right\}.
\tag{35}
\]

### 5. The tangent point and its exact slope bounds

In the chamber
\[
b>\frac14,\qquad z>(2\sqrt b-1)^2,\qquad z<4b,
\]
direct integration in (24) gives
\[
\Phi(b,z)=
\frac{
-16\sqrt b\,z^{3/2}-16\sqrt b-16b^2
+24bz+24b+3z^2+3
}{6},
\tag{36}
\]
\[
\Phi_z=(2\sqrt b-\sqrt z)^2,
\qquad
\Phi_b=
\frac{4\bigl(\sqrt b(-4b+3z+3)-z^{3/2}-1\bigr)}
{3\sqrt b}.
\tag{37}
\]
Put
\[
z_0=\frac{r+g}{3},\qquad
b_0=q-z_0=\frac23-\frac r3-g,
\qquad
\lambda=\Phi_z(b_0,z_0)-\Phi_b(b_0,z_0).
\tag{38}
\]
The parameter envelope (10) and (11) imply
\[
\frac49<b_0\leq\frac{19}{40},
\qquad
\frac{23}{120}\leq z_0<\frac5{24}.
\tag{39}
\]
For the lower bound on \(b_0\), at fixed \(r\) its minimum occurs at
\(g=g_c(r)\); along that boundary its derivative is
\(-1/3-g_c'(r)>1/3\), and at the left endpoint it equals
\[
\frac{961}{2160}>\frac49.
\]
For the upper bound on \(z_0\), the boundary value increases with \(r\)
and is at most \(r_c/3<5/24\).  The other two bounds follow directly from
\(r\geq23/40\) and \(g\geq0\).  These bounds also put \((b_0,z_0)\) in
the chamber of (36): \(b_0>1/4\), while
\[
2\sqrt{b_0}-1
\leq2\sqrt{19/40}-1<\frac25<\sqrt{23/120}\leq\sqrt{z_0}.
\]

On the rectangle (39), differentiating \(\lambda=\Phi_z-\Phi_b\) gives
\[
\lambda_b=
\frac{2(14b^{3/2}-3b\sqrt z-z^{3/2}-1)}{3b^{3/2}},
\tag{40}
\]
\[
-\lambda_z=
3+2\sqrt{\frac bz}-2\sqrt{\frac zb}.
\tag{41}
\]
In fact,
\[
\lambda_b>\frac{11}{2},\qquad 0<-\lambda_z<5.
\tag{42}
\]
For the first inequality, after clearing the positive denominator it is
equivalent to
\[
G(b,z):=23b^{3/2}-12b\sqrt z-4z^{3/2}-4>0.
\]
The function \(G\) increases with \(b\) and decreases with \(z\) on (39).
At the limiting corner,
\[
G\!\left(\frac49,\frac5{24}\right)
=\frac{608-111\sqrt{30}}{216}>0,
\]
because
\[
608^2-111^2\cdot30=34>0.
\]
For (41), put \(u=b/z\).  The rectangle gives
\[
1<u\leq\frac{57}{23}.
\]
The inequality \(\sqrt u-1/\sqrt u<1\) follows from
\((u-1)^2<u\); the latter holds at both endpoints of this interval, with
the upper-endpoint margin
\[
\frac{57}{23}-\left(\frac{34}{23}\right)^2
=\frac{155}{529}>0.
\]
This proves (42).

At fixed \(r\), \(\lambda\) decreases with \(g\).  Along \(g=g_c(r)\),
(11), (40), and (42) give
\[
\frac{d\lambda}{dr}
=\lambda_b\left(-\frac13-g_c'(r)\right)
+\lambda_z\frac{1+g_c'(r)}3
>
\frac{11}{6}-\frac59
=\frac{23}{18}>0.
\tag{43}
\]
Along \(g=0\), \(\lambda\) decreases with \(r\), since
\[
\left.\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial r}\right|_g
=\frac{-\lambda_b+\lambda_z}{3}<0.
\]
Hence the maximum of \(\lambda\) on (10) is at
\((r,g)=(23/40,0)\), and its minimum is at
\((r,g)=(23/40,13/432)\).  Direct substitution gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}
=-\frac{17}{120}
+\frac{360\sqrt{190}-74\sqrt{1311}}{2565}
<\frac{1199707}{1603125}<\frac34,
\tag{44}
\]
\[
\lambda_{\min}
=-\frac{2933}{6480}
+\frac{116640\sqrt{15}-3671\sqrt{3921}}{225990}
>\frac{119576003}{225990000}>\frac{19}{36}.
\tag{45}
\]
The rational radical certificates used here are
\[
\left(\frac{137841}{10000}\right)^2-190
=\frac{141281}{10^8}>0,
\qquad
1311-\left(\frac{362077}{10000}\right)^2
=\frac{246071}{10^8}>0,
\]
\[
15-\left(\frac{38729}{10000}\right)^2
=\frac{64559}{10^8}>0,
\qquad
\left(\frac{31309}{500}\right)^2-3921
=\frac{3481}{250000}>0.
\]
The two final rational margins in (44)-(45) are respectively
\[
\frac{10547}{6412500},\qquad
\frac{303503}{225990000}.
\]
We shall also use the coupled improvement obtained from (43): whenever
\(r\geq3/5\),
\[
\lambda>
\frac{119576003}{225990000}
+\frac{23}{18}\left(\frac35-\frac{23}{40}\right)

\tag{46}

where the last margin is
\[
\frac{30091}{28248750}>0.

### 6. The global tangent majorant

Define the tangent at \(z_0\) by
\[
L(z)=f_q(z_0)+\lambda(z-z_0).
\tag{47}
\]
We prove that both profiles in (35) are at most \(L(z)\) for every
\(0\leq z\leq q\).

First consider \(f_q\).  In its central chamber, put
\[
b=q-z,\qquad t=\sqrt{\frac zb}.
\]
Differentiating (36) along \(b=q-z\) gives
\[
f_q''(z)=
-\frac{37}{3}+4t-\frac2t+\frac23t^3
+\frac{2}{3b^{3/2}}.
\tag{48}
\]
Here \(b\geq1/4\), \(z<5/12\), and hence \(t<4/3\).  The
\(t\)-dependent part of (48) is increasing, so
\[
f_q''(z)<
-\frac{37}{3}+\frac{16}{3}
+\frac{16}{3}-\frac32+\frac{128}{81}
=-\frac{257}{162}<0.
\tag{49}
\]
Thus \(f_q\) is strictly concave throughout this chamber.

Before the chamber, the curved height in (24) is at least \(1\) on the
whole integration interval, so \(f_q(z)=z\).  Let \(z_a\) be the transition
point.  It is the positive solution of
\[
5z_a+2\sqrt{z_a}+1=4q,
\]
namely
\[
z_a=
\left(\frac{2\sqrt{5q-1}-1}{5}\right)^2.
\tag{50}
\]
Concavity between \(z_a\) and \(z_0\) gives
\(L(z_a)\geq f_q(z_a)=z_a\).  Since \(\lambda<1\), for \(z\leq z_a\),
\[
L(z)-z
=L(z_a)-z_a+(1-\lambda)(z_a-z)\geq0.
\tag{51}
\]

After the central chamber, \(f_q\) is nonincreasing.  Indeed, while the
curve is active with \(b\leq1/4\), one has \(1\leq t\leq2\) and
\[
f_q'(z)=
b\left(4-4t-3t^2+\frac43t^3\right)<0.
\tag{52}
\]
The polynomial in parentheses is negative at \(t=1\), and its derivative
is \(2(2t+1)(t-2)\leq0\) on this interval.  After the curve is exhausted,
\[
f_q(z)=\frac83b^2,\qquad
f_q'(z)=-\frac{16}{3}b\leq0.
\tag{53}
\]
Since \(L\) is increasing and already majorizes \(f_q\) at the end of the
central chamber, equations (49)-(53) prove
\[
f_q(z)\leq L(z)\qquad(0\leq z\leq q).
\tag{54}
\]

For the balanced split, discard the curved constraint and retain only the
union of the vertical and horizontal strips.  Since \(z/2<r\),
\[
D_r\!\left(q-z;\frac z2,\frac z2\right)
\leq P_r(z):=\frac z2\left(1+r-\frac z2\right).
\tag{55}
\]
We next give a uniform exact lower bound on \(z_a\).  From (6),
\[
g\leq g_c(23/40)=\frac{13}{432},
\qquad
q\geq\frac{419}{648}.
\]
If \(\tau_a=\sqrt{z_a}\), then
\(5\tau_a^2+2\tau_a+1=4q\).  At \(\tau=159/400\),
\[
4\frac{419}{648}
-\left(5\tau^2+2\tau+1\right)
=\frac{3599}{2592000}>0.
\]
The left polynomial is increasing in \(\tau\geq0\), so
\[
\tau_a>\frac{159}{400},
\qquad
z_a>\frac{25281}{160000}>\frac{79}{500}.
\tag{56}
\]

Equations (47), (50), and \(L(z_a)\geq z_a\) give
\[
L(z)\geq\lambda z+(1-\lambda)z_a.
\tag{57}
\]
The maximum over real \(z\) of \(P_r(z)-\lambda z\) is
\[
\left(\frac{1+r}{2}-\lambda\right)^2.
\tag{58}
\]
Here
\[
\frac{1+r}{2}-\lambda>
\frac{1+23/40}{2}-\frac34=\frac3{80}>0,
\]
so replacing \(r\) by an upper bound in the squared term below preserves
the inequality direction.
It therefore suffices to prove
\[
(1-\lambda)z_a
\geq
\left(\frac{1+r}{2}-\lambda\right)^2.
\tag{59}
\]
The difference in (59) is concave in \(\lambda\), so its minimum on each
of the two slope intervals below is attained at an endpoint.

If \(23/40\leq r\leq3/5\), use (44)-(45), (56), and
\((1+r)/2\leq4/5\).  At the two slope endpoints,
\[
\frac{17}{36}\frac{79}{500}
-\left(\frac45-\frac{19}{36}\right)^2
=\frac{41}{81000}>0,
\tag{60}
\]
\[
\frac14\frac{79}{500}
-\left(\frac45-\frac34\right)^2
=\frac{37}{1000}>0.
\tag{61}
\]
If \(3/5\leq r\leq r_c\), use (9), (44), (46), (56), and
\((1+r)/2<13/16\).  The corresponding endpoint margins are
\[
\frac{11}{25}\frac{79}{500}
-\left(\frac{13}{16}-\frac{14}{25}\right)^2
=\frac{4611}{800000}>0,
\tag{62}
\]
\[
\frac14\frac{79}{500}
-\left(\frac{13}{16}-\frac34\right)^2
=\frac{1139}{32000}>0.
\tag{63}
\]
Thus (59) holds throughout (10).  Combining (55), (57)-(59) gives
\[
D_r\!\left(q-z;\frac z2,\frac z2\right)\leq L(z).
\tag{64}
\]
Finally, (35), (54), and (64) prove the rowwise bound
\[
d_i\leq L(z_i)\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{65}
\]

### 7. Aggregation and the final scalar inequality

Because \(\lambda>0\), equations (16), (38), and (65) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i d_i
&\leq\sum_iL(z_i)\\
&=3f_q(z_0)+
\lambda\left(\sum_i z_i-3z_0\right)\\
&<3f_q(z_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{66}
\]
It remains to prove that the last quantity is below \(r\).

Define
\[
\mathcal H(r,g)=
r-3\Phi\left(\frac23-\frac r3-g,\frac{r+g}{3}\right).
\tag{67}
\]
At fixed \(g\), equations (37)-(38) give
\[
\mathcal H_r=1-\lambda>0.
\tag{68}
\]
At fixed \(r\),
\[
\mathcal H_g=3\Phi_b-\Phi_z=-\mathcal E,
\qquad
\mathcal E:=\Phi_z-3\Phi_b.
\tag{69}
\]
On the rectangle (39),
\[
\mathcal E_b=
\frac{2(10b^{3/2}-b\sqrt z-z^{3/2}-1)}{b^{3/2}}>10,
\tag{70}
\]
\[
-\mathcal E_z=
11+2\sqrt{\frac bz}-6\sqrt{\frac zb},
\qquad
0<-\mathcal E_z<11.
\tag{71}
\]
For (70), the required positive numerator after subtracting
\(5b^{3/2}\) increases with \(b\) and decreases with \(z\); at the limiting
corner it is
\[
\frac{416-47\sqrt{30}}{864}>0.
\]
The last comparison is exact because
\[
416^2-47^2\cdot30=106786>0.
\]
For (71), put \(t=\sqrt{z/b}\).  The rectangle gives
\[
\frac13<t^2<1.
\]
Thus \(2/t-6t<0\), while
\(11+2/t-6t>11+2-6>0\).

At fixed \(r\),
\[
\mathcal E_g=-\mathcal E_b+\frac13\mathcal E_z<0.
\]
Along \(g=g_c(r)\), equations (11), (70), and (71) give
\[
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dr}>
10\cdot\frac13-11\cdot\frac19
=\frac{19}{9}>0.
\tag{72}
\]
Hence the minimum of \(\mathcal E\) on (10) occurs at
\[
r=\frac{23}{40},\qquad g=\frac{13}{432}.
\]
There,
\[
\mathcal E=
-\frac{34477}{6480}
-\frac{394\sqrt{3921}}{37665}
+\frac{48\sqrt{15}}{31}.
\tag{73}
\]
The exact rational bounds
\[
\sqrt{15}>\frac{38729}{10000},
\qquad
\sqrt{3921}<\frac{31309}{500}
\]
give
\[
\mathcal E>
\frac{1596947}{75330000}>0.
\tag{74}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal H_g<0\).  For fixed \(r\), the minimum of \(\mathcal H\)
is therefore on \(g=g_c(r)\).  Along that boundary,
\[
\frac{d\mathcal H}{dr}
=\mathcal H_r+\mathcal H_g g_c'(r)>0
\]
by (7), (68), and (74).  Hence the minimum of \(\mathcal H\) occurs at the
same corner.  Direct substitution gives
\[
\mathcal H=
-\frac{162872689}{27993600}
+\frac{40517\sqrt{3921}}{5248800}
+\frac{62\sqrt{15}}{45}.
\tag{75}
\]
Besides the lower bound on \(\sqrt{15}\) already used, take
\[
\sqrt{3921}>\frac{62617}{1000},
\qquad
3921-\left(\frac{62617}{1000}\right)^2
=\frac{111311}{10^6}>0.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal H>
\frac{11994547}{10497600000}>0.
\tag{76}
\]
Equations (67) and (76) prove
\[
3f_q(z_0)<r.
\tag{77}
\]

Finally, (18), (66), and (77) give the impossible chain
\[
r\leq\sum_i d_i<3f_q(z_0)<r.
\]
Therefore no counterexample has \(r\geq23/40\), which proves the statement.
