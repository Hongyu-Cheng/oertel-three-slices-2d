## Statement

Let
\[
S=\bigcup_{i=0}^2(\{i\}\times A_i),
\qquad
\mu(H)=\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|\{w\in A_i:(i,w)\in H\}\right|,
\]
where every \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a nonempty compact convex set, and put \(T=\sum_i|A_i|\) and \(t=2T/9\). Define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]

The following are equivalent.

1. There is no \(y\in S\) satisfying \(h_S(y)\ge t\).

2. For every \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), there exist an integer \(m_j\in\{1,2,3\}\) and proper closed halfspaces \(H_{j1},\ldots,H_{jm_j}\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
\mu(H_{jr})<t\quad(1\le r\le m_j)
\]
and
\[
\{j\}\times A_j
\subseteq
\bigcup_{r=1}^{m_j}\operatorname{int}H_{jr}.
\]

Consequently, if \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\]
and the halfspaces in item 2 are exhibited, then these finite data certify a counterexample to the locked universal claim. In the balanced search it is enough to consider
\[
|A_i|<\frac{T}{2}\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\]

## Proof

Assume first that item 2 holds. Take any \(y\in S\), and let \(j\) be its slice index. Some \(H_{jr}\) has \(y\in\operatorname{int}H_{jr}\), hence \(y\in H_{jr}\). Therefore
\[
h_S(y)\le\mu(H_{jr})<t.
\]
Thus every point of \(S\) has depth strictly below \(t\), proving item 1.

Conversely, assume item 1. Fix \(j\) and \(y=(j,z)\in\{j\}\times A_j\). Then \(h_S(y)<t\). By accepted fact \(400272f6de6c67bb\), the infimum may be restricted to proper closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(y\). Hence there is a nonzero normal \(n_y\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
K_y=\{p:n_y\cdot(p-y)\le0\}
\]
satisfies \(\mu(K_y)<t\).

For \(k\ge1\), set
\[
K_{y,k}=\{p:n_y\cdot(p-y)\le 1/k\}.
\]
These are proper closed halfspaces, \(y\in\operatorname{int}K_{y,k}\), and \(K_{y,k}\downarrow K_y\). Since \(\mu\) is finite, continuity from above gives
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(K_{y,k})=\mu(K_y)<t.
\]
Thus some \(k(y)\) satisfies \(\mu(K_{y,k(y)})<t\). Write \(G_y=\operatorname{int}K_{y,k(y)}\). The sets \(G_y\cap(\{j\}\times\mathbb R^2)\), indexed by \(y\in\{j\}\times A_j\), form a relatively open cover of the compact set \(\{j\}\times A_j\). Compactness therefore gives finitely many halfspaces \(K_1,\ldots,K_m\), each of mass below \(t\), whose interiors cover that slice.

Identify the affine plane \(\{j\}\times\mathbb R^2\) with \(\mathbb R^2\), and define
\[
P_r=
(\{j\}\times\mathbb R^2)\setminus\operatorname{int}K_r.
\]
Each \(P_r\) is a closed convex set, possibly empty or the entire plane. The covering condition is equivalent to
\[
(\{j\}\times A_j)\cap\bigcap_{r=1}^mP_r=\varnothing.
\]

We now prove the required planar Helly step. For any finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^2\) with empty intersection, choose an inclusion-minimal empty subfamily \(C_1,\ldots,C_q\). If \(q\ge4\), minimality gives points
\[
x_i\in\bigcap_{k\ne i}C_k,\qquad i=1,\ldots,q.
\]
Radon's lemma in the plane partitions \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) into two nonempty groups whose convex hulls meet at some point \(x\). Indeed, an affine dependence among four planar points, separated into its positive and negative coefficients, gives such a partition. For each \(k\le4\), use the group not containing \(x_k\); all its points lie in \(C_k\), so convexity gives \(x\in C_k\). For \(k>4\), all four points lie in \(C_k\), hence \(x\in C_k\). This contradicts emptiness. Thus an empty finite family has an empty subfamily of at most three members.

Apply this result to
\[
\{\{j\}\times A_j,P_1,\ldots,P_m\}.
\]
There is an empty subfamily of at most three sets. If it contains \(\{j\}\times A_j\), it contains at most two of the \(P_r\). If it does not, at most three of the \(P_r\) already have empty intersection. In either case there is a set \(R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(1\le |R|\le3\), such that
\[
(\{j\}\times A_j)\cap\bigcap_{r\in R}P_r=\varnothing.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\{j\}\times A_j\subseteq\bigcup_{r\in R}\operatorname{int}K_r.
\]
Taking these \(K_r\) as the \(H_{jr}\) proves item 2.

It remains to verify the certificate interpretation. If the slices are polytopes, accepted fact \(8ad0785e5b4ae9ef\) shows that
\[
(A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq A_1
\]
is exactly the condition that they occur as the three integer-height sections of a compact polytope. Thus the displayed inclusion plus item 2 produces an admissible \(C\) for which every \(y\in S\) has \(h_S(y)<2T/9\), which disproves the locked claim. Accepted fact \(2f9be517307232c7\) shows that any counterexample necessarily has \(|A_i|<T/2\) for every \(i\), so imposing these strict balance inequalities loses no counterexample.

Finally, write each certificate halfspace as
\[
H_{jr}=\{(x,w):\alpha_{jr}x+u_{jr}\cdot w\le b_{jr}\}.
\]
Its mass is the finite sum
\[
\mu(H_{jr})
=
\sum_{i=0}^2
\left|
A_i\cap
\{w:u_{jr}\cdot w\le b_{jr}-\alpha_{jr}i\}
\right|.
\]
For polygonal \(A_i\), every summand is the area of a polygon obtained by one halfplane clipping operation. Its vertices are original polygon vertices satisfying the inequality together with intersections of polygon edges with the boundary line, so a cyclic vertex list and the shoelace formula verify the strict inequality \(\mu(H_{jr})<2T/9\) by finite exact arithmetic.

The slice-cover condition is exactly infeasibility of
\[
w\in A_j,\qquad
u_{jr}\cdot w\ge b_{jr}-\alpha_{jr}j
\quad(1\le r\le m_j),
\]
a finite system of linear inequalities. Hence, for rational or real-algebraic input data, polygon clipping and linear infeasibility give a finite exact verification of every part of the certificate. The strict mass inequalities are essential: replacing \(<2T/9\) by \(\le2T/9\) would not negate the locked \(\ge2T/9\) conclusion.
