## Statement

Assume the normalized homothetic Regime-A setting of accepted fact
`f37aced9de558c8d`.  Thus
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=h^2,\qquad |K|=s,\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+h^2+s)}9,
\]
where \(h>0\), \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a compact
two-dimensional convex body, and
\[
s>1-h^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the two-cut coordinates \(x,y\) from that accepted fact and put
\[
\sigma=x+y,\qquad \eta=x-y.
\]
Define
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{\eta\leq-\sigma\}|,\qquad
u_\ell=|A_0\cap\{\eta\geq\sigma\}|,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{\eta\leq-\sigma\}|,\qquad
c_\ell=|K\cap\{\eta\geq\sigma\}|,\qquad
c_j=|K\cap\{\sigma\geq-1\}|.
\tag{3}
\]
Suppose that all three original row masses are strictly below \(t\):
\[
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i<t,\qquad
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell<t,\qquad
c_j+h^2<t.
\tag{4}
\]
Let
\[
X=K\cap\{\sigma<-1,\ \eta<\sigma\},\qquad
Y=K\cap\{\sigma<-1,\ \eta>-\sigma\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
|X|>
\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}+(1+h^2)u_\ell,}
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\boxed{
|Y|>
\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}+(1+h^2)u_i.}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular,
\[
|X|>\frac19,\qquad |Y|>\frac19.
\tag{8}
\]
The conclusion includes \(u_i=0\) or \(u_\ell=0\), cap lines supporting
faces of either body, and every equality or zero-area cell on a cutting
line.

## Proof

In the middle plane, accepted fact `f37aced9de558c8d` gives
\[
f_j=-1-x-y=-1-\sigma.
\]
Also
\[
x=\frac{\sigma+\eta}{2},\qquad
y=\frac{\sigma-\eta}{2}.
\]
Consequently the three middle caps \(f_i\leq0\), \(f_\ell\leq0\), and
\(f_j\leq0\) are exactly the three sets displayed in (3).  The endpoint
definitions in (2) are the corresponding actual shifted cuts from the
same accepted normalization, so (4) consists of the three original
low-row inequalities.

Put
\[
E=K\cap\{\sigma<-1\}.
\]
The two sets \(E\) and \(K\cap\{\sigma\geq-1\}\) partition \(K\).
Therefore, exactly,
\[
|E|=s-c_j.
\tag{9}
\]
By (5),
\[
E\setminus X
=E\cap\{\eta\geq\sigma\}
\subseteq K\cap\{\eta\geq\sigma\}.
\]
The set on the right has area \(c_\ell\), so
\[
|X|=|E|-|E\setminus X|
\geq s-c_j-c_\ell.
\tag{10}
\]
The last and the second inequalities in (4) give
\[
c_j<t-h^2,\qquad
c_\ell<t-(1+h^2)u_\ell.
\tag{11}
\]
Substituting both strict bounds from (11) into (10) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|X|
&>
s-(t-h^2)-\bigl(t-(1+h^2)u_\ell\bigr)\\
&=
s-2t+h^2+(1+h^2)u_\ell\\
&=
\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}+(1+h^2)u_\ell,
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (6).

Similarly,
\[
E\setminus Y
=E\cap\{\eta\leq-\sigma\}
\subseteq K\cap\{\eta\leq-\sigma\}.
\]
This time the containing cap has area \(c_i\).  Hence
\[
|Y|\geq s-c_j-c_i.
\tag{12}
\]
The last and the first inequalities in (4) give
\[
c_j<t-h^2,\qquad
c_i<t-(1+h^2)u_i.
\]
Substitution in (12) gives (7) by the same calculation.

Both endpoint cap areas in (2) are nonnegative.  From (1),
\[
\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}
>
\frac{5(1-h^2)+5h^2-4}{9}
=\frac19.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (6), (7), and (13) prove (8).

For completeness, \(X\) and \(Y\) are convex because each is the
intersection of \(K\) with two open halfplanes.  They are disjoint:
when \(\sigma<-1\), one has \(\sigma<-\sigma\), so no \(\eta\) can
satisfy both \(\eta<\sigma\) and \(\eta>-\sigma\).

Every boundary used above is an affine line and therefore has planar
area zero.  More strongly, (9)-(12) use the displayed strict and weak
inequalities as exact set partitions, assigning each boundary to one
side, so no boundary area was discarded.  A cutting line may support a
nontrivial face, but that face is one-dimensional and still has planar
area zero.  No cap area is divided by and no positivity of \(u_i\),
\(u_\ell\), or any endpoint cell is used.  This proves all stated
null-boundary and zero-cell cases.
