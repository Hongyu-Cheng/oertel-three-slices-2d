## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, relabeled so that
\[
M=|A_0|\ge |A_2|=m>0.
\]
Set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad b=|B|,\qquad
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM,
\]
let \(g_B\) be the centroid of \(B\), and put
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]

Define
\[
\rho=\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25},
\]
\[
L(r)=
\begin{cases}
1-r,&0<r\le9/25,\\[2pt]
(3r+2\sqrt r-1)/2,&9/25<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
1+r,&0<r\le1/2,\\[2pt]
2-r,&1/2<r<\rho,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal D=\{(r,s):0<r<\rho,\ L(r)<s<U(r)\}.
\]

For \((r,s)\in\mathcal D\), set
\[
C_{r,s}=4s-1-r,
\qquad
t=t(r,s)=\frac{C_{r,s}-\sqrt{C_{r,s}^2-4r}}{2r},
\]
and
\[
W(r,s)=\frac{r(1+t)^2}{1+rt^2}.
\]
Let
\[
P(a)=1-2a+\frac94a^2-\frac34a^3
\]
and define the exact continuously optimized lower envelope
\[
\Gamma(r,s)=
\min_{1/3\le a\le2/3}
\left[
sP(a)+\min\{r,W(r,s)a^2\}
\right].
\tag{1}
\]

Then
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
M\min\{1+s,\ s+r,\ \Gamma(r,s)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

The optimizer in (1) is analytic. Put
\[
a_0=\sqrt{\frac rW},
\qquad
a_u=
\frac{9s+4W-\sqrt{9s^2+72sW+16W^2}}{9s}.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(a_0\ge1/3\), let
\[
\widehat a
=
\max\left\{\frac13,\,
\min\left\{a_u,\frac23,a_0\right\}\right\},
\qquad
G_u=sP(\widehat a)+W\widehat a^2.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(a_0\le2/3\), let
\[
G_s=\frac{4s}{9}+r.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(a_0<1/3\), then \(\Gamma=G_s\) and \(a=2/3\) is an optimizer. If \(a_0>2/3\), then \(\Gamma=G_u\) and \(a=\widehat a\) is an optimizer. If \(1/3\le a_0\le2/3\), then
\[
\Gamma=\min\{G_u,G_s\},
\]
with the corresponding optimizer, or both optimizers in the equality case.

Moreover,
\[
\Gamma(r,s)\ge\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
\tag{6}
\]
throughout both regions
\[
0<r\le\frac15,\qquad 1-r<s<1+r,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
\frac15<r<\frac13,\qquad
\frac{(10+\sqrt{70r-10})^2}{180}<s<1+r.
\tag{8}
\]
These regions contain all the exact open gaps identified in accepted fact `e27aad4aa20bc698`. Hence, for every \((r,s)\in\mathcal D\),
\[
h_S((1,g_B))\ge\frac{2(M+m+b)}9.
\tag{9}
\]

## Proof

Fix a direction \(q\in\mathbb S^1\). Write the lower projection supports and widths of \(A_0,A_2\) as
\[
\ell_i=\min_{z\in A_i}\langle q,z\rangle,\qquad
w_i=\max_{z\in A_i}\langle q,z\rangle-\ell_i
\quad(i=0,2).
\]
Then
\[
\ell_B=\frac{\ell_0+\ell_2}{2},
\qquad
w_B=\frac{w_0+w_2}{2}.
\]
Let
\[
\delta=\langle q,g_B\rangle-\ell_B,
\qquad
a=\frac{\delta}{w_B}.
\tag{10}
\]

We first determine the exact middle-cap lower function for the full possible range of \(a\), including \(a>1/2\). Normalize the section-length density of \(B\) on its projection interval to a probability density \(f\) on \([0,1]\). Its mean is \(a\), and its cumulative mass at \(a\) is the lower centroid-cap fraction.

The tent decomposition in accepted fact `c415ce7703455561`, after rescaling its support to \([0,1]\), gives a probability measure \(\nu\) on peak locations \(\tau\in[0,1]\) such that \(f\) is a mixture of the probability tents \(2k_\tau\) used in accepted fact `d0e8181f1bc85874`, and
\[
\int_0^1\tau\,\nu(d\tau)=3a-1.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac13\le a\le\frac23.
\tag{12}
\]

For a tent with peak \(\tau\), its cumulative mass at \(a\) is
\[
P_\tau(a)=
\begin{cases}
1-\dfrac{(1-a)^2}{1-\tau},&0\le\tau\le a,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{a^2}{\tau},&a\le\tau\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
Set
\[
\tau_*=\frac{2a}{2-a},
\qquad
\Lambda_a(\tau)
=
a(2-a)-\frac{(2-a)^2}{4}\tau.
\tag{14}
\]
For every \(a\in[1/3,2/3]\), one has \(a\le\tau_*\le1\). On \([a,1]\), \(\Lambda_a\) is the tangent line to the convex function \(a^2/\tau\) at \(\tau_*\), so
\[
P_\tau(a)\ge\Lambda_a(\tau)
\qquad(a\le\tau\le1).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(0\le\tau\le a\), direct subtraction gives
\[
P_\tau(a)-\Lambda_a(\tau)
=
\frac{
\tau\bigl[a(4-3a)-\tau(2-a)^2\bigr]
}{4(1-\tau)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\tau\le a\),
\[
a(4-3a)-\tau(2-a)^2
\ge
a(4-3a)-a(2-a)^2
=
a^2(1-a)\ge0.
\]
Thus (15) holds on all of \([0,1]\), directly, without reflection.

Averaging (15) and using (11) yields
\[
\int_0^a f(u)\,du
\ge
\Lambda_a(3a-1)
=
1-2a+\frac94a^2-\frac34a^3
=
P(a).
\tag{17}
\]
This is exact. Indeed,
\[
0\le3a-1\le\tau_*,
\]
because
\[
\tau_*-(3a-1)
=
\frac{(3a-2)(a-1)}{2-a}\ge0.
\]
The mixture supported on \(0\) and \(\tau_*\), with mass
\[
\frac{3a-1}{\tau_*}
\]
at \(\tau_*\), has the required mean peak and attains equality in (17). Consequently the middle cap
\[
c=
\left|
B\cap\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le0\}
\right|
\]
satisfies
\[
c\ge bP(a).
\tag{18}
\]

For a slope \(\lambda\), define the two outer caps
\[
x=
\left|
A_0\cap
\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le\lambda\}
\right|,
\qquad
y=
\left|
A_2\cap
\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le-\lambda\}
\right|.
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
\theta=\frac{w_0}{w_2},
\qquad
K_r(\theta)=\frac{r(1+\theta)^2}{1+r\theta^2}.
\tag{20}
\]
The exact support-clearance estimate in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8`, with
\[
2\delta=a(w_0+w_2),
\]
gives
\[
\frac{x+y}{M}
\ge
\min\{r,a^2K_r(\theta)\}.
\tag{21}
\]

For completeness, all cap endpoints in (21) are valid. If both caps are positive and nonsaturated, (21) is the weighted Cauchy-Schwarz estimate in that accepted fact. If \(x=0\), its sharper empty-cap estimate gives
\[
\frac yM
\ge
r\min\{1,a^2(1+\theta)^2\}
\ge
\min\{r,a^2K_r(\theta)\}.
\]
If \(y=0\), the symmetric estimate gives
\[
\frac xM
\ge
\min\{1,a^2(1+\theta^{-1})^2\}
\ge
\min\{r,a^2K_r(\theta)\},
\]
because \(r\le1\) and
\[
\frac{r}{1+r\theta^2}\le\frac1{\theta^2}.
\]
Finally, if \(x=M\) or \(y=m\), then \(x+y\ge m\), while the right side of (21) is at most \(rM=m\). Hence empty, positive, and saturated caps are exhausted.

The mixed-area width restriction in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8` is
\[
4s\ge1+r+\frac1\theta+r\theta.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus
\[
\theta_-\le\theta\le\theta_+,
\qquad
\theta_\pm=
\frac{C_{r,s}\pm\sqrt{C_{r,s}^2-4r}}{2r}.
\tag{23}
\]
The derivative of \(K_r(\theta)\) has the sign of \(1-r\theta\), so its minimum on this interval occurs at an endpoint. Writing \(t=\theta_-\), one has
\[
\theta_+=\frac1{rt},
\qquad
K_r(t)-K_r(\theta_+)
=
\frac{(1-r)(rt^2-1)}{1+rt^2}<0.
\tag{24}
\]
Therefore
\[
K_r(\theta)\ge K_r(t)=W(r,s).
\tag{25}
\]
This is the same endpoint branch isolated in accepted fact `e27aad4aa20bc698`, now retained with the actual \(a^2\). Combining (18), (21), and (25), every tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\) has mass at least
\[
M\left[sP(a)+\min\{r,W(r,s)a^2\}\right].
\tag{26}
\]
Minimizing over the only possible centroid locations \(a\in[1/3,2/3]\) proves the tilted term in (2). The two slice-parallel masses are \(M+b\) and \(b+m\), and the halfspace reduction in accepted fact `d0e8181f1bc85874` proves (2).

We next verify the optimizer. On the unsaturated interval \(a\le a_0=\sqrt{r/W}\), the objective is
\[
sP(a)+Wa^2.
\]
Its second derivative is
\[
\frac{9s}{2}(1-a)+2W>0,
\]
and its unique unconstrained stationary point is the smaller root of
\[
9sa^2-(18s+8W)a+8s=0,
\]
namely \(a_u\) in (3). Projection onto the unsaturated interval gives (4). On the saturated interval \(a\ge a_0\), the objective is \(sP(a)+r\). Since
\[
P'(a)=-\frac{(3a-2)(3a-4)}4\le0
\]
on \([1/3,2/3]\), its minimum on every nonempty saturated interval is at \(a=2/3\), with value (5). This proves every optimizer case stated above.

It remains to prove (6) on the two regions covering the old gaps. Throughout those regions,
\[
s<1+r,
\]
so
\[
C_{r,s}<3(1+r).
\tag{27}
\]
The smaller root \(t\) lies in \((0,1/\sqrt r)\) and satisfies
\[
rt+\frac1t=C_{r,s}.
\]
The function \(u\mapsto ru+u^{-1}\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1/\sqrt r)\). At
\[
t_0=\frac1{3(1+r)}
\]
its value is
\[
rt_0+\frac1{t_0}
=
3(1+r)+\frac{r}{3(1+r)}
>
3(1+r).
\]
Thus (27) implies \(t>t_0\). Since \(K_r\) is increasing on \((0,1/\sqrt r)\),
\[
W(r,s)>
K_r(t_0)
=
\frac{r(4+3r)^2}{9+19r+9r^2}.
\tag{28}
\]

Suppose first that \(0<r\le1/5\). Direct comparison in (28) gives
\[
W(r,s)\ge\frac{8r}{5},
\tag{29}
\]
because
\[
5(4+3r)^2-8(9+19r+9r^2)
=
8-32r-27r^2
\ge\frac{13}{25}>0.
\]
Also
\[
\frac85a^2\le\frac{32}{45}<1,
\]
so
\[
\min\{r,Wa^2\}\ge\frac{8r}{5}a^2.
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(P(a)\ge4/9>2/9\), the difference from the target is increasing in \(s\). It is therefore enough to use \(s=1-r\), where the desired inequality becomes
\[
(1-r)P(a)+\frac{8r}{5}a^2\ge\frac49.
\tag{31}
\]
The left side minus \(4/9\) is affine in \(r\). Put
\[
u=2-3a\in[0,1].
\]
At \(r=0\),
\[
P(a)-\frac49=\frac{u^2(u+3)}{36}\ge0.
\tag{32}
\]
At \(r=1/5\),
\[
\frac45P(a)+\frac8{25}a^2-\frac49
=
\frac{5u^3+23u^2-32u+12}{225}.
\tag{33}
\]
Moreover,
\[
23u^2-32u+12
=
23\left(u-\frac{16}{23}\right)^2+\frac{20}{23}>0.
\]
Thus (33) is positive, and affinity in \(r\) proves (31) for every \(0\le r\le1/5\). This proves (6) on (7).

Now suppose
\[
\frac15<r<\frac13
\]
and
\[
s>
\alpha_{\mathrm{area}}(r)
:=
\frac{(10+\sqrt{70r-10})^2}{180}.
\]
From (28),
\[
W(r,s)\ge\frac{3r}{2},
\tag{34}
\]
because
\[
2(4+3r)^2-3(9+19r+9r^2)
=
5-9r-9r^2
\ge1.
\]
Since
\[
\frac32a^2\le\frac23<1,
\]
equation (34) gives
\[
\min\{r,Wa^2\}\ge\frac{3r}{2}a^2.
\tag{35}
\]

Furthermore,
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{area}}(r)
=
\frac12+\frac{7r}{18}
+\frac{\sqrt{70r-10}}9
\ge
\frac12+\frac{3r}{2}.
\tag{36}
\]
Indeed,
\[
70r-10-100r^2
=
-10(5r-1)(2r-1)\ge0
\]
for \(1/5\le r\le1/3\). Again using \(P(a)>2/9\), it is enough to set
\[
s=\frac12+\frac{3r}{2}.
\]
The difference from the target is then
\[
\left(\frac12+\frac{3r}{2}\right)P(a)
+\frac{3r}{2}a^2
-\frac{3+5r}{9}.
\tag{37}
\]
This is affine in \(r\), and its coefficient is
\[
\frac32\bigl(P(a)+a^2\bigr)-\frac59
\ge
\frac32\cdot\frac59-\frac59
=
\frac5{18}>0.
\]
Thus its minimum for \(r\ge1/5\) occurs at \(r=1/5\). With \(u=2-3a\),
\[
\frac45P(a)+\frac3{10}a^2-\frac49
=
\frac{2u^3+9u^2-12u+4}{90}
=
\frac{2u^3+(3u-2)^2}{90}
\ge0.
\tag{38}
\]
This proves (6) on (8).

Accepted fact `e27aad4aa20bc698` identifies its exact uncovered regions as follows: for \(0<r\le1/5\), every uncovered point lies in (7); for \(1/5<r<r_*\), every uncovered point lies in (8), where \(r_*<1/3\); and no uncovered point remains for \(r\ge r_*\). Hence (6) covers all of its open gaps.

Finally, throughout \(\mathcal D\), one has \(s>1-r\). Therefore
\[
1+s>\frac{2(1+r+s)}9,
\qquad
s+r>\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\]
On points already certified by accepted fact `e27aad4aa20bc698`, its previous envelope reaches the target. On every remaining point, (2) and (6) do so. This proves (9).
