## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system of accepted facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2`, `13c0df55864b79eb`, and `64760c76cadff481`.  Thus \(P_1\) is a nondegenerate triangle in \(\{x_1+x_2+x_3=1\}\), its three vertices are the columns of
\[
L=(\ell_{ij})_{i,j=1}^3,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_{ij}=1\quad(j=1,2,3),
\qquad \det L\ne0,
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
c_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\ge0\}|,
\qquad
s_i:=\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_{ij}=3(g_1)_i.
\tag{2}
\]
The endpoint areas are \(A<B\),
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9>\frac12,
\qquad
\kappa:=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49,
\qquad
\beta:=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9<\frac{4A}{9},
\tag{3}
\]
and every row satisfies
\[
Au_i+c_i+Bv_i<t.
\tag{4}
\]
In fact \(t<1\).  The incidence sets \(I_0,I_1\) are disjoint, and, after a simultaneous coordinate relabeling, they are exactly
\[
\mathcal O_{11}:(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2\}),
\quad
\mathcal O_{12}:(I_0,I_1)=(\{1\},\{2,3\}),
\quad
\mathcal O_{21}:(I_0,I_1)=(\{1,2\},\{3\}).
\tag{5}
\]
The exact mean conditions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\mathcal O_{11}&s_2>3,\ s_1,s_3<0,\\
\mathcal O_{12}&s_1<0,\ s_2,s_3\ge0,\ s_1+s_2+s_3=3,\\
\mathcal O_{21}&s_3>3,\ s_1,s_2<0.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, (4) and the accepted cross-cap bounds give
\[
i\in I_0:\quad c_i+Bv_i<\kappa\quad\hbox{and hence }c_i<\kappa,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
i\in I_1:\quad Au_i+Bv_i<t-c_i\le t-\frac49=\beta,
\quad \frac49\le c_i<t,
\tag{8}
\]
while \(c_i<t\) for every remaining row.  The column identities also give the exact cover inequality
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge1.
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose first that all nine entries of \(L\) are nonzero, and put
\[
J_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}>0\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Every \(J_i\) is a proper subset of \(\{1,2,3\}\); \(J_i\ne\varnothing\) for \(i\in I_1\).  For either singleton-\(I_1\) orbit, let \(h\) be its unique \(I_1\)-row and let \(a,b\) be the other two rows.  Then the exact support criterion is
\[
\{1,2,3\}\setminus J_h\subseteq J_a\cup J_b,
\qquad
J_h\nsubseteq J_a\cap J_b.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), the exact criterion is
\[
J_1\subsetneq\{1,2,3\},
\quad
\varnothing\ne J_2,J_3\subsetneq\{1,2,3\},
\quad
J_1\cup J_2\cup J_3=\{1,2,3\}.
\tag{12}
\]
These criteria are necessary and sufficient for a matrix satisfying (1) and the strict/interior versions of the mean signs in (6).  They are a support-level statement: endpoint compatibility and (4), (7), (8), and (9) remain as exact inequalities inside the listed chambers and may exclude some of them.

The allowed group is a common permutation of the three vertex columns together with a coordinate permutation preserving the canonical pair \((I_0,I_1)\).  Thus the row stabilizer is trivial for \(\mathcal O_{11}\), is generated by the swap \(2\leftrightarrow3\) for \(\mathcal O_{12}\), and is generated by \(1\leftrightarrow2\) for \(\mathcal O_{21}\).  There are no independent rowwise vertex permutations.  Under exactly this group, the exhaustive representative lists are as follows.

(p,-a,-b),\qquad p,a,b>0,

c=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}.
\tag{18}

(-d,x,y),\qquad d,x,y>0,

c=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)}.

p\ge a+b,qquad p^2<t(p+a)(p+b),

x+y\ge d,qquad (1-t)(d+x)(d+y)<d^2.

In \(\mathcal O_{12}\), equality in either displayed mean inequality is retained subject to (27)-(28) below.  A two-positive \(I_1\)-row always has \(c>1/2\), which is the new support type that cannot occur under the \(t\le1/2\) hypothesis used in accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791`.

p<a+b,qquad p^2<\kappa(p+a)(p+b),

p<a+b,qquad p^2<t(p+a)(p+b),

d>x+y,qquad (1-t)(d+x)(d+y)<d^2.

In all cases the stronger full inequality (4), and (7) or (8) when applicable, remains imposed.  Equal negative values \(a=b\), equal positive values \(x=y\), the \(\mathcal O_{12}\) mean equalities \(p=a+b\) and \(x+y=d\), and every mixed-column equality forced by

are retained.  In particular, if both other entries in column \(j\) are positive then \(\ell_{hj}<1\); if both are negative then \(\ell_{hj}>1\); in a mixed column either inequality, and the boundary \(\ell_{hj}=1\), is possible.

## Proof

Accepted fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` supplies (1), the transformed triangles, the strict rows (4), and \(t<1\).  Parts 1 and 2 of accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` supply (5)-(8), including the singleton strengthening in (6).  Only these above-half-valid parts are used.  Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` supplies the exact closed triangle-cap formulas, including all repeated and zero projection values.  Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` is used only as the stated below-half comparison: its five-chamber argument assumes \(t\le1/2\), so none of its support exclusions is imported here.

For every \(\lambda\in\Delta\), (1) gives
\[
\sum_i L_{i*}\lambda=1.
\]
Hence at least one row value is positive, proving both the vertex support cover and (9).  Since \(t<1\), a row with three positive entries has \(c_i=1\) and is impossible.  A row in \(I_1\) cannot have three negative entries because its mean is nonnegative.  This proves the basic support-cardinality assertions.

For a nonzero row with one positive entry, sorting it as in (18) and applying the unique-positive branch of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives (18).  Its mean has the sign of \(p-a-b\).  For a row with two positive entries, the same fact gives (19), and its mean has the sign of \(x+y-d\).  Substitution of these two formulas into \(c<t\) or \(c<\kappa\) gives (20)-(25) by reversible multiplication by positive denominators.  If \(x+y\ge d\), then
\[
(d+x)(d+y)=d^2+d(x+y)+xy>2d^2,
\]
so (19) gives \(c>1/2\).  If \(p<a+b\), then
\[
(p+a)(p+b)=p^2+p(a+b)+ab>2p^2,
\]
so (18) gives \(c<1/2\).  This proves every asserted cap and mean inequality, including why the two-positive \(I_1\) branch is genuinely above-half.

We next prove the support criteria exactly.  Consider first a singleton \(I_1\)-row \(h\).  Since \(s_h>3\), if \(j\notin J_h\), then
\[
\ell_{aj}+\ell_{bj}=1-\ell_{hj}>1,
\]
so at least one of the other entries is positive.  This proves the first condition in (11).  If every \(j\in J_h\) belonged to \(J_a\cap J_b\), then \(\ell_{hj}<1\) at every positive position of row \(h\).  Since \(|J_h|\le2\) and all its other entries are negative, this would give \(s_h<2\), contradicting \(s_h>3\).  This proves the second condition.

Conversely, the two conditions in (11) are sufficient at the middle sign-and-mean level.  For each column, first put a positive total (1) on its prescribed positive positions and zero on its prescribed negative positions, using a positive amount at every prescribed positive position.  Replace every placeholder at a prescribed negative position by a small negative number \(-\varepsilon\) and add the same total amount back to positive positions in that column.  This gives the required strict signs and column sums.  A transfer in a column from a negative row \(n\) to a positive row \(p\),
\[
(\ell_{nj},\ell_{pj})\longmapsto
(\ell_{nj}-M,\ell_{pj}+M),
\tag{33}
\]
preserves the column sum and all signs.  The second condition in (11) supplies a direct transfer edge from one of \(a,b\) to \(h\).  If the other row also has such an edge, drain both rows directly into \(h\).  Otherwise that other row is positive at every positive position of \(h\).  It has a negative position because its mean must be negative; that position lies outside \(J_h\), and the first condition in (11) makes the first row positive there.  Thus there is a two-edge path from the other row through the first row to \(h\).  Taking transfers successively, and taking the last one larger than the accumulated incoming amount, makes both non-\(I_1\) row sums negative.  The total row sum is \(3\), so \(s_h>3\).  This proves sufficiency and (11).

For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), necessity of (12) follows from the already proved properness, nonemptiness for \(I_1\), and the column cover.  Conversely, start with the same small-\(\varepsilon\) column construction.  Row \(1\) has a negative position because \(J_1\) is proper; by the column cover, one of rows \(2,3\) is positive there.  One transfer (33), taken sufficiently large, makes \(s_1<0\) while leaving both \(s_2,s_3>0\).  This proves sufficiency of (12).  If, say, \(s_2=0\), then \(s_3=3-s_1>3\).  Applying the singleton necessity argument to row \(3\) proves (27).  Under (27), the same transfer-path construction drains row \(2\) exactly to zero, drains row \(1\) strictly below zero, and sends the remainder to row \(3\); hence (27) is also sufficient.  This proves (27)-(28), and total row sum \(3\) excludes simultaneous equality.

The strict sign and strict mean conditions used in both constructions define a nonempty relatively open subset of the six-dimensional affine space of matrices satisfying the three column identities.  The determinant is not the zero polynomial on that affine space, since the identity matrix belongs to it and has determinant one.  Hence no such relatively open sign cell is contained in \(\det L=0\), and each contains a matrix with \(\det L\ne0\).  The same argument applies inside either mean-equality hyperplane used in (27)-(28): for example,
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
1&-1&0\\
-1&2&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]
has column sums one, second-row sum zero, and determinant \(-1\); swapping rows \(2,3\) handles the other hyperplane.  This completes the claimed support-level sufficiency with all of (1).

It remains to audit the quotient.  Encode a subset of vertices by its three-bit mask, ordered
\[
\varnothing<\{1\}<\{2\}<\{1,2\}<\{3\}<\{1,3\}<\{2,3\}.
\tag{34}
\]
For a singleton \(I_1\)-row, first use a vertex permutation to normalize \(J_h\) to \(\{1\}\) or \(\{1,2\}\), and then select the lexicographically least remaining pair under the residual vertex stabilizer and the allowed row stabilizer.  For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), select the lexicographically least full triple under its order-\(12\) group.  With \(|J_h|=1\), of the \(7^2=49\) ordered proper-subset pairs \((J_a,J_b)\), covering vertices \(2,3\) leaves \(49-16-16+4=21\), and exactly two of these have both sets containing vertex \(1\); hence (11) leaves \(19\).  The residual column transposition \(2\leftrightarrow3\) fixes exactly the five pairs obtained from the invariant proper subsets \(\varnothing,\{1\},\{2,3\}\), so Burnside gives \((19+5)/2=12\), exactly (13).  If \(|J_h|=2\), normalize \(J_h=\{1,2\}\).  Covering vertex \(3\) leaves \(49-16=33\) raw pairs; the second condition in (11) is then automatic because both other rows are proper.  The residual transposition \(1\leftrightarrow2\) again fixes five, giving \((33+5)/2=19\), exactly (14).

For \(\mathcal O_{21}\), the additional swap of the two \(I_0\)-rows commutes with the residual column transposition.  For \(|J_3|=1\), the four fixed-point counts for identity, column swap, row swap, and both swaps are (19,5,1,3), giving
\[
\frac{19+5+1+3}{4}=7.
\]
For \(|J_3|=2\), they are (33,5,3,3), giving
\[
\frac{33+5+3+3}{4}=11.
\]
These are precisely (16)-(17).

For \(\mathcal O_{12}\), before quotienting there are exactly (150) triples satisfying (12).  Indeed, there are \(7\cdot6\cdot6=252\) choices before requiring the column cover; inclusion-exclusion over a missing vertex gives
\[
252-3(4\cdot3\cdot3)+3(2\cdot1\cdot1)=150.
\tag{35}
\]
For the action of the order-\(12\) group, without swapping rows \(2,3\), the fixed counts are \(150\) for the identity, \(8\) for each of the three column transpositions, and \(0\) for each three-cycle.  For a fixed column transposition, the invariant proper subsets are \(\varnothing\) and the two nontrivial unions of its vertex orbits; requiring both \(I_1\)-sets to be nonempty and the union to cover gives the count \(8\).  With the row swap and the identity column permutation, equality \(J_2=J_3\) gives \(3+9=12\) choices according as their common support has size one or two.  With the row swap and a column transposition, the three possible invariant \(J_1\)-sets contribute \(2,5,3\) choices, giving \(10\).  A three-cycle fixes none because it has no nonempty proper invariant subset, and the row-swap composition would force invariance under its square.  Thus the row-swap fixed counts are \(12,10,10,10,0,0\).  Therefore Burnside gives
\[
\frac{150+3\cdot8+12+3\cdot10}{12}=18.
\tag{36}
\]
Direct selection by (34) gives exactly the (18) distinct triples in (15).  The same fixed-point calculation proves both exhaustion and absence of accidental duplicates in all three tables.

Let \(L\) now be an actual surviving matrix with at least one zero entry.  Definitions (29) give a unique weak-sign triple before quotienting.  The column sum \(1\) forces every column to contain a positive entry.  Since \(\det L\ne0\), no row is identically zero; since \(c_i<t<1\), no row can be nonnegative, proving \(N_i\ne\varnothing\).  A nonnegative-mean row with a negative entry must also have a positive entry, proving the asserted \(I_1\) condition.

For a singleton \(I_1\)-row \(h\), if \(j\notin P_h\), then \(\ell_{hj}\le0\), so
\[
\ell_{aj}+\ell_{bj}=1-\ell_{hj}\ge1
\]
and at least one other entry is positive.  This proves the first condition in (30).  If no \(j\in P_h\) belonged to \(N_a\cup N_b\), both other entries would be nonnegative in every positive position of row \(h\), giving \(\ell_{hj}\le1\) there.  Because \(N_h\ne\varnothing\), one has \(|P_h|\le2\), and hence \(s_h\le |P_h|\le2\), contradicting \(s_h>3\).  This proves the second condition in (30).  The same argument applies to the row \(h\) in \(\mathcal O_{12}\) when the other \(I_1\)-row has mean zero, and proves (31).

Sorting the three entries and applying the repeated-value and threshold-equality branches of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` gives (32).  In particular, with one positive \(p\), one negative \(-a\), and one zero, the cap is \(p/(p+a)\); a nonconstant nonpositive row has cap area zero; and a nonnegative row has full closed cap.  Thus every actual zero-entry system lies in the stated finite weak-sign over-index and carries the exact cap formula and all numerical conditions displayed above.  The proof makes no perturbation or transfer argument on a fixed-zero affine stratum and makes no claim that an indexed weak-sign pattern contains a nonsingular matrix; instead, \(\det L\ne0\) remains an explicit condition on every actual boundary stratum.
