## Statement

Let \(N=\{1,2,3\}\), and let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be nonconstant affine
functions on a common plane such that
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1.
\]
For every nonempty \(G\subseteq N\), use the exact middle-cell convention
\[
\mathcal C_G=
\{x:f_i(x)\leq0\ (i\in G),\ f_i(x)>0\ (i\notin G)\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2\) be fixed two-dimensional convex bodies, put
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},
\]
and let \(u_i,v_i\) be their fixed endpoint cap contributions.  For a
measurable middle body \(X\), write
\[
c_i(X)=|X\cap\{f_i\leq0\}|.
\]
Define the three crossing residuals at \(B\) by
\[
\rho_i=u_i+v_i+c_i(B)-\frac{2(M+m+|B|)}9.
\]

Let \(K\) be any two-dimensional compact convex body with \(B\subseteq K\),
and, in the column order
\[
1,2,3,12,13,23,123,
\]
put
\[
z_G=|(K\setminus B)\cap\mathcal C_G|
=|K\cap\mathcal C_G|-|B\cap\mathcal C_G|\geq0.
\]
Then the three crossing equalities
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i(K)=\frac{2(M+m+|K|)}9
\qquad(i=1,2,3)
\]
hold if and only if
\[
\mathsf A z=-9\rho,\qquad z\geq0,
\]
where
\[
\mathsf A=
\begin{pmatrix}
7&-2&-2&7&7&-2&7\\
-2&7&-2&7&-2&7&7\\
-2&-2&7&-2&7&7&7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular, every such crossing extension necessarily satisfies, with
\(\Gamma=\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3\),
\[
\widehat z_i=-\rho_i-\frac23\Gamma\geq0
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
More exactly, its seven added cell areas obey
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat z_1\\
\widehat z_2\\
\widehat z_3
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&5/3&5/3&2/3&7/3\\
0&1&0&5/3&2/3&5/3&7/3\\
0&0&1&2/3&5/3&5/3&7/3
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
z_1\\z_2\\z_3\\z_{12}\\z_{13}\\z_{23}\\z_{123}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For a fixed residual vector \(\rho\), the abstract system
\(\mathsf A z=-9\rho,\ z\geq0\) is feasible if and only if all three
\(\widehat z_i\) are nonnegative.  This last equivalence is only an algebraic
cell-area statement and does not assert the existence of a convex
\(K\supseteq B\) realizing the chosen areas.  No middle cell is assumed to
have positive area, and all zero-cell, equality, and cutting-line boundary
cases are included.

## Proof

For each point \(x\), the set
\[
G(x)=\{i:f_i(x)\leq0\}
\]
is nonempty, because \(f_1(x)+f_2(x)+f_3(x)=-1\).  The seven sets
\(\mathcal C_G\), \(G\ne\varnothing\), therefore form a disjoint exact
partition of the plane.  Consequently
\[
|K|-|B|=\sum_{G\ne\varnothing}z_G
\]
and, for each \(i\),
\[
c_i(K)-c_i(B)=\sum_{G\ni i}z_G.
\]
Substituting these two identities and the definition of \(\rho_i\) into the
\(i\)-th crossing equality gives
\[
0=\rho_i+\sum_{G\ne\varnothing}
\left(\mathbf 1_{\{i\in G\}}-\frac29\right)z_G.
\]
Multiplication by \(9\) shows that the column belonging to \(G\) is exactly
\[
a_G=9\mathbf 1_G-2\mathbf 1.
\]
Thus the seven columns, in the stated order, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
G&1&2&3&12&13&23&123\\ \hline
a_G&
(7,-2,-2)&(-2,7,-2)&(-2,-2,7)&
(7,7,-2)&(7,-2,7)&(-2,7,7)&(7,7,7),
\end{array}
\]
which is precisely \(\mathsf A z=-9\rho\).  This also checks directly the
factor \(9\): membership in a cap contributes \(1-2/9=7/9\), while
nonmembership contributes \(-2/9\).

The singleton columns form
\[
\mathsf S=9I-2\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its eigenvalue is \(9\) on \(\mathbf 1^\perp\) and \(3\) on
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), so it is invertible and
\[
\mathsf S^{-1}
=\frac19I+\frac2{27}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathsf S^{-1}(-9\rho)
=-\rho-\frac23(\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_3)\mathbf 1
=\widehat z.
\]
Equivalently, the three Farkas multipliers are
\[
w_1=\frac1{27}(5,2,2),\qquad
w_2=\frac1{27}(2,5,2),\qquad
w_3=\frac1{27}(2,2,5).
\]
They are the rows of \(\mathsf S^{-1}\), and direct multiplication against
all seven columns gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
w_1^{\mathsf T}\\w_2^{\mathsf T}\\w_3^{\mathsf T}
\end{pmatrix}
\mathsf A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&5/3&5/3&2/3&7/3\\
0&1&0&5/3&2/3&5/3&7/3\\
0&0&1&2/3&5/3&5/3&7/3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus a singleton cell contributes \(1\) to its own effective coefficient
and \(0\) to the other two.  A pair cell contributes \(5/3\) at each of its
two indices and \(2/3\) at the remaining index.  The central cell contributes
\(7/3\) at every index.  Every displayed coefficient is nonnegative.
Therefore, whenever \(\mathsf A z=-9\rho\) and \(z\geq0\),
\[
\widehat z_i=w_i^{\mathsf T}(-9\rho)
=w_i^{\mathsf T}\mathsf A z\geq0.
\]
This proves the universal Farkas necessity, including equality.

Conversely, suppose only that the three numbers \(\widehat z_i\) are
nonnegative.  In the abstract seven-variable system, set
\[
z_i=\widehat z_i\quad(i=1,2,3),\qquad
z_{12}=z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{123}=0.
\]
Then
\[
\mathsf A z=\mathsf S\widehat z=-9\rho.
\]
This proves exact feasibility of the nonnegative linear system.  It does not
construct a planar region, and convexity or the location of \(B\) can prevent
these abstract singleton areas from being realized by any actual extension.

Finally, every cutting set \(\{f_i=0\}\) is a line and has planar area zero.
The weak-versus-strict convention already assigns every boundary point to one
exact cell, and changing that assignment would not alter any area identity,
even if an edge of \(B\) or \(K\) lies on a cutting line.  If an ambient cell
is empty, or if its intersection with \(K\setminus B\) has zero area, the
corresponding \(z_G\) is simply zero and the same calculations apply.  If
\(\widehat z_i=0\), the nonnegative column formula remains valid and forces
every term with a positive coefficient in its \(i\)-th row to vanish; no
strict inequality was used.  In the extreme case \(K=B\), all seven
\(z_G\) vanish, and the crossings hold exactly when \(\rho=0\), in which
case all three gate inequalities hold with equality.  This covers all zero
and boundary cases without a general-position assumption.
