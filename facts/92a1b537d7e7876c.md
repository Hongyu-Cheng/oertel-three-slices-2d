## Statement

Under all hypotheses and definitions of accepted fact
`bc026780453747b8`, put
\[
r_\pm=\frac{87\pm3\sqrt{65}}{388}.
\tag{1}
\]
There are translations \(u,t\in\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
u+r_-P\subseteq Q\subseteq t+r_+P.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(m_x(P)\) and \(m_y(P)\) be the maximum lengths of horizontal and
vertical chords of \(P\), and put
\[
w_Q=\max_Qx-\min_Qx,\qquad
h_Q=\max_Qy-\min_Qy.
\]
Then the two individual aspect bounds are
\[
\frac{w_Q}{\ell_x}
\leq
\frac{r_+}{r_-}\frac{w_P}{m_x(P)},
\qquad
\frac{h_Q}{\ell_y}
\leq
\frac{r_+}{r_-}\frac{h_P}{m_y(P)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{w_Q}{\ell_x}+\frac{h_Q}{\ell_y}
\leq
\frac{r_+}{r_-}
\left(
\frac{w_P}{m_x(P)}+\frac{h_P}{m_y(P)}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]
The inner translation in (2) can be chosen with
\[
u_x,u_y\geq0,
\tag{5}
\]
and it gives the southwest-anchor estimate
\[
\left|\operatorname{conv}(0,P)\right|
\leq
\frac{9}{200r_-^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

For planar convex bodies \(C,D\), use the mixed-area normalization
\[
|C+\lambda D|
=|C|+2\lambda V(C,D)+\lambda^2|D|
\qquad(\lambda\geq0).
\tag{7}
\]
We first prove the relative Bonnesen inequality needed below.

Let \(A,B\) be compact planar convex bodies with positive areas, and
define their relative inradius by
\[
\rho(A,B)
=
\max\{\rho:\text{a translate of }\rho B\text{ is contained in }A\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
2\rho(A,B)V(A,B)
\geq
|A|+\rho(A,B)^2|B|.
\tag{9}
\]

Translate \(B\) so that \(0\in\operatorname{int}B\); this changes
neither side of (9) nor the value in (8).  First suppose that \(A\) is
a polygon.  For \(0\leq\lambda\leq\rho(A,B)\), let
\[
K_\lambda
=A\mathbin{\ominus}\lambda B
:=\{z:z+\lambda B\subseteq A\}.
\tag{10}
\]
If
\[
A=\bigcap_{i=1}^N
\{x:\langle n_i,x\rangle\leq h_i\},
\]
with unit outer normals \(n_i\), then
\[
K_\lambda
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^N
\{x:\langle n_i,x\rangle
\leq h_i-\lambda h_B(n_i)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(0\leq\lambda<\rho(A,B)\), the set \(K_\lambda\) has nonempty
interior: a contained translate of \(\rho(A,B)B\) leaves a translate
of \((\rho(A,B)-\lambda)B\) inside the translation set in (10).

There are only finitely many values of \(\lambda\) at which the active
edge set in (11) changes.  Between consecutive such values, the edge
lengths vary linearly and the elementary first-variation formula for
moving polygonal support lines gives
\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}|K_\lambda|
=
-\sum_i
\bigl(\text{length of the \(i\)-th active edge}\bigr)h_B(n_i)
=-2V(K_\lambda,B).
\tag{12}
\]
The last equality is the polygonal mixed-area formula in the
normalization (7).  Thus (12) holds except at finitely many values,
which is sufficient for integration.

Definition (10) gives
\[
K_\lambda+\lambda B\subseteq A.
\]
Mixed area is monotone under inclusion: if \(C\subseteq D\), then
\[
0\leq |D+\tau B|-|C+\tau B|
=|D|-|C|+2\tau\bigl(V(D,B)-V(C,B)\bigr)
\]
for every \(\tau\geq0\), so the coefficient of \(\tau\) cannot be
negative.  This monotonicity and bilinearity therefore give
\[
V(K_\lambda,B)+\lambda|B|
=V(K_\lambda+\lambda B,B)
\leq V(A,B).
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (12) and (13), at every differentiability value,
\[
\frac{d}{d\lambda}|K_\lambda|
\geq
-2V(A,B)+2\lambda|B|.
\]
Integration from \(0\) to \(\lambda\) yields
\[
|K_\lambda|
\geq
|A|-2\lambda V(A,B)+\lambda^2|B|.
\tag{14}
\]

At \(r=\rho(A,B)\), the compact translation body \(K_r\) has area
zero.  Indeed, if it had positive area, it would have nonempty
interior.  Some \(y+\varepsilon B\) would then lie in \(K_r\), because
\(0\in\operatorname{int}B\).  For every \(z\in\varepsilon B\), the
definition of \(K_r\) gives
\[
y+z+rB\subseteq A.
\]
Taking the union over \(z\in\varepsilon B\) and using
\(\varepsilon B+rB=(r+\varepsilon)B\) would give
\[
y+(r+\varepsilon)B\subseteq A,
\]
contrary to maximality of \(r\).  Letting
\(\lambda\uparrow r\) in (14) and using \(|K_r|=0\) proves (9) when
\(A\) is a polygon.

For a general compact convex \(A\), choose nested outer polygons
\[
A_1\supseteq A_2\supseteq\cdots,\qquad
\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}A_j=A,
\]
converging to \(A\) in Hausdorff distance.  Put
\(r_j=\rho(A_j,B)\).  Then \(r_j\) decreases to \(\rho(A,B)\).
To check the only nonautomatic direction, choose translations
\(z_j+r_jB\subseteq A_j\).  Since \(0\in B\), the sequence \(z_j\)
lies in the fixed compact set \(A_1\).  Along a convergent
subsequence, \(z_j\to z\) and \(r_j\to r_\infty\), and nestedness gives
\[
z+r_\infty B\subseteq\bigcap_jA_j=A.
\]
Thus \(r_\infty\leq\rho(A,B)\), while the reverse inequality follows
from \(A\subseteq A_j\).  Hence equality holds.  Area and mixed area
are continuous under Hausdorff convergence, so passing to the limit
in (9) for \(A_j\) proves (9) for all compact convex \(A,B\).

Return to \(P,Q\), and put
\[
p=|P|=\frac{97}{100},\qquad
q=|Q|=\frac9{200},\qquad
v=V(P,Q).
\tag{15}
\]
Accepted fact `bc026780453747b8` gives
\[
|P+Q|<\frac{29}{20}.
\]
Equations (7) and (15) therefore give
\[
\sqrt{pq}\leq v<\frac{87}{400},
\tag{16}
\]
where the first inequality is planar Minkowski inequality.

Let \(r=\rho(Q,P)\).  Applying (9) with \(A=Q,B=P\) gives
\[
pr^2-2vr+q\leq0.
\tag{17}
\]
The area of a contained homothet gives \(r^2p\leq q\).  Hence \(r\)
lies between the smaller root of (17) and the geometric mean
\(\sqrt{q/p}\) of its two roots.  Thus
\[
r\geq
\frac{v-\sqrt{v^2-pq}}p.
\tag{18}
\]
The right side of (18) is continuous at \(v=\sqrt{pq}\) and strictly
decreasing thereafter.  Direct calculation gives
\[
\sqrt{
\left(\frac{87}{400}\right)^2-pq
}
=\frac{3\sqrt{65}}{400}.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict upper bound in (16) and equations (18)-(19) imply
\[
r>r_-.
\tag{20}
\]
Choose \(a+rP\subseteq Q\) and any \(p_0\in P\).  Convexity gives
\[
(r-r_-)p_0+r_-P\subseteq rP.
\]
Thus
\[
u=a+(r-r_-)p_0
\]
satisfies \(u+r_-P\subseteq Q\), proving the first containment in
(2).

Let \(s=\rho(P,Q)\).  Reversing \(P,Q\) in (9) gives
\[
qs^2-2vs+p\leq0,\qquad s^2q\leq p,
\]
and hence
\[
s\geq
\frac{v-\sqrt{v^2-pq}}q.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(c+sQ\subseteq P\), then
\[
Q\subseteq-\frac cs+\frac1sP.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (16), (19), and (21) give
\[
\frac1s
\leq
\frac{q}{v-\sqrt{v^2-pq}}
=
\frac{v+\sqrt{v^2-pq}}p
<r_+.
\tag{23}
\]
Write \(R=1/s\) and \(a=-c/s\), so (22) is \(Q\subseteq a+RP\).
For any \(p_0\in P\), convexity gives
\[
RP+(r_+-R)p_0\subseteq r_+P.
\]
Therefore
\[
t=a-(r_+-R)p_0
\]
satisfies \(Q\subseteq t+r_+P\), proving the second containment in
(2).

Horizontal and vertical chord lengths and coordinate projection
widths are translation invariant and scale linearly under homothety.
The inner containment in (2) therefore gives
\[
\ell_x\geq r_-m_x(P),\qquad
\ell_y\geq r_-m_y(P),
\tag{24}
\]
while the outer containment gives
\[
w_Q\leq r_+w_P,\qquad
h_Q\leq r_+h_P.
\tag{25}
\]
Division proves the two inequalities in (3), and their sum proves
(4).

It remains to prove the signs and the anchor bound.  Positivity of the
negative-positive and positive-positive \(A_0\) cells gives a segment
of \(A_0\) crossing the positive \(y\)-axis.  Hence
\(\min_Px=0\).  The analogous two cells across the \(x\)-axis give
\(\min_Py=0\).

Positivity of all four \(A_2\) cells implies
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}A_2.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, otherwise a supporting or strictly separating line through
the origin would place \(A_2\) in one closed halfplane.  For every
nonzero normal, the opposite open coordinate quadrant lies strictly
in the other halfplane, contradicting positivity of that cell.  Thus
\(0\in Q\).

Choose the first translation in (2) and write it as
\[
u+r_-P\subseteq Q.
\]
Since \(Q\) lies in the positive quadrant and both coordinate minima
of \(P\) are zero, necessarily \(u_x,u_y\geq0\), proving (5).  Put
\[
z=-\frac{u}{r_-},
\]
so \(z_x,z_y\leq0\).  Convexity of \(Q\), together with \(0\in Q\),
gives
\[
\operatorname{conv}(0,u+r_-P)\subseteq Q.
\]
The affine map \(x\mapsto(x-u)/r_-\) sends the left side to
\(\operatorname{conv}(z,P)\).  Hence
\[
r_-^2|\operatorname{conv}(z,P)|
\leq |Q|=\frac9{200}.
\tag{27}
\]

The same cell-crossing argument used above supplies points
\[
E=(e,0)\in P,\qquad F=(0,f)\in P
\]
with \(e,f>0\).  Write \(z=(-\alpha,-\beta)\), where
\(\alpha,\beta\geq0\).  With
\[
\lambda=
\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{e}+\frac{\beta}{f}\right)^{-1},
\quad
\mu=\frac{\lambda\alpha}{e},
\quad
\nu=\frac{\lambda\beta}{f},
\]
the three nonnegative coefficients sum to \(1\) and satisfy
\[
\lambda z+\mu E+\nu F=0.
\]
Thus \(0\in\operatorname{conv}(z,P)\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{conv}(0,P)
\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}(z,P).
\]
Combining this inclusion with (27) proves (6).
