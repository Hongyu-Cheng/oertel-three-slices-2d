## Statement

Assume the common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`.  Thus \(N=\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,\qquad L_1+L_2+L_3<-1,
\qquad \frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\]
where \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) are nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes.  Define the exact cells \(P_J,Q_H,R_G\), their areas \(p_J,q_H,r_G\), and
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad \kappa=|K|,
\]
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
v_i=\sum_{H\ni i}q_H,\qquad
c_i=\sum_{G\ni i}r_G
\]
as in accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c`.  Assume the three crossing equalities
\[
u_i+v_i+c_i=t,\qquad
t=\frac{2(M+m+\kappa)}9
\qquad(i\in N).
\tag{1}
\]
No separate hypothesis \(W=0\) is imposed.

Fix \(j\in N\), write \(N\setminus\{j\}=\{k,\ell\}\), and put
\[
\delta=-1-(L_1+L_2+L_3)>0,\qquad D=\delta+2.
\]
Use the affine normalization of accepted fact `03ead32fab5dad16`:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
p_k=\alpha_k-r,&p_\ell=\alpha_\ell+w-r,
&p_j=2r-w-2\alpha_j,\\
q_k=\beta_k-r,&q_\ell=\beta_\ell+w-r,
&q_j=2r-w-2\beta_j,
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
\alpha_k+\alpha_\ell-2\alpha_j=\delta,\qquad
\beta_k+\beta_\ell-2\beta_j=-D.
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(\gamma_i=(\alpha_i+\beta_i)/2\), so that
\[
\gamma_k+\gamma_\ell-2\gamma_j=-1.
\tag{4}
\]
Area is normalized by the common positive Jacobian of this change.

For \(X\in\{A_0,A_2,K\}\), write
\[
\pi_w(X)=J_X=[s_X,s_X+h_X],\qquad
X_w=\{r:(r,w)\in X\}=[a_X(w),b_X(w)],
\]
where
\[
h_{A_0}=h_0,\qquad h_{A_2}=h_2,\qquad h_K=h.
\]
For \(\eta=(\eta_j,\eta_k,\eta_\ell)\), set
\[
x_{\eta,k}(w)=\eta_k,\qquad
x_{\eta,\ell}(w)=\eta_\ell+w,\qquad
x_{\eta,j}(w)=\eta_j+\frac w2,
\]
and define the signed clipping functional
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E(X;\eta)=\int_{J_X}\bigl[&
(x_{\eta,k}-b_X)_+-(a_X-x_{\eta,k})_+\\
&+(x_{\eta,\ell}-b_X)_+-(a_X-x_{\eta,\ell})_+\\
&+2(a_X-x_{\eta,j})_+-2(x_{\eta,j}-b_X)_+
\bigr]\,dw,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
where all section endpoints and zero graphs in the integrand are evaluated at \(w\).  Then arbitrary convex sections satisfy the exact crossing identity
\[
\boxed{\;
\delta h_0-Dh_2-h
-\mathcal E(A_0;\alpha)
-\mathcal E(A_2;\beta)
-\mathcal E(K;\gamma)
=\frac{10}{9}(M+m+\kappa).
\;}
\tag{6}
\]

Now assume sectionwise transversality: for almost every \(w\in J_{A_0}\), all three \(x_{\alpha,i}(w)\) lie strictly between \(a_{A_0}(w)\) and \(b_{A_0}(w)\); the analogous assertion holds for \((A_2,\beta)\) and \((K,\gamma)\).  Assume also that the exact positive \(Q\)-support and the almost-everywhere order on \(J_{A_2}\) are either
\[
\{j\},\{j,k\},\{k\},\{k,\ell\},
\qquad
\beta_k<\beta_j+\frac w2<\beta_\ell+w
\tag{7o}
\]
or
\[
\{j\},\{j,k\},N,\{k,\ell\},
\qquad
\beta_k<\beta_\ell+w<\beta_j+\frac w2.
\tag{7c}
\]
In the outer case (7o),
\[
(\delta-38)h_0>(91\delta+190)h_2,
\tag{8o}
\]
and in the central case (7c),
\[
(3\delta-14)h_0>(33\delta+70)h_2.
\tag{8c}
\]
Thus the outer case is impossible if \(\delta\le38\), or if \(\delta>38\) and
\[
\frac{h_0}{h_2}\le
\frac{91\delta+190}{\delta-38};
\]
the central case is impossible if \(\delta\le14/3\), or if \(\delta>14/3\) and
\[
\frac{h_0}{h_2}\le
\frac{33\delta+70}{3\delta-14}.
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` identifies \(u_i,v_i,c_i\) with the areas cut from \(A_0,A_2,K\) by \(p_i\le0,q_i\le0,f_i\le0\), respectively; cutting-line boundaries have area zero.  Accepted fact `03ead32fab5dad16` gives the normalization (2)--(4), while accepted fact `a2a9387275a2c97c` supplies the crossing equations and the value of \(t\).

For an interval \([a,b]\) and a real threshold \(x\),
\[
|[a,b]\cap[x,\infty)|
=b-x+(x-b)_+-(a-x)_+,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
|[a,b]\cap(-\infty,x]|
=x-a+(a-x)_+-(x-b)_+.
\tag{10}
\]
On a section of \(A_0\), the \(k\)- and \(\ell\)-cuts are the parts to the right of \(x_{\alpha,k}\) and \(x_{\alpha,\ell}\), while the \(j\)-cut is the part to the left of \(x_{\alpha,j}\).  Adding (9) for \(k,\ell\) and twice (10) for \(j\), the unclipped terms are
\[
2(b_{A_0}-a_{A_0})
-\bigl(\alpha_k+\alpha_\ell-2\alpha_j\bigr)
=2(b_{A_0}-a_{A_0})-\delta,
\]
and the remaining terms are exactly the integrand in
\(\mathcal E(A_0;\alpha)\).  Fubini's theorem therefore gives
\[
u_k+u_\ell+2u_j
=2M-\delta h_0+\mathcal E(A_0;\alpha).
\tag{11}
\]
The identical calculation using (3) and (4) gives
\[
v_k+v_\ell+2v_j
=2m+Dh_2+\mathcal E(A_2;\beta),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
c_k+c_\ell+2c_j
=2\kappa+h+\mathcal E(K;\gamma).
\tag{13}
\]
The \(1,1,2\) combination of (1) is \(4t\).  Substituting
(11)--(13) and \(4t=8(M+m+\kappa)/9\) proves (6).  In particular,
(5) is the exact obstruction outside the sectionwise-transverse
regime; no projection-width identity alone can omit it.  Under
sectionwise transversality every positive-part term in (5) vanishes,
so (6) reduces to
\[
\delta h_0-Dh_2-h=\frac{10}{9}(M+m+\kappa).
\tag{14}
\]

We next prove the shape-independent replacement for the rectangular
width calculation.  Write
\[
J_{A_0}=[s_0,s_0+h_0],\qquad
J_{A_2}=[s_2,s_2+h_2],
\]
and let
\[
\lambda_0(w)=b_{A_0}(w)-a_{A_0}(w),\qquad
\lambda_2(w)=b_{A_2}(w)-a_{A_2}(w).
\]
For \(0\le\tau\le1\), set
\[
w_0=s_0+h_0\tau,\qquad
w_2=s_2+h_2\tau,\qquad
\bar w=\frac{w_0+w_2}{2}.
\]
The inclusion \((A_0+A_2)/2\subseteq K\) implies
\[
\frac{(A_0)_{w_0}+(A_2)_{w_2}}2\subseteq K_{\bar w}.
\]
The left side is an interval of length
\((\lambda_0(w_0)+\lambda_2(w_2))/2\).  As \(\tau\) runs from \(0\)
to \(1\), \(\bar w\) runs injectively through an interval of length
\((h_0+h_2)/2\).  Hence
\[
h\ge\frac{h_0+h_2}{2}
\tag{15}
\]
and, after integrating with
\(d\bar w=(h_0+h_2)d\tau/2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa
&\ge\frac{h_0+h_2}{4}
\int_0^1\bigl(\lambda_0(s_0+h_0\tau)
+\lambda_2(s_2+h_2\tau)\bigr)\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{h_0+h_2}{4}
\left(\frac{M}{h_0}+\frac{m}{h_2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This is the required normalized-section functional; no section is
assumed to have constant length.

For almost every \(w\in J_{A_0}\), the three \(\alpha\)-zero
positions lie inside the section and satisfy
\[
x_{\alpha,k}(w)+x_{\alpha,\ell}(w)
-2x_{\alpha,j}(w)=\delta.
\]
At least one of the first two positions is at least \(\delta/2\)
to the right of the third.  Thus
\[
\lambda_0(w)>\frac{\delta}{2}
\quad\hbox{for almost every }w,
\qquad
M>\frac{\delta h_0}{2}.
\tag{17}
\]

In the outer order (7o), the distance from the \(j\)-zero to the
\(k\)-zero minus the distance from the \(\ell\)-zero to the
\(j\)-zero equals \(D\).  Both distances are positive, so the span
of the three zero positions is greater than \(D\).  Sectionwise
transversality gives
\[
m>Dh_2.
\tag{18o}
\]
Equations (16)--(18o) yield
\[
\kappa>
\frac{h_0+h_2}{4}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}+D\right)
=\frac{3\delta+4}{8}(h_0+h_2).
\tag{19o}
\]
Using (14), (15), (17), (18o), and (19o),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\delta-\frac12\right)h_0
-\left(D+\frac12\right)h_2
&\ge \delta h_0-Dh_2-h\\
&=\frac{10}{9}(M+m+\kappa)\\
&>
\frac{10}{9}\left[
\frac{\delta h_0}{2}+Dh_2
+\frac{3\delta+4}{8}(h_0+h_2)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting the last line from the first gives
\[
\frac{(\delta-38)h_0-(91\delta+190)h_2}{36}>0,
\]
which is (8o).

In the central order (7c), if the distances from the rightmost
\(j\)-zero to the \(k\)- and \(\ell\)-zeros are \(d_k>d_\ell>0\),
then \(d_k+d_\ell=D\).  The span is \(d_k>D/2\), so
\[
m>\frac{Dh_2}{2}.
\tag{18c}
\]
Equations (16)--(18c) give
\[
\kappa>
\frac{h_0+h_2}{4}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac D2\right)
=\frac{\delta+1}{4}(h_0+h_2).
\tag{19c}
\]
Now (14), (15), (17), (18c), and (19c) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\delta-\frac12\right)h_0
-\left(D+\frac12\right)h_2
&>
\frac{10}{9}\left[
\frac{\delta h_0}{2}+\frac{Dh_2}{2}
+\frac{\delta+1}{4}(h_0+h_2)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
The difference between the two sides is
\[
\frac{(3\delta-14)h_0-(33\delta+70)h_2}{18},
\]
which proves (8c).  Since \(h_0,h_2>0\), the stated impossibility
ranges follow.
