## Statement

Let \(\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(D\), with all homotheties centered at \(v_0\), and let
\[
A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_0=v_0+\frac9{10}(\Delta-v_0),\qquad
A_2=v_0+\frac12(\Delta-v_0).
\]
Let \(\eta\) be the independent midpoint-mixture measure of accepted fact `1790a353b242874b`:
\[
\eta(E)=|\Delta\cap E|+\frac D4\,\mathbb P(M\in E),
\]
where \(M=(X_0+X_2)/2\), \(X_0,X_2\) are independent and uniform on \(A_0,A_2\). Then
\[
c=\frac25v_0+\frac3{10}v_1+\frac3{10}v_2
\]
satisfies
\[
\inf\{\eta(H):H\subset\mathbb R^2\text{ is a closed halfplane containing }c\}
\ge\frac{106}{225}D
>\frac{103}{225}D.
\]

## Proof

Apply an affine map taking \(v_0,v_1,v_2\) to \(0,e_1,e_2\). It preserves area ratios, halfplanes, and all probabilities below. Thus write
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,e_2\},\qquad
c=\frac3{10}(e_1+e_2).
\]
If \(U,V\) are independent and uniform on \(\Delta\), then
\[
M=\frac9{20}U+\frac14V,
\qquad
g:=\mathbb E M=\frac7{30}(e_1+e_2)=\frac79c.
\tag{1}
\]

We first compute the halfspace depth of the law \(\nu\) of \(M\) at \(g\). Fix a nonconstant linear functional. After adding a constant, multiplying by a positive scalar, and relabeling the vertices, its values on the vertices of \(\Delta\) are
\[
v_0=0,\qquad v_1=t,\qquad v_2=1
\]
for some \(t\in[0,1]\). Let \(R,S\) be its values at \(U,V\). For \(0<t<1\), put
\[
d_i=\prod_{k\ne i}(v_k-v_i).
\]
Direct slicing of the triangle gives
\[
\mathbb P(R\le x)=\sum_{i=0}^2\frac{(x-v_i)_+^2}{d_i},
\qquad
f_R(x)=2\sum_{i=0}^2\frac{(x-v_i)_+}{d_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(a=9/20\), \(b=1/4\), convolution of the densities in (2), using
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(u-A)_+(x-u-B)_+\,du
=\frac{(x-A-B)_+^3}{6},
\]
and then integrating once, yields the exact formula
\[
\mathbb P(aR+bS\le x)
=
\frac1{6a^2b^2}
\sum_{i,j=0}^2
\frac{(x-av_i-bv_j)_+^4}{d_id_j}.
\tag{3}
\]
The centroid threshold is
\[
x_t=(a+b)\mathbb ER=\frac7{30}(1+t).
\]
Write
\[
F(t)=\mathbb P(aR+bS\le x_t).
\]
For \(0\le t\le1/2\), the active knots in (3) change only at \(t=1/14\). Exact expansion gives
\[
F(t)=
\frac{264992-529984t+212295t^2+52697t^3+19208t^4}
     {492075(1-t)^2},
\qquad 0\le t\le\frac1{14},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
F(t)=
\frac{1+529927t-1058736t^2+412438t^3+154786t^4}
     {984150t(1-t)^2},
\qquad \frac1{14}\le t\le\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]
Formula (4) at \(t=0\) is the limiting value of (3).

For \(0\le t\le1/14\), subtracting \(1/2\) and \(F(t)\) from \(5/9\) in (4) gives numerators
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1(t)&=37909(1-2t)-67485t^2+105394t^3+38416t^4,\\
Q_2(t)&=8383(1-2t)+61080t^2-52697t^3-19208t^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
They are positive because
\[
Q_1(t)\ge37909\frac67-\frac{67485}{196}>0
\]
and
\[
Q_2(t)\ge8383\frac67-\frac{52697}{2744}
-\frac{19208}{38416}>0.
\]
Hence \(1/2\le F(t)\le5/9\) on this interval.

For \(1/14\le t\le1/2\), formula (5) gives
\[
F(t)-\frac12
=
\frac{(t-\frac12)
(154786t^3-2244t^2-75708t-2)}
{984150t(1-t)^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since
\[
154786t^3\le77393t^2\le\frac{77393}{2}t,
\]
the second factor in the numerator of (6) is at most
\[
-2-2244t^2-\frac{74023}{2}t<0.
\]
Thus \(F(t)\ge1/2\).

Also,
\[
\frac59-F(t)
=
\frac{-1+16823t-34764t^2+134312t^3-154786t^4}
{984150t(1-t)^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Write the numerator as
\[
-1+t(16823-34764t)+t^3(134312-154786t).
\]
For \(1/14\le t\le12/25\), it is at least
\[
-1+\frac1{14}\frac{3407}{25}>0.
\]
For \(12/25\le t\le1/2\), it is at least
\[
-1-\frac{559}{2}+56919\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^3>0.
\]
Therefore \(F(t)\le5/9\) throughout \([1/14,1/2]\).

Replacing the projected variable by \(1-R\) shows
\[
F(t)=1-F(1-t).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac49\le F(t)\le\frac59
\qquad(0\le t\le1).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every closed halfplane containing \(g\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(4/9\): translate its boundary toward \(g\), if necessary, and apply (8).

It remains to combine this exact convolution bound with the uniform part of \(\eta\). Use barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) on \(\Delta\). At \(c\),
\[
(\lambda_0(c),\lambda_1(c),\lambda_2(c))
=\left(\frac25,\frac3{10},\frac3{10}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
A triangular halfplane cap isolating vertex \(i\) has relative area
\[
\frac{\lambda_j(c)\lambda_k(c)}{r(1-r)},
\qquad
r\in[\lambda_j(c),1-\lambda_k(c)],
\tag{10}
\]
where \(\{i,j,k\}=\{0,1,2\}\). Formula (10) follows by writing the two edge-intercept fractions as
\(\lambda_j(c)/r\) and \(\lambda_k(c)/(1-r)\).

For the cap at \(v_0\), (10) ranges between \(9/25\) and \(3/7\). For caps at \(v_1\) or \(v_2\), it ranges between \(12/25\) and \(4/7\). Their complementary sections have relative area at least \(3/7\). Hence every halfplane through \(c\) cuts from \(\Delta\) area at least \(9D/25\), and every such halfplane not containing \(v_0\) cuts area at least \(12D/25\).

Now let \(H\) be any closed halfplane containing \(c\), and translate its boundary toward \(c\) so that \(c\in\partial H\), which can only decrease \(\eta(H)\).

If \(v_0\notin H\), then
\[
\eta(H)\ge|\Delta\cap H|
\ge\frac{12}{25}D
=\frac{108}{225}D.
\]
If \(v_0\in H\), then (1) gives \(g\in[v_0,c]\subseteq H\), so (8) yields \(\nu(H)\ge4/9\). Therefore
\[
\eta(H)
\ge\frac9{25}D+\frac D4\frac49
=\frac{106}{225}D.
\]
Both cases give the claimed all-halfplane bound, and \(106/225>103/225\).
