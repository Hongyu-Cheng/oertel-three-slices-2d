## Statement

Let \(\mathcal R_n\) be a sequence of full common-plane realizations
satisfying the complete canonical finite-interior hypotheses and exact-cell
convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, \(M\geq m\), the three
crossings, the canonical tails, \(W=0\), the all-double positive
\(Q\)-support
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
and \(r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0\).  Scale areas so that
\[
q_n=1,\qquad x_{1,n}=x_{2,n}=x_{3,n}=\frac5{21},
\qquad m_n=\frac{12}{7},
\]
and suppose
\[
D_n=\frac1{|\mathcal T_{Q,n}|}\longrightarrow0.
\]

For each \(n\), apply one affine map to the whole common plane which puts
\(A_{2,n}\) in John position:
\[
B\subseteq \widetilde A_{2,n}\subseteq2B,
\]
where \(B\) is the closed unit disk.  The map is not required to preserve
area.  Write \(q'_n\) for the transformed central \(Q\)-cell area.  Define
\[
\gamma_n=1-\sum_{\ell=1}^3\Lambda_{\ell,n}>2,\qquad
\alpha_{\ell,n}
=-\frac{f_{\ell,n}+\Lambda_{\ell,n}}{\gamma_n}.
\]
Thus \(\sum_\ell\alpha_{\ell,n}=1\), and the transformed \(Q\)-halfspace
with index \(\ell\) is
\[
H_{\ell,n}=\{\alpha_{\ell,n}\geq0\}.
\]
Write it uniquely as
\[
H_{\ell,n}
=\{z:\nu_{\ell,n}\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq a_{\ell,n}\},
\qquad
\alpha_{\ell,n}(z)
=\kappa_{\ell,n}
 \bigl(a_{\ell,n}-\nu_{\ell,n}\mathbin{\cdot}z\bigr),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(|\nu_{\ell,n}|=1\) and \(\kappa_{\ell,n}>0\).

After passage to a subsequence and a permutation \((i,j,k)\) of
\((1,2,3)\), there are a convex body \(A\), unit vectors
\(\nu,\mu\), and real numbers \(a_i,a_j,a_k\) such that
\[
\widetilde A_{2,n}\longrightarrow A
\quad\hbox{in Hausdorff distance},
\]
\[
\nu_{i,n}\longrightarrow\nu,\qquad
\nu_{j,n}\longrightarrow-\nu,\qquad
\nu_{k,n}\longrightarrow\mu,\qquad
\det(\nu,\mu)\ne0,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
a_{\ell,n}\longrightarrow a_\ell,\qquad
w:=a_i+a_j>0.
\tag{3}
\]
The limiting lines in (2) are distinct.  In particular, the first two
halfspaces form the positive-width strip
\[
-a_j\leq\nu\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq a_i,
\]
and the third line is transverse.  No same-oriented parallel pair, no
coincident pair with either orientation, and no all-parallel limit can
occur.

The coefficients in (1) satisfy
\[
\kappa_{i,n}\longrightarrow\frac1w,\qquad
\kappa_{j,n}\longrightarrow\frac1w,\qquad
\kappa_{k,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]
If
\[
q'_\infty=\lim_nq'_n=\frac7{12}|A|,
\]
then the collapse has the exact first-order rate
\[
\frac{\kappa_{k,n}}{D_n}
\longrightarrow
\frac{w}{2q'_\infty|\det(\nu,\mu)|}>0,
\tag{5}
\]
and hence, locally uniformly in \(z\),
\[
\frac{\alpha_{k,n}(z)}{D_n}
\longrightarrow
\frac{w\bigl(a_k-\mu\mathbin{\cdot}z\bigr)}
 {2q'_\infty|\det(\nu,\mu)|}.
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, after one fixed affine coordinate change following the
subsequence, put
\[
x=\lim_n\alpha_{j,n},\qquad
y=\lim_n\frac{\alpha_{k,n}}{D_n}.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_{i,n}\longrightarrow1-x,\qquad
\alpha_{j,n}\longrightarrow x,\qquad
\frac{\alpha_{k,n}}{D_n}\longrightarrow y
\tag{7}
\]
locally uniformly, and the limiting \(Q\)-halfspaces are
\[
x\leq1,\qquad x\geq0,\qquad y\geq0.
\tag{8}
\]
The limiting body has the exact cell areas
\[
|A\cap\{0\leq x\leq1,\ y\geq0\}|=\frac7{12}|A|,
\tag{9}
\]
\[
|A\cap\{x>1,\ y\geq0\}|
=|A\cap\{x<0,\ y\geq0\}|
=|A\cap\{0\leq x\leq1,\ y<0\}|
=\frac5{36}|A|,
\tag{10}
\]
all other exact sign regions have area zero, and
\[
A\subseteq\{y\geq0\}\cup\{0\leq x\leq1\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(\widetilde K_n,\widetilde A_{0,n}\) be the images of the other two
bodies under the same John maps.  After a further subsequence, exactly one
of the following alternatives holds.

(E) The middle bodies are not contained in any common compact set:
\[
\sup\{|z|:z\in\widetilde K_n\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{12}
\]

(T) The middle bodies are contained in one fixed ball.  Then the
\(\widetilde A_{0,n}\) are also contained in one fixed ball, and there are
convex bodies \(A_0,K\), a real number \(\tau\), and a real number
\(\theta\) such that
\[
\widetilde A_{0,n}\longrightarrow A_0,\qquad
\widetilde K_n\longrightarrow K,
\qquad
\frac{A_0+A}{2}\subseteq K,
\tag{13}
\]
and, with
\[
t_{\ell,n}=-\frac{\Lambda_{\ell,n}}{\gamma_n},
\]
\[
\gamma_n\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
t_{i,n}\longrightarrow\tau,\qquad
t_{j,n}\longrightarrow1-\tau,\qquad
\frac{t_{k,n}}{D_n}\longrightarrow\theta.
\tag{14}
\]
In the coordinates (7), the limiting middle halfspaces are
\[
x\leq1-\tau,\qquad
x\geq1-\tau,\qquad
y\geq\theta,
\tag{15}
\]
and the limiting \(P\)-halfspaces are
\[
x\leq1-2\tau,\qquad
x\geq2-2\tau,\qquad
y\geq2\theta.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently, after dividing every transformed cell area by \(q'_n\),
\[
r_{ij,n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
r_{k,n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
r_{123,n}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
p_{ij,n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]

For clarity, the complete surviving scalar limit in alternative (T) is
as follows.  Normalize its central \(Q\)-mass to one and put bars over
limiting masses.  The only possibly nonzero \(P\)-masses are
\[
\bar p_0,\bar p_i,\bar p_j,\bar p_k,\bar p_{ik},\bar p_{jk},
\]
and the only possibly nonzero middle masses are
\[
\bar r_i,\bar r_j,\bar r_{ik},\bar r_{jk}.
\]
Thus
\[
\bar M
=\bar p_0+\bar p_i+\bar p_j+\bar p_k+\bar p_{ik}+\bar p_{jk},
\qquad
\bar k=\bar r_i+\bar r_j+\bar r_{ik}+\bar r_{jk},
\tag{18}
\]
\[
\bar u_i=\bar p_i+\bar p_{ik},\quad
\bar u_j=\bar p_j+\bar p_{jk},\quad
\bar u_k=\bar p_k+\bar p_{ik}+\bar p_{jk},
\tag{19}
\]
\[
\bar c_i=\bar r_i+\bar r_{ik},\quad
\bar c_j=\bar r_j+\bar r_{jk},\quad
\bar c_k=\bar r_{ik}+\bar r_{jk}.
\tag{20}
\]
The crossings, tails, and \(W=0\) identity pass to
\[
\bar u_\ell+\frac{31}{21}+\bar c_\ell
=\frac{2(\bar M+12/7+\bar k)}9
\quad(\ell=i,j,k),
\tag{21}
\]
\[
0\leq\bar u_\ell\leq\frac5{21},\qquad
\bar M-\bar u_\ell-\frac{31}{21}\geq0,
\tag{22}
\]
\[
2\bar p_0=\frac{69}{7}+\bar k+\bar\eta,
\tag{23}
\]
where
\[
\bar\eta
=\bar p_i+\bar p_j+\bar p_k
+4(\bar p_{ik}+\bar p_{jk})
+3(\bar r_{ik}+\bar r_{jk})
\geq\frac67.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (8), (13), and (15)-(24) are the exact bounded full-body limit
which remains to be excluded.

## Proof

Affine maps preserve all cell-area ratios and \(D=q/|\mathcal T_Q|\).
By accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8`, the four positive \(Q\)-cells have
areas \(q,5q/21,5q/21,5q/21\), and their union is \(A_2\) up to
zero-area cells.  Hence in John position
\[
q'_n=\frac7{12}|\widetilde A_{2,n}|,
\qquad
|Q'_{N\setminus\{\ell\},n}|=\frac5{36}|\widetilde A_{2,n}|.
\tag{25}
\]
The John inclusions give
\[
\pi\leq|\widetilde A_{2,n}|\leq4\pi.
\tag{26}
\]
The bodies therefore have a Hausdorff-convergent subsequence with a
two-dimensional convex limit \(A\).

For every \(\ell\), the central cell has positive area on the
\(H_{\ell,n}\)-side and the cap \(Q_{N\setminus\{\ell\},n}\) has positive
area on the opposite side.  Thus each boundary line meets the interior of
\(\widetilde A_{2,n}\), and consequently
\[
|a_{\ell,n}|\leq2.
\tag{27}
\]
After another subsequence, all three oriented unit normals and offsets
converge.  Away from \(\partial A\) and the three limiting lines, body and
halfspace membership eventually stabilizes.  These four boundaries have
area zero, so dominated convergence in \(2B\) proves convergence of every
exact-cell area.  In particular, all four positive fractions in (25)
persist in the limit.

Put
\[
T_n=\bigcap_{\ell=1}^3H_{\ell,n}.
\]
Accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8` gives
\[
|T_n|=\frac{q'_n}{D_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{28}
\]
For every \(n\), \(T_n\) is a bounded triangle, so
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}
\operatorname{conv}\{\nu_{1,n},\nu_{2,n},\nu_{3,n}\}.
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed, otherwise a separating vector would be a nonzero recession
direction of \(T_n\).

The origin cannot remain in the interior of the convex hull of the three
limiting normals.  If it did, compactness of the unit circle would give an
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that, for every unit vector \(v\) and all large
\(n\),
\[
\max_\ell\nu_{\ell,n}\mathbin{\cdot}v\geq\varepsilon.
\]
For \(z\in T_n\), apply this with \(v=z/|z|\) and use (27).  It gives
\(|z|\leq2/\varepsilon\), contradicting (28).  Thus the origin lies on the
boundary of the convex hull of the limiting unit normals.  Three unit
vectors whose convex hull contains the origin on its boundary must contain
an antipodal pair: a supporting line through the origin puts all three in
one closed halfplane, and the vectors on that supporting line which
convexly generate the origin are the two opposite unit vectors.  Relabel
this pair as \(i,j\), proving the first three limits in (2).

Two equal oriented limiting normals are impossible.  The corresponding
halfspaces would be nested, so one of the two exact caps
\(Q_{N\setminus\{i\}}\), \(Q_{N\setminus\{j\}}\) would be empty, contrary
to its limiting area \(5|A|/36\).  For the antipodal pair, the intersection
of the two limiting halfspaces is
\[
-a_j\leq\nu\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq a_i.
\]
Its intersection with the third halfspace contains the limiting central
cell of area \(7|A|/12\).  Therefore \(a_i+a_j>0\); equality would leave
only one line and negative width would leave the empty set.  The third
normal cannot be parallel to \(\nu\), because it would then equal one of
the already present oriented normals, which was just excluded.  This
proves (2)-(3) and all assertions about parallelism and coincidence.

Sum (1) over \(\ell\).  Since \(\sum_\ell\alpha_{\ell,n}=1\),
\[
\sum_\ell\kappa_{\ell,n}\nu_{\ell,n}=0,\qquad
\sum_\ell\kappa_{\ell,n}a_{\ell,n}=1.
\tag{30}
\]
Let
\[
\omega_{\ell,n}
=\frac{\kappa_{\ell,n}}
 {\kappa_{1,n}+\kappa_{2,n}+\kappa_{3,n}}.
\]
Every limit of these positive weights has sum one and gives a convex
combination of the limiting normals equal to zero.  Because
\(\nu,-\nu,\mu\) are present and \(\mu\) is transverse, the only such
combination is
\[
(\omega_i,\omega_j,\omega_k)=\left(\frac12,\frac12,0\right).
\]
The second identity in (30) now gives
\[
\sum_\ell\kappa_{\ell,n}
\longrightarrow\frac2{a_i+a_j}.
\]
This proves (4).

The affine map
\[
z\longmapsto(\alpha_{i,n}(z),\alpha_{j,n}(z))
\]
sends \(T_n\) onto the standard triangle of area \(1/2\).  Its absolute
Jacobian is
\[
\kappa_{i,n}\kappa_{j,n}
 |\det(\nu_{i,n},\nu_{j,n})|.
\]
Using the first identity in (30),
\[
\kappa_{j,n}\nu_{j,n}
=-\kappa_{i,n}\nu_{i,n}-\kappa_{k,n}\nu_{k,n},
\]
so the same Jacobian is
\[
\kappa_{i,n}\kappa_{k,n}
|\det(\nu_{i,n},\nu_{k,n})|.
\]
Therefore
\[
|T_n|
=\frac1{
2\kappa_{i,n}\kappa_{k,n}
|\det(\nu_{i,n},\nu_{k,n})|}.
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (28), (31), and \(D_n=q'_n/|T_n|\) gives the exact identity
\[
D_n
=2q'_n\kappa_{i,n}\kappa_{k,n}
|\det(\nu_{i,n},\nu_{k,n})|.
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (2), (4), (25), and area continuity give (5), and (1) then
gives (6).

Take \(x\) to be the limiting affine function
\((a_j+\nu\mathbin{\cdot}z)/w\), which is the limit of
\(\alpha_{j,n}\), and take \(y\) to be the nonconstant affine function
in (6).  Their linear parts are independent by (2), so they are affine
coordinates.  This proves (7)-(8).  Dominated convergence and (25) give
(9)-(10).  Every point in
\[
\{y<0,\ x<0\}\cup\{y<0,\ x>1\}
\]
has at most one positive limiting \(Q\)-coordinate.  All such exact cells
have area zero.  If \(A\) met this open set, the fact that a convex body is
the closure of its interior would produce an interior point there and
hence a positive-area forbidden cell.  This proves (11).

We turn to the full bodies.  If their radii are unbounded, pass to a
subsequence on which (12) holds.  Otherwise the \(\widetilde K_n\) lie in
one fixed ball.  The John center \(0\) belongs to
\(\widetilde A_{2,n}\).  Full compatibility gives
\[
\frac{\widetilde A_{0,n}+0}{2}\subseteq\widetilde K_n,
\]
so the \(\widetilde A_{0,n}\) also lie in one fixed ball.  Their area
ratios have positive lower bounds: compatibility and Brunn--Minkowski give
\[
k_n\geq
\frac{(\sqrt{M_n}+\sqrt{m_n})^2}{4}\geq m_n,
\]
while the \(W=0\) identity recorded in accepted fact
`444fb905d0cc5122` gives \(p_{0,n}\geq69/14\) after \(q_n=1\).
Thus Hausdorff limits \(A_0,K\) remain two-dimensional.  Taking limits of
midpoints proves (13).

The middle boundary \(\alpha_{k,n}=t_{k,n}\) crosses
\(\operatorname{int}\widetilde K_n\): the positive cell \(R_k\) is on its
indexed side and either positive cell \(R_i\) or \(R_j\) is on the other
side.  Since \(\alpha_{k,n}/D_n\) is uniformly bounded on the fixed ball
containing \(\widetilde K_n\), (6) implies
\[
\frac{t_{k,n}}{D_n}=O(1).
\tag{33}
\]
The same crossing argument for \(i,j\), now using (4), bounds
\(t_{i,n},t_{j,n}\).

The full-triangle homothety in accepted fact `c49613ac26c0c4a8` gives
\[
\frac1{D_n}
=\gamma_n^2\frac{r_{123,n}}{q_n}.
\tag{34}
\]
In alternative (T), \(r_{123,n}/q_n\leq k_n/q_n\) is bounded because the
John-normalized \(K_n\) lie in a fixed ball while \(q'_n\) is bounded below
by (25)-(26).  Hence (34) forces \(\gamma_n\to\infty\).  Finally,
\[
t_{i,n}+t_{j,n}+t_{k,n}
=-\frac{\sum_\ell\Lambda_{\ell,n}}{\gamma_n}
=1-\frac1{\gamma_n}.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (33)-(35), followed by a subsequence, prove (14).

The exact threshold identities following from the definitions in accepted
fact `0e51a47a9add571f` are
\[
Q:\ \alpha_\ell\geq0,\qquad
R:\ \alpha_\ell\geq t_\ell,\qquad
P:\ \alpha_\ell\geq2t_\ell.
\tag{36}
\]
Substitute (7) and (14) into (36).  This gives (15)-(16).  The first two
middle halfspaces in (15) meet only on the line \(x=1-\tau\), while their
two complements are disjoint.  Hence every middle cell requiring both
indices \(i,j\), and every middle cell requiring neither of them, has
limiting area zero.  This proves the first three limits in (17).  The
first two \(P\)-conditions in (16) require simultaneously
\[
x\leq1-2\tau,\qquad x\geq2-2\tau,
\]
whose endpoints differ by one.  Their intersection is empty, proving the
last limit in (17).  Hausdorff and halfspace convergence justify passage
of all exact-cell areas exactly as after (27).

It remains only to record what the canonical scalar relations become,
not to add any new inequality.  The vanishing cells in (17) give
(18)-(20).  At the fixed ratios,
\[
v_\ell=m-x_\ell=\frac{31}{21}.
\]
Passing the three crossings and the two canonical tails to the limit gives
(21)-(22).  Passing the exact \(W=0\) identity from accepted fact
`444fb905d0cc5122` gives (23).  Finally, at
\(\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3=5/21\), the subsets of sizes
\(0,1,2,3\) in the minimum of accepted fact `0009896ebff12f31` give
\[
\frac{10}{7},\qquad\frac{20}{21},\qquad\frac57,\qquad\frac57.
\]
Thus \(g=5/7\), \(\epsilon=6/7\), and that accepted fact gives
\(\eta_n/q_n>6/7\).  Together with (17), this passes exactly to (24).
This proves the stated compact-limit classification and full-body
dichotomy.
