## Statement

Use the notation and actual-allocation conventions of accepted fact
`58b8e7bb5c3be36f`, and assume its full strict residual strip. Suppose
\(A_0,A_1,A_2\) are centrally symmetric about one common point \(z\).
Choose \(j\in\{0,2\}\) with \(|A_j|=M\), put \(k=2-j\), and assume
\[
A_k\subseteq A_j,\qquad |A_k|=m.
\tag{1}
\]
Then, for every \(q\in\mathbb S^1\),
\[
\rho_q(2T/9)<q\cdot z.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently every noncollinear positive-spanning triple satisfies
\[
W=\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_{q_\ell}(2T/9)<0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (CB) holds on the class (1), without any restriction on zero demand,
capacity-active demand, or nonunique endpoint contacts, because its
antecedent is false.

For a separate exact calibration, let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\frac14\Delta.
\tag{4}
\]
These rational triangles are compatible and lie in the full strict
residual strip. For
\[
q_1=(-1,0),\qquad q_2=(0,-1),\qquad
q_3=\frac{(1,1)}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1,\quad\alpha_3=\sqrt2,
\tag{5}
\]
the actual unique allocations and complete contact sets are
\[
p_\ell=\frac{19}{96},\qquad
b_\ell=\frac1{32},\qquad
\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)=\left\{\frac1{64}\right\}
\quad(\ell=1,2,3).
\tag{6}
\]
Putting \(\sigma=\sqrt{19/48}\), their exact thresholds satisfy
\[
\rho_{q_1}(2T/9)=\rho_{q_2}(2T/9)=-1+\sigma,
\qquad
\rho_{q_3}(2T/9)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt2},
\tag{7}
\]
and hence
\[
W=-2+3\sigma<0.
\tag{8}
\]
This calibration verifies only the triple (5), not all triples for (4).

## Proof

Fix \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and abbreviate
\(F_i(c)=F_{i,q}(c)\). Reflection in the common center \(z\) preserves
area. Since a line has planar area zero,
\[
F_k(2q\cdot z-c)=m-F_k(c)
\tag{9}
\]
for every real \(c\), including support values. Inclusion (1) gives
\(F_j(c)\ge F_k(c)\), and therefore
\[
F_j(c)+F_k(2q\cdot z-c)\ge m.
\tag{10}
\]
For sufficiently negative \(c\), the first cap is empty and the second cap
is full. Equality is then attained in (10). Thus the exact endpoint
infimal convolution of accepted fact `17ca54faa05ad878` obeys
\[
I_q(2q\cdot z)=m.
\tag{11}
\]
Central symmetry of \(A_1\) likewise gives
\[
F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)=\frac{a_1}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]

Write \(r=m/M\) and \(s=a_1/M\). The residual-strip characterization in
accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` includes the strict inequality
\(s>1-r\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a_1}{2}+m-\frac{2T}{9}
&=\frac{5a_1+14m-4M}{18}\\
&=\frac{M(5s+14r-4)}{18}
>\frac{M(1+9r)}{18}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (11)-(13) show that
\[
\Phi_q(q\cdot z)
=F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)+I_q(2q\cdot z)>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{14}
\]
The continuity and exact superlevel-ray identity in accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878` now imply (2). Using the positive balance in the
statement of (CB),
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell\rho_{q_\ell}(2T/9)
<\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell\cdot z=0,
\]
which proves (3). Because this argument never refers to \(p_\ell\),
\(b_\ell\), or \(\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\), it includes all endpoint
and contact-set cases within the class (1).

It remains to prove the calibration. Convexity of \(\Delta\) and
\(0\in\Delta\) give
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac58\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{15}
\]
Accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` therefore gives compatibility. Direct area
calculation yields
\[
(M,a_1,m,T,t)
=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac1{32},
       \frac{33}{32},\frac{11}{48}\right).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(r=1/16\) and \(s=1\). Accepted fact
`15bb2c1680a0254e` gives
\[
r<\frac9{25}<\frac12<
\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
1-r=\frac{15}{16}<1<\frac{17}{16}=1+r,
\]
so its exact characterization places (4) strictly in the residual strip.

The feasible allocation interval from accepted fact
`65512ded3a06d971` is
\[
[L,U]=[t-m,t]
=\left[\frac{19}{96},\frac{11}{48}\right].
\tag{17}
\]
First compute the positively rescaled third direction
\(\widetilde q_3=(1,1)\). For \(0\le u\le1/32\), direct triangle-cap
inversion gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
q&Q_{0,q}(u)&Q_{2,q}(u)\\ \hline
q_1\text{ or }q_2&-1+\sqrt{2u}&-\frac14+\sqrt{2u}\\
\widetilde q_3&\sqrt{2u}&\sqrt{2u}.
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]
For every \(0<b\le1/32\), each endpoint-convolution maximand in (18)
differs by a constant from
\[
u\longmapsto\sqrt{2u}+\sqrt{2(b-u)}.
\tag{19}
\]
The function (19) is strictly concave on \([0,b]\), symmetric about
\(b/2\), and has its unique maximum there. Consequently the full argmax,
including both boundary candidates, is
\[
\mathcal U_q(b)=\left\{\frac b2\right\}
\quad(q=q_1,q_2,\widetilde q_3).
\tag{20}
\]
Positive rescaling of a direction does not change its allocation or
contact set, so (20) also applies to \(q_3\).

At \(p=L=19/96\), the outer demand is \(b=t-p=1/32=m\). For
\(q_1,q_2\), the two allocation branches of accepted fact
`65512ded3a06d971` are
\[
A(L)=-1+\sigma,\qquad
B(L)=-\frac58+\sqrt{\frac1{32}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Their exact difference is positive:
\[
A(L)-B(L)
=-\frac38+\sigma-\sqrt{\frac1{32}}>0,
\tag{22}
\]
because \(\sigma>3/5\) and \(\sqrt{1/32}<1/5\). For
\(\widetilde q_3\), the two values are
\(\sigma\) and \(\sqrt{1/32}\), again with positive difference.
The exact unique-optimizer classification in accepted fact
`65512ded3a06d971` therefore puts all three directions at the left
endpoint \(p=L\). Equation (20) then gives (6), including the
capacity-active demand and the complete contact sets.

The attained values are \(-1+\sigma,-1+\sigma,\sigma\) before normalizing
the third direction. Positive normalization divides the third threshold by
\(\sqrt2\), which proves (7). The weights in (5) satisfy
\(\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell q_\ell=0\), and (7) gives (8). Finally,
\(19/48<4/9\) implies \(\sigma<2/3\), so the inequality in (8) is strict.
