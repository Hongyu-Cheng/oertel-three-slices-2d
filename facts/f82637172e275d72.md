## Statement

Let \(s,a,b,\delta>0\), and let \(P\) be a strictly convex rational pentagon
with clockwise vertices
\[
p_0=(-a,\delta+a),\qquad
p_1=(\delta+b,-b),\qquad
p_2,p_3,p_4.
\]
Assume \(p_2,p_3,p_4\) have positive coordinates and lie strictly below
\(x+y=\delta\), so \(p_0\) is the only vertex with \(x<0\), \(p_1\) is the
only vertex with \(y<0\), and \(p_0p_1\) is the support edge for \(x+y\).
Then there is no such \(P\) satisfying the complete system
\[
|P|=16s,\qquad
|P\cap\{x\le0\}|=\frac{s}{8},\qquad
|P\cap\{y\le0\}|=\frac{s}{5},\qquad
\delta<a+b.
\tag{1}
\]

## Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that all four conditions in (1) hold.  Apply
the affine map
\[
\Phi(x,y)=(\xi,h)=(x+a,\delta-x-y).
\tag{2}
\]
Its linear part has determinant \(-1\), so it preserves area and reverses
orientation.  Put
\[
\widetilde P=\Phi(P),\qquad L=\delta+a+b.
\tag{3}
\]
The first two vertices become
\[
q_0=(0,0),\qquad q_1=(L,0).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(i=2,3,4\), write \(q_i=(\xi_i,h_i)\).  The strict sign hypotheses give
\[
\xi_i>a,\qquad h_i>0,\qquad
\xi_i+h_i=L-b-y(p_i)<L-b.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, \(0<\xi_i<L\).  Thus \(q_0q_1\) is the bottom edge of
\(\widetilde P\), all its other vertices lie strictly above that edge, and
the vertical sections of \(\widetilde P\) have the form
\[
\widetilde P\cap\{\xi=\tau\}
=\{(\tau,h):0\le h\le f(\tau)\},
\qquad 0\le\tau\le L,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(f\) is a nonnegative concave piecewise-affine function with
\[
f(0)=f(L)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
The function is concave because \(\widetilde P\) is convex.

Write
\[
q_4=(E,v),\qquad q_2=(C,u).
\tag{8}
\]
By (5), \(E>a\), \(v>0\), \(C<L\), and \(u>0\).  The two endpoint edges of
the upper boundary give
\[
f(\tau)=\alpha\tau\quad(0\le\tau\le E),
\qquad
f(\tau)=\beta(L-\tau)\quad(C\le\tau\le L),
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
\alpha=\frac vE>0,\qquad
\beta=\frac{u}{L-C}>0.
\tag{10}
\]

The line \(x=0\) becomes \(\xi=a\).  Since \(E>a\), the \(x\)-cap in (1)
is exactly the triangle below the first endpoint edge in (9).  Its exact
area equation is
\[
\frac{\alpha a^2}{2}=\frac{s}{8},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{s}{4a^2}.
\tag{11}
\]

The inverse of (2) gives
\[
y=L-b-\xi-h,
\tag{12}
\]
so \(y=0\) becomes \(h=L-b-\xi\).  On the right endpoint edge, put
\(r=L-\xi\).  Its upper boundary is \(h=\beta r\), while \(y=0\) is
\(h=r-b\).  Because \(p_2\) has positive \(y\), these lines meet in the
relative interior of \(q_1q_2\), and \(0<\beta<1\).  At their intersection,
\[
r=\frac{b}{1-\beta},
\qquad
h=\frac{b\beta}{1-\beta}.
\tag{13}
\]
The \(y\)-cap in (1) is consequently the triangle with base \(b\) on
\(q_0q_1\) and height \(b\beta/(1-\beta)\).  Its exact area equation is
\[
\frac{b^2\beta}{2(1-\beta)}=\frac{s}{5},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{2s}{5b^2+2s}.
\tag{14}
\]

Concavity bounds every intermediate vertex.  For \(E<\tau\le L\),
concavity between \(0\) and \(\tau\) gives
\[
f(E)\ge\frac{E}{\tau}f(\tau),
\]
and hence \(f(\tau)\le\alpha\tau\).  Equality already holds for
\(0\le\tau\le E\).  Applying the same argument to the concave function
\(r\mapsto f(L-r)\) gives the right-endpoint bound.  Therefore
\[
f(\tau)\le
\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}
\qquad(0\le\tau\le L).
\tag{15}
\]
The two lines in (15) meet at
\(\tau=\beta L/(\alpha+\beta)\).  Since \(\Phi\) preserves area,
integration of this tent, followed by (11) and (14), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|P|
&=|\widetilde P|
=\int_0^L f(\tau)\,d\tau\\
&\le
\int_0^L\min\{\alpha\tau,\beta(L-\tau)\}\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{\alpha\beta L^2}{2(\alpha+\beta)}
=\frac{L^2}{2(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1})}\\
&=\frac{sL^2}{8a^2+5b^2+2s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The first condition in (1), together with (16), gives
\[
L^2\ge16(8a^2+5b^2+2s)
=128a^2+80b^2+32s.
\tag{17}
\]
This lower bound is strictly larger than \(4(a+b)^2\), since
\[
\begin{aligned}
&128a^2+80b^2+32s-4(a+b)^2\\
&\qquad=120a^2+72b^2+4(a-b)^2+32s>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Because \(L,a,b>0\), equations (17)-(18) imply
\[
L>2(a+b).
\]
Using \(L=\delta+a+b\), this is
\[
\delta>a+b.
\tag{19}
\]
But the fourth condition in (1) explicitly assumes
\(\delta<a+b\).  The two strict inequalities contradict each other.
Therefore no pentagon satisfying the complete system (1) exists.
