## Statement

Let \(q>1\), let \(\{1,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), and let \(P_0,P_2\) be nondegenerate triangles in
\[
H_{1+q}=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1+q\right\},
\qquad
H_{1-q}=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\sum_{i=1}^3x_i=1-q\right\},
\]
respectively. Use the common normalized planar area on these parallel planes, put \(A=|P_0|\), \(B=|P_2|\), and assume
\[
0<A\leq B,
\qquad
\frac AB<\frac38,
\qquad
A+B>1,
\qquad
B<1+A.
\]
Let \(g_0\) be the centroid of \(P_0\), and assume
\[
(g_0)_1>1+q,
\qquad
(g_0)_j<0,
\qquad
(g_0)_k<0,
\]
\[
P_0\cap\{x_j>0,\ x_1<0,\ x_k<0\}\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
P_2\subseteq\{x_1\leq0,\ x_2\leq0,\ x_3\leq0\}.
\]
Define
\[
K=\frac{P_0+P_2}{2},
\qquad
\Pi(K)=\sup\{|Q|:Q\subseteq K\text{ is a parallelogram}\}.
\]
For \(f(x)=x_1-x_j\), one has the affine-invariant estimate
\[
\Pi(K)
\geq
\frac B2\,
\frac{\operatorname{width}_f(P_0)}{\operatorname{width}_f(P_2)}
>
\frac{3B(q+1)}{4(q-1)}
>
\frac{6}{11}
>
\frac12.
\]
This conclusion includes repeated support values, support along a full edge, parallel edges between the two endpoint triangles, and every cyclic Minkowski edge order. In particular, there is no triangle \(P_1\subset H_1\) of normalized area one with \(K\subseteq P_1\).

## Proof

Let \(E=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\sum_i x_i=0\}\) be the common direction space of the three parallel planes, and let \(\Omega\) be an oriented area form on \(E\) whose absolute value gives the project's normalized planar area. Thus, for any triangle with edge vectors \(e_1,e_2\in E\), its normalized area is \(|\Omega(e_1,e_2)|/2\). Write \(\ell=f|_E\) for the linear part of \(f\), and set
\[
w_0=\operatorname{width}_f(P_0),
\qquad
w_2=\operatorname{width}_f(P_2),
\qquad
R=\frac{w_0}{w_2}.
\]
Accepted fact 003adef77e7c9efe applies verbatim to the stated centroid, active-cell, cap-free, and area-ratio hypotheses. In particular, \(w_2>0\) and
\[
R>\frac{3(q+1)}{2(q-1)}>\frac32.
\tag{1}
\]
We next prove a general triangle lemma that does not choose a Minkowski edge-order chamber. Label the vertices \(z_-,z_+,z_*\) of \(P_2\) so that \(f\) is minimal at \(z_-\), maximal at \(z_+\), and \(z_*\) is the remaining vertex. This labeling allows ties: if a support set is a full edge, choose either endpoint of that edge as the corresponding extreme vertex. Put
\[
e_1=z_+-z_-,
\qquad
e_2=z_*-z_-,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{f(z_*)-f(z_-)}{w_2}.
\]
Then \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\), including the two support-edge boundary values, and
\[
\ell(e_1)=w_2,
\qquad
\ell(e_2)=\alpha w_2,
\qquad
|\Omega(e_1,e_2)|=2B.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose vertices \(x_-,x_+\) of \(P_0\) at which \(f\) is respectively minimal and maximal, and put \(u=x_+-x_-\). Again, ties cause no problem because the segment joining the chosen extreme vertices lies in \(P_0\). Since \(e_1,e_2\) form a basis of \(E\), write
\[
u=\lambda e_1+\mu e_2.
\]
Applying \(\ell\) and using (2) gives
\[
R=\frac{\ell(u)}{w_2}=\lambda+\alpha\mu
=(1-\alpha)\lambda+\alpha(\lambda+\mu).
\]
Because \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\),
\[
R\leq\max\{|\lambda|,|\lambda+\mu|\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The two vectors \(e_2\) and \(e_2-e_1\) are directions of the two edges \([z_-,z_*]\) and \([z_+,z_*]\) of \(P_2\). Equations (2) and (3) yield
\[
\max\bigl\{|\Omega(u,e_2)|,
|\Omega(u,e_2-e_1)|\bigr\}
=2B\max\{|\lambda|,|\lambda+\mu|\}
\geq2BR.
\tag{4}
\]
Choose from those two edges a segment with direction \(e\) attaining the maximum in (4). The Minkowski average of that edge segment with \([x_-,x_+]\subseteq P_0\) is a parallelogram \(Q\subseteq K\), with edge vectors \(u/2\) and \(e/2\). Hence
\[
|Q|=\frac{|\Omega(u,e)|}{4}\geq\frac{BR}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
The right side is positive, so \(Q\) is nondegenerate. This proves the first asserted estimate. Notice that \(\alpha=0\) and \(\alpha=1\) were retained in (3); if one candidate edge is parallel to \(u\), (4) forces the other one to supply the bound. Thus support-edge contacts and edge-direction coincidences are literal cases of the same argument. No edge ordering of \(P_0+P_2\) was used, so all cyclic-order chambers and their boundaries are covered.

It remains only to use the strict residual area inequalities. Since \(A/B<3/8\) and \(A+B>1\),
\[
1<A+B<\frac{11B}{8},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
B>\frac8{11}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (1), (5), and (6) gives
\[
\Pi(K)\geq|Q|>\frac{3B}{4}>\frac6{11}>\frac12.
\]
Finally, accepted fact 72810a77f1328d7c proves the affine-invariant bound that every parallelogram contained in a triangle has at most half the triangle's area. Therefore a normalized-area-one triangle containing \(K\) would contain the parallelogram \(Q\) of area greater than \(1/2\), a contradiction. This proves the stated exclusion.

The construction and every inequality use only the area form, an affine functional, widths, and Minkowski addition. Under a simultaneous affine change of planar coordinates, both sides of (5) acquire the same absolute determinant factor, while \(R\) is unchanged after transporting \(f\). Thus the argument is affine-invariant.
