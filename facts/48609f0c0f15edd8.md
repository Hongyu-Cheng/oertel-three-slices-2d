## Statement

Assume the locked problem.  Put
\[
m=\min(a_0,a_2),\qquad M=\max(a_0,a_2),
\]
choose \(j\in\{0,2\}\) with \(a_j=M\), and let \(k=2-j\), so \(a_k=m\).
For a planar convex body \(A\), write
\[
d_A(p)=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left|A\cap\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le\langle u,p\rangle\}\right|,
\qquad
D_\alpha(A)=\{p\in A:d_A(p)\ge\alpha\}.
\]
At least one point of \(S\) has halfspace depth at least \(2T/9\) whenever
one of the following conditions holds.

1. Endpoint branch:
\[
\max_{p\in A_j}d_{A_j}(p)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{E}
\]

2. Hybrid middle branch:
\[
a_1+m\ge2M.
\tag{H}
\]

3. Saturation middle branch: there are \(z\in A_1\) and
\(\alpha,\beta\ge0\) such that
\[
d_{A_1}(z)\ge\beta,\qquad
2z-A_k\subseteq D_\alpha(A_j),\qquad
\beta+\min\{m,\alpha\}\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{S}
\]

The following specialization gives an explicit area-and-shape subregion.
Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty two-dimensional centrally
symmetric convex polygon with center \(0\), let \(o\in\mathbb R^2\), and
let
\[
A_0=o+K,\qquad A_2=o+\varepsilon K,\qquad A_1=o+tK,
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
0<\varepsilon\le\sqrt2-1,\qquad
t\ge\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Writing
\[
x=\varepsilon^2,\qquad y=t^2,
\tag{3}
\]
condition (S) holds with \(z=o\), \(\alpha=m=xM\), and
\(\beta=a_1/2=yM/2\) whenever
\[
5y+14x\ge4.
\tag{4}
\]
Hence every family (1)-(2) with (4) has a point of depth at least \(2T/9\).
In particular, this closes the nonempty open residual shape subregion
\[
0<x<3-2\sqrt2,\qquad
\frac54<x+y<2,\qquad
\left(\frac{1+\sqrt x}{2}\right)^2<y<1+x.
\tag{5}
\]
On (5), no endpoint point reaches \(2T/9\), and the hybrid condition (H)
also fails.

By contrast, the triangle equality in accepted fact
`43e70f89f224c5d7` gives
\(\max_{p\in\Delta}d_\Delta(p)=4|\Delta|/9\).  Thus for a triangular
larger endpoint, (E) reduces exactly to
\[
m+a_1\le M,
\tag{6}
\]
which is the large-slice boundary and gives no improvement in the strict
balanced residual band.  In the sharp family
\(A_0=A_1=\Delta,\ A_2=\varepsilon\Delta\), one has
\((x,y)=(\varepsilon^2,1)\), so both (E) and (H) fail for
\(0<\varepsilon<1\).  This identifies exactly why an area-only
endpoint-versus-hybrid split cannot move the pinch boundary; the new
gain in (S) is shape-sensitive.

## Proof

Accepted fact `949ce2fdf2e1e240` gives the exact endpoint identities
\[
h_S((0,p))=d_{A_0}(p),\qquad h_S((2,p))=d_{A_2}(p),
\tag{7}
\]
and, for \(z\in A_1\),
\[
h_S((1,z))
=
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^1}
\left[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+(G_0^u\square G_2^u)(2\langle u,z\rangle)
\right].
\tag{8}
\]
It also proves that the infimal convolution is attained and that the two
slice-parallel halfspaces are strictly larger than the right side of (8).
Thus there is no omitted horizontal case.  Equation (7) immediately proves
the endpoint branch (E).

We next prove (H) from first principles.  Let \(X_j,X_k\) be independent
uniform random points in \(A_j,A_k\), and let \(\widehat\mu\) be the
probability law of \((X_j+X_k)/2\).  Convexity of the original polytope
implies
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1,
\tag{9}
\]
because the midpoint of \((0,w_0)\in C\) and \((2,w_2)\in C\) lies in
the \(x=1\) section.  Therefore the finite planar measure
\[
\nu=\mathbf 1_{A_1}\,dw+m\widehat\mu
\tag{10}
\]
has total mass \(a_1+m\) and is supported in \(A_1\).

For completeness, \(\nu\) has a planar centerpoint \(z\in A_1\): every
line has \(\nu\)-measure zero, since the Lebesgue part is line-null and,
after conditioning on \(X_k\), the event
\((X_j+X_k)/2\) lies on a fixed line requires \(X_j\) to lie on a line.
Consider the closed halfplanes having \(\nu\)-mass greater than
\(2(a_1+m)/3\).  Any three of their intersections with the compact convex
set \(A_1\) meet, since otherwise their open complements cover the support
of \(\nu\), while the sum of the three complement masses is strictly less
than \(a_1+m\).  The planar Helly theorem and compactness give a common
point \(z\in A_1\).  If a closed halfplane containing \(z\) had mass
strictly less than \((a_1+m)/3\), a small parallel shift of the opposite
halfplane, using the line-null property, would give a closed halfplane of
mass greater than \(2(a_1+m)/3\) that omits \(z\), a contradiction.
Thus every closed halfplane containing \(z\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least
\((a_1+m)/3\).

Fix \(u\in\mathbb S^1\), put \(r=2\langle u,z\rangle\), and let
\(\tau\in\mathbb R\).  The event
\[
\{\langle u,X_j+X_k\rangle\le r\}
\]
is contained in
\[
\{\langle u,X_j\rangle\le\tau\}
\ \cup\
\{\langle u,X_k\rangle\le r-\tau\}.
\]
Since \(a_j=M\) and \(a_k=m\), the union bound gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m\,\widehat\mu\{w:\langle u,w\rangle\le\langle u,z\rangle\}
&\le
\frac{m}{M}G_j^u(\tau)+G_k^u(r-\tau)\\
&\le G_j^u(\tau)+G_k^u(r-\tau).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\tau\), adding the \(A_1\)-cap, and using the
centerpoint property of \(z\) yields, for every \(u\),
\[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)
+(G_j^u\square G_k^u)(2\langle u,z\rangle)
\ge\frac{a_1+m}{3}.
\tag{12}
\]
The infimal convolution is commutative, so (12) is the expression in (8)
regardless of whether \(j=0\) or \(j=2\).  Finally,
\[
\frac{a_1+m}{3}\ge\frac{2(M+a_1+m)}9
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a_1+m\ge2M.
\tag{13}
\]
This proves (H), including normalization, closed boundaries, the location
of the centerpoint, and the horizontal cases.

We now prove the saturation branch.  For every unit \(u\), real \(r\), and
\(\tau\), let \(h_k(u)=\max_{w\in A_k}\langle u,w\rangle\).  If
\(\tau\le r-h_k(u)\), then \(G_k^u(r-\tau)=m\).  If
\(\tau\ge r-h_k(u)\), monotonicity gives
\(G_j^u(\tau)\ge G_j^u(r-h_k(u))\).  Hence
\[
(G_j^u\square G_k^u)(r)
\ge
\min\{m,G_j^u(r-h_k(u))\}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is the required saturation inequality, with the endpoint labels
interchanged when necessary.

Assume (S), fix \(u\), and choose \(q\in A_k\) attaining \(h_k(u)\).
Then \(p=2z-q\) belongs to \(D_\alpha(A_j)\), and therefore
\[
G_j^u(2\langle u,z\rangle-h_k(u))
=G_j^u(\langle u,p\rangle)
\ge d_{A_j}(p)\ge\alpha.
\tag{15}
\]
Also
\[
G_1^u(\langle u,z\rangle)\ge d_{A_1}(z)\ge\beta.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (8), (14)-(16) give
\[
h_S((1,z))\ge\beta+\min\{m,\alpha\}\ge\frac{2T}{9},
\tag{17}
\]
which proves (S).  Notice that (14) couples the two endpoint caps before
the direction \(u\) is minimized; no separate bonus is assigned to either
cap.

It remains to verify the claimed shape subregion.  In (1), let
\(M=|K|\).  Then
\[
m=\varepsilon^2M,\qquad a_1=t^2M.
\tag{18}
\]
Because \(K\) is convex and contains \(0\),
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=o+\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}K
\subseteq o+tK=A_1
\tag{19}
\]
under (2).  Thus the convex hull of the three displayed sections is a
compact polytope with exactly these sections at integer heights
\(0,1,2\).

For any \(p=o+\varepsilon q\in o+\varepsilon K\), the set
\[
p+(1-\varepsilon)K
\tag{20}
\]
is contained in \(o+K=A_0\) by convexity, is centrally symmetric about
\(p\), and has area \((1-\varepsilon)^2M\).  Every closed halfplane
containing \(p\) contains a parallel halfplane through \(p\), which cuts
(20) into two equal-area parts.  Consequently
\[
d_{A_0}(p)\ge\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^2M}{2}.
\tag{21}
\]
The inequality \(\varepsilon\le\sqrt2-1\) is exactly
\[
\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^2}{2}\ge\varepsilon^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(K=-K\), equations (18), (21), and (22) imply
\[
2o-A_2=o-\varepsilon K=o+\varepsilon K
\subseteq D_m(A_0).
\tag{23}
\]
Central symmetry of \(A_1\) gives \(d_{A_1}(o)=a_1/2\).  Thus (S) applies
with \(\alpha=m\), \(\beta=a_1/2\), and its last inequality becomes
\[
\frac{a_1}{2}+m\ge\frac{2(M+a_1+m)}9
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
5y+14x\ge4.
\tag{24}
\]

For a centrally symmetric planar body, its maximum planar depth is exactly
half its area: the center attains one half, while the two complementary
halfplanes through any point show that its depth is at most one half.
Therefore the best endpoint depth in (1) is \(M/2\), and
\[
\frac M2\ge\frac{2T}{9}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x+y\le\frac54.
\tag{25}
\]
Condition (H) is \(x+y\ge2\).  On (5), both (25) and (H) fail, whereas
\(5y+14x=5(x+y)+9x>4\), so (24) holds.  The region is nonempty: the exact
point \(x=4/25,\ y=11/10\) satisfies every strict inequality in (5).
At that point the best endpoint depth is \(M/2\), strictly below
\(2T/9=113M/225\), while (17) gives the middle lower bound
\[
\frac{a_1}{2}+m=\frac{71M}{100}.
\]

Finally, accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` gives the universal centroid
bound \(\max_p d_{A_j}(p)\ge4M/9\), so the area-only consequence of (E)
is precisely \(m+a_1\le M\).  Accepted fact `43e70f89f224c5d7` proves
equality for triangles and supplies the stated sharp family.  This proves
(6) and the asserted sharp-family behavior.
