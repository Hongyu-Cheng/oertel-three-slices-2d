## Statement

Let
\[
\mathcal L=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x\le-|y|\},
\qquad
\mathcal R=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x\ge|y|\}.
\]
Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty compact two-dimensional convex polygons such that
\[
A_0\subseteq\mathcal L,\qquad A_2\subseteq\mathcal R,\qquad
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\qquad M:=|A_0|>0.
\]
Assume the two diagonal maximizing-support equalities
\[
\max_{(x,y)\in A_2}(-(x+y))=0,
\qquad
\max_{(x,y)\in A_2}(y-x)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
D(x,y)=(u,v)=(x+y,x-y),
\qquad
D^{-1}(u,v)=\left(\frac{u+v}{2},\frac{u-v}{2}\right),
\]
and put
\[
P=-D(A_0),\qquad Q=D(A_2),\qquad K'=D(K),
\]
\[
\alpha=-\min_{z\in K'}z_u,\qquad
\beta=-\min_{z\in K'}z_v.
\]
Then
\[
P\subseteq[0,2\alpha]\times[0,2\beta],
\qquad
M\le2\alpha\beta.
\tag{2}
\]
The conclusion holds whether either maximizing face in (1) is a vertex or an edge, including when both contacts are the same vertex. Moreover \(\alpha,\beta>0\), and equality in (2) holds if and only if
\[
P=[0,2\alpha]\times[0,2\beta].
\tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, equality holds if and only if
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-\alpha,-\alpha),
(-\beta,\beta),(-\alpha-\beta,\beta-\alpha)\}.
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof

Because \(A_2\) is compact, both maxima in (1) are attained. Choose \(a_u,a_v\in A_2\) such that
\[
x(a_u)+y(a_u)=0,\qquad x(a_v)-y(a_v)=0,
\]
and write
\[
q_u=D(a_u),\qquad q_v=D(a_v).
\]
Then
\[
(q_u)_u=0,\qquad(q_v)_v=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(A_2\subseteq\mathcal R\), one has \(Q\subseteq[0,\infty)^2\). Thus
\[
F_u=Q\cap\{u=0\},\qquad F_v=Q\cap\{v=0\}
\]
are nonempty exposed faces of the two-dimensional polygon \(Q\). Each is either a vertex or a nondegenerate edge. Choosing any point of the appropriate face gives (5), so the proof is unchanged in the edge-contact cases. If the two faces meet, their intersection is the vertex \((0,0)\), which may be used for both contacts.

The coordinate map sends the two diagonal cones exactly to the coordinate quadrants:
\[
D(\mathcal L)=(-\infty,0]^2,\qquad
D(\mathcal R)=[0,\infty)^2.
\]
The reflection in the definition of \(P\) therefore gives
\[
P,Q\subseteq[0,\infty)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
For arbitrary \(p\in P\) and \(q\in Q\), choose \(a_0\in A_0\) and \(a_2\in A_2\) with
\[
p=-D(a_0),\qquad q=D(a_2).
\]
By linearity and midpoint inclusion,
\[
\frac{q-p}{2}
=D\left(\frac{a_0+a_2}{2}\right)\in D(K)=K'.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(p\in P\). Applying (7) with \(q=q_u\), and using \(\min_{K'}u=-\alpha\), gives
\[
-\frac{p_u}{2}
=\left(\frac{q_u-p}{2}\right)_u
\ge-\alpha,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad p_u\le2\alpha.
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (7) with \(q=q_v\), and using \(\min_{K'}v=-\beta\), gives
\[
-\frac{p_v}{2}
=\left(\frac{q_v-p}{2}\right)_v
\ge-\beta,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad p_v\le2\beta.
\tag{9}
\]
Together with (6), (8) and (9) prove the box inclusion in (2). All inequalities are non-strict, so contacts along vertices or edges and points of \(P\) on any boundary of the box require no separate argument.

The matrix of \(D\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(-2\). The reflection used to define \(P\) has area factor \(1\). Therefore
\[
|P|=2|A_0|=2M.
\tag{10}
\]
The box inclusion and (10) yield
\[
2M=|P|\le(2\alpha)(2\beta)=4\alpha\beta,
\]
and hence \(M\le2\alpha\beta\).

Equations (6), (8), and (9) imply \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\). If \(\alpha=0\), then \(P\subseteq\{u=0\}\), contradicting \(|P|=2M>0\). The same argument excludes \(\beta=0\). Thus \(\alpha,\beta>0\).

Finally, \(M=2\alpha\beta\) is equivalent by (10) to \(P\) having the full area of its containing box. Since \(P\) is compact, any point of the box omitted by \(P\) has a relative neighborhood of positive area disjoint from \(P\). Equality therefore forces (3), and (3) plainly suffices. Applying \(a_0=D^{-1}(-p)\) to the four corners of (3) gives the four vertices in (4). Thus equality permits the two edges of \(P\) on \(u=0\) and \(v=0\), which correspond to edges of \(A_0\) on the two boundary rays of \(\mathcal L\).
