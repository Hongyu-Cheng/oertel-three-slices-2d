## Statement

Assume all hypotheses and notation of accepted facts
`bc026780453747b8` and `92a1b537d7e7876c`.  Thus, with quadrant
boundaries ignored as null sets,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&(+,+)&(-,+)&(+,-)&(-,-)\\ \hline
A_0&97/100&3/200&3/200&0\\
A_2&9/200&1/160&1/160&1/200,
\end{array}
\]
\[
P=A_0\cap\{x\geq0,y\geq0\},\qquad
Q=A_2\cap\{x\geq0,y\geq0\},
\]
and
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|<\frac{29}{80}.
\]
For a planar convex body \(K\), let \(w_K,h_K\) be its coordinate
widths, and let \(m_x(K),m_y(K)\) denote its maximum horizontal and
vertical chord lengths.  Put
\[
X_P=\frac{w_P}{m_x(P)},\qquad
Y_P=\frac{h_P}{m_y(P)},\qquad
X_Q=\frac{w_Q}{\ell_x},\qquad
Y_Q=\frac{h_Q}{\ell_y},
\]
where \(\ell_x=m_x(Q)\) and \(\ell_y=m_y(Q)\), and put
\[
\kappa=\frac{r_+}{r_-}
=\frac{29+\sqrt{65}}{29-\sqrt{65}}.
\]
Assume that some \(Z\in P\) simultaneously realizes
\(\max_Px\) and \(\max_Py\).

Let
\[
a_h=-\min_{A_h}x,\qquad b_h=-\min_{A_h}y,
\]
\[
L_h=|A_h\cap\{x\leq0\}|,\qquad
B_h=|A_h\cap\{y\leq0\}|\qquad(h=0,2),
\]
and define the mean cap traces
\[
\overline L_h=\frac{L_h}{a_h},\qquad
\overline B_h=\frac{B_h}{b_h}.
\]
The cell data give
\[
L_0=B_0=\frac3{200},\qquad
L_2=B_2=\frac9{800}.
\]
Define
\[
\Gamma=a_0b_0+a_2b_2
+\frac{12}{25}\left(
\frac{a_2}{\ell_x}\frac{b_0}{h_P}
+\frac{a_0}{w_P}\frac{b_2}{\ell_y}
\right).
\]
Then
\[
X_P+Y_P\leq\frac{5000}{291}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover the exact Alternative 1 trace allocation satisfies
\[
\Gamma
<
\frac9{40000\,\overline L_0\overline B_0}
+\frac{81}{640000\,\overline L_2\overline B_2}
+\frac{12\kappa}{125}(X_P+Y_P)
<4.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, the shape budget \(\Gamma\) defined in accepted fact
`bc026780453747b8` is strictly less than \(4\).

## Proof

Write
\[
p=\frac{97}{100},\qquad
q=\frac9{200},\qquad
c=\frac3{200},\qquad
d=\frac9{800}.
\tag{3}
\]
We first prove (1) directly from the full \(A_0\) geometry.

The zero southwest cell and convexity imply that \(A_0\) has no point
in the open southwest quadrant.  Indeed, if such a point existed,
mixing it with an interior point of \(A_0\), with sufficiently small
positive mixing parameter, would produce an interior southwest point
and hence positive southwest area.  The positive northwest and
southeast cells then also exclude points on the negative \(x\)- and
negative \(y\)-axes: mixing such an axis point with an interior point
of the opposite mixed cell would again produce an interior southwest
point.

The intersections of \(A_0\) with the positive coordinate axes are
nondegenerate segments.  Let their physical lengths be \(e,v>0\).
Apply the diagonal map
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(x/e,y/v)
\]
and write \(D=ev\).  Areas are divided by \(D\), while \(X_P\) and
\(Y_P\) are unchanged.  In the normalized coordinates the two traces
have the form
\[
[a,a+1]\times\{0\},\qquad
\{0\}\times[b,b+1],
\qquad a,b\geq0.
\tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
A=a+1,\qquad B=b+1,
\]
and write the normalized common apex as
\[
Z=(W,H),\qquad W=\max_Px,\quad H=\max_Py.
\]
Thus \(W\geq A\) and \(H\geq B\).  Define
\[
\alpha=\frac{W-A}{H},\qquad
\beta=\frac{H-B}{W},\qquad
r=\sqrt{\alpha\beta}.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(\alpha,\beta\geq0\), and
\[
\alpha\beta
=\frac{W-A}{W}\frac{H-B}{H}<1.
\tag{6}
\]

The convex hull of
\[
(a,0),\ (A,0),\ Z,\ (0,B),\ (0,b)
\]
lies in the normalized positive cell.  At height \(B\), its
horizontal chord has length
\[
A+\frac{(W-A)B}{H}
=A+\alpha B
=W(1-\alpha\beta),
\tag{7}
\]
where the last equality uses
\[
W=A+\alpha H,\qquad H=B+\beta W.
\tag{8}
\]
At abscissa \(A\), its vertical chord has length
\[
B+\frac{(H-B)A}{W}
=B+\beta A
=H(1-\alpha\beta).
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently
\[
X_P+Y_P
\leq\frac2{1-r^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
These chord inclusions use only the segments joining the trace
endpoints to \(Z\); uniqueness or exposedness of the supporting
faces at \(x=W\) and \(y=H\) is not required.

The area of the same normalized hull is
\[
T=\frac{AH+WB-ab}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Solving (8), with \(\delta=1-\alpha\beta\), gives
\[
W=\frac{A+\alpha B}{\delta},\qquad
H=\frac{B+\beta A}{\delta},
\]
and hence
\[
2T
=\frac{\beta A^2+2AB+\alpha B^2}{\delta}
-(A-1)(B-1).
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(\alpha,\beta\), the two partial derivatives of the
right-hand side with respect to \(A,B\) are
\[
\frac{2\beta A+2B}{\delta}-(B-1)>0,\qquad
\frac{2A+2\alpha B}{\delta}-(A-1)>0.
\]
Thus (12) is minimized over \(A,B\geq1\) at \(A=B=1\).  Arithmetic-
geometric mean now gives
\[
T\geq
\frac{2+\alpha+\beta}{2(1-\alpha\beta)}
\geq
\frac{2+2r}{2(1-r^2)}
=\frac1{1-r}.
\tag{13}
\]
The normalized positive-cell area is \(p/D\), so (13) implies
\[
D\leq p(1-r).
\tag{14}
\]

We next use both full \(A_0\) caps.  There is a single finite
parameter \(\tau>0\) that gives the two required inner halfplanes.
If \(a,b>0\), take
\[
\tau=\frac ba.
\tag{15}
\]
For a point \((x,y)\in A_0\) with \(y<0\), the segment from
\((0,b)\) to \((x,y)\) meets the \(x\)-axis in the trace (4), at an
abscissa at least \(a\).  Therefore
\[
\frac{bx}{b-y}\geq a,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
y\geq b-\tau x=-\tau(x-a).
\tag{16}
\]
Similarly, for a point \((x,y)\in A_0\) with \(x<0\), the segment
from \((a,0)\) to \((x,y)\) meets the \(y\)-axis at an ordinate at
least \(b\), and
\[
\frac{ay}{a-x}\geq b,
\qquad\text{so again}\qquad
y\geq b-\tau x.
\tag{17}
\]

If one of \(a,b\) is zero, then both are zero: the origin belongs to
one trace, and convexity of the other axis section forces it to be
the inner endpoint of the other trace as well.  The origin is then a
boundary point of \(A_0\).  Choose a supporting line there and orient
its normal \((n_1,n_2)\) so that
\[
n_1x+n_2y\geq0\qquad\text{on }A_0.
\]
The two positive axis traces give \(n_1,n_2\geq0\).  Positive area in
each mixed cell rules out either coefficient being zero.  Hence
\(\tau=n_1/n_2\in(0,\infty)\), and the same common inner inequality
\[
y\geq-\tau x
\tag{18}
\]
holds on the whole body.  This is the zero-offset case of
(16)-(17).

The common apex supplies the two outer halfplanes without any
smoothness assumption.  If \((x,y)\in A_0\) and \(y<0\), its segment
to \(Z\) meets the \(x\)-axis at
\[
\frac{Hx-yW}{H-y}\leq A.
\]
Using \(W-A=\alpha H\), this is equivalent to
\[
x-A\leq\alpha y.
\tag{19}
\]
If \((x,y)\in A_0\) and \(x<0\), its segment to \(Z\) meets the
\(y\)-axis at
\[
\frac{Wy-xH}{W-x}\leq B.
\]
Using \(H-B=\beta W\), this is equivalent to
\[
y-B\leq\beta x.
\tag{20}
\]

Equations (16), (18), and (19) contain the normalized lower cap in
the triangle cut out by
\[
y\leq0,\qquad
y\geq-\tau(x-a),\qquad
x-A\leq\alpha y.
\]
Its base has length \(1\), and the other two boundary lines meet at
depth
\[
\frac{\tau}{1+\alpha\tau}.
\]
Since the physical lower-cap area is \(c\), its normalized area is
\(c/D\), and therefore
\[
\frac cD\leq
\frac{\tau}{2(1+\alpha\tau)}
=\frac1{2(\tau^{-1}+\alpha)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (17), (18), and (20) contain the normalized left cap in the
triangle cut out by
\[
x\leq0,\qquad
y\geq b-\tau x,\qquad
y\leq B+\beta x.
\]
Its base has length \(1\) and its horizontal altitude is
\(1/(\tau+\beta)\).  Hence
\[
\frac cD\leq\frac1{2(\tau+\beta)}.
\tag{22}
\]
The case \(\alpha=0\) in (21) is the vertical outer side \(x\leq A\);
the displayed area is then its direct limiting value \(\tau/2\).
The case \(\beta=0\) in (22) is the horizontal outer side
\(y\leq B\).  Flat support faces cause no change because
(16)-(20) use only closed halfplanes and axis crossings.  Thus
(21)-(22) include vertical, horizontal, support-face, and zero-offset
limits.

Rearranging (21)-(22) gives
\[
D\geq2c(\tau^{-1}+\alpha),\qquad
D\geq2c(\tau+\beta).
\tag{23}
\]
Adding the two inequalities and applying
\(\tau+\tau^{-1}\geq2\) and \(\alpha+\beta\geq2r\) yields
\[
D\geq
c(\tau+\tau^{-1}+\alpha+\beta)
\geq2c(1+r).
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (14) and (24),
\[
2c(1+r)\leq p(1-r),
\]
so
\[
r\leq\frac{p-2c}{p+2c}
=\frac{47}{50}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substitution in (10) proves
\[
X_P+Y_P
\leq\frac2{1-(47/50)^2}
=\frac{5000}{291},
\]
including the claimed exact constant.

It remains to convert this aspect bound to (2).  We use the following
elementary two-direction cap estimate.  Let \(K\) be a convex body
whose positive cell \(C\) has area \(\mathcal A\), coordinate widths
\(w,h\), and maximum horizontal and vertical chord lengths \(m_x,m_y\).
Let \(a,b\) be the negative coordinate support depths of \(K\), and
let \(\mathcal L,\mathcal B\) be its full left and lower cap areas.
Choose a vertical chord of \(C\), of length \(m_y\), at abscissa
\(s\leq w\), and a point of \(K\) on the support line \(x=-a\).
The portion in \(x\leq0\) of their convex hull is a triangle of area
\(m_ya^2/[2(a+s)]\).  The horizontal analogue is identical.  Hence
\[
\mathcal L\geq\frac{m_ya^2}{2(a+w)},\qquad
\mathcal B\geq\frac{m_xb^2}{2(b+h)}.
\tag{26}
\]
With
\[
\lambda=\frac aw,\qquad
\mu=\frac bh,\qquad
X=\frac w{m_x},\qquad
Y=\frac h{m_y},
\]
multiplication of (26) gives
\[
ab\leq
2\sqrt{\mathcal L\mathcal B(1+\lambda)(1+\mu)XY}.
\tag{27}
\]
Also \(\mathcal A\leq wm_y\) and
\(\mathcal A\leq hm_x\).  Each inequality in (26) therefore gives
\[
\frac{\lambda^2}{1+\lambda}
\leq\frac{2\mathcal L}{\mathcal A},
\qquad
\frac{\mu^2}{1+\mu}
\leq\frac{2\mathcal B}{\mathcal A}.
\tag{28}
\]

For \(A_0\), equations (3) and (28) give
\[
\frac{\lambda_0^2}{1+\lambda_0},
\frac{\mu_0^2}{1+\mu_0}
\leq\frac{2c}{p}
=\frac3{97}
<\frac1{30}
=\frac{(1/5)^2}{1+1/5}.
\]
Thus
\[
\lambda_0=\frac{a_0}{w_P}<\frac15,\qquad
\mu_0=\frac{b_0}{h_P}<\frac15.
\tag{29}
\]
Using (27) and \(\sqrt{X_PY_P}\leq(X_P+Y_P)/2\),
\[
a_0b_0<\frac9{500}(X_P+Y_P).
\tag{30}
\]

For \(A_2\), its full left and lower cap areas are
\[
\frac1{160}+\frac1{200}=\frac9{800}=d.
\]
Equations (3) and (28) give
\[
\frac{\lambda_2^2}{1+\lambda_2},
\frac{\mu_2^2}{1+\mu_2}
\leq\frac{2d}{q}=\frac12,
\]
and hence
\[
\lambda_2=\frac{a_2}{w_Q}\leq1,\qquad
\mu_2=\frac{b_2}{h_Q}\leq1.
\tag{31}
\]
Equation (27) now gives
\[
a_2b_2
\leq\frac9{400}(X_Q+Y_Q).
\tag{32}
\]

Accepted fact `92a1b537d7e7876c` gives the exact aspect transfer
\[
X_Q+Y_Q\leq\kappa(X_P+Y_P).
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (29), (31), and (33) also give the two cross-term allocation
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{a_2}{\ell_x}\frac{b_0}{h_P}
+\frac{a_0}{w_P}\frac{b_2}{\ell_y}\\
&\quad<
\frac15(X_Q+Y_Q)
\leq\frac{\kappa}{5}(X_P+Y_P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
The definition of \(\Gamma\) in accepted fact
`bc026780453747b8` and (34) show that
\[
\Gamma
<
a_0b_0+a_2b_2
+\frac{12\kappa}{125}(X_P+Y_P).
\tag{35}
\]

Since \(L_0=B_0=c\), \(L_2=B_2=d\), and
\(\overline L_h=L_h/a_h\),
\(\overline B_h=B_h/b_h\), we have the exact identities
\[
a_0b_0
=\frac9{40000\,\overline L_0\overline B_0},
\qquad
a_2b_2
=\frac{81}{640000\,\overline L_2\overline B_2}.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus the middle expression in (2) is exactly the right-hand side of
(35).  From (30), (32), and (33), that expression is strictly less
than
\[
\left(
\frac9{500}+\frac{9\kappa}{400}
+\frac{12\kappa}{125}
\right)(X_P+Y_P)
=\frac{36+237\kappa}{2000}(X_P+Y_P).
\tag{37}
\]
Finally,
\[
\sqrt{65}<\frac{81}{10}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\kappa<\frac{371}{209}<\frac{716}{395},
\tag{38}
\]
where the last comparison is by cross multiplication.  Using (1),
(37), and \(237/395=3/5\), the allocation is strictly less than
\[
\frac{36+237\kappa}{2000}\frac{5000}{291}
=\frac{5(36+237\kappa)}{582}
<
\frac{5(36+237\cdot716/395)}{582}
=4.
\]
This proves both inequalities in (2) and completes the common-apex
case only.
