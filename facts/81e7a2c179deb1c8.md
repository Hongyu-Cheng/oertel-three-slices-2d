## Statement

Let
\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\rho,\sigma,\tau>0,\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1,
\]
and assume
\[
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}\le \rho\le1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta},
\qquad
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\le \sigma\le1+\frac{\beta}{\gamma},
\qquad
\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\le \tau\le1+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma},
\]
\[
\sigma+\tau\ge1,\qquad
\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho\ge1,\qquad
\rho+\frac1\tau\ge1.
\]
Define
\[
A_1=2\beta\gamma+\beta^2(1-\rho)+\gamma^2(1-\sigma),
\]
\[
A_2=2\gamma\alpha+\gamma^2(1-\tau)
     +\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\rho\right),
\]
\[
A_3=2\alpha\beta+\alpha^2\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)
     +\beta^2\left(1-\frac1\tau\right),
\]
and
\[
b=\gamma^2(\sigma+\tau-1)
  +\alpha^2\left(\frac1\sigma+\frac1\rho-1\right)
  +\beta^2\left(\rho+\frac1\tau-1\right).
\]
Then \(A_i\ge0\), and every \(0\le d_i\le A_i\) satisfies
\[
b\ge
\sqrt{d_1d_2}+\sqrt{d_2d_3}+\sqrt{d_3d_1}
-\frac12(d_1+d_2+d_3).
\]
Equality holds if and only if
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,\qquad
d_1=d_2=d_3=\frac29.
\]

## Proof

The endpoint bounds give \(A_i\ge0\), as recorded in both accepted
predecessor facts. Hence the three nonnegative numbers
\[
\sqrt{A_1},\qquad \sqrt{A_2},\qquad \sqrt{A_3}
\]
fall into exactly one of the following exhaustive cases.

First, suppose they satisfy all three triangle inequalities. Accepted fact
`b798e4964b158052` then gives the required inequality for every
\(0\le d_i\le A_i\). It also proves that equality in this case holds exactly
when
\[
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\frac13,\qquad
\rho=\sigma=\tau=1,\qquad
d_1=d_2=d_3=\frac29.
\]

Otherwise, one triangle inequality fails. After a cyclic relabeling, this
means
\[
\sqrt{A_1}>\sqrt{A_2}+\sqrt{A_3}.
\]
Accepted fact `e3e665e2576b37cc` applies to the weakly dominant condition
\(\sqrt{A_1}\ge\sqrt{A_2}+\sqrt{A_3}\) and gives, for every
\(0\le d_i\le A_i\),
\[
b>
\sqrt{d_1d_2}+\sqrt{d_2d_3}+\sqrt{d_3d_1}
-\frac12(d_1+d_2+d_3).
\]
The same accepted fact contains the two cyclic analogues, so this covers
whichever root is dominant. In particular, equality is impossible in every
unbalanced case.

The two cases are exhaustive. The balanced case supplies the stated
equality configuration, and the unbalanced case is strict. This proves both
the universal inequality and the claimed unique equality case.
