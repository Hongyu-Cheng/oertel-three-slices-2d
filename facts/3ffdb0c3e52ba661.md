## Statement

Assume the common affine sign-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, and suppose that the exact scalar table of accepted fact `c5aad5c9408fcf74` is geometrically realized. Thus
\[
p_\varnothing=\frac{121}{10},\qquad
q_{12}=q_{13}=q_{23}=\frac15,\qquad
q_{123}=\frac25,
\]
\[
r_1=r_2=r_3=\frac{19}{10},\qquad
r_{123}=\frac1{10},\qquad
|A_2|=1.
\]
Put
\[
\delta=-1-\sum_{i=1}^3L_i>0,\qquad
\gamma=\delta+2,
\]
\[
q_i=f_i+L_i,
\qquad
\Lambda_Q(x)
=
\left(
-\frac{q_1(x)}{\gamma},
-\frac{q_2(x)}{\gamma},
-\frac{q_3(x)}{\gamma}
\right).
\]
Then \(\Lambda_Q\) is an affine isomorphism onto
\[
\Pi=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^3:\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1\}.
\]
There is no such realization for which \(\Lambda_Q(A_2)\) is invariant under all coordinate permutations of \(\Pi\).

## Proof

Set
\[
\Lambda_K(x)=(-f_1(x),-f_2(x),-f_3(x)).
\]
Accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` shows that this is an affine isomorphism onto \(\Pi\). All three singleton middle cells have positive area in the table. Accepted fact `ce343e749df349fe` therefore gives
\[
\Lambda_K^{-1}(\Delta)\subseteq K,
\qquad
\Delta
:=\{\lambda\in\Pi:\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\ge0\},
\]
and identifies \(R_{123}\) with \(\Lambda_K^{-1}(\Delta)\), up to cutting-line boundaries. Since \(r_{123}=1/10\),
\[
|\Lambda_K^{-1}(\Delta)|=\frac1{10}.
\tag{1}
\]
The coordinate area of \(\Delta\) in \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) is \(1/2\). Hence the constant factor converting \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\)-area under \(\Lambda_K\) to physical area is
\[
\eta=\frac15.
\tag{2}
\]

Define
\[
p_i=f_i-L_i.
\]
Then
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3=\delta.
\]
The ambient \(P_\varnothing\)-chamber is the open triangle
\[
\{p_1,p_2,p_3>0,\ p_1+p_2+p_3=\delta\}.
\]
The affine map \(x\mapsto(p_1(x),p_2(x),p_3(x))\) has the same linear area factor \(\eta\) as \(\Lambda_K\). Its ambient central triangle therefore has physical area
\[
\eta\frac{\delta^2}{2}
=\frac{\delta^2}{10}.
\]
Because \(P_\varnothing\) is contained in this triangle and has area \(121/10\),
\[
\frac{121}{10}
\le
\frac{\delta^2}{10}.
\]
Thus
\[
\delta\ge11,
\qquad
\gamma=\delta+2\ge13.
\tag{3}
\]

Since
\[
q_1+q_2+q_3=-\gamma,
\]
the map \(\Lambda_Q\) indeed takes values in \(\Pi\). Its linear part is \(1/\gamma\) times the linear part of \(\Lambda_K\). Consequently the factor converting coordinate area in \(\Pi\) under \(\Lambda_Q\) to physical area is
\[
\eta_Q=\eta\gamma^2=\frac{\gamma^2}{5}.
\tag{4}
\]

Assume now that
\[
B:=\Lambda_Q(A_2)
\]
is invariant under every coordinate permutation. The positive area
\[
q_{23}=\frac15
\]
allows a point \(z\) to be chosen in the strict interior of that exact cell. Its normalized coordinates have exactly one negative entry and two positive entries. Write the three entries in increasing order as
\[
a<b\le c,
\qquad
a+b+c=1,
\qquad
a<0<b\le c.
\]
Permutation invariance puts every coordinate permutation of \((a,b,c)\) in \(B\). Convexity therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{conv}(S_3\cdot(a,b,c))\subseteq B.
\tag{5}
\]

Put
\[
x=b-a\ge0,\qquad
y=c-b\ge0.
\]
In the planar coordinates \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\), the six permutations occur cyclically as
\[
(a,b),\ (a,c),\ (b,c),\ (c,b),\ (c,a),\ (b,a),
\]
with repetitions allowed when two entries coincide. The shoelace formula gives
\[
\left|
\operatorname{conv}(S_3\cdot(a,b,c))
\right|_{\rm coord}
=
\frac{x^2+4xy+y^2}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
The sum condition is
\[
3a+2x+y=1.
\]
Set
\[
s=2x+y=1-3a.
\]
Since \(a<0\), one has \(s>1\). Also \(y=s-2x\ge0\), so \(x\le s/2\). Substitution in (6) yields
\[
\frac{x^2+4xy+y^2}{2}
=
\frac{s^2-3x^2}{2}
\ge
\frac{s^2}{8}
>
\frac18.
\tag{7}
\]

Equations (4), (5), and (7) now force
\[
|A_2|
>
\frac{\gamma^2}{5}\cdot\frac18
=
\frac{\gamma^2}{40}
\ge
\frac{169}{40}
>
1.
\]
This contradicts \(|A_2|=1\). Therefore no realization of the table can have an \(S_3\)-invariant endpoint \(\Lambda_Q(A_2)\). The proof uses no vertex bound and includes all permutation-invariant convex polygons and their degeneracies; it makes no assertion about nonsymmetric endpoint shapes.
