## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact two-dimensional convex bodies of positive areas \(a_0,a_2\), and put \(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}\).  For an affine function \(f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), define
\[
E_f=
\left\{(x_0,x_2)\in A_0\times A_2:
f\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)<0\right\},
\]
\[
\tau(E_f)=
\inf\left\{
|U|+|V|:
\begin{array}{l}
U\subseteq A_0,\ V\subseteq A_2\text{ are measurable},\\
E_f\subseteq(U\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V)
\end{array}
\right\},
\]
and
\[
\kappa(f)=
\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb R}
\left(
\left|A_0\cap\{f-\sigma\le0\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{f+\sigma\le0\}\right|
\right).
\]
If \(f\) is nonconstant, then
\[
\tau(E_f)=\kappa(f).
\tag{1}
\]
For a constant \(f\equiv c\), the complete table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&c<0&c=0&c>0\\ \hline
\tau(E_f)&m&0&0\\
\kappa(f)&m&m&0.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus (1) also holds for every nonzero constant, and \(f\equiv0\) is the unique one-row exception.

Now let \(A_1\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact two-dimensional convex body of area \(a_1\).  Let \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) be affine with
\[
f_1+f_2+f_3=-1,
\]
put
\[
B_i=A_1\cap\{f_i\le0\},\qquad b_i=|B_i|,
\]
and, for independently chosen \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
R_i(\sigma_i)=
b_i+
\left|A_0\cap\{f_i-\sigma_i\le0\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{f_i+\sigma_i\le0\}\right|.
\]
For every real \(L\),
\[
\left[
\forall(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)\in\mathbb R^3,\quad
\max_i R_i(\sigma_i)\ge L
\right]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\max_i\bigl(b_i+\kappa(f_i)\bigr)\ge L.
\tag{3}
\]

Assume in addition the strict balanced inequalities
\[
a_j<\frac{T}{2}\quad(j=0,1,2),
\qquad T=a_0+a_1+a_2.
\tag{4}
\]
Then, at \(L=2T/9\), the slope criterion is exactly equivalent to the pullback vertex-cover criterion:
\[
\left[
\forall(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)\in\mathbb R^3,\quad
\max_i R_i(\sigma_i)\ge\frac{2T}{9}
\right]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\max_i\left(b_i+\tau(E_{f_i})\right)\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]
This includes every constant row.  More precisely:

1. If \(f_i\equiv c<0\), then
\[
b_i+\tau(E_{f_i})=a_1+m>\frac{T}{2}>\frac{2T}{9},
\]
and \(R_i(\sigma)\ge a_1+m\) for every \(\sigma\).

2. If \(f_i\equiv c>0\), then
\[
b_i=\tau(E_{f_i})=\kappa(f_i)=0,
\]
and \(-c<\sigma<c\) makes both endpoint caps empty.

3. If \(f_i\equiv0\), then \(R_i(\sigma)\ge a_1+m>T/2\) for every \(\sigma\).  If \(\{j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}\), then
\[
b_j+b_k\ge a_1,\qquad
\tau(E_{f_j})+\tau(E_{f_k})\ge m,
\tag{6}
\]
and consequently
\[
\max\{b_j+\tau(E_{f_j}),\,b_k+\tau(E_{f_k})\}
\ge\frac{a_1+m}{2}
>\frac{T}{4}
>\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{7}
\]

## Proof

Write \(f(w)=v\cdot w+c\).  Affineness gives
\[
2f\left(\frac{x_0+x_2}{2}\right)=f(x_0)+f(x_2).
\tag{8}
\]
Assume first that \(f\) is nonconstant, so \(v\ne0\).  For any \(\sigma\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
U_\sigma=A_0\cap\{f\le\sigma\},\qquad
V_\sigma=A_2\cap\{f\le-\sigma\}.
\]
If \(x_0\notin U_\sigma\) and \(x_2\notin V_\sigma\), then
\[
f(x_0)>\sigma,\qquad f(x_2)>-\sigma,
\]
so (8) gives \(f((x_0+x_2)/2)>0\).  Thus
\[
E_f\subseteq(U_\sigma\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V_\sigma),
\]
and
\[
\tau(E_f)\le |U_\sigma|+|V_\sigma|.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\sigma\) proves
\[
\tau(E_f)\le\kappa(f).
\tag{9}
\]

For the reverse inequality, let measurable \(U\subseteq A_0\) and \(V\subseteq A_2\) satisfy
\[
E_f\subseteq(U\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V),
\tag{10}
\]
and put
\[
P=A_0\setminus U,\qquad Q=A_2\setminus V.
\]
Suppose first that \(P\ne\varnothing\) and \(Q\ne\varnothing\).  Equations (8) and (10) give
\[
f(x_0)+f(x_2)\ge0
\qquad(x_0\in P,\ x_2\in Q).
\tag{11}
\]
Set
\[
\alpha=\inf_{x_0\in P}f(x_0).
\]
This is finite because \(f\) is bounded on compact \(A_0\), but it need not be attained.  Every \(x_0\in A_0\) with \(f(x_0)<\alpha\) lies outside \(P\), so
\[
A_0\cap\{f<\alpha\}\subseteq U.
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(x_2\in Q\), choose \(x_0^{(n)}\in P\) such that
\[
f(x_0^{(n)})\longrightarrow\alpha.
\]
Equation (11) gives \(f(x_2)\ge-f(x_0^{(n)})\); passing to the limit yields
\[
f(x_2)\ge-\alpha.
\]
Therefore
\[
A_2\cap\{f<-\alpha\}\subseteq V.
\tag{13}
\]
Every level set of the nonconstant affine function \(f\) is a line and has planar area zero.  Hence (12)-(13) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|U|+|V|
&\ge
\left|A_0\cap\{f<\alpha\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{f<-\alpha\}\right|\\
&=
\left|A_0\cap\{f\le\alpha\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{f\le-\alpha\}\right|\\
&\ge\kappa(f).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to check the empty-complement cases.  If \(P=\varnothing\), then \(U=A_0\) and \(|U|+|V|\ge a_0\).  Boundedness of \(f\) on the compact bodies lets one choose a sufficiently large positive \(\sigma\) for which \(U_\sigma=A_0\) and \(V_\sigma=\varnothing\).  Thus \(\kappa(f)\le a_0\le|U|+|V|\).  If \(Q=\varnothing\), a sufficiently large negative \(\sigma\) makes \(U_\sigma=\varnothing\) and \(V_\sigma=A_2\), so \(\kappa(f)\le a_2\le|U|+|V|\).  These arguments also cover \(P=Q=\varnothing\).  Taking the infimum over all measurable covers (10) gives
\[
\tau(E_f)\ge\kappa(f).
\tag{15}
\]
Together, (9) and (15) prove (1), without assuming that either infimum is attained.

Now let \(f\equiv c\).  If \(c<0\), then \(E_f=A_0\times A_2\).  The complements of a vertex cover cannot both be nonempty, so every cover has cost at least \(m\), and taking one whole side attains \(m\).  Thus \(\tau(E_f)=m\).  Also, for every \(\sigma\), at least one of
\[
c\le\sigma,\qquad c\le-\sigma
\]
holds, so at least one endpoint cap is a whole body.  A choice \(\sigma<c\) gives cost \(a_2\), while \(\sigma>-c\) gives cost \(a_0\), proving \(\kappa(f)=m\).

If \(c>0\), then \(E_f=\varnothing\), so \(\tau(E_f)=0\).  Every \(-c<\sigma<c\) makes both endpoint caps empty, hence \(\kappa(f)=0\).

If \(c=0\), strictness in the definition of \(E_f\) again gives \(E_f=\varnothing\), so \(\tau(E_f)=0\).  If \(\sigma<0\), the \(A_0\)-cap is empty and the \(A_2\)-cap is all of \(A_2\).  If \(\sigma>0\), the \(A_0\)-cap is all of \(A_0\) and the \(A_2\)-cap is empty.  At \(\sigma=0\), both caps are whole.  Therefore \(\kappa(f)=m\).  This proves (2), including the unique discrepancy caused by the strict pullback and closed endpoint caps.

We next prove (3).  If
\[
\max_i\bigl(b_i+\kappa(f_i)\bigr)\ge L,
\]
choose an index attaining the finite maximum.  For every \(\sigma_i\),
\[
R_i(\sigma_i)\ge b_i+\kappa(f_i)\ge L.
\]
Thus every independent triple of slopes satisfies \(\max_iR_i(\sigma_i)\ge L\).

Conversely, suppose
\[
b_i+\kappa(f_i)<L\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{16}
\]
For each row separately, the definition of the infimum supplies a finite \(\sigma_i\) such that
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{f_i-\sigma_i\le0\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{f_i+\sigma_i\le0\}\right|
<L-b_i.
\tag{17}
\]
The three choices can be made simultaneously because the slopes are independent variables.  Equations (16)-(17) give \(R_i(\sigma_i)<L\) for all three indices, so the universal row inequality fails.  This proves the converse by contraposition.  It also shows explicitly why unattained one-row infima cause no gap.

It remains to derive (5), especially when a zero-constant row makes \(\kappa\ne\tau\).  Put \(M=\max\{a_0,a_2\}\).  The strict balanced inequality \(M<T/2\) is equivalent to
\[
M<a_1+m.
\]
Consequently
\[
a_1+m>\frac{M+a_1+m}{2}=\frac T2.
\tag{18}
\]

If \(f_i\equiv c<0\), then \(B_i=A_1\), and (2) gives
\[
b_i+\tau(E_{f_i})=a_1+m.
\]
Every actual endpoint contribution is at least \(\kappa(f_i)=m\), so both sides of (5) hold by (18).  If \(f_i\equiv c>0\), then \(B_i=\varnothing\), and (2) gives the exact zero values stated above.  Hence positive constant rows obey \(\kappa=\tau\) and require no exception.

Suppose finally that \(f_i\equiv0\), and denote the other indices by \(j,k\).  Normalization gives
\[
f_j+f_k=-1.
\tag{19}
\]
For every \(w\in A_1\), at least one of \(f_j(w),f_k(w)\) is strictly negative.  Hence
\[
A_1\subseteq B_j\cup B_k
\]
and finite subadditivity gives
\[
b_j+b_k\ge a_1.
\tag{20}
\]
Similarly, for every \((x_0,x_2)\in A_0\times A_2\), equation (19) at \((x_0+x_2)/2\) implies that at least one of \(f_j,f_k\) is strictly negative there.  Thus
\[
A_0\times A_2=E_{f_j}\cup E_{f_k}.
\tag{21}
\]

Choose arbitrary measurable vertex covers
\[
E_{f_j}\subseteq(U_j\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V_j),
\qquad
E_{f_k}\subseteq(U_k\times A_2)\cup(A_0\times V_k).
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (21)-(22) imply
\[
A_0\times A_2
\subseteq
\bigl((U_j\cup U_k)\times A_2\bigr)
\cup
\bigl(A_0\times(V_j\cup V_k)\bigr).
\tag{23}
\]
If neither \(U_j\cup U_k=A_0\) nor \(V_j\cup V_k=A_2\), choose
\[
x_0\in A_0\setminus(U_j\cup U_k),\qquad
x_2\in A_2\setminus(V_j\cup V_k).
\]
Then \((x_0,x_2)\) is outside the right side of (23), a contradiction.  Therefore at least one of the two unions is its whole body.  In the first case,
\[
|U_j|+|U_k|\ge|U_j\cup U_k|=a_0\ge m;
\]
in the second,
\[
|V_j|+|V_k|\ge|V_j\cup V_k|=a_2\ge m.
\]
Thus every pair of vertex covers in (22) satisfies
\[
(|U_j|+|V_j|)+(|U_k|+|V_k|)\ge m.
\tag{24}
\]
Taking the infimum over the two independent cover choices gives
\[
\tau(E_{f_j})+\tau(E_{f_k})\ge m.
\tag{25}
\]
This passage does not assume attainment: the infimum of a sum over a Cartesian product of two nonempty feasible families equals the sum of the two separate infima, as follows in one direction from the individual lower bounds and in the other by choosing an \(\varepsilon\)-minimizer in each family.

Adding (20) and (25) proves (6), so one of the two indexed quantities is at least \((a_1+m)/2\).  Equation (18) gives
\[
\frac{a_1+m}{2}>\frac T4>\frac{2T}{9},
\]
which proves (7) and the right side of (5).  The left side of (5) also holds because the zero row has \(B_i=A_1\) and actual endpoint contribution at least \(\kappa(f_i)=m\), hence \(R_i(\sigma)\ge a_1+m>T/2\) for every \(\sigma\).

If no zero-constant row exists, (1)-(2) give \(\kappa(f_i)=\tau(E_{f_i})\) for all indices, so (5) follows directly from (3).  If a zero-constant row exists, the preceding argument proves both sides of (5).  This completes every constant and nonconstant case.
