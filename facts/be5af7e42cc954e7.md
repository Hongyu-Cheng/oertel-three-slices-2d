## Statement

Normalize the area of
\(\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) to one.  For a row
vector \(h\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(z\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
\mathcal C(h;z)
=
\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:h\lambda\geq z\}\right|.
\tag{1}
\]

The following asymmetric two-cap theorem holds for every pair of
nonconstant affine functions \(f,g\) on \(\Delta\):
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(f;0)\leq\frac9{20},\qquad
\mathcal C(g;0)\leq\frac3{10}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left|\{f+g\leq0\}\right|>\frac12.}
\tag{2}
\]
It includes vertex-on-line, repeated-value, parallel-line, and
constant-sum boundary cases.

Consider the fixed calibration from accepted fact
`998fb0c4cc064e88`:
\[
A=\frac1{10},\qquad
B=\frac{10999}{10000},\qquad
t=\frac{7333}{15000},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
-584&1167&-584\\
-415&-416&830\\
1000&-750&-245
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]
There are no real matrices \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) and no
\(\sigma\in\mathbb R^3\) satisfying
\[
\mathbf1^\top U=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
\mathbf1^\top V=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
|\det U|=A,\qquad |\det V|=B,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\min_jU_{kj}+\min_jV_{kj}\geq0
\qquad(k=1,2,3),
\tag{6}
\]
and all three strict rows
\[
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the proposed rational middle calibration has a complete exact
endpoint obstruction, not merely a failure of its displayed scalar
split.

## Proof

We first prove (2).  Put
\[
p=\frac9{20},\qquad q=\frac3{10}.
\tag{8}
\]
The complete fixed-cap parametrization and its boundary audit in
accepted fact `b91db809305bf041` apply to every
\(0<p,q<1/2\).  We record the literal specialization used here:
\[
S_1^r(u)=
\left(1,\ 1-u,\ 1-\frac1{ru}\right),
\qquad
S_2^r(u)=
\left(1-\frac1{ru},\ 1,\ 1-u\right),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
D_1^r(u)=
\left(-1,\ u-1,\ \frac1{(1-r)u}-1\right),
\tag{10}
\]
\[
D_2^r(u)=
\left(\frac1{(1-r)u}-1,\ -1,\ u-1\right),
\qquad
D_3^r(u)=
\left(u-1,\ \frac1{(1-r)u}-1,\ -1\right).
\tag{11}
\]
An \(S\)-parameter lies in \([1,1/r]\), and a \(D\)-parameter
lies in \([1,1/(1-r)]\).  Up to simultaneous vertex permutations,
every pair satisfying
\(\mathcal C(f;0)=p,\mathcal C(g;0)=q\), including all support
boundaries, occurs in one of the following eight
support-incidence orbits, followed by an arbitrary relative scale
\(\lambda>0\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{orbit}&f&g\\ \hline
SS_{\rm same}&S_1^p(u)&S_1^q(v)\\
SS_{\rm diff}&S_1^p(u)&S_2^q(v)\\
SD_{\rm in}&S_1^p(u)&D_3^q(v)\\
SD_{\rm out}&S_1^p(u)&D_1^q(v)\\
DS_{\rm in}&D_1^p(u)&S_2^q(v)\\
DS_{\rm out}&D_1^p(u)&S_1^q(v)\\
DD_{\rm same}&D_1^p(u)&D_1^q(v)\\
DD_{\rm diff}&D_1^p(u)&D_2^q(v).
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus it suffices to put
\[
h=f+\lambda g
\tag{13}
\]
in each row of (12).

Write \(h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\).  The condition
\[
\left|\{h\leq0\}\right|\leq\frac12
\tag{14}
\]
is exactly the disjunction of the following seven rational
semialgebraic predicates:
\[
\begin{split}
&h_1,h_2,h_3\geq0,\qquad(h_1,h_2,h_3)\ne(0,0,0); \tag{15}\\
&h_i<0,\quad h_j,h_k\geq0,\quad
2h_i^2\leq(h_j-h_i)(h_k-h_i)
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}); \tag{16}\\
&h_i,h_j\leq0<h_k,\quad
2h_k^2\geq(h_k-h_i)(h_k-h_j)
\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}). \tag{17}
\end{split}
\]
Indeed, (16) is the exact one-negative cap formula, and (17) is the
exact complement formula when \(h_k\) is the unique positive value.
The all-nonpositive and identically zero cases have full lower cap,
so they do not satisfy (14).  Equations (15)-(17) retain every zero
and repeated-value boundary.

For each orbit in (12), substitute (9)-(13) into each of the seven
predicates (15)-(17), together with the corresponding two closed
parameter intervals and \(\lambda>0\).  All denominators are
strictly positive on those domains.  Exact real cylindrical
algebraic decomposition evaluated each query
\[
\operatorname{Resolve}\!\left[
\exists(u,v,\lambda)\in\mathbb R^3:
\text{domain}\wedge\text{one predicate from (15)--(17)},
\mathbb R
\right].
\tag{18}
\]
The complete result was
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
&N&P_1&P_2&P_3&T_1&T_2&T_3\\ \hline
SS_{\rm same}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
SS_{\rm diff}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
SD_{\rm in}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
SD_{\rm out}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
DS_{\rm in}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
DS_{\rm out}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
DD_{\rm same}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F\\
DD_{\rm diff}&F&F&F&F&F&F&F.
\end{array}
\tag{19}
\]
Here \(N\) denotes (15), \(P_i\) denotes (16) with unique negative
index \(i\), \(T_k\) denotes (17) with unique positive index \(k\),
and \(F\) means that the closed existential formula is `False`.
All \(56\) inputs contained only integers and exact rationals;
`FreeQ[queries,_Real]` returned `True`.  WolframScript 1.13.0 with
the local Wolfram Language 14.3.0 kernel returned `False` for all
\(56\) calls and exited with status zero.  Therefore equality in the
two cap hypotheses implies the strict conclusion in (2).

For the monotone form, suppose
\(\mathcal C(f;0)\leq p\) and
\(\mathcal C(g;0)\leq q\).  There are unique thresholds
\(a,b\leq0\) such that
\[
\mathcal C(f;a)=p,\qquad \mathcal C(g;b)=q.
\]
The equality case applied to \(f-a,g-b\) gives
\[
\left|\{f+g\leq a+b\}\right|>\frac12.
\]
Since \(a+b\leq0\), this set is contained in
\(\{f+g\leq0\}\).  This proves (2).

We now apply (2) to (3)-(7).  Accepted fact
`998fb0c4cc064e88` gives the middle caps
\[
c_1=\frac{1361889}{3066001},\qquad
c_2=\frac{830}{1869},\qquad
c_3=\frac{800}{1743}.
\tag{20}
\]
Put
\[
\alpha_i=\frac{t-c_i}{A}.
\]
Exact substitution gives
\[
(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)
=
\left(
\frac{2054650333}{4599001500},
\frac{418459}{934500},
\frac{260473}{871500}
\right).
\tag{21}
\]
The exact margins needed below are
\[
\frac9{20}-\alpha_1
=\frac{7450171}{2299500750}>0,
\qquad
\frac3{10}-\alpha_3
=\frac{977}{871500}>0,
\tag{22}
\]
\[
\frac12-\alpha_2
=\frac{48791}{934500}>0,
\qquad
B-A=\frac{9999}{10000}>0.
\tag{23}
\]
All three \(\alpha_i\) are positive.  SageMath 10.8 over
\(\mathbb Q\) independently checked (20)-(23), the column sums of
\(L\), and \(\det L=2179995\).

We use the following threshold-sum consequence twice.  Let
\(w_1,w_2,w_3\) be nonconstant affine functions satisfying
\[
w_1+w_2+w_3=\kappa.
\]
Let \(\psi_1,\psi_2,\psi_3\) be their unique upper-cap thresholds
at levels
\[
\mathcal C(w_1;\psi_1)=\frac9{20},\qquad
\mathcal C(w_2;\psi_2)=\alpha_2,\qquad
\mathcal C(w_3;\psi_3)=\frac3{10}.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply (2) to \(w_1-\psi_1\) and \(w_3-\psi_3\).  It gives
\[
\left|\{w_2\geq\kappa-\psi_1-\psi_3\}\right|
>\frac12>\alpha_2.
\]
Strict monotonicity of a nonconstant triangle cap and (24) imply
\[
\boxed{\psi_1+\psi_2+\psi_3>\kappa.}
\tag{25}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that (5)-(7) hold.  We first normalize
\(V\).  Define
\[
\nu_k=\min_jV_{kj},\qquad
\nu=(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)^\top,\qquad
s_V=\mathbf1^\top\nu,\qquad
\eta=1-s_V.
\tag{26}
\]
Every column of \(V-\nu\mathbf1^\top\) is entrywise nonnegative and
has coordinate sum \(\eta\).  Hence \(\eta\geq0\).  Equality would
make every column of \(V\) equal to \(\nu\), contrary to
\(\det V\ne0\).  Thus \(\eta>0\), and
\[
Y=\frac{V-\nu\mathbf1^\top}{\eta}
\tag{27}
\]
is nonnegative and column-stochastic.  Also
\[
V=(\eta I+\nu\mathbf1^\top)Y,\qquad
\det V=\eta^2\det Y,
\]
so \(Y\) is invertible.

Define
\[
y_i(\lambda)=\eta(LY)_{i*}\lambda
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{28}
\]
The three functions are nonconstant.  Indeed, if
\((LY)_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top\), then
\(\mathbf1^\top Y^{-1}=\mathbf1^\top\) would give
\(L_{i*}=a\mathbf1^\top\), contrary to (4).  The column-sum
identities give
\[
y_1+y_2+y_3=\eta.
\tag{29}
\]
Let \(\phi_i\) be the three thresholds in (24) for \(y_i\).
Equation (25) gives
\[
\phi_1+\phi_2+\phi_3>\eta.
\tag{30}
\]

Put
\[
e=L\nu-\sigma.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (27)-(28) give
\[
\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)
=\mathcal C(y_i;-e_i).
\tag{32}
\]
The \(U\)-cap in (7) is nonnegative, so (7) and
\(t-c_i=A\alpha_i\) imply
\[
\mathcal C(y_i;-e_i)
<\frac AB\alpha_i<\alpha_i.
\tag{33}
\]
For row \(1\), equations (22), (24), and (33) give
\(-e_1>\phi_1\).  For row \(3\), the same argument using the
\(3/10\) margin gives \(-e_3>\phi_3\).  For row \(2\), (24) and
(33) give \(-e_2>\phi_2\).  Hence (30) yields
\[
\sum_i e_i<-\sum_i\phi_i<-\eta=s_V-1.
\tag{34}
\]
On the other hand, if
\[
Q_\sigma=\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3,
\]
then the column sums of \(L\) and (31) give
\[
\sum_i e_i=s_V-Q_\sigma.
\]
Comparison with (34) proves
\[
Q_\sigma>1.
\tag{35}
\]

Finally normalize \(U\).  Define
\[
\ell_k=\min_jU_{kj},\qquad
\ell=(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)^\top,\qquad
s_U=\mathbf1^\top\ell,\qquad
\rho=1-s_U.
\tag{36}
\]
Exactly as above, \(\rho>0\), and
\[
X=\frac{U-\ell\mathbf1^\top}{\rho}
\tag{37}
\]
is nonnegative, invertible, and column-stochastic.  The nonconstant
functions
\[
z_i(\lambda)=\rho(LX)_{i*}\lambda
\tag{38}
\]
satisfy
\[
z_1+z_2+z_3=\rho.
\tag{39}
\]
Let \(\theta_i\) be the three thresholds in (24) for \(z_i\).
Equation (25) gives
\[
\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3>\rho.
\tag{40}
\]

Put
\[
d=L\ell+\sigma.
\tag{41}
\]
Equations (37)-(38) give
\[
\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
=\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i).
\tag{42}
\]
Dropping the nonnegative \(V\)-cap from (7) gives
\[
\mathcal C(z_i;-d_i)<\alpha_i.
\tag{43}
\]
Using (22), (24), and strict cap monotonicity exactly as above,
(43) implies
\[
d_i<-\theta_i
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Therefore (40) gives
\[
\sum_i d_i<-\sum_i\theta_i<-\rho=s_U-1.
\tag{44}
\]
But the column sums of \(L\), (35), and (41) give
\[
\sum_i d_i=s_U+Q_\sigma>s_U+1,
\tag{45}
\]
contradicting (44).

Thus (5)-(7) have no real solution.  Compatibility (6), the signs
of the two determinants, and any ordering of endpoint projection
values were not used, so the impossibility holds uniformly over all
of them.
