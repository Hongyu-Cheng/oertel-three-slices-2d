## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2,K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be compact convex bodies with
nonempty interiors and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq K.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(f_i,f_\ell,f_j\) be nonconstant affine functions satisfying
\[
f_i+f_\ell+f_j=-1,
\tag{2}
\]
and fix \(a_i,a_\ell\in\mathbb R\). Use the closed cells
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
P=A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i,\ f_\ell\ge a_\ell\},&
X_i=A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i,\ f_\ell\ge a_\ell\},\\
X_\ell=A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i,\ f_\ell\le a_\ell\},&
Z=A_0\cap\{f_i\le a_i,\ f_\ell\le a_\ell\},
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
Q=A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i,\ f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\},&
B_i=A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a_i,\ f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\},\\
B_\ell=A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i,\ f_\ell\le-a_\ell\},&
R=A_2\cap\{f_i\le-a_i,\ f_\ell\le-a_\ell\}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
p=|P|,\qquad q=|Q|,\qquad
x_i=|X_i|,\qquad x_\ell=|X_\ell|,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
b_i=|B_i|,\qquad b_\ell=|B_\ell|,\qquad w=|R|,
\tag{6}
\]
and assume
\[
p>0,\qquad q>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Define the high-\(\ell\) endpoint bodies
\[
A_\ell^+=A_0\cap\{f_\ell\ge a_\ell\}=P\cup X_i,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
B_\ell^+=A_2\cap\{f_\ell\ge-a_\ell\}=Q\cup B_i,
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
G_\ell=\frac{A_\ell^++B_\ell^+}{2},
\qquad
D_R=\frac{P+R}{2}.
\tag{10}
\]
These are the common-source bodies in the setting of accepted fact
`7d5ee83bd565a34f`; the present statement adds the full endpoint
adjacency through \(A_0,A_2\).
Empty \(R\) gives an empty \(D_R\). Define the uncovered \(i\)-sector
\[
\mathcal L_i=
\left(
D_R\cap\{f_j>0,\ f_i\le0,\ f_\ell>0\}
\right)\setminus G_\ell.
\tag{11}
\]

For a nonempty compact convex set \(C\), let
\[
h_C(u)=\max_{x\in C}u\mathbin{\cdot}x
\qquad(u\in(\mathbb R^2)^*).
\tag{12}
\]
If \(R\ne\varnothing\), define
\[
\Delta_\ell(u)=
h_R(u)-h_{B_\ell^+}(u)
-h_{A_\ell^+}(u)+h_P(u).
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
D_R\subseteq G_\ell
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta_\ell(u)\le0
\quad\text{for every covector }u.
}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, if \(\mathcal L_i\ne\varnothing\), there are
\(z\in\mathcal L_i\) and a nonzero covector \(u\) such that
\[
2u\mathbin{\cdot}z>
h_{A_\ell^+}(u)+h_{B_\ell^+}(u),
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\boxed{
h_R(u)-h_{B_\ell^+}(u)
>
h_{A_\ell^+}(u)-h_P(u)
\ge0.
}
\tag{16}
\]
More quantitatively,
\[
\Delta_\ell(u)
\ge
2u\mathbin{\cdot}z
-h_{A_\ell^+}(u)-h_{B_\ell^+}(u)
>0.
\tag{17}
\]

For such a covector, define the exposed endpoint caps
\[
C_0(u)=
A_\ell^+\cap
\{x:u\mathbin{\cdot}x>h_P(u)\},
\tag{18}
\]
\[
C_2(u)=
A_2\cap
\{x:u\mathbin{\cdot}x>h_{B_\ell^+}(u)\}.
\tag{19}
\]
They satisfy
\[
\boxed{
C_0(u)\subseteq X_i,\qquad
C_2(u)\subseteq B_\ell\cup R,
}
\tag{20}
\]
\[
|C_0(u)|\le x_i,\qquad
0<|C_2(u)|\le b_\ell+w.
\tag{21}
\]
The support depth of \(C_0(u)\) beyond \(P\) is
\[
h_{A_\ell^+}(u)-h_P(u),
\]
whereas \(C_2(u)\) has support depth at least
\[
h_R(u)-h_{B_\ell^+}(u).
\]
Thus (16) says that the target low-\(\ell\) protrusion is strictly
deeper than the source \(i\)-mixed protrusion.

At the exact calibration
\[
x_i=x_\ell=\frac3{200},\qquad
b_i=b_\ell=\frac1{160},\qquad
w=\frac1{200},
\tag{22}
\]
equation (21) becomes
\[
|C_0(u)|\le\frac3{200},
\qquad
0<|C_2(u)|\le\frac9{800}.
\tag{23}
\]

The symmetric statement is obtained only after the \(i\)-sector
argument above: put
\[
A_i^+=A_0\cap\{f_i\ge a_i\}=P\cup X_\ell,
\qquad
B_i^+=A_2\cap\{f_i\ge-a_i\}=Q\cup B_\ell,
\tag{24}
\]
\[
G_i=\frac{A_i^++B_i^+}{2},
\]
and replace
\[
(\ell,i,X_i,B_\ell)
\quad\text{by}\quad
(i,\ell,X_\ell,B_i).
\]
Noncoverage of the \(\ell\)-sector then forces
\[
h_R(u)-h_{B_i^+}(u)
>
h_{A_i^+}(u)-h_P(u),
\tag{25}
\]
with exposed caps contained in \(B_i\cup R\) and \(X_\ell\).

## Proof

The positive-area assumptions in (7) ensure that
\(P,A_\ell^+,B_\ell^+\) are nonempty compact convex bodies. If
\(R=\varnothing\), then \(D_R=\varnothing\), so
\(\mathcal L_i=\varnothing\) and there is nothing to prove. Assume
\(R\ne\varnothing\).

Support functions add under Minkowski addition and scale under positive
homothety. Therefore
\[
h_{2D_R}(u)=h_P(u)+h_R(u),
\qquad
h_{2G_\ell}(u)=h_{A_\ell^+}(u)+h_{B_\ell^+}(u).
\tag{26}
\]
Containment of compact convex sets is equivalent to pointwise
domination of their support functions. Thus
\[
D_R\subseteq G_\ell
\]
is equivalent to
\[
h_P(u)+h_R(u)
\le h_{A_\ell^+}(u)+h_{B_\ell^+}(u)
\quad\text{for every }u,
\]
which is exactly (14).

Suppose \(\mathcal L_i\ne\varnothing\), and take
\(z\in\mathcal L_i\). The set \(G_\ell\) is compact and convex, while
\(z\notin G_\ell\). Strict separation gives a nonzero covector \(u\)
satisfying (15). Since \(z\in D_R\), write
\[
2z=x+y,\qquad x\in P,\quad y\in R.
\]
Then
\[
2u\mathbin{\cdot}z
\le h_P(u)+h_R(u).
\]
Combining this with (15) gives (17), hence the strict inequality in
(16). The nonnegative inequality in (16) follows from
\(P\subseteq A_\ell^+\).

If \(x\in C_0(u)\), then \(x\in A_\ell^+=P\cup X_i\), while
\[
u\mathbin{\cdot}x>h_P(u)
\]
excludes \(x\in P\). Therefore \(x\in X_i\), proving the first
inclusion in (20). If \(x\in C_2(u)\), the strict inequality in (19)
excludes
\[
x\in B_\ell^+=Q\cup B_i.
\]
The four closed cells in (4) cover \(A_2\), with overlaps only on
cutting lines, so \(x\in B_\ell\cup R\). This proves the second
inclusion in (20).

Equation (16) gives
\[
h_R(u)>h_{B_\ell^+}(u).
\]
A support point of \(R\) therefore belongs to \(C_2(u)\). Since
\(A_2\) has nonempty interior and the cutting threshold in (19) is
strictly below its support value, \(C_2(u)\) has positive planar area.
The area bounds in (21) now follow from (20), including zero-area
source caps \(C_0(u)\). Substitution gives (23).

For affine covariance, let \(T\) be invertible. The support function
transforms by
\[
h_{TC}(u)=h_C(T^*u).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus (14)-(17) for the transformed configuration are exactly the same
inequalities evaluated at the pulled-back covector \(T^*u\).
If \(|\det T|=1\), the areas in (21) are unchanged. Consequently a
determinant-one thin-spike stress can change Euclidean normal depths
and widths but cannot change the support-order alternative or its cap
areas.

Concretely, for any \(m,d>0\), the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,-m/d),(0,m/d),(d,0)\}
\]
has area \(m\) and support depth \(d\) in the first coordinate.
Applying \((x,y)\mapsto(\tau x,y/\tau)\) preserves its area and
multiplies that depth by \(\tau\). Thus cap area alone supplies no
upper or lower control on the depth appearing in (16).

Finally, the inequalities
\[
|C_2(u)|\le\frac9{800}
<\frac3{200}
\]
do not contradict the strict depth ordering (16): planar cap area is
not ordered by support depth without a transverse-chord bound. Hence
the first unsupported step in an area-only closure would be to infer
from (16) that the target cap must have at least the source cap's area.
The symmetric argument follows from the substitutions in (24)-(25).
