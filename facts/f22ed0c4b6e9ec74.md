## Statement

Assume the normalized Regime A Alternative 1 homothetic setting of accepted fact `f37aced9de558c8d`.  In its common two-cut coordinates put
\[
\sigma=x+y,\qquad \eta=x-y,
\]
and assume the normalized strip endpoint of accepted fact `ab193fb25d3fcfa9`,
\[
A_0=\{(x,y):0\leq\sigma\leq1/w,\ |\eta|\leq w\},
\qquad A_2=hA_0,
\qquad w>0.
\]
Thus \(|A_0|=1\), \(|A_2|=h^2\), and, with
\[
\lambda=\frac{1+h}{2},\qquad d=\lambda^2,
\]
the actual middle body \(K\), of area \(s\), contains
\[
D=\lambda A_0.
\]
Suppose
\[
0<h<h_0:=\frac{2\sqrt{71}-9}{29},
\qquad
1-h^2<s<1+h^2,
\]
and suppose that the strip is non-thin:
\[
M:=w\left(\frac{4s}{\lambda}-\lambda\right)>1.
\]
Write
\[
c_i=|K\cap\{x\leq0\}|,\qquad
c_\ell=|K\cap\{y\leq0\}|,\qquad
c_j=|K\cap\{\sigma\geq-1\}|,
\]
\[
u_i=|A_0\cap\{x\leq0\}|,\qquad
u_\ell=|A_0\cap\{y\leq0\}|,
\qquad
t=\frac{2(1+h^2+s)}9.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\max\left\{
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i,\
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell,\
c_j+h^2
\right\}\geq t.}
\]
The conclusion includes supporting cuts, nontrivial support faces, the equality \(h=1/5\) in the proof split, and every target equality.  Together with `ab193fb25d3fcfa9` at \(M\leq1\) and `f37aced9de558c8d` at \(h\geq h_0\), it also covers both interface equalities \(M=1\) and \(h=h_0\).

## Proof

The number \(h_0\) is the positive root of
\[
29h^2+18h-7=0.
\]
This quadratic is strictly increasing for \(h>0\) and is positive at \(h=1/3\), so the present range has
\[
h<\frac13,\qquad h^2<\frac19<\frac15.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the displayed Regime A strip for \(s\) is exactly the first branch stated above.

Assume for contradiction that all three quantities in the maximum are strictly below \(t\):
\[
c_i+(1+h^2)u_i<t,\qquad
c_\ell+(1+h^2)u_\ell<t,\qquad
c_j+h^2<t.
\tag{2}
\]
We first recover the simultaneous two-side estimate
\[
c_i+c_\ell\geq\frac{8s}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
This is the threshold-independent part of the geometry behind accepted fact `f37aced9de558c8d`, but we give the argument because here \(h<h_0\).

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(K\).  The planar centroid halfplane inequality recorded in accepted fact `6e68b44550cac3c8` says that every closed halfplane containing \(g\) cuts area at least \(4s/9\).  The Regime A lower bound gives
\[
\frac{4s}{9}-(t-h^2)
=\frac{2s+7h^2-2}{9}
>\frac{5h^2}{9}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently \(f_j(g)>0\), since otherwise (2) and the centroid inequality would contradict (4).  Since
\[
f_i+f_j+f_\ell=-1,
\]
at least one of \(f_i(g),f_\ell(g)\) is nonpositive.  Interchange \(i,\ell\) if necessary and assume \(f_i(g)\leq0\).  Then
\[
c_i\geq\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{5}
\]

Put
\[
F(z)=2\sqrt{sz}-2z.
\]
The function \(F\) is decreasing on \([4s/9,s]\).  Also \(t<s\), because
\[
7s>7(1-h^2)>2(1+h^2)
\]
by (1).  Direct substitution gives
\[
4st-(3t-h^2)^2
=
\frac{
4(s-1+h^2)(s+1+2h^2)+h^2(8-9h^2)
}{9}>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(3t-h^2>0\), so (6) is equivalent to
\[
F(t)>t-h^2>c_j.
\tag{7}
\]

If \(f_\ell(g)\leq0\), then
\[
c_\ell\geq\frac{4s}{9}
=F\left(\frac{4s}{9}\right)
\geq F(c_i).
\]
If \(f_\ell(g)>0\), apply accepted fact `0688f679297c8baa` with
\[
(f_1,f_2,f_3)=(f_\ell,f_j,f_i).
\]
It gives
\[
\max\{c_\ell,c_j\}\geq F(c_i).
\]
Equations (2), (5), (7), and the monotonicity of \(F\) give
\[
F(c_i)>F(t)>c_j,
\]
so again \(c_\ell\geq F(c_i)\).  Finally,
\[
z+F(z)=2\sqrt{sz}-z
\]
is increasing for \(0<z<s\).  Hence
\[
c_i+c_\ell
\geq c_i+F(c_i)
\geq\frac{4s}{9}+F\left(\frac{4s}{9}\right)
=\frac{8s}{9},
\]
proving (3).

The Jacobian of \((x,y)\mapsto(\sigma,\eta)\) has absolute value \(2\).  A direct integration in the strip gives
\[
u_i=u_\ell=:u(w)
=
\begin{cases}
w^2/4,&0<w\leq1,\\[1mm]
1/2-1/(4w^2),&w\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
Adding the first two inequalities in (2) and using (3) yields
\[
2(1+h^2)u(w)
<
2t-\frac{8s}{9}
=\frac{4(1+h^2-s)}9.
\tag{9}
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=1+h^2-s.
\]
Then \(0<\Delta<2h^2\).  Equations (1), (8), and (9) imply \(u(w)<1/4\), so \(w<1\).  Therefore
\[
u(w)=\frac{w^2}{4},
\qquad
w^2<\frac{8\Delta}{9(1+h^2)}
<\frac{16h^2}{9(1+h^2)}.
\tag{10}
\]

We next use the full rectangle \(D\), not merely its area.  In the \((\sigma,\eta)\)-plane its image is
\[
R=[0,\lambda/w]\times[-\lambda w,\lambda w].
\]
Write
\[
L=\frac{\lambda}{w},\qquad a=\lambda w.
\]
The image of \(K\) has area \(2s\).  For any point \((\sigma,\eta)\) in that image, convexity puts into the image of \(K\) the convex hull of the point with each horizontal edge
\[
[0,L]\times\{-a\},\qquad [0,L]\times\{a\}
\]
of \(R\).  The two triangle areas are
\[
\frac{L}{2}|\eta+a|,
\qquad
\frac{L}{2}|\eta-a|,
\]
and each is at most \(2s\).  Consequently every point of \(K\) satisfies
\[
|\eta|\leq
w\left(\frac{4s}{\lambda}-\lambda\right)
=M.
\tag{11}
\]

Consider the root exterior \(K\cap\{\sigma<-1\}\).  A point of this exterior which is not in the \(i\)-cap has
\[
\eta>-\sigma.
\]
Together with (11), this places it in the ambient triangle
\[
-M<\sigma<-1,\qquad -\sigma<\eta\leq M.
\]
Its \((\sigma,\eta)\)-area is \((M-1)^2/2\), hence its physical area is
\[
\theta:=\frac{(M-1)^2}{4}.
\]
The reflected statement holds for the part of the root exterior outside the \(\ell\)-cap.  Thus
\[
c_i\geq s-c_j-\theta,\qquad
c_\ell\geq s-c_j-\theta.
\tag{12}
\]
Combining either inequality in (12) with (2) gives
\[
\theta
>
s-2t+h^2+(1+h^2)\frac{w^2}{4}.
\tag{13}
\]
But
\[
s-2t+h^2=\frac{5s+5h^2-4}{9}>\frac19
\tag{14}
\]
because \(s>1-h^2\).  Since \(M>1\), equations (13)-(14) force
\[
\boxed{M>\frac53.}
\tag{15}
\]

We now obtain a contradiction in two exact parameter ranges.
Containment gives \(s\geq d\), so \(4s-d>0\).

First suppose \(0<h\leq1/5\).  From (10), \(s<1+h^2\), and
\[
d=\lambda^2=\frac{(1+h)^2}{4},
\]
we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
M^2
&=w^2\frac{(4s-d)^2}{d}\\
&<
\frac{16h^2}{9(1+h^2)}
\frac{(4(1+h^2)-d)^2}{d}\\
&=
U(h):=
\frac{
4h^2(15h^2-2h+15)^2
}{
9(1+h)^2(1+h^2)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Exact differentiation gives
\[
U'(h)=
\frac{
8h(15h^2-2h+15)
(15h^5+30h^4+13h^3+43h^2-4h+15)
}{
9(1+h)^3(1+h^2)^2
}>0
\tag{17}
\]
on \(0<h\leq1/5\).  Therefore
\[
M^2<U(h)\leq U(1/5)
=\frac{2888}{1053}
<\frac{25}{9},
\]
contradicting (15).

It remains to suppose
\[
\frac15\leq h<h_0.
\tag{18}
\]
Using \(a=\lambda w\), \(d=\lambda^2\), the definition of \(M\) becomes
\[
M=a\,\frac{4s-d}{d}.
\]
Thus (15) gives
\[
a>\frac{5d}{3(4s-d)}.
\tag{19}
\]

The third inequality in (2) implies \(c_j<s\), since
\[
s-(t-h^2)
=\frac{7s+7h^2-2}{9}
>\frac59.
\]
Hence \(K\) contains a point \(p\) with \(\sigma(p)=-b<-1\), where \(b>1\).  Convexity puts into \(K\) the triangle joining \(p\) to the full vertical edge
\[
\{0\}\times[-a,a]
\]
of \(D\).  The part of this triangle in \(-1\leq\sigma\leq0\) has physical area
\[
a\left(1-\frac{1}{2b}\right)>\frac a2.
\]
It is disjoint in area from \(D\), and both sets lie in the closed root cap \(\{\sigma\geq-1\}\).  Therefore (19) yields
\[
c_j>d+\frac a2
>
d+\frac{5d}{6(4s-d)}.
\tag{20}
\]

Since \(s<1+h^2\),
\[
d+\frac{5d}{6(4s-d)}
>
d+\frac{5d}{6(4(1+h^2)-d)},
\tag{21}
\]
while
\[
t-h^2<\frac{4-5h^2}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]
Exact simplification gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&d+\frac{5d}{6(4(1+h^2)-d)}
-\frac{4-5h^2}{9}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{
435h^4+212h^3+324h^2+344h-75
}{
36(15h^2-2h+15)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
The denominator is positive.  The numerator is strictly increasing for \(h>0\), and at \(h=1/5\) it equals \(1144/125>0\).  Equations (18) and (21)-(23) therefore give
\[
c_j>t-h^2,
\]
contradicting the third inequality in (2).

Both parameter ranges are impossible, so the three strict inequalities (2) cannot hold simultaneously.  This proves the asserted nonstrict maximum inequality.  Every line used as a cap boundary has planar area zero.  The triangle argument allows supporting faces and becomes only stronger as \(b\) increases.  No division by a cap area, chord length, or contact angle occurred, so all support and zero-cell degeneracies are included.
