## Statement

Use all notation and conventions of accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.
For arbitrary choices
\[
u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)
\qquad(\ell=1,2,3),
\]
put
\[
v_\ell=b_\ell-u_\ell,\qquad
c_\ell=Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell),\qquad
d_\ell=2\rho_\ell-c_\ell,\qquad
w_\ell=F_{k,q_\ell}(d_\ell).
\tag{1}
\]
Then, including zero demand, capacity demand, and nonunique contacts,
\[
w_\ell\ge v_\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{
 \bigl(A_j\cap\{q_\ell\cdot x\le c_\ell\}\bigr)+{}
 \bigl(A_k\cap\{q_\ell\cdot x\le d_\ell\}\bigr)}
 {2}
\subseteq
A_1\cap\{q_\ell\cdot x\le\rho_\ell\},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
2\sqrt{p_\ell}\ge \sqrt{u_\ell}+\sqrt{w_\ell}.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(W>0\) and
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell c_\ell<0,
\tag{5}
\]
then the three reflected lower halfplanes
\[
\{x:q_\ell\cdot x\le d_\ell\}
\tag{6}
\]
cover \(\mathbb R^2\), and therefore
\[
m\le\sum_{\ell=1}^3 w_\ell.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, contact balance would follow from the following exact
simultaneous reflected-cap deficit:
\[
\boxed{
W>0\ \Longrightarrow\
\min_{\substack{u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\\ \ell=1,2,3}}
\sum_{\ell=1}^3
F_{k,q_\ell}\!\left(
 2\rho_\ell-Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)
\right)<m.}
\tag{SRD}
\]
Condition (SRD) is not claimed here.

## Proof

Fix \(\ell\).  We first prove (2) and establish the nonemptiness needed
for Brunn--Minkowski.

Suppose \(b_\ell>0\).  Since \(u_\ell\) is an actual maximizing contact
and \(v_\ell=b_\ell-u_\ell\),
\[
c_\ell+Q_{k,q_\ell}(v_\ell)
=E_{q_\ell}(b_\ell).
\tag{8}
\]
The actual optimizer classification gives
\[
E_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)\le2\rho_\ell.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, equality holds in the crossing regime.  It also holds at the
left endpoint when that endpoint has \(p_\ell=0\), and at the right
endpoint whenever \(b_\ell>0\), because the active value is then the
outer branch.  At the outer-capacity endpoint \(b_\ell=m\), the middle
branch can dominate, which gives the weak inequality in (9).  These are
all positive-demand optimizer cases.

Equations (8)--(9) give
\[
d_\ell=2\rho_\ell-c_\ell
\ge Q_{k,q_\ell}(v_\ell).
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore the reflected cap
\[
A_k\cap\{q_\ell\cdot x\le d_\ell\}
\]
is nonempty, including when \(v_\ell=0\), and cap monotonicity gives
\[
w_\ell=F_{k,q_\ell}(d_\ell)\ge v_\ell.
\tag{11}
\]

Now suppose \(b_\ell=0\).  Then the complete contact set is
\(\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(0)=\{0\}\), so \(u_\ell=v_\ell=0\).  The
zero-demand endpoint case in accepted fact `65512ded3a06d971` gives
\[
2\rho_\ell\le
Q_{j,q_\ell}(0)+Q_{k,q_\ell}(0).
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(d_\ell\le Q_{k,q_\ell}(0)\).  The closed cap at or below the
lower support endpoint has area zero, so \(w_\ell=0=v_\ell\).  This
proves (2) in every case without replacing the actual value
\(E_q(0)=-\infty\) by its finite extension.

We next prove (3)--(4).  Full compatibility gives
\[
\frac{A_j+A_k}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{13}
\]
For a point from each cap on the left of (3), its \(q_\ell\)-coordinate
after averaging is at most
\[
\frac{c_\ell+d_\ell}{2}=\rho_\ell.
\]
This proves (3), even if one cap is empty.

If \(b_\ell>0\), the first cap in (3) is nonempty because
\(c_\ell=Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\) is at or above its lower support
endpoint.  The second cap is nonempty by (10).  Their areas are
\(u_\ell\) and \(w_\ell\), while the middle cap on the right has area
\(p_\ell\), by accepted fact `58b8e7bb5c3be36f`.  Planar
Brunn--Minkowski applied to these two nonempty compact convex sets and
then (3) gives
\[
\sqrt{p_\ell}\ge
\frac{\sqrt{u_\ell}+\sqrt{w_\ell}}2,
\]
which is (4).  If \(b_\ell=0\), the preceding zero-demand argument gives
\(u_\ell=w_\ell=0\), so (4) holds directly; no Brunn--Minkowski
application to a possibly empty reflected cap is used.

Now assume \(W>0\) and (5).  From (1),
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell d_\ell
=2W-\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell c_\ell>0.
\tag{14}
\]
If a point \(x\) lay outside all halfplanes (6), then
\[
0=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell q_\ell\right)\cdot x
>\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell d_\ell>0,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence (6) covers the plane.  Intersecting with
\(A_k\), taking areas, and using subadditivity gives (7).

The contact sets are compact and the maps in (1) are continuous, so the
minimum in (SRD) is attained.  If contact balance failed under \(W>0\),
then every contact choice would satisfy (5).  If (SRD) were available,
apply it to its minimizing choice.  Equation (7) would give the opposite
weak inequality, a contradiction.  Thus (SRD) would prove
\[
\max_{u_\ell\in\mathcal U_{q_\ell}(b_\ell)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^3\alpha_\ell Q_{j,q_\ell}(u_\ell)\ge0.
\]

No derivation of (SRD) from full midpoint containment is supplied here.
The directionwise argument above yields only the three separate bounds
\[
w_\ell\le
\left(2\sqrt{p_\ell}-\sqrt{u_\ell}\right)^2,
\tag{15}
\]
which follow from (4).  A proof of (SRD) still needs a simultaneous
cross-direction estimate for the three actual contact sets.  The
pointwise bounds (15) alone do not provide that estimate.
