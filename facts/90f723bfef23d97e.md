## Statement

Under the displayed Regime A and Alternative 1 hypotheses of accepted fact
`879a3c45b5f50670`, let
\[
\sigma=x+y,\qquad \eta=x-y,
\]
\[
A_0=\{(x,y):0\leq\sigma\leq10,\ |\eta|\leq1/10\},
\qquad A_2=\frac1{10}A_0,
\]
take the actual shifts \(a_i=a_\ell=0\), and let
\[
f_i=\alpha x,\qquad f_\ell=\beta y,\qquad
f_j=-1-\alpha x-\beta y,
\qquad \alpha,\beta>0.
\]
There is no compact two-dimensional convex body \(K\) satisfying
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac{11}{20}A_0\subseteq K
\]
and every displayed hypothesis of accepted fact `879a3c45b5f50670`.

## Proof

The coordinate map from \((\sigma,\eta)\) to \((x,y)\) has absolute
Jacobian \(1/2\).  Hence
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad r=|A_2|=\frac1{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
The \(x\)-low part of \(A_0\) is the triangle
\[
0\leq\sigma\leq\frac1{10},\qquad
-\frac1{10}\leq\eta\leq-\sigma.
\]
Its physical area is \(1/400\).  Reflection in \(\eta=0\) gives the
\(y\)-low part, and homothety by \(1/10\) gives the corresponding two
small-endpoint caps.  Therefore
\[
u_i=u_\ell=\frac1{400},\qquad
v_i=v_\ell=\frac1{40000},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
e:=u_i+v_i=u_\ell+v_\ell=\frac{101}{40000}.
\tag{3}
\]
The two low triangles in \(A_0\) have disjoint interiors.  Thus the
positive-positive cells and simultaneous low-low cell have areas
\[
p=\frac{199}{200},\qquad
q=\frac{199}{20000}>0,\qquad
\omega=0.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular this family lies in the \(q>0\) alternative of accepted fact
`879a3c45b5f50670`.

Put
\[
s=|K|,\qquad t=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\]
Since \(r=1/100\leq1/5\), the full Regime A strip in that accepted fact is
\[
\frac{99}{100}<s<\frac{101}{100}.
\tag{5}
\]
Its three strict root low-mass inequalities give
\[
c_i,c_\ell<t-e,\qquad c_j<t-r.
\tag{6}
\]

Define the concurrent, unshifted third middle cap by
\[
\widehat C_j
:=K\cap\{\alpha x+\beta y\geq0\},
\qquad
\widehat c_j:=|\widehat C_j|.
\tag{7}
\]
The actual \(j\)-cap is
\[
K\cap\{f_j\leq0\}
=K\cap\{\alpha x+\beta y\geq-1\},
\]
so
\[
\widehat c_j\leq c_j<t-r.
\tag{8}
\]

Let
\[
D:=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac{11}{20}A_0.
\]
The positive-positive cell of \(D\) lies in \(\widehat C_j\), because
\(\alpha,\beta>0\).  Its exact area is
\[
\left(\frac{11}{20}\right)^2p
=\frac{121}{400}\frac{199}{200}
=\frac{24079}{80000}.
\tag{9}
\]
The upper strip endpoint in (5) is used here:
\[
\widehat c_j
\geq\frac{24079}{80000}
>\frac{101}{400}
>\frac s4.
\tag{10}
\]

Also
\[
t-r=\frac{2s}{9}+\frac{193}{900}.
\]
The lower strip endpoint in (5) gives the strict estimate
\[
\frac{79s}{180}-(t-r)
>
\frac{79}{180}\frac{99}{100}
-\left(\frac29\frac{99}{100}+\frac{193}{900}\right)
=\frac1{18000}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (8), (10), and (11),
\[
\frac s4<\widehat c_j<\frac{79s}{180}.
\tag{12}
\]

Similarly,
\[
t-e=\frac{2s}{9}+\frac{79891}{360000},
\]
and the lower strip endpoint gives
\[
\frac{447s}{1000}-(t-e)
>
\left(\frac{447}{1000}-\frac29\right)\frac{99}{100}
-\frac{79891}{360000}
=\frac{1099}{1800000}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (6) and (13) imply
\[
c_i,c_\ell<\frac{447s}{1000}<\frac s2.
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to treat the location of the common fan center \(0\).  Since
\(0\in D\subseteq K\), either \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\) or
\(0\in\partial K\).

Suppose first that \(0\in\partial K\).  Take a nonzero supporting linear
functional at \(0\), oriented to be nonnegative on \(K\).  It is
nonnegative on \(D\), and \(D\) contains the segment
\[
\{(\sigma,\eta):\sigma=0,\ |\eta|\leq11/200\}.
\]
The functional must therefore have zero \(\eta\)-coefficient.  Since \(D\)
also contains points with \(\sigma>0\), it is a positive multiple of
\(\sigma\), and hence
\[
K\subseteq\{\sigma\geq0\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Write
\[
\alpha x+\beta y=A\sigma+B\eta,
\qquad
A=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}>0,\qquad
B=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2},\qquad |B|<A.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(B>0\), then on \(\{\sigma\geq0\}\)
\[
A\sigma+B\eta<0
\ \Longrightarrow\
\eta<-\frac AB\sigma<-\sigma
\ \Longrightarrow\ x<0.
\]
If \(B<0\), the symmetric implication is \(y<0\).  If \(B=0\), the
inequality \(A\sigma+B\eta<0\) is impossible.  Thus, up to interchanging
\(i,\ell\),
\[
K\setminus\widehat C_j\subseteq K\cap\{x<0\}.
\]
All cutting lines have planar area zero, so
\[
s\leq\widehat c_j+c_i
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
s\leq\widehat c_j+c_\ell.
\tag{17}
\]
But (12) and (14) make the relevant right side strictly smaller than
\[
\left(\frac{79}{180}+\frac{447}{1000}\right)s
=\frac{7973}{9000}s<s,
\]
contradicting (17).  Hence the boundary-center case is impossible.

Finally suppose that \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\).  Apply accepted fact
`18a556eb816782fb` with
\[
B=K,\qquad S=s,\qquad O=0,\qquad
g_1(x,y)=\alpha x,\qquad g_2(x,y)=\beta y.
\]
The body is compact and two-dimensional, the fan center is in its
interior, and \(g_1,g_2\) are nonparallel linear forms vanishing at \(0\).
Their three cap areas are exactly
\[
b_1=c_i,\qquad b_2=c_\ell,\qquad b_3=\widehat c_j.
\]
Equations (12) and (14) verify every hypothesis of the consequent
three-fan implication in that accepted fact:
\[
\frac s4<b_3<\frac{79s}{180},\qquad b_1,b_2<\frac s2.
\]
It follows that
\[
\max\{c_i,c_\ell\}\geq\frac{447s}{1000},
\]
contrary to (14).  The interior-center case is also impossible.

Both possible positions of \(0\) have been excluded.  Therefore no
\(\alpha,\beta>0\) and no admissible actual middle body \(K\) can satisfy
the registered proportional-strip Regime A hypotheses.
