## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\},
\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,
\qquad
A_2=\tfrac14\Delta .
\]
Then the polygons are rational, compatible, and balanced. Writing
\[
\Phi(c)=|A_1\cap(2c-A_1)|
       +2|A_2\cap(2c-A_0)|,
\]
one has
\[
\max_{c\in A_1}\Phi(c)=\frac{57}{16}
<\frac{33}{8}=\frac{4T}{9}.
\]
Nevertheless, the locked target holds for this family by
`a4fa27298af87644`.

## Proof

The centroid of \(\Delta\) is \(0\), and its area is
\[
D=|\Delta|=\frac92.
\]
Consequently,
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)
=\left(\frac92,\frac92,\frac9{32}\right),
\qquad
T=\frac{297}{32}.
\]

Since \(0\in\Delta\),
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}
=\frac58\Delta
\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\]
Thus compatibility follows from `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef`. Moreover,
\[
\max_i a_i=\frac92=\frac{288}{64}
<\frac{297}{64}=\frac T2,
\]
so the family is strictly balanced.

For any \(c\in\mathbb R^2\), set
\[
I(c)=|\Delta\cap(2c-\Delta)|.
\]
If this intersection is nonempty, then \(c\) is the midpoint of two points
of \(\Delta\), hence \(c\in\Delta\). Thus \(I(c)=0\) outside \(\Delta\).

Let \(c\in\Delta\) have barycentric coordinates
\(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3\). A point with barycentric coordinates
\(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) belongs to
\(\Delta\cap(2c-\Delta)\) exactly when
\[
0\le\lambda_i\le2\gamma_i,
\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.
\]
In the barycentric simplex, the excluded corner
\(\lambda_i>2\gamma_i\) has relative area
\(\max(1-2\gamma_i,0)^2\). The intersection of the two excluded corners
indexed by \(i,j\) has relative area
\[
\max(1-2\gamma_i-2\gamma_j,0)^2
=\max(2\gamma_k-1,0)^2.
\]
A triple intersection is impossible. Hence
\[
\frac{I(c)}D
=
1-\sum_{i=1}^3\max(1-2\gamma_i,0)^2
+\sum_{i=1}^3\max(2\gamma_i-1,0)^2.
\]

If every \(\gamma_i\le1/2\), then
\[
\frac{I(c)}D
=2-4\sum_i\gamma_i^2
\le2-\frac43=\frac23.
\]
If, say, \(\gamma_1\ge1/2\), then
\[
\frac{I(c)}D
=8\gamma_2\gamma_3
\le2(\gamma_2+\gamma_3)^2
\le\frac12.
\]
Therefore
\[
I(c)\le\frac{2D}{3}
\]
for every \(c\). Equality holds at \(c=0\), whose barycentric coordinates
are \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\).

Next set
\[
J(c)=\left|\tfrac14\Delta\cap(2c-\Delta)\right|.
\]
Its support is contained in
\[
\frac{\tfrac14\Delta+\Delta}{2}=\frac58\Delta,
\]
and globally
\[
J(c)\le\left|\tfrac14\Delta\right|=\frac{D}{16}.
\]
This bound is attained at \(c=0\). Indeed, if \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) are the
vertices of \(\Delta\), then \(v_1+v_2+v_3=0\), and
\[
-\frac{v_i}{2}=\frac{v_j+v_k}{2}\in\Delta.
\]
Thus \(-\frac12\Delta\subseteq\Delta\), so
\[
\frac14\Delta\subseteq\frac12\Delta\subseteq-\Delta.
\]
Hence \(J(0)=D/16\).

It follows for every \(c\in A_1\) that
\[
\Phi(c)=I(c)+2J(c)
\le\frac{2D}{3}+\frac{2D}{16}
=\frac{19D}{24}.
\]
Both bounds are attained simultaneously at \(c=0\), so
\[
\max_{c\in A_1}\Phi(c)
=\frac{19D}{24}
=\frac{57}{16}.
\]
Meanwhile,
\[
\frac{4T}{9}
=\frac49\cdot\frac{297}{32}
=\frac{33}{8}
=\frac{66}{16}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\max_{c\in A_1}\Phi(c)
=\frac{57}{16}
<\frac{66}{16}
=\frac{4T}{9}.
\]

Finally, `a4fa27298af87644` applies with
\[
(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(1,1,\tfrac14),
\qquad
1+\tfrac14\le2.
\]
It guarantees that one of the three slice centroids has depth at least
\(2T/9\). Thus this family invalidates only the overlap-selection claim.
