## Statement

Assume the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses,
exact-cell convention, all-double positive \(Q\)-support, crossings, tails,
\(M\geq m\), and \(W=0\) hypotheses of accepted fact
`084bf2f472bf29c7`.  Thus, with \(N=\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
q=q_N>0,\qquad x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\]
\[
p_0=p_\varnothing,\qquad
s_P=\sum_i p_i,\qquad d_P=\sum_{i<j}p_{ij},
\]
\[
r_i=r_{\{i\}}>0,\qquad
d_R=\sum_{i<j}r_{ij},\qquad r=r_N>0,
\]
\[
M=p_0+s_P+d_P,\qquad
k=\sum_i r_i+d_R+r,
\qquad
\eta=s_P+4d_P+3d_R+6r.
\]
Put
\[
\lambda_i=\frac{x_i}{q},\qquad
R=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3.
\]
Define the homogeneous cap-slack function
\[
g(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)
=
\min_{S\subseteq N}
\left(
2\sum_{i\notin S}\lambda_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j}
\right)
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\epsilon(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)
=
\max\left\{
0,\
\min\left\{
\frac32,\
\frac{1-R}{2}+g(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)
\right\}
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then every such positive-middle table satisfies the strict defect bound
\[
\boxed{\ \eta>q\,\epsilon(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3).\ }
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, (3) is a uniform positive lower bound exactly on the
explicit ratio region
\[
\frac{1-R}{2}+g(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)>0.
\tag{4}
\]
This region contains every positive ratio triple with \(R\leq1\), or
equivalently \(q\geq m/2\).

For completeness, use the explicit cap functions in accepted facts
`6aa12d1b76699246` and `c44e67089f10e853`: for
\(\{i,j,k\}=N\), put
\[
A_k=\frac{1+R}{\lambda_k},\qquad
c_k=2\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j},
\qquad
d_{1k}=\frac{4A_kc_k^2}{(A_k+c_k^2)^2},
\]
\[
d_{2k}
=1-\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{\sqrt{A_k}-c_k}{1+c_k}
\right)^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{clip}(t)=\min\{1,\max\{0,t\}\},
\]
\[
d_0=\min\left\{
1,\frac3{\lambda_1^{-1}+\lambda_2^{-1}+\lambda_3^{-1}}
\right\},
\qquad
\delta=\min\{d_0,d_{11},d_{12},d_{13},d_{21},d_{22},d_{23}\},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\tau(R)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(\sqrt{1+R}+\sqrt{204+120R}\right)^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
On the region (4), define the strictly smaller threshold
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{def}}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)
=
\frac{49}{
\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+
\sqrt{204+120R+28\epsilon(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)}
\right)^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{def}}<\tau,
\tag{8}
\]
and no \(W=0\) full common-plane realization exists whenever
\[
\delta\geq\tau_{\mathrm{def}}.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently, inside the open \(Q\)-only survivor
\(\delta<\tau\) of accepted fact `c44e67089f10e853`, the whole exact
strip
\[
\boxed{\quad
\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\leq\delta<\tau
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
is excluded.  Equality \(\delta=\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\) is included in the
exclusion.

The strip (10) is nonempty.  On the exact symmetric ray
\[
\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac{169}{454},
\tag{11}
\]
one has
\[
R=\frac{507}{454},\qquad

Thus (11) is a genuine point of the old survivor that is closed only after

The strict inequality (3) has no equality case under \(r_N>0\).  Equality

## Proof

Write
\[
u_i=\sum_{J\ni i}p_J,\qquad
U=\sum_i u_i=s_P+2d_P,
\qquad
h=\frac{2(M+m+k)}9.
\]
For \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), put
\[
b_i=r_{ij}+r_{i\ell}.
\]
The all-double support gives
\[
v_i=m-x_i.
\]
The left tails and crossings used in accepted fact
`2bb72698f0248a65` give
\[
t_i:=c_i+m-h=x_i-u_i\geq0.
\tag{14}
\]
Since \(u_i\geq0\), one also has
\[
0\leq t_i\leq x_i,\qquad
\sum_i t_i=m-q-U.
\tag{15}
\]

Put \(B=h-m\).  Equation (14) says
\[
c_i=B+t_i.
\tag{16}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\sum_i c_i
=\sum_i r_i+2d_R+3r
=k+d_R+2r.
\]
Summing (16) and using (15) therefore gives the exact identity
\[
B=\frac{k+d_R+2r-m+q+U}{3}.
\tag{17}
\]

Row \(\mathrm{F0}\) of accepted fact `2186f01c16b1f4e3` gives
\[
(\sqrt M+\sqrt m)^2\leq4k.
\]
Because \(M\geq m\), this implies
\[
k\geq m.
\tag{18}
\]

For every \(i\),
\[
r_i+\frac{2r}{9}
=c_i-b_i-r+\frac{2r}{9}.
\]
Since \(b_i\leq d_R\), equations (16)-(17) imply
\[
r_i+\frac{2r}{9}
\geq L+t_i,
\qquad
L:=B-d_R-\frac{7r}{9},
\tag{19}
\]
where
\[
9L=3(k-m+q+U)-6d_R-r.
\tag{20}
\]
We split according to the sign of \(L\).

Suppose first that \(L\leq0\).  Equation (20) gives
\[
6d_R+r\geq3(k-m+q+U).
\tag{21}
\]
Since
\[
\eta
=s_P+4d_P+3d_R+6r
=s_P+4d_P+\frac{6d_R+r}{2}+\frac{11r}{2}
\]
and \(r>0\), equations (18) and (21) yield
\[
\eta
>
s_P+4d_P+\frac32(k-m+q+U)
\geq
\frac{3q}{2}+\frac{5U}{2}
\geq\frac{3q}{2}.
\tag{22}
\]

Now suppose that \(L>0\).  Accepted fact `e1361027c1dda6fa` gives the
positive-middle inequality
\[
\left(
\sqrt{r_1+\frac{2r}{9}}
+
\sqrt{r_2+\frac{2r}{9}}
+
\sqrt{r_3+\frac{2r}{9}}
\right)^2
\leq2k.
\tag{23}
\]
For \(i\ne j\),
\[
\sqrt{(L+t_i)(L+t_j)}
\geq L+\sqrt{t_it_j},
\tag{24}
\]
because the difference of the squares of the two sides is
\[
L(\sqrt{t_i}-\sqrt{t_j})^2\geq0.
\]
Combining (19), (23), and (24), and writing
\[
P_t=\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{t_it_j},
\]
gives
\[
2k
\geq
\left(\sum_i\sqrt{L+t_i}\right)^2
\geq
9L+\sum_i t_i+2P_t.
\]
Using (15) and (20), this is
\[
6d_R+r
\geq
k-2m+2q+2U+2P_t.
\tag{25}
\]
The same strict decomposition of \(\eta\), together with
\(s_P+4d_P\geq U\) and (18), now gives
\[
\eta
>
s_P+4d_P+\frac{6d_R+r}{2}
\geq
q-\frac m2+2U+P_t.
\tag{26}
\]

It remains to eliminate the \(P\)-marginals from (26).  Define
\[
\Gamma(x_1,x_2,x_3)
=
\min_{S\subseteq N}
\left(
2\sum_{i\notin S}x_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\sqrt{x_ix_j}
\right).
\tag{27}
\]
Set \(y_i=\sqrt{t_i}\).  By (15),
\[
2U+P_t
=
2\sum_i(x_i-y_i^2)+\sum_{i<j}y_iy_j,
\qquad
0\leq y_i\leq\sqrt{x_i}.
\tag{28}
\]
For fixed values of the other two coordinates, the right side of (28) is
a strictly concave quadratic in \(y_i\).  Its minimum on the interval
\([0,\sqrt{x_i}]\) is therefore attained at an endpoint.  Applying this
successively to all three coordinates shows that the minimum over the box
is attained at a vertex.  If
\[
S=\{i:y_i=\sqrt{x_i}\},
\]
the vertex value is exactly the corresponding expression in (27).
Consequently
\[
2U+P_t\geq\Gamma(x_1,x_2,x_3)
=q\,g(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3).
\tag{29}
\]
The strict concavity also proves the stated equality characterization.

In the branch \(L>0\), equations (26) and (29) give
\[
\eta
>
q-\frac m2+\Gamma
=
q\left(\frac{1-R}{2}+g\right).
\tag{30}
\]
In the branch \(L\leq0\), equation (22) gives
\[
\eta>\frac{3q}{2}.
\tag{31}
\]
Finally, \(\eta\geq6r>0\).  Taking the smaller of the right sides of
(30)-(31), and then the larger with zero, proves (3) exactly.  In
particular, \(g>0\) for positive ratios, so (4) contains \(R\leq1\).
The term \(11r/2>0\) in both branches proves that equality in (3) is
impossible.

We next strengthen the scale threshold.  Put
\[
D=\frac q{|\mathcal T_Q|}.
\]
Apply accepted fact `084bf2f472bf29c7` with the exact upper bound
\[
\mathcal U=|\mathcal T_Q|=\frac qD.
\]
Using (3) and \(r>0\), its strengthened scalar bridge gives
\[
\frac qD
\geq
\left(

>

\left(
\sqrt{1+R}
+

\right)^2
=\frac q{\tau_{\mathrm{def}}}.
\tag{32}
\]
All quantities are positive, so (32) is equivalent to
\[
D<\tau_{\mathrm{def}}.
\tag{33}
\]
On the region (4), \(\epsilon>0\), so comparison of the denominators in
(6)-(7) proves (8).

Accepted fact `6aa12d1b76699246` applies on every endpoint stratum and
gives
\[
D\geq\delta.
\tag{34}
\]
Equations (33)-(34) are contradictory whenever
\(\delta\geq\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\), including equality.  Accepted fact
`c44e67089f10e853` identifies \(\delta<\tau\) as the remaining open
\(Q\)-only region.  Intersecting it with (9) proves the new survivor-strip
closure (10).  Because (34) already includes endpoint coincidences and
all ratio boundaries, and the derivation of (3) used only exact cell
areas and non-strict inequalities before the final \(r>0\) step, no
accepted boundary stratum was discarded.

We finish by verifying the exact nonempty witness.  On (11),
\[
1+R=\frac{961}{454},\qquad
A_k=\frac{961}{169}=\left(\frac{31}{13}\right)^2,
\qquad

\tag{35}

\]
and hence
\[

\tag{36}

Also \(d_0=169/454>1/8\).  Since
\[

\[

>

>

\qquad

give
\[
\frac{43}{5}<\frac1\delta<\frac{69}{8}.
\tag{37}
\]

For equal ratios in (11), the minimum in (1) is attained by every

\[

\]
Thus
\[
\frac{1-R}{2}+g=\frac{961}{908}<\frac32,

\(\tau_{\mathrm{def}}\) are continuous there as finite minima and
compositions of continuous functions.  Hence (13) persists on an open
neighborhood of (11), completing the proof.
