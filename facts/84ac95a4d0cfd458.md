## Statement

Assume the two-triple six-cap normal form of accepted fact
`142f7ecf24bb0a10`.  For \(z\in R_2\), define
\[
N_z(x)=
\sum_{j\in L}
\mathbf 1_{\{g_j(0,x)\le-g_j(2,z)\}},
\qquad
X(z)=\int_{A_0}(N_z(x)-1)\,dx.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(N_z\ge1\) on \(A_0\), \(X(z)\ge0\), and
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
=E_0-X(z).}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
\Lambda_U:=
\sum_{k\in U}(c_{0k}+c_{1k})
+\left(\sum_{k\in U}c_{2k}-a_2\right)
\tag{3}
\]
satisfies the joint transport inequality
\[
|R_2|+\inf_{z\in R_2}X(z)
\le E_0+E_1+C_2+\Lambda_U,
\tag{4}
\]
then
\[
\sup_{z\in R_2}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
+E_1+C_2
>a_2-\frac T3.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, (4) implies the weak shifted-cap reversal registered in
`six-cap-shifted-cap-reversal`.

If \(\gamma_i\) denotes the centroid of \(A_i\), then
\[
\boxed{\gamma_2\in R_2},\qquad
\boxed{a_2<\frac T2},\qquad
\boxed{a_0<\frac{2T}{5}}.
\tag{6}
\]
More precisely, there is one \(j\in L\) for which
\[
g_j(0,\gamma_0)<-g_j(2,\gamma_2)<0,
\tag{7}
\]
the shifted cap
\[
K_j:=
A_0\cap
\{x:g_j(0,x)\le-g_j(2,\gamma_2)\}
\tag{8}
\]
has area at least \(4a_0/9\), and its same-row midpoint copy
\[
\frac{K_j+\gamma_2}{2}
\tag{9}
\]
is contained in \(C_{1j}\) and has area \(|K_j|/4\).

## Proof

First note that every planar normal in either Farkas triple is nonzero.
Indeed, accepted fact `142f7ecf24bb0a10` gives a strictly positive
dependence among the three planar normals and says that they span
\(\mathbb R^2\).  If one normal were zero, the other two would be
positively dependent and hence collinear, contradicting the rank-two
span.  Thus every level set used below is a line and has planar area
zero.  This settles all strict-versus-closed boundary conventions in the
area identities.

Fix \(z\in R_2\).  The strict inward-translate cover in the accepted fact
states that, for every \(x\in A_0\), some \(j\in L\) satisfies
\[
g_j(0,x)<-g_j(2,z).
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore \(N_z(x)\ge1\).  Tonelli's theorem and the definition of the
actual cap profiles give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{A_0}N_z(x)\,dx
&=
\sum_{j\in L}
\left|
A_0\cap
\{x:g_j(0,x)\le-g_j(2,z)\}
\right|\\
&=
\sum_{j\in L}F_j(-g_j(2,z)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{j\in L}F_j(0)
=\sum_{j\in L}c_{0j}
=a_0+E_0,
\tag{12}
\]
equations (1), (11), and (12) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
&=a_0+E_0-\int_{A_0}N_z(x)\,dx\\
&=E_0-X(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (2), including the equality cases.  It also gives the exact
supremum identity
\[
\sup_{z\in R_2}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
=E_0-\inf_{z\in R_2}X(z);
\tag{13}
\]
no attainment is required on the open set \(R_2\).

Accepted fact `142f7ecf24bb0a10` gives
\[
|R_2|>\frac T3
\tag{14}
\]
and the strict upper leakage budget
\[
\Lambda_U<\frac{2T}{3}-a_2.
\tag{15}
\]
If (4) holds, then (13)-(15) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{z\in R_2}
\sum_{j\in L}
\left(F_j(0)-F_j(-g_j(2,z))\right)
+E_1+C_2\\
&\qquad
=E_0+E_1+C_2-\inf_{z\in R_2}X(z)\\
&\qquad
\ge |R_2|-\Lambda_U\\
&\qquad
>\frac T3-\left(\frac{2T}{3}-a_2\right)
=a_2-\frac T3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This proves (5).  Notice that (4) is used only as a hypothesis; it is
not asserted or proved here.

It remains to prove (6)-(9).  We use the planar centroid-halfplane
inequality: if \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a two-dimensional convex body
of area \(a\) and centroid \(\gamma\), then every closed halfplane
containing \(\gamma\) cuts from \(A\) area at least \(4a/9\).  The
statement applies unchanged when the centroid is in the interior of the
halfplane.  Indeed, translate the boundary parallel toward the centroid
and apply the boundary-through-centroid case to the resulting smaller
cap.

The upper open sections cover the complete plane at height \(2\).
Hence some \(k\in U\) satisfies
\[
g_k(2,\gamma_2)<0.
\tag{17}
\]
The corresponding actual top cap contains \(\gamma_2\), so the centroid
inequality and the strict mass bound for this same three-dimensional cap
give
\[
\frac{4a_2}{9}
\le c_{2k}
\le c_{0k}+c_{1k}+c_{2k}
<\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus
\[
a_2<\frac T2.
\tag{19}
\]

The strict lower-triple budget and \(E_0,E_1\ge0\) give
\[
C_2<a_2-\frac T3.
\tag{20}
\]
By (19), \(T>2a_2\), and therefore
\[
C_2<\frac{a_2}{3}.
\tag{21}
\]
Suppose \(\gamma_2\notin R_2\).  By the strict definition of \(R_2\),
there is \(j\in L\) with
\[
g_j(2,\gamma_2)\le0.
\]
Then the closed planar cap \(C_{2j}\) contains the centroid of \(A_2\).
The centroid inequality gives
\[
C_2\ge c_{2j}\ge\frac{4a_2}{9},
\]
contradicting (21).  Hence all three lower inequalities at \(\gamma_2\)
are strict and \(\gamma_2\in R_2\).

Midpoint containment gives
\[
\frac{\gamma_0+\gamma_2}{2}\in
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\subseteq A_1.
\tag{22}
\]
The lower open sections cover the complete plane at height \(1\).
Consequently, one \(j\in L\) satisfies
\[
g_j\left(1,\frac{\gamma_0+\gamma_2}{2}\right)<0.
\tag{23}
\]
Affineness gives the exact midpoint identity
\[
g_j(0,\gamma_0)+g_j(2,\gamma_2)
=
2g_j\left(1,\frac{\gamma_0+\gamma_2}{2}\right)<0.
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(\gamma_2\in R_2\), one has
\(g_j(2,\gamma_2)>0\).  Equation (24) proves (7).

The halfplane defining \(K_j\) contains \(\gamma_0\) strictly.  The
centroid inequality therefore gives
\[
|K_j|\ge\frac{4a_0}{9}.
\tag{25}
\]
Because \(-g_j(2,\gamma_2)<0\), one has \(K_j\subseteq C_{0j}\).
For \(x\in K_j\), midpoint containment and affineness give
\[
\frac{x+\gamma_2}{2}\in A_1,\qquad
g_j\left(1,\frac{x+\gamma_2}{2}\right)
=\frac{g_j(0,x)+g_j(2,\gamma_2)}2\le0.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the same-row copy (9) is contained in \(C_{1j}\).  The map
\(x\mapsto(x+\gamma_2)/2\) scales planar area by \(1/4\), so
\[
c_{0j}\ge|K_j|,
\qquad
c_{1j}\ge\frac{|K_j|}{4}.
\tag{27}
\]
Using (25), (27), and the strict mass budget for this same lower cap,
\[
\frac{5a_0}{9}
\le |K_j|+\frac{|K_j|}{4}
\le c_{0j}+c_{1j}+c_{2j}
<\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{28}
\]
Therefore \(a_0<2T/5\).  This proves every assertion.

No area, uniform separation, or quantitative mass is assigned to any
private sector in this proof.

## External sources

by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), no. 4,
1257-1261, Theorem 2.  In dimension two, every closed halfplane
containing the centroid of a convex body has area at least \(4/9\) of
