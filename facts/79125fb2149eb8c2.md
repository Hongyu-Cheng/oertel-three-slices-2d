## Statement

Let \(\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle of area \(s>0\), and let \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\) be its affine barycentric coordinate functions on the whole plane. For \(0\le b_i\le1\), set
\[
L_i=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\beta_i(x)\ge b_i\},
\qquad
p_i=|\Delta\cap L_i|.
\]
Then \(L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3=\mathbb R^2\) if and only if \(b_1+b_2+b_3\le1\), and
\[
p_i=s(1-b_i)^2
\]
for every \(i\), including \(b_i=0\) and \(b_i=1\). If the three halfplanes cover \(\mathbb R^2\), then for every \(e_1,e_2,e_3\ge0\),
\[
\max_{1\le i\le3}(p_i+e_i)
\ge \frac49s+\frac{e_1+e_2+e_3}{3}.
\]
Equality holds if and only if
\[
b_1=b_2=b_3=\frac13
\quad\text{and}\quad
e_1=e_2=e_3=\lambda
\]
for some \(\lambda\ge0\).

## Proof

The affine barycentric coordinates satisfy
\(\beta_1(x)+\beta_2(x)+\beta_3(x)=1\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), and every real triple with sum \(1\) is the barycentric-coordinate triple of a unique point of the plane. If a point \(x\) lies outside all three \(L_i\), then
\[
1=\sum_{i=1}^3\beta_i(x)<\sum_{i=1}^3b_i.
\]
Consequently, \(b_1+b_2+b_3\le1\) implies that the three halfplanes cover the plane. Conversely, if \(B:=b_1+b_2+b_3>1\), the three numbers
\[
\gamma_i=b_i-\frac{B-1}{3}
\]
sum to \(1\) and satisfy \(\gamma_i<b_i\). The point whose barycentric coordinates are \((\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)\) therefore lies outside every \(L_i\). This proves the cover equivalence, including the boundary case \(B=1\).

Fix \(i\), and let \(j,k\) be the other two indices. Inside \(\Delta\), the condition \(\beta_i\ge b_i\) cuts out the triangle
\[
\operatorname{conv}\!\left\{
v_i,\,
b_iv_i+(1-b_i)v_j,\,
b_iv_i+(1-b_i)v_k
\right\}.
\]
Its two edge vectors based at \(v_i\) are \(1-b_i\) times the corresponding edge vectors of \(\Delta\). Hence its area is
\[
p_i=s(1-b_i)^2.
\]
For \(b_i=0\) this is all of \(\Delta\), and for \(b_i=1\) it is the single vertex \(v_i\), so the same formula covers both endpoints.

Assume now that the halfplanes cover the plane. Put \(q_i=1-b_i\) and
\[
M=\max_{1\le i\le3}\bigl(sq_i^2+e_i\bigr).
\]
The cover criterion gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3q_i=3-\sum_{i=1}^3b_i\ge2.
\]
For every \(i\), the definition of \(M\) gives
\[
e_i\le M-sq_i^2.
\]
Summing these inequalities and using Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3e_i
\le 3M-s\sum_{i=1}^3q_i^2
\le 3M-\frac{s}{3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^3q_i\right)^2
\le 3M-\frac43s.
\]
Rearranging gives
\[
M\ge\frac49s+\frac13\sum_{i=1}^3e_i.
\]
Since \(sq_i^2=p_i\), this is the claimed inequality.

It remains to determine equality. Suppose equality holds in the claimed inequality. Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3e_i=3M-\frac43s,
\]
so every inequality in the preceding chain is an equality. Equality in the last two inequalities forces
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3q_i=2
\quad\text{and}\quad
q_1=q_2=q_3.
\]
Thus \(q_1=q_2=q_3=2/3\), or equivalently \(b_1=b_2=b_3=1/3\). Moreover, each quantity
\[
M-sq_i^2-e_i
\]
is nonnegative, and equality after summing forces all three to vanish. Therefore
\[
e_1=e_2=e_3=M-\frac49s=:\lambda\ge0.
\]
Conversely, if \(b_1=b_2=b_3=1/3\) and \(e_1=e_2=e_3=\lambda\ge0\), then both sides of the asserted inequality equal \(4s/9+\lambda\). Hence these and only these are the equality cases. In particular, \(\lambda=0\) is included; a strict cover \(b_1+b_2+b_3<1\), unequal \(b_i\) on the cover boundary, or an endpoint \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) cannot give equality.
