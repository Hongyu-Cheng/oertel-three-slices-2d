## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be convex bodies with nonempty interiors, and set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad
S_B=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Write
\[
M=\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
m=\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\},\qquad
b=|B|,\qquad
T=M+m+b,
\]
and normalize
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM.
\]
Let
\[
z_0=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2},\qquad
z_\theta=(1-\theta)g_B+\theta z_0,
\]
where \(g_0,g_2,g_B\) are the respective centroids. Put
\[
\kappa=\frac{256}{625},\qquad
K=9\kappa=\frac{2304}{625},\qquad
c=\frac K2=\frac{1152}{625},
\]
and
\[
p(\theta)=\theta(2-\theta).
\]

For \(r,s>0\), define
\[
\mathcal L_{r,s}(\theta)
=
\min\left\{
\kappa r+\frac{4s}{9}(1-\theta)^2,\,
\frac{4s}{9}+\kappa r\theta^4
\right\},
\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
This function has a unique maximizer \(\widehat\theta\in(0,1)\), characterized exactly by
\[
Kr(1-\widehat\theta^4)
=
4s\,p(\widehat\theta).
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose
\[
0<r<\frac13,\qquad 1-r<s<1+r,
\tag{3}
\]
and put
\[
\delta=1+r-s.
\]
For each such \(r\), there is a unique \(\Theta(r)\in(0,1-1/\sqrt2)\) satisfying
\[
Kr(1-\Theta(r)^4)
=
4\left(1+r-cr\Theta(r)^4\right)p(\Theta(r)).
\tag{4}
\]
Equivalently, \(\Theta(r)\) is the unique root in that interval of
\[
-1152r\theta^6+2304r\theta^5-576r\theta^4
+625(1+r)\theta^2-1250(1+r)\theta+576r=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The exact coverage of (1) at the target \(2(1+r+s)/9\) is
\[
\max_{0\le\theta\le1}\mathcal L_{r,s}(\theta)
\ge
\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0<\delta\le cr\Theta(r)^4.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the full certified part of the small-\(r\) residual strip is
\[
0<r<\frac13,\qquad
1+r-cr\Theta(r)^4\le s<1+r.
\tag{7}
\]

For every pair in (7), the explicit choice
\[
\theta_\delta
=
\left(\frac{\delta}{cr}\right)^{1/4}
=
\left(\frac{625(1+r-s)}{1152r}\right)^{1/4}
\tag{8}
\]
satisfies
\[
h_{S_B}\bigl((1,z_{\theta_\delta})\bigr)
\ge\frac{2T}{9}.
\tag{9}
\]

The endpoint function \(\Theta(r)\) is strictly increasing, with
\[
\lim_{r\downarrow0}\Theta(r)=0,\qquad
\lim_{r\uparrow1/3}\Theta(r)=\tau,
\tag{10}
\]
where \(\tau\) is the unique root in \((3/25,1/8)\) of
\[
144-1250\theta+625\theta^2-144\theta^4
+576\theta^5-288\theta^6=0.
\tag{11}
\]

The region is geometrically nonempty beyond the crude subregion in accepted fact `e34ffd778c178549`. An exact example has
\[
r=\frac14,\qquad
\theta_\delta=\frac3{32},\qquad
\delta=\frac{729}{20480000},\qquad
s=\frac{25599271}{20480000}.
\tag{12}
\]

## Proof

Write the two entries in (1) as
\[
A(\theta)=\kappa r+\frac{4s}{9}(1-\theta)^2,
\qquad
D(\theta)=\frac{4s}{9}+\kappa r\theta^4.
\]
The function \(A\) is strictly decreasing on \([0,1)\), while \(D\) is strictly increasing on \((0,1]\). Moreover,
\[
A(0)-D(0)=\kappa r>0,
\qquad
A(1)-D(1)=-\frac{4s}{9}<0.
\]
Hence they cross exactly once. Before the crossing their minimum is \(D\), and after the crossing it is \(A\). This proves the unique-maximizer assertion and (2).

Now assume (3). By accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, since \(r<1/3<9/25\), condition (3) is exactly the corresponding residual area strip. Its upper strict inequality gives
\[
0<\delta<2r.
\tag{13}
\]
Let
\[
t_0=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\]
Using \(s=1+r-\delta\), direct subtraction gives
\[
9\bigl(A(\theta)-t_0\bigr)
=
Kr-2\delta-4s\,p(\theta),
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
9\bigl(D(\theta)-t_0\bigr)
=
Kr\theta^4-2\delta.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(D(\theta)\ge t_0\) first occurs at
\[
\theta=\theta_\delta
=
\left(\frac{2\delta}{Kr}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
Since \(A\) is decreasing, some \(\theta\in[0,1]\) satisfies both \(A(\theta)\ge t_0\) and \(D(\theta)\ge t_0\) if and only if \(\theta_\delta\le1\) and \(A(\theta_\delta)\ge t_0\).

For \(0\le\theta\le1\), define
\[
Q_r(\theta)
=
Kr(1-\theta^4)
-
4\left(1+r-cr\theta^4\right)p(\theta).
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(\delta=cr\theta_\delta^4\), equation (14) becomes
\[
9\bigl(A(\theta_\delta)-t_0\bigr)
=
Q_r(\theta_\delta).
\tag{17}
\]
We next determine the sign of \(Q_r\) exactly.

Set
\[
a=1-\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]
so that \(p(a)=1/2\). For
\[
s_\theta=1+r-cr\theta^4,
\]
differentiation gives
\[
Q_r'(\theta)
=
4Kr\theta^3\bigl(2p(\theta)-1\bigr)
-
8s_\theta(1-\theta).
\tag{18}
\]
For \(0\le\theta\le a\), one has \(p(\theta)\le1/2\), while \(s_\theta>0\). Hence
\[
Q_r'(\theta)<0
\qquad(0\le\theta\le a).
\tag{19}
\]
Also \(Q_r(0)=Kr>0\).

For \(a\le\theta\le1\), one has \(p(\theta)\ge1/2\). Since \(0<r<1/3\), \(c=1152/625\), and \(\theta^4\le1\),
\[
s_\theta\ge 1-\frac{527}{1875}
=\frac{1348}{1875}.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q_r(\theta)
\le
\frac K3-2\frac{1348}{1875}
=
\frac{2304-2696}{1875}
=
-\frac{392}{1875}<0.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (19) and (20) prove that \(Q_r\) has exactly one zero \(\Theta(r)\) in \((0,a)\), and
\[
Q_r(\theta)\ge0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\le\theta\le\Theta(r).
\tag{21}
\]
Expanding \(Q_r(\Theta(r))=0\) gives (5).

Combining (17) and (21), the two entries in (1) can simultaneously reach \(t_0\) exactly when
\[
\theta_\delta\le\Theta(r).
\]
Using \(\delta=cr\theta_\delta^4\), this is precisely
\[
\delta\le cr\Theta(r)^4.
\]
This proves (6). Since \(c<2\) and \(\Theta(r)<1\),
\[
cr\Theta(r)^4<2r,
\]
so (7) lies inside the strict strip (3) and is nonempty for every \(0<r<1/3\).

At the explicit choice (8), equation (15) gives
\[
D(\theta_\delta)=t_0,
\]
while (17), (21), and (6) give
\[
A(\theta_\delta)\ge t_0.
\]
Accepted fact `e34ffd778c178549` therefore yields
\[
z_{\theta_\delta}
\in
\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}R_q(2T/9).
\tag{22}
\]
Moreover,
\[
t_0
=
\frac{4s}{9}+\kappa r\theta_\delta^4
<
s+r,
\]
because \(4s/9<s\) and \(\kappa\theta_\delta^4<1\). Hence
\[
\frac{2T}{9}<b+m\le b+M.
\]
The two horizontal conditions in accepted fact `a6099e9164a694ce` hold, and (22) therefore proves (9).

For the endpoint assertions, write
\[
Q_r(\theta)=rE(\theta)-4p(\theta),
\]
where
\[
E(\theta)
=
K(1-\theta^4)-4(1-c\theta^4)p(\theta).
\]
At \(\theta=\Theta(r)\),
\[
E(\Theta(r))=\frac{4p(\Theta(r))}{r}>0.
\]
If \(0<r_1<r_2\le1/3\), then
\[
Q_{r_2}(\Theta(r_1))
=
4p(\Theta(r_1))
\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}-1\right)>0.
\]
Since \(Q_{r_2}\) decreases up to its unique zero, this implies
\[
\Theta(r_1)<\Theta(r_2).
\]
Passing to the limits in \(Q_r(\Theta(r))=0\) gives the two limits in (10). At \(r=1/3\), multiplying \(Q_{1/3}(\theta)=0\) by \(1875/16\) gives (11). Its left side has the exact values
\[
\frac{728421123}{244140625}>0
\quad\text{at }\theta=\frac3{25},
\]
and
\[
-\frac{20505}{8192}<0
\quad\text{at }\theta=\frac18.
\]
The uniqueness already proved for \(Q_{1/3}\) establishes the exact characterization of \(\tau\).

It remains to realize (12). Put
\[
\bar\delta=\frac{729}{20480000},
\qquad
\ell=\frac{15}{4}-4\bar\delta
=\frac{19199271}{5120000},
\]
and let \(\alpha,\beta\) be the two positive roots of
\[
u^2-\ell u+\frac14=0.
\]
Take
\[
A_0=\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2,
\qquad
A_2=
\left[-\frac\alpha2,\frac\alpha2\right]
\times
\left[-\frac\beta2,\frac\beta2\right].
\]
Then
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\alpha\beta=\frac14.
\]
The Minkowski average \(B\) is a rectangle of area
\[
|B|
=
\frac{(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}4
=
\frac{1+\ell+1/4}{4}
=
\frac54-\bar\delta
=
\frac{25599271}{20480000}.
\]
Thus its normalized parameters are exactly (12). Direct substitution gives
\[
Q_{1/4}\left(\frac3{32}\right)
=
\frac{146798367}{5242880000}>0,
\]
so this point lies in (7). All bodies are centered, hence \(g_B=z_0=0\), and the explicit witness is \((1,0)\).

Finally, the old crude condition in accepted fact `e34ffd778c178549` would require
\[
\bar\delta
\le
\frac{72(1/4)^5}{390625(5/4)^4}
=
\frac{18}{244140625}.
\]
Instead,
\[
\bar\delta-\frac{18}{244140625}
=
\frac{284175801}{8000000000000}>0.
\]
Thus the exact optimized region is genuinely larger. Taking the convex hull of \(\{0\}\times A_0\) and \(\{2\}\times A_2\) realizes these three slices in a compact polytope.
