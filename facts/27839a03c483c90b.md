## Statement

Assume the actual dominant all-double residual-counterexample hypotheses of accepted facts `2f637c4da2df28f1` and `174b54e9843ef8fc`, with the canonical common-plane and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Normalize
\[
m=|A_2|=1.
\]
Thus the positive \(Q\)-support is
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
q+x_1+x_2+x_3=1,
\]
and, after labeling the dominant index as \(1\),
\[
x_1>\frac59,\qquad q+x_2+x_3<\frac49.
\]

Let \(C_0<\infty\) be the universal constant supplied by accepted fact `366887b2b328c518`, so \(M\le C_0\). Put \(A_2\) in John position by a common planar affine map:
\[
B\subseteq A_2\subseteq2B.
\]
Choose any selected point \(z\in A_0\), put \(v=z/2\), and apply the slice-preserving height shear
\[
(h,w)\longmapsto(h,w-(2-h)v).
\]
Write the resulting endpoint and middle bodies again as \(A_0,A_2,K\). Then there is a universal \(R_0<\infty\), depending only on \(C_0\), such that
\[
0\in A_0,\qquad \frac B2\subseteq K,
\]
\[
A_0\cup A_2\cup K\subseteq R_0B.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, all three support functions are uniformly bounded.

Let
\[
\lambda_i=-f_i,\qquad \lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1,
\]
\[
s_i=|\nabla\lambda_i|,\qquad
\phi_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{s_i},\qquad
S=s_1+s_2+s_3,\qquad
\alpha_i=\frac{s_i}{S},
\]
\[
\beta_i=\frac{L_i}{s_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every sequence of such residual realizations, after passing to a subsequence, the bodies converge in Hausdorff distance, and
\[
\phi_{i,n}\to\phi_i,\qquad
\beta_{i,n}\to\beta_i,\qquad
\alpha_{i,n}\to\alpha_i,\qquad
\frac1{S_n}\to\sigma\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]
Each \(\phi_i\) is a nonconstant affine function with unit gradient,
\[
\alpha_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\alpha_i=1,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i\phi_i=\sigma.
\tag{4}
\]
The limiting endpoint offsets also satisfy
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\beta_i\le-\sigma,
\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i(\phi_i-\beta_i)
=\sigma-\sum_i\alpha_i\beta_i\ge2\sigma.
\tag{4a}
\]
If
\[
I=\{i:\alpha_i>0\},
\]
then \(|I|\ge2\), and exactly one of the following four types occurs.

1. Two-weight type: \(I=\{i,j\}\). Then
\[
\alpha_i=\alpha_j=\frac12,\qquad
\phi_j=-\phi_i+2\sigma.
\tag{5}
\]
The two surviving middle lines are parallel and oppositely oriented; they coincide exactly when \(\sigma=0\).

2. Three-weight, rank-two, finite-scale type:
\[
\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{\nabla\phi_1,\nabla\phi_2,\nabla\phi_3\}
=\mathbb R^2,\qquad \sigma>0.
\tag{6}
\]
The three middle lines form a nondegenerate triangle, and \(S_n\to1/\sigma\).

3. Three-weight, rank-two, fan type: the first two conditions in (6) hold and \(\sigma=0\). Then the three middle lines are concurrent and
\[
S_n\to\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

4. Three-weight, rank-one type:
\[
\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\nabla\phi_i=\varepsilon_i u,\quad
\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\},
\tag{8}
\]
for one unit vector \(u\), with
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\varepsilon_i=0.
\tag{9}
\]
All middle and endpoint lines are parallel. This type may have either
\(\sigma>0\) or \(\sigma=0\).

In every type, the normalized endpoint lines
\[
\phi_i=\beta_i\quad\text{and}\quad \phi_i=-\beta_i
\tag{10}
\]
remain in a fixed compact set of affine lines. Thus raw gradient data can escape only when \(\sigma=0\), equivalently \(S_n\to\infty\). If \(\sigma>0\), all \(s_{i,n}\) are bounded; any zero limiting weight then means the corresponding raw gradient tends to zero.

Finally, after passage to the same subsequence,
\[
(q_n,x_{1,n},x_{2,n},x_{3,n})
\longrightarrow
(\bar q,\bar x_1,\bar x_2,\bar x_3),
\]
\[
\bar q+\bar x_1+\bar x_2+\bar x_3=1,\qquad
\bar x_1\ge\frac59,\qquad
\bar q+\bar x_2+\bar x_3\le\frac49.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence the limiting positive-area \(Q\)-support is one of the eight sets
\[
\{23\}\cup\mathcal U,
\qquad
\mathcal U\subseteq\{123,13,12\}.
\tag{12}
\]
If the three limiting \(Q\)-threshold lines in (10) are pairwise nonparallel and nonconcurrent, convexity reduces (12) to the five connected supports
\[
\{23\},\quad
\{23,123\},\quad
\{23,123,13\},\quad
\{23,123,12\},\quad
\{23,123,13,12\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The three remaining algebraic patterns
\[
\{23,13\},\qquad
\{23,12\},\qquad
\{23,13,12\}
\tag{14}
\]
can occur, if at all, only together with a parallel, coincident, or concurrent degeneration of the limiting \(Q\)-threshold arrangement.

## Proof

Start before the John map, with \(m=1\). Accepted fact `366887b2b328c518` gives
\[
M\le C_0.
\tag{15}
\]
The no-half-slice condition in accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives
\[
k<M+1\le C_0+1.
\tag{16}
\]
Let \(E\) be the John ellipse of \(A_2\), and map it to the unit disk \(B\). The planar John inclusions give
\[
B\subseteq A_2\subseteq2B.
\]
If \(d>0\) is the area multiplier of this common affine map, then, because the original area of \(A_2\) is \(1\),
\[
d=|A_2|.
\]
The John inclusions yield
\[
\pi\le d\le4\pi.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus, after the map,
\[
|A_0|\le4\pi C_0,\qquad
|K|\le4\pi(C_0+1).
\tag{18}
\]

The height shear in the statement sends
\[
A_0\mapsto A_0-z,\qquad
K\mapsto K-\frac z2,\qquad
A_2\mapsto A_2.
\]
It is the exact gauge used in accepted fact `043e84c0c8d6e05c`. Directly,
\[
\frac{(A_0-z)+A_2}{2}
=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}-\frac z2
\subseteq K-\frac z2.
\tag{19}
\]
Hence \(0\in A_0\) and \(B/2\subseteq K\).

We now rederive the two geometric estimates from this gauge, so no fixed-ratio hypothesis from accepted fact `043e84c0c8d6e05c` is used. Let \(\mathcal V\) be planar mixed area. Midpoint containment gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
4|K|
&\ge |A_0+A_2|\\
&=|A_0|+|A_2|+2\mathcal V(A_0,A_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Choose a diameter segment \(I\subseteq A_0\). Since \(B\subseteq A_2\), monotonicity of mixed area gives
\[
\mathcal V(A_0,A_2)
\ge\mathcal V(I,B)
=\operatorname{diam}(A_0).
\tag{21}
\]
The last equality follows because \(I+B\) is the radius-one stadium, whose area is \(\pi+2\operatorname{diam}(I)\). Equations (18), (20), and (21) give
\[
\operatorname{diam}(A_0)
\le2|K|
\le8\pi(C_0+1).
\tag{22}
\]

For \(K\), put
\[
R=\max\{|w|:w\in K\}.
\]
The convex body \(K\) contains \(B/2\). The convex hull of a farthest point and the unit-diameter chord of \(B/2\) perpendicular to it is a triangle of area \(R/2\). Therefore
\[
|K|\ge\frac R2.
\]
Since \(0\in K\),
\[
\operatorname{diam}(K)\le2R\le4|K|
\le16\pi(C_0+1).
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(0\in A_0\cap K\), the diameter bounds are also support-radius bounds. Together with \(A_2\subseteq2B\), this proves (1), for example with
\[
R_0=16\pi(C_0+1)+2.
\]

We next control all normalized cutting lines. In the original \(m=1\) area units, the tails and crossings give
\[
u_i+v_i\le1.
\tag{24}
\]
Under all-double support,
\[
v_i=1-x_i,
\]
so
\[
0\le u_i\le x_i<1.
\tag{25}
\]
Accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives \(M>3\), while accepted fact
`2f637c4da2df28f1` gives \(k>2+3q>2\). Hence
\[
h=\frac{2(M+1+k)}9>\frac43.
\]
The crossing therefore gives
\[
c_i=h-u_i-v_i>\frac13.
\tag{26}
\]

There is also a uniform positive-side bound. The two convex endpoint sections
\[
A_0\cap\{f_i>L_i\},\qquad
A_2\cap\{f_i>-L_i\}
\]
have areas \(M-u_i\) and \(x_i\), respectively. Their midpoint body is contained in
\[
K\cap\{f_i>0\},
\]
whose area is \(k-c_i\). Planar Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
4(k-c_i)
\ge\left(\sqrt{M-u_i}+\sqrt{x_i}\right)^2.
\]
Using \(M>3\) and \(u_i\le x_i<1\),
\[
k-c_i>\frac12.
\tag{27}
\]
After the John map, all areas in (26)-(27) are multiplied by \(d\ge\pi\). Thus every middle zero line cuts from \(K\) at least \(\pi/3\) on one side and at least \(\pi/2\) on the other. In particular, every zero line meets the uniformly bounded body \(K\).

Write
\[
\phi_i(w)=n_i\cdot w+b_i,\qquad |n_i|=1.
\]
Since \(\{\phi_i=0\}\) meets \(K\subseteq R_0B\),
\[
|b_i|\le R_0.
\tag{28}
\]
The \(Q\)-threshold line is
\[
\lambda_i=L_i,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\phi_i=\beta_i.
\]
Both sides meet \(A_2\), because their areas are \(v_i=1-x_i>0\) and
\(x_i>0\). Hence this line meets \(A_2\subseteq2B\), and (28) gives
\[
|\beta_i|\le R_0+2.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (28)-(29) prove compactness of all normalized middle, \(P\), and \(Q\) lines.

The bodies in (1) form Hausdorff-precompact families. Their limits remain two-dimensional: \(A_2\) contains \(B\), \(K\) contains \(B/2\), and
\[
|A_0|=dM>3\pi.
\]
Pass to convergent subsequences of the bodies, the unit normals, the constants \(b_i\), the thresholds \(\beta_i\), and the weights
\[
\alpha_i=\frac{s_i}{S}.
\]
The identity \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) is exactly
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\phi_i=\frac1S.
\tag{30}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\beta_i=\frac{\sum_iL_i}{S}<-\frac1S,
\tag{30a}
\]
because \(\sum_iL_i<-1\). Thus the shifted \(Q\)-functions obey
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i(\phi_i-\beta_i)
=\frac{1-\sum_iL_i}{S}>\frac2S.
\tag{30b}
\]
The left side at the origin is uniformly bounded by (28), so \(1/S\) is bounded. Passing to the limit proves (3)-(4).
Passing (30a)-(30b) to the limit also proves (4a).

It remains to classify (4). If only one \(\alpha_i\) were positive, (4) would make a nonconstant affine function constant. Hence \(|I|\ge2\).

Suppose \(I=\{i,j\}\). Taking gradients in (4) gives
\[
\alpha_i\nabla\phi_i+\alpha_j\nabla\phi_j=0.
\]
Both gradients have unit norm, so
\[
\alpha_i=\alpha_j=\frac12,\qquad
\nabla\phi_j=-\nabla\phi_i.
\]
The affine identity itself then gives (5). This proves type 1.

Now suppose all three weights are positive. Their unit gradients satisfy
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i\nabla\phi_i=0.
\tag{31}
\]
Their span has dimension either one or two. In dimension one, write
\[
\nabla\phi_i=\varepsilon_i u,\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Equation (31) is exactly (9), proving type 4.

In dimension two, no two gradients are parallel: two equal or opposite unit gradients in a positive three-term dependence would force all three gradients to be collinear. Thus the three zero lines are pairwise nonparallel. If \(\sigma>0\), they cannot be concurrent, because a common zero would make the left side of (4) vanish there. Their positive normal dependence makes
\[
\{\phi_i\ge0:\ i=1,2,3\}
\]
a bounded nondegenerate triangle. This is type 2. If \(\sigma=0\), the intersection point of any two zero lines also lies on the third by (4), proving concurrency and type 3. Finally,
\[
\sigma=\lim_n\frac1{S_n}
\]
gives exactly the scale conclusions stated after (10). This proves the exhaustive four-type classification.

We finish with the endpoint support boundary. By compactness of the closed simplex, pass to a subsequence satisfying (11). The inequalities in (11) are precisely the closure of the strict dominant inequalities in accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1`. Put
\[
\chi_i=\phi_i-\beta_i.
\]
The limiting \(Q\)-cell indexed by \(H\) is the intersection of the limiting \(A_2\) with
\[
\chi_i\ge0\ (i\in H),\qquad
\chi_i<0\ (i\notin H).
\]
Hausdorff convergence, local uniform convergence, and zero area of the finitely many limiting lines give
\[
|Q_{123}|=\bar d\,\bar q,\qquad
|Q_{23}|=\bar d\,\bar x_1,\qquad
|Q_{13}|=\bar d\,\bar x_2,\qquad
|Q_{12}|=\bar d\,\bar x_3,
\tag{32}
\]
where \(\bar d\in[\pi,4\pi]\) is the limiting John area multiplier. Every other limiting \(Q\)-cell has area zero. Since
\[
\bar x_1\ge\frac59,
\]
\(Q_{23}\) always has positive limiting area. This proves the eight algebraic supports in (12).

Assume now that the three limiting threshold lines \(\{\chi_i=0\}\) are pairwise nonparallel and nonconcurrent. Their chamber adjacency graph is the usual one: the central chamber \(123\) is adjacent to the three doubleton chambers, while two distinct doubletons are connected without the central chamber only through a singleton chamber. For a convex body, the positive-area chamber support is connected in this adjacency graph. Indeed, join interior points from two positive chambers by a segment inside \(\operatorname{int}A_2\); every open chamber crossed by the segment has positive area, with an arbitrarily small perturbation handling a crossing point of two lines. All singleton \(Q\)-cells have zero limiting area by (32). Therefore, if two doubleton cells have positive area, the central cell must also have positive area. This leaves exactly the five supports in (13). The other three patterns require degeneration of the threshold arrangement, proving (14).

For reference, the accepted centroid localizations also pass to the boundary. If \(g_{0,n},g_{2,n}\) are the transformed centroids and converge to
\(\bar g_0,\bar g_2\), accepted facts `174b54e9843ef8fc` and
`2f637c4da2df28f1` give
\[
\phi_i(\bar g_0)\le-\beta_i\quad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{33}
\]
\[
\phi_1(\bar g_2)\le\beta_1,\qquad
\phi_2(\bar g_2)\ge\beta_2,\qquad
\phi_3(\bar g_2)\ge\beta_3.
\tag{34}
\]
All scalar crossings, tails, and \(W=0\) identities persist with weak inequalities after passage to the same subsequence. Thus (1)-(14), (33)-(34), and those limiting scalar identities give a finite list of compact boundary problems. No body, support function, or normalized line can escape. The first and only remaining raw line-parameter escape is the explicitly isolated mode
\[
\sigma=0,\qquad S_n\to\infty.
\]
