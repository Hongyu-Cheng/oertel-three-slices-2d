## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\},
\qquad
A_0=A_1=\Delta,\qquad A_2=\frac14\Delta .
\tag{1}
\]
Then all three polygons are rational and
\[
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac58\Delta\subseteq\Delta=A_1.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus accepted fact `8ad0785e5b4ae9ef` supplies a compact polytope having
exactly these three integer-height sections.

Their areas and target are
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2)=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac1{32}\right),
\qquad
T=\frac{33}{32},
\qquad
t=\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{11}{48}.
\tag{3}
\]
All three area-triangle inequalities are strict.  With
\(m=\min\{a_0,a_2\}=1/32\), the exact allocation interval of accepted fact
`66f17f4981c30108` is
\[
J=
\left[t-\frac{4m}{9},\frac{4a_1}{9}\right]
=
\left[\frac{31}{144},\frac29\right].
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(a\in J\), putting \(b=t-a\) gives
\[
\frac1{144}\leq b\leq\frac1{72},
\qquad
\mathcal M(a)\cap\mathcal E(b)=\varnothing.
\tag{5}
\]

Nevertheless, if \(g=(1/3,1/3)\) is the centroid of \(A_1\), then
\[
h_S((1,g))\geq\frac{11}{48},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
g\in D_t.
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

Because \(\Delta\) is convex and contains the origin,
\[
r\Delta+s\Delta=(r+s)\Delta
\qquad(r,s\geq0).
\]
This proves (2).  Equation (3) follows from
\(|\Delta|=1/2\), and the strict area inequalities are
\[
\frac12<\frac12+\frac1{32},
\qquad
\frac12<\frac12+\frac1{32},
\qquad
\frac1{32}<1.
\]
The arithmetic in (4) is
\[
t-\frac{4m}{9}
=\frac{11}{48}-\frac1{72}
=\frac{31}{144},
\qquad
\frac{4a_1}{9}=\frac29<\frac{11}{48}.
\]
Subtracting the endpoints of \(J\) from \(t\) gives the full interval for
\(b\) in (5); in particular \(b>0\) for every allocation under
consideration.

Put
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}=\frac58\Delta.
\tag{7}
\]
We first show that
\[
\mathcal E(b)\subseteq B
\qquad(0<b\leq1/32).
\tag{8}
\]
Fix \(z\in\mathcal E(b)\) and \(q\in\mathbb S^1\).  In the defining
constraints for \(\mathcal E(b)\), use the endpoint allocation \(u=0\).
The endpoint-normalized quantiles of accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878` give
\[
\begin{aligned}
2q\cdot z
&\geq Q_{0,q}(0)+Q_{2,q}(b)\\
&\geq Q_{0,q}(0)+Q_{2,q}(0)\\
&=\min_{x\in A_0}q\cdot x+\min_{x\in A_2}q\cdot x\\
&=2\min_{x\in B}q\cdot x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The support-halfplane representation
\[
B=\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb S^1}
\left\{z:q\cdot z\geq\min_{x\in B}q\cdot x\right\}
\tag{10}
\]
now proves (8).  Notice that (9) uses the actual finite individual
zero-demand support value \(Q_{0,q}(0)\), not the distinct total
zero-demand convention \(\mathcal E(0)=\mathbb R^2\).

It remains to show that no point of \(B\) belongs to any middle region in
(5).  If \(z=(x,y)\in B\), then \(x+y\leq5/8\).  For
\[
q=\frac{(1,1)}{\sqrt2},
\]
the lower \(q\)-cap of \(A_1=\Delta\) through \(z\) has area
\[
F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)
=\left|\Delta\cap\{(u,v):u+v\leq x+y\}\right|
=\frac{(x+y)^2}{2}
\leq\frac{25}{128}.
\tag{11}
\]
The exact strict comparison is
\[
\frac{25}{128}
<
\frac{31}{144},
\qquad
\frac{31}{144}-\frac{25}{128}
=\frac{23}{1152}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus for every \(a\in J\),
\[
F_{1,q}(q\cdot z)<a.
\]
Strict invertibility of the cap profile, again from accepted fact
`17ca54faa05ad878`, yields
\[
q\cdot z<Q_{1,q}(a)=D_{1,q}(a).
\]
Therefore \(z\notin\mathcal M(a)\).  Combining this with (8) proves
\[
\mathcal M(a)\cap\mathcal E(t-a)=\varnothing
\]
for every point of the full allocation interval (4), not only for its
endpoints or for a finite sample.

We finally determine the true middle depth region separately.  The triple
(1) is the common-vertex homothetic triangle family in accepted fact
`aef70a60593c53a2`, with its parameters
\[
a=1,\qquad c=\frac14,\qquad D=|\Delta|=\frac12.
\]
Its exact residual hypotheses hold:
\[
a\geq c>0,\qquad
1<a^2+c^2=\frac{17}{16},\qquad
a+c=\frac54<2,\qquad
a^2=1<\frac{17}{16}=1+c^2.
\]
That accepted theorem gives
\[
h_S((1,g))
\geq\frac{2(a^2+1+c^2)D}{9}
=\frac{11}{48},
\]
which is (6).  Hence the counterexample refutes only the registered
fixed-allocation architecture.

FIRST UNSUPPORTED STEP
Compatibility proves only that every positive-demand endpoint region is
confined to the midpoint body \(B\subseteq A_1\).  Opposite nesting and
separate nonemptiness do not force that body to meet a middle depth
region.  The same-direction cap geometry can instead exclude it: here one
middle cap of area \(25/128<\inf J\) contains all of \(B\).  Thus the first
unsupported step in a universal proof is any inference from
\(\mathcal E(t-a)\subseteq B\subseteq A_1\), together with the opposite
nesting, to a common point.

NEXT TEST
Replace the false common-allocation statement by the following exact
direction-dependent Farkas lemma.  With the continuous unique allocation
\(a_*(q)\) and threshold \(\rho_q(t)\) of accepted fact
`65512ded3a06d971`, prove for every noncollinear positive-spanning triple
\(q_1,q_2,q_3\) and every \(\alpha_i>0\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_iq_i=0
\]
that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\alpha_i\rho_{q_i}(t)\leq0.
\tag{13}
\]
This condition is coordinate-invariant and is exactly equivalent, by
Farkas, to intersection of the three profile halfplanes
\(\{z:q_i\cdot z\geq\rho_{q_i}(t)\}\).  Accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` shows that a global failure of the true middle depth
region in the strict area-triangle regime produces precisely such an empty
three-halfplane certificate.  Thus (13) is a sharply testable replacement
that permits the allocation to vary with direction and does not reintroduce
the disproved fixed-allocation step.
