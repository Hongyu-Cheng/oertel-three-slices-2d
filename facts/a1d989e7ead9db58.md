## Statement

Let
\[
r\in\mathbb Q,\qquad
\frac1{200}\leq r\leq\frac1{100},
\qquad
w:=\frac r{6250}.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
X_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
12&-1/2&12+w/2\\
-8&9/2&-8+w/2\\
-1&-1&-1-w
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
Z=
\begin{pmatrix}
-3/20&-7/20&-29/200\\
-7/20&-3/20&-69/200\\
-1/2&-1/2&-51/100
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(P_0\subset H_3\) and \(P_2\subset H_{-1}\) be the nondegenerate rational
triangles whose vertices are the columns of \(X_r\) and \(Z\).  Then:

1. \(P_0\) is in the missing-\(X_3\) chamber with vertex sign types
\(X_1,X_2,X_1\), its centroid index is \(I_0=\{1\}\), \(P_2\) is strictly
all-negative, and
\[
\frac{|P_0|}{|P_2|}=r.
\tag{4}
\]
After normalizing \(B=1\), one has \(A=r\), \(q=2\), and the strict residual
strip and \(A/B<3/8\).

2. The exact \(P_0\) cap fractions are
\[
u_1=1-\frac1{25(25+w)},\qquad
u_2=\frac{81}{25(25-w)},\qquad
u_3=0.
\tag{5}
\]
With
\[
c_1=\frac{225}{529},\qquad
d_1=\frac{729}{2116},\qquad
c_2=\frac49+\frac1{20000},\qquad
c_3=\frac{43}{100},
\tag{6}
\]
and \(v_1=v_2=v_3=0\), all three strict row inequalities, the strict budget
of accepted fact `0c27d2a820afec95`, and the two active-cell capacity
inequalities supplied by accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` hold uniformly
throughout (1).  The pair/triple restriction of accepted fact
`53099f38de81eac5` is also satisfied.

3. Nevertheless,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{\left|(P_0+P_2)/2\right|}{|P_2|}
=\frac{126+r}{4}>1.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently no triangle \(P_1\subset H_1\) with \(|P_1|=|P_2|\) can contain
\((P_0+P_2)/2\).  In particular, no member of the family (1)--(3) can be
completed to the \(B=1\) system of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`, for any
rational \(L,U,V,\sigma\).

## Proof

The columns of (2) sum to \(3\), and those of (3) sum to \(-1\).  Since
\(w>0\), both triangles are nondegenerate.  The three columns of \(X_r\) have
sign types \(X_1,X_2,X_1\), respectively, and its entire third row is
strictly negative.  Its centroid is
\[
g_0=
\left(
\frac{47+w}{6},
\frac{-23+w}{6},
-1-\frac w3
\right).
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(0<w\leq1/625000\), equation (8) gives
\[
(g_0)_1>3=1+q,\qquad
(g_0)_2<0,\qquad
(g_0)_3<0.
\]
Every entry of (3) is strictly negative, so its three cap fractions are zero
and its centroid has no nonnegative coordinate.

For the exact area calculation, use planar coordinates
\[
d=x_1-x_2,\qquad p=x_1+x_2.
\tag{9}
\]
The projected vertices of \(P_0\) in these coordinates are
\[
(20,4),\qquad(-5,4),\qquad(20,4+w),
\tag{10}
\]
and those of \(P_2\) are
\[
\left(\frac15,-\frac12\right),\qquad
\left(-\frac15,-\frac12\right),\qquad
\left(\frac15,-\frac{49}{100}\right).
\tag{11}
\]
The change (9), and the projection from any \(H_s\) to the first two
coordinates, have the same constant area factors for all three parallel
planes.  Hence area ratios may be computed directly from (10)--(11).
They are right triangles of respective areas
\[
\frac{25w}{2},
\qquad
\frac12\frac25\frac1{100}=\frac1{500}.
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(w=r/6250\), equation (12) gives
\[
\frac{|P_0|}{|P_2|}
=\frac{25w/2}{1/500}
=6250w=r,
\]
which proves (4).

Thus \(A=r\), \(B=1\), and \(R=r,S=1\).  Since \(r\leq1/100\), the
small-\(R\) residual branch is
\[
1-r<1<1+r,
\tag{13}
\]
and \(r<3/8\) as required.  The remaining residual upper bound is also
strict: \(\sqrt{26}<51/10\) gives
\[
\frac{27-2\sqrt{26}}{25}
>\frac{84}{125}
>\frac1{100}\geq r.
\]
Also \(q=2>1\).

We next audit all scalar and cap slack.  The three row-value lists in (2) are
\[
\left(-\frac12,12,12+\frac w2\right),
\qquad
\left(-8,-8+\frac w2,\frac92\right),
\qquad
(-1,-1,-1-w).
\]
The complete exact triangle-cap formulas of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` give (5).  Moreover,
\[
u_2<\frac{13}{100}<\frac59,\qquad u_1<1.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, \(u_2<13/100\) is equivalent to \(w<1/13\), which follows from
(1).  Since the third coordinate is negative everywhere, the only possible
two-positive cell is \(R_{12}\); hence the premise of the three-pair
implication in accepted fact `53099f38de81eac5` is false.

The middle scalars (6) come from a genuine first-coordinate threshold
chamber: the vertex values
\[
\left(-\frac{16}{3},-\frac{16}{3},10\right)
\]
have mean \(-2/9\), and their threshold-\(0\) and threshold-\(1\) cap
fractions are \(c_1\) and \(d_1\).  Uniformly in (1),
\[
\frac19<d_1<c_1<\frac{2(2-r)}9,
\qquad
\frac49<c_2<\frac{2(2+r)}9,
\tag{15}
\]
\[
c_2,c_3<\frac89,\qquad
c_2+c_3>1-d_1.
\tag{16}
\]
For reference, the smallest margins needed in (15)--(16) include
\[
\frac{2(2-1/100)}9-c_1=\frac{4021}{238050}>0,
\]
\[
d_1-\frac19=\frac{4445}{19044}>0,
\qquad
c_2+c_3-(1-d_1)
=\frac{20854361}{95220000}>0.
\]

Put
\[
t=\frac{2(2+r)}9=\frac49+\frac{2r}{9}.
\]
Using (14), the first two row margins have the uniform lower bounds
\[
t-(ru_1+c_1)
>
\frac49+\frac{2(1/100)}9-\frac1{100}-c_1
=\frac{1799}{158700}>0,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
t-(ru_2+c_2)
>
\frac1{200}\left(\frac29-\frac{13}{100}\right)
-\frac1{20000}
=\frac{37}{90000}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
In (17), the expression \(\frac49-c_1+r(2/9-1)\) decreases with \(r\), so
the upper endpoint \(r=1/100\) is the correct worst case.  In (18), the
expression \(r(2/9-13/100)-1/20000\) increases with \(r\), so the lower
endpoint \(r=1/200\) is the correct worst case.  The third row has
\[
t-c_3>\frac49-\frac{43}{100}=\frac{13}{900}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
The forced two-cap cover also has uniform strict room:
\[
2t-r-c_1-c_2
\geq
\frac89-\frac5{9}\frac1{100}-c_1-c_2
=\frac{1286239}{95220000}>0.
\tag{20}
\]

Let
\[
C=c_1+c_2+c_3-1
=\frac{28549361}{95220000}
<\frac3{10}.
\tag{21}
\]
Because \(P_2\) is all-negative, \(\beta=0\).  Since the coordinate caps
\(1,2\) cover \(P_0\),
\[
\alpha=r(u_1+u_2-1)<ru_2
<\frac{13r}{100}
\leq\frac{13}{10000}.
\tag{22}
\]
Also
\[
\delta=\frac{1-r}{3}\geq\frac{33}{100}.
\]
Equations (21)--(22) give the uniform strict budget
\[
\alpha+\beta+C
<
\frac3{10}+\frac{13}{10000}
<\frac{33}{100}
\leq\delta,
\tag{23}
\]
which is exactly the necessary budget of accepted fact
`0c27d2a820afec95`.

For completeness, the active-cell Brunn--Minkowski capacity also survives.
The two one-positive \(P_0\) cell areas are
\[
\xi_1=r(1-u_2)<r\leq\frac1{100},
\]
\[
\xi_2=r(1-u_1)
=\frac{r}{25(25+w)}
<\frac1{62500}.
\tag{24}
\]
The all-negative \(P_2\) cell has area \(1\).  Therefore the two middle
one-positive cell areas required by the boundary-inclusive inequality in
accepted fact `756b87a16a89ea8f` have total strictly less than
\[
\frac14\left[
\left(1+\frac1{10}\right)^2
+
\left(1+\frac1{250}\right)^2
\right]
=\frac{69313}{125000}.
\tag{25}
\]
Both cells lie in the lower third-coordinate cap, and
\[
1-c_3-\frac{69313}{125000}
=\frac{1937}{125000}>0.
\tag{26}
\]
The individual requirements fit as well:
\[
\frac{121}{400}<c_1,
\qquad
\frac14\left(\frac{251}{250}\right)^2<c_2.
\tag{27}
\]
This completes the claimed strict scalar and active-cell audit.

It remains to enforce the compatibility area gate.  Translations do not
affect Minkowski area, so in the \((d,p)\)-coordinates subtract the first
vertex of each triangle and write
\[
T_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-25,0),(0,w)\},
\]
\[
T_2=\operatorname{conv}
\left\{(0,0),\left(-\frac25,0\right),
\left(0,\frac1{100}\right)\right\}.
\tag{28}
\]
Because
\[
\frac{w}{25}
<
\frac{1/100}{2/5},
\]
the exact Minkowski sum in (28) is the quadrilateral with cyclic vertices
\[
(0,0),\quad
\left(-25-\frac25,0\right),\quad
\left(-25,\frac1{100}\right),\quad
\left(0,w+\frac1{100}\right).
\tag{29}
\]
The shoelace formula gives
\[
|T_0+T_2|
=\frac{25w}{2}
+\frac1{500}
+\frac14.
\tag{30}
\]
The first two terms in (30) are the endpoint areas; the last term is the
exact mixed-area contribution.  Scaling the sum by \(1/2\) divides its area
by \(4\).  Using (12) and \(25w/2=r/500\), one obtains
\[
\frac{|(P_0+P_2)/2|}{|P_2|}
=
\frac{r/500+1/500+1/4}{4/500}
=\frac{126+r}{4},
\]
which proves (7).

If a matrix completion existed, its transformed endpoint equations would be
\[
X_r=LU+\sigma\mathbf1^\top,\qquad
Z=LV-\sigma\mathbf1^\top,
\]
and its middle triangle would be \(P_1=L\Delta\).  Accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` gives
\((U\Delta+V\Delta)/2\subseteq\Delta\); applying \(L\) makes the opposite
translations cancel and yields
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,
\]
It also gives
\[
\frac{|P_2|}{|P_1|}=|\det V|=B=1.
\]
But containment and (7) imply
\[
|P_1|
\geq\left|\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\right|
=\frac{126+r}{4}|P_2|
>|P_2|,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence the whole rational family is incompatible before
any \(L,U,V,\sigma\) reconstruction.
