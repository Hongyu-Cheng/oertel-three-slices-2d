## Statement

Let \(C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a nonempty compact convex set. For \((x,z)\in C\), put
\[
C_x=\{u\in\mathbb R^2:(x,u)\in C\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
d_C(x,z)=\inf_{G\ni z}|C_x\cap G|,
\]
where the infimum is over closed planar halfplanes and \(|\cdot|\) is planar Lebesgue area. Then, for all \((x_0,z_0),(x_1,z_1)\in C\) and all \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
\sqrt{d_C\bigl((1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1,\,
                    (1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1\bigr)}
\ge
(1-\lambda)\sqrt{d_C(x_0,z_0)}
+\lambda\sqrt{d_C(x_1,z_1)}.
\]
Thus \((x,z)\mapsto\sqrt{d_C(x,z)}\) is concave on \(C\).

In particular, suppose \((0,z_0),(2,z_2)\in C\), put
\(z_1=(z_0+z_2)/2\), and, for a closed three-dimensional halfspace
\(H\), put \(H_j=\{u:(j,u)\in H\}\). Every such \(H\) containing
\((1,z_1)\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{j=0}^2|C_j\cap H_j|
\ge d_C(1,z_1)+\min\{d_C(0,z_0),d_C(2,z_2)\},
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{d_C(1,z_1)}
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{d_C(0,z_0)}+\sqrt{d_C(2,z_2)}}2.
\]

## Proof

We first prove the exact planar area inequality needed below. If \(K,L\)
are nonempty compact convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^2\) and
\(\lambda\in[0,1]\), then
\[
\sqrt{|(1-\lambda)K+\lambda L|}
\ge (1-\lambda)\sqrt{|K|}+\lambda\sqrt{|L|}. \tag{1}
\]
Here is a direct proof. First suppose \(|K|=|L|=1\) and
\(0<\lambda<1\). For a horizontal coordinate \(r\), let \(f(r)\) and
\(g(r)\) be the lengths of the vertical sections of \(K\) and \(L\).
Their supports are compact intervals, and \(f\) and \(g\) are positive
on the interiors of those intervals. Define the cumulative-area
functions
\[
F(r)=\int_{-\infty}^r f(s)\,ds,
\qquad
G(r)=\int_{-\infty}^r g(s)\,ds,
\]
and let \(X=F^{-1}\) and \(Y=G^{-1}\) on \((0,1)\). These inverse
functions are increasing and absolutely continuous, and ordinary
one-dimensional change of variables gives, almost everywhere,
\[
X'(q)=\frac1{f(X(q))},
\qquad
Y'(q)=\frac1{g(Y(q))}.
\]
These facts can also be obtained first on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\),
where the section lengths are bounded away from zero, and then by
letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

Let \(M=(1-\lambda)K+\lambda L\), let \(h(r)\) be the length of its
vertical section at \(r\), and set
\[
Z(q)=(1-\lambda)X(q)+\lambda Y(q).
\]
The vertical sections of a planar convex set are intervals. Hence the
section of \(M\) at \(Z(q)\) contains the interval obtained by taking
the same convex combination of the sections of \(K\) at \(X(q)\) and
of \(L\) at \(Y(q)\). Consequently,
\[
h(Z(q))\ge
(1-\lambda)f(X(q))+\lambda g(Y(q)). \tag{2}
\]
The increasing absolutely continuous map \(Z\) runs from the left
endpoint to the right endpoint of the horizontal projection of \(M\).
Using (2), changing variables by \(Z\), and abbreviating
\(a=f(X(q))\), \(b=g(Y(q))\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
|M|
&=\int_0^1 h(Z(q))Z'(q)\,dq\\
&\ge
\int_0^1\bigl((1-\lambda)a+\lambda b\bigr)
\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{a}+\frac{\lambda}{b}\right)dq\\
&\ge\int_0^1 1\,dq=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows from
\(a/b+b/a\ge2\). This proves (1) for two unit-area sets.

Now suppose \(|K|,|L|>0\), and write
\(a=\sqrt{|K|}\), \(b=\sqrt{|L|}\), and
\(R=(1-\lambda)a+\lambda b\). The sets \(a^{-1}K\) and \(b^{-1}L\)
have unit area, while
\[
(1-\lambda)K+\lambda L
=R\left(
\frac{(1-\lambda)a}{R}\,a^{-1}K
+
\frac{\lambda b}{R}\,b^{-1}L
\right).
\]
The unit-area case therefore gives area at least \(R^2\), proving
(1). If \(|K|=0\), choose \(k\in K\). The left-hand set in (1)
contains the translate \((1-\lambda)k+\lambda L\), whose area is
\(\lambda^2|L|\), so (1) again follows. The case \(|L|=0\) is
symmetric, and if both areas vanish the right-hand side is zero.
The cases \(\lambda=0,1\) are identities. Thus (1) holds also for
segments, points, and all other lower-dimensional compact convex sets.

We next reduce the depth infimum to halfplanes whose boundary passes
through the distinguished point. If
\[
G=\{u:\langle v,u\rangle\ge c\},
\qquad v\ne0,
\]
contains \(z\), then \(c\le\langle v,z\rangle\), and the parallel
halfplane
\[
G(v,z)=\{u:\langle v,u-z\rangle\ge0\}
\]
contains \(z\) and is contained in \(G\). Conversely, every
\(G(v,z)\) is admissible. Therefore
\[
d_C(x,z)=\inf_{v\ne0}|C_x\cap G(v,z)|. \tag{3}
\]
This also deals with a convention allowing the whole plane as a
halfplane, since a proper halfplane through \(z\) is contained in it.

Fix \((x_0,z_0),(x_1,z_1)\in C\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), and put
\[
x_\lambda=(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1,
\qquad
z_\lambda=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1.
\]
Convexity of \(C\) gives \((x_\lambda,z_\lambda)\in C\). For any
\(v\ne0\), set
\[
K_i=C_{x_i}\cap G(v,z_i)\quad(i=0,1).
\]
Both sets are nonempty compact convex sets, even when a section is
lower-dimensional. If \(u_i\in K_i\), then convexity of \(C\) and
linearity of the scalar product give
\[
(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1\in C_{x_\lambda},
\qquad
\left\langle v,
(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1-z_\lambda
\right\rangle\ge0.
\]
Hence
\[
(1-\lambda)K_0+\lambda K_1
\subseteq C_{x_\lambda}\cap G(v,z_\lambda). \tag{4}
\]
Applying (1) to (4) and then using the definition of depth yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{|C_{x_\lambda}\cap G(v,z_\lambda)|}
&\ge(1-\lambda)\sqrt{|K_0|}+\lambda\sqrt{|K_1|}\\
&\ge(1-\lambda)\sqrt{d_C(x_0,z_0)}
   +\lambda\sqrt{d_C(x_1,z_1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side is independent of \(v\). Taking the infimum over
\(v\ne0\) and using (3), together with monotonicity and continuity of
the square root, proves the asserted concavity. If either depth is
zero, the same argument applies because the zero-area cases of (1)
were included. If an entire section is lower-dimensional, its depth
is zero because all its planar intersections have zero area. No
interiority of \(x_0\) or \(x_1\) in the projection of \(C\) was used,
and the parameter endpoints \(\lambda=0,1\) give equality.

For the three-slice conclusions, let
\[
p_0=(0,z_0),\qquad p_1=(1,z_1),\qquad p_2=(2,z_2).
\]
Then \(p_1=(p_0+p_2)/2\), so the depth inequality follows immediately
from concavity with \(\lambda=1/2\).

It remains to prove the mixed-mass assertion. The whole-space case is
immediate, so write a nontrivial closed halfspace as
\[
H=\{(x,u):\ell(x,u)\ge0\},
\qquad
\ell(x,u)=\alpha x+\langle v,u\rangle-\beta,
\]
with \((\alpha,v)\ne0\). Since \(p_1\in H\) and \(\ell\) is affine,
\[
0\le\ell(p_1)=\frac{\ell(p_0)+\ell(p_2)}2.
\]
Thus \(\ell(p_i)\ge0\) for at least one \(i\in\{0,2\}\). In particular,
\(p_i\in H\); moreover, convexity of \(H\) shows that the whole segment
\([p_1,p_i]\), one complete side of the affine transversal
\([p_0,p_2]\), lies in \(H\).

The middle restriction \(H_1\) contains \(z_1\), and the chosen outer
restriction \(H_i\) contains \(z_i\). If \(v\ne0\), these restrictions
are planar closed halfplanes; if \(v=0\), every nonempty restriction is
the whole plane. In either case,
\[
|C_1\cap H_1|\ge d_C(1,z_1),
\qquad
|C_i\cap H_i|\ge d_C(i,z_i).
\]
The remaining slice contributes nonnegative area, and hence
\[
\sum_{j=0}^2|C_j\cap H_j|
\ge d_C(1,z_1)+d_C(i,z_i)
\ge d_C(1,z_1)+\min\{d_C(0,z_0),d_C(2,z_2)\}.
\]
This includes all cases in which a restriction, a section, or a depth
has zero area.
