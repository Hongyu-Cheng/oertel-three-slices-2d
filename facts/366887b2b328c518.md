## Statement

Assume the complete canonical finite-interior common-plane hypotheses and exact-cell convention of accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`, and the actual dominant all-double residual-counterexample hypotheses of accepted facts `2f637c4da2df28f1` and `174b54e9843ef8fc`. Thus
\[
M=|A_0|,\qquad m=|A_2|,\qquad k=|K|,
\]
the positive \(Q\)-support is exactly
\[
\{123,12,13,23\},
\]
\[
q=q_{123}>0,\qquad x_i=q_{N\setminus\{i\}}>0,\qquad
m=q+x_1+x_2+x_3,
\]
and, after labeling the unique dominant index as \(1\),
\[
x_1>\frac{5m}{9},\qquad q+x_2+x_3<\frac{4m}{9}.
\]
Assume also the three crossings, canonical tails, \(W=0\),
\[
r_1r_2r_3r_{123}>0,
\]
and the residual condition that no one of \(M,m,k\) is at least half of \(M+m+k\).

There is a finite universal constant \(C\), depending only on this canonical normalized class and not on the four positive cap ratios, such that
\[
\frac{M}{m}\le C.
\]
Equivalently, after the common planar area normalization \(m=1\), there is no sequence of such actual residual counterexamples with \(M_n\to\infty\). This remains true when any or all of \(q_n,x_{2,n},x_{3,n}\) tend to zero, subject only to their strict positivity at each finite stage.

## Proof

Normalize \(m_n=1\) and suppose for contradiction that a sequence exists with
\[
M_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{1}
\]
All notation below refers to this sequence.

First record the exact scalar limits. The no-half-slice conclusion in accepted fact `174b54e9843ef8fc` gives
\[
M_n<1+k_n,\qquad k_n<M_n+1.
\tag{2}
\]
Hence
\[
|M_n-k_n|<1,\qquad \frac{k_n}{M_n}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{3}
\]
Under all-double support,
\[
v_{i,n}=1-x_{i,n}.
\]
The \(i\)-th canonical tail and crossing from accepted fact `2f637c4da2df28f1` give
\[
c_{i,n}+1\ge h_n,\qquad
u_{i,n}+v_{i,n}+c_{i,n}=h_n,
\qquad
h_n=\frac{2(M_n+1+k_n)}9.
\]
Consequently
\[
u_{i,n}+v_{i,n}\le1,\qquad
0\le u_{i,n}\le x_{i,n}.
\tag{4}
\]
Writing \(U_n=\sum_i u_{i,n}\), the positive \(Q\)-cell partition gives
\[
0\le U_n\le x_{1,n}+x_{2,n}+x_{3,n}=1-q_n<1.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(p_n=p_{\varnothing,n}\). Every nonempty \(P\)-cell is counted at least once in \(U_n\), while \(P_{123}\) is empty by accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f`. Therefore
\[
0\le M_n-p_n\le U_n<1.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (1), (3), and (6) imply
\[
p_n\to\infty,\qquad
\frac{M_n}{p_n}\to1,\qquad
\frac{k_n}{p_n}\to1.
\tag{7}
\]
The crossing identity and (4) give
\[
\frac{c_{i,n}}{M_n}
=\frac{h_n-u_{i,n}-v_{i,n}}{M_n}
\longrightarrow\frac49,
\qquad
\frac{c_{i,n}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\frac49
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, the exact all-double defect identity in accepted fact `0819231d0af83197` is
\[
2M_n=4+k_n+3(U_n+q_n+E_{R,n}),
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
E_{R,n}=r_{12,n}+r_{13,n}+r_{23,n}+2r_{123,n}.
\]
Equations (3), (5), and (9) yield
\[
\frac{E_{R,n}}{M_n}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\tag{10}
\]
Also, if \(\{i,j,\ell\}=N\), inclusion-exclusion and the impossibility of the middle empty cell give
\[
r_{i,n}
=k_n-c_{j,n}-c_{\ell,n}
+|K_n\cap\{f_{j,n}\le0,\ f_{\ell,n}\le0\}|,
\]
so
\[
\liminf_n\frac{r_{i,n}}{M_n}\ge\frac19.
\tag{11}
\]

We next classify the only possible geometric limit. Apply a common invertible affine map to put each middle body in John position:
\[
B\subseteq \widehat K_n\subseteq2B.
\tag{12}
\]
Apply the same map to \(A_{0,n},A_{2,n}\), all cutting lines, and all affine functions. We suppress hats and continue to use the same letters for the transformed cell areas. The map multiplies all of them by one common positive factor, so every ratio in (7)-(11) is unchanged. After passage to a subsequence,
\[
K_n\longrightarrow K
\tag{13}
\]
in Hausdorff distance, where \(B\subseteq K\subseteq2B\).

Put
\[
\lambda_{i,n}=-f_{i,n},\qquad
\lambda_{1,n}+\lambda_{2,n}+\lambda_{3,n}=1.
\tag{14}
\]
The middle marginal is
\[
c_{i,n}=|K_n\cap\{\lambda_{i,n}\ge0\}|.
\]
All ratios in (7)-(11) are unchanged by the common affine map. In particular,
\[
\frac{|K_n\cap\{\lambda_{i,n}\ge0\}|}{|K_n|}
\longrightarrow\frac49.
\tag{15}
\]
For all large \(n\), both sides of each zero line have positive area in \(K_n\). Define
\[
s_{i,n}=|\nabla\lambda_{i,n}|>0,\qquad
\phi_{i,n}=\frac{\lambda_{i,n}}{s_{i,n}},
\]
\[
S_n=s_{1,n}+s_{2,n}+s_{3,n},\qquad
\alpha_{i,n}=\frac{s_{i,n}}{S_n}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_i\alpha_{i,n}\phi_{i,n}=\frac1{S_n},
\qquad
\alpha_{i,n}>0,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_{i,n}=1.
\tag{17}
\]
Each \(\phi_{i,n}\) has unit gradient. Its zero line meets \(2B\), so its constant term is bounded. Evaluating (17) at the origin also bounds \(1/S_n\). After another subsequence,
\[
\phi_{i,n}\to\phi_i,\qquad
\alpha_{i,n}\to\alpha_i,\qquad
\frac1{S_n}\to c\ge0,
\tag{18}
\]
locally uniformly, where each \(\phi_i\) is nonconstant with unit gradient,
\[
\alpha_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\alpha_i=1,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i\phi_i=c.
\tag{19}
\]
Hausdorff convergence, local uniform convergence, and the zero area of affine lines give
\[
|K\cap\{\phi_i\ge0\}|=\frac49|K|
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{20}
\]

Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(K\). We claim
\[
\phi_i(g)\le0\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{21}
\]
If \(\phi_i(g)>0\), the smaller halfplane
\[
\{\phi_i\ge\phi_i(g)\}
\]
has boundary through \(g\). The planar Grünbaum inequality in accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` gives this smaller centroidal section area at least \(4|K|/9\). Since \(g\) is interior to \(K\), the strip removed from \(\{\phi_i\ge0\}\) has positive area. This contradicts (20), proving (21).

Evaluating (19) at \(g\) now gives
\[
c=0,\qquad
\phi_i(g)=0\quad\text{whenever }\alpha_i>0.
\tag{22}
\]
There cannot be only one positive \(\alpha_i\), because (19) would make a nonconstant affine function identically zero. There cannot be exactly two: if, say, only \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) are positive, then
\[
\alpha_1\phi_1=-\alpha_2\phi_2,
\]
so the two halfplanes are complementary up to their common line. Their areas would sum to \(|K|\), whereas (20) makes their sum \(8|K|/9\). Hence
\[
\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3>0,\qquad
\phi_1(g)=\phi_2(g)=\phi_3(g)=0.
\tag{23}
\]

Equality in the planar Grünbaum inequality now applies to each halfplane in (20). Its equality characterization says that \(K\) is a triangle, each zero line is through its centroid and parallel to one side, and the indexed halfplane points toward the opposite vertex. Two indexed halfplanes cannot be the same vertex cap: their two positive terms in the zero affine dependence (19) would force the third term to have the complementary orientation on that same line, whose area is \(5|K|/9\), contradicting (20). Thus, after relabeling the barycentric coordinates \(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3\) of \(K\),
\[
\{\phi_i\ge0\}=\{\mu_i\ge1/3\}
\quad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{24}
\]
There are \(t_i>0\) such that
\[
\phi_i=t_i(\mu_i-1/3).
\]
The unique affine dependence among the three functions
\(\mu_i-1/3\) is their sum zero. Hence (19) and (23) give a number
\(\tau>0\) such that
\[
\alpha_1t_1=\alpha_2t_2=\alpha_3t_3=\tau.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (16), (18), and (25) imply, locally uniformly,
\[
\frac{\lambda_{i,n}}{S_n\tau}
=\frac{\alpha_{i,n}\phi_{i,n}}{\tau}
\longrightarrow\mu_i-\frac13.
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (22) gives \(S_n\to\infty\).

This also determines every limiting middle cell fraction. The three vertex caps in (24) have area \(4|K|/9\); every pairwise intersection has area \(|K|/9\); their triple intersection is the centroid alone. Therefore dominated convergence for the exact cells gives
\[
\frac{r_{i,n}}{k_n}\to\frac29,\qquad
\frac{r_{ij,n}}{k_n}\to\frac19,\qquad
\frac{r_{123,n}}{k_n}\to0.
\tag{27}
\]
In particular, (10) is recovered geometrically.

We now turn the limiting triangle into a finite-stage contradiction. Define the common lower offset
\[
a_n=\max_{1\le i\le3}
\left(-\min_{z\in K_n}\lambda_{i,n}(z)\right).
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(\min_K(\mu_i-1/3)=-1/3\), equations (13) and (26) give
\[
\frac{a_n}{S_n\tau}\longrightarrow\frac13,
\qquad a_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{29}
\]
Put
\[
T_n=\{\lambda_{i,n}\ge-a_n:\ i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{30}
\]
By definition, \(K_n\subseteq T_n\). Dividing the three inequalities in (30) by \(S_n\tau\), equations (26) and (29) show that their limit is
\[
\mu_i\ge0\quad(i=1,2,3),
\]
whose intersection is exactly \(K\). The three limiting normals are pairwise nonparallel and positively span the plane. Hence, for all large \(n\), \(T_n\) is a bounded triangle,
\[
T_n\longrightarrow K
\]
in Hausdorff distance, and
\[
\theta_n:=\frac{|K_n|}{|T_n|}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{31}
\]

Let
\[
\Delta_n=\{\lambda_{i,n}\ge0:\ i=1,2,3\}.
\]
The affine map \(z\mapsto(\lambda_{1,n}(z),\lambda_{2,n}(z),\lambda_{3,n}(z))\) is an affine isomorphism onto the plane of coordinate sum \(1\). Thus \(\Delta_n\) is exactly the ambient central middle triangle. The hypotheses \(r_{1,n}r_{2,n}r_{3,n}r_{123,n}>0\) allow direct application of accepted fact `e40abbed940a40ab`: it gives \(\Delta_n\subseteq K_n\) and identifies \(\Delta_n\), up to null cutting lines, with the exact cell \(R_{123,n}\). Hence
\[
|\Delta_n|=r_{123,n}.
\tag{32}
\]
In the same coordinate plane, changing the three lower bounds from \(0\) to \(-a_n\) dilates the standard simplex by factor \(1+3a_n\). Consequently
\[
|T_n|=r_{123,n}(1+3a_n)^2.
\tag{33}
\]

It remains to retain the positive \(Q\)-cells even when their areas vanish in the limit. Put
\[
\beta_{i,n}=-L_{i,n},\qquad
\delta_n=\beta_{1,n}+\beta_{2,n}+\beta_{3,n}-1
=-1-\sum_iL_{i,n}>0.
\tag{34}
\]
The exact empty-index \(P\)-cell satisfies
\[
P_{\varnothing,n}\subseteq
\{\lambda_{i,n}<\beta_{i,n}:\ i=1,2,3\}.
\tag{35}
\]
Fix \(i\). At every finite stage the all-double support gives
\[
q_{N\setminus\{i\},n}=x_{i,n}>0.
\]
Choose
\[
y_{i,n}\in Q_{N\setminus\{i\},n}.
\]
Because this exact \(Q\)-cell omits \(i\), accepted fact `0e51a47a9add571f` gives
\[
\lambda_{i,n}(y_{i,n})<L_{i,n}=-\beta_{i,n}.
\tag{36}
\]
For every \(x\in P_{\varnothing,n}\), midpoint compatibility and
\(K_n\subseteq T_n\) imply
\[
\lambda_{i,n}(x)+\lambda_{i,n}(y_{i,n})\ge-2a_n.
\]
Using (36),
\[
\lambda_{i,n}(x)>\beta_{i,n}-2a_n.
\tag{37}
\]
No quantitative lower bound on \(x_{i,n}\) was used.

On the ambient reflected \(P\)-triangle, use barycentric coordinates
\[
\nu_{i,n}
=\frac{\beta_{i,n}-\lambda_{i,n}}{\delta_n}.
\tag{38}
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). Equations (35) and (37) put
\(P_{\varnothing,n}\) inside
\[
\left\{
\nu_i\ge0,\quad \sum_i\nu_i=1,\quad
\nu_i<\frac{2a_n}{\delta_n}\ (i=1,2,3)
\right\}.
\tag{39}
\]
The reflected ambient \(P\)-triangle has area
\(\delta_n^2r_{123,n}\), because (38) is a reflected dilation by
\(\delta_n\) from \(\Delta_n\). For the unit barycentric simplex, the
area fraction on which \(\max_i\nu_i\le s\) is
\[
F(s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le s\le1/3,\\
(3s-1)^2,&1/3\le s\le1/2,\\
1-3(1-s)^2,&1/2\le s\le1,\\
1,&s\ge1.
\end{cases}
\tag{40}
\]
Therefore
\[
p_n\le
\delta_n^2r_{123,n}F(2a_n/\delta_n).
\tag{41}
\]
For every \(a,\delta>0\),
\[
\delta^2F(2a/\delta)\le6a^2.
\tag{42}
\]
Indeed, for \(\delta\le2a\) the left side is at most \(4a^2\). For
\(2a\le\delta\le4a\) it is
\[
\delta^2-3(\delta-2a)^2\le6a^2.
\]
For \(4a\le\delta\le6a\) it is
\[
(6a-\delta)^2\le4a^2,
\]
and for \(\delta\ge6a\) it is zero. Thus
\[
p_n\le6a_n^2r_{123,n}.
\tag{43}
\]

Finally, (31), (33), and (43) give
\[
\frac{k_n}{p_n}
\ge
\theta_n\frac{(1+3a_n)^2}{6a_n^2}
\tag{44}
\]
The right-hand side tends to \(3/2\), so
\[
\liminf_n\frac{k_n}{p_n}\ge\frac32.
\]
This contradicts \(k_n/p_n\to1\) in (7).

Thus no normalized sequence with \(M_n\to\infty\) exists. If the ratios
\(M/m\) were not uniformly bounded, scaling each configuration by the
common planar homothety that makes \(m=1\) would produce exactly such a
sequence. Hence a finite universal \(C\) exists. The proof used only the
strict finite-stage positivity \(x_{i,n}>0\), so every boundary limit
with \(q_n,x_{2,n},x_{3,n}\to0\) is covered.
