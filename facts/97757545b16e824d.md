## Statement

Let
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
A_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3/2,0),(0,5/4)\},
\]
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(1/2,1/4)\},
\]
and let
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times A_1)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
For every proper closed halfspace \(H\subset\mathbb R^3\), define its unnormalized three-slice mass by
\[
\mu(H)=\sum_{k=0}^2
\left|A_k\cap\{w\in\mathbb R^2:(k,w)\in H\}\right|.
\]
For \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y)=\inf\{\mu(H):H\subset\mathbb R^3
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }y\in H\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_S\!\left(\left(1,\frac12,\frac5{12}\right)\right)\ge \frac49.
\]
Consequently, there do not exist at most three proper closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^3\), each of total three-slice mass strictly below \(4/9\), whose height-one trace interiors cover \(\{1\}\times A_1\).  More strongly, no family of such halfspaces has height-one trace interiors covering \(\{1\}\times A_1\).

## Proof

Write
\[
z_c=\left(\frac12,\frac5{12}\right).
\]
We first record two elementary triangle-cap facts, including the only tied-value case used below.  Let \(K\) be a triangle of area \(A\), let \(L\) be affine on \(K\), and suppose its ordered vertex values satisfy
\[
a<b\le c.
\]
Then
\[
\left|K\cap\{L\le t\}\right|
=A\,\frac{(t-a)^2}{(b-a)(c-a)}
\qquad(a\le t\le b).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the two intersection points on the edges issuing from the \(a\)-vertex occur at the fractions \((t-a)/(b-a)\) and \((t-a)/(c-a)\), so their product is the area ratio.  This proof and formula remain valid when \(b=c\); no tied minimum is allowed in (1).

For the second fact, put \(m=\min_K L\) and \(M=\max_K L\).  If \(m<M\) and \(m\le t\le M\), then
\[
\left|K\cap\{L\le t\}\right|
\ge A\left(\frac{t-m}{M-m}\right)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
This follows by dilating all of \(K\) about a vertex at which \(L=m\).  If \(t\ge M\), the cap is all of \(K\).  If \(L\) is constant, so \(M=m\), then the cap is empty for \(t<m\) and is all of \(K\) for \(t\ge m\); no quotient in (2) is used in that case.

Introduce the barycentric coordinates on \(A_1\)
\[
\alpha_0=1-\frac23x-\frac45y,\qquad
\alpha_1=\frac23x,\qquad
\alpha_2=\frac45y.
\]
They are nonnegative on \(A_1\), sum to \(1\), and each equals \(1\) at the corresponding vertex
\[
V_0=(0,0),\qquad V_1=(3/2,0),\qquad V_2=(0,5/4).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\alpha_0(z_c)=\alpha_1(z_c)=\alpha_2(z_c)=\frac13.
\tag{3}
\]

Consider a planar halfplane whose boundary passes through \(z_c\).  If its cap in \(A_1\) contains one vertex \(V_i\), its boundary meets the two edges issuing from \(V_i\) at fractions \(p,q\) from \(V_i\).  Since the centroid lies on the segment joining these two intersection points,
\[
\frac1p+\frac1q=3.
\]
Thus, after choosing an order for the other two barycentric coordinates, there is an \(r\in[1,2]\) such that
\[
\frac1p=r,\qquad \frac1q=3-r,
\]
and the cap is
\[
r\alpha_j+(3-r)\alpha_k\le1.
\tag{4}
\]
Its area is
\[
\frac{|A_1|}{r(3-r)}=\frac{15}{16r(3-r)}.
\tag{5}
\]
The complementary two-vertex cap has at least half the area of \(A_1\), hence area at least \(15/32>4/9\).  Therefore, if a halfplane through \(z_c\) cuts from \(A_1\) an area \(<4/9\), it must be a one-vertex cap as in (4).  By (5),
\[
\frac{15}{16r(3-r)}<\frac49
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac98<r<\frac{15}8.
\tag{6}
\]
For every \(r\in[1,2]\), (5) is at least \(5/12\).

We next prove the exact endpoint-payment bound needed for all the directions in (6).  For \(9/8\le r\le15/8\), choose the following affine functions for the three possible one-vertex caps:
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=r\alpha_1+(3-r)\alpha_2
     =\frac{2r}{3}x+\frac{4(3-r)}5y,\\
L_1&=r\alpha_0+(3-r)\alpha_2
     =r-\frac{2r}{3}x+\frac{12-8r}{5}y,\\
L_2&=r\alpha_0+(3-r)\alpha_1
     =r+\frac{6-4r}{3}x-\frac{4r}{5}y.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Their values at the vertices of \(A_0\) and \(A_2\), in the vertex orders displayed in the statement, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &A_0&A_2\\ \hline
L_0&
\left(0,\frac{4r}{3},\frac{4(3-r)}5\right)&
\left(0,\frac r3,\frac{2r+9}{15}\right)\\[2mm]
L_1&
\left(r,-\frac r3,\frac{12-3r}{5}\right)&
\left(r,\frac{2r}{3},\frac{4r+9}{15}\right)\\[2mm]
L_2&
\left(r,4-\frac{5r}{3},\frac r5\right)&
\left(r,1+\frac r3,1+\frac{2r}{15}\right).
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
We claim that, for \(i=0,1,2\) and every real \(s\),
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{L_i\le s\}\right|
+
\left|A_2\cap\{L_i\le2-s\}\right|
\ge\frac1{32}.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(i=0\), the maximum of \(L_0\) on \(A_2\) is
\[
M_2=\frac{2r+9}{15}\le\frac{17}{20}.
\]
If \(s\le2-M_2\), the entire \(A_2\) is in its cap, giving mass \(1/16\).  If \(s\ge2-M_2\), then on \(A_0\) the minimum is \(0\), the maximum is \(4r/3\le5/2\), and \(2-M_2\ge23/20\).  The affine function is nonconstant, and the homothety bound (2), or the full-cap case if the threshold is already above its maximum, gives
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{L_0\le s\}\right|
\ge\left(\frac{23}{50}\right)^2>\frac1{32}.
\]

For \(i=1\), the maximum of \(L_1\) on \(A_2\) is \(M_2=r\).  If \(s\le2-r\), the \(A_2\)-cap is all of \(A_2\).  Otherwise, the minimum of \(L_1\) on \(A_0\) is \(-r/3\), its range on \(A_0\) is positive and at most
\[
\frac{15}{8}+\frac58=\frac52,
\]
and
\[
(2-r)-\left(-\frac r3\right)=2-\frac{2r}{3}\ge\frac34.
\]
Thus (2), or the full-cap conclusion when \(2-r\) lies above the maximum, gives
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{L_1\le s\}\right|
\ge\left(\frac3{10}\right)^2>\frac1{32}.
\]

It remains to prove (9) for \(i=2\).  First suppose
\[
\frac98\le r\le\frac{30}{23}.
\]
The maximum of \(L_2\) on \(A_2\) is \(1+r/3\).  Put \(s_0=1-r/3\).  If \(s\le s_0\), the \(A_2\)-cap is all of \(A_2\).  If \(s\ge s_0\), the threshold \(s_0\) lies in the first interval of the strictly ordered \(A_0\)-vertex values
\[
\frac r5<r<4-\frac{5r}{3}.
\]
Formula (1) yields
\[
\left|A_0\cap\{L_2\le s\}\right|
\ge
\frac{(15-8r)^2}{48r(15-7r)}
\ge\frac1{32}.
\tag{10}
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to
\[
149r^2-525r+450\ge0.
\]
This quadratic is decreasing on \([9/8,30/23]\), and its value at \(30/23\) is \(9900/529>0\).

Now suppose
\[
\frac{30}{23}\le r\le\frac{15}{8}.
\tag{11}
\]
Write
\[
F_0(s)=|A_0\cap\{L_2\le s\}|,\qquad
F_2(q)=|A_2\cap\{L_2\le q\}|.
\]
Assume for contradiction that \(F_0(s)+F_2(2-s)<1/32\), so each summand is \(<1/32\).  The least \(A_0\)-vertex value is
\[
a_0=\frac r5.
\]
The middle \(A_0\)-vertex value is \(b_0=r\) for \(r\le3/2\), and \(b_0=4-5r/3\) for \(r\ge3/2\).  At \(r=3/2\) the two larger vertex values are tied, which is the allowed \(b=c\) case of (1).  Formula (1) at the middle value gives
\[
F_0(b_0)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3r}{15-7r},&r\le3/2,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{15-7r}{3r},&r\ge3/2,
\end{cases}
\qquad F_0(b_0)\ge\frac13.
\tag{12}
\]
The least \(A_2\)-vertex value is
\[
a_2=1+\frac{2r}{15}.
\]
The middle \(A_2\)-vertex value is \(b_2=r\) for \(r\le3/2\), and \(b_2=1+r/3\) for \(r\ge3/2\).  At \(r=3/2\) the two larger values are again tied.  Formula (1) gives
\[
16F_2(b_2)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{13r-15}{3r},&r\le3/2,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{3r}{13r-15},&r\ge3/2,
\end{cases}
\qquad 16F_2(b_2)\ge\frac12.
\tag{13}
\]
Hence \(s<b_0\) and \(2-s<b_2\).

If \(s\le a_0\), then \(2-s\ge b_2\): for \(r\le3/2\) this is
\[
2-\frac r5\ge r,
\]
and for \(r\ge3/2\) it is
\[
2-\frac r5\ge1+\frac r3,
\]
the latter following from \(r\le15/8\).  This contradicts (13).  Thus \(s>a_0\).

If \(2-s\le a_2\), then \(s\ge2-a_2\).  The threshold \(2-a_2\) lies strictly between the least and middle \(A_0\)-vertex values throughout (11), so (1) gives
\[
F_0(2-a_2)
=\frac{75(1-r/3)^2}{16r(15-7r)}
>\frac1{32}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, after clearing positive denominators, (14) is
\[
71r^2-345r+450>0,
\]
whose discriminant is \(-8775\).  This is also a contradiction.  Therefore
\[
x=s-\frac r5>0,\qquad
y=(2-s)-\left(1+\frac{2r}{15}\right)>0.
\]
Both thresholds now lie strictly above the least and strictly below the middle vertex values.  Formula (1) and \(|A_2|=1/16\) give the exact cap masses
\[
F_0(s)=\frac{75x^2}{16r(15-7r)},\qquad
F_2(2-s)=\frac{75y^2}{16r(13r-15)}.
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
x+y=1-\frac r3=\frac{3-r}{3}.
\]
Since \(15-7r>0\) and \(13r-15>0\), Cauchy's inequality applied to (15) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(s)+F_2(2-s)
&=\frac{75}{16r}
\left(\frac{x^2}{15-7r}+\frac{y^2}{13r-15}\right)\\
&\ge
\frac{75}{16r}\,
\frac{(x+y)^2}{(15-7r)+(13r-15)}\\
&=\frac{25(3-r)^2}{288r^2}
\ge\frac1{32},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality is exactly \(r\le15/8\).  This contradiction proves (9).

We now return to three-dimensional halfspaces.  Let \(H\) be any proper closed halfspace containing \((1,z_c)\).  Translate its boundary inward, without changing its normal, until it passes through \((1,z_c)\); the resulting halfspace \(H'\) is contained in \(H\), so \(\mu(H)\ge\mu(H')\).  If the planar part of the normal is zero, \(H'\) contains \(A_1\) together with at least one of the two outer slices, and its mass is already greater than \(4/9\).

Otherwise, the height-one trace of \(H'\) is a halfplane through \(z_c\).  If its \(A_1\)-area is at least \(4/9\), there is nothing to prove.  If it is below \(4/9\), (4)--(7) show that, after positive rescaling, \(H'\) has the form
\[
L_i(x,y)+\kappa(h-1)\le1
\]
for some \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\) and some \(r\in(9/8,15/8)\).  With \(s=1+\kappa\), its thresholds on \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) are \(s\) and \(2-s\).  Its middle mass is at least \(5/12\), while (9) gives endpoint mass at least \(1/32\).  Hence
\[
\mu(H)
\ge \mu(H')
\ge\frac5{12}+\frac1{32}
=\frac{43}{96}
>\frac49.
\]
Thus every proper closed halfspace containing \((1,z_c)\) has unnormalized three-slice mass at least \(4/9\).  Taking the infimum in the displayed definition of \(h_S\) proves
\[
h_S((1,z_c))\ge\frac49.
\]

Finally, if a proper closed halfspace has mass \(<4/9\), its height-one trace interior cannot contain \(z_c\).  Therefore \(z_c\) belongs to the closed complement of every such trace interior.  The closed complements of any collection of strict-low-mass traces consequently intersect at \(z_c\), so their trace interiors cannot cover \(A_1\).  This proves the claimed exact infeasibility, in particular for every family of at most three halfspaces.
