## Statement

Assume the full surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system and incidence orbit of accepted facts `82b7c8c40e88aaf1` and `f8e871d10dac1791`.  Thus the middle vertex matrix \(L=(\ell_{ij})\) has column sums one, all its entries are nonzero,
\[
J_1=\{1,2\},\qquad J_2=\{2,3\},\qquad J_3=\{1\},
\]
the first two row means are negative, the third row mean is greater than one, and
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9\leq\frac12,
\qquad A=|\det U|>0,\qquad B=|\det V|>0.
\]
For \(r\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), let
\[
\mathcal C(r;\tau)
=\left|\{\lambda\in\Delta:r\cdot\lambda\geq\tau\}\right|
\]
under the normalization \(|\Delta|=1\) of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`.  There do not exist compatible nonsingular endpoint matrices \(U,V\), such an \(L\), and an arbitrary shift vector \(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)^\top\) satisfying all three strict inequalities
\[
\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)
+A\mathcal C((LU)_{i*};-\sigma_i)
+B\mathcal C((LV)_{i*};\sigma_i)<t
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]

## Proof

Suppose such a full system exists.  The chamber signs and negative means of the first two rows allow the unique notation
\[
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
x&y&-d\\
-e&z&w\\
p&-a&-b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
x,y,d,e,z,w,p,a,b>0,
\]
with
\[
d>x+y,\qquad e>z+w.
\tag{1}
\]
The column-sum identities give
\[
p=1-x+e,\qquad a=y+z-1,\qquad b=w-d-1.
\tag{2}
\]
Put
\[
D=d-x-y>0,qquad E=e-z-w>0,
\qquad \delta=D+E+3.
\tag{3}
\]
Subtracting the last two identities in (2) from the first gives
\[
p-a-b=3+(d-x-y)+(e-z-w)=\delta,
\qquad p=a+b+\delta.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(c_i=\mathcal C(L_{i*};0)\).  Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791`, applied to the full strict system in this incidence orbit, gives
\[
c_1,c_2<\kappa<\frac49,
\qquad c_3<t\leq\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]
The exact open-chamber formulas in the same accepted fact, equivalently the cap formulas of accepted fact `64760c76cadff481`, are
\[
c_1=1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)},\qquad
c_2=1-\frac{e^2}{(e+z)(e+w)},\qquad
c_3=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}.
\tag{6}
\]

We first extract the strict consequences of the first two inequalities in (5).  From \(c_1<4/9\),
\[
9d^2-5(d+x)(d+y)>0.
\tag{7}
\]
After substituting \(d=x+y+D\), the left side of (7) is
\[
F(D)=4D^2+3D(x+y)-x^2-7xy-y^2.
\]
The function \(F\) is strictly increasing for \(D>0\), while
\[
F\!\left(\frac{x+y}{4}\right)=-5xy<0.
\]
Consequently
\[
D>\frac{x+y}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]
The identical calculation using \(c_2<4/9\), \(e=z+w+E\), and the positive variables \(z,w\) gives
\[
E>\frac{z+w}{4}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)-(9) imply
\[
D+E>\frac{x+y+z+w}{4}.
\tag{10}
\]
On the other hand, (2) gives
\[
x+y+z+w-(a+b)=x+d+2>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (3), (10), and (11),
\[
\delta=D+E+3>\frac{a+b}{4}.
\tag{12}
\]

Set \(s=a+b>0\).  Since \(\delta>s/4\), (4) and monotonicity of \(\xi\mapsto(s+\xi)\xi\) for \(\xi>0\) give
\[
p\delta=(s+\delta)\delta>
\left(s+\frac{s}{4}\right)\frac{s}{4}
=\frac{5s^2}{16}.
\tag{13}
\]
The weak arithmetic-geometric-mean boundary is retained exactly:
\[
ab\leq\frac{(a+b)^2}{4}=\frac{4s^2}{16}.
\tag{14}
\]
Even if equality holds in (14), the strict inequality in (13) yields
\[
p\delta>ab.
\tag{15}
\]
Using \(\delta=p-a-b\), this is precisely
\[
2p^2-(p+a)(p+b)
=p\bigl(p-a-b\bigr)-ab
=p\delta-ab>0.
\tag{16}
\]
The third formula in (6) therefore gives
\[
c_3>\frac12,
\]
contradicting \(c_3<t\leq1/2\) from (5).

This contradiction was obtained from necessary middle-cap consequences of the complete strict system, not from a relaxation in which the endpoints were deleted.  Accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` makes each endpoint term a normalized cap area and hence nonnegative for every ordering, repeated endpoint value, constant endpoint projection, and independent value of \(\sigma_i\); it also uses \(|\det U|\) and \(|\det V|\), so both determinant signs are included.  Accepted fact `f8e871d10dac1791` supplies (5) uniformly under the exact compatibility constraints.  Thus no endpoint cap order, determinant sign, compatibility boundary, or independent shift can evade the middle contradiction.  The only weak inequality used in the new argument is (14), and its equality case still leads to the strict contradiction by (13).
