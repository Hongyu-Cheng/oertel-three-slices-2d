## Proof

The totals and positivity in (1)--(3) are direct rational sums.  Their
cap vectors are
\[
u=
\left(
\frac{1129}{30800},
\frac{1129}{30800},
\frac{3271}{30800}
\right),
\qquad
v=
\left(
\frac{439}{3850},
\frac{439}{3850},
\frac{5631}{30800}
\right),
\tag{4}
\]
\[
c=
\left(
\frac{663}{4400},
\frac{663}{4400},
\frac{4451}{15400}
\right),
\qquad
b=
\left(
\frac{28079}{92400},
\frac{28079}{92400},
\frac{956}{5775}
\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Since
\[
T=M+m+k,\qquad
\frac{2T}{9}=\frac{21001}{46200},
\]
equations (4)--(5) give
\[
c_i+b_i=\frac{2T}{9}\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus all three crossings, and hence their summed cell-balance identity,
hold exactly.

For the canonical tails of accepted fact `624b129b68e0fdee`, the
crossings reduce the left and right tail conditions to
\(c_i\leq m\) and \(c_i<M\).  Here
\[
m-\max_i c_i
=\frac{2047}{46200}
>\varepsilon,
\tag{7}
\]
so both tails are strict.

The opposite cells are strictly positive:
\[
p_0=\frac67,\qquad q_3=\frac{13}{100},\qquad
q_{12}=\frac1{80}.
\]
Moreover, the filling required by accepted fact
`78d1d57e3961c4e4` is strict because
\[
q_{13}+q_{23}+q_{123}
=\frac{1627}{30800}>0.
\tag{8}
\]

We next check the three convexity gates.  For the \(P\)-table, let
\(p_i=f_i-L_i\), so \(\sum_i p_i=\delta>0\).  Applying accepted fact
`5b75ecd9ec86dbcf` to \(-p_i/\delta\) uses the three cap masses
\(M-u_i\).  Its residual is
\[
5\sum_i(M-u_i)-6\min_i(M-u_i)-4M
=\frac{13271}{2800}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
For the \(Q\)-table, positively rescale the three affine functions
\(q_i=f_i+L_i\), whose sum is a negative constant.  Their cap masses
are \(v_i\), and the residual is
\[
5\sum_i v_i-6\min_i v_i-4m
=\frac{487}{13200}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
For the middle table, the cap masses are \(b_i\), and
\[
5\sum_i b_i-6\min_i b_i-4k
=\frac{1129}{46200}
=2\varepsilon>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus all three separate applications of the accepted nonconcurrent
three-cap theorem hold strictly.  The equivalent exact-cell forms hold
because (1)--(3) are exact disjoint cell tables.

The strict slice balances required by the contrapositive use of
accepted fact `2f9be517307232c7` follow from
\[
k-(M-m)=\frac{4209}{92400}>\varepsilon,
\qquad
M+m-k=\frac{57391}{92400}>0,
\]
while \(m<M+k\) is immediate.  Also
\[
\frac38-\frac mM=\frac1{24}>\varepsilon.
\tag{12}
\]

The general completion filter in accepted fact
`a7747c32a219e2c9` has the exact strict margin
\[
\begin{aligned}
&M+m-k_0-\frac52(c_1+c_2+c_3)
+3\max_i c_i-A\\
&\qquad=\frac{1129}{92400}
=\varepsilon>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, \(A=\varepsilon>0\) and the filter is not merely met at
equality.

The ordinary compatibility lower bound is passed with more than the
same margin:
\[
k_0-\varepsilon
-\frac14\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)^2
=\frac{10917}{30800}-\frac1{2\sqrt3}>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, \(10917/30800>1/3>1/(2\sqrt3)\).

There is also room for the stronger opposite-pair bound.  With the
formal slab mass \(s_3=\varepsilon\), its two endpoint terms are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi
&=
\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{q_3}\right)^2
+\frac14\left(\sqrt{p_0}+\sqrt{q_{12}}\right)^2\\
&=
\frac{5199+80\sqrt{546}+40\sqrt{210}}{11200}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Exact subtraction gives
\[
k_0-\Phi-s_3
=
\frac{82637-2640\sqrt{546}-1320\sqrt{210}}{369600}>0.
\tag{15}
\]
For an elementary exact sign certificate, use
\[
\sqrt{546}<\frac{117}{5},\qquad
\sqrt{210}<\frac{29}{2}.
\]
The numerator in (15) is then strictly larger than
\[
82637-61776-19140=1721>0.
\]
Thus the scalar lower-bound consequence of accepted fact
`9922bcace1cdc113` also leaves a strict positive gap.

For margin selection, an exact rational linear program introduced a
common \(\varepsilon\) as a lower bound on every allowed cell and on
the tails, slice-balance, low-ratio, three-gate, extension, and
completion-filter slacks.  The three possible indices for
\(\max u_i\), \(\min v_i\), and \(\max c_i\) were enumerated as
\(27\) exact order branches.  Even after dropping the radical
compatibility constraints, the exact global LP upper bound was
\(\varepsilon\leq1/58\).

To retain the stronger opposite-pair bound with a substantial margin,
exact LPs were then solved on the rational grid
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0&\in\left\{\frac45,\frac56,\frac67,\frac78,\frac89,\frac9{10}\right\},\\
q_3&\in\left\{\frac1{10},\frac{11}{100},\frac3{25},
\frac{13}{100},\frac7{50}\right\},\\
q_{12}&\in\left\{\frac1{100},\frac1{90},\frac1{85},
\frac1{80},\frac1{75},\frac1{70}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Among the \(127\) grid LP optima passing both exact radical bounds with
their full common margin, the largest was
\(\varepsilon=1129/92400\), attained by (1)--(3).  This grid statement
is only a margin-selection report, not a claim of global
semialgebraic optimality.

SageMath \(10.8\) independently checked every rational total, cap,
crossing, order, tail, gate, balance, and filter calculation over
\(\mathbb Q\).  Wolfram Language \(14.3\) independently checked
(14)--(15) with exact algebraic numbers.  No floating-point decision
enters the certificate.

The calibration is not asserted to have a simultaneous rational
common-fan polygonal realization.  It proves only that the corrected
scalar relaxation, including all three convexity gates, remains
strictly feasible.
