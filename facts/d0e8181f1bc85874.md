## Statement

Let \(A_0,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2\) be nonempty two-dimensional convex polytopes, relabeled so that
\[
M=|A_0|\ge |A_2|=m>0.
\]
Set
\[
B=\frac{A_0+A_2}{2},\qquad b=|B|,
\]
let \(g_B\) be the centroid of \(B\), and put
\[
S=(\{0\}\times A_0)\cup(\{1\}\times B)\cup(\{2\}\times A_2).
\]
Define
\[
\Phi(M,m,b)
=
\frac{4b}{9}
+
\max\left\{
0,\,
m-
\left(
\max\left\{0,2\sqrt{\frac{5b}{9}}-\sqrt M\right\}
\right)^2
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_S((1,g_B))
\ge
\min\left\{
M+b,\,
b+m,\,
\max\left\{
\Phi(M,m,b),\,
\min\left\{\frac{15b}{32},\frac{4b}{9}+\frac m4\right\}
\right\}
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, with
\[
r=\frac mM,\qquad s=\frac bM,
\]
the inequalities
\[
71s\ge64(1+r),
\qquad
8s+r\ge8
\tag{2}
\]
imply
\[
h_S((1,g_B))\ge\frac{2(M+m+b)}9.
\tag{3}
\]
The region (2) contains a nonempty part of the residual strip in accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`. One exact point is
\[
r=\frac1{10},\qquad s=1.
\tag{4}
\]
At this point the bounds in accepted facts `f8a1649f7072dce1` and `c2c5ebf411bfdef8` are both strictly below the target, so (1) is a genuine improvement.

## Proof

We first prove a one-dimensional centroid-cap lemma. Let \(f\) be a nonzero nonnegative concave piecewise-affine function on \([0,1]\), normalized by
\[
\int_0^1 f(t)\,dt=1,
\qquad
\alpha=\int_0^1 t f(t)\,dt.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha\le\frac12
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_0^\alpha f(t)\,dt\ge\frac{15}{32}.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(0<\tau<1\), define
\[
k_\tau(t)=
\begin{cases}
t/\tau,&0\le t\le\tau,\\[2mm]
(1-t)/(1-\tau),&\tau\le t\le1,
\end{cases}
\]
and set \(k_0(t)=1-t\), \(k_1(t)=t\). Every nonnegative concave piecewise-affine \(f\) has a representation
\[
f=\sum_j a_j k_{\tau_j},
\qquad
a_j\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, first subtract the affine chord joining the endpoint values of \(f\). The remaining concave function is nonnegative, vanishes at both endpoints, and each downward jump of its slope at \(\tau_j\) is supplied by a nonnegative multiple of \(k_{\tau_j}\). The difference between the resulting sum and the original function is affine and vanishes at both endpoints, hence is zero.

For every \(\tau\),
\[
\int_0^1 k_\tau(t)\,dt=\frac12,
\qquad
\frac{\int_0^1 t k_\tau(t)\,dt}
{\int_0^1 k_\tau(t)\,dt}
=\frac{1+\tau}{3}.
\tag{7}
\]
After normalizing the coefficients in (6), \(f\) is therefore a convex mixture of the probability densities \(2k_\tau\). If \(p_j\) are the mixture weights, then
\[
\sum_jp_j\tau_j=3\alpha-1.
\tag{8}
\]
The lower cumulative mass of \(2k_\tau\) at \(\alpha\) is
\[
P_\tau(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\alpha^2}{\tau},&\alpha\le\tau,\\[3mm]
1-\dfrac{(1-\alpha)^2}{1-\tau},&\tau\le\alpha.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]

Assume \(1/3\le\alpha\le1/2\), and define the affine function
\[
L_\alpha(\tau)
=
\alpha(2-\alpha)
-\frac{(2-\alpha)^2}{4}\tau.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\tau\ge\alpha\), this is the tangent line to the convex function \(\alpha^2/\tau\) at
\[
\tau_*=\frac{2\alpha}{2-\alpha},
\]
so \(P_\tau(\alpha)\ge L_\alpha(\tau)\). For \(0\le\tau\le\alpha\), direct subtraction gives
\[
P_\tau(\alpha)-L_\alpha(\tau)
=
\frac{
\tau\left[
\alpha(4-3\alpha)-\tau(2-\alpha)^2
\right]
}{4(1-\tau)}
\ge0,
\tag{11}
\]
because \(\tau\le\alpha\) and
\[
\alpha(4-3\alpha)-\alpha(2-\alpha)^2
=\alpha^2(1-\alpha)\ge0.
\]
Averaging (10) using (8) yields
\[
\int_0^\alpha f(t)\,dt
\ge
L_\alpha(3\alpha-1)
=
1-2\alpha+\frac{9\alpha^2}{4}-\frac{3\alpha^3}{4}.
\tag{12}
\]
The last expression is decreasing on \([1/3,1/2]\), since its derivative is
\[
-\frac{(3\alpha-2)(3\alpha-4)}4\le0.
\]
Its value at \(\alpha=1/2\) is \(15/32\), proving (5).

Fix now a direction \(q\in\mathbb S^1\) and a slope \(\lambda\), and use the tilted-cap notation of accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1`:
\[
x=
\left|
A_0\cap
\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le\lambda\}
\right|,
\]
\[
c=
\left|
B\cap
\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le0\}
\right|,
\]
\[
y=
\left|
A_2\cap
\{\langle q,z-g_B\rangle\le-\lambda\}
\right|.
\tag{13}
\]
Write
\[
\ell_B=\min_{z\in B}\langle q,z\rangle,\qquad
w_B=\max_{z\in B}\langle q,z\rangle-\ell_B,
\]
and
\[
\delta_q=\langle q,g_B\rangle-\ell_B.
\]
After rescaling the projection interval of \(B\) to \([0,1]\), its section-length function satisfies the hypotheses of (5), its normalized mean is
\[
\alpha=\frac{\delta_q}{w_B},
\]
and its cumulative mass at that mean is \(c/b\). Hence
\[
c<\frac{15b}{32}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta_q>\frac{w_B}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]

Let \(w_0,w_2\) be the two outer projection widths. Since
\[
w_B=\frac{w_0+w_2}{2},
\]
equation (14) gives
\[
\delta_q>\frac{w_0+w_2}{4}.
\tag{15}
\]
We claim that in this case
\[
x+y\ge\frac m4.
\tag{16}
\]

All outer-cap cases are as follows.

If \(x=0\), the sharper empty-cap estimate (2) in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8` gives
\[
y\ge
m\min\left\{
1,\left(\frac{2\delta_q}{w_2}\right)^2
\right\}
\ge\frac m4,
\]
because (15) implies
\[
\frac{2\delta_q}{w_2}>
\frac{1+w_0/w_2}{2}>\frac12.
\]
If \(y=0\), the symmetric empty-cap estimate gives
\[
x\ge
M\min\left\{
1,\left(\frac{2\delta_q}{w_0}\right)^2
\right\}
\ge\frac M4\ge\frac m4.
\]

Suppose next that both caps are positive and nonsaturated. The support-clearance estimate (1) in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8` and (15) give
\[
x+y
\ge
\min\left\{
m,\,
\frac{(w_0+w_2)^2Mm}
{4(mw_0^2+Mw_2^2)}
\right\}.
\]
With \(r=m/M\) and \(\theta=w_0/w_2\), the second entry divided by \(M\) is
\[
\frac{r(1+\theta)^2}{4(1+r\theta^2)}
\ge\frac r4,
\tag{17}
\]
because
\[
(1+\theta)^2-(1+r\theta^2)
=2\theta+(1-r)\theta^2\ge0.
\]
Thus (16) also holds in the positive nonsaturated case. Finally, if \(x=M\) or \(y=m\), then \(x+y\ge m\), so every saturated case satisfies (16) as well. This exhausts empty, positive, and saturated outer caps.

Accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1` gives
\[
c\ge\frac{4b}{9}
\tag{18}
\]
and imposes its conditional upper-cap Brunn-Minkowski constraint whenever \(x<M\) and \(y<m\). The estimate (16) was proved on a superset of the pairs satisfying that upper-cap constraint, so it remains valid after the constraint is imposed. Combining (14), (16), and (18), every tilted halfspace through \((1,g_B)\) has mass at least
\[
\min\left\{
\frac{15b}{32},\,
\frac{4b}{9}+\frac m4
\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
The same accepted fact, using the conditional upper-cap constraint, independently gives the exact area-only bound \(\Phi(M,m,b)\). Therefore every tilted halfspace has mass at least the maximum of \(\Phi\) and (19).

The two slice-parallel halfspaces through \((1,g_B)\) have masses \(M+b\) and \(b+m\). Translating any containing halfspace inward until its boundary passes through \((1,g_B)\) proves (1).

Under (2),
\[
\frac{15s}{32}\ge\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
\]
is exactly the first inequality in (2), while
\[
\frac{4s}{9}+\frac r4
\ge\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
\]
is exactly the second. Also
\[
1+s>\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
\]
for \(0<r\le1\), and the first inequality in (2) gives
\[
s+r>\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\]
Thus (1) implies (3).

At the exact point (4),
\[
\frac{15s}{32}-\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
=
\frac1{480},
\]
and
\[
\frac{4s}{9}+\frac r4-\frac{2(1+r+s)}9
=
\frac1{360}.
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e` places this point strictly in its residual strip because
\[
1-\frac1{10}<1<1+\frac1{10}.
\]

To check that the advance is genuinely beyond the two predecessor envelopes, first
\[
\frac{20s}{9}
>
\left(1+\sqrt r\right)^2,
\]
since
\[
1+\frac1{\sqrt{10}}<\frac43
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{16}{9}<\frac{20}{9}.
\]
Thus accepted fact `f8a1649f7072dce1` gives only
\[
\frac{\Phi}{M}=\frac49<\frac7{15}.
\]
For the support envelope in accepted fact `c2c5ebf411bfdef8`, put
\[
\theta=\frac{29-\sqrt{801}}2.
\]
This is the lower root of
\[
\theta^2-29\theta+10=0,
\]
hence the lower endpoint allowed by its mixed-width restriction at \(r=1/10,s=1\). Since
\[
\theta<\frac5{14},
\]
the corresponding support term satisfies
\[
\Psi_{1/10}(\theta)
=
\frac{(1+\theta)^2}{9(10+\theta^2)}
=
\frac{31\theta-9}{261\theta}
<
\frac1{45}.
\]
The area-only term \(H(1/10,1)\) is zero, so the tilted term in that accepted support-envelope synthesis is strictly below
\[
\frac49+\frac1{45}=\frac7{15}.
\]

Finally, the exact parameters (4) are geometrically realizable. Let
\[
A_0=\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2
\]
and let \(A_2\) be the centered rectangle with side lengths
\[
p=\frac{29+\sqrt{801}}{20},
\qquad
q=\frac{29-\sqrt{801}}{20}.
\]
Then
\[
pq=\frac1{10},\qquad p+q=\frac{29}{10},
\]
so
\[
|A_0|=1,\qquad |A_2|=\frac1{10},
\]
and
\[
\left|\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\right|
=
\frac{(1+p)(1+q)}4
=
1.
\]
The convex hull of \(\{0\}\times A_0\) and \(\{2\}\times A_2\) has these three integer slices, completing the nonempty residual realization.
