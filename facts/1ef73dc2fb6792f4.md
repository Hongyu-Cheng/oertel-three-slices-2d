## Statement

Let \(A,D,\lambda,\mu>0\), and put
\[
z=\frac{6}{200(A^2+D^2)-3(A+D+1)^2}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
U_0=\left(-A\lambda,\frac{(1+A)z}{\lambda}\right),
\qquad
V_0=\left((1+D)\lambda,-\frac{Dz}{\lambda}\right),
\tag{2}
\]
\[
E_0=\left(0,\frac z\lambda+\frac{3}{100A\lambda}\right),
\qquad
F_0=\left(\lambda+\frac{3\lambda}{100Dz},0\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(W_0\) be the intersection of the lines \(U_0E_0\) and
\(V_0F_0\), and assume that the indicated incidences are the edge
incidences of the nondegenerate triangle
\[
A_0=\operatorname{conv}\{U_0,V_0,W_0\},
\]
with \(W_0\) in the open first quadrant.

Let \(A',D'>1\) satisfy
\[
82A'^2-36A'D'+36A'+82D'^2+36D'-243=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
U_2=\left(-A'\mu,\frac{A'-1}{100\mu}\right),
\qquad
V_2=\left((D'-1)\mu,-\frac{D'}{100\mu}\right),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
E_2=\left(0,-\frac1{100\mu}+\frac9{400A'\mu}\right),
\qquad
F_2=\left(-\mu+\frac{9\mu}{4D'},0\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(W_2\) be the intersection of the lines \(U_2E_2\) and
\(V_2F_2\), and assume that the indicated incidences are the edge
incidences of the nondegenerate triangle
\[
A_2=\operatorname{conv}\{U_2,V_2,W_2\},
\]
with \(W_2\) in the open first quadrant. These are precisely the valid
members of the triangle construction; the rational parametrization of
\((A',D')\) is immaterial to the conclusion.

Let
\[
P=A_0\cap\{x\ge0,\ y\ge0\},
\qquad
Q=A_2\cap\{x\ge0,\ y\ge0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Assume
\[
\left|\frac{P+Q}{2}\right|<\frac{29}{80}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
\left|
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap\{x+y\ge-1\}
\right|
\ge
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{197}{200}}+\sqrt{\frac{41}{800}}
\right)^2
=
\frac{829+4\sqrt{8077}}{3200}
>
\frac{29}{80}.
\tag{9}
\]

## Proof

First record the exact cell areas. The line \(U_0V_0\) meets the
positive coordinate axes at
\[
(\lambda,0),\qquad \left(0,\frac z\lambda\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The two mixed cells have areas
\[
\frac{A\lambda}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{100A\lambda}
=\frac3{200},
\qquad
\frac{Dz/\lambda}{2}\cdot\frac{3\lambda}{100Dz}
=\frac3{200}.
\tag{11}
\]
A direct determinant evaluation gives
\[
|A_0|
=
\frac{3z(A+D+1)^2}
{2\left(100z(A^2+D^2)-3\right)}
=1,
\tag{12}
\]
where the last equality is (1). Hence
\[
|P|=\frac{97}{100}.
\tag{13}
\]

The line \(U_2V_2\) meets the negative coordinate axes at
\[
(-\mu,0),\qquad \left(0,-\frac1{100\mu}\right).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the negative-negative cell has area \(1/200\). Each mixed cell
has area
\[
\frac{A'\mu}{2}\cdot\frac9{400A'\mu}-\frac1{200}
=\frac1{160},
\tag{15}
\]
and analogously with \(D'\). A determinant evaluation gives
\[
|A_2|
=
\frac{9(A'+D'-1)^2}
{200(4A'^2+4D'^2-9)}
=\frac1{16};
\tag{16}
\]
the last equality is equivalent to (4). Consequently
\[
|Q|=\frac9{200}.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, the construction retains the advertised endpoint cell
vector.

Write
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=A\lambda,&
b&=\frac{Dz}{\lambda},\\
a'&=A'\mu,&
b'&=\frac{D'}{100\mu}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
These are respectively the negative \(x\)-depths at \(U_0,U_2\) and
the negative \(y\)-depths at \(V_0,V_2\).

We next extract the two exact consequences of the high-high
constraint. Put
\[
q_x=\mu\frac{9-4D'}{4D'},
\qquad
q_y=\frac{9-4A'}{400A'\mu}.
\tag{19}
\]
The conic (4), with
\[
S'=A'+D',
\qquad
P'=A'D',
\]
is
\[
82S'^2-200P'+36S'-243=0.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(S'^2\ge4P'\), equation (20) gives
\[
32S'^2+36S'-243\le0.
\]
The left side is increasing for \(S'>0\) and vanishes at \(S'=9/4\).
Therefore
\[
2<S'\le\frac94,
\qquad
1<A',D'<\frac54.
\tag{21}
\]
It follows that \(q_x,q_y>0\). By (6), the two axis segments
\[
[0,F_2]\subseteq Q,
\qquad
[0,E_2]\subseteq Q
\tag{22}
\]
have lengths \(q_x\) and \(q_y\), respectively.

Let \(w_P\) and \(h_P\) be the horizontal and vertical widths of
\(P\). Minkowski addition of a planar convex body and a horizontal
segment of length \(q_x\) increases area by exactly \(q_xh_P\):
integrate the interval lengths of the horizontal sections. The
vertical analogue increases area by \(q_yw_P\). Equations (8) and
(22), together with \(|P|=97/100\), therefore give
\[
q_xh_P<\frac{29}{20}-\frac{97}{100}
=\frac{12}{25},
\qquad
q_yw_P<\frac{12}{25}.
\tag{23}
\]

The points in (10) imply
\[
w_P\ge\lambda,
\qquad
h_P\ge\frac z\lambda.
\tag{24}
\]
Also
\[
q_xq_y
=
\frac{(9-4A')(9-4D')}{1600A'D'}
\ge\frac1{100},
\tag{25}
\]
because (25) is equivalent to \(A'+D'\le9/4\). Multiplying the
weaker versions of (23) supplied by (24) yields
\[
q_xq_yz<\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^2.
\]
Thus
\[
z<\frac{576}{25}.
\tag{26}
\]

We will also use the stronger bounds
\[
w_P\ge a,\qquad h_P\ge b.
\tag{27}
\]
Here is an elementary verification. Solving for \(W_0\) from (2)-(3)
and then using (1) gives
\[
(W_0)_x
=
\lambda\frac{A(197A-3D-3)}{3(A+D+1)},
\tag{28}
\]
\[
(W_0)_y
=
\frac z\lambda\cdot
\frac{D(197D-3A-3)}{3(A+D+1)}.
\tag{29}
\]
The horizontal width is at least the larger of \((W_0)_x\) and the
\(x\)-coordinate of \(F_0\). If \(A\le1\), the latter is
strictly larger than \(\lambda\ge A\lambda\). If \(A>1\) and
\[
97A\ge3(D+1),
\]
then (28) is at least \(A\lambda\). It remains to consider
\[
D>\frac{97A-3}{3}.
\tag{30}
\]
Let
\[
\Delta=200(A^2+D^2)-3(A+D+1)^2=\frac6z.
\]
The difference needed to compare \(F_0\) with \(A\lambda\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta-200D(A-1)
={}&197A^2-206AD+197D^2\\
&{}-6A+194D-3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
At \(D=(97A-3)/3\), the right side of (31) is
\[
\frac{9400}{9}A(191A-6)>0,
\]
and its derivative with respect to \(D\) there is
\[
\frac{37600}{3}A-200>0.
\]
The right side of (31) is a convex quadratic in \(D\). Hence (30)
implies \(\Delta>200D(A-1)\), so
\[
\frac{(F_0)_x}{\lambda}
=1+\frac{\Delta}{200D}>A.
\]
This proves \(w_P\ge a\). Interchanging \(A,D\) and \(x,y\) proves
\(h_P\ge b\), establishing (27).

Equations (19), (21), (23), and (27) now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
a'b
&=
\frac{4A'D'}{9-4D'}\,q_xb
<
\frac{25}{16}\cdot\frac{12}{25}
=\frac34,\\
ab'
&=
\frac{4A'D'}{9-4A'}\,q_ya
<
\frac34.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Also
\[
a'b'=\frac{A'D'}{100}<\frac1{64}.
\tag{33}
\]

It remains only to bound \(ab=ADz\). Put
\[
S=A+D,\qquad R=AD,\qquad w=\frac1z.
\]
Equation (1) is equivalent to
\[
400R=197S^2-6S-3-6w.
\tag{34}
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(R/w=ADz\ge1\), so \(R\ge w\).
Together with \(R\le S^2/4\), equation (34) gives
\[
197S^2-6S-3\ge406w,
\tag{35}
\]
\[
97S^2-6S-3\le6w.
\tag{36}
\]
Eliminating \(w\) from (35)-(36) gives
\[
191S^2-12S-6\le0.
\tag{37}
\]
At \(S=1/4\), the left side of (37) is \(47/16>0\), and it is
strictly increasing for \(S\ge1/4\). Hence \(S<1/4\), so
\[
R\le\frac{S^2}{4}<\frac1{64}.
\]
On the other hand, (26) says
\[
w>\frac{25}{576}>\frac1{64},
\]
contradicting \(R\ge w\). Therefore
\[
ab<1.
\tag{38}
\]

Combining (32), (33), and (38) proves the support-free symbolic
inequality
\[
(a+a')(b+b')
=ab+ab'+a'b+a'b'
<
1+\frac34+\frac34+\frac1{64}
=\frac{161}{64}
<4.
\tag{39}
\]
Thus at least one of the following two alternatives holds:
\[
a+a'<2
\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
b+b'<2.
\tag{40}
\]
This is the promised finite decomposition.

If \(b+b'<2\), put
\[
C_{0,x}=A_0\cap\{x\ge0\},
\qquad
C_{2,x}=A_2\cap\{x\ge0\}.
\]
Every point of \((C_{0,x}+C_{2,x})/2\) has \(x\ge0\) and
\[
y\ge-\frac{b+b'}2>-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{C_{0,x}+C_{2,x}}2
\subseteq
\frac{A_0+A_2}{2}\cap\{x+y\ge-1\}.
\tag{41}
\]
The endpoint cell vector gives
\[
|C_{0,x}|=\frac{197}{200},
\qquad
|C_{2,x}|=\frac{41}{800}.
\tag{42}
\]
Planar Brunn-Minkowski applied in (41) proves the first inequality in
(9). If \(a+a'<2\), the identical argument with
\[
C_{0,y}=A_0\cap\{y\ge0\},
\qquad
C_{2,y}=A_2\cap\{y\ge0\}
\]
uses the same two areas and gives the same conclusion.

Finally,
\[
\frac14\left(
\sqrt{\frac{197}{200}}+\sqrt{\frac{41}{800}}
\right)^2
=
\frac{829+4\sqrt{8077}}{3200}.
\]
Its gap above \(29/80\) is
\[
\frac{4\sqrt{8077}-331}{3200}>0,
\]
because \(16\cdot8077=129232>109561=331^2\). This proves (9).
