## Statement

Accepted fact `eef70ce3abe7606e` makes
\[
\delta=\frac4{169}
\]
the critical upper value of its normal-form parameter \(D\). There do not exist real numbers \(L,a,c,k\) such that
\[
\frac3{10}\le L<1,\qquad
0<a<\frac{507}{467},\qquad c>0,\qquad k>0,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
t:=a-c-k<0,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
c+a\bigl(1-\delta(c+k)\bigr)+Lk\le1,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\frac{k}{1-L}+\frac{t}{1-\delta a}\le2c^2,
\tag{4}
\]
and, on putting
\[
P=2L^2+\delta-\frac{1-L}{k},
\qquad
Q=2a^2+t,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
P>0,\qquad Q>0,\qquad PQ\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the displayed algebraic \(t<0\) relaxation is empty. Consequently, once
the reported reduction from the \(D\le4/169\) stratum to (1)--(6) is verified,
that geometric branch is closed.

## Proof

Put
\[
u=-t=c+k-a>0,
\qquad
x=1-L.
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
c=a+u-k,
\qquad
0<x\le\frac7{10},
\tag{8}
\]
and (3) becomes
\[
c+a\bigl(1-\delta(a+u)\bigr)+Lk\le1.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(L\ge3/10\) and \(k=a+u-c\), equation (9) gives
\[
\frac7{10}c+\frac{13}{10}a+\frac3{10}u
-\delta a(a+u)\le1.
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore
\[
c\le
\frac{10-13a-3u+10\delta a(a+u)}7.
\tag{11}
\]

The bound on \(a\) in (1) gives
\[
10\delta a=\frac{40a}{169}<1.
\]
Since \(a+u>0\), equation (11) implies the simpler strict bound
\[
c<
\frac{10-12a-2u}{7}
=\frac{2(5-6a-u)}7.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
6a+u<5,
\qquad
0<a<\frac56.
\tag{13}
\]
The last inequality also gives
\[
\delta a<\frac{10}{507},
\qquad
\frac1{1-\delta a}
<\frac{507}{497}
<\frac{50}{49}.
\tag{14}
\]

Equation (4), together with (7), says
\[
\frac{k}{x}
\le
2c^2+\frac{u}{1-\delta a}.
\tag{15}
\]
By (12)--(14), the right side of (15) is strictly smaller than
\[
R
:=
\frac{8(5-6a-u)^2+50u}{49}.
\tag{16}
\]
All quantities being positive, (15) yields
\[
\frac{x}{k}>\frac1R.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(L<1\), equations (5) and (17) give
\[
P
<2+\delta-\frac1R
=\frac{342}{169}-\frac1R.
\tag{18}
\]
Also, by (2) and (5),
\[
Q=2a^2-u>0.
\tag{19}
\]

We now prove the exact two-variable inequality
\[
\left(\frac{342}{169}-\frac1R\right)Q<1
\tag{20}
\]
throughout the triangle (13), whenever the parenthesis is positive. Define
\[
H(a,u)
:=
Q-\left(\frac{342}{169}Q-1\right)R.
\tag{21}
\]
Then (20) is equivalent to \(H(a,u)>0\). On the closed triangle
\[
a\ge0,\qquad u\ge0,\qquad 6a+u\le5
\]
the exact expansion is
\[
\begin{aligned}
8281H(a,u)={}&33800-81120a-71566a^2+328320a^3-196992a^4\\
&+55049u-147936au+119016a^2u-65664a^3u\\
&-8908u^2+32832au^2-5472a^2u^2+2736u^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Parameterize this triangle
by
\[
a=\frac56 y,
\qquad
u=5(1-y)z,
\qquad
0\le y,z\le1.
\tag{22}
\]
After substituting (16), (19), and (22), \(H\) is a polynomial of bidegree
\((4,3)\) in \((y,z)\).

For a polynomial of bidegree \((r,s)\) on the unit square, write it in the tensor Bernstein basis
\[
\sum_{i=0}^r\sum_{j=0}^s
\beta_{ij}
\binom ri y^i(1-y)^{r-i}
\binom sj z^j(1-z)^{s-j}.
\tag{23}
\]
Every basis function in (23) is nonnegative. Hence positivity of all
\(\beta_{ij}\) proves nonnegativity of the polynomial.

Partition the \(y\)-interval into four equal parts and the \(z\)-interval into two equal parts. On the rectangle indexed by
\[
(i,j)\in\{0,1,2,3\}\times\{0,1\},
\]
substitute
\[
y=\frac{i+Y}{4},
\qquad
z=\frac{j+Z}{2},
\qquad
0\le Y,Z\le1.
\tag{24}
\]
Direct expansion of (21)--(24) in the bidegree-\((4,3)\) Bernstein basis has the following least coefficient on each rectangle:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
i\backslash j&0&1\\ \hline
0&\dfrac{2360375}{1192464}&\dfrac{7117855}{733824}\\[5pt]
1&\dfrac{387925}{596232}&\dfrac{3205}{936}\\[5pt]
2&\dfrac{2398075}{6359808}&\dfrac{674095}{1059968}\\[5pt]
3&\dfrac{14485}{37632}&\dfrac{1615345}{14309568}
\end{array}
\tag{25}
\]
Every entry is strictly positive. Thus every Bernstein coefficient on every rectangle is positive, and
\[
H(a,u)>0
\tag{26}
\]
on the open triangle (13). This proves (20).

If the right side of (18) were nonpositive, it would contradict \(P>0\). Hence it is positive. Multiplying (18) by \(Q>0\) and using (20) gives
\[
PQ<1,
\]
contrary to (6). Therefore the displayed \(t<0\) system has no solution.
