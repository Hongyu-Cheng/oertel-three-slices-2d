## Statement

For \(b,c,z\geq0\), put
\[
J(b,c,z)=\int_0^z\min\{c,(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\}\,dp.
\]
For \(0<r\leq1\), \(0\leq x\leq r\), and \(0\leq y\leq1\), put
\[
D_r(b;x,y)=J(b,1,x)+J(b,y,r)-J(b,y,x),
\]
and put
\[
F(q)=\max_{0\leq z\leq q}J(q-z,1,z).
\]
Then:

1. If \(11/18\leq q\leq2/3\), \(r\leq16q/17\), and
   \(b+x+y\leq q\), then
   \[
   D_r(b;x,y)\leq F(q).
   \]
   In fact this remains true for \(1/2\leq q\leq2/3\).

2. If \(11/18\leq q\leq2/3\), then
   \[
   F(q)\leq\frac{9q}{16}-\frac5{32}.
   \]

Consequently every counterexample to the locked three-slice problem
satisfies
\[
\frac{\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\}}{\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\}}<\frac38.
\]

## Proof

### 1. Exact formulas for \(J\) and \(F\)

For \(u\geq0\), define the uncapped curved area
\[
K_b(u)=\int_0^u(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\,dp
=
\begin{cases}
4bu-\dfrac83\sqrt b\,u^{3/2}+\dfrac{u^2}{2},
&0\leq u\leq4b,\\[2mm]
\dfrac83b^2,&u\geq4b.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
u=\min\{z,4b\}.
\]
If \(4b\leq c\), then
\[
J(b,c,z)=K_b(u).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(4b>c\), set
\[
\alpha_c=(2\sqrt b-\sqrt c)^2.
\]
Then the exact formula is
\[
J(b,c,z)
=c\min\{u,\alpha_c\}
+K_b(u)-K_b(\min\{u,\alpha_c\}).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)-(3) include \(b=0\), \(c=0\), all touching cases, and
exhaustion at \(z=4b\).

Write
\[
f_q(z)=J(q-z,1,z),\qquad 0\leq z\leq q.
\]
For \(1/2\leq q\leq2/3\), define
\[
z_a(q)=
\left(\frac{2\sqrt{5q-1}-1}{5}\right)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
The four exact pieces of \(f_q\) are
\[
f_q(z)=
\begin{cases}
z,&0\leq z\leq z_a(q),\\[2mm]
\Phi(q-z,z),&z_a(q)\leq z\leq q-\dfrac14,\\[2mm]
K_{q-z}(z),&q-\dfrac14\leq z\leq\dfrac{4q}{5},\\[2mm]
\dfrac83(q-z)^2,&\dfrac{4q}{5}\leq z\leq q,
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
\Phi(b,z)=
\frac{-16\sqrt b\,z^{3/2}-16\sqrt b-16b^2
+24bz+24b+3z^2+3}{6}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the first transition is
\[
z=(2\sqrt{q-z}-1)^2,
\]
which is equivalent, after putting \(u=\sqrt z\), to
\[
5u^2+2u+1=4q
\]
and gives (4). The second transition is \(q-z=1/4\), and the
last is \(z=4(q-z)\). Thus (5) also identifies every transition in the
integral defining \(F\).

There is exactly one maximizer of \(f_q\), and it lies strictly in the
second piece of (5). To prove this, put
\[
b=q-z,\qquad t=\sqrt{\frac zb}.
\]
Direct differentiation of (6) along \(b=q-z\) gives
\[
f_q''(z)=
-\frac{37}{3}+4t-\frac2t+\frac23t^3
+\frac{2}{3b^{3/2}}.
\tag{7}
\]
On the second piece, \(b\geq1/4\), \(z\leq5/12\), and
\(t<4/3\). The \(t\)-dependent part in (7) is increasing, so
\[
f_q''(z)
<
-\frac{37}{3}+\frac{16}{3}-\frac32+\frac{128}{81}
+\frac{16}{3}
=-\frac{257}{162}<0.
\tag{8}
\]
The first piece has slope \(1\). At \(b=1/4\), and throughout the
third piece, one has \(1\leq t\leq2\) and
\[
f_q'(z)
=b\left(4-4t-3t^2+\frac43t^3\right)<0.
\tag{9}
\]
The polynomial in parentheses is negative at \(t=1\), and its derivative
is \(2(2t+1)(t-2)\leq0\). The fourth piece is decreasing. Equations
(8)-(9), together with continuity at the transitions, prove the asserted
unique central maximizer.

We now give an exact parametrization of that maximizer. Put
\[
a=\sqrt b,\qquad u=\sqrt z,\qquad h=2a-u.
\tag{10}
\]
At the central maximizer, \(0<h<1\), and differentiating (6) gives
\[
3a f_q'(z)=4-12a+15ah^2-4h^3.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently
\[
a=A(h):=\frac{4(1-h^3)}{12-15h^2},
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
q=Q(h):=A(h)^2+(2A(h)-h)^2,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
F(q)=V(h):=
\frac{3-16A(h)+24A(h)^2-8A(h)h^3+3h^4}{6}.
\tag{14}
\]

For completeness, \(Q\) is strictly increasing on the entire part of this
branch relevant below. Since the maximizer has \(a>1/2\), equation (12)
implies
\[
8h^3-15h^2+4<0.
\]
The polynomial on the left is decreasing up to \(h=3/5\) and has value
\(41/125>0\) there, so
\[
h>\frac35.
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover
\[
A'(h)=
\frac{4h(10-12h+5h^3)}{3(4-5h^2)^2}>0.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(U(h)=2A(h)-h\), then
\[
U'(h)=
-\frac{48-80h-24h^2+35h^4}{3(4-5h^2)^2}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, the numerator in (17) equals \(-513/125\) at \(h=3/5\), and
its derivative is
\[
-80-48h+140h^3<-80+64h<0
\]
because \(h^2<4/5\). Thus
\[
Q'(h)=2A(h)A'(h)+2U(h)U'(h)>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Direct differentiation also gives the useful identity
\[
V'(h)=h^2Q'(h).
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, if \(h(q)\) denotes the unique solution of \(Q(h)=q\),
then
\[
F'(q)=h(q)^2.
\tag{20}
\]

### 2. The affine upper bound for \(F\)

Two exact rational evaluations are
\[
Q\left(\frac{73}{100}\right)-\frac{11}{18}
=\frac{3411690757}{3210408450000}>0,
\tag{21}
\]
\[
\frac3{16}-V\left(\frac{73}{100}\right)
=\frac{1354884109883}{7134241\cdot10^9}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(Q\) and \(V\) are increasing, (21)-(22) imply
\[
F\left(\frac{11}{18}\right)<\frac3{16}.
\tag{23}
\]
At the other end,
\[
Q\left(\frac34\right)
=\frac{37529}{51984}
>\frac23,
\qquad
Q\left(\frac34\right)-\frac23
=\frac{2873}{51984}.
\tag{24}
\]
It follows from (18) and (24) that \(h(q)<3/4\) for
\(q\leq2/3\). Hence (20) gives
\[
F'(q)<\frac9{16}
\qquad
\left(\frac{11}{18}\leq q\leq\frac23\right).
\tag{25}
\]
Integrating (25) from \(11/18\), and using (23), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(q)
&<\frac3{16}+\frac9{16}\left(q-\frac{11}{18}\right)\\
&=\frac{9q}{16}-\frac5{32}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
This proves the second registered inequality, with a strict margin on the
whole closed interval.

We shall also need a lower comparison for \(F\). For
\(1/2\leq q\leq2/3\), the point \(z=2q/5\) is in the central piece:
\(b=3q/5>1/4\), \(z<4b\), and
\[
\sqrt{\frac q5}(2\sqrt3-\sqrt2)<1.
\]
The last inequality follows at \(q=2/3\) from
\(13<8\sqrt6\), and is stronger for smaller \(q\). Substitution in
(6) gives
\[
F(q)\geq
\frac12-\frac{8\sqrt q}{\sqrt{15}}+\frac{12q}{5}
-\frac{2(-3+8\sqrt6)q^2}{75}.
\tag{27}
\]
After subtracting \(12q^2/25\), the right side is
\[
\Delta(q)=
\frac{75-80\sqrt{15q}+360q-60q^2-32\sqrt6\,q^2}{150}.
\tag{28}
\]
Its derivative is
\[
\Delta'(q)=
-\frac{4}{75\sqrt q}
\left(5\sqrt{15}-45\sqrt q+(15+8\sqrt6)q^{3/2}\right).
\tag{29}
\]
The expression in parentheses, as a function of \(x=\sqrt q\), has
derivative
\[
-45+3(15+8\sqrt6)x^2>0
\qquad(x^2\geq1/2).
\]
Thus \(\Delta'\) changes sign at most once, from positive to negative,
so the minimum of \(\Delta\) on \([1/2,2/3]\) is at an endpoint.
Those endpoint values are
\[
\Delta\left(\frac12\right)
=\frac{4(30-\sqrt6-5\sqrt{30})}{75}>0,
\tag{30}
\]
\[
\Delta\left(\frac23\right)
=\frac{2595-128\sqrt6-720\sqrt{10}}{1350}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
For exact elementary certificates, use
\[
\sqrt6<\frac{49}{20},\qquad
\sqrt{30}<\frac{137}{25},\qquad
\sqrt{10}<\frac{3163}{1000}.
\]
They make the numerator in (30) greater than \(3/20\), and the numerator
in (31) greater than \(101/25\). Therefore
\[
F(q)>\frac{12q^2}{25}
\qquad
\left(\frac12\leq q\leq\frac23\right).
\tag{32}
\]

### 3. Exact fixed-sum split

We prove the first inequality in the slightly stronger range
\(1/2\leq q\leq2/3\). Set
\[
R=\frac{16q}{17}.
\tag{33}
\]
The function \(D_r\) is nondecreasing in \(r\), and \(J\) is
nondecreasing in its first argument. Hence it is enough to take
\[
r=R,\qquad b=q-x-y.
\]
Put \(z=x+y\). For fixed \(z\), a maximizing split can be chosen with
\(x\geq y\). If \(x<y\leq R\), reflect the symmetric curved region
\[
\sqrt p+\sqrt w\leq2\sqrt{q-z}
\]
in the square \([0,R]^2\); outside that square, swapping \(x,y\)
enlarges the vertical threshold. If \(y>R\), replace the split by
\((R,z-R)\), whose vertical strip covers the full \(p\)-range.
Thus write
\[
x=z-y,\qquad
\max\{0,z-R\}\leq y\leq\frac z2.
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal H(y)=D_R(q-z;z-y,y),
\]
\[
A(p)=\min\{1,(2\sqrt{q-z}-\sqrt p)_+^2\},
\qquad
p_y=(2\sqrt{q-z}-\sqrt y)_+^2.
\]
The function \(\mathcal H\) is absolutely continuous, and at every
differentiability point
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=
-A(x)+\min\{y,A(x)\}
+(\min\{R,p_y\}-x)_+.
\tag{34}
\]
If \(y\geq A(x)\), then \(p_y\leq x\) and
\(\mathcal H'(y)=0\); this is a terminal interval. If
\(y<A(x)\) and \(p_y<R\), direct simplification gives
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=
2(\sqrt x-\sqrt y)
(2\sqrt{q-z}-\sqrt x-\sqrt y)\geq0.
\tag{35}
\]

It remains to rule out an interior local maximum in the chamber
\(p_y\geq R\). There
\[
\mathcal H'(y)=R-z+2y-A(x).
\tag{36}
\]
If \(A(x)=1\), the right side has derivative \(2\), so it cannot cross
from positive to negative. Otherwise put
\[
c=2\sqrt{q-z},\qquad u=\sqrt x,\qquad W=\sqrt z.
\]
At a zero of (36),
\[
R=c^2-2cu+3u^2-W^2,\qquad
q=\frac{c^2}{4}+W^2.
\tag{37}
\]
A downward crossing would require
\[
3-\frac cu<0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad u<\frac c3.
\tag{38}
\]
Since \(x\geq y\), one has \(W^2=z\leq2u^2\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
17R-16q
&=13c^2-34cu+51u^2-33W^2\\
&\geq13c^2-34cu-15u^2\\
&=c^2\left(1-3\frac uc\right)
\left(13+5\frac uc\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
But (33) makes the left side zero. This contradiction excludes every
interior local maximum. The formulas in (34)-(36) agree at their
transition boundaries, so no nondifferentiable boundary creates one.
Thus the maximum is at the lower split endpoint or at \(y=z/2\).

At the lower endpoint, if \(z\leq R\), its value is
\[
J(q-z,1,z)=f_q(z).
\]
If \(z>R\), its value is \(J(q-z,1,R)\leq f_q(z)\). Consequently
\[
D_R(q-z;x,y)\leq
\max\left\{
f_q(z),
D_R\left(q-z;\frac z2,\frac z2\right)
\right\}.
\tag{40}
\]

### 4. The balanced endpoint

It remains to bound the second term in (40). Write
\[
t=\frac z2,\qquad b=q-2t,\qquad R=\frac{16q}{17}.
\]
Since \(t<R\),
\[
D_R(b;t,t)
=J(b,1,t)+\int_t^R\min\{t,(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\}\,dp.
\tag{41}
\]
Dropping the cap at height \(1\) in the first term gives
\[
D_R(b;t,t)\leq
K_b(t)+\int_t^R\min\{t,(2\sqrt b-\sqrt p)_+^2\}\,dp.
\tag{42}
\]
Put \(v=t/q\). By the homogeneity \(p=qP\), the right side of
(42) is \(q^2H(v)\), where \(0\leq v\leq1/2\),
\[
B=1-2v,\qquad R_0=\frac{16}{17},
\]
and
\[
H(v)=K_B(v)+
\int_v^{R_0}\min\{v,(2\sqrt B-\sqrt P)_+^2\}\,dP.
\tag{43}
\]
We prove
\[
H(v)<\frac{12}{25}.
\tag{44}
\]

First suppose \(0\leq v\leq4/17\). The function
\[
2\sqrt{1-2v}-\sqrt v
\]
is decreasing and equals \(4/\sqrt{17}\) at \(v=4/17\).
Thus the second integrand in (43) is identically \(v\), and
\[
H(v)=
\frac{84}{17}v-\frac{17}{2}v^2
-\frac83\sqrt{1-2v}\,v^{3/2}.
\tag{45}
\]
Using \(\sqrt{1-2v}\geq3/\sqrt{17}\), and setting
\(y=\sqrt{17v}\in[0,2]\), gives
\[
H(v)\leq
U(y):=
\frac{84y^2-8y^3}{289}-\frac{y^4}{34}.
\tag{46}
\]
Now
\[
U'(y)=\frac{2y(84-12y-17y^2)}{289}.
\]
Its unique interior maximum is at
\[
y_*=\frac{-6+2\sqrt{366}}{17}.
\]
An exact evaluation gives
\[
\frac{12}{25}-U(y_*)
=\frac{12(-922093+48800\sqrt{366})}{35496425}>0.
\tag{47}
\]
Both terms inside the last parentheses are positive, and the squared
margin is
\[
48800^2\cdot366-922093^2=21351539351>0.
\]
This proves (44) on the first interval.

Now let \(4/17\leq v\leq1/3\). Put
\[
s=\sqrt{\frac{v}{1-2v}},\qquad
p_v=(2\sqrt{1-2v}-\sqrt v)^2.
\]
Then \(s\geq2/3\), \(v\leq p_v\leq R_0\), and differentiation of
(43), with cancellation at the moving boundary \(p_v\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H'(v)
={}&2(p_v-v)\\
&-2\left(
\int_0^v\partial_B(2\sqrt B-\sqrt P)^2\,dP
+\int_{p_v}^{R_0}
\partial_B(2\sqrt B-\sqrt P)^2\,dP
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
The second integral is nonnegative, while
\[
\int_0^v\partial_B(2\sqrt B-\sqrt P)^2\,dP
=4B\left(s^2-\frac{s^3}{3}\right),
\]
and
\[
p_v-v=4B(1-s).
\]
Since
\[
s^2-\frac{s^3}{3}\geq1-s
\qquad(2/3\leq s\leq1),
\tag{49}
\]
equation (48) gives \(H'(v)\leq0\). Inequality (49) holds at
\(s=2/3\) with margin \(1/81\), and its left side minus its right
side has derivative \(2s-s^2+1>0\).

Finally, if \(v\geq1/3\), then \(B\leq v\), so for \(P\geq v\),
\[
(2\sqrt B-\sqrt P)_+^2\leq v.
\]
Thus (43) is the full curved area
\[
H(v)=\int_0^{R_0}(2\sqrt{1-2v}-\sqrt P)_+^2\,dP,
\]
which is nonincreasing in \(v\). At the joining point
\[
H\left(\frac4{17}\right)=\frac8{17}<\frac{12}{25}.
\]
This proves (44) on all of \([0,1/2]\).

Combining (32), (42), and (44) gives
\[
D_R\left(q-z;\frac z2,\frac z2\right)
<\frac{12q^2}{25}<F(q).
\tag{50}
\]
Equations (40) and (50) prove
\[
D_r(b;x,y)\leq F(q)
\]
for \(1/2\leq q\leq2/3\), and hence prove the first registered
inequality.

### 5. Global three-eighths deduction

Assume for a contradiction that the locked problem has a counterexample
with
\[
r=\frac{\min\{|A_0|,|A_2|\}}
        {\max\{|A_0|,|A_2|\}}\geq\frac38.
\]
Scale area so that the larger endpoint has area \(1\), the smaller has
area \(r\), and write
\[
s=|A_1|,\qquad q=\frac{2(1+r+s)}9.
\tag{51}
\]
Accepted fact `34518372116b7c5f` already gives
\[
r<\frac{23}{40}.
\tag{52}
\]
Accepted fact `15bb2c1680a0254e`, which is one of the predecessors used in
`34518372116b7c5f`, gives the residual inequalities
\[
1-r<s<\min\{1+r,2-r\}.
\tag{53}
\]
In the present range \(r\geq3/8>9/25\), the same accepted residual
strip gives
\[
s>\frac{3r+2\sqrt r-1}{2}>\frac{16}{25}>\frac59.
\tag{53a}
\]

We record the parameter bounds needed to apply the scalar result. Put
\[
\delta=\frac{2(1+r-s)}9.
\]
The universal centroid criterion in accepted fact `c02e26ea799dadd7`, used
in accepted fact `34518372116b7c5f`, says that a counterexample must satisfy
\[
\left(1+\sqrt{r-\delta}\right)^2<\frac{20s}{9}.
\tag{54}
\]
Reversibly taking square roots and then squaring, using the residual
lower bound \(s>5/9\), shows that (54) is equivalent to
\[
7r-11-18s+12\sqrt{5s}<0.
\]
The relevant larger root is
\[
S_c(r)=
\left(\frac{2\sqrt5+\sqrt{14r-2}}6\right)^2,
\]
so every such counterexample satisfies
\[
s>S_c(r).
\tag{55}
\]
The smaller root is below \(5/9\), so it cannot occur in the residual
strip.

Equations (51), (52), and (55) imply
\[
\frac{8}{15}<q<\frac23,
\qquad
r<\frac{16q}{17}.
\tag{56}
\]
Here is an exact audit. The lower bound
\[
\frac{2(1+r+S_c(r))}{9}>\frac8{15}
\]
is weakest at \(r=3/8\), where its margin is
\[
-\frac{91}{1080}+\frac{\sqrt{65}}{81}>0;
\]
equivalently \(40\sqrt{65}>273\). The upper bound follows from
\(s<2-r\). For the second inequality in (56), one has throughout
\([3/8,23/40]\)
\[
S_c(r)-\left(\frac{121r}{32}-1\right)>0.
\tag{57}
\]
The left side decreases on this interval and at \(r=23/40\) equals
\[
\frac{1849}{11520}>0.
\]
Indeed, its derivative is
\[
\frac{112\sqrt{10}-977\sqrt{7r-1}}
     {288\sqrt{7r-1}}<0.
\]
Equation (57) gives \(q>17r/16\), proving (56).

Invoke the canonical three-row certificate of accepted fact
`d2d117bcc58a1ba0` and the canonical rank-product reduction proved in
accepted fact `34518372116b7c5f`. The boundary-inclusive planar
Brunn--Minkowski input used by that rank reduction is accepted fact
`756b87a16a89ea8f`. Together they supply three rows with middle cap areas
\(b_i\), endpoint cap areas \(x_i,y_i\), and midpoint graph areas
\(d_i\), satisfying
\[
b_i+x_i+y_i<q,\qquad
r\leq\sum_{i=1}^3d_i,\qquad
d_i\leq D_r(b_i;x_i,y_i).
\tag{58}
\]
Let \(g\) be the centroid of \(A_1\). Since the three row functions sum
to \(-1\), some row \(k\) satisfies \(f_k(g)<0\). The halfplane through
\(g\) contained in that middle cap has area at least \(4s/9\) by the
planar centroid inequality recorded in accepted fact
`2f9be517307232c7`. Hence
\[
b_k\geq\frac{4s}{9}.
\tag{59}
\]
The cylinder bound from the same rank reduction gives
\[
d_k\leq x_k+ry_k\leq x_k+y_k
<q-\frac{4s}{9}
=\delta.
\tag{60}
\]
For the other two rows, (56), (58), and the first scalar inequality give
\[
d_i\leq F(q).
\]
Thus
\[
r<\delta+2F(q).
\tag{61}
\]

We show that the right side of (61) is at most \(r\). First suppose
\[
\frac38\leq r<\frac12
\]
and put
\[
\bar q=\frac{4(1+r)}9.
\tag{62}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{11}{18}\leq\bar q<\frac23,\qquad
q<\bar q,\qquad
\delta=\bar q-q.
\tag{63}
\]
On the maximizer branch,
\[
Q\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
=\frac{35}{6}-\frac{304\sqrt2}{81}
<\frac8{15};
\tag{64}
\]
the exact margin in (64) is
\[
\frac{-4293+3040\sqrt2}{810}>0,
\qquad
3040^2\cdot2-4293^2=53351.
\]
Equations (20), (56), and (64) therefore give
\[
F'(q)>\frac12
\]
throughout the interval from \(q\) to \(\bar q\). Hence
\(-q+2F(q)\) is strictly increasing, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta+2F(q)
&<2F(\bar q)\\
&\leq\frac{9\bar q}{8}-\frac5{16}\\
&=\frac{3+8r}{16}\leq r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{65}
\]
The last inequality is strict for \(r>3/8\). At \(r=3/8\), the first
inequality in (65) remains strict, so (65) still contradicts (61).

Finally suppose \(r\geq1/2\). Equation (55) gives
\[
q>\frac{11}{18},
\]
with equality on the lower boundary at \(r=1/2\). Applying (26) directly,
and using \(q<2/3\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta+2F(q)
&\leq\frac{4(1+r)}9-q+\frac{9q}{8}-\frac5{16}\\
&<\frac{4(1+r)}9-\frac{11}{48}\\
&<r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{66}
\]
At \(r=1/2\), the final margin in (66) is \(1/16\), and it increases
with \(r\). This is the separate \(r\geq1/2\) endpoint branch.

Both (65) and (66) contradict (61). Therefore every counterexample has
\[
r<\frac38,
\]
as claimed.
