## Statement

Put
\[
\Pi=\{(y_1,y_2,y_3):y_1+y_2+y_3=1\},\qquad
p=\frac{199}{300},
\]
\[
D=\{y\in\Pi:y_i\leq p\ (i=1,2,3)\},\qquad
\delta=3p-1=\frac{99}{100},\qquad
\lambda_i=\frac{p-y_i}{\delta}.
\]
Thus \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) are barycentric coordinates on
\(D\), and its three sides are
\(S_i=\{\lambda_i=0\}\).

Let \(A_2\) and \(K\) be the fixed rational polygons in accepted facts
`f22ebf3f8e4e4666` and `007cd9d294748c86`. Let \(A\) be any compact
two-dimensional convex polygon satisfying the invariant consequences
of an actual terminal first-event release:
\[
|A|=
M:=
\frac{481\,886\,268\,000\,037\,895\,503}
     {2\,295\,616\,388\,316\,451\,200\,000},
\tag{1}
\]
each endpoint cap \(A\cap\{y_i>p\}\) has area zero,
\[
\frac{A+A_2}{2}\subseteq K,
\tag{2}
\]
and every genuine vertex of \(A\) lies on \(\partial D\). Repeated
listed points and points made collinear at the release event may have
been deleted, in any number.

Then
\[
A\cap S_i\neq\varnothing\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, the full crossing, cell-positivity, contact, and
zero-charge capacity hypotheses of the actual terminal state imply
(3), since accepted facts `f22ebf3f8e4e4666` and
`007cd9d294748c86` certify (1), (2), preservation of the zero caps,
two-dimensionality through the first event, and deletion without
changing the convex set.

## Proof

First, the zero-cap condition and two-dimensionality imply
\(A\subseteq D\). Indeed, suppose that some \(x\in A\) had
\(y_i(x)>p\). Choose interior points \(z_1,z_2\) such that
\(x,z_1,z_2\) are noncollinear. For all sufficiently small
\(\varepsilon>0\), both
\(x+\varepsilon(z_j-x)\) still satisfy \(y_i>p\). Their convex hull
with \(x\) is a positive-area triangle contained in
\(A\cap\{y_i>p\}\), contradicting the zero area of that cap.

For every \(x\in A\) and every vertex \(q\) of \(A_2\), (2) gives
\((x+q)/2\in K\), hence \(x\in2K-q\). Therefore
\[
A\subseteq
D\cap\bigcap_{q\in\operatorname{vert}(A_2)}(2K-q)=:F.
\tag{4}
\]
Accepted fact `faa6f23d89f50659` gives this exact admissible endpoint
region and, in the barycentric coordinates above, its
counterclockwise vertices:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\lambda_1&\lambda_2&\lambda_3\\ \hline
v_0&\dfrac{1\,604\,909}{4\,410\,450}&
     \dfrac{2\,805\,541}{4\,410\,450}&0\\[1mm]
v_1&\dfrac{54\,077}{381\,150}&
     \dfrac{327\,073}{381\,150}&0\\[1mm]
v_2&0&\dfrac{99\,373}{153\,450}&
     \dfrac{54\,077}{153\,450}\\[1mm]
v_3&0&\dfrac{437}{1782}&\dfrac{1345}{1782}\\[1mm]
v_4&\dfrac{437}{4455}&0&\dfrac{4018}{4455}\\[1mm]
v_5&\dfrac{877\,259}{3\,682\,800}&0&
     \dfrac{2\,805\,541}{3\,682\,800}.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
F\cap S_1=[v_2,v_3],\qquad
F\cap S_2=[v_4,v_5],\qquad
F\cap S_3=[v_0,v_1].
\tag{6}
\]

Suppose that \(A\cap S_i=\varnothing\) for some \(i\). Every genuine
vertex of \(A\) lies on \(\partial D=S_1\cup S_2\cup S_3\), so every
such vertex lies on the other two sides. By (4) and (6), \(A\), which
is the convex hull of its genuine vertices, is contained in the
corresponding four-endpoint hull:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{missing side}&\text{containing hull}\\ \hline
S_1&Q_1=\operatorname{conv}\{v_4,v_5,v_0,v_1\}\\
S_2&Q_2=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\}\\
S_3&Q_3=\operatorname{conv}\{v_2,v_3,v_4,v_5\}.
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
This containment does not require either contact interval in (6) to
have two distinct terminal vertices.

The physical area of \(D\) is
\[
\Delta=\frac{\delta^2}{2}=\frac{9801}{20000}.
\tag{8}
\]
Using \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\), the standard barycentric triangle has
coordinate area \(1/2\), so a shoelace area in those coordinates is
multiplied by \(2\Delta\). Exact substitution of (5) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &|Q_i|&M-|Q_i|\\ \hline
Q_1&
\dfrac{4\,700\,003\,535\,239}{33\,145\,200\,000\,000}&
\dfrac{305\,404\,067\,125\,655\,072\,143}
      {4\,483\,625\,758\,430\,568\,750\,000}\\[3mm]
Q_2&
\dfrac{2\,394\,647\,111\,471}{21\,751\,537\,500\,000}&
\dfrac{2\,772\,093\,294\,078\,263\,403\,463}
      {27\,769\,553\,084\,473\,200\,000\,000}\\[3mm]
Q_3&
\dfrac{1\,986\,705\,764\,389}{31\,136\,400\,000\,000}&
\dfrac{324\,929\,350\,963\,953\,271\,213\,529}
      {2\,223\,878\,376\,181\,562\,100\,000\,000}.
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]
Every entry in the last column is strictly positive. Thus (7) would
give
\[
|A|\leq |Q_i|<M,
\]
contradicting (1). Hence no side can be missing, which proves (3).

It remains to check that deletion and degeneracy do not evade the
argument. Deleting a repeated point or a point lying between its two
neighbors does not change the convex set, its area, (2), its caps, or
its side contacts. If such a redundant point lies on a supporting
side \(S_i\), both endpoints of the containing segment also lie on
\(S_i\), because the nonnegative affine coordinate \(\lambda_i\)
vanishes at their convex combination only when it vanishes at both.
The same observation covers simultaneous collinear deletions.
One-point side contacts, triangle-corner contacts, and zero-length
support faces merely reduce the set of genuine vertices used in (7).
Finally, collapse to a segment or point is impossible because the
preserved value \(M\) in (1) is positive. Thus all deletion and
terminal-incidence degeneracies are included.
