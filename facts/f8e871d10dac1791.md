## Statement

Assume the surviving transformed triangular \(N=3\) system of accepted
facts `09393c6edf1fc4e2` and `64760c76cadff481`.  Thus
\(P_0,P_1,P_2\) are nondegenerate triangles in the parallel planes
\[
\sum_i x_i=1+q,\qquad \sum_i x_i=1,\qquad
\sum_i x_i=1-q,
\]
respectively, where \(q>1\), their normalized areas are
\[
|P_0|=A,\qquad |P_1|=1,\qquad |P_2|=B,
\qquad 0<A<B,
\]
and
\[
\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
For \(i=1,2,3\), put
\[
u_i=\frac{|P_0\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|}{A},\qquad
c_i=|P_1\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|,\qquad
v_i=\frac{|P_2\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|}{B},
\]
\[
t=\frac{2(1+A+B)}9,
\]
and assume the three strict inequalities
\[
Au_i+c_i+Bv_i<t.
\]
Let \(g_j\) be the area centroid of \(P_j\), and assume
\[
I_0:=\{i:(g_0)_i\geq0\}=\{1,2\},\qquad
I_1:=\{i:(g_1)_i\geq0\}=\{3\}.
\]
Write
\[
r=\frac AB,\qquad S=\frac1B.
\]
By accepted facts `291c24bb45bd4946` and `6f36d5017fd780b5`, every
counterexample in this orbit has \(r<3/8\), and for \(r>1/5\) it already
lies above
\[
S_c(r)
=\left(\frac{2\sqrt5+\sqrt{14r-2}}6\right)^2
=\frac{9+7r+\sqrt{40(7r-1)}}{18}.
\]

Suppose first that \(t\leq1/2\).  Label the vertices of \(P_1\), let
\(\ell_{ij}\) be coordinate \(i\) of vertex \(j\), and put
\[
J_i=\{j:\ell_{ij}>0\}.
\]
After a column permutation,
\[
(\ell_{31},\ell_{32},\ell_{33})=(p,-a,-b),
\qquad p,a,b>0,\qquad p-a-b>3,
\]
and
\[
c_3=\frac{p^2}{(p+a)(p+b)}<t\leq\frac12.
\]
For \(i=1,2\),
\[
0<c_i<
\kappa:=\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49,
\qquad \sum_j\ell_{ij}<0.
\]
Consequently, on every open vertex-sign stratum, \(J_1\) and \(J_2\)
each have cardinality one or two.  Up to interchanging rows \(1,2\) and
interchanging columns \(2,3\), the complete list of remaining open sign
chambers is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
J_1&J_2\\ \hline
\{2\}&\{3\}\\
\{2\}&\{1,3\}\\
\{2\}&\{2,3\}\\
\{1,2\}&\{2,3\}\\
\{2,3\}&\{2,3\}.
\end{array}
\]
No singleton \(J_i=\{1\}\) is possible.  Zero vertex entries are not
discarded: they are boundary strata of this finite list and remain
governed by the repeated-value formulas of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481`.

The exact cap constraints inside any listed chamber are as follows.  A
negative-mean one-positive row, after permuting its entries, has the form
\[
(x,-d,-e),\qquad x,d,e>0,\qquad x<d+e,
\]
and satisfies
\[
\frac{x^2}{(x+d)(x+e)}<\kappa.
\]
A negative-mean two-positive row has the form
\[
(-d,x,y),\qquad d,x,y>0,\qquad d>x+y,
\]
and satisfies
\[
1-\frac{d^2}{(d+x)(d+y)}<\kappa.
\]
Together with
\(\sum_i\ell_{ij}=1\), these are the literal middle-row constraints in
the five chambers.

Moreover the large-endpoint centroid satisfies
\[
(g_2)_1<0,\qquad (g_2)_2<0,\qquad (g_2)_3<0.
\]
Hence
\[
h:=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}\in P_1,\qquad h_3<0,\qquad
h_1+h_2>1.
\]
There is therefore an \(i\in\{1,2\}\) with \(h_i>1/2\), and for that
index
\[
c_i\geq\frac B9.
\]
More precisely, the compatible homothetic triangles
\[
\frac{g_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1,\qquad
\frac{P_0+g_2}{2}\subseteq P_1
\]
have centroid \(h\) and areas \(B/4\) and \(A/4\), respectively, so
their coordinate-\(i\) caps have areas at least \(B/9\) and \(A/9\).

Finally, without assuming \(t\leq1/2\), row \(3\) satisfies the exact
necessary complement-allocation inequality
\[
\sqrt{A-\left(t-\frac49\right)}+\sqrt B
<\frac{2\sqrt5}{3}.
\]
For \(r>1/5\) in the residual strip, this inequality is equivalent to
\[
S>S_c(r).
\]
Thus this rowwise complement argument has exactly the already accepted
global boundary and yields no additional scalar exclusion above it.

## Proof

Accepted fact `13c0df55864b79eb` gives, in the assigned incidence orbit,
\[
u_1,u_2\geq\frac49,\qquad c_3\geq\frac49,
\]
\[
c_i+Bv_i<\frac{2(1+B-A)}9<\frac49
\quad(i=1,2),
\]
and
\[
Au_3+Bv_3<
\frac{2(A+B-1)}9<\frac{4A}{9}.
\]
It also gives \(A+B>1\) and \(B<1+A\).  Accepted fact
`291c24bb45bd4946` gives \(r=A/B<3/8\).

Assume \(t\leq1/2\).  Part 3 of accepted fact
`13c0df55864b79eb`, applied to the singleton \(I_1=\{3\}\), says that
the third middle row has exactly one positive vertex value and two
strictly negative values.  After a column permutation it is
\((p,-a,-b)\), with \(p,a,b>0\).  The singleton strengthening in part
1 of that fact gives \((g_1)_3>1\).  Since the centroid of a triangle is
the average of its three vertices,
\[
\frac{p-a-b}{3}>1,
\]
which proves \(p-a-b>3\).  The cap formula of accepted fact
`64760c76cadff481` gives the displayed expression for \(c_3\).

For \(i=1,2\), the cross-cap inequality above gives
\[
c_i<\kappa<\frac49.
\]
The coordinate caps of \(P_1\) cover \(P_1\), because every point of
\(P_1\) has coordinate sum one.  Therefore
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3\geq1.
\]
If, for example, \(c_1=0\), then
\[
c_1+c_2+c_3<0+\frac49+\frac12<1,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \(c_1,c_2>0\).  Since \(1,2\notin I_1\), the
first two middle-row means are strictly negative.  On an open
vertex-sign stratum, each of those two rows therefore has at least one
and at most two positive entries.

The column sums now give the finite sign reduction.  Since
\(p-a-b>3\), in particular \(p>1\), and hence the sums of the first two
rows in columns \(1,2,3\) are
\[
1-p<0,\qquad 1+a>0,\qquad 1+b>0.
\]
Thus the two positive supports cannot both contain column \(1\), and
their union must contain columns \(2\) and \(3\).

It remains to exclude a singleton support at column \(1\).  Suppose,
after interchanging rows \(1,2\), that the first row is
\[
(x,-d,-e),\qquad x>0,\quad d,e\geq0,\quad d+e>0.
\]
The third row is \((p,-a,-b)\), so the column identities force the
second row to be
\[
(-u,v,w)
=(1-x-p,\ 1+d+a,\ 1+e+b),
\]
where \(u=x+p-1>0\) and \(v,w>0\).  Since the first and third caps are
strictly below \(1/2\), their one-positive cap formulas give
\[
(x+d)(x+e)>2x^2,\qquad
(p+a)(p+b)>2p^2.
\]
Also
\[
u+v=(x+d)+(p+a),\qquad
u+w=(x+e)+(p+b).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(u+v)(u+w)
&\geq
\left(
\sqrt{(x+d)(x+e)}
+\sqrt{(p+a)(p+b)}
\right)^2\\
&>2(x+p)^2>2(x+p-1)^2=2u^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The exact two-positive formula now yields
\[
\mathcal C(-u,v,w;0)
=1-\frac{u^2}{(u+v)(u+w)}
>\frac12,
\]
contradicting \(c_2<4/9\).  Hence no singleton support is \(\{1\}\).

Two nonempty subsets of \(\{1,2,3\}\), each of size at most two, with
neither a singleton \(\{1\}\), not both containing \(1\), and whose
union contains \(2,3\), give exactly the five displayed pairs after the
two allowed interchanges.  This proves the open-chamber list.  Allowing
one or more vertex values to tend to zero gives its boundary strata;
accepted fact `64760c76cadff481` supplies their exact closed-cap
formulas.  The two displayed one-/two-positive cap formulas and their
mean inequalities follow directly from the same accepted cap formula.

We next determine the large-endpoint centroid.  For \(i=1,2\),
\[
v_i<
\frac{2(1+B-A)}{9B}<\frac49,
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \(1<A+B\).  For \(i=3\),
\[
v_3<\frac{4A}{9B}=\frac{4r}{9}<\frac16<\frac49.
\]
The elementary exact triangle implication proved in accepted fact
`a61bda3f7065e3e1` says that a coordinate-cap fraction strictly below
\(4/9\) forces the corresponding centroid coordinate to be strictly
negative.  It follows that every coordinate of \(g_2\) is negative.

Since \(3\notin I_0\), also \((g_0)_3<0\).  Compatibility from accepted
fact `09393c6edf1fc4e2` gives
\[
h=\frac{g_0+g_2}{2}
\in\frac{P_0+P_2}{2}\subseteq P_1.
\]
The coordinate sums of \(g_0,g_2\) are \(1+q,1-q\), so the coordinates
of \(h\) sum to one.  Hence \(h_3<0\) and
\[
h_1+h_2=1-h_3>1.
\]
Choose \(i\in\{1,2\}\) with \(h_i>1/2\).

The set
\[
K_2:=\frac{g_0+P_2}{2}
\]
is a homothetic copy of the triangle \(P_2\), lies in
\((P_0+P_2)/2\subseteq P_1\), has area \(B/4\), and has centroid \(h\).
The coordinate halfplane \(x_i\geq0\) contains that centroid.  The
planar triangle centroid inequality used in accepted fact
`13c0df55864b79eb` therefore gives
\[
|K_2\cap\{x_i\geq0\}|
\geq\frac49\,\frac B4=\frac B9.
\]
Since this cap is contained in the corresponding cap of \(P_1\),
\(c_i\geq B/9\).  Applying the same argument to
\[
K_0:=\frac{P_0+g_2}{2}
\]
gives the companion lower bound \(A/9\).  This uses homothetic
centroid additivity only; it does not make the false assertion that the
area centroid of a general Minkowski sum is the sum of the two area
centroids.

For completeness, we finish with the exact complement-allocation
calibration.  Put
\[
X=Au_3,\qquad Y=Bv_3,\qquad
b_0=t-\frac49=\frac{2(A+B-1)}9.
\]
The third strict row and \(c_3\geq4/9\) give
\[
X+Y<b_0.
\]
The cross-cap bound gives
\[
0<b_0<\frac{4A}{9}<A\leq B.
\]
Let
\[
D_j=P_j\cap\{x_3\leq0\}\qquad(j=0,1,2).
\]
No coordinate functional is constant on a nondegenerate triangle in a
coordinate-sum plane, so its zero line has area zero.  Therefore
\[
|D_0|=A-X,\qquad |D_1|=1-c_3,\qquad |D_2|=B-Y.
\]
Compatibility gives
\[
\frac{D_0+D_2}{2}\subseteq D_1.
\]
Planar Brunn--Minkowski yields
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}\leq2\sqrt{1-c_3}
\leq\frac{2\sqrt5}{3}.
\]

Set \(s=X+Y<b_0\).  For fixed \(s\), concavity of
\[
\xi\longmapsto
\sqrt{A-\xi}+\sqrt{B-s+\xi},
\qquad 0\leq\xi\leq s,
\]
puts its minimum at an endpoint.  The smaller endpoint is
\(\xi=s\), because for \(0\leq s<A\leq B\),
\[
\sqrt A-\sqrt{A-s}
\geq\sqrt B-\sqrt{B-s}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sqrt{A-X}+\sqrt{B-Y}
\geq\sqrt{A-s}+\sqrt B
>\sqrt{A-b_0}+\sqrt B.
\]
Combining the last two displays proves the asserted strict necessary
inequality.

Substituting \(A=r/S\), \(B=1/S\) gives
\[
A-b_0=\frac{7r+2S-2}{9S}>0,
\]
and the necessary inequality becomes
\[
3+\sqrt{7r+2S-2}<2\sqrt{5S}.
\]
The residual strip has \(S>16/25>9/20\), so
\(2\sqrt{5S}-3>0\); the other radical is real and strictly positive by
the preceding formula.  Thus isolating and squaring are reversible and
give
\[
7r-11-18S+12\sqrt{5S}<0.
\]
For \(r>1/5\), accepted fact `6f36d5017fd780b5` proves that this is
equivalent in the residual strip to \(S>S_c(r)\).  Direct expansion
checks that its accepted expression is exactly
\[
\left(\frac{2\sqrt5+\sqrt{14r-2}}6\right)^2
=\frac{9+7r+\sqrt{40(7r-1)}}{18}.
\]
Hence the complement-allocation boundary is precisely the already
accepted global centroid boundary, completing the claimed calibration
and the orbit-specific structural reduction above it.
